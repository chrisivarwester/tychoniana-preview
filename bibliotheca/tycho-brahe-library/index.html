Bibliotheca Tychoniana — The Library of Tycho Brahe

Reconstruction key tycho-brahe-library · edition of 2026-09-05 · provisional and growing.

A reconstruction of the library of Tycho Brahe: what is attested as his, where each surviving copy is today, how the collection scattered after his death in 1601, and what remains to be found. The full edition — catalogue, numbered register, surviving volumes with their links, concordance, holdings, identification key, chronology, desiderata, history and sources — is the explorer; this page is the summary.

The figures, counted to the unit

  • 204 attested entries — documented claims that a work was in Tycho's possession (survivor-derived, reconstruction-derived, or letter-derived; grain varies per entry).
  • 107 works located as Tycho's own, surviving in 66 physical volumes — 19 volumes carry more than one itemised work (60 works between them); 47 carry a single itemised work, several of these being physically Sammelbände whose other constituents are not yet itemised.
  • 51 volumes / 88 works are in the Prague Clementinum (National Library of the Czech Republic).
  • 61 works readable online in 32 digitised volumes.
  • 89 attested titles still missing — 56 judged findable, 31 retired as unlocatable, 2 recorded as lost.
  • 71 survivor records = 66 of his own + 2 collection-afterlife (post-Tycho imprints / not-a-Tychonianum) + 3 candidate copies examined and ruled out.

None of these is a survival rate. Most located entries are known precisely because a copy survives; of the whole library — perhaps 1,000–3,000 volumes (Mortensen's calibration vs. the disputed larger figure) — the 66 located volumes are roughly 2–7% of it. Any ratio of located to attested measures the compilation, not the survival: a work enters the located tier because a copy of it was found, and most of that tier was compiled from catalogues of the surviving Prague volumes in the first place.

Source stratigraphy

The two tiers are built in opposite directions: the located spine works backwards, from a surviving volume to the entry it proves; the attested-but-lost tier works forwards, from Norlind's catalogue and Tycho's own letters to a book that has never been found.

The Clementinum gap. Kleinschnitzová (1933) records 97 works in 47 volumes; Šolc's chapter in Tycho Brahe and Prague: Crossroads of European Science (2002, pp. 329–332) extends this to about 110 titles in ~50 volumes. This store itemises 88 Prague works in 51 volumes, including every addition Šolc flags: the newly-identified Sign. XXXI H 26 (a Vorillong Sentences commentary carrying Tycho's own handwritten initials), and the bound-in titles — Commandino, Pitatus and Valesius inside the FK27 gnomonics volume; a second Rantzau tract in FK3; Candidus's De regibus Bohemicis in FK32; the two dialogues (Antimachus, Nicomedes) in FK15; the Brevis explicatio doctrinae sinuum in FK21. Part of the residual difference to about 110 is a matter of grain: four constituents Šolc counts separately and this store folds into their parent works — the Regiomontanus oration and the Melanchthon epistle that are front-matter of the FK16 Alfraganus, and two further cometary tracts inside the FK46 collection. That accounts for four of the 22, and the rest of the gap is not explained. It is stated here rather than closed: this store has not yet itemised every work Šolc counts.
