7The journeys

Twenty-one documented route sequences. Only the stations the sources name are listed, in the order the evidence supports; the lines drawn on the map join those stations and are not claimed roads. Where the accounts differ — and for the flight from Denmark in 1597 three incompatible routes are on record — the disagreement is printed rather than resolved.

First study journey: Copenhagen to Leipzig, February–March 1562

A14 February 1562 – 24 March 1562journey-copenhagen-leipzig-1562
PurposeThe prescribed foreign educational journey of a young Danish noble: sent by Jørgen Brahe and Inger Oxe to Germany's best law school, nominally for jurisprudence, with the twenty-year-old Anders Sørensen Vedel as preceptor, guardian and treasurer.
Copenhagen → Wittenberg → Leipzig
Departed Copenhagen 14 February 1562, arrived Leipzig 24 March — five and a half weeks by ship across the southern Baltic, then on horseback along the Elbe and Saale, passing through Wittenberg and riding two more days to Leipzig. Wittenberg is the only intermediate station any source names; Dreyer, Friis, Norlind, Jönsson and Christianson 2020 name none. Kinsmen had arranged a good reception in Leipzig in advance.
Evidence (9)
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), p. 16
“Vedel and his pupil left Copenhagen on the 14th February 1562, and arrived at Leipzig on the 24th March following”
Friis, Tyge Brahe (1871), Friis 1871, p. 12
“De forlode Kjøbenhavn ... den 14. Februar 1562, og ankom til Leipzig den 24. Marts.”
Norlind, Tycho Brahe: En levnadsteckning (1970), p. 16
“till vilken han anlände d. 24 mars 1562 efter att ha lämnat Köpenhamn d. 14 febr.”
Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990), p. 14
“The journey by ship across the Baltic and on horseback along the roads that followed the Elbe and the Saale rivers took five weeks”
Håkansson (ed.), Att låta själen flyga (2006), p. 44
“I februari 1562 korsade de södra Östersjön, för att därefter färdas längs Elbe till Wittenberg, varifrån de landvägen fortsatte till Leipzig”
Jönsson, Tycho Brahe: Världsmedborgaren från Ven (2025), pp. 14–16
“Den 14 februari 1562 lämnade han Danmark i sällskap med den 20-årige Anders Sorensen Vedel.”
Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020), p. 22
“in February 1562 they set off to Germany's best law school at Leipzig University”
Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei Vita (1654/55), p. 5
“destinatus cum Paedagogo Lipsiam, illac sub Martii finem pervenit”
Christianson, 'Tycho Brahe's Facts of Life' (1970), p. 24
“He left Copenhagen on February 14, 1562.”
The sources disagree. A source record spanning the whole decade of the 1560s, with an ordering its own source cannot verify after Leipzig, is reduced here to the attested Copenhagen–Leipzig leg.

Return from Leipzig to wartime Denmark, May–June 1565

A17 May 1565 – June 1565journey-leipzig-home-1565
Purposetravel-transit
Leipzig → Wittenberg → Rostock → Copenhagen → Helsingborg → Malmö → Knutstorp
The best-dated of the early journeys: Tycho and Vedel left Leipzig on 17 May 1565 (Hegelund's date), passed through Wittenberg, reached Rostock on 25 May (Stephanius), and crossed the war-time Baltic to Copenhagen without meeting hostile cruisers, the Danish and Swedish fleets massing in the Sound. The onward legs — Copenhagen, Helsingborg, Malmö, Knutstorp, with latitude determinations under way — are Norlind's probable reading of the pole-height list in the oldest observation journal. Gassendi has a direct sail from Rostock to the fatherland.
Evidence (7)
Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990), pp. 19–20
“They passed through Wittenberg to see Schultz... reached Rostock on 25 May... Tycho proceeded to Helsingborg, Malmö, and finally Knudstrup Manor”
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), pp. 21–22
Friis, Tyge Brahe (1871), Friis 1871 p. 18
Norlind, Tycho Brahe: En levnadsteckning (1970), p. 20
“Annotationes interiectae occasione puto itineris Lipsia per Witebergam, Rostochium”
Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei Vita (1654/55), p. 8
Christianson, 'Tycho Brahe's Facts of Life' (1970), Christianson, Facts of Life (F&F 1970), p. 24
“He left Leipzig on May 17, 1565. He came to Rostock on May 25, 1565.”
Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020), p. 29
“In late May 1565, after three years in Leipzig, Tycho and Vedel came home to a Denmark locked in war”
The sources disagree. Gassendi's reading of the return as a recall occasioned by Jørgen Brahe's death is rejected (Norlind): the uncle died 21 June, a month after departure. The Scanian waypoints rest on the journal inference; Gassendi has the ship sail 'recta in patriam' from Rostock. Christianson 2020 ends the journey at Aalborghus rather than Knutstorp (see stay-knutstorp-1565 disputes). A parallel stationless record of the same return (Jönsson: probably at Jørgen Brahe's urging; Christianson 2020: home in late May) was merged here.

Second departure: Denmark to Wittenberg, April 1566

Ato 15 April 1566 (arrival within the month)journey-second-study-tour-1566
Purposeeducation
Knutstorp → Wittenberg
Early in 1566 Tycho left Denmark for the second time — without a noble hovmester, in the company of the M.A. Hans Aalborg and at least one other Dane — arriving at Wittenberg on 15 April 1566 (Stephanius: 'Returned to Wittenberg on April 15, 1566, from his fatherland'). Gassendi frames the departure as hastened 'not without a show of anger' at his relations' contempt for his studies.

Plague flight: Wittenberg to Rostock, September 1566

A16 September 1566 – 24 September 1566journey-plague-flight-1566
Purposetravel-transit
Wittenberg → Rostock
An epidemic emptied the congested little university town: Tycho and Aalborg left Wittenberg on 16 September 1566 and reached Rostock on 24 September, 'fleeing on account of the plague' (Stephanius), an eight-day journey; he matriculated at Rostock the following month.

Southward across Germany: Rostock to Basel, 1568

Bto September 1568 (arrival within the year)journey-south-to-basel-1568
Purposeeducation
Rostock → Nuremberg → Basel
During 1568 — under the shadow of the unpaid duel fine, and freed from law by the canonry promise — Tycho travelled south across Germany, purchasing instruments at Nuremberg (Christianson 2020), and was matriculated at Basel by the autumn. The exact route is otherwise unrecorded: Thoren allows a possible meeting with his brother Steen at Arnstadt for the dubbing by Count Günther of Schwarzburg, and a 'perhaps Wittenberg' — both too hedged to log as stays. The traditional intermediate Wittenberg residence (Gassendi, Dreyer, Friis) is rejected by Norlind.
Evidence (8)
Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990), pp. 30, 93
“During the summer of 1568, Tycho traveled southward across Germany. Perhaps he met his brother Steen at Arnstadt”
Norlind, Tycho Brahe: En levnadsteckning (1970), pp. 23–24 left Rostock spring 1568; Basel autumn 1568; the 1568 Wittenberg stay rejected as a misunderstanding
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), pp. 28–29
“he was at Wittenberg some time in 1568, and went to Basle in the course of the same year”
Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020), pp. 32, 34 hedged early legs; Nuremberg and Ingolstadt placed on this southbound route in this account
Håkansson (ed.), Att låta själen flyga (2006), Håkansson ed. 2006, scan p. 43
“Från Rostock reste han till Wittenberg, varifrån han snart fortsatte till Basel”
Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei Vita (1654/55), p. 11
“Anno postea partim illeic partim Wittebergae transacto, lustrare ulterius Germaniam decrevit”
Jönsson, Tycho Brahe: Världsmedborgaren från Ven (2025), p. 17
Friis, Tyge Brahe (1871), Friis 1871 p. 27 Friis has winter 1568–69 at Wittenberg and routes Rostock–Wittenberg–Augsburg with no Basel residence at all
The sources disagree. The intermediate 1568 Wittenberg stay (Gassendi 'partim illeic partim Wittebergae'; Dreyer with a Mechanica citation; Friis, who even winters him there 1568–69 and omits Basel entirely) is rejected by Norlind — Dreyer's citation is to the wrong edition's foliation, and the documented Basel matriculation supersedes Friis's route; the Zusammenfassung keeps only 'über Wittenberg (?)'. Thoren's Arnstadt/Castle Neideck meeting for Steen Brahe's dubbing is speculative in both Thoren and Christianson 2020 and is not logged as a stay.

Basel to Augsburg by the Danube road, early 1569

BDecember 1568 – 14 April 1569journey-basel-to-augsburg-1569
Purposeeducation
Basel → Freiburg im Breisgau → Lauingen → Ingolstadt → Augsburg
Leaving Basel at the turn of 1568/69, Tycho turned the road to Augsburg into a tour of south-German astronomical practitioners: Schreckenfuchs's planetary models at Freiburg, the night-long dinner with Leovitius at Lauingen, and (on Norlind's redating) the call on Philip Apian at Ingolstadt, reaching Augsburg in time to observe from 14 April 1569.
Evidence (6)
Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990), p. 30
“In Freiburg he noted some celestial models... In Lauingen he was invited home... In Ingolstadt he met Phillip Apian... Only in the spring of 1569 when he reached the old imperial city of Augsburg”
Norlind, Tycho Brahe: En levnadsteckning (1970), pp. 23–24 Lauingen and Ingolstadt on the Basel–Augsburg leg, argued to spring 1569
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), pp. 28–30
Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020), pp. 34–36
Friis, Tyge Brahe (1871), Friis 1871 pp. 27–28
Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei Vita (1654/55), p. 11
The sources disagree. Waypoint order and attachment vary: Thoren has Freiburg–Lauingen–Ingolstadt after Basel; Christianson 2020 places Nuremberg and Ingolstadt before Basel and Freiburg as an excursion from it; Gassendi, Dreyer and Håkansson move the Ingolstadt call to the 1570 homeward journey. Norlind's spring-1569 placement of Ingolstadt is followed.

Homeward from Augsburg to Helsingborg, May–December 1570

A16 May 1570 – 30 December 1570journey-augsburg-home-1570
Purposebirth-family
Augsburg → Wittenberg → Rostock → Helsingborg
An unhurried homecoming whose route, previously unknown, is partly fixed by Norlind's find: last Quadrans Maximus observation at Augsburg 16 May 1570; the Textor album leaf proving Wittenberg on 9 September 1570; probably a call on the Rostock professors (Thoren); across the Baltic; and observing at Helsingborg Castle by 30 December 1570, the sextant and unfinished globe left in Paul Hainzel's keeping at Augsburg. The older tradition routed him through Ingolstadt with a Copenhagen landfall; the motive — his father's illness — is itself questioned.
Evidence (7)
Norlind, Tycho Brahe: En levnadsteckning (1970), pp. 26–28
“Witteberge: Anno: 1570: sept: 9.” last Augsburg observation 16 May 1570; Helsingborg observations 30 December 1570
Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990), p. 36
“Tycho did not reach Wittenberg until September and probably visited with his former professors in Rostock before crossing the Baltic to Denmark”
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), pp. 34–35
“Tycho Brahe left Augsburg in 1570, but the exact month is not known, nor the route by which he travelled”
Friis, Tyge Brahe (1871), Friis 1871 p. 30
“forlod idetmindste Augsburg henimod Slutningen af Aaret, og allerede inden Aarets Udgang var han i Helsingborg”
Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei Vita (1654/55), p. 16
“Traiecto Danubio, vidit Ingolstadii Philippum Appianum... postquam tamen Hafniam appulsus”
Håkansson (ed.), Att låta själen flyga (2006), Håkansson ed. 2006, p. 49
Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020), p. 40
The sources disagree. Friis's late-1570 departure from Augsburg is superseded by the album leaf (Wittenberg 9 September). The Ingolstadt call placed on this journey by Gassendi, Dreyer and Håkansson is redated to spring 1569 by Norlind (followed). Gassendi's Copenhagen landfall, court salute and 1571 homecoming date are rejected on the 30 December 1570 Helsingborg observations (Norlind). The father's-illness motive (Friis, Jönsson, Håkansson 'probably') is doubted by Norlind given the unhurried pace and Otte's death only in May 1571. The Rostock call is Thoren's 'probably'.

The early-1574 Skane-Oresund observing circuit

CJanuary 1574 – May 1574journey-skane-oresund-circuit-1574
PurposeObservation circuit between the family estates, the Bille abbey and the capital, documented from Tycho's manuscript observation books by the 1746 Danske Magazin compiler.
Knutstorp → Herrevad Abbey → Copenhagen
The compiler's continuous itinerary sentence has Tycho at the start of 1574 on his Skåne estates, observing at Herrevad Abbey in March and April (Dreyer, from the same books, extends the run into May), and in Copenhagen from late April and May, where he mostly lived for the rest of the year. The compiler also places him on Hven on 22 February 1574 'af hvad Anledning vides ikke'; that leg is excluded here because Dreyer showed the observed Mars-moon conjunction fits 22 February 1576, not 1574 — the first documented Hven visit therefore falls outside this period.
Evidence (2)
Kancelliets Brevbøger vedr. Danmarks indre Forhold (Rigsarkivet, ed. Laursen), Danske Magazin II (1746), pp. c. 189–190
“Udi Begyndelsen af Aaret 1574. haver han opholdet sig paa sit Gods i Skaane, og i Februario ... har han været paa Hveen ... J Martio og April Maaneder har han observeret paa Herisvad Kloster, og sidst in Aprili saa vel som in Majo befindes han at have været i Kiobenhavn”
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), p. 73; p. 86 note
“In March, April, and May 1574 Tycho observed at Heridsvad” Dreyer's note refuting the 1574 Hven dating: moon and Mars fit 22 February 1576
The sources disagree. The compiler (after Langebek, Danske Magazin ii. 194) has Tycho on Hven with observations on 22 February 1574; Dreyer (p. 86 n.) refutes the dating — on that day the moon was a few days old and Mars on the other side of the heavens, whereas the observed close Mars-moon conjunction fits 22 February 1576 exactly. The Hven leg is excluded from the route; the first Hven visit belongs to 1576.

The grand tour of 1575

AMarch 1575 – December 1575journey-grand-tour-1575
PurposeDouble agenda: openly, Frederick II's commission to scout artists, artisans and technicians for the Kronborg project (reported at Sorø at Yuletide); privately, reconnaissance for a permanent foreign home — resolved secretly in favour of Basel.
Copenhagen → Kassel → Frankfurt am Main → Basel → Venice → Innsbruck → Augsburg → Regensburg → Nuremberg → Saalfeld → Wittenberg → Sorø Abbey → Knutstorp
Departing about March 1575 as a grand seigneur with servants, portable instruments and probably Flemløs, Tycho went first to Kassel (late March-April, eight to ten days with Wilhelm IV), then to the Frankfurt spring book fair, southwest to Basel (the emigration target chosen), over the Alps to Venice — his farthest south — and back over the Brenner via Innsbruck to Augsburg (late summer to late October: the cracked Schissler globe, Gemperlin recruited), downriver to Regensburg for Rudolf II's coronation on 1 November (Hagecius, the Commentariolus), then home via Nuremberg (Camerarius, Labenwolf), Saalfeld (Reinhold's manuscripts) and Wittenberg (Schuler's triquetrum, the crypto-Calvinist purge), reaching the Yuletide court at Soro by December and Knutstorp for the winter. The outward order Kassel-Frankfurt-Basel-Venice and the return order Augsburg-Regensburg-Nuremberg-Saalfeld-Wittenberg are the consensus of the ordered sources; the two fixed anchors are the spring fair and the 1 November coronation.
Evidence (10)

Friis, Tyge Brahe (1871), Friis 1871 pp. 53–56
“Han begav sig først til Cassel ... Fra Cassel begav han sig til Frankfurt am Main ... Herfra drog han til Basel ... opholdt sig nogle Dage i Venedig ... Fra Venedig drog han til Augsburg ... Han begav sig nu til Regensburg”
Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei Vita (1654/55), pp. 27–31
“Contendit porro primum in Hassiam... Francofurtum ad Maenum petiit... Basilea placuit... dies aliquot transegit Venetiis... Vindelicorum Augusta transiit... Ratisbonam cum pervenisset... Inter redeundum vidit Saltfeldiae... Vidit etiam Wittebergae”
Håkansson (ed.), Att låta själen flyga (2006), Håkansson ed. 2006, pp. 54–55

Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990), pp. 93–100, 102 the full sequential itinerary; the court at Sorø Abbey for the Christmas season

“Tycho Brahe traveled as a courtier, and his route went mainly to courtly centers of learning.”

The sources disagree. Christianson 2000's station list (Kassel, Venice, Regensburg, Augsburg, Nuremberg, Basel) is an unordered narrative enumeration; the ordered sources (Dreyer, Friis, Gassendi, Christianson 2020, Jönsson, Håkansson, Thoren) agree on Kassel-Frankfurt-Basel-Venice-Augsburg-Regensburg. Gassendi, Dreyer, Friis and Jönsson omit Nuremberg on the return; Christianson 2020, Norlind and Thoren place it after Regensburg — included. Norlind leaves the Saalfeld and Wittenberg placements hedged; the consensus 1575-return placement is followed. Padua (Jönsson 'hogst troligt') and Trento/Treviso (Norlind probable route) are excluded from the canonical route. Departure date: Thoren c. March 1575; Dreyer 'shortly after' the lectures ended early in 1575; Jönsson 'at the beginning of 1575'.

The Ibstrup summons and Hven decision circuit, February 1576

A11 February 1576 – 22 February 1576journey-hven-decision-circuit-1576
Purposecourt-patronage
Knutstorp → Ibstrup → Knutstorp → Herrevad Abbey → Copenhagen → Frederiksborg → Ven (Hven)
A fully dated circuit, documented by Tycho's own account written three days after the audience, that converted the planned Basel emigration into the Hven enterprise: the pre-dawn royal courier at Knutstorp (11 February), the same-day ride to the private audience at Ibstrup, back to Knutstorp to deliberate, consultation rides to Herrevad (Steen Bille) and Copenhagen (Pratensis, Dançay), acceptance at Frederiksborg on 18 February with the 500-daler pension, and the first crossing to Hven with its first observation on 22 February 1576.

Christianson, On Tycho's Island (2000), scan p. 29–32
“He arrived at Ibstrup that same day... he renewed his service to King Frederick II... on 18 February 1576... Four days after his royal audience at Frederiksborg Castle, Tycho Brahe was on the island of Hven.”
Håkansson (ed.), Att låta själen flyga (2006), Håkansson ed. 2006, scan pp. 50–52
“En tidig morgon i februari 1576 kom en kunglig budbarare till Knutstorp... redan samma eftermiddag anlande han till det kungliga jaktgodset Ibstrup... red han en vecka senare till Frederiksborgs slott”
Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), pp. 84–85
“He started the same day, and arrived in the evening at the king's hunting-lodge at Ibstrup, near Copenhagen”
Jönsson, Tycho Brahe: Världsmedborgaren från Ven (2025), p. 27
“Tycho Brahe gav sig omedelbart i vag mot Sjalland och hann samma dag fram till jaktslottet Ibstrup, dar kungen befann sig.”
The sources disagree. Thoren places the Herrevad and Copenhagen consultation legs in January 1576, before the summons; Christianson's mid-February ordering is followed for the route. Four per-source journey records collapsed into this one canonical circuit.

Final removal from Hven to Copenhagen (February–April 1597)

AFebruary 1597 – 11 April 1597journey-hven-copenhagen-1597
Purposeexile
Ven (Hven) → Copenhagen
The rolling evacuation of the observatory: Tycho's letter to Rantzau of 22 February 1597 — overlooked by previous biographers (Thoren) — shows the move to Copenhagen already in progress; the last Hven observation was made 15 March; the first crossing over was 18 March, the day the pension stopped; movable instruments, library, laboratory and press were boated to the Farvergade complex through the spring; and on 11 April 1597 the diary records the household's entry into the 'novum Museum Hafniense'. He never returned to the island.

Out of Denmark: Copenhagen to Rostock (2 June – mid-June 1597)

B2 June 1597 (departure within the month)journey-copenhagen-rostock-1597
Purposeexile
Copenhagen → Køge → Vordingborg → Nykøbing Falster → Gedser → Rostock
On Thursday 2 June 1597 (Julian) Tycho, Kirsten, the six children, chaplain Wensøsil, students and servants — more than a score of people, with wagons bearing library, instruments, presses, the great globe and ironbound treasure chests, under armed escort — left Copenhagen and reached Rostock by mid-June. Christianson reconstructs the southern road through Køge and Vordingborg (his foster father's boyhood castles), the Falster crossing, Nykøbing, and embarkation at Gedser; the endpoints and dates are secure, the interior stations are reconstruction. The route deliberately retraced Tycho's boyhood geography, framed by the exempla of returned exiles Peter Oxe, Knud Brahe, Erik Sparre and Erik Lange.

The sources disagree. Three incompatible route versions: (1) Christianson's overland caravan through Køge–Vordingborg–Falster–Nykøbing with the sea crossing from Gedser; (2) Norlind's 'probably over Køge and Lübeck to Rostock'; (3) a ship boarded at Copenhagen itself (Gassendi's hired cargo vessel; Jönsson; Friis 'fragtede et Skib'). The 2 June departure from Copenhagen is Tycho's own dated statement and secure; the mode and stations of the crossing are not — all three versions preserved. Gassendi's 'aestate paene media' (almost midsummer) is loose but compatible. Christianson 2000 calls 2 June a Saturday; 2 June 1597 (Julian) fell on a Thursday — the date itself, Tycho's own (Acta 124), is secure; the weekday is Christianson's slip.

Rostock to Wandsburg via Segeberg and Bramstedt (8 September – mid-October 1597)

A8 September 1597 – October 1597journey-rostock-wandsbek-1597
Purposeexile
Rostock → Doberan → Wismar → Segeberg → Bramstedt → Wandsbek
Fleeing the Rostock pestilence with no royal answer in hand, the familia left Rostock on 8 September 1597 and moved westward through Doberan (the Amt just pledged for Tycho's loan) and Wismar into Holstein, reaching the viceregal fortress of Segeberg by 17 September. Rantzau being at Bramstedt, Tycho wrote ahead stating his need of a residence near a printing city; at Bramstedt the viceroy received the party with open arms and lent Wandsburg over its lessee's head. The move formalised the friend-and-guest, not patron-client, relation with Rantzau. Settlement at Wandsbek followed in mid-October (Thoren, Dreyer, Friis) or late September (Christianson).

The interrupted Prague journey: Wandsburg to Wittenberg via Harburg, Magdeburg and Dresden (29 September – 4 December 1598)

A29 September 1598 – 4 December 1598journey-wandsbek-wittenberg-1598
Purposecourt-patronage
Wandsbek → Harburg → Magdeburg → Wittenberg → Dresden → Wittenberg
The first push toward Rudolf II, doubling as a patronage campaign — each halt yielded a recommendation, deposition or princely contact. Out of Wandsburg just after Michaelmas, 29 September 1598, in a caravan of three or more coaches: the Harburg call on Duke Otto II (Mechanica inscribed 5 October); about eight days at Magdeburg (Rollenhagen; Erik Lange's notarised Ursus deposition; instruments stored; Kirsten and the daughters sent back to Wandsburg with Longomontanus); up the Elbe through Wittenberg (album leaf 25 October, the Jessenius acquaintance made in passing); a month at Dresden awaiting imperial instructions (Venice and Magini letters 28 November); then, advised to defer Prague for the plague, the hundred-kilometre retreat to Wittenberg, reached 4 December 1598, for the winter.

Wittenberg to Prague via Pretzsch and Dresden, 14 June – early July 1599

A14 June 1599 – July 1599journey-wittenberg-prague-1599
Purposecourt-patronage
Wittenberg → Pretzsch → Dresden → Prague
The definitive move to imperial service on Coraduz's summons. The whole familia left Wittenberg on 14 June 1599 — the departure self-dated in Tycho's farewell album leaf, 'quo Witebergā discessi' — spent the first night at Castle Pretzsch with Johann Löser, and reached Dresden on 16 June, where the female household was left with Johann Tauchmann. Tycho crossed the Ore Mountains with his sons and Claus Mule in his finest carriage, Ursus fleeing Prague at his approach; arrival fell in late June or the first days of July, and the von Wicken album entry of 13 July 1599 attests him at Prague within the month. The family followed from Dresden after the first audience.

The castle-inspection circuit: Lysá, Brandeis, Benátky (summer 1599)

BJuly 1599 – August 1599journey-castle-circuit-1599
Purposeproperty-administration
Prague → Lysá nad Labem → Brandýs nad Labem → Benátky nad Jizerou
Offered the choice of three imperial castles outside Prague 'zur Exercirung seines Studii' — each under a day or half-day from the city — Tycho was conducted around to see them all: Lysá, Brandeis and Benátky. Brandeis, sitting low and the Emperor's favourite hunting-lodge, and Lysá were passed over; Benátky was chosen for its raised site and open horizon. The order of the circuit is not given by any source; the route order here is the sources' listing order, not an itinerary.

Final removal from Benátky to Prague, June 1600

AJune 1600 – August 1600journey-benatky-prague-1600
Purposecourt-patronage
Benátky nad Jizerou → Prague
The end of the Benátky observatory project. Rudolf II returned from Pilsen on 10 June 1600 and summoned his astronomer to the capital for constant consultation — a move Tycho's own 26 September 1600 letter calls made by imperial order ('beschehenes Anordnenn von dannen hieher nach Prage'), and which proved sudden and permanent. The Benátky observation series breaks off at the end of June 1600; the household passed through the Golden Griffin and the Hoffmann-neighbourhood house before the Curtius house; the instruments followed from Benátky, and the great ones from Hven via the Magdeburg transit dispute, to the Belvedere by October-November 1600. Only Tycho's effects, not Tycho, passed through Magdeburg.
