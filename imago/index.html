Imagines — how Uraniborg was pictured

Every depiction of Uraniborg and Stjerneborg printed in Tycho’s lifetime, graded for reliability — and the measured reconstruction that corrects them, after Francis Beckett & Charles Christensen, Uraniborg og Stjærneborg (Copenhagen 1921), built on the foundations excavated in 1901.

Tycho was his own image-maker. The famous views of Uraniborg in his books were issued to impress patrons, not to record a building; copied and re-copied, they grew steadily less accurate. When C. V. L. Charlier excavated the foundations in 1901 they did not match the printed plans — so the architect Charles Christensen reconstructed the two buildings afresh from the dug evidence. Below, each subject is shown through the competing contemporary images and, in green, Christensen’s reconstruction. Click any image to enlarge.

How the images descend — and which to trust

Every printed view of Uraniborg goes back, through a short chain of copies, to images Tycho himself issued for his own glory; each copy coarsened the last. Measured against the foundations C. V. L. Charlier excavated in 1901, none is reliable in architectural detail. Charles Christensen’s 1921 reconstruction is the correction.

The east front

The principal façade, shown four ways. The Marburg woodcut is the most faithful; the Epistolae engraving prettifies it (and first adds the swept octagonal portal-cupola); the Mechanica re-cuts it again, dropping detail; Christensen's measured elevation, built on the excavated footprint, is the corrective.

The ground plan

The plan the contemporary prints give is wrong even in its main outlines — the south ambulatory was wider, the portal-projections square not rectangular. Christensen's plan follows the excavated foundations.

The whole establishment, from above

The bird's-eye views record how the gardens and ramparts changed over time — but they coarsen and distort the architecture. The Vienna drawing and Braunius descend from a lost 1585 woodcut; the Marburg and Epistolae cuts are Tycho's own successive versions.

The island of Hven

Tycho controlled the map of his island as carefully as his own likeness. Braun & Hogenberg printed it from the derivative tradition; the Epistolae map goes back to a drawing by Willem Blaeu, then Tycho's young pupil.

Stjerneborg, the sunken observatory

Tycho printed only a plan and a single perspective of the star-castle; the rest is reconstruction. Christensen recovered its elevations and sections, and the painted warming-room beneath the turf.
