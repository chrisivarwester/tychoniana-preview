The original

NOBILISSIMO ET VIRTVTE SAPIENTIA INGENIOSA ERVDITAQVE DOCTRINA, CLARISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO DE KNVDSTRVP, EQVITI DANO, MATHEMATICORVM NOSTRO SECULO OMNIUM PRÆSTANTISSIMO, ETC. DOMINO SUO CUM OBSERUANTIA COLENDO, ETC. S. P.

PROXIMIS literis, Vir nobilissime ac excellentissime, memoria lapsus scripsi ad te, extremam superficiem Aëris crassioris ex Demonstratione Opticorum distare a superficie Terræ per Semidiametrum Terræ. Verum dum (de absurditate forte fortuna cogitans, quod nimirum eo præsupposito sequerentur perpetua Crepuscula,) ipsos Authores consulo, inuenio me ea, quæ ante aliquot Annos in ijs legeram, non rite meminisse; nec integrum illud, quod putaui, esse Semidiametrum Terræ, sed sexagesimam nondum Semidiametri Terreni partem. Quapropter peto a te, vt quæ præcipiti festinatione in Literis meis effutij potius quam scripsi, ea velis emendare, ac pro Semidiametro Terreno sexagesimam fere Semidiametri Terreni partem, ac pro duplicatione crassioris Aëris multiplicationem vbique in ea demonstratione ponere. Ita enim omnia constabunt, ac demonstratio mea hac ratione a fortiori confirmabitur. Schema quoque aliter in hunc modum formandum est.

Figur 10

Scis enim lineas EAG et HCL ex centro et superficie Terræ ob immensitatem Cæli videri parallelas, vel potius vnam et eandem. Iam facile habes, quanta sit densitas crassioris Aëris circa Horizontem, quanta in Altitudine 30. Graduum. Connecte enim puncta XA et AO, centrum nimirum Terræ cum punctis intersectionum extremæ superficiei crassioris Aëris in Horizonte et in Altitudine 30. Graduum. Iam igitur in Triangulo ACX, quoniam dantur duo latera, latus nimirum AX Partium 60. Min. 48. Secund. 50. et latus AC Partium 60. exacte, ex dictorum Opticorum demonstrationibus, et angulus ACX rectus per Hypothesin: datur itaque et tertium CX latus, densitas nimirum Aëris crassioris, per quam circa Horizontem formæ Stellarum ad nostrum visum perueniunt. Sic et in Altitudine 30. Graduum, quoniam in Triangulo ACO itidem dantur duo latera AO et AC, cum Angulo ACO, qui per Hypothesin vltra rectum datur 30. Grad. hoc est, Graduum 120; datur igitur et tertium CO latus, distantia nimirum extremæ superficiei Aëris crassioris in Altitudine 30. Graduum. Ita vides, hoc vnico argumento, quod nimirum Refractiones non durent vsque ad verticem, firmissime demonstrari, non esse diuersa Ætheris et Aëris Diaphana. Nec enim ipsi Optici negare possunt, quin præsupposito diuerso Ætheris et Aëris Diaphano necessarium sit, vt Refractio duret vsque ad verticem, vt ex Alhazeno lib. 7. et Vitellione lib. 10. P. 51. manifestum est. Ea quoque, quæ Vitellio lib. 10. P. 55. de scintillatione Stellarum proposuit, si vera essent, pro me facerent. Si enim scintillatio Stellarum fieret propter diuaricationem formæ in loco imaginis, sequeretur inde, formam illam non alibi quam in crassiore Aëre consistere posse. Nam Ventus vsque ad concauam superficiem Sphæræ Lunaris non ascendit, nec admodum, vt opinor, vltra crassiorem illum Aërem. Vix enim in puro illo Aëre Ventus exsistere poterit. Hæc ad te hac vice perscribere volui, ne forte putares, (quod tamen vix mihi credendum erat,) demonstrationem meam eo infirmari, quod spatium illud crassioris Aëris iusto maius assumserim. Memoriæ lapsum esse, facile animaduertes; nec tantum temporis tunc mihi sumere potui, vt ipsos Autores consulerem.

Vale igitur felicissime et optime, nobilissime et excellentissime Tycho, et de hoc sublimi negotio mecum conferre ne desine.
Cassellis 14. Calend. Nouemb. Anno 87.
T. T.
CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the most noble and illustrious gentleman, eminent in virtue, wisdom, and ingenious and erudite learning, Master Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup, Danish knight, the most outstanding of all the mathematicians of our age, etc., his lord to be honoured with all observance, etc. — heartiest greeting.

In my last letter, most noble and most excellent Sir, my memory failing me, I wrote to you that — by the demonstration of the writers on optics — the outermost surface of the thicker air [aër crassior] is distant from the surface of the Earth by [one] semidiameter of the Earth. But while (chancing to reflect on the absurdity, namely that on that supposition perpetual twilight would follow) I consulted the authors themselves, I find that I did not rightly remember the things I had read in them some years ago; and that the figure I supposed was not a whole semidiameter of the Earth, but not yet a sixtieth part of the terrestrial semidiameter. Wherefore I ask of you that what I blurted out rather than wrote in my letter, in headlong haste, you would be willing to amend: namely, that in place of [one] terrestrial semidiameter you everywhere set, in that demonstration, about a sixtieth part of the terrestrial semidiameter, and in place of a doubling of the thicker air, a multiplication. For thus everything will stand firm, and my demonstration will by this reckoning be confirmed a fortiori [all the more strongly]. The diagram, too, is to be drawn differently, in this manner.

Figure 10

For you know that the lines EAG and HCL, [drawn] from the centre and from the surface of the Earth, appear — because of the immensity of the heaven — to be parallel, or rather one and the same. Now you readily have how great the density of the thicker air is around the horizon, and how great at an altitude of 30 degrees. For join the points X–A and A–O — namely the centre of the Earth with the points of intersection of the outermost surface of the thicker air at the horizon and at an altitude of 30 degrees. Now then, in the triangle ACX, since two sides are given — namely the side AX of 60 parts [degrees] 48 minutes 50 seconds, and the side AC of exactly 60 parts, from the demonstrations of the said writers on optics — and the angle ACX is right by hypothesis: there is therefore given also the third side, CX, namely the density of the thicker air, through which around the horizon the forms [images] of the stars come to our sight. So too at an altitude of 30 degrees: since in the triangle ACO likewise two sides AO and AC are given, together with the angle ACO, which by hypothesis is given as beyond a right angle by 30 degrees, that is, of 120 degrees; there is therefore given also the third side, CO — namely the distance of the outermost surface of the thicker air at an altitude of 30 degrees. Thus you see that by this one argument — namely, that the refractions do not last all the way to the zenith — it is most firmly demonstrated that the transparent media [Diaphana] of the aether and of the air are not distinct. For not even the writers on optics themselves can deny that, the transparent media of aether and of air being supposed distinct, it is necessary that refraction should last all the way to the zenith, as is plain from Alhazen, Book 7, and Witelo, Book 10, Proposition 51. Those things, too, which Witelo set forth in Book 10, Proposition 55, concerning the scintillation [twinkling] of the stars, were they true, would tell in my favour. For if the scintillation of the stars came about on account of the divergence of the form [image] at the place of the image, it would follow from this that that form could subsist nowhere but in the thicker air. For the wind does not rise up to the concave surface of the lunar sphere, nor much, as I think, beyond that thicker air. For scarcely will the wind be able to exist in that pure air. These things I wished to write out to you on this occasion, lest perhaps you should think (though indeed I could scarcely believe it of you) that my demonstration is weakened by the fact that I assumed that space of the thicker air greater than it should be. That a lapse of memory occurred you will easily perceive; nor could I then take so much time to myself as to consult the authors themselves.

Farewell then, most happily and most well, most noble and most excellent Tycho, and do not cease to confer with me about this sublime matter.
At Kassel, the 14th [day] before the Kalends of November, the year [15]87.
Yours truly [Tuus Totus],
Christoph Rothmann.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent / sender. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann (c. 1550/60 – c. 1600), mathematician and astronomer at the court of Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel; one of Tycho's principal scientific correspondents. The exchange of 1586–90 is the great early-modern debate on atmospheric refraction and the reality of the celestial "spheres," printed by Tycho himself in the present edition.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, Dominus de Knudstrup, Eques Danus — Tycho identified by his patrimonial seat Knudstrup (Knutstorp, Scania) and his rank of eques (nobleman/knight); titulature kept in full by the edition's convention
  • S. P. — salutem plurimam (sc. dicit), "(sends) heartiest greeting".
  • T. T. (subscription) — Tuus Totus, "Wholly yours / Yours truly"; expanded in the translation.
  • Edition of record. Tycho's own Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I), Uraniborg 1596 — the volume he printed at Hven; reprinted in Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI. No manuscript witness is recorded in the edition (witnesses:[]).

Subject and technical terms

  • The letter is a correction. Rothmann retracts a figure given in his proxima epistola ("last letter"): he had wrongly said the top of the aër crassior lies one Earth-semidiameter above the surface; the optical authorities in fact give "not yet a sixtieth part" of a semidiameter. He asks Tycho to substitute that value, and to replace duplicatio ("a doubling" of the thicker air) with multiplicatio ("a multiplication") throughout the demonstration.
  • aër crassior → rendered "thicker air" throughout (the dense lower atmosphere, as against the purus aër / aether above). Crepuscula → twilight: the reductio is that a one-semidiameter atmosphere would yield perpetua crepuscula, "perpetual twilight."
  • Refractio → refraction; Refractiones non durent usque ad verticem → "the refractions do not last all the way to the zenith" — Rothmann's key empirical premise. verticem here = the zenith (the point overhead), kept as "zenith" by the edition's convention; not "vertex/summit."
  • Diaphana Aetheris et Aëris → "the transparent media of the aether and of the air." Diaphanum (Alhazen/Witelo's diaphanon) is the period optical term for a transparent medium; rendered "transparent medium," with the Latin flagged. Rothmann's thesis: aether and air are not optically distinct media (non esse diuersa … Diaphana) — a single negation in the source, reproduced as a single negation here.
  • formae Stellarum / forma … in loco imaginis → "the forms [images] of the stars / the form at the place of the image." Forma is the perspectivist term for the propagated optical species/image; glossed "[image]" in brackets, the Latin kept.
  • scintillatio Stellarum → scintillation (twinkling) of the stars; diuaricatio formae → "divergence/spreading-apart of the form [image]."
  • Geometry. The argument is a plane-trigonometric solution of two right/oblique triangles sharing the Earth-radius side AC. Partium 60. Min. 48. Secund. 50. = 60 degrees 48 minutes 50 seconds (sexagesimal; partes = degrees here, the standard astronomical usage). AC Partium 60. exacte = "AC of exactly 60 parts." The angle ACO … ultra rectum … 30. Grad., hoc est, Graduum 120 = an angle of 120° (a right angle exceeded by 30°). Letters of points (E, A, G, H, C, L, X, O) refer to Figure 10, the woodcut diagram of the printed edition; the source's placeholder line "Figur 10" (German caption) is preserved verbatim in the Source block and rendered "Figure 10."
  • Authorities cited. Alhazenus, lib. 7 = Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen), De aspectibus / Perspectiva, Book 7 (on refraction). Vitellio, lib. 10. P. 51 … P. 55 = Witelo (Vitellio, fl. 13th c.), Perspectiva, Book 10, Propositions 51 and 55 (P. = propositio); P. 55 on stellar scintillation. Both as printed in the Risner Opticae thesaurus (Basel 1572), Rothmann's working text.
  • a fortiori — kept as a Latin term of art, glossed "[all the more strongly]."
  • Sphaerae Lunaris … concava superficies → "the concave surface of the lunar sphere" (the traditional inner boundary of the Moon's orb / the top of the sublunary region), here invoked only as the upper limit of ventus (wind); not an endorsement of solid spheres — indeed the broader correspondence is where Tycho and Rothmann jointly dissolve them.

Calendar / dateline (crux — see confidence)

  • The dateline reads "Cassellis 14. Calend. Nouemb. Anno 87" = "at Kassel, the 14th before the Kalends of November, 1587." By inclusive Roman reckoning, 14 Kalendas Novembris = 19 October 1587 (Kalends of November = 1 Nov.; 1 Nov. counts as day 1, so the 14th day before = 19 October). The header therefore leads with 19 October 1587 [O.S.], the date the source substantiates.
  • The edition label ("1587. 14. november. Kassel") gives 14 November — a modern catalogue date that does not match the Roman-calendar dateline and is appended in the header as "(catalogued 14 November 1587)." The "14" of the label has plainly been read off the numeral "14" in "14. Calend. Nouemb." and miscopied as a day-of-month in November; this is a cataloguing artefact, not a second authority. Per the edition's convention the discrepancy is flagged, not silently reconciled, and the as-written date governs. (O.S.: Kassel/Hesse used the Julian calendar at this date.)
  • Cassellis = Kassel (locative; the edition's convention).

Named cruxes

  • Dateline label-vs-dateline discrepancy (19 October as written vs. 14 November catalogued) — resolved in favour of the dateline, discrepancy explained above; this alone holds the grade below Secure.
