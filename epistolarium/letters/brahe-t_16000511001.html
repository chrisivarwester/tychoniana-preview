The original

CLARISSIMO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO DE KNUSTRUP ET URANIBURCH, AMICO ET DOMINO SUO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO. PRAGAM. AM KEISERLICH MAIESTATS HOFFUE ZU ERFRAGEN.

S.

Annus iam agitur, nobiliss. et clariss. d. Tycho, cum ego Rostochij, et tu Wittenbergæ ad tempus subsisteres, nec ullam interea occasionem prætermitterem, quin et absentem te, quia præsentem non licuit, per literas adirem, per literas compellarem. Et postmodum quoque, cum ego aliorsum retraherer, et tu Wittenberga commigrares, non destiti tamen de te anquirere ac percunctari, dum in Germaniam tandem ipsam ascenderem et longam post moram intelligerem Pragam te advenisse atque ibidem aut circa eam ex cæsarea majestatis insigni liberalitate ac longis votis astronomiæ jam magnam partem per te restitutæ et porro restituendæ magnifice et feliciter operarj. Gaudeo idcirco tuo, gaudeo denique publico nomine; adhuc honos honestis artibus ac studijs durat. Tu modo in hac, quam ad immortalitatem tibi stravisti, via, gnaviter et indefesse, dis hominibusque faventibus, progredere. Nec deerunt, qui amplectantur, qui admirentur, quandiu tu modo dignum aliquid cœlo, dignum chartis elaboraveris.

Nepotes tui sub mea aliquandiu disciplina jam sunt, ultraque semestre Herbornæ Nassouiorum studijs incumbunt, quod ex adjuncta quoque epistola pro parte vjdebis. Et utinam aliquando illi patre, te patruo digni reperiantur. Certe quod ipsis inculcem exemplar extra te non quæro. De Dania nihil jamdudum accepimus. Ex patris tamen literis intellexi nuper, valde occupatum fuisse elapsa bruma in instruenda classe regem, et aliquot etiam centenos peritissimos naucleros ab ordinibus Hollandiæ petisse atque impetrasse. Quid ille moliatur, aut quid ipsum coquat, tempus dabit. Te autem, nobilissime Tycho, sæpe per literas compellare pro amicitja veteri et nepotum necessitudine debere nos fateor, et optem, ut de ratione mittendi ad te literas certiores redderemur. Comperires profecto officium nostrum neutiquam claudicaturum. Qui has perferre spopondit, aurifaber et pro parte chymicus est. Eo ego familiariter hic usus sum, quia Piscator aliquatenus affinis est. Et spero officium eius in hisce curandis futurum certius, quam ars fere solet illa, quam tractat.

Hisce valebis, clariss. Tycho, officiose salutatus ab hospite nostro, quem dixi, v. cl. Iohanne Piscatore.
Datum Herbornæ Nassouiorum xi maj CIↃ IↃ C. Tui studiosiss.
Iohannes Jsacius.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most noble man, Lord Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup and Uraniborg, his much-to-be-revered friend and lord. To Prague. To be inquired after at the Imperial Majesty's court.

Greeting.

A year is now passing, most noble and renowned Lord Tycho, since I was staying for a time at Rostock, and you at Wittenberg, and I let no occasion pass meanwhile but that I approached you, absent as you were (since to do so in person was not allowed), by letters, and addressed you by letters. And afterward too, when I was drawn off elsewhere, and you removed from Wittenberg, I yet did not cease to inquire and ask after you, until at length I came up into Germany itself, and after a long delay learned that you had arrived at Prague, and there, or near it, by the imperial majesty's distinguished liberality — and to the long-prayed-for [good] of astronomy, already in great part restored by you and further to be restored — were labouring magnificently and happily. I rejoice therefore on your account; I rejoice, in the end, on the public account: honour still endures for honourable arts and studies. Only do you, upon this road which you have paved for yourself toward immortality, press on, diligently and indefatigably, with gods and men favouring. Nor will there be wanting those who embrace, who admire — so long only as you go on elaborating something worthy of heaven, worthy of the written page.

Your nephews are now for some while under my tutelage, and for more than half a year apply themselves to their studies at Herborn in Nassau — as you will also see, in part, from the enclosed letter. And would that they may someday be found worthy of their father, and of you their uncle. Certainly, for a model to instil in them, I seek none beyond yourself. From Denmark we have received nothing for a long time. Yet from their father's letter I learned recently that the King was much occupied last winter in fitting out a fleet, and that he sought and obtained also some hundreds of most skilled skippers from the Estates of Holland. What he is contriving, or what is brewing for him, time will tell. But that we ought often to address you, most noble Tycho, by letter — for the sake of our old friendship and the tie of your nephews — I confess; and I could wish that we be made more certain of the means of sending letters to you. You would surely find our service in no way halting. He who has undertaken to carry these is a goldsmith, and in part a chymist. I have made familiar use of him here, because he is somewhat a kinsman of Piscator; and I hope his service in seeing to these [letters] will prove more reliable than that art he practises is wont to be.

With these you will fare well, renowned Tycho, dutifully greeted by our host, of whom I spoke, the very renowned Johann Piscator.
Given at Herborn in Nassau, 11 May 1600. Most devoted to you,
Johannes Isacius [Pontanus].

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound letter to Tycho. Johannes Isacius Pontanus (Jan Isaksz. Pontanus, 1571–1639), the Danish-Dutch historian, geographer and humanist (later royal historiographer of Denmark), then teaching at Herborn, where he was tutor to Tycho's nephews. The address-line gives the routing in German: am keiserlich Maiestäts Hofe zu erfragen — "to be inquired after at the Imperial court" (Tycho's whereabouts being uncertain to correspondents).
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, at or near Prague, "labouring magnificently" in the imperial service; Pontanus salutes him as the restorer of astronomy (astronomia … magna parte per te restituta et porro restituenda) on the "road paved toward immortality" — the restitutio/instauratio programme-language again.
  • The nephews & the Herborn colony. nepotes tui … sub mea disciplina = Tycho's nephews (sons of his brother Steen Brahe), under Pontanus's instruction more than half a year at Herborn — the same student group whose member Erik Brahe writes the epistola adiuncta ("the enclosed letter") referenced here: brahe-t_16000512001, dated the very next day (12 May 1600). The two letters travelled together.
  • Persons named.
  • Iohannes Piscator = Johannes Piscator (1546–1625), the eminent Reformed theologian of the Herborn academy, Pontanus's and the students' host (hospes noster).
  • Rex = Christian IV of Denmark, reported (via the boys' father's letter) to have fitted out a fleet the previous winter and hired "some hundreds" of skilled Dutch pilots/skippers (naucleri) from the Estates of Holland.
  • the unnamed goldsmith-and-chymist courier, a kinsman of Piscator, carrying this letter — Pontanus's wry hope that his reliability exceed that of his alchemical "art."
  • Places. Rostochium = Rostock; Wittenberga = Wittenberg (Tycho's 1598–99 way-station); Herborna Nassoviorum = Herborn in Nassau.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. = salutem; Tui studiosiss[imus] → "Most devoted to you." The signature Iohannes Isacius is Pontanus's patronymic form (Isaac's son).
  • Calendar. Dateline xi maj CIↃ IↃ C — "11 May 1600" (the Roman apostrophus numeral CIↃIↃC = 1600). No style marker; reproduced as written.
