The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD LUCAM BACMEISTERUM.

CLARISSIME et praestantissime Vir, Domine et Amice honorande, quod per Clarissimum Virum Dn. D. IOH. GEORG. GÖDELMANNUM, communem nostrum amicum, sexcentos illos thaleros a quaestore Iuniorum Principum loco interesse mihi debitos transmiseris, ijs qui Belgici erant in imperiales maiore ex parte commutatis, recte a te factum est, eoque nomine plurimas tibi habeo gratias. Quod autem idem quaestor reliquos 600, qui mihi nunc debentur, tunc numerare noluerit, non equidem miror, utut non iniquum fuisset, si id officioli mihi praestitisset, sed quod totam summam satis mature resignatam et consensu et iussu Illustriss. Ducis UDALRICI mihi ad hoc Pascha restituendam solvere tergiversetur, nescio quas alias praetendens expensas et calvas excusationes, id mihi magis displicet, nec ferendum duco. Scripsi itaque ad dictum Illustriss. Ducem UDALRICUM, sponsorem primarium, ut ad constitutum tempus integram summam decem millium Ioachimicorum mihi restitui sua authoritate curet, quod et non invite effecturum optimum illum et integerrimum Principem, mihi clementissime faventem, nullatenus diffido. Obtigit mihi occasio, qua in hisce oris plus commodi ex illa pecunia nancisci queam quam isthic, alias non tam cito eam repetijssem.

Quin et praeterea ad praefatum Illustriss. Ducem dedi literas, qua ipsius Celsitudinem ad nuptias filiae cuidam ex meis ELISABETHAe nomine submisse invitavi, cum quodam Nobili et erudito Iuvene FRANCISCO TENGNAGLIO e Westphalia oriundo atque in hac aula versante, ad instans Pentecostes, favente Coelesti Numine, celebrandas. Cum enim non saltem Caesaream Maiestatem, Dn. nostrum clementiss. et praecipuos eius Consiliarios et aliquot Germaniae Principes, tam Ecclesiastici quam politici status, quos scio mihi clementer favere, humilime per literas invitare animus sit (quod et nonnullorum Legati, qui hîc sunt, sponte a me petierunt, atque suis Dominis gratum futurum innuerunt) Illustrissimum Ducem Megapolitanum, Dn. meum clementiss. et quam optime de me meritum, praeterire nefas duxi, non quod ipsius Celsitudinem aut alios in Germania Principes, ad quos hac de re reverenter scribo, aliquem suo nomine ideo huc missuros petere audeam, sed saltem quo conscij sint, ut si forte aliquem ex suis Legatis tum temporis hîc in aula habuerint, illis festivitatem hanc loco sui Principis coram condecorare liceat, quod et te, charissime Domine D. BACHMAISTERE, ob veterem inter nos necessitudinem non caelandum duxi, quo vota tua ad Deum omnis boni et felicitatis largitorem nostris coniungens, fausta et salutaria huic actui preceris.

Miror te literas D. D. CRAGIO inscriptas non accepisse. Nullas nunc ad ipsum ulterius mitto, siquidem intellexerim ipsum in Legatione Polonica abesse, sed tabellario huic nonnullas tradidi, quibusdam in Dania praecipuis amicis destinatas, quarum maxima pars est invitatoria ad iam dictas nuptias; eas vero omnes ipse tabellarius facile per quendam nobilem, ESCHILLUM BILLÆUM nomine, res meas istic procurantem, expediet. Addo minutulas quasdam ad eundem Regis Musicum, cui alterae prius tibi missae (ut opinor) inscribebantur, quas hic ipse grammatophorus, si tibi commodior non suppetit ratio, illi Hafniae inquisito tradet; sunt a quodam hîc Italo, in cuius aliquantul[a fideiussione?] Musicus est, scriptae.

Magistro BRASCHIO ipsemet nunc de negocio prius insinuato rescribo; antea enim non vacabat.

Quae a CAROLO Sueciae Duce in Livonia acta sunt, iam antea hîc cognovimus; nimis tardus esse videtur in resistendo Poloniae Rex, et vix ut ENNIUS de altero isto Romano aiebat, cunctando restituet rem: celeritate potius in talibus opus est. WAIWODA, qui in Comitijs Polonicis etiam hostis eius Regni declaratus est, nuper hîc fuit, et gratiam Caesaris impetravit, acceptaque magna pecuniae vi Transsylvaniae partem Polonorum consilijs et Tartarorum invasionibus nuper amissam pro Caesare recuperare meditatur, omniaque strenue ad hanc expeditionem praeparat. Magnis etiam viribus contra Turcam exercitum parat Imperator noster, validiorem quam unquam antea, siquidem necessitas sic postulet, ob amissum superiori Octobri Canisium, fortissimum Hungariae propugnaculum, quo occupato Turca sibi viam, sive Styriam sive Austriam infestare velit, praestravit; armipotens et fortis Deus eius ferociam et tyrannidem retundat atque a cervicibus Christianorum avertat. Ubi plura aliquid momenti habentia resciero, te eorum faciam participem. Vale cum omnibus tuis quam felicissime, et senij, si quae, uti fit, incidunt gravamina, patientia vince. Dabantur Pragae ex aedibus olim Curtianis, quas Caesar pro 10000 millibus Ioachimicorum in meos usus liberaliter emit vix duplo maioris exaedificatas, quae novum Uraniburgum Rudolphaeum appellanda.

Iamque iterum et saepius vale.
Die 15 Aprilis stylo novo Anno 1601.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO LUCAS BACMEISTER.

Most renowned and excellent Man, honoured Lord and Friend: that through the most renowned Lord Doctor Johann Georg Gödelmann, our common friend, you transmitted to me those six hundred thalers owed me by the treasurer of the Younger Princes by way of interest — the Belgian ones being for the greater part exchanged into imperial — was rightly done by you, and on that account I give you many thanks. That the same treasurer was then unwilling to pay out the other 600 now owed me, I do not indeed wonder at (though it would not have been unfair, had he done me that small service); but that he shirks paying the whole sum, duly assigned long enough beforehand and, by the consent and command of the most illustrious Duke Ulrich, to be repaid me by this Easter — pretending I know not what other expenses and bald excuses — this displeases me more, and I judge it not to be borne. I have therefore written to the said most illustrious Duke Ulrich, my principal guarantor, that he see to it by his authority that the whole sum of ten thousand joachimsthalers be repaid me at the appointed time — which I in no way doubt that excellent and most upright Prince, who most clemently favours me, will not unwillingly bring about. An occasion has befallen me whereby I may obtain more advantage from that money in these parts than there; otherwise I should not have called it in so soon.

Moreover, I have besides sent a letter to the aforesaid most illustrious Duke, by which I have humbly invited His Highness to the wedding of one of my daughters, Elisabeth by name, to a certain noble and learned young man, Franz Tengnagel, sprung from Westphalia and resident at this court, to be celebrated at the coming Whitsun [Pentecost], Heaven favouring. For since it is my intent humbly to invite by letter not only the Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, and his chief Councillors, and several Princes of Germany — both of ecclesiastical and of political estate — whom I know to favour me kindly (which several of the envoys here have even of their own accord asked of me, and intimated would be welcome to their masters), I judged it a sin to pass over the most illustrious Duke of Mecklenburg, my most clement Lord, who has deserved so very well of me — not that I should dare to ask His Highness, or the other Princes of Germany to whom I write reverently on this matter, to send anyone hither in their name on that account, but only that they may be aware, so that, if perchance they happen to have one of their envoys here at court at that time, it may be allowed him to grace this festivity in person in his Prince's stead. And this, dearest Lord Doctor Bacmeister, on account of the old bond between us, I judged not to be hidden from you, that — joining your prayers with ours to God, the bestower of all good and happiness — you may pray for things auspicious and wholesome for this act.

I wonder that you have not received the letters addressed to Dr. Craig. I now send no further ones to him, since I understand he is away on a Polish legation; but I have given this courier several [letters] destined for certain chief friends in Denmark, the greatest part of which are invitations to the said wedding. The courier himself will easily despatch them all through a certain nobleman, Eske Bille by name, who manages my affairs there. I add some small notes to the King's Musician, to whom the others sent you earlier were (I think) addressed; these this same letter-carrier, if a more convenient means is not at hand for you, will deliver to him, sought out at Copenhagen — they were written by a certain Italian here, in whose [surety?] the Musician stands.

To Master Brasch I myself now write back about the business previously hinted at; for earlier I had no leisure.

What Charles, Duke of Sweden, has done in Livonia we had already learned here before; the King of Poland seems too slow in resisting, and scarcely, as Ennius said of that other Roman, will he "by delaying restore the state": rather, in such matters there is need of speed. The Voivode, who in the Polish Diet was even declared an enemy of that Kingdom, was here lately and obtained the Emperor's favour, and — having received a great force of money — purposes to recover for the Emperor that part of Transylvania lately lost by Polish counsels and Tartar invasions, and is vigorously preparing everything for this expedition. With great forces too our Emperor is preparing an army against the Turk, stronger than ever before, should necessity so demand — on account of the loss, last October, of Kanizsa, the strongest bulwark of Hungary, by the taking of which the Turk has paved himself a road, whether he wish to infest Styria or Austria. May God, mighty and strong in arms, blunt his ferocity and tyranny, and turn it from the necks of Christians. When I learn of more things of some moment, I will make you partaker of them. Farewell most happily with all yours, and conquer by patience the burdens of old age, if any (as happens) befall. Given at Prague, from the house once Curtius's, which the Emperor liberally bought for my use for 10,000 joachimsthalers — a house built at a cost scarcely twice as great — to be called the new Rudolphine Uraniborg.

And now again and oftener, farewell.
The 15th of April, New Style, the year 1601.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Lucas Bacmeister (Bacmeister the Younger, 1570–1638), Rostock theologian-physician, Tycho's friend and financial intermediary for the Mecklenburg loan (cf. the Brasch/Cramer correspondence routed through him, brahe-t_16000401001). Spelled Bachmaister mid-letter.
- Significance — the Mecklenburg debt and the Tengnagel wedding (two of the great threads of Tycho's last year).
1. The 10,000-thaler Mecklenburg loan. Tycho had lent Duke Ulrich III of Mecklenburg-Güstrow ("Duke Ulrich," sponsor primarius = principal guarantor) 10,000 joachimsthalers; the treasurer of the "Younger Princes" owes interest (two instalments of 600 thalers, the first received via Gödelmann, Belgian thalers converted to imperial) and now shirks repaying the principal due at Easter 1601. Tycho, having "an occasion to use the money more advantageously in these parts," appeals over the treasurer's head to Duke Ulrich to enforce the full repayment.
2. The wedding of Elisabeth Brahe to Franz Tengnagel. Tycho's daughter Elisabeth is to wed Frans Gansneb genaamd Tengnagel (the Westphalian nobleman and Tycho's assistant) at Pentecost 1601. Tycho is inviting Rudolf II, the imperial councillors, and German princes, and asks Duke Ulrich (and, through Bacmeister, Danish friends) that any envoys present at court may grace the festivity in their princes' stead. (The wedding duly took place 17 June 1601.)
- Other contents.
- John Craig ("Dr. Craig") is away on a Polish legation, so Tycho sends him nothing further; Danish letters (mostly wedding invitations) go via the courier and Eske Bille (Eschillus Billaeus), Tycho's Danish agent.
- Notes to the King's Musician (of Denmark), penned by "a certain Italian here, in whose [surety?] the Musician stands" — in cuius aliquantul[a fideiussione?], an editorial reconstruction of a damaged phrase (flagged).
- Master Brasch (the poet, cf. brahe-t_16000401001's circle) — Tycho writes back at last.
- The news bulletin (spring 1601).
- Charles, Duke of Sweden (the future Charles IX) campaigning in Livonia — the opening of the Polish–Swedish War; Tycho thinks Sigismund III of Poland too slow, quoting Ennius on Fabius Maximus Cunctator (cunctando restituit rem, "by delaying he restored the state") against delay — here speed is needed.
- The "Voivode" — declared an enemy by the Polish Diet, lately at Prague, won Rudolf's favour and money, and purposes to recover Transylvania for the Emperor: most plausibly Michael the Brave (Mihai Viteazul), Voivode of Wallachia (in Prague early 1601, reconciled with Rudolf, sent with Basta to retake Transylvania — they won at Guruslău that August; Michael was assassinated days later). Identification by circumstance; the name is not given (flagged).
- The fall of Kanizsa (Canisium, Nagykanizsa) in October 1600 — "the strongest bulwark of Hungary," opening the Turk's road to Styria/Austria; Rudolf preparing his largest army yet for the Long Turkish War.
- The dateline — "the new Rudolphine Uraniborg." Tycho writes from the former Curtius house (the Kurz/Curtius palace in Prague), which Rudolf II had bought for his use for 10,000 joachimsthalers — vix duplo maioris exaedificatas, "[a house] built at a cost scarcely twice as great" — "to be called the new Rudolphine Uraniborg" (novum Uraniburgum Rudolphaeum). A poignant naming: the Prague mansion as heir to the lost island observatory. (The source's 10000 millibus Ioachimicorum, lit. "10,000 thousands," is a scribal slip for 10,000 thalers — the same round figure as the Mecklenburg loan above; rendered as 10,000.)
- Persons & places named. Lucas Bacmeister; Johann Georg Gödelmann; Duke Ulrich III of Mecklenburg-Güstrow; Elisabeth Brahe & Franz Tengnagel; Rudolf II; John Craig; Eske Bille; the King's Musician; Master Brasch; Charles, Duke of Sweden; Sigismund III of Poland; Ennius / Fabius Cunctator; the Voivode (Michael the Brave?). Places: Prague (the Curtius house), Livonia, Transylvania, Kanizsa, Styria, Austria, Denmark/Copenhagen.
- Calendar. Die 15 Aprilis stylo novo Anno 1601 → 15 April 1601 [N.S.]; header and label agree.
- Correction. Latin 'versasante' corrected to 'versante' (Dreyer TBOO VIII p.413), a non-word glue-error; no change to the English, which already read correctly.
