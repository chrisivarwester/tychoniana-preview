The original

MAGNIFICO AMPLISSIMOQUE VIRO, NOBILITATE GENERIS, PRUDENTIA ATQUE ERUDITIONE ADMODUM CONSPICUO DOMINO IACOBO CURTIO A SENFFTENAV, CÆSAREAe MAIESTATIS CONSILIARIO ATQUE IMPERIJ PROCANCELLARIO DIGNISSIMO, AMICO SUO IN PRIMIS HONORANDO. S.

MAGNIFICE et generosissime Domine CURTI, amice summopere colende, non sine grata mente et memoria vetera tua in me praestita officia etiamnum recolo et amplector, dum tua, qua plurimum vales apud augustissimam Caesaream Maiestatem, auctoritate et consilio, amplissimum , huius Maiestatis Privilegium meis Operibus Astronomicis praefigendum mihi antea clementer impertiri procuraveris, atque gravissimam tuam de his artibus censuram ac propensissimam de ijs bene merendi voluntatem, literis tuis insignem quoque candoris et benevolentiae erga me tuae significationem ostendentibus affatim declarâris. Quibus pristinis in me exhibitis promeritis non parvum insuper cumulum iam nuper aggregaveris, dum recognitum hoc et quibusdam auctum Caesareae Maiestatis Diploma, quo omni ex parte laborum ac impensarum mearum indemnitati caveretur, gratiosissime mihi concedi et transmitti effeceris. Qua de causa et sincerum tuum erga me et haec sublimia studia (quibus ipsemet etiam non modice afficeris, adeo ut si Reipub. administrandae cura his altioribus coelestium siderum contemplationibus non invideret, facile in his utilissimum quid e divite praestantis tui ingenij penu depromeres) amorem luculentius perspiciam, et me rursum debendae gratitudinis nomine tibi in posterum arctius devinciri agnoscam. Tibi itaque, amplifice et praestantissime vir, pro tantis totiesque in me elocatis officijs gratias ago ingentes et immortales, meque tibi vicissim recipio, si quam re unquam grati erga te animi declarare indicia concedatur, perpetuo fore quam promptissimum. Caeterum ex Domini D. THADDÆI literis unâ allatis, insolens illud mei Antagonistae scriptum ad tuas pervenisse manus cognovi. Quem quidem virum in Mathematicis apprime versatum esse non diffiteor. Verumtamen Aristotelicis Disciplinis, quibus ab ineunte aetate (prout plerisque in illa regione usu venire solet) imbutus, usqueadeo serviliter se mancipavit, ut quae ab his vel latum ungvem dissentiant, quantumvis veriora et aliter invictis Apodixibus demonstrata agnoscere et fateri aut nolit aut nequeat. Proinde ex contentioso et arroganti hoc scripto satis perspicere potes, te nimis de eo candide antea sensisse, dum eiusmodi contradictiones ita pro summa animi tui ingenuitate excusaveris, ac si id ille eiusque similes vellent, ut simulate obiectiones quasdam speciosas proponendo pleniorem et exactiorem harum rerum doctrinam publici commodi et posterorum gratia extorquerent. Illum enim rem serio agere et pro ARISTOTELE suo tanquam pro aris et focis pugnare, hinc non obscure animadvertes. Rogo itaque T. M. ut antagonisticam hanc compilationem, utut pretiosissimis suis otijs indignam, diligentius introspicere, mecumque unâ de hac gravissimum suum communicare iudicium non dedignetur. Quamvis autem insulsae eius argutiae confestim a me non immerito essent redarguendae, malo tamen Progymnasmatis Astronomicis, quibus nunc incumbo, in lucem edendis operam locare, quam id generis cavillationes inanes et sophisticas refellendo temporis iacturam facere. Ubi vero primus noster, qui de Nova Stella ubertim tractat Tomus, unâ cum subsequentibus Cometarum libris, in publicum exierit, tum demum candidi et cordati aestimatores non difficulter discernent, quam perperam et inique CRAIGUS his nostris, a Philosophia Peripateticorum dissentientibus placitis tantopere adversetur. Prodibit forte suo etiam tempore discipulorum meorum aliquis, qui eius invalidas obiectiones facile diluat. Misi autem unâ cum his ipsis Domino THADDÆO primum horum (de quibus dixi) Progymnasmatum nostrorum librum, tibi simul exhibendum, ut si quando a gravioribus Reipub. administrandae munijs nonnihil superesset temporis, eum evolvere atque solertissimam tuam de eodem censuram literis aperire non degravarere. Quod ut facias, maiorem in modum te rogatum habeo. Sequentem Tomum, qui trium superiorum Planetarum Phaenomena emendate expediet, mecum aliquantisper reservo, cum ut unam adhuc ultra res, quas in promptu habeo, acronychiam in Saturno animadversionem, ob tarditatem motus eius, qua lente peripheriam sui Eccentrici emetitur, nanciscar, tum praesertim, ut Martem, quando Soli primam decuriam Virginis obtinenti iuxta Perigaeum sui Eccentrici hoc anno opponetur, sedulo observare liceat. Incidit enim tunc diversitas utriusque calculi Alphonsini et Copernicei, quae maxima pene in eius motu contingere potest, adeo ut haec trientem fere unius signi attingat. Quare restitutio nostra in huius Sideris apparentijs tunc maxime probabitur. Apparebit autem tunc Mars solito maior, ita ut ab imperitis pro insueto aliquo sidere reputabitur. Sed ne te Politicis rebus ob illustre quod sustines officium indesinenter occupatum, meis his literis nimium interpellem, pluribus supersedeo. Vale et amicitiam nostram semel per literas incoatam, ijsdem, siquidem aliter fieri nequit, conservare et stabilire non gravere. Quod et vicissim a me expectabis.

Iterum quam felicissime vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 19. Aprilis Anno 1593.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and most ample man, most conspicuous in nobility of family, in prudence, and in erudition, Master Jacob Kurz von Senftenau, most worthy Councillor of His Imperial Majesty and Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, his friend to be honoured above all — greeting.

Magnificent and most noble Master Kurz, friend to be cherished in the highest degree: not without a grateful mind and memory do I even now recall and embrace the services you of old rendered me — when, by that authority and counsel of yours, in which you have great weight with His most august Imperial Majesty, you formerly took care that the most ample Privilege of His Majesty, to be set at the front of my astronomical Works, should be graciously bestowed upon me; and when, by letters of yours that displayed also a notable token of your candour and goodwill toward me, you abundantly declared your most weighty judgement of these arts and your most ready disposition to deserve well of them. To these former merits shown toward me you have lately added no small further heap, when you most graciously brought it about that this Diploma of His Imperial Majesty — revised and augmented in certain particulars, by which provision was made on every side for the indemnity of my labours and expenses — should be granted and transmitted to me. For which reason I more clearly perceive both your sincere love toward me and toward these lofty studies (by which you yourself too are not slightly affected, so that, were the care of administering the Commonwealth not to begrudge you these higher contemplations of the heavenly stars, you would readily draw forth from the rich storehouse of your excellent genius something most useful in them); and I acknowledge that I am once again, by the name of the gratitude I owe, more closely bound to you for the future. To you, therefore, most ample and most excellent man, for services so great and so often laid out upon me, I give immense and undying thanks, and I in turn pledge myself to you, [promising] that — if ever it be granted me to declare by some deed the tokens of a mind grateful toward you — I shall perpetually be most ready [to do so]. For the rest, from the letter of Master Doctor Thaddaeus brought along [with yours], I have learned that that insolent writing of my Antagonist has come into your hands. The man I do not deny to be exceedingly well versed in mathematics. Nevertheless he has so slavishly made himself a bondman to the Aristotelian disciplines — in which he was steeped from his earliest years (as is wont to happen with most people in that region) — that whatever dissents from them by even a finger's breadth, however truer it be and otherwise demonstrated by invincible proofs [Apodixes], he either will not or cannot acknowledge and confess. Accordingly, from this contentious and arrogant writing you can see well enough that you earlier judged of him too charitably, when out of the great ingenuousness of your mind you so excused contradictions of this sort, as though he and his like wished, by feigningly putting forward certain specious objections, to extort a fuller and more exact doctrine of these matters for the public good and for the sake of posterity. For that he is acting in earnest, and fighting for his Aristotle as if for altar and hearth, you will here not obscurely observe. I therefore ask Your Magnificence not to disdain to look into this antagonistic compilation more diligently — unworthy as it is of your most precious leisure-hours — and to share with me your most weighty judgement upon it. But although his insipid quibbles ought rightly to be refuted by me without delay, I prefer nevertheless to bestow my labour on bringing into the light the Astronomical Progymnasmata on which I am now engaged, rather than to waste time in confuting cavils of that kind, empty and sophistical. But once our first Volume — which treats copiously of the New Star — together with the ensuing books on the Comets, has gone forth into public, then at last candid and judicious appraisers will not find it hard to discern how wrongly and unfairly Craig so greatly opposes these doctrines of ours, which dissent from the Philosophy of the Peripatetics. Perhaps in due time some one of my pupils too will come forth, who may easily dissolve his feeble objections. I have sent, however, along with this very [letter], to Master Thaddaeus the first book of these Progymnasmata of ours (of which I have spoken), to be shown to you as well, so that, if ever a little time should remain over from the weightier duties of administering the Commonwealth, you would not find it burdensome to unroll it and to disclose by letter your most skilful judgement of it. That you may do so, I have asked you most especially. The next Volume, which will treat correctly the Phenomena of the three superior Planets, I reserve with me for some while — both that I may obtain one further observation, beyond the matters I have ready to hand, namely an acronychal [rising] in Saturn, on account of the slowness of its motion, by which it measures out the circumference of its eccentric so slowly; and especially that I may be permitted diligently to observe Mars when this year it will be in opposition to the Sun, which then holds the first decan of Virgo, near the perigee of its eccentric. For there falls then the divergence of the two computations, the Alphonsine and the Copernican, which can occur in its [Mars's] motion well-nigh at its greatest, so that this [divergence] reaches almost a third of one sign. Wherefore our restoration [of astronomy] will then be most amply proved in the appearances of this Star. Mars will then appear larger than usual, so that by the unskilled it will be reckoned some unaccustomed star. But lest, with these letters of mine, I interrupt you too much — you who, on account of the illustrious office you bear, are ceaselessly occupied with political affairs — I forbear to say more. Farewell, and do not find it burdensome to preserve and make firm the friendship between us, once begun through letters, by those same [letters], since indeed it cannot be done otherwise. Which you shall in turn expect from me.

Again, most happily, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 19 April, the year 1593.
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe writing from Uraniborg (on Hven) to Iacobus Curtius a Senfftenav = Jacob Kurz von Senftenau (d. 1594), Imperial Councillor and Vice-Chancellor of the Empire (Imperij Procancellarius) at the court of Emperor Rudolf II in Prague. He had secured for Tycho the Imperial printing privilege (Privilegium) for his astronomical works, and procured a revised and augmented form of it (the Diploma), as this letter of thanks records.
  • Domini D. Thaddaei — Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), Imperial physician at Prague, astronomer, and Tycho's long-standing correspondent and intermediary at the Imperial court. The book is to be sent to Hájek and shown by him to Kurz.
  • mei Antagonistae … CRAIGUS — the "Antagonist" named later as Craig: John Craig (Joannes Craigus, d. 1620), the Scottish (Edinburgh) physician and Aristotelian. In 1589 Craig, having received Tycho's book as a gift, sent (per Dreyer, Opera Omnia IV) three letters of objections defending the Aristotelian doctrine that comets are atmospheric and exhibit no sensible parallax, against Tycho's observationally-grounded claim that they lie far beyond the Moon. The present letter responds to a renewed polemical tract by Craig that had reached Kurz's hands.
  • Tycho's Opera / the Progymnasmata. Progymnasmata Astronomica = Tycho's Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata (eventually published 1602). primus … Tomus … de Nova Stella = the first volume, treating the new star of 1572, "together with the ensuing books on the Comets" (Cometarum libris; cf. De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, 1588). Sequentem Tomum, qui trium superiorum Planetarum Phaenomena — "the next Volume … the Phenomena of the three superior Planets" (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars), then still in preparation and held back pending further observation.
  • Technical terms (per glossary–5.3). Eccentricus → eccentric; Perigaeum → perigee; restitutio (nostra) → restoration (Tycho's astronomia instaurata programme — rendered "our restoration [of astronomy]"); opponetur / oppositio → opposition; acronychia(m) … animadversionem in Saturno → an acronychal observation of Saturn (i.e. at acronychal/heliacal-opposite rising, when the planet rises as the Sun sets); kept and glossed. prima decuria Virginis → "the first decan of Virgo" (decuria here = the astrological decan, the first 10° of the sign); flagged as the decan-sense rather than a literal "company of ten."
  • Calculation tables. calculus Alphoninus / Coperniceus → the Alphonsine and Copernican computations (the Alfonsine Tables and the tables derived from Copernicus's De revolutionibus); trientem fere unius signi = "almost a third of one sign," i.e. nearly 10°.
  • Apodixes / Peripatetics. invictis Apodixibus → "by invincible proofs" (apodixis, Greek-derived: demonstrative proof). Philosophia Peripateticorum → the Peripatetic (Aristotelian) philosophy.
  • Honorifics. S. = salutem (dicit), "greeting." T. M. = Tua Magnificentia, "Your Magnificence." Caesarea Maiestas → "His Imperial Majesty" (Rudolf II). pro aris et focis → "for altar and hearth," the proverbial phrase for fighting for one's most sacred possessions; kept.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgi 19. Aprilis Anno 1593 = "Given at Uraniborg, 19 April 1593" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline date and the edition label ("1593. 19. april. Uraniborg") agree.
  • Textual cruxes (in the witness / transcription).
  • "amplissicum," (with trailing comma and apparent word-break): the transcription is corrupt here. Sense and parallel usage (amplissimum … Privilegium) indicate the intended word is amplissimum, "most ample," qualifying Privilegium; rendered "the most ample[?]" with the printed form preserved in the Source block and the reading flagged rather than silently emended.
  • "amplifice" (in amplifice et praestantissime vir): an irregular/non-standard vocative, evidently for amplissime ("most ample"); the address-formula sense is secure, the exact form is not. Rendered "most ample" and flagged.
  • Correction. One Latin corruption corrected per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII p.348: the non-word 'amplissicum' restored to 'amplissimum'; English's bracketed '[?]' flag removed accordingly. A second audited defect (claimed missing 'his' before 'altioribus') was checked against the page image and rejected: Dreyer does print 'his', matching the source text already published — no change made.
