The original

EXCELLENTISSIMO ET ERUDITISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO IOHANNI ANTONIO MAGINO, MATHEMATUM IN ILLUSTRI BONONIENSIUM GYMNASIO PROFESSORI, AMICO SUO PLURIMUM DILECTO.

REVERSUS ad me FRANCISCUS TENGNAGLIUS noster tuas attulit, Excellentissime et Eruditissime plurimumque mihi dilecte Domine D. MAGINE, meis quidem respondentes, sed tamen de singulis, de quibus tecum agebam, non sufficienter agentes, quod occupationibus tuis lubens tribuo. Gratum vero mihi in primis est, quod ijs quae ille proponebat assentitus sis, atque in illis tuam operam sive per temetipsum sive per alios in Italiâ Eruditione pollentes effectui daturum benevole pollicitus.

Existimo te a dicto TENGNAGLIO summa rerum huc pertinentium fastigia hausisse. Adiunxi hisce brevem discursum de occasionibus mei e Daniâ discessus, ut eo promptius negocium concipi possit, et quasdam alias literas huc pertinentes. Coetera ipsius relatio ac Carmina tradita, quae nunc prioribus addo, supplebunt. Da itaque operam, optime MAGINE, ut mature res omnis conficiatur, mihique primae conceptiones intra duos menses (si ullo modo fieri possit) huc in Bohemiam mittantur, quo quae necessaria duxero, mutem, addam vel adaugeam, cum nemini melius quam mihi ipsi mea perspecta sint, moxque vobis revisa et locupletata remittam. Placet vero admodum illa ratio, de quâ TENGNAGLIUS admonuit, ut potius Panaegyricum quid sit quam per Dialogos. Sic enim res omnis concinnius comprehendi poterit et maiori authoritate: ita tamen, ut a generalioribus ad particularia descendendo primum universae Artis Astronomicae ortus, incrementa, promotiones, augmenta et variae mutationes ac peregrinationes recenseantur; quae omnia ab alijs scriptoribus, qui hoc argumentum tractârunt, facile cum delectu quodam hauriri poterunt. Et si alij defuerint, IOHANNES STADIUS in ijs, quae Tabulis Bergensibus abs se dictis subiunxit, occasionem suppeditabunt: quae tamen in quibusdam, ubi liberius excurrit, castiganda erunt, uti erat vir ille in dicendo facilior quam verior. Utinam in calculo Astronomico, qui fucum (uti historica) non patitur, idem non commisisset. Quâ in re non immerito a te redarguitur. Sed parcamus iam manibus defuncti. Est aliquid prodire tenus, si non datur ultra.

Deinde postquam narratum est, quomodo e Graeciâ in Italiam et Hispaniam, postmodum vero ad Germanos et deinde in Daniam versus Septentrionem migrârit Astronomia; quo cum ulterius ob Arctoum frigus nimiamque sphaerae inclinationem progredi commode non posset, neque etiam diu ibi subsistere, tandem in Germaniam reversa est, adeoque in ipsam Bohemiam, Germaniae quasi cor et meditullium, ubi apud Augustissimum Imperatorem RUDOLPHUM II clemens et benignum hospitium nacta est, ut demum verificaretur, quod olim MANILIUS ad AUGUSTUM Caesarem protulit: [Regales animos primum dignata movere, / Proxima tangentes Coelo fastigia rerum.]

Hinc sumptâ occasione poterit postea digressio quaedam fieri ad me et ea, quae mihi in Daniâ evenerunt, tanquam relatione eorum, qui ex Germaniâ et Bohemiâ in Italiam proficiscuntur, comperta. Decet tamen in his modum aliquem servare et modeste ac moderate omnia agere, ita ut Patriae nostrae et Regi plurimisque in ijs praeclarâ et antiquâ Nobilitate viris integris et cordatis, quibus partim inscijs partim invitis ea evenerunt, quae discessui meo ansam praebuerunt, parcatur, utque illi sint excusabiles. Perstringendi vero solummodo pro merito Cancellarius modernus et Aulae magister, qui cum Patriae honorem ex officio promovere debuissent, eum potius ob avaritiam et sorditiem pari invidiâ, malignitate et odio coniunctam (cum ipsi liberalibus scientijs vel nihil vel admodum parum tincti essent) impediverunt et exterminârunt. Nomina eorum invenies in ijs, quae de caussis discessus mei Latine exarata nunc mitto. Nec interest, si illi expresse nominentur in scripto illo, tanquam nomina eorum hac occasione aliunde in Italiâ relata fuerint, cum alias obscuri sint, digni tamen, ut ex tali facinore famosi evadant, quemadmodum ille, qui Dianae templum Ephesijs incendio absumpsit, cuius tamen nomen hoc pacto deletum est. Atque in hunc vel similem modum possunt ea, quae Uraniae nostrae et mihi contigerunt, amplificari.

Inaudivi te Ephemerides tuas magnâ equidem diligentiâ supputatas (utut quidam Francofurti ad Oderam, êditis nuper quibusdam alijs ab anno 1595 incipientibus et ultra tuas decennio prolongatis, minutula quaedam, quae momentum non habent, in ijs carpere praesumpserit) auxisse ad minimum integro decennio. Exemplar non vidi: neque enim fuit in Catalogo Nundinarum Francofurtensium proxime antecedentium. Cuperem itaque ex te habere, ut videam, an aliqua inibi praeter priorem diligentiam exantlaveris. Neque enim dubito, tua in hoc genere (cum numerorum tractatione atque compendijs praepolleas) emuncta et solida esse, quantum fert fundamentum illud cui superstructa. Coeterum quae de Tabulis meis in motu Luminarium restituto expetis, ut ijs, donec reliquorum Planetarum numeratio ipsi Coelo consona subsequatur, in commodum Reip. literariae uti possis, libenter tecum communicarem, si ea quae non tam typis excusa quam manuscripta in his parata habeo, nunc ad manus essent. Nam ut de rerum mearum vicissitudine, quam a praenominato TENGNAGLIO intellexisti, nihil hîc repetam (piget enim istorum recordari, nedum ea memorare) scias me praecipuam partem suppellectilis meae interea, dum huc in Bohemiam a Caesarea Maiestate Clementissime evocatus transeo, Magdaeburgi ad Albim quasi in medio Germaniae reliquisse, donec de ipsius Maiestatis Clementissimâ voluntate certior redderer; inter quam non saltem Instrumenta quaedam ex meis, plura quam huc attuli, sed et maxima pars Operum meorum tam impressorum quam manuscriptorum conservatur. Et licet Caesarea Maiestas ante complures hebdomadas Magdaeburgensibus clementer scripserit atque mandârit, ut omnes res meas ibi reservatas huc in Bohemiam intermediante Albi adduci procurent, tamen interveniente hyeme, quae fluminis congelationem atque impedimenta varia facile suggerere potuit, id usque in instans vernum tempus procrastinare necessum habuerunt. Unde fit, ut tuis votis hac in parte, etiamsi quam maxime vellem, nunc subvenire nequeam, sed spero Progymnasmatum nostrorum Astronomiae instauratae primum Tomum brevi, favente Coelesti Numine, in publicum êditum iri: moxque exemplar tibi impertiar. Isthic capite primo, quae in calculo curriculi utriusque Luminaris desideras, sufficienter invenies. Interim hanc incidentem morulam patienter habe. In reliquis Planetis adhuc strenue laboro. Res enim est maioris difficultatis, pluribusque anfractibus obnoxia, quam hactenus creditum. Quid in his atque alijs ad negocium Astronomicum facientibus hucusque aspirante ipso Coeli authore perfecerim, liber is, quem TENGNAGLIUS tibi ex me dono dedit, de Mechanicâ Astronomiae parte circa finem edocebit. Quem unâ cum altero de affixarum maiore labore quam aestimatione restitutis locis te accepisse gaudeo; velimque ut tibi soli reserves, vel saltem ne indignis et ingratis communices.

Observationes, quas per nova Instrumenta ad imitationem quorundam ex meis fabrefacta te in Italiâ fieri effecturum iam dudum pollicitus es (de quibus etiam antea per literas inculcavi) necdum conspicere licuit. Averem sane tale quidpiam isthic diligenter effici, ex quo minor Sphaerae apud vos quam hîc inclinatio maiorem largiatur commoditatem stellas praesertim Australiores rite examinandi. Nec desunt vobis ingenia ad quidvis apta, modo in talibus instruantur et adhibeantur. Neque dubito quin ditiores, qui hac sublimi cognitione afficiuntur, sumptus non invite sint subministraturi. Quod et illis erit honorificum. Quae vero in his praecipue facienda atque eligenda sint, ut Mechanice omnia ad amussim et utiliter conficiantur, liber, de quo dixi, quemque habes, ansam praebebit. Omnem itaque move lapidem, ut non sub tecto et tuguriolis (uti nunc communiter fit) sed sub dio atque ipso Coelo Astronomiam (velut par est) veterum imitatione exerceamus, et sic spectemur agendo.

Vale, et Annum hunc Iubilaeum, multosque alios in Artis huius nunquam satis laudatae promulgationem feliciter exige.

Dabantur ex Arce Caesareanâ Benaticâ 3 Ian. 1600.
Excellentiae tuae studiosissimus
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most excellent and most learned man, Lord Giovanni Antonio Magini, Professor of Mathematics in the illustrious Gymnasium of Bologna, his most beloved friend.

Franz Tengnagel, our [man], returning to me, brought your [letters], most excellent and learned and most beloved Lord Magini — answering mine, yet not sufficiently treating the several points I was raising with you, which I gladly attribute to your occupations. It is most welcome to me that you assented to what he proposed, and benevolently promised to give effect to it, whether by yourself or through others in Italy excelling in Erudition.

I think you have drawn from the said Tengnagel the chief heads of the matters pertaining hither. I have adjoined to these a brief discourse on the occasions of my departure from Denmark, that the business may be the more readily conceived, and certain other relevant letters. The rest his report, and the Verses handed over (which I now add to the earlier), will supply. Take pains, then, excellent Magini, that the whole thing be finished in good time, and that the first conceptions be sent me here into Bohemia within two months (if it can by any means be done), so that I may change, add, or augment what I judge necessary — since my affairs are better known to no one than to me myself — and I will soon send them back to you, approved and enriched.

I much approve that plan of which Tengnagel advised: that it be rather a Panegyric than [done] through Dialogues. For so the whole matter can be more elegantly comprehended, and with greater authority — yet so that, descending from the more general to the particular, first the origin, increments, promotions, augments, and various changes and migrations of the whole Astronomical Art be recounted — all of which can easily be gathered, with some selection, from other writers who have treated this argument. And if others fail, Johannes Stadius, in what he subjoined to his Tabulae Bergenses, will supply occasion — though in some places, where he runs too freely, [they] must be corrected, for that man was readier than truer in speaking. Would that, in astronomical calculation — which (unlike history) suffers no false colouring — he had not committed the same! In which matter he is not undeservedly refuted by you. But let us now spare the shade [manes] of the dead. "It is something to advance thus far, if it is not given to go beyond" [Horace].

Then, after it is narrated how Astronomy migrated from Greece into Italy and Spain, afterward to the Germans, and then into Denmark, toward the North — whither, since it could not conveniently proceed further (on account of the Arctic cold and the excessive inclination of the sphere), nor stay there long, it at last returned into Germany, and indeed into Bohemia itself, the heart and midmost, as it were, of Germany, where it found a clement and kindly lodging with the most august Emperor Rudolf II — so that at last there be verified what Manilius once uttered to Augustus Caesar: “[Astronomy,] first deigning to stir the minds of kings, and those reaching the topmost summits of affairs that border on Heaven.” [the couplet — Manilius, Astronomica I.7–8 — is blank in the working transcription but printed by Dreyer; supplied editorially: Regales animos primum dignata movere, / Proxima tangentes Coelo fastigia rerum.]

Hence, occasion being taken, a digression can afterward be made to me, and what befell me in Denmark — as if learned by the report of those who travel from Germany and Bohemia into Italy. Yet it befits to keep some measure in these, and do all modestly and moderately, so that our fatherland, and the King, and the many men of distinguished and ancient Nobility, upright and sensible — by whom (partly unknowing, partly unwilling) those things happened that gave occasion to my departure — be spared, and be excusable. Only to be censured, for their desert, are the modern Chancellor and the Court-Master — who, when they ought by their office to have promoted the fatherland's honour, rather, out of avarice and sordidness joined with equal envy, malignity, and hatred (since they were either not at all, or very little, tinged with the liberal sciences), impeded and exterminated it. Their names you will find in what I now send, written in Latin, about the causes of my departure. Nor does it matter if they be expressly named in that writing — as if their names were on this occasion reported from elsewhere in Italy — since they are otherwise obscure, yet worthy that, from such a deed, they become infamous: like the man who consumed the temple of Diana of the Ephesians by fire, whose name was nonetheless [by decree] deleted [Herostratus]. And in this or a like manner, what befell our Urania and me can be amplified.

I have heard that you have augmented your Ephemerides — calculated indeed with great diligence (although a certain man at Frankfurt-on-Oder, having lately published certain others, beginning from the year 1595 and prolonged a decade beyond yours, presumed to carp at certain trifles in them that have no moment) — by at least a whole decade. I have not seen the copy; for it was not in the Catalogue of the most recent Frankfurt Fair. I should therefore wish to have it from you, to see whether you have drawn out anything therein beyond your former diligence. For I do not doubt that your [work] in this kind is refined and solid (since you excel in the handling of numbers and in compendia), so far as the foundation on which it is built allows. As for what you seek concerning my Tables of the restored motion of the Luminaries — that you may use them, for the advantage of the literary Commonwealth, until the numeration of the remaining Planets, consonant with heaven itself, follow — I would gladly share them with you, if what I have prepared in these (not so much printed as in manuscript) were now at hand. For (not to repeat here the vicissitude of my affairs, which you learned from the aforesaid Tengnagel — for it pains me to recall such things, much less narrate them), know that I left the chief part of my stuff, meanwhile as I crossed hither into Bohemia, called most clemently by the Imperial Majesty, at Magdeburg on the Elbe, as it were in the middle of Germany, until I should be made more certain of His Majesty's most clement will — among which are kept not only some of my Instruments (more than I brought here), but also the greatest part of my Works, both printed and manuscript. And although the Imperial Majesty several weeks ago clemently wrote and ordered the Magdeburgers to see all my things kept there brought hither into Bohemia by way of the Elbe, yet, winter intervening (which could easily bring the river's freezing and various impediments), they had to postpone it to the coming spring. Whence it comes that I cannot now succour your wishes in this part, however much I would. But I hope the first Volume of our Progymnasmata of restored Astronomy will shortly, Heaven favouring, be published; and I will soon impart a copy to you. There, in the first chapter, you will sufficiently find what you desire in the calculation of the course of both Luminaries. Meanwhile bear this incidental little delay patiently. On the remaining Planets I still strenuously labour; for the matter is of greater difficulty, and liable to more convolutions, than has hitherto been believed. What I have so far accomplished in these and other things conducing to the Astronomical business — the heavenly Author himself aspiring [it] — the book which Tengnagel gave you as a gift from me, on the Mechanical part of Astronomy [the Mechanica], will teach near its end. Which, together with the other, on the positions of the fixed stars restored with greater labour than esteem, I rejoice that you received; and I should wish you to keep them to yourself alone, or at least not share them with the unworthy and ungrateful.

The observations which you long ago promised you would have made in Italy, by new Instruments fashioned in imitation of certain of mine (of which I urged you before too by letter), I have not yet been able to behold. I should indeed long for some such thing to be diligently done there — whence the smaller inclination of the Sphere with you than here would grant greater convenience for rightly examining the stars, especially the more southern. Nor do you lack talents apt for anything, if only they be instructed and applied in such; nor do I doubt that the richer, who are taken with this sublime knowledge, will not unwillingly supply the costs — which will be honourable to them too. What is chiefly to be done and chosen in these, that all may be made mechanically exact and useful, the book of which I spoke, which you have, will give occasion. Move every stone, then, that we may exercise Astronomy not under roof and little huts (as is now commonly done), but under the open sky and the very Heaven (as is fitting), in imitation of the ancients — and so be regarded in the doing.

Farewell, and spend this Jubilee Year, and many others, happily in the promulgation of this never-sufficiently-praised Art.

Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, 3 January 1600.
Most zealous of Your Excellency,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), professor of mathematics at Bologna (cf. his letter, brahe-t_16010220001). The letter survives in Magini's printed Tabulae Primi Mobilis.
  • Significance — the Italian parallel to the Brasch commission: a panegyric history of astronomy, and the naming of Tycho's enemies.
  • The panegyric-history project (cf. brahe-t_16010414001 to Brasch). Tycho asks Magini to arrange a Latin panegyric (not dialogues) on the history of astronomy — the origin and migrations of the Art, culminating in Rudolf II and Tycho — sending Tycho the draft within two months for revision. He supplies a "brief discourse on the occasions of my departure from Denmark," relevant letters, and verses. (Tycho is orchestrating his own literary monument in parallel across Germany [Brasch] and Italy [Magini].)
  • The translatio astronomiae. Greece → Italy/Spain → the Germans → Denmark ("toward the North"); there it could go no further "for the Arctic cold and the excessive inclination of the sphere," so it returned to Germany — to Bohemia, "the heart of Germany," to Rudolf II — fulfilling a (here-omitted) line of Manilius to Augustus.
  • Naming the villains (the Herostratus device). A pointed contrast to the Pinelli/Grynaeus letters: Tycho insists his fatherland, King, and the upright nobility be spared and excused — but that "the modern Chancellor and the Court-Master" (his enemies, "very little tinged with the liberal sciences," who "out of avarice and sordidness … impeded and exterminated" Danish astronomy) be expressly named and made infamous, "like the man who burned the temple of Diana at Ephesus" (Herostratus). Their names are in the enclosed Latin account of his departure. (A rare, explicit demand to immortalise his persecutors by infamy.)
  • Stadius. Recommends (with correction) Johannes Stadius's Tabulae Bergenses notes as a source — "readier than truer," rightly refuted by Magini; "let us spare the shade of the dead" (Est aliquid prodire tenus, Horace).
  • Ephemerides & tables. Magini has augmented his Ephemerides by a decade; the rival "at Frankfurt-on-Oder" (the ephemerides from 1595 = David Origanus) carped at trifles. Tycho cannot yet share his own luminary tables (manuscript, stuck with the bulk of his works and instruments at Magdeburg awaiting the Elbe thaw), but promises the first volume of the Progymnasmata soon (its ch. 1 = the luminary calculations).
  • The Mechanica gift to Magini. Confirms Tengnagel gave Magini the Mechanica ("on the Mechanical part of Astronomy") and the fixed-star book ("restored with greater labour than esteem") — to be kept private. (A Mechanica distribution event.)
  • The southern-observation appeal & the open-sky credo. Tycho urges Magini to build Tycho-style instruments and observe in Italy, exploiting Italy's lower latitude for the southern stars (cf. the Egypt plan, brahe-t_16000124001), funded by wealthy enthusiasts — and ends with his manifesto: "exercise Astronomy not under roof and little huts … but under the open sky, in imitation of the ancients, and so be regarded in the doing."
  • Cross-references. Pair with brahe-t_16010414001 (the Brasch History-of-Astronomy) as the twin literary-monument commissions; with brahe-t_16010220001 (Magini's letter) and brahe-t_16000103001 (Pinelli, same day, same Italian burst); the southern-observation thread (brahe-t_16000124001); and the Magdeburg-storage and Mechanica-distribution threads.
  • Persons & works named. Magini; Franz Tengnagel; Johannes Stadius (Tabulae Bergenses); Manilius / Augustus; Rudolf II; the modern [Danish] Chancellor and Court-Master (Tycho's enemies, named only in the enclosure); Herostratus (the unnamed Ephesus arsonist); David Origanus (the Frankfurt-on-Oder ephemerides rival, unnamed); the Progymnasmata, the Mechanica. Places: Benátky, Bologna, Magdeburg/Elbe, Greece/Italy/Spain/Germany/Denmark (the translatio).
  • Text note (RESOLVED §1 collation). The Manilius couplet "to Augustus" is blank after protulit: in the working transcription, but Dreyer prints it (Opera Omnia VIII p. 244): Regales animos primum dignata movere, / Proxima tangentes Coelo fastigia rerum (= Astronomica I.7–8).
