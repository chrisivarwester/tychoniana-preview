The original

GEORGIUS ROLLENHAGIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

S.

ETSI nihil erat, Nobilissime et Doctissime TYCHO, quod ad te praecipue nunc scriberem, tamen cum forte accederet me Tabellarius Lipsicus, volui te hisce salutare, et quae meus affinis ad me de URSO et de exspectatione tui scribit, tecum communicare. Is affinis meus arte quidem est gemmarum scalptor ingeniosus, sed officio apparitor Caesaris, estque hoc eius, quod mitto, chirographum, ut si nota tibi iam tamen sint, intelligas ibi quoque innotuisse; sin ignota, deliberes, quid ad eam exspectationem tibi sit faciendum. Quod bene vertat ad salutem tuam et Uranies Deus Opt. Max., cum Illmo D. Brunsvicensi de negotio tuo locutus sum. Ille quidem praeclare de te sentit, sed Regni et Regis desertionem causatur. Et quamvis causas declararem tuas, et Regem ab alijs regi dicerem, tamen mansit in sententia, ideo quod a suis Nobilibus se deseri, et coram camera accusari molestissime fert. Coniunx existimabat exspectandam esse occasionem, qua coram Regem ipsum convenires: sic omnia facile te impetraturum. Princeps in vinculis habet PETRUM HAGEN L. L. Salderiorum advocatum. Ipsos quoque fratres Salderios Lemgaviae sub Comite Lipiensi nuper coepit, propediem eosdem in Lycaoniam suam attracturus. Habent enim Leones Leonina ingenia. Deus, in cuius manu est cor Principis, dirigat illud ad clementiam et iustitiam. Saluto generosum senem Maecenatem meum D. HENRICUM RANZOVIUM, eique facilem, viridem, et laetam senectutem precor. Pestis grassatur passim in vicinia, praesertim Halberstadij, et ad nos eius semina quaedam allata sunt. Deus avertat ulteriora.

Vale.
Datae die 18. Iunij Anno 98.

Georg Rollenhagen to Tycho Brahe.

Greeting.

Although there was nothing, most noble and most learned Tycho, that I should especially write to you now, yet, since the Leipzig courier happened to come to me, I wished to greet you with these [lines], and to share with you what my kinsman writes to me about Ursus and about the expectation of you. This kinsman of mine is, by art, indeed an ingenious engraver of gems, but, by office, an apparitor [usher] of the Emperor; and this which I send is his own handwriting, so that — if these things are nonetheless already known to you — you may understand that they have become known there too; but if [they are] unknown, you may deliberate what you ought to do in regard to that expectation. May God Best and Greatest turn it well to your welfare and Urania's: I have spoken with the Most Illustrious Lord of Brunswick about your business. He indeed thinks excellently of you, but pleads [as an objection] the desertion of the Kingdom and the King. And although I declared your reasons, and said that the King is ruled by others, he yet remained in [his] opinion — for the reason that he most grievously resents being deserted by his own nobles, and accused before the [imperial] chamber. His consort thought that the occasion must be awaited on which you might meet the King himself in person: thus you would easily obtain everything. The Prince holds in chains Peter Hagen, advocate-at-law of the Salderns. The Saldern brothers themselves, too, he lately seized at Lemgo, under the Count of Lippe — [intending] shortly to drag the same into his own Lycaonia. For Lions have leonine natures. May God, in whose hand is the heart of the Prince, direct it to clemency and justice. I salute the generous old man, my Maecenas, Master Heinrich Rantzau, and pray him an easy, green, and glad old age. Plague rages here and there in the vicinity, especially at Halberstadt, and certain seeds of it have been brought to us. May God avert worse.

Farewell.
Given the 18th day of June, in the year [15]98.

Copy of a German letter, which was enclosed within this Latin one.

My wholly willing service, always beforehand. Especially favouring Lord brother-in-law: that it should go well with you, together with your dear wife, that you be fresh and healthy, would be dear to me. I thank God the Almighty for my own dear health; may He preserve us further according to His will, amen.

Since my Lord brother-in-law wrote to me before this concerning Ursus, I have made a little inquiry here with His Majesty's chamber-servant, whether the same Ursus were in disfavour with His Majesty; and I have received word that he is indeed not in disfavour, only that he gave himself out for much [more than he is], and now does not find himself equal to it, and understands the least thing in astrology not at all, etc. It has thus come about that he was put to the test by His Majesty with the nativity of the Prince of Transylvania, in which he knew neither the beginning nor the end, whereby he has fallen into contempt and mockery; and since he did not set down the mark and the truth, and his judgment was false, he now wishes to write against it [astrology], and disdains and reviles Astrology to the very utmost, [saying] that there is nothing in it but sheer lies and deceit — though he indeed praised it at first, and thereby came to His Majesty. The other reason is that, when he was asked why he had put himself forward at court on this account, and had cast the horoscope [Thema] of His Majesty's late brother of most blessed memory (which was found to be correct), and is now against it, he had nothing he knew how to answer. But it has been found out that he did not make this himself, but took it out of the books of Elias Preuss, and thereby made himself prominent, etc.

I have also learned that Tycho Brahe is to come here to our court, and that His Majesty has a great longing for him, and that they will give him a castle three miles' way from Prague, where he is to have his residence — a very pleasant place, called Brandeis. The sons of [Herr] von Dieffenbach are here at Prague; they are to be sent to Strasbourg to study; and if my Lord brother-in-law wishes to write to the places he has in mind, I will gladly deliver it, having good occasion for it. Otherwise, for this time, no more.

Herewith commended to God, together with your dear wife and children, and greeted from me and my wife.

Given at Prague, the last of May, new style, in the year [15]98.

Your Lordship's willing [servant,] brother-in-law and disciple, Caspar Lehman, halberdier of the Roman Imperial Majesty.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609) of Magdeburg (the place not in the dateline but established by the later instrument-transport letter brahe-t_16000619001); Tycho's agent and friend.
  • The Ursus intelligence. Rollenhagen forwards news, in the hand (chirographum) of his kinsman — "by art an ingenious gem-engraver, by office an Imperial apparitor [court usher]" — about Ursus (Nicolai Reimers Bär, Tycho's rival, then Imperial Mathematician) and about the expectation of Tycho at the imperial court; Tycho is to weigh how to act. (This is two years before the Ursus suppression of brahe-t_16000727001.)
  • The Duke of Brunswick. Illmus D. Brunsvicensis = Heinrich Julius, Duke of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel (1564–1613), often at the imperial court. He esteems Tycho but objects to his "desertion of the Kingdom and the King" (of Denmark) — the recurrent reproach of Tycho's self-exile; Rollenhagen pleaded that "the [Danish] King is ruled by others." The Duke's consort advised that Tycho should wait to meet the Danish king in person.
  • The Saldern affair. Petrus Hagen, L[egum] L[icentiatus], Salderiorum advocatus — Peter Hagen, legal advocate of the von Saldern family, imprisoned by the Duke; the Saldern brothers seized at Lemgo (under the Count of Lippe) — a contemporary noble feud. in Lycaoniam suam attractare / Leones Leonina ingenia — a play on the Duke's wolfish/leonine harshness (Lycaon the wolf-king; "lions have leonine natures"). The whole sentence is wordplay on the Duke's reputation for ruthlessness toward refractory nobles.
  • Maecenas meus … Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich Rantzau, the aged patron, wished "an easy, green, and glad old age." Pestis … Halberstadij — plague at Halberstadt, spreading.
  • Calendar. die 18. Iunij Anno 98 = 18 June 1598; Magdeburg (Lutheran) = Old Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • What it is. A German newsletter from Prague, dated "the last of May, new style, [15]98," enclosed as an exemplum literarum Germanicarum inside Georg Rollenhagen's Latin letter to Tycho of 18 June 1598 O.S. Rollenhagen — the Magdeburg schoolmaster-poet and regular Tycho/Rantzau correspondent (attested repeatedly in Oestmann's Rantzau und die Astrologie and Zeeberg's Rantzau studies) — evidently forwarded it to Tycho as court intelligence.
  • The writer, identified. Signed "Caspar Lehman, Röm. Kay. Mayt. Hartschier" [halberdier/guardsman of the Roman Imperial Majesty] — this is almost certainly Caspar Lehmann (d. 1622), Rudolf II's imperial gem- and glass-cutter at Prague, later renowned as the founder of German glass engraving. Christianson, On Tycho's Island (Cambridge, 2000), independently records that "an imperial astrologer and gem artist, Caspar Lehmann, acted as Tycho's informer" and "sent the welcome news that Ursus had displayed his own dishonesty and lack of skill to the emperor and was no longer in favor" (n.56) — almost word for word the content recovered here, strongly suggesting this letter is, or directly feeds, that very report.
  • Ursus's disgrace. Nicolaus Reimers Ursus — Tycho's rival, then Rudolf II's Imperial Mathematician — is reported to have been tested by the emperor with the nativity of the Prince of Transylvania and to have failed conspicuously, his earlier (correct) horoscope of the Emperor's late brother exposed as lifted from the books of one "Elias Preuss" — not identified in the works consulted, and to be distinguished from Tycho's own former Uraniborg assistant Elias Olai Cimber (Elias Olsen Morsing).
  • Tycho's move to Prague, anticipated over a year early. Already by May 1598 — a full year before Tycho actually reached Bohemia and settled at Benátky in August 1599 — Prague court gossip had it that Tycho "will come here to our court" and would be given a castle three miles from Prague called "Brandeis" [Brandýs nad Labem]. Dreyer records that Rudolf later did offer Tycho a choice among Lyssa, Brandeis and Benátky, of which Tycho chose Benátky; this letter shows the plan, and the specific castle, already circulating as rumor well before the event.
  • Crux: "Elias Preusens," "von Dieffenbach," and the unnamed deceased "Herr Bruder" of the Emperor (whose nativity Ursus reportedly cast correctly — most likely Archduke Ernst of Austria, d. 1595, but not independently confirmed here) are not resolved in the works consulted. The closing abbreviation "E. D. W." before "Swager vndt Discipulus" is of uncertain expansion; rendered loosely as "Your Lordship's willing [servant]."
  • Correction. Latin corrected from 'attractare' to 'attracturus' per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.72.
