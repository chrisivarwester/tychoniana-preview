The original

GENEROSA NOBILITATE ET POLITIORIBUS LITERIS CLARISSIMO VIRO D. TYCHONI BRAHÆO DOMINO IN KNUTSTORFF ET CÆSAREÆ MAIESTATIS CONSILIARIO ET MATHEMATICO EXCELLENTISSIMO DOMINO ET AMICO SUO.

S.

CUM ad me ex itinere suo, quod ad te instituerat, Generose et Doctiss. D. TYCHO, divertisset optimus vir DAVID FABRICIUS, et non modo tua studia, ut merentur, crebris sermonibus commendaret, sed observationes quoque suas coram ostenderet, dolui profecto GANYMEDEM tam elegantem Frisios potius Boves pascere, quam ab Aquila Ioviali raptum, Dijs in Cœlo ministrare, ut DAVID ab ovibus in aulam propter Musicam adoptabatur, ut certe hic DAVID GANYMEDEM habet ascendentem. Sed quisque suos patimur manes. Ego certe multis nominibus eum mihi optârim propiorem.

Fuit ante paucos dies hîc quasi privatus Episcopus Coloniensis, iturus ad Cæsarem, ut ei Papæ nomine electionem Regis Romanorum persuadeat, atque sic et reditus Cæsarios et autoritatem in plures partes distrahat. Ne dicam de nuncio Belgico, in quam terram iam redijt exercitus Hispanicus. Ab altera parte redit Turca. Domine, serva nos, perimus.

Favorem Episcopi Saltzburgensis tibi ex animo gratulor, utinam plures adducat tibi suffragantes, ut tandem utilissimos tuos labores ad umbilicum deducas.

De prognostico, quod HENRICUS IULIUS de se usurpare dicitur, iam olim audivi doctorem MEGABACCHUM, ROTHIUM et alios, qui a me existimabant esse profectum. Sed neque ego hoc dixi aut scripsi unquam, neque ab ipso audivi. Hoc autem dicere solet GARCÆUM prædixisse, aliquando se præclaram ex bello victoriam relaturum esse, itaque ad bella esse animosiorem. Quod negavi ego ex fontibus, quos epistola tua allegat. Et si possem, in viam potius pacis reducerem, quam ut currentem instigarem. Sed fertur equis auriga, nec audit currus habenas.

Spero me propediem in aulam venturum ad consultationem de vocando Theologo in locum HOFMANNI. Ibi, ut decet, privatim de tua pro eius salute solicitudine, et commoditate negociorum apud Cæsarem privatim expediendorum, movebo fideliter omnia. Et rescribam.

Bene et quam diutissime vale.
15 Maij 1601.
Tuæ Gen. N.
addictiss.
GEORGIUS ROLLENHAGEN.

HENR. IULIUS natus est 1564, 14 Octobris, 17 hora P. M. in arce Hessen. Hoc tempus GARCÆUS
corrupit per falsissimam HERMETIS trutinam et correctiones similes.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man renowned for his generous nobility and his more polished learning, Lord Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup and most excellent councillor and mathematician of His Imperial Majesty, his lord and friend.

Greeting.

When the excellent man David Fabricius had turned aside to me out of his journey, which he had set toward you, generous and most learned Lord Tycho, and not only commended your studies (as they deserve) in frequent conversation, but also showed me his own observations in person, I grieved indeed that so elegant a Ganymede should pasture Frisian oxen, rather than — snatched up by Jove's Eagle — minister to the gods in heaven; just as David was adopted from the sheep into the court on account of his music, so surely this David has Ganymede in the ascendant. But each of us must endure his own appointed lot. I, for my part, could on many counts have wished him nearer to me.

A few days ago the Bishop of Cologne was here, as it were in a private capacity, on his way to the Emperor, to persuade him in the Pope's name to the election of a King of the Romans, and so to distract the imperial revenues and authority into ever more directions. Not to speak of the Belgian dispatch — into which land the Spanish army has now returned. From the other quarter the Turk returns. Lord, save us, we perish!

On the favour of the Bishop of Salzburg I congratulate you from the heart; would that he bring you more supporters, so that you may at last carry your most useful labours through to completion.

As for the prognostication which Henricus Iulius is said to claim of himself, I heard long ago that Doctor Megabacchus, Roth, and others reckoned it had proceeded from me. But I neither ever said or wrote this, nor heard it from himself. What he is wont to say, however, is that Garcaeus predicted he would one day carry off a glorious victory from war, and that he is therefore the more spirited toward wars. This I have denied, on the sources your letter alleges. And, if I could, I would rather lead him back into the path of peace than goad him on as he runs. But "the charioteer is carried off by his horses, nor does the chariot heed the reins."

I hope to come shortly to court, for the consultation about calling a Theologian into the place of Hofmann. There, as is fitting, I shall faithfully set everything in motion, in private — concerning your solicitude for his welfare, and the convenience of business to be dispatched privately with the Emperor. And I shall write back.

Fare well, and for the longest time.
15 May 1601.
Most devoted to Your Generous Nobility,
Georg Rollenhagen.

Heinrich Julius was born in 1564, on the 14th of October, at the 17th hour P.M., in the castle of Hessen. This time Garcaeus corrupted, by means of the utterly false Balance of Hermes and similar corrections.

Apparatus

  • Direction. This letter is to Tycho, from a correspondent — one of the relatively few inbound letters in the edition. The sender writes from Magdeburg (the place is unstated, s.l., but Rollenhagen was rector there).
  • Sender. Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), Lutheran theologian, schoolman and poet (author of the mock-epic Froschmeuseler, 1595), rector of the Altstadt Gymnasium at Magdeburg. His letters reached Tycho enclosed in Winshemius's packets (cf. Rollenhagianae [literae] in brahe-t_16000420001).
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, here titled Caesareae Maiestatis consiliarius et mathematicus — imperial councillor and mathematician.
  • The David Fabricius passage (the literary heart).
  • David Fabricius = David Fabricius (1564–1617), Frisian pastor at Resterhafe/Osteel in East Frisia and a gifted naked-eye observer, correspondent of both Tycho and Kepler; he was en route to visit Tycho and stopped with Rollenhagen, showing his observations.
  • The conceit turns on a triple play: Fabricius as Ganymede (the beautiful youth snatched to heaven by Jove's Eagle to serve the gods) "pasturing Frisian oxen" — i.e. wasted as a rural pastor instead of raised to astronomy; and the biblical David "adopted from the sheep into the court on account of his music" (1 Samuel 16). Hic David Ganymedem habet ascendentem puns on the astrological ascendant: this David "has Ganymede [= Aquarius, the water-bearer] rising" — both a horoscopic figure and the wish that he rise to the heavens. The wordplay is preserved, not flattened.
  • Sed quisque suos patimur manes quotes Virgil, Aeneid VI.743 ("but each of us suffers his own shade") — "each must endure his own appointed lot."
  • The political news.
  • Episcopus Coloniensis = the Elector-Archbishop of Cologne, Ernst of Bavaria, travelling "as if private" to the Emperor to press, in the Pope's name, the election of a King of the Romans (the contested Habsburg succession question).
  • nuncius Belgicus … exercitus Hispanicus = news from the Low Countries, where the Spanish army of the Eighty Years' War had returned; redit Turca = the renewed Ottoman threat of the Long Turkish War. Domine, serva nos, perimus echoes Matthew 8:25 (the disciples in the storm) — "Lord, save us, we perish."
  • Episcopus Saltzburgensis = the Prince-Archbishop of Salzburg, Wolf Dietrich von Raitenau, whose favour toward Tycho Rollenhagen congratulates; ad umbilicum deducas = "bring [your labours] to completion" (Horace's ad umbilicum, the roller-end of a book-scroll).
  • The Heinrich Julius / astrology dispute.
  • Henricus Iulius = Heinrich Julius, Duke of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel (1564–1613), then a leading figure at the imperial court. A prognostic (prognosticum) of a future war-victory was being attributed to him; Rollenhagen denies being its source.
  • Garcaeus = Johannes Garcaeus the Younger (1530–1574), Lutheran theologian and astrological author, named as the supposed authority for the war-prophecy.
  • Doctor Megabacchus, Rothius = two further parties to the rumour, not securely identifiable (see Confidence).
  • Sed fertur equis auriga, nec audit currus habenas quotes Virgil, Georgics I.514 ("the charioteer is swept along by his horses, and the car heeds not the reins") — the runaway-chariot image for Heinrich Julius's martial ardour.
  • The Hofmann succession. de vocando Theologo in locum Hofmanni = the consultation over calling a theologian to replace Hofmann — i.e. Daniel Hofmann (the Helmstedt theologian deposed in the "Hofmann controversy," 1598–1601), in whose vacated place Rollenhagen expects to help, while privately advancing Tycho's interests with the Emperor.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. = salutem; Bene et quam diutissime vale → "Fare well, and for the longest time"; subscription Tuae Gen[erosae] N[obilitati] addictiss[imus] → "Most devoted to Your Generous Nobility."
  • What this is. A geniture (nativity) record appended after the letter's close — a piece of raw astrological data, not epistolary prose: birth date, time, and place for a named prince, together with a technical complaint about how a published source mishandled that time.
  • The subject. Heinrich Julius, Duke of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel and Prince-Bishop of Halberstadt, a major figure at the imperial court and a patron with scientific interests. Dates, hour, and place are preserved here exactly as given in the source, per instruction, without correction; note for the record that standard modern reference works generally give his birth as 15 (not 14) October 1564, so the day here may reflect either an authentic variant tradition, an error already present in Rollenhagen's source, or a transcription slip — flagged as a crux rather than silently harmonised.
  • The sender and his own astrological standing. Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), rector of the Magdeburg Gymnasium from 1575 and author of the celebrated didactic beast-epic Froschmeuseler, was also himself a practising astrological author — his 1583 lectures on calendar-practice and astrology were later absorbed into David Origanus's Ephemerides [Oestmann, Rantzau und die Astrologie (2004)]. This explains why Rollenhagen is competent to adjudicate a technical dispute over Garcaeus's geniture-correction method, not merely relaying it. Elsewhere in the correspondence Rollenhagen is known to have fed Tycho inside information from the imperial court via his brother-in-law Caspar Lehmann, and to have first alerted Tycho to Ursus's plagiarism [Christianson, On Tycho's Island (2000)].
  • Johannes Garcaeus and the "Trutina Hermetis." Johannes Garcaeus (1530–1574), author of Astrologiae methodus (Basel, 1570), a widely used printed collection of nativities/genitures [Oestmann, Rantzau und die Astrologie, citing a Garcaeus volume from the Breitenburg library]. The "Trutina Hermetis" ("Balance/Scale of Hermes") is a standard astrological rectification technique, drawn from the pseudo-Ptolemaic Centiloquium ("Fructus," aphorism 51): the Moon's zodiacal position at the moment of conception should match the ascendant at birth (or its opposite sign), and the technique is used to correct or verify an uncertain birth time [Oestmann, Rantzau und die Astrologie, quoting the rule in German translation]. Rollenhagen's charge is that Garcaeus misapplied this rule ("falsissimam... trutinam") and thereby corrupted Heinrich Julius's recorded birth hour.
  • Why this matters. This is a rare glimpse of Tycho's circle doing hands-on astrological source-criticism — checking a published prince's geniture against the technical rectification method that was supposed to validate it, and finding the published source wanting.
