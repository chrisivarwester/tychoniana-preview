The original

CLARISSIMO ET EXCELLENTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO IOHANNI ANTONIO MAGINO PATAVINO, RERUM MATHEMATICARUM IN ILLUSTRI BONONIENSEM GYMNASIO PROFESSORI ET AMICO SUO HONORANDO. BONONIAM.

S.

LITERAS tuas humanissimas pariter atque eruditissimas, Clarissime et Excellentissime Domine MAGINE, 4 Martij superioris Anni datas, recte quidem accepi; nulla tamen ad eas rescribendi hucusque sese mihi obtulit certa commoditas, priusquam nunc per filium meum natu maiorem, TYCHONEM nomine, Illustrissimum et Amplissimum Magni Persarum Regis Legatum comitantem. Cumque a primario Legato, natione Anglo, ipsum Bononiam, in itinere suo Romam versus, transiturum, perceperim, non omittendum duxi, quin meis te salutarem, atque ad ultimas tuas nonnulla rescriberem.

Quod negotium illud, tuæ fidei secreto commissum, tam diligenter procurare non degraveris, summas tibi habeo debeoque gratias. Et placet sane ista ratio, quam proponis, de Excellentissimo illo BERNARDINO BALDO Urbinate, Guastallæ Abbate, qui vitas Mathematicorum conscribere proposuit. Ibi enim id, quod molimur, quam commodissime inseri poterit. Cuperem tamen (quod et antea rogavi) primas conceptiones mecum communicari, ut meum etiam aliquale iudicium interponam, ex quo res meæ nemini rectius quam mihi ipsi constare possint. Carmina in mei gratiam concinnata, et ulterius, quam priora erant, aucta, filius monstrabit. Si quid in prioribus mutatum est, potest hinc restitui, et ea quæ ibi desiderantur compleri. Cætera tuæ prudentiæ et benevolæ erga me voluntati committo.

Quod Illustrissimus et Reverendissimus Cardinalis SS. QUATUOR, Vir procul dubio eximiæ et variæ doctrinæ, rerumque Mathematicarum amantissimus, Eclipsin ☉lis Anni 98, qua licuerat diligentia, observârit, perlubenter accepi, grataque est eorum, quæ ipsius Dominatio Illustrissima denotavit, mecum communicata descriptio, licet ob serenitatis raritatem, tam præcise omnia (uti voluerit) designare nequierit. Gaudeo, atque hisce studijs plurimum congratulor, quod Romæ inter Illustrissimos etiam Cardinales reperiantur, qui Divinam Astronomiam (uti vel ex hoc solo liquet) in precio habeant. Hæc Eclipsis Uraniburgi in Dania meis adhuc isthic residuis Instrumentis observata est in mea absentia. Habitabam enim tunc prope Hamburgum, ubi serenitas requisita eius apparentias satis accurate rimandi non affulsit. Quæ vero in dicto Uraniburgo animadversa sunt, in adiuncta charta habes, unâque mei calculi Epilogismum, prout is ex novissima restitutione nostra in motu Luminarium diductus est. Quin et huius Anni Eclipsin ☉larem, quæ 10 die Iulij contigit, simul assignavi, tam quoad observationem quam calculum meum reformatum, ut cum vestris in Italia considerationibus, sive a prænominato Illustrissimo Cardinale, sive te ipso, aut quovis alio habitis, conferri queat. Erues ex hisce quos nunc mitto numeris aliam rationem, eandemque exactiorem, quam prior erat, in curriculo Lunari eiusque Phænomenis præfiniendis.

Non ignoravi, Tabulas illas Lunaris motus, quas a Nobili iuvene FRANCISCO TENGNAGLIO, meo domestico, accepisti, immaturas esse; ideoque eas tunc temporis tibi mittere inconsultum duxi. Præterquam enim quod ob rerum mearum, cum e Dania discederem, conturbationem, ea quæ de ☾â prius conceperam, diligentius examinare nequierim, ij etiam numeri, qui a quodam studiosorum meorum, Typographo cuidam imperito Hamburgi, post meum discessum, commissi sunt, admodum negligenter et depravate imprimebantur, ut omnem istum laborem frustraneum habuerim. Dum vero aliquantisper in Saxonia postmodum subsisto, clementissime id concedente Cæsarea Maiestate (quæ me Pragam vocârat) donec pestis in Bohemia remitteret, Tabulas illas Prostaphæresium Lunarium denuo ad scrupula secunda diligentissime supputari feci; ubi non saltem ille, de quo tu mones, sed et alij errores, negligentia tam supputatoris, quam Typographi prius commissi, satis mihi patuerunt. Sed cum ne sic quidem apparentiæ Lunares hinc derivatæ cœlesti normæ tam præcise congruerant, prout desiderabam, aliam inivi rationem, et nactus melius hîc in Bohemia otium, tam Hypothesin Lunaris motus, quam ei superstructos numeros denuo redintegravi, omniaque huc facientia nunc prælo adorno. Quæ ubi Typis absoluta, et Progymnasmatum Astronomicorum e nostris Tomo primo debito loco inserta fuerint, te eorundem, unâ cum toto libro, qui Solaris quoque motionis normam expedit, et millenas circiter fixas stellas accurate emendat, reddam participem. Interea hanc intervenientem morulam candide accipe. Licet autem Tabulæ illæ priores, Lunam concernentes, non exactæ fuerint, nec mihi ipsi suffecerint, tantam tamen deviationem, quantam tu in supputatione trium exemplorum, quæ in Epistolis Astronomicis reperiuntur, inducere videris, non admittunt, prout repetitio calculi iuxta nostram sententiam hisce adiungenda (modo Legatus, opinione citius, non abierit) te edocebit. Ubi vero librum nostrum Progymnasmatum, cuius memini, nactus fueris, omnia plenius et subtiliori indagine inde percipies. Tabulam illam primi mobilis universaliorem a te ad singula 10 minuta extensam si per occasionem mecum communicaveris, rem gratissimam præstiteris. Ego vicissim nonnulla ex meis, sive prælo absoluta vel secus, data oportunitate, retribuam.

Ephemerides tuas 10 Annorum postremo emissas Francofurto accepi. Nihil in ijs, quod a prioribus differat, te correxisse animadverto, sed solummodo quædam supplementi loco addidisse, mea equidem opinione non inutilia. Facis spem reliquorum 20 Annorum mox subsequentium, quos pariter obtinere averem.

ORIGANUM illum, Francofurtensem ad Oderam Mathematicum et Ephemeridum supputatorem, in hisce tuis ultimo editis necdum, prout meruit, redarguisti, facturus fortassis idipsum in residuo Ephemeridum Tomo. Mihi sane eximia tua diligentia in hac pragmateiâ semper placuit, eamque alijs commendare non desino. Et abhorret animus ab ijs, qui philautia quadam et arrogantia moti aliorum labores vel levissima de causa insectantur, sæpe nodum in scirpo quærentes; sed accidit ijs, nec iniuria, quod Comicus aiebat: dicendo quæ volunt, rursus quæ nollent audiunt. Quod Tabulas primi mobilis præstantiores procul dubio, quam hactenus habuimus, mediteris, probo, utque Opus illud lucem publicam mature videat, exopto.

De Heliacis emersionibus et occultationibus Planetarum et fixarum non est, quod admodum sis sollicitus. Res hæc præcisione rata tractari nequit, eo quod limites illi multifariam varient, non saltem ratione diversæ inclinationis sphæræ, sed prout aëris puritas in quovis Horizonte maior minorve fuerit. Nec est in omnibus stellis fixis primæ aut secundæ magnitudinis, par ratio, uti neque in cæteris: differentia etiam non minima incidit, prout stellæ in parallelo aliquo Horizontis, a loco ubi Sol oritur vel occidit, versus meridiem aut septentrionem, plus minusve distiterint. Et Planetæ, prout visibiles diametros, iuxta ☌ vel ☍ ☉lis occasione variæ remotionis a Terra alterant, nonnihil etiam discriminis pariunt, Latitudine ipsorum, quæ necdum satis comperta est, etiam difficultatem non postremam ingerente, ut ob id hoc negotium, per se etiam non admodum necessarium, vix a quoquam iusta amussi extricari posse autumem. Unde etiam in eo hactenus curiosam operam non locavi. Crepusculorum terminos quoad ☉lem a veteribus Astronomis constitutos, mediocriter bene se habere animadverti. Nam ☉lem iuxta æquinoctia arcum crepusculi efficere part. 17½ quam proxime, iam antea in Dania comperi. An id in Italia et alibi, sub rectiore sphæra consentiat, indecisum relinquo. Ephemerides ☉ huius anni, aut nunc per filium, aut postmodum, si tam cito exemplum describi nequeat, obtinebis. Addidi 4 annorum Ephemerides ☉ interea supputatas; ex his facile plures derivabis. Apogæum eius nunc versatur in P. 5 M. 40 ♋: et Eccentricitas est P. 2 M. 9 qualium semidiameter Eccentrici 60. Longitudinem eius non ab inæquali Æquinoctiorum præcessione, qualem COPERNICUS frustra admisit, derivo, sed ab Æquinoctio simplici, quod proximis sæculis insensibiliter variat, et singulis nunc annis 51'' præcise, qualis etiam est fixarum motus, antevertit. Si vero Æquinoctiorum præcessio e Spica ♍ ad imitationem COPERNICI nunc constituenda foret, esset illa hoc ipso anno part. 28 M. 16 exuperans Copernicæam numerationem sexta Grad. parte, licet prima stella ♈ ab Æquinoctio verno non elongetur ultra Gradus 27 M. 37. Quæ discrepantia hinc originem trahit, quod COPERNICUS intervallum longitudinis Spicæ et primæ ♈ ex PTOLEMÆI abaco, non ipso cœlo (uti oportuit) perperam assumpserit, discrimine incidente M. 39, quod est intolerabile. Verum de his et similibus alias forte copiosius tecum philosophari licebit, ubi Opera mea Astronomica, quorum editionem, quantum in me est, urgeo et accelero, lucem publicam viderint. Plurima inibi invenies ab ijs, quæ hactenus in Astronomicis tradita sunt, non parum discrepantia.

Si quid in elevationibus Poli illic in Italia, cuius exactiorem Geographiam moliris, exploratum habes, aut (quod mallem) longitudinibus locorum rectius limitandis, id, quicquid est, mihi impertiaris velim. Conducerent huc ad longitudinis et Meridianorum differentias apposite determinandas Eclipses Lunares, modo aliquas ibi diligenter præteritis annis cœlitus denotatas in promptu haberemus. Quod si non concedatur, attende diligenter per tua Instrumenta ad illam, quam hic labens annus subministrat, qui etiam in fine, statim a brumali Solstitio, quandam evidentem Solarem exhibebit, pariter apud vos studiose observandam, cuius calculum non minus quam Lunarem e numeris nostris, ijs, quæ prius pollicitus sum, subiungo, ut cum animadversione cœlesti isthic conferri queat, et certitudo numerationis nostræ explorari.

Instrumenta mea omnia, quibus in Dania utebar, numero 28, modo hîc Pragæ, non longe ab Arce, in Cæsaris quadam magnifice extructa domo, disposita sunt. Quod Colophonis loco indicare volui. Neque enim plura nunc scribere temporis penuria patitur. Id saltem peramanter te rogo, ut Filium meum, præsentium exhibitorem, tibi diligenter commendatum habeas, atque ut ea, quæ Bononiæ visu digna sunt, monstrari cures, eumque pariter Romæ tuis amicis et familiaribus commendes. Iamque te, Excellentissime Domine MAGINE, quam optime et diutissime valere cupio.

Dabantur Pragæ Bohæmorum Die 24 Ianuarij Anno 1601.
Excellentiæ tuæ studiosissimus
TYCHO BRAHE, manu propria scripsi.

To the most renowned and most excellent man, Lord Giovanni Antonio Magini of Padua, Professor of the Mathematical sciences in the illustrious Gymnasium of Bologna, and his honoured friend. To Bologna.

Greeting.

Your most courteous and most learned letter, most renowned and excellent Lord Magini, dated 4 March of last year, I duly received; but no sure means of writing back to it has offered itself to me until now — by my elder son, Tycho by name, accompanying the most illustrious and ample Legate of the Great King of the Persians. And since I have learned that, from the chief Legate (an Englishman by nation), he will pass through Bologna on his journey toward Rome, I judged it not to be omitted that I should greet you by my [letter], and write back somewhat to your last.

That you have not found it a burden to procure so diligently that business secretly committed to your trust, I owe and give you the greatest thanks. And the plan you propose pleases me well — about that most excellent Bernardino Baldi of Urbino, Abbot of Guastalla, who has proposed to write the Lives of the Mathematicians. For there, what we are contriving can be most conveniently inserted. Yet I should wish (as I asked before) that the first conceptions be communicated to me, that I may interpose some judgement of mine too — since my affairs can be known to no one more rightly than to me myself. The verses composed in my honour, and further augmented than the earlier were, the son will show. If anything in the earlier was changed, it can be restored hence, and what is wanting there completed. The rest I commit to your prudence and your benevolent will toward me.

That the most illustrious and most reverend Cardinal of the Santi Quattro [SS. Quatuor] — a man, doubtless, of eminent and varied learning, and most loving of Mathematical things — observed the Solar Eclipse of the year [15]98, with what diligence was possible, I received most gladly; and the description, communicated to me, of what His most illustrious Lordship noted, is welcome — though, on account of the rarity of clear weather, he could not designate everything so precisely as he wished. I rejoice, and greatly congratulate these studies, that at Rome, even among the most illustrious Cardinals, are found those who hold divine Astronomy in esteem (as is clear from this alone). This Eclipse was observed at Uraniborg in Denmark, with my instruments still remaining there, in my absence — for I was then dwelling near Hamburg, where the clear weather requisite for searching out its appearances accurately did not shine. What was noted at the said Uraniborg you have in the adjoined paper, together with the epilogue of my calculation, as it is drawn from our newest restitution of the motion of the Luminaries. Nay, I have at the same time assigned this year's Solar Eclipse, which happens on the 10th of July [1601], both as to the observation and to my reformed calculation, that it may be compared with your considerations in Italy — whether had by the aforesaid most illustrious Cardinal, or by yourself, or by any other. From these numbers I now send, you will draw another reckoning, and that more exact than the former, in defining the Lunar course and its phenomena.

I was not ignorant that those Tables of the Lunar motion, which you received from the noble youth Franz Tengnagel, my household-member, were immature; and so I judged it ill-advised to send them you at that time. For besides that, on account of the disturbance of my affairs when I left Denmark, I could not examine more diligently what I had earlier conceived about the Moon, those numbers too, which were committed by one of my students to a certain unskilled Printer at Hamburg after my departure, were printed very negligently and corruptly, so that I held all that labour frustrated. But while I afterward stayed a while in Saxony (the Imperial Majesty, who had called me to Prague, most clemently granting it, until the plague in Bohemia should abate), I had those Tables of the Lunar Prosthaphaereses computed anew, most diligently, to the seconds; where not only the error you warn of, but other errors too — committed before by the negligence as much of the computer as of the Printer — became clear enough to me. But since, even so, the Lunar appearances derived hence did not agree so precisely with the celestial norm as I desired, I entered upon another method; and, having got better leisure here in Bohemia, I restored anew both the Hypothesis of the Lunar motion and the numbers built upon it, and am now setting all that conduces hither at the press. Which, when finished in print, and inserted in the due place of the first Volume of our Astronomical Progymnasmata, I will make you partaker of — together with the whole book, which also expedites the norm of the Solar motion, and accurately emends about a thousand fixed stars. Meanwhile take this intervening little delay in good part. But although those earlier Tables, concerning the Moon, were not exact, nor sufficient to me myself, yet they do not admit so great a deviation as you seem to introduce in the computation of the three examples found in the Astronomical Letters — as the repetition of the calculation, according to my view, to be adjoined to these (provided the Legate has not left sooner than expected), will teach you. But when you have got our book of the Progymnasmata, of which I spoke, you will perceive all more fully and by a subtler inquiry. If, by occasion, you would communicate to me that more universal Table of the primum mobile, extended by you to single 10 minutes, you would do a most welcome thing; I in turn, when occasion is given, will repay some of mine, whether finished at the press or not.

Your Ephemerides of 10 years, last published, I received from Frankfurt. I notice that you have corrected nothing in them that differs from the earlier, but only added certain things by way of supplement — not useless, in my opinion. You give hope of the remaining 20 years soon to follow, which I should likewise wish to obtain.

That Origanus, the Mathematician of Frankfurt-on-Oder and computer of Ephemerides, you have not yet refuted in these last-published [of yours], as he deserved — perhaps to do the same in the remaining Volume of Ephemerides. To me indeed your eminent diligence in this work has always pleased, and I do not cease to commend it to others. And my mind abhors those who, moved by a certain self-love and arrogance, persecute others' labours for the slightest cause, often seeking a knot in a bulrush [a problem where none is]; but it happens to them — and not unjustly — what the Comic [Terence] said: "by saying what they want, they hear in turn what they would not." That you meditate Tables of the primum mobile more excellent, doubtless, than we have hitherto had, I approve; and I wish that that Work may see the public light in good time.

About the heliacal emersions and occultations [risings and settings] of the Planets and the fixed [stars], there is no [cause] that you should be very anxious. This matter cannot be treated with sure precision, because those limits vary manifoldly — not only by reason of the sphere's different inclination, but as the purity of the air, in any given Horizon, is greater or less. Nor is the reckoning equal in all fixed stars of the first or second magnitude, as neither in the rest; a not-small difference too falls in, as the stars in some parallel of the Horizon are more or less distant — toward south or north — from the place where the Sun rises or sets. And the Planets, as they alter their visible diameters near the conjunction [☌] or opposition [☍] of the Sun, by occasion of their varying distance from the Earth, also produce some difference; their Latitude (which is not yet sufficiently established) introduces a difficulty not the least; so that I reckon this business — not very necessary in itself either — can scarcely be extricated by anyone with just exactness. Whence I have not hitherto placed curious labour on it. The limits of the twilights with respect to the Sun, set by the ancient Astronomers, I have found moderately well [established]; for I found before, in Denmark, that the Sun, near the equinoxes, makes a twilight-arc of 17½° most nearly. Whether this agrees in Italy and elsewhere, under a more upright sphere, I leave undecided. The Ephemeris of the Sun for this year you will obtain, either now by the son, or afterward, if a copy cannot be made so quickly; I have added the Ephemerides of the Sun for 4 years, computed meanwhile; from these you will easily derive more. Its Apogee is now at 5° 40′ of Cancer [♋]; and the Eccentricity is 2° 9′, of which the semidiameter of the Eccentric is 60. Its longitude I derive not from an unequal precession of the equinoxes (such as Copernicus vainly admitted), but from a simple Equinox — which in the recent centuries varies imperceptibly, and now anticipates precisely 51″ each year, such as is also the motion of the fixed [stars]. But if the precession of the equinoxes were now to be set from Spica of Virgo [♍], in imitation of Copernicus, it would be, this very year, 28° 16′ — exceeding the Copernican numeration by a sixth of a degree — although the first star of Aries [♈] is not elongated from the vernal equinox beyond 27° 37′. Which discrepancy draws its origin from this: that Copernicus wrongly assumed the interval of longitude of Spica and of the first of Aries from Ptolemy's table, not from the sky itself (as he ought) — a difference of 39′ falling in, which is intolerable. But of these and the like it will perhaps be allowed to philosophise with you more copiously another time, when my Astronomical Works (whose edition I urge and hasten, so far as in me lies) shall have seen the public light. Very many things you will find therein not a little discrepant from what has hitherto been handed down in Astronomical [matters].

If you have anything explored in the elevations of the Pole there in Italy (of which you are preparing a more exact Geography), or (which I should prefer) in the more rightly limiting of the longitudes of places, share with me whatever it be. Lunar Eclipses would conduce hither, for aptly determining the differences of longitude and of Meridians, had we but some diligently noted there in past years. And if that be not granted, attend diligently, with your Instruments, to the one that this passing year furnishes — which also at its end, right after the winter Solstice, will exhibit a certain evident Solar one, equally to be studiously observed with you; whose calculation, no less than the Lunar, from our numbers, I subjoin to those I before promised, that it may be compared with the celestial observation there, and the certainty of our numeration explored.

All my Instruments, which I used in Denmark, 28 in number, are now arranged here at Prague, not far from the Castle, in a certain magnificently-built house of the Emperor's. Which I wished to indicate by way of a colophon; for the scarcity of time does not allow me to write more now. This only I lovingly ask: that you hold my Son, the bearer of the present [letter], diligently commended to you, and take care that the things worth seeing at Bologna be shown him, and that you likewise commend him at Rome to your friends and familiars. And now, most excellent Lord Magini, I wish you to fare most well and most long.

Given at Prague of the Bohemians, the 24th day of January, the year 1601.
Most zealous of Your Excellency,
Tycho Brahe, written in my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Giovanni Antonio Magini at Bologna (cf. brahe-t_16010220001, brahe-t_16000103002). Carried by Tycho's son travelling with the Persian embassy (cf. brahe-t_16010430001, brahe-t_16000124001).
  • Significance — the Baldi self-monument, the Roman cardinal's eclipse, the lunar-tables saga, and Tycho's solar/precession theory.
  • The Bernardino Baldi insertion (a key self-monument document). Tycho approves Magini's plan to insert Tycho's biography into Bernardino Baldi of Urbino's Lives of the Mathematicians (Le vite de' matematici) — the Italian counterpart to the Brasch/Magini panegyric-history commissions (brahe-t_16010414001, brahe-t_16000103002). He wants to vet "the first conceptions" himself (and the augmented verses).
  • The Cardinal's eclipse. A Roman Cardinal of the Santi Quattro [Coronati] observed the 1598 solar eclipse; Tycho is delighted "even Cardinals esteem divine Astronomy," and sends his own Uraniborg observation + calculation of that eclipse, plus the 10 July 1601 solar eclipse, for Italian comparison.
  • The lunar-tables saga (candid). Tycho explains why the lunar Tables Tengnagel gave Magini were immature: the disruption of leaving Denmark; a student's negligent, corrupt Hamburg printing; a Saxony-interval recomputation "to the seconds" that exposed more errors; and finally a wholly restored lunar hypothesis now at press for the Progymnasmata (with the solar norm + the c. 1000-star catalogue). He insists the old tables don't admit the large deviation Magini found in the three Epistolae examples (cf. brahe-t_16010220001), and asks for Magini's 10-minute primum mobile table.
  • Ephemerides & Origanus. Acknowledges Magini's 10-year Ephemerides (mere supplements, no corrections); urges Magini to refute Origanus (David Origanus of Frankfurt-on-Oder) "as he deserved," with a Terence tag ("by saying what they want, they hear what they would not") and the proverb "seeking a knot in a bulrush."
  • Heliacal risings & twilight. A careful statement that heliacal risings/settings cannot be treated with precision (atmospheric purity, latitude, star magnitude, planetary diameter/latitude); and the datum that the twilight-arc near the equinoxes is c. 17½° (the solar depression), found in Denmark.
  • Solar parameters & precession theory. Tycho's solar elements: Apogee 5° 40′ Cancer, Eccentricity 2° 9′ (of 60); longitude from a simple (uniform) equinox precessing 51″/year, not Copernicus's variable precession (cf. the Scaliger debate, brahe-t_16000723001); and a sharp catch that Copernicus took the Spica–Aries interval from Ptolemy's table, not the sky — a 39′ error "intolerable."
  • The 28 instruments at Prague. The closing colophon: "All my instruments, 28 in number, are now arranged at Prague … in a magnificently-built house of the Emperor's" (cf. brahe-t_16001110001, brahe-t_16000926003).
