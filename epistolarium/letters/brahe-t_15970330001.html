The original

TYCHONI BRAHE

NOBILISSIME et clarissime domine. Vix inter varias occupationes hoc momentum surripio, quod liceat tribus verbis tibi salutem dicere, et has theses in fasciculum conijcere, quas spero fore ad gustum tuum. Ego certe et auctori auctor fui edendi et Typographo excudendi. Epistolarum tuarum volumen primum percurri et ad hunc ELYSÆUM exemplum statim misi. Is te et colit et admiratur, Sed tu præstantissime Domine vale, et si ego tibi memoria excidi, velle ipse tibi aurem et succurrent anno 1584 hospites tibi GUILELMUS CURCELLIUS et ego, qui tui studiosissimus.

Scripsi Francofurti penult. Martij 1597.
[IACOBUS BONGARSIUS.]

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Tycho Brahe

Most noble and most renowned lord. Amid my various occupations I scarcely snatch this moment in which I may be allowed to say a greeting to you in three words, and to throw these theses together into a packet — theses which I hope will be to your taste. I, for my part, was indeed both the prompter to the author for publishing them and to the printer for striking them off. I have run through the first volume of your letters, and at once sent a copy to this Elysaeus [Élie]. He both esteems and admires you. But fare you well, most excellent lord; and if I have slipped from your memory, [pull] your own ear [to remind yourself], and there will come to your aid your guests of the year 1584, Wilhelm Curcellius [Guillaume de Courcelles] and I, who am most devoted to you.

Written at Frankfurt, on the penultimate [day] of March 1597.
[Jacques Bongars.]

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iacobus Bongarsius = Jacques Bongars (1554–1612), the French Huguenot diplomat, classical philologist and book-collector, in the service of Henri IV of France; long resident in and around Frankfurt and Strasbourg. His library is the core of the Bongarsiana at the Burgerbibliothek Bern, whence the MS witness ("A Bern, B 149 n. 323"). Bongars and a companion had been Tycho's guests on Hven in 1584 (see below).
  • Recipient address. TYCHONI BRAHE — a bare dative heading ("To Tycho Brahe"), not a full honorific salutation; kept as written.
  • has theses … in fasciculum conijcere — "to throw these theses together into a packet": Bongars is forwarding, with the letter, a set of printed academic theses (disputation propositions) he had a hand in seeing into print, auctori auctor fui edendi et Typographo excudendi ("I was the prompter [literally: author/instigator] to the author for publishing them, and to the printer for striking them off"). The wordplay auctori auctor ("to the author an author/prompter") is kept by rendering the second auctor as "prompter." The particular theses are not identified in the letter.
  • Epistolarum tuarum volumen primum — "the first volume of your letters": Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I), printed at Uraniborg (1596) — the only volume issued. Bongars reports having read it and forwarded a copy.
  • ad hunc ELYSAeUM … misi — "sent [a copy] to this Elysaeus." Elysaeus is most plausibly the Latinised first name Élie / Elisaeus of a contemporary (a humanist correspondent in Bongars's circle); the demonstrative hunc ("this [man]") implies someone known to both parties, but the letter gives no surname, so the identification is left open. The accusative exemplum is read as "a copy" (of the volume just mentioned).
  • hospites tibi … anno 1584 GUILELMUS CURCELLIUS et ego — "your guests of the year 1584, Wilhelm Curcellius and I." Guilelmus Curcellius = Guillaume de Courcelles (Latinised Curcellius), evidently Bongars's travelling companion when the two visited Tycho on Hven in 1584. Bongars invokes the shared visit as the token by which Tycho should recall him.
  • Crux (corrupt clause): "si ego tibi memoria excidi, velle ipse tibi aurem et succurrent … hospites …" The working transcription reads velle ipse tibi aurem, which is not construable as it stands (velle = present infinitive "to wish"; no governing verb). The clause almost certainly contains the classical idiom aurem (sibi) vellere / vellicare — "to pull/tweak the ear," the Roman gesture of jogging one's memory (cf. Virgil, Ecl. 6.3–4). The intended reading is therefore most likely "vellica [or: velle] ipse tibi aurem" = "pull your own ear [to remind yourself]," after which et succurrent … hospites ("and your guests … will come to your aid/mind") completes the conceit. The translation supplies "[pull] … [to remind yourself]" in brackets and renders on that reading; the printed form velle is kept in the Source block. The crux is flagged, not smoothed — hence the overall grade.
  • qui tui studiosissimus — "who [am] most devoted to you": the subscription formula (this chapter's convention, E. Tuae studiosiss.), with the verb elided.
  • Dateline. Scripsi Francofurti penult. Martij 1597 = "Written at Frankfurt [am Main], on the penultimate [day] of March 1597." March having 31 days, penultimo Martii = 30 March; this agrees with the catalogue label ("30. marts"). Bongars, a French subject, might be expected to write New Style by 1597, but the dateline gives no style marker and the edition does not flag the letter "n.s."; the date is therefore reproduced as written without conversion, and the style is left undetermined (O.S. assumed by default for consistency with the Danish/Imperial-side correspondence, but not asserted by the text).
  • Place. Francofurtum = Frankfurt am Main (the great book-fair city; consistent with Bongars's role in the printing of the forwarded theses).
