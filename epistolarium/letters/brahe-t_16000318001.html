The original

NOBILISSIMO ATQUE EXCELLENTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI DE BRAHE, EQUITI ET DOMINO IN KNUDSDORP, SOLLERTISSIMO ASTRONOMIÆ INSTAURATORI ASTRONOMORUMQUE NOSTRI SECULI PRIMARIO, IN ARCE CÆSAREA BENATICA IAM AGENTE, DOMINO SUO DEBITÆ OBSERVANTIÆ STUDIJS COLENDO.

S. P. D.

QUANTO me gaudio affecerit litteræ MONAVIJ mei, Vir magnifice atque excellentissime, Domine et amice observare, vix queo dicere. Non enim solum te veniam mihi dare nunciabant, qui ignotus ad tantum virum meas mittere ausus fueram, sed et eas tibi gratas fuisse, meque in amicorum tuorum numerum abs te susceptum esse, ac memoriam mei animo tuo servare, quemadmodum tuis ad ipsum litteris sis attestatus. Gratias itaque tibi ago, quas mente meâ concipere possum maximas, pro tantâ tuâ humanitate tantâque in me benevolentiâ, et tam benignâ tamque amicâ ad amicitiam tui admissione. Amavi equidem te et colui, suspexi, miratusque sum iampridem tacito quodam sensu, qui nunc lubens se promit, magisque aspirat ad tam suavem iucundamque tuæ benevolentiæ auram. Merentur id virtus tua, ingenium, eruditio, experientia, dignitas, maiestas, quibus me velut adamantino vinculo nexuisti, quod nulla vis laxabit aut dissolvet umquam, nulla, crede mihi, obscuratura est oblivio, donec

Lumine ademto animam moribundo corpore fundam.
Quod tibi hîc non scriptum, sed veluti per syngrapham sponsum volo.

Rogavi, opera tua a typographo ut mihi comparares tuo, quæcunque abs te edita, debitoque precio me illa persoluturum dixi. Vidi apud nonnullos quædam, non omnia, quod non ad nos perferantur a Bibliopolis nostris, quo divina huius scientiæ studia precio nunc apud plerosque sint, non male perspicientibus. Ea quantopere desiderem, nequeo scribere. Age ergo, Vir magnifice, domine et amice observande, ut tuâ operâ ab illo ea impetrem, et quamprimum Pragam ad ERNESTUM HEINCIUM, Cameræ Cæsareæ Registratorem et Expeditorem, coniugis meæ fratrem germanum, vel Vratislaviam ad MONAVIUM mitte, nec me, quod callidæ amantes solent, diu desiderio isto torque. Precium, quod volet ille, statim numerabitur. Velim addi Instrumentorum tuorum delineationem, quam nuper mihi ostendit Medicus D. BARTHOLOMÆUS SWALBIUS, intimus meus, qui et versus meos tibi gratos fuisse narrabat, sed a te recoctos ad salivam et gustum tuum, quod gaudeo; tamen me tanto viro placuisse, summæ laudi duco. Verum, pace tuâ, paucis mentem meam explicabo. Sensus meus est, te errores astrorum motusque dissipatos, quales fuere hactenus, singulari arte et mirâ industriâ in ordinem redegisse certiorem, Deumque tibi uni, quem eruditio et virtus in sublime vexerunt, quod antehac paucis particulatim concessit, nunc divite et pleno Cornu plenâque manu, quasi nimis tantarum opum largum profusorem, suggerere, ut quemadmodum Sibylla paucis contingere dicit apud MARONEM, ut ex Orco ad superas revertantur, et ijs tantum,

quos æquus amavit
Iuppiter, aut ardens evexit ad æthera virtus:
ita etiam tu solus repertus sis, cui digne ista contigerint ante omnes, teque merito nunc, quæ tam large accepisti, largâ manu quoque posteris largiri debere. Particulam Cœlitus pro In, vel Sub Cœlo, semper ego de loco cum omnibus autoribus intellexi, et obscuriorem mihi reddere videtur sensum, quam si legatur: astra sub cœli gemino axe. Sed ignosce, per Deum te oro, excellentissime domine, quod tam libere hæc scribo. Tibi enim manum do, tuo iudicio acquiesco, tuæ censuræ subscribo, teque valere et astronomiæ stabulum, Herculeis tuis laboribus, abs te magis magisque expurgari cupio,

Quæque opto, rata sint, faxit rex ætheris alti,
Ingeniumque tibi, vires, vitamque ministret.
Vigore animum nervosque robore impleat.

Vale ergo, Vir excellentissime, domine et amice, perpetuis debitæ observantiæ studijs a me colende.
Dabam Suidnicij Silesiorum XIIX die Martij anno epochæ Christianorum MDC.
Excell. atque dignitatis tuæ observantissimus
TOBIAS FISCHERUS, Philos. et Med. Doctor, manu propria.

Editi sunt a MERCATORE anno 1541 Lovanij
duo globi, Cœlestis et terrenus, quos ego ipse meis manibus nuper reduxi.
Nunciatum autem mihi est, paucis abhinc annis eosdem correctiores editos
esse Coloniæ, et formâ adhuc maiore, nec
in alia loca chartas istas divulgari. Peramanter itaque etiam atque etiam te
oro, num vera hæc sint, et an non a typographis illis comparari, si non per
me, per aliquem excellentis autoritatis virum queant, significa.

D. ERNESTUS HENCIUS affinis
meus singulis hebdomadibus ad me suas potest mittere litteras.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most excellent man, Lord Tycho de Brahe, knight and lord in Knudstrup, most skilful Restorer of Astronomy and chief of the astronomers of our age, now residing in the imperial castle of Benátky, his Lord, to be cultivated with the zeal of due respect.

Heartiest greeting.

With how great joy the letter of my Monau affected me, magnificent and most excellent Man, Lord and friend to be respected, I can scarcely tell. For it announced not only that you grant me pardon — I who, unknown, had dared to send mine to so great a man — but also that they were welcome to you, and that I have been received by you into the number of your friends, and that you keep memory of me in your mind, as you attested in your letter to him. I therefore give you the greatest thanks I can conceive, for your so great humanity and benevolence toward me, and for so kindly and friendly an admission to your friendship. I have indeed loved and cultivated, looked up to, and admired you long since with a certain silent feeling, which now gladly puts itself forth, and aspires the more to so sweet and pleasant a breeze of your benevolence. This your virtue, genius, erudition, experience, dignity, majesty deserve — by which you have bound me as with an adamantine chain, which no force will ever loosen or dissolve, nor (believe me) shall any oblivion darken, until

"When light is taken away, I pour forth my soul from my dying body."

Which I wish [regarded] by you here not [as merely] written, but as pledged, as it were, by a bond.

I asked that you procure for me from your printer your works — whatever has been published by you — and I said I would pay for them at the due price. I have seen some among certain [people], not all, since they are not brought to us by our booksellers (who perceive well enough at what [low] price the divine studies of this science now stand with most people). How greatly I desire them, I cannot write. Come then, magnificent Man, Lord and friend to be respected, that by your effort I may obtain them from him; and send them as soon as possible to Prague, to Ernst Heincius, Registrar and Dispatcher of the Imperial Chamber, my wife's own brother, or to Breslau to Monau — and do not, as cunning lovers are wont, torment me long with that longing. The price he wishes will be paid at once. I should wish to be added the drawing of your Instruments, which the physician Lord Bartholomäus Schwalb, my intimate, lately showed me — who also related that my verses were welcome to you, but recooked by you to your taste and palate (which I rejoice at; nevertheless I count it the highest praise to have pleased so great a man). But, with your leave, I will explain my mind in a few words. My opinion is that you have, with singular art and wonderful industry, reduced into surer order the errors and scattered motions of the stars, such as they have been hitherto; and that God supplies to you alone — whom erudition and virtue have raised on high — what before he granted to few, and [only] piecemeal, now with a rich and full Horn [of plenty] and full hand, as if [you were] too lavish a dispenser of such great riches: so that, just as the Sibyl says in Maro [Virgil] that it befalls few to return from Orcus to the upper world, and to those only

"whom favouring Jupiter loved, or whose blazing virtue raised to the heavens" —

so you too alone have been found, to whom these things have worthily befallen before all; and that you deservedly ought now to bestow with liberal hand on posterity what you have so liberally received. The particle Coelitus ["from heaven"], for In or Sub Coelo ["in" or "under heaven"], I have always understood, with all authors, of place; and it seems to render the sense more obscure to me than if it be read: "the stars under the twin axis of heaven." But forgive me — by God I beseech you, most excellent Lord — that I write these things so freely; for I give you my hand, I acquiesce in your judgement, I subscribe to your censure, and I desire that you fare well, and that the stable of astronomy be more and more cleansed by you, by your Herculean labours:

And may the things I wish be ratified — may the King of high heaven grant it,
and supply you genius, strength, and life;
may he fill your mind with vigour, and your sinews with strength.

Farewell, then, most excellent Man, Lord and friend, to be cultivated by me with perpetual zeal of due respect.
Given at Schweidnitz [Świdnica] of the Silesians, the 18th day of March, in the year of the Christian epoch 1600.
Most respectful of Your Excellence and dignity,
Tobias Fischer, Doctor of Philosophy and Medicine, with his own hand.

Two globes, a celestial and a terrestrial, were published by Mercator in the year 1541 at Louvain, which I myself recently brought home with my own hands. But I have been informed that a few years ago the same [globes], more corrected, were published at Cologne, and in a still larger format, and that these sheets are not made available anywhere else. I therefore beg you, most affectionately, again and again, to inform me whether this is true, and whether they cannot be procured from those printers — if not through me, then through some man of excellent standing.

Master Ernestus Hencius, my kinsman, can send his letters to me every week.

Apparatus

- Sender. Tobias Fischer, Silesian poet and physician (Philosophiae et Medicinae Doctor), of Schweidnitz (Świdnica) — the "Doctor Fischer" of the Monau correspondence (brahe-t_16000110001, brahe-t_16000516001, brahe-t_16000614001), whose commendatory poem Tycho had emended. This is his effusive letter of thanks on being admitted to Tycho's friendship (which Tycho had signalled in his letter to Monau).
- Significance — a humanist fan-letter, and a book-request. Beyond the lavish encomium, two concrete points:
1. Request for Tycho's published works. Fischer asks Tycho to have his printed works sent (he has seen "some, not all," the booksellers not stocking such little-valued science), to be delivered to Ernst Heincius — Registrar and Dispatcher of the Imperial Chamber at Prague, Fischer's brother-in-law — or to Monau at Breslau; "the price will be paid at once." He also wants the engraved delineation of Tycho's instruments, which his intimate, the physician Bartholomäus Schwalb (Swalbius — the Schwalmius courier of brahe-t_16000110001), had shown him. Schwalb reported Fischer's verses "welcome to [Tycho], but recooked to your taste" — i.e. Tycho's emendation (cf. brahe-t_16000110001/516001).
2. A textual nicety in Tycho's verse. Fischer, "with your leave," questions the word Coelitus ("from heaven") in one of Tycho's verses, preferring (for clearer local sense) "astra sub coeli gemino axe" ("the stars under the twin axis of heaven") — a courteous scholarly demurral, instantly subordinated to Tycho's judgement.
- The verse quotations (kept as verse; the edition's convention).
- "Lumine ademto animam moribundo corpore fundam" — "when light is taken away, I pour forth my soul from my dying body" (a hexameter, apparently Fischer's own / adapted), pledging undying friendship.
- Virgil, Aeneid VI.129–130: "…quos aequus amavit / Iuppiter, aut ardens evexit ad aethera virtus" — "whom favouring Jupiter loved, or whose blazing virtue raised to heaven" (the Sibyl, on the few who may return from the underworld) — applied as a figure for Tycho's unique divine gift; the surrounding Sibylla … apud Maronem … ex Orco ad superas revertantur paraphrases the same passage (Aen. VI.126–129).
- The closing tricolon ("Quaeque opto, rata sint…") — Fischer's own verse-prayer for Tycho's genius, strength, and life.
- The astronomiae stabulum … Herculeis tuis laboribus … expurgari figure casts Tycho's reform of astronomy as Hercules cleansing the (Augean) stable.
- Persons named. Tobias Fischer (sender); Jacob Monau; Ernst Heincius (imperial Chamber registrar, Fischer's brother-in-law); Bartholomäus Schwalb (physician). Tycho styled Astronomiae Instaurator ("Restorer of Astronomy").
- Calendar. Dateline Suidnicij Silesiorum XIIX [=18] die Martij anno … MDC; Silesia (Schweidnitz) kept the Gregorian (New Style) by 1600; marked [N.S.].
- What it is. The recovered opening of a letter from Tobias Fischer, a physician and philosopher writing from Świdnica (Schweidnitz) in Silesia, to Tycho Brahe, dated 18 March 1600 New Style — the surviving published text is only the closing valediction and verses.
- The globes. Fischer reports having personally brought home a set of Gerard Mercator's celebrated 1541 Louvain celestial and terrestrial globes, and asks Tycho to confirm a rumour of an improved, larger Cologne reissue of restricted distribution. Adam Mosley's Bearing the Heavens quotes this identical passage independently (drawing on the printed Tychonis Brahe Opera Omnia) in a discussion of Tycho's own instrument- and globe-collecting around 1600 [Mosley, Bearing the Heavens (2007)] — strong independent corroboration of both text and sense.
- Context. Mercator produced this globe pair at Louvain within the circle of Gemma Frisius and the other Louvain cosmographers who, a decade later, also shaped John Dee's training — the same intellectual milieu behind Sophia's "Louvain Core Group" concept in Dee studies, though this letter itself carries no Dee connection.
- Ernestus Hencius. Fischer names "D. Ernestus Hencius," his kinsman ("affinis meus"), as a weekly courier for correspondence between them; no independent identification of this person has been found among the works consulted — worth flagging as an open prosopographical lead.
