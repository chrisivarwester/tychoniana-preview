The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTIANUM LONGOMONTANUM.

S.

SCRIPSI tibi eruditissime et mihi plurimum dilecte M. CHRISTIANE bis, postquam huc Wittebergam veni, et semel a te literas accepi sub initium huius anni Ripis datas, ubi et priores meas tibi redditas ex tuis percepi, alteras postmodum Rostochium misi, quas te, quamprimum per hyemis asperitatem eo navigare licuit, ibi reperisse existimo. Ad tuas vero nunc brevibus, cum antea non sit oblata occasio, respondebo. Gratissimam rem praestas, quod in refutatione Capnuraniae Scoticae diligentem operam loces. Sunt saltem speciosa et pomposa eius omnia, ac veritati fucum faciunt. Invenies in libro nostro secundo de Cometa anni 77. suo loco, praesertim ubi in NOLTIJ placita disquiro, quantum in devia ab observatis detrudatur Cometes, si 5 proxime graduum parallaxin admisisset. Subsidium, quod expetis ab alijs Cometis, evidentius rem ostendentibus, satis firmum suppetit Cometa anni 80, qui et in meridiano et in 90 gradu iuxta horizontem non aliam declinationem mutavit, quam postulavit eius alteratio diurna, ex meridianis altitudinibus successive explorata, habita tamen refractionis in decliviori situ insinuatione, ut de alijs nunc nihil addam. Verum has confirmationes a pluribus Cometis petitas postea subiungere licebit, indicatione aliqua prius facta in Antapologia tua, qua introspecta, ulterius quid sentiam scies, quod mallem praesenti oretenus, quam absenti per literas indicare. De bruto Dithmarsico non ob id scripsi, quod a te redargutionem calumniarum eius expeterem, neque enim dignus est; sed saltem ut denotares obiter in charta aliqua, quae a nostris domesticis de plagio eius inaudivisti, quemadmodum fecit M. CHRISTIANUS IOHANNIS Ripensis, alterum expediet D. D. IOHANNES ROTHMANNUS, uti manes fratris defuncti postulant. Constitueram hîc edere sex illos quaterniones, qui in ☾ desiderantur, atque sic Tomum primum Progymnasmatum absolvere, ut in hisce Francofurdianis nundinis venalis extare possit, et iam inchoatus erat labor ille Typographicus, postquam papyrum meam impressoriam Wandesburgo acceperam. Verum dum Eclipsi ☾ die ultimo Ianuarij diligenter hîc per mea instrumenta attendo, deprehendi eius initium per ⅖ unius horae quam proxime calculum praevertere, ideoque a proposito destiti, donec huic deviationi provisum fuerit. Scis enim me exactam requirere concordantiam, quoad fieri possit. Nec satis mirari possum, quî fiat, quod Eclipsis anni 81, in Ianuario etiam facta et prope Apogaeum, tam belle nostris numeris conveniat, haec autem pariter iuxta Apogaeum et eodem anni tempore ita discrepet. Suspicor igitur aliquam adhuc inaequalitatem Anomaliae Lunae subesse, quae post aliquot annos peracta Eclipsium ad similia ferme loca restitutione praesertim iuxta Apogaeum et Perigaeum evidentius pateat, qua de re tecum, qui omnes nostras Eclipses diligenter examinasti, atque ipsarum varietates perspectas habes, conferre averem, ut eo citius huic obstaculo succurratur, quod facile fieri posse existimo. Sum in alijs rebus occupatissimus, ut satis huic disquisitioni vacare nequeam. Si igitur me accesseris cum auriga Wittebergensi, ubi isthuc reversus fuerit, rem mihi apprime gratam nec tibi inutilem praestiteris.

Sum denuo evocatus Pragam ex Clementissimo Caesaris mandato, per literas amplissimi Domini Procancellarij CORADUCIJ, ut ex adiuncto exemplari vides; et paro nunc omnia ad iter, quod tamen ante Pascha ingredi non licebit ob aquarum nimiam copiam, postquam nives subito dissolutae sunt, vias difficillimas et periculosas reddentem. Si adfueris et me comitari libuerit, dabo operam, ut tuis rebus rectius consulatur, quam uspiam alibi, sin vero hîc manere aut redire isthuc mavis, erit et hoc tibi liberum. Ego tuis commodis quocunque modo, quantumque in me est, sedulo studebo. Non patiar, ullum erga te officium desiderari.

Vale et literas has nemini ostende.
Dabantur Wittebergae die 21. Martij 1599.

TYCHO BRAHE TO CHRISTEN LONGOMONTANUS.

Greeting.

I have written to you twice, most learned and most beloved Master Christen, since I came hither to Wittenberg, and once received a letter from you, sent from Ribe at the beginning of this year — wherein I also perceived from yours that my earlier ones had been delivered to you; the others I afterward sent to Rostock, which I think you found there as soon as it was possible to sail thither through the harshness of winter. But to yours I will now reply briefly, since no occasion was offered before. You do a most welcome thing, in placing diligent effort on the refutation of the Scottish Capnurania. All his things are at least specious and pompous, and put a [false] colour on the truth. You will find, in our second book on the Comet of [15]77, in its place — especially where I examine Noltius's opinions — how far the Comet would be thrust into deviations from the observations, had it admitted a parallax of nearly 5 degrees. The support you seek from other Comets, more evidently showing the matter, is firmly enough supplied by the Comet of [15]80, which, both on the meridian and at the 90th degree along the horizon, changed no other declination than its diurnal alteration required (explored successively from the meridian altitudes, the influence of refraction in the more inclined position being however taken into account) — to say nothing now of the others. But these confirmations sought from several Comets may be appended afterward, some indication being first made in your Antapologia; which once examined, you will further know what I think — which I should prefer to indicate to you present, by word of mouth, than to you absent, by letter. Concerning the Dithmarsic brute I did not write that I might seek from you a refutation of his calumnies — for he is not worthy of it — but only that you might note down in passing, on some paper, what you have heard from our household about his plagiarism, as Master Christian Jensen of Ribe did; the other [part] Lord Doctor Johannes Rothmann will expedite, as the shade of his deceased brother demands. I had resolved to publish here those six quaternions that are wanting in [the theory of] the Moon, and so to complete the first Tome of the Progymnasmata, that it might be on sale at this Frankfurt fair; and that typographic labour was already begun, after I had received my printing-paper from Wandsbek. But while I diligently attend here, with my instruments, to the Eclipse of the Moon on the last day of January, I found that its beginning anticipated the calculation by very nearly ⅖ of one hour — and therefore I desisted from my purpose, until this deviation be provided for. For you know I require exact concordance, so far as can be done. Nor can I sufficiently wonder how it comes about that the eclipse of [15]81 — also in January and near the Apogee — agrees so beautifully with our numbers, while this one, likewise near the Apogee and at the same season, so discrepates. I suspect, therefore, that some inequality of the Moon's Anomaly still underlies it, which after some years — by the return of eclipses to nearly the same places, especially near Apogee and Perigee — may appear more evidently; about which I should wish to confer with you, who have diligently examined all our eclipses and have their varieties thoroughly known, that this obstacle may the sooner be remedied (which I think can easily be done). I am most occupied with other matters, so that I cannot be sufficiently free for this disquisition. If, therefore, you come to me with the Wittenberg coachman, when he has returned thither, you will do me a thing exceedingly welcome, and not unprofitable to you.

I am again summoned to Prague by the Emperor's most clement mandate, through a letter of the most ample Lord Vice-Chancellor Coraduz — as you see from the enclosed copy; and I now prepare everything for the journey, which, however, it will not be permitted to begin before Easter, on account of the excessive abundance of waters (after the snows were suddenly thawed), rendering the roads most difficult and dangerous. If you are present and it please you to accompany me, I will take pains that your affairs be better provided for than anywhere else; but if you prefer to stay here, or to return thither, this too will be free to you. I will assiduously strive for your interests, in whatever way, and as far as in me lies; I will not allow any service toward you to be wanting.

Farewell, and show this letter to no one.
Given at Wittenberg, 21 March 1599.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Christen Sørensen Longomontanus, then in Denmark (at Ribe), Tycho's senior assistant (cf. brahe-t_15981231001, brahe-t_15990111001). Written from the Wittenberg winter of 1598–99. The MS is a later Langebek afskrift.
  • The Craig refutation — "Capnurania Scotica." Tycho welcomes Longomontanus's work on refuting "the Scottish Capnurania" — Tycho's mocking coinage (Greek kapnos "smoke" + Urania) for John Craig's cometary doctrine that comets are terrestrial smoke/vapour: "all his things are specious and pompous, and put a false colour on the truth." (The commission of brahe-t_15981231001; Antapologia = Longomontanus's counter-defence against Craig's Apologia.) Tycho points him to his second book on the comet of 1577 (the De mundi … recentioribus phaenomenis) — esp. against Noltius — and to the comet of 1580 as decisive evidence (its declination changed only by diurnal parallax, refraction accounted for), arguing comets show no large (≈5°) parallax and so are celestial, not sublunary.
  • The Ursus plagiarism dispute ("the Dithmarsic brute"). brutum Dithmarsicum = Nicolaus Reimers Bär ("Ursus") of Dithmarschen, who had plagiarised Tycho's geo-heliocentric system and attacked him (in De hypothesibus astronomicis, 1597). Tycho — holding him "not worthy" of formal refutation — asks Longomontanus only to note down what the household heard of his plagiarism, as Master Christian Jensen of Ribe had; "the other part Doctor Johannes Rothmann will expedite, as the shade of his deceased brother demands" — apparently Johannes Rothmann vindicating the memory of his late brother (most plausibly Christoph Rothmann, the Hesse-Kassel mathematician, whose work Ursus had also touched; identification flagged, not asserted).
  • The lunar-theory crux (technical; this chapter's convention). Tycho had begun printing the six quaternions of the lunar theory to finish the first Tome of the Progymnasmata for the Frankfurt fair — but halted when the lunar eclipse of 31 January [1599] began ≈ ⅖ hour (24 min) before his calculation. He marvels that the eclipse of 1581 (also January, near apogee) fitted his numbers "beautifully," while this one (likewise near apogee, same season) "discrepates," and suspects an unaccounted inequality of the Moon's anomaly — to be tested by eclipses returning near apogee/perigee — wanting Longomontanus (who knows all his eclipses) to help. A first-rate document of Tycho's still-unfinished lunar theory.
  • The Prague summons. Tycho is again summoned to Prague by Coraduz's letter (enclosed in copy), preparing the journey — not before Easter, the thaw-flooded roads being "most difficult and dangerous." He offers Longomontanus to accompany him (with better provision than anywhere else) or to stay/return, as he prefers.
  • Glyphs/terms. ☾ = Moon; parallaxis → parallax; refractio → refraction; declinatio → declination; Apogaeum/Perigaeum → apogee/perigee; Anomalia → anomaly; inaequalitas → inequality.
  • Persons named. Longomontanus; John Craig (the Capnurania); Noltius; Nicolaus Reimers Bär (Ursus) ("the Dithmarsic brute"); Christian Jensen of Ribe; Johannes Rothmann (and his late brother — Christoph Rothmann?); Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor); Rudolf II.
  • Calendar. Dateline Wittebergae die 21. Martij 1599; Wittenberg/Saxony kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Latin corrected 'detrudetur' to the subjunctive 'detrudatur' (Dreyer VIII p.149); English unaffected.

- text error (low). Dreyer prints the present subjunctive 'detrudatur' (required inside the indirect question governed by 'disquiro'); our transcribed original has the indicative-looking 'detrudetur'. A single-letter corruption of the verb's mood. The English translation is unaffected — it happens to render the counterfactual sense correctly regardless.
