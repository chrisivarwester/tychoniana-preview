The original

DAVID CHYTRÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

S. P.

NOBILIS et Magnifice Domine Patrone colende! Ὅσσον ἔαρ χειμῶνος, ὅσον μᾶλον Βραβύλοιο ἅδιον, tanto mihi gratior et dulcior tua Epistola multis alijs eodem tempore allatis fuit, quam a summo et laudatissimo Mathematum artifice, ac vere (ut Summus et Clarissimus ille Princeps ad Cæsaream Mtem de te testatur) patre Astronomorum huius Seculi et unico huius laudatissimæ scientiæ instauratore scriptam, singulari mihi et liberis meis honori et ornamento esse iudico. Periucunda etiam, adeo benigna et humanissima voluntatis tuæ significatio hac in parte fuit, quod exiguum et tenue observantiæ erga te huius Academiæ officium, tibi Rostochium ingresso exhibitum, quod sine scelere prætermitti non poterat, mentione literarum tuarum non indignum esse censes, tibique non ingratum nec iniucundum accidisse; τούς τε ἀγαμοὺς καὶ ἀντὶ μικρῶν οἴδασθαι χάριν ostendis. Ad Eruditos vero et Claros viros scribens, cum amicorum omnia communia sint, maxime duxi communes amicorum amicos esse oportere. Ideoque de amici præstantissimi Viri statu præsenti, cuius cognoscendi desiderio teneri illos non dubitabam, significandum existimavi, præsertim cum eruditissimæ illius et sublimi ingenio tuo accurate elaboratæ epistolæ (ad quendam Midam his studijs iniquiorem) exemplum ideo te mihi coram dedisse intelligerem, ut cum pluribus etiam alijs amicis communicarem. Quare non solum IACOBO MONAVIO, verum PAULO SCHEDIO MELISSO, etiam Poëtarum nostræ ætatis Principi, et IOHANNI POSTHIO, utriusque Artis Apollineæ antistiti, et ABRAHAMO ORTELIO Geographo, quem proximus Mensis Iunius Terris eripuit, et aliquot alijs minus celebribus impertivi legendam; qui animo et voce grata officium meum vicissim prædicarunt. Nobilissimo autem Heroi HENRICO RANZOVIO, sapientia et virtute præstanti, de hac munificentia gratulor, quod cæteris laudibus amplissimis hanc quoque addidit, ut ATLANTI Herculeis laboribus axem stellis ardentibus aptum sustinenti hospitium ex Patria discedenti præberet, cuius memoriam URANIA ad omnem posteritatem grata recolet et diuturnius quam ullæ Pyramides aut Obelisci propagatam celebrabit. Quæ URANIA

Regales animos primum dignata movere,
Proxima tangentes Cœlo fastigia rerum,
nunc etiam invictissimi Imperatoris RUDOLPHI animum flectit, ut patrem Astronomorum huius seculi et unicum laudatissimæ huius scientiæ instauratorem, in patria Centauris quibusdam ac Midis fastiditum, ad se invitet et Regiam hanc ac cœlestem sapientiam Cæsareo suo Patrocinio et sumptu excoli et in sublimi illa arce ætheream artem conservari et longe lateque in suo Imperio spargi cupiat, sicut primi CÆSARIS IULIJ, confusam eo tempore Anni Romani rationem restituere molientis, animum movit, ut SOSIGENEM Mathematicum in Ægypti regno minus honorifice a quibusdam habitum, Romam ad se traduceret, ut sublimem illam ac Cœlestem siderum doctrinam in sublimi summi Imperatoris terrarum theatro, maiori cum sua gloria et Reip. fructu propagaret. Proficiscere igitur Deo duce et auspice CHRISTO, quo te Augustissimus Imperator et tua τύχη vocat et viri Excellentissimi ac optimi D. D. THADDÆI HAGGÆCIJ, studiorum tuorum socij, et amici coniunctissimi, cum quo annis abhinc triginta Viennæ mihi etiam necessitudo intercessit, consuetudine et familiaritate iucundissima, et Reipublicæ profutura, felix fruere; et opera, tot annorum vigilijs et curis elaborata, feliciter pertexe. Nec prolixiore Epistola nunc te ad iter accinctum remorari volo, sed Deum ardentibus votis precor, ut te incolumem ac florentem optimis studijs servet et cursum huius viæ et vitæ totius, ad Dei gloriam et Reipublicæ salutem gubernet. Poëmata in tui gratiam scripta ad Daniam, et Germaniæ de tali hospite lætantis, et BRASCHIJ nostri RUDOLPHO Cæsari et tibi de tuo adventu gratulantis, inprimis vero Epigramma, quod restitutioni Siderum, Duci ULRICO inscriptæ, præmisisti, summa cum voluptate legi ac coniunctim ea typis descripta extare et in eruditorum manibus versari, toto pectore opto.

Bene et feliciter vale.
Rhodopoli Nonis Octobris Anno 1598.
Mtiam . reverenter colens
DAVID CHYTRÆUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

DAVID CHYTRAEUS TO TYCHO BRAHE.

Heartiest greeting.

Noble and Magnificent Lord, Patron to be revered! "As much as spring [is sweeter] than winter, as much as the apple [is] sweeter than the sloe" — by so much was your Letter more pleasing and sweeter to me than the many others brought at the same time; which, written by the highest and most praised craftsman of the Mathematical arts, and truly (as that Highest and most renowned Prince testifies of you to the Imperial Majesty) the father of the Astronomers of this age and the sole restorer of this most praised science, I judge to be a singular honour and ornament to me and my children. Very pleasant, too — so kind and most humane — was the signification of your goodwill in this respect: that you judge the slight and thin act of respect toward you of this University [Rostock], shown to you on your entering Rostock (which could not without crime be omitted), not unworthy of mention in your letter, and [say it] befell you not unwelcome nor unpleasant; you show [the saying] that "the good know how to be grateful even for small things." But writing to learned and renowned men — since all things of friends are common — I have especially thought that friends' friends ought to be shared; and therefore I judged I should signify [to them] the present state of my most excellent friend, with the desire of knowing which I did not doubt they were held — especially since I understood you had given me, in person, a copy of that most learned letter, accurately elaborated by your sublime genius (to a certain Midas, rather hostile to these studies), for this very reason: that I might share it with several other friends. Wherefore I imparted it to be read not only to Jacob Monau, but to Paul Schede Melissus, the Prince of the Poets of our age, and to Johann Posthius, master of both Apollonian arts [poetry and medicine], and to Abraham Ortelius the Geographer — whom this past June snatched from the earth — and to some others less famous, who with grateful mind and voice in turn proclaimed my service. And to the most noble Hero Henrik Rantzau, excelling in wisdom and virtue, I give congratulation on this munificence: that to his other most ample praises he added this too, that he gave hospitality, to you departing from your fatherland — an Atlas sustaining, by Herculean labours, the axis fitted with burning stars — whose memory Urania, grateful, will cherish to all posterity, and will celebrate, propagated longer than any Pyramids or Obelisks. Which Urania —

who first deigned to move royal minds,
[those] touching the summits of things nearest to Heaven —

now also bends the mind of the most invincible Emperor Rudolf, that he invites to himself the father of the Astronomers of this age and the sole restorer of this most praised science — disdained in [his] fatherland by certain Centaurs and Midases — and desires that this royal and celestial wisdom be cultivated by his imperial patronage and expense, the ethereal art preserved in that lofty citadel, and spread far and wide through his Empire: just as Urania moved the mind of the first Caesar, Julius — striving to restore the then-confused reckoning of the Roman Year — that he brought over to himself at Rome Sosigenes the Mathematician (held less honourably by some in the kingdom of Egypt), that he might propagate that sublime and celestial doctrine of the stars in the lofty theatre of the highest Emperor of the lands, with greater glory to himself and fruit to the Commonwealth. Set out, therefore, with God as leader and Christ as guide, whither the most august Emperor and your Tyche [Fortune] call you; and enjoy happily — to the Commonwealth's profit — the most pleasant company and familiarity of the most excellent and best man Lord Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius, the companion of your studies and most closely-joined friend (with whom, thirty years ago at Vienna, a bond also passed for me); and happily weave to the end the works elaborated by the vigils and cares of so many years. Nor will I now delay you, girded for the journey, with a longer letter, but pray God with ardent vows that he keep you safe and flourishing in your best studies, and govern the course of this journey and of your whole life, to God's glory and the Commonwealth's salvation. The poems written in your honour for Denmark, and [those] of Germany rejoicing over such a guest, and [those] of our Brasch congratulating Rudolf Caesar and you on your coming — but especially the Epigram which you prefixed to the restitution of the Stars, inscribed to Duke Ulrich — I read with the greatest pleasure; and I desire with my whole heart that they be set forth in print together, and circulate in the hands of the learned.

Fare well and happily.
At Rostock, on the Nones of October [7 October], the year 1598.
Reverently revering Your Magnificence,
David Chytraeus.

Apparatus

- Sender. David Chytraeus (1530–1600), the great Rostock theologian and historian — Tycho's friend, and author of the annual history / Saxonia into which Tycho wished his migration-narrative inserted (cf. brahe-t_16010415002, brahe-t_16000920002, brahe-t_16000916002). This is one of Chytraeus's last letters to Tycho (he died in 1600). The MSS are copies at Prague and in Langebek's Diplomatarium.
- Significance — a humanist congratulation on the imperial call (autumn 1598). Written as Tycho is "girded for the journey" to Bohemia. It is a showpiece of learned encomium, with two load-bearing points:
1. The testimonial quoted. Chytraeus twice calls Tycho "the father of the Astronomers of this age and the sole restorer of this most praised science," explicitly "as that Highest and most renowned Prince testifies of you to the Imperial Majesty" — i.e. quoting Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg's commendation of Tycho to Rudolf II (the letter translated at brahe-t_15980816001). A direct cross-reference confirming how Ulrich's praise circulated.
2. The Sosigenes/Julius Caesar parallel (the centrepiece). Just as Julius Caesar, reforming the confused Roman calendar, brought Sosigenes the Alexandrian mathematician (slighted in Egypt) to Rome, so Rudolf II now invites Tycho — "disdained in his fatherland by certain Centaurs and Midases" — to cultivate astronomy under imperial patronage. The flattering analogy (Tycho: Rudolf:: Sosigenes: Caesar) places Tycho's transplantation in the grandest historical frame.
- The "anti-Midas" letter and its circulation. Chytraeus reports sharing a polemical letter of Tycho's "to a certain Midas, rather hostile to these studies" (a Danish/learned detractor — "Midas" with ass's ears, the type of the philistine) with a roster of the learned: Jacob Monau; Paul Schede Melissus ("Prince of the Poets"); Johann Posthius (poet-physician, "master of both Apollonian arts"); and Abraham Ortelius the geographer — "snatched from the earth this past June" (Ortelius d. 28 June 1598), a precise dating. He also praises Henrik Rantzau for hosting "an Atlas sustaining the starry axis" (Tycho at Wandsbek).
- Greek and verse.
- Ὅσσον ἔαρ χειμῶνος, ὅσον μᾶλον Βραβύλοιο ἅδιον — Theocritus, Idylls V.92–93 ("as spring is sweeter than winter, the apple than the sloe"); kept verbatim, translated.
- τούς τε ἀγαμοὺς … χάριν — a Greek maxim, "the good know how to be grateful even for small things" (the ἀγαμούς/ἀγαθούς reading uncertain; rendered by sense, flagged).
- τύχη — "Tyche/Fortune," a pun on Tycho's name ("whither the Emperor and your Tyche call you").
- The hexameter couplet "Regales animos primum dignata movere…" (Urania moving royal minds) is kept as verse.
- The Duke-Ulrich epigram. Chytraeus singles out the epigram Tycho prefixed to the "restitution of the Stars," inscribed to Duke Ulrich (cf. brahe-t_15980914002, the star-catalogue presented to Ulrich) — and Brasch's congratulatory poems to Rudolf and Tycho — wishing them all printed together.
- Persons named. David Chytraeus (sender); Duke Ulrich (the "Prince" who testified); Jacob Monau, Paul Melissus, Johann Posthius, Abraham Ortelius (d. June 1598); Henrik Rantzau; Rudolf II; Sosigenes and Julius Caesar; Tadeáš Hájek (Hagecius); Martin Brasch.
- Calendar / place. Rhodopoli Nonis Octobris Anno 1598 — Rhodopolis ("City of Roses") = the learned name for Rostock; Nones of October = 7 October. Lutheran Rostock kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar. Header agrees with the label.
