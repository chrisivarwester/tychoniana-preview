The original

FERDINANDUS MAGNUS ETRURIÆ DUX AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

VIR Illris. Gratissimæ fuerunt mihi literæ Dominationis Tuæ, tum quod præcipuum erga me præseferant amorem atque benevolentiam, tum etiam quod per eas occasionem oblatam video mutuæ voluntatis meæ aliquo erga eius Dm officij genere declarandæ. Nam licet ipsa mihi de facie, ut eius quoque testantur literæ, ignota fuerit, ea tamen, quæ de ipsius nomine ac virtute circunferuntur, Dm Tm non minus mihi claram quam notam reddere potuerunt. Sed quando ipsa fama ad id non sufficeret, hanc tamen ex ijs duobus voluminibus quæ ad me misit, et in quibus vera hominis species innotescit, abunde consequi potuissem. Cuius quidem muneris pro eo ac debeo Di Tæ gratias ago. Illud enim propter subiectæ rei et novi operis rationem charissimum mihi fuit, et si quid otij dabitur aliquando, utar etiam ad illud opus visendum, alicuius in ea re versato legentis opera. Et si illud commode fieri poterit, ut reliqua Dis T. opera, qualiacunque hactenus in lucem prodierunt aut in posterum prodibunt, ad me transmittantur, pro certo habeat in loco me non exigui muneris habiturum. Quod vero ad illum adolescentem attinet, quem mihi maxime commendavit, dabo operam, quemadmodum etiam hactenus factum est, ut et ipse intelligat, quanti ponderis apud me fuerit Dis Tæ commendatio, et ipsa quoque cognoscat posse se ac debere non in hac solummodo re, sed in quacunque alia, quæ videri possit ad eius Dis existimationem et commodum pertinere, omnia a me semper officia polliceri. Quod reliquum est, Deum opt. max. præcor ut Dm Tm diutius servet incolumem, quo de præclarissimis artibus ac literarum studijs benemereri longius possit.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Ferdinand, Grand Duke of Tuscany, to Tycho Brahe.

Illustrious Man. Your Lordship's letters were most pleasing to me — both because they bear before [me] a special love and benevolence toward me, and also because through them I see an occasion offered for declaring my mutual goodwill toward your Lordship by some kind of office. For although you have been unknown to me by face — as your letters too attest — yet those things which are circulated about your name and virtue have been able to render your Lordship to me no less illustrious than known. But even if fame itself did not suffice for that, I could nonetheless have abundantly attained it from those two volumes which you sent me, and in which the true likeness of the man becomes known. For which gift, indeed, I give your Lordship thanks, as I ought. For it was most dear to me, on account of the nature of the subject-matter and of the new work; and if any leisure shall ever be given, I will also use, for viewing that work, the help of some reader versed in that matter. And if it can conveniently be done that the rest of your Lordship's works — whatever have hitherto come into the light, or shall come forth hereafter — be transmitted to me, let him hold for certain that I shall reckon it in the place of no small gift. But as concerns that young man whom he most strongly commended to me, I will give effort — as also has been done hitherto — both that he himself may understand how much weight your Lordship's recommendation had with me, and that your Lordship too may know that you can and ought to promise yourself all good offices from me always — not in this matter only, but in any other which may seem to pertain to your Lordship's estimation and advantage. For what remains, I pray God Best and Greatest to preserve your Lordship long unharmed, that you may be able the longer to deserve well of the most excellent arts and the studies of letters.

Apparatus

  • The Grand Duke's reply. This is Ferdinando I de' Medici's gracious answer to Tycho's recommendation-and-book-gift of 1598 (brahe-t_15981108001). He thanks Tycho for the two volumes sent ("in which the true likeness of the man becomes known"), asks that the rest of Tycho's works, past and future, be sent too, and confirms his patronage of the young man Tycho had recommended. The witness survives in the Medici archive at Florence (Archivio Mediceo, vol. 65).
  • duo volumina = the two of Tycho's printed works given in 1598 (see brahe-t_15981108001 apparatus); ille adolescens, quem mihi commendavit = the recommended youth (probably Tengnagel — identification flagged there).
  • Chancery third person. The Grand Duke writes of Tycho in the third person throughout (Dominatio Tua / eius Dominatio — "your Lordship" / "his Lordship"), the formula of princely letters; rendered accordingly.
  • ⚠ Date. The source breaks off at Datum ……. — the dateline is lost in the witness. The edition assigns the year 1599 (identifier …0000…, label "1599, empty"); the day and month are not recoverable and are not invented. Florence (Tuscany, Catholic) used New Style, but no date survives to mark.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: located at Opera Omnia VIII, p. 156 (witness E codice Florentino Archivii publici Medicei vol. 65); Dreyer's index pointer "VIII 154" is an index error. The lost dateline (Datum......) is reproduced by Dreyer — genuinely lost in the edition of record, not a transcription gap. Open reading: Dreyer prints praescieras (2nd-sg.) where the working transcription has praeseferant (3rd-pl.); the translation follows praeseferant — flagged for review.
