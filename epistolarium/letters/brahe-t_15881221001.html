The original

TYCHO BRAHE
S. D.

EX literis tuis mihi non ita dudum perscriptis intellexi, nobilissime et eruditissime Domine HENRICE RANZOVI, te Sciotericum illud horizontale, quod tibi confieri feci, unâ cum fasciculis literarum adiunctis atque organo illo, quo nostrae hypotheses revolutionum coelestium commodius explicantur, accepisse, eaque omnia tibi placuisse, plurimum tibi congratulor. Quantum vero ad lineae meridianae, quam desideras, ordinationem attinet, eam peculiari quadam ratione e stellis circumpolaribus exacte derivare soleo, ut azimutha instrumentorum meorum in ipso scrupulo recte constent (quod alias difficulter fit). Verum in Scioterico isto tanta praecisio non est necessaria, sufficit itaque ad unum vel alterum recte elaboratum compassum lineam horae duodecimae meridiano coelesti correspondentem statuere atque ita leviter firmare laminam. Deinde instante vere, ubi Sol altior fuerit, videndum diligenter, ut extremitas umbrae in certa aliqua hora antemeridiana eiusdem sit a centro longitudinis cum aliqua alia aequaliter post meridiem remota, quod circa ipsum aequinoctium commodius praestatur; tunc enim extremitas umbrae percurrit transversam rectam, quae Aequatorem five initia ♈ et ♎ repraesentat, estque ea linea meridiei orthogonalis. Quare si hora 9a 8a 7a matutina et 3a 4a 5a vespertina extremitas umbrae eodem modo illi lineae adhaeret, recte dispositum est Sciotericum; sin minus, ita hinc inde componendum, donec omnia rite quadrent; interea vero ut in aequilibrio Horizontis continue permaneat, diligenter est cavendum. Potes etiam uti, si lubet, vulgari illa ratione inquirendi lineam meridiei per erectum Gnomonem a plano quodam horizontali, cui circumductis aliquot circulis videre, in quibus locis ante et post meridiem eos umbrae extremitas transeat, perque harum intersectionum medietatem ad centrum lineam ducere, quae meridiem per umbram Gnomonis monstrabit, unde etiam Sciotericum eodem instanti ita rectificari potest, ut etiam unâ horam 12am Gnomon eius indicet; sed haec sunt vulgaria.

Accepi interim et alias semel atque iterum a te literas unâque pallam papyri impressoriae meae officinae admodum idoneam et quodam etiam tempore clarissimi et doctissimi viri GEORGIJ BRAUN, Decani Coloniensis, quartam partem Theatri urbium, et praeterea delineationem exequiarum ac sepulturae regiae Hamburgi per te aeneis figuris procuratam; pro quibus omnibus plurimas et ingentes habeo gratias, quod si referendi occasio oblata unquam fuerit, dabo operam, ut intelligas me omni gratitudine et officio tibi futurum addictissimum. Papyrus autem impressoria ultimo missa est quidem satis apta meo operi, sed aliquanto maiuscula est, ut videre licet ex utroque adiuncto quaternione, nec tamen refert paululum maiorem esse, ac si minor foret.

De opere Theatrico civitatum BRAUNIJ, scias illud admodum mihi placere, et praeclare te de Patria nostra meritum iudico, quod urbium nostratium delineationem illi procurâris. Video etiam Huenulam nostram non neglectam, quam tua opera procul dubio obtinuit, quod mihi pergratum est. Reliquos tres tomos istius operis nondum vidi, neque enim quod sciam Bibliopolae nostri eos hactenus attulerunt. Si constaret mihi, ubi venales forent, et eos coëmerem, quam primum vero per otium licuerit, data commoditate ipsi BRAUNIO scribam.

Sculptorem sive incisorem talem, qui in aere vel etiam buxo figuras diligenter ad impressionem adornare norit, admodum expeterem. Constitui enim primo quoque tempore omnia instrumenta mea Astronomica competenter delineata unâ cum declaratione fabricae et usus, additis quibusdam etiam alijs huc pertinentibus, in primo libro operis Astronomiae instauratione comprehendenda publicare. Si itaque aliquem eiuscemodi artificem, qui perfecte haec ipsa effingere et postea vel cupro aut buxo incidere norit, tuo consilio et auxilio obtinuero, nihil mihi acceptius evenire poterit. Mercedem ei libenter solvam, quantam alibi promereri poterit, et per totam aestatem, quod agat, illi procuraturus sum.

Fecisti in aliquibus etiam nuper ad me datis literis mentionem cuiusdam Dithmarsi nomine et re Ursini unâque misisti extractum literarum clarissimi et doctissimi D. GEORGIJ ROLLENHAGIJ simul cum libro istius impudentis Plagiarij quem tamen antea aliunde habueram. Ut vero tam tu quam ROLLENHAGIUS recte suspicamini istum hypothesium innovationem, quam sibi inverecunde attribuit, a me suffuratum esse, id ita revera se habet: Nam licet is nunquam mihi inservierit vel domesticus fuerit, ut vos forte existimatis, tamen cum ante quadriennium iam elapsum cum nobilissimo et doctissimo viro ERICO LANGIO de Engelstholm, tibi bene cognito, cui tunc inserviebat, 14 circiter dies hîc fuisset, interea dum ego cum ERICO amico meo singulari et quibusdam alijs unâ hic praesentibus nobilibus hilarius conversor, iste suffurator mihi plurima ex ijs, quae magno labore et diligentia conquisita habui, clam descripsit et instrumentorum omnium, quae tunc in promptu erant, formam ac dispositionem delineavit plurimaque alia, quae conquirere potuit, subtraxit, similans tamen se nihil horum agere, imo haec non curare quidem sed vili pendere. Postquam tamen hanc eius vafriciem suboluissem, alium quendam subornavi, qui magnam eorum partem, quae abstulerat, reciperet, quae adhuc praesto habeo. Video nunc illum plura quam recuperâram designasse vel etiam memoria retinuisse. Nam innovatio haec hypothesium nemini unquam ante me, quod sciam, in mentem venit, occasionem vero huius inventi ex ijs, quae nuper ad D. PEUCERUM scripsi, satis intelliges. Quod autem hanc hypothesin tunc mihi surripuerit, hinc satis evidenter colligo: Cum enim praedicto ERICO amico meo, sciscitanti a me rationem diversitatis apparentiarum et retrogradationum Planetarum, eam tam secundum veterem Ptolemaicam quam Copernianam ratiocinationem illi in mensa depictam exposuissem, subiunxi adhuc alia quadam assumptione idem effici posse, et absurda quaedam in his prioribus aperui, ille vero (ut est ingenio admodum docili et sciendi cupido) hanc formam a me sibi etiam ostendi petijt, quod cum astante et aspiciente isto Dithmarso servo suo me invite facturum animadverteret (indicâram enim illi antea me e moribus et physiognomia hominis perspexisse quinam esset) ille hoc foras amandato, ut aliud quid ageret, id quod voluit a me obtinuit, nostrasque etiam hypotheses generaliter et breviter indicatas ipsi delineavi, quas tamen mox expunxi. At Dithmarsus iste, cum aliquid secreti tractari persentisceret, ut erat nasutulus, eo diligentius inquirere, an aliquid eiuscemodi reperire posset et furtim subtrahere, itaque inter chartas meas in fenestra repositas abiectam quandam delineationem huius dispositionis motuum coelestium adinvenit, quae tamen, incuria nescio qua, ita erat depicta, ut orbis Martius Solarem totaliter ambiret, cumque id non recte se haberet, tanquam inepta et dissonans cassata atque neglecta est, is tamen hanc ipsam pro vera et congrua arripiens, aut tacite postea delineavit, vel memoria conservavit, quam pro sua inventione impudenti mendacio nunc venditat, seque eam ante triennium in angulo quodam Regni Poloniae (ubi procul dubio nunquam fuerat) excogitasse finxit, imo et illustrissimo Principi Landtgravio eam tanquam a se excogitatam dedicare non erubescit; tanta est hominis vel potius ursi istius inverecunda arrogantia. Verum dum falsam, quam hîc reperit, designationem eodem modo imitatur et eandem sequitur magnitudinem (erat enim et ea erronea delineatio, quam hîc vidit, in integro folio patenti depicta) satis ipsemet furtum suum propalat, et haec ipsa, quae iactitat, revera se non intelligere, multo minus, ut eorum autor et inventor sit, non obscuris indicijs prodit. Sic plurima alia, imo praecipua et fere omnia, quae in isto libro pro suis venditat, etsi non totaliter a me profecta sint, tamen ab alijs Mathematicis sive scientibus illis sive clam habuit, adeo ut nihil fere aliud agat in toto isto libello, quam ut alienis se ornet plumis, nimis perfricta fronte aliorum inventa et labores sibi attribuendo, eaque quae nunquam didicit vel praestare potest, promittendo. Sed indignus est iste inveterator, de quo plura scribam, et forte haec ipsa nimia sunt; non potui tamen intermittere, quin tibi significarem, quî factum sit, ut iste hypotheses nostras acceperit easque prorsus publicârit, ut non solum tu, sed etiam ROLLENHAGIUS certo sciretis, merum furtum esse eius in hac hypothesi ostentationem; et recte quidem asseveras URSUM istum, licet meos labores surripuerit, tamen eum fundamentaliter omnia non intellexisse, ut patet vel ex falsa illa quam proponit delineatione, dum proportionem orbis Martij ad Solarem turpiter ignorat. Et ROLLENHAGIUS candide atque prudenter indicat ea, quae a fugitivo aliquo ministro a me abalienata et foras eliminata sunt, mihi indicanda, ut vero inventori postliminio restituantur. Intelligo enim ipsum statim ex URSI istius scripto perspexisse hanc, quam pro sua venditat, inventionem a me prodijsse, siquidem antea ad te perscripserat, sibi ante biennium a fugitivo quodam meo ministro hanc hypothesin communicatam esse. Neque tamen satis inde colligere possum, an hunc ipsum URSUM notet, putans forte illum aliquando mihi inservijsse, an vero alium quendam, qui hinc clam e mea domo decesserit, existimet. Peto itaque peramanter, ut, quam primum detur occasio ad illum scribendi, velis expiscari, quisnam et quo nomine fuerit fugitivus ille, de quo scribit, minister meus, cuius opera huius hypotheseos formam adeptus est. Averem enim scire, an hîc ipsus fuerit URSUS vel alius quispiam; ego etenim neminem scio ex omnibus studiosis, qui mihi hactenus in Astronomicis rebus inservierunt, me inscio vel invito clam hinc discessisse. Nec satis cogitare possum, quisnam ille fuerit fugitivus, cuius semel atque iterum ROLLENHAGIUS mentionem facit. Eum itaque si is mihi indicârit, rem pergratam fecerit. Video hunc ROLLENHAGIUM non saltem excellenter eruditum et iudicio gravi praepollentem, sed etiam virum integrum et syncerum benevoleque de aliorum studijs sentientem; quare data aliqua occasione cum illo per literas amicitiam contrahere non intermittam, quod si is praevenerit, eo ipso satis oportunitatis erit exhibitum. Sed haec prolixius quam volebam; tuum erit hanc immorationem, quam iusta quaerela extorsit, candide interpretari. Si literas a Doctore PEUCERO mihi inscriptas receperis, rogo, ut eas inprimis huc mittere non gravari velis. Vale, vir nobilissime et praeclarissime, deque artibus eruditis bene mereri, uti facis, tibique hac ratione laudem nunquam intermorituram comparare ne intermitte.

Iterum quam felicissime et diutissime vive valeque.
Datae Uraniburgi, die 21 Decembris, Anno 1588.

TYCHO BRAHE
sends greeting.

From your letter written to me not so long ago, most noble and most learned Master Henrik Rantzau, I learned that you have received that horizontal sundial which I had made for you, together with the bundles of letters enclosed with it and that instrument by which our hypotheses of the celestial revolutions are more conveniently set out; and that all these have pleased you — on which I most heartily congratulate you. As regards the setting-out of the meridian line that you wish for: I am accustomed to derive it exactly, by a certain peculiar method, from the circumpolar stars, so that the azimuths of my instruments may stand correct to the very [arc-]scruple (which is otherwise done with difficulty). But in that sundial such precision is not necessary; it is therefore enough, with one or another well-worked compass, to set the line of the twelfth hour answering to the celestial meridian, and so to fix the plate lightly. Then, as spring comes on, when the Sun shall be higher, one must look carefully that the tip of the shadow at some certain forenoon hour be at the same distance from the centre as another [hour] equally far past noon — which is more conveniently done about the equinox itself; for then the tip of the shadow runs along a transverse straight line, which represents the Equator, that is [sive], the beginnings of ♈ Aries and ♎ Libra, and that line is orthogonal to the meridian. Therefore, if at the 9th, 8th, [and] 7th morning hour, and the 3rd, 4th, [and] 5th evening hour, the tip of the shadow clings in the same way to that line, the sundial is rightly disposed; if not, it must be adjusted this way and that until everything squares duly; and meanwhile it must be carefully seen to that it remain continually in the level of the Horizon. You may also use, if you please, that common method of finding the meridian line by an erected gnomon on some horizontal plane, around which some circles are drawn, to see at what places before and after noon the tip of the shadow crosses them, and through the midpoint of these intersections to draw a line to the centre, which will show the meridian by the gnomon's shadow; from which the sundial too can at the same instant be so rectified that its gnomon may likewise indicate the 12th hour — but these are common matters.

Meanwhile I have received from you yet other letters, once and again, and together a ball of printing-paper very well suited to my workshop, and at one time also, from that most distinguished and most learned man Georg Braun, Dean of Cologne, the fourth part of the Theatre of Cities, and besides a delineation of the royal obsequies and burial at Hamburg, procured by you in copper figures; for all of which I give the most and the greatest thanks, [and] should the occasion of repaying ever offer itself, I shall take pains that you may understand me to be in the future most devoted to you in all gratitude and service. The printing-paper last sent is indeed fairly suited to my work, but it is somewhat too large, as may be seen from each of the two enclosed quaternions; yet it does not matter that it be a little larger, [more] than if it were smaller.

As to Braun's Theatrical work of cities, know that it pleases me greatly, and I judge that you have deserved excellently of our fatherland in that you procured for him the delineation of our [home] cities. I see too that our little Hven [Huenula] has not been neglected, which it doubtless obtained through your effort — which is most welcome to me. The remaining three volumes of that work I have not yet seen, for so far as I know our booksellers have not hitherto brought them in. If it were clear to me where they are for sale, I would buy them up; and as soon as leisure shall allow, given the opportunity, I shall write to Braun himself.

Such a carver or engraver as knows how to dress figures carefully for printing, in copper or even in boxwood, I should very much desire. For I have resolved, at the very first opportunity, to publish all my astronomical instruments suitably delineated, together with a declaration of their construction and use, with certain other matters pertaining hereto added, in the first book of the work to be comprised under [the title of] the Restoration of Astronomy. If therefore by your counsel and help I shall obtain some craftsman of that kind, who knows how to portray these very things perfectly and afterwards to engrave them in copper or boxwood, nothing more welcome could befall me. I will gladly pay him a wage, as much as he could earn elsewhere, and through the whole summer I will provide him with what he is to do.

You made mention, too, in some letters lately sent to me, of a certain man, Dithmarsian by name and Ursine in fact, and together you sent an extract of the letter of that most distinguished and most learned Master Georg Rollenhagen, along with the book of that shameless plagiarist — which, however, I had previously had from another quarter. As to your supposing rightly, both you and Rollenhagen, that this innovation of hypotheses, which he shamelessly attributes to himself, was filched from me — that is in truth so. For although he never served me nor was a member of my household, as you perhaps suppose, yet, when four years ago — now already past — he had been here about fourteen days with that most noble and most learned man Erik Lange of Engelstholm, well known to you, whom he then served, in the meantime, while I was conversing rather merrily with Erik my singular friend and certain other nobles present here together, that filcher secretly copied out for himself very many of those things which I had gathered with great labour and diligence, and delineated the form and disposition of all the instruments that were then to hand, and abstracted very many other things that he was able to gather — yet feigning that he was doing none of these things, nay, that he did not even care about them but held them cheap. Yet after I had got wind of this cunning of his, I suborned a certain other [man] to recover a great part of those things which he had carried off, which I still have ready at hand. I now see that he had drawn out, or even kept in memory, more than I had recovered. For this innovation of hypotheses never came into the mind of anyone before me, so far as I know; and the occasion of this discovery you will sufficiently understand from those things which I lately wrote to Master Peucer. But that he then stole this hypothesis from me, I gather from this evidence clearly enough: For when, to the aforesaid Erik my friend — who was inquiring of me the reason for the diversity of the appearances and retrogradations of the planets — I had set it forth to him, depicted on the table both according to the old Ptolemaic and the Copernican reasoning, I added that the same could moreover be effected by a certain other assumption, and disclosed certain absurdities in those earlier [systems]; and he (being of a very teachable disposition and eager to know) asked me to show this form to him too. And when he perceived that I would do this reluctantly while that Dithmarsian, his servant, stood by and looked on (for I had earlier indicated to him that I had seen through, from the man's manners and physiognomy, what sort he was), he, having dispatched the man outside to do something else, obtained from me what he wished; and I delineated for him our hypotheses too, indicated generally and briefly, which however I soon erased. But that Dithmarsian, when he sensed that something secret was being treated of — being a nosy little fellow [nasutulus] as he was — searched the more diligently whether he could find anything of the sort and stealthily abstract it; and so, among my papers laid by in the window, he came upon a certain cast-off delineation of this disposition of the celestial motions — which, however, by some carelessness, was so drawn that the orb of Mars wholly encircled that of the Sun, and since that was not correct, it was annulled and discarded as inept and discordant. He, however, seizing this very one as true and fitting, either silently delineated it afterwards or kept it in memory; and this he now hawks, with shameless lying, as his own invention, and has feigned that he devised it three years ago in some corner of the Kingdom of Poland (where doubtless he had never been) — nay, he does not even blush to dedicate it to the Most Illustrious Prince the Landgrave as though devised by himself: so great is the shameless arrogance of the man, or rather of that bear [ursus]. But while he imitates in the same way the false design which he found here, and follows the same magnitude (for that erroneous delineation too, which he saw here, was drawn on a whole open folio sheet), he sufficiently betrays his own theft, and by no obscure tokens discloses that these very things which he vaunts he does not in truth understand, much less that he is the author and discoverer of them. Thus very many other things — nay, the chief and almost all that he hawks as his own in that book — though they were not wholly derived [profecta] from me, he yet had from other mathematicians, whether with those men's knowledge or secretly; so that he does almost nothing else in that whole little book than deck himself in others' feathers, attributing to himself, with too brazen a brow, others' inventions and labours, and promising those things which he never learned nor can perform. But that inveterate [rascal] is unworthy [of more], of whom I will write more [another time], and perhaps even these things are too much; yet I could not forbear to make known to you how it came about that he got hold of our hypotheses and published them outright — so that not only you, but Rollenhagen too, might know for certain that his parading of this hypothesis is sheer theft. And you rightly assert that this Ursus, although he stole my labours, nevertheless did not fundamentally understand the whole, as is plain even from that false delineation which he proposes, while he basely is ignorant of the proportion of the orb of Mars to that of the Sun. And Rollenhagen candidly and prudently indicates that those things which were alienated from me by some fugitive servant and carried off abroad ought to be made known to me, that they may by right of return [postliminio] be restored to their true discoverer. For I understand that he at once perceived, from that Ursus's writing, that this invention which he hawks as his own had issued from me — seeing that he had previously written to you that this hypothesis had been communicated to him two years ago by some fugitive servant of mine. Yet I cannot sufficiently gather from this whether he means this very Ursus, thinking perhaps that he once served me, or whether he supposes some other [man] who departed secretly from my house here. I therefore most affectionately ask that, as soon as occasion is given of writing to him, you would be willing to fish out who and by what name was that fugitive — of whom he writes — servant of mine, by whose agency he got the form of this hypothesis. For I should be keen to know whether it was this very Ursus or some other; for I know of no one among all the students who have hitherto served me in astronomical matters as having departed secretly from here without my knowledge or against my will. Nor can I sufficiently think who that fugitive was of whom Rollenhagen makes mention once and again. If, then, he should indicate him to me, he will have done a most welcome thing. I see this Rollenhagen to be not only excellently learned and excelling in weighty judgement, but also a man upright and sincere, and well-disposed toward others' studies; wherefore, should any occasion be given, I will not fail to contract a friendship with him by letter — and if he forestalls [me], by that very fact opportunity enough will have been afforded. But these things [run] more at length than I wished; it will be for you to interpret candidly this delay, which a just complaint has wrung from me. If you receive letters addressed to me from Doctor Peucer, I beg that you would not be loth to send them here first of all. Farewell, most noble and most illustrious man, and do not cease to deserve well of the learned arts, as you do, and by this means to procure for yourself praise that will never die away.

Again, live and fare you well most happily and most long.
Given at Uraniborg, on the 21st day of December, in the year 1588.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven.
  • Recipient. Henricus Ranzovius = Henrik Rantzau (Heinrich Rantzau, 1526–1598), Royal Statthalter of the Danish king in the Duchies of Schleswig-Holstein, humanist polymath, patron, and Tycho's chief German correspondent and intermediary — here acting both as instrument-recipient and as conduit of the Ursus affair to the wider learned world.
  • Persons named. Georgius Braun = Georg Braun (Georgius Bruin, 1541–1622), Cologne canon and editor of the Civitates orbis terrarum ("Theatre of Cities"); the "Dean of Cologne" (Decanus Coloniensis) is Rantzau/Braun's title as given. Ericus Langius de Engelstholm = Erik Lange of Engelstholm (c. 1553–1613), Danish nobleman and alchemist, Tycho's friend (later betrothed to Tycho's sister Sophie); Ursus was in his service. Georgius Rollenhagius = Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), Magdeburg rector and writer, the candid informant. D. Peucerus = Caspar Peucer (1525–1602), Wittenberg physician-astronomer. Illustrissimus Princeps Landtgravius = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel; Ursus dedicated his Fundamentum astronomicum (1588) to him.
  • The plagiarist. Dithmarsus … Ursinus … Ursus = Nicolaus Reimers Baer / Ursus (Nicolaus Raimarus Ursus Dithmarsus, c. 1551–1600), from Dithmarschen, who in his Fundamentum astronomicum (Strasbourg, 1588) published a geo-heliocentric world-system closely resembling Tycho's. The whole core of the letter is Tycho's formal accusation of theft — the opening shot of the long Tycho–Ursus priority war. Tycho puns relentlessly on his name: Dithmarsi nomine et re Ursini "Dithmarsian by name and Ursine [bear-like] in fact"; hominis vel potius ursi istius "of the man, or rather of that bear." The bear-pun is rendered with the gloss kept visible by the audit's rule (no flattening of the wordplay).
  • Instruments / sundial passage. Sciotericum (horizontale) = a (horizontal) sundial / sciotericon (Greek-derived term, skiotherikon, shadow-marker); kept. organo illo, quo nostrae hypotheses … explicantur = "that instrument by which our hypotheses … are set out" — a planetary/world-system demonstration device (an organum, this chapter's convention). linea meridiana / linea meridiei = meridian line; Gnomon = gnomon; azimutha … in ipso scrupulo = "the azimuths … to the very [arc-]scruple" (a scrupulus = one minute of arc; rendered "[arc-]scruple"). compassum here = a pair of compasses / drawing-compass (cf. the analogous compass crux in brahe-t_15841211001); rendered "compass" with the technical sense noted. in aequilibrio Horizontis = "in the level of the Horizon" (i.e. kept truly horizontal).
  • Astronomy — concepts. hypotheses revolutionum coelestium = "hypotheses of the celestial revolutions" (here hypotheses = world-system models, the Tychonic geo-heliocentric arrangement — not "guesses"); retrogradationes Planetarum = retrogradations of the planets; diversitas apparentiarum = "the diversity of the appearances"; secundum veterem Ptolemaicam quam Copernianam ratiocinationem = "according to the old Ptolemaic and the Copernican reasoning." Astronomiae instauratione — Tycho's programme term, the Restoration of Astronomy (astronomia instaurata, this chapter's convention); the planned Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica / Progymnasmata corpus.
  • Symbols / Greek. ♈ and ♎ named Aries and Libra in the translation, glyphs kept in the Source block; they mark the equinoctial points (the Equator's intersections). The technical adverb five in the Source is the period print-form of sive ("or, that is"), retained verbatim in the Source block and rendered "that is" in the translation (noted, not silently corrected).
  • Legal / rhetorical terms. postliminio = "by right of postliminium / right of return" — the Roman-law term for the restoration of rights/property to one who returns from captivity; here metaphorical: Tycho's stolen hypothesis should "return" to its true author. inveterator = a hardened/inveterate wrongdoer ("that inveterate [rascal]"). alienis se ornet plumis = "deck himself in others' feathers" (the Aesopic jackdaw-in-borrowed-plumes proverb); kept as the metaphor it is.
  • Crux — prfsecta. In the clause etsi non totaliter a me prfsecta sint, the Source's prfsecta is a typographic corruption (the f standing for the ae/œ ligature). The intended word is almost certainly profecta ("derived/issued from me"), giving "though not wholly derived from me." A reading praesecta/perfecta is far less likely on sense. Translated as "derived" with the printed form bracketed and flagged; this is the one genuinely uncertain word and is the basis for the Probable/Possible borderline below.
  • Other phrasing. quartam partem Theatri urbium … reliquos tres tomos — the fourth volume of Braun & Hogenberg's Civitates orbis terrarum (vol. IV, 1588) had reached Tycho; he still lacked the earlier three. Huenula nostra = "our little Hven" (affectionate diminutive of Hven), referring to the engraved view of Uraniborg/Hven included in the atlas. pallam papyri impressoriae = "a ball of printing-paper" (palla/balla = a bale or ball/ream-bundle of paper for the Uraniborg press). quaternio = a quaternion (a gathering of four sheets), here samples enclosed to show the paper's size.
  • Internal date references (not in-letter discrepancies, but flagged for the record). ante quadriennium iam elapsum "four years ago now past" places Ursus's visit with Erik Lange c. 1584; ante triennium "three years ago" (Ursus's claimed independent invention) and ante biennium "two years ago" (Rollenhagen's report) are Tycho's reports of others' datings, preserved as written, not reconciled.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgi, die 21 Decembris, Anno 1588 = "Given at Uraniborg, on the 21st day of December, in the year 1588" (Old Style; Denmark was on the Julian calendar). The dateline date (21 December 1588) agrees with the edition label; no discrepancy.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: the corrupt prfsecta is printed by Dreyer (Opera Omnia VII, p. 388) as prosecta — confirming the word and sense ("derived/cut from me"); the earlier conjecture profecta is superseded, the translation "derived" stands.
  • Correction. Latin 'prfsecta' (a corrupted non-word) corrected to Dreyer's 'profecta' (TBOO VII p.388, line 28); the English's bracketed Latin gloss updated to match. The translation 'derived' was already correct and required no change.
