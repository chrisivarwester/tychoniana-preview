The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

PRÆVIA precatione fœlicis et auspicatissimi novi anni, Vir Nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile Princeps, mitto Alphonsinas Tabulas recognitas, Parisijs Ano 53 impressas, No. 43 signatas. Et simul unâ METHODIJ uti fertur Revelationem, quam describi curavi, cum liber, cui inserta fuerat, ob molem transferri nequeat. Neque iam occurrit aliud, quod ad te modo scribendum esse ducam. Summa enim illa tua ἀκρίβεια, quâ vel ad sexagesimam gradus partem novissimus tuus calculus Solis et Lunæ, cum exactissimâ tuâ cœlestium apparentium contemplatione, coincidit, eo me redegit angustiarum, ut quo me vertam, non habeam, et ob id ipsum Matheseos operationes tantisper omnino seponendas esse decreverim, donec calculum istum tuum novissimum abs te absolutum, quem liberali tuâ spontaneâ et benevolâ pollicitatione iam quodammodo meum fecisse videris, mihi impertiri dignatus fueris. Ea siquidem, quæ Arithmeticam concernunt, satis hac ætate elaborata esse videntur. Quæ vero Astronomica sunt, vel saltem cum ijs coniuncta, manifestis difficultatibus sunt implicita. Ut sive PTOLOMÆUM quis sequatur, sive ALPHONSUM, sive COPERNICUM, in multis offendat necesse est. Unice itaque restaurationem hanc tuam exopto, cui, tametsi forte nondum extremam manum adhibueris, tamen ea sine omni dubio aliorum calculos longe superabit. Atque hisce tibi fœlicissima quæque præsagio et ex animo precor.

Datæ Monachij 10 Decemb. A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

With a prefatory prayer for a happy and most auspicious New Year, most noble man and easily Prince of all mathematicians, I send the Alphonsine Tables, revised, printed at Paris in the year [15]53, marked No. 43. And, at the same time, the Revelation (as it is called) of Methodius, which I had transcribed, since the book in which it had been inserted cannot be transferred [sent] on account of its bulk. Nor does anything else now occur which I judge ought, just now, to be written to you. For that supreme exactness [ἀκρίβεια] of yours, by which your newest calculation of the Sun and Moon coincides — even to the sixtieth part of a degree [i.e. one arc-minute] — with your most exact contemplation of the celestial appearances, has reduced me to such straits that I have nowhere to turn; and for that very reason I have resolved that the operations of Mathematics must be altogether set aside for a while, until you have deigned to share with me that newest calculation of yours, completed by you — which, by your liberal, spontaneous, and benevolent promise, you seem already in some manner to have made mine. For those things which concern Arithmetic seem, in this age, sufficiently elaborated; but the things which are Astronomical, or at least conjoined with them, are entangled in manifest difficulties — so that, whether one follow Ptolemy, or Alfonso, or Copernicus, one must necessarily stumble in many [points]. Uniquely, therefore, do I long for this restoration of yours, which — although perhaps you have not yet applied the final hand to it — will nonetheless, beyond all doubt, far surpass the calculations of others. And with these I presage for you, and pray from my heart, every happiness.

Given at Munich, 10 December, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (the Munich correspondent of the 1600 series), here in fulsome praise of Tycho's computational accuracy and impatient for his renewed tables.
  • Books sent. Alphonsinae Tabulae recognitae, Parisijs Ano 53 impressae, No. 43 = the Alphonsine Tables in the revised Paris 1553 printing (item No. 43 on Tycho's desiderata list). Methodij … Revelatio = the apocalyptic Revelations of [Pseudo-]Methodius (the medieval Apocalypse of Pseudo-Methodius), transcribed for Tycho because the volume containing it was too bulky to send.
  • The praise of Tycho's accuracy. summa illa tua ἀκρίβεια — "that supreme exactness [akribeia] of yours"; the claim that Tycho's "newest calculation of the Sun and Moon" agrees with observation vel ad sexagesimam gradus partem — "even to the sixtieth part of a degree," i.e. to one arc-minute (the scrupulum primum, 1/60°; corrected from an earlier "arc-second" gloss — the same correspondent uses the same phrase for the arc-minute in his 8 Nov letter, 16001108001). Herwart says this has driven him to suspend his own mathematical work until Tycho shares the finished tables — which Tycho's restauratio (his renewal of astronomy) will, he is sure, set above Ptolemy, Alfonso, and Copernicus alike. extremam manum adhibere = "to apply the final hand," i.e. to complete/polish.
  • ἀκρίβεια (Greek) — "precision, exactness"; transcribed and rendered "exactness." Alphonsum = Alfonso X (the Alphonsine Tables' royal patron).
  • Calendar. Monachij 10 Decemb. 1600 = 10 December 1600; Munich (Catholic Bavaria) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
