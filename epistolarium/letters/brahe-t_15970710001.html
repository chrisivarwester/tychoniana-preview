The original

STUORMECTIGE hoyeborne allernaadigste herre. Nest min villige och troff thienistis vnderdanige formelding E: M: giffuer ieg paa det vnderdanigste thilkende, at som ieg ingen leylighedt haffde att komme E: K: M: thil orde, førend ieg affdrog, och ey viste, om dett kunde vere E: K: M: angenem eller icke, saa foraarsagis ieg nu E: K: M: paa dett korteste schrifftligen att lade vide, huis ieg ellers mundtligen haffde y sinde vnderdanigst att berette. Daa, effthersom ieg aff vngdommen haffuer haffdt sønderlig thilgeneyenhedt den löfflig astronomische konst grundelig att lære och forstaa och siden att bringe rett paa fode, och der offuer haffde achtit thilforn att bliffue vdi Tysklandt bequemmeligen dett att vdrette, saa haffuer E: K: M: herre fader, hoyeloufflige ihukommelse, der Hans Mayt: saadant fornam, naadigste begerit och bevegitt mig till ditt samme paa Huæn att anstille och giøre. Huilchett ieg och paa anden och tyffvende aar medt alsomstørste fliidt, vmage och bekaastning efftherkommit haffuer formeenendis mig nochsom dermedt haffue bevist, att ieg dett helst haffde villet giordt min egenn herre och koning och fæderne landt thil ære aff yderste formue. Och haffde E: K: M: herre fader naadigste for gott anseedt och loffuit, att huis ieg der anrettit thil samme konst schulle medt en fundatz nødtorffteligen forsørgis och perpetueris, med flere guode wilkaar, som mig bleff naadigste thilsagdt. Huilchet E: K: M: frue moder, min naadigste droning, vden tuiffvell endnu dragis thil minde, och thilforn hoes Dannemarchis raadt haffuer ladit thilkende giffue, saa ieg derfor haffuer bekommit det hele Dannemarchis riges raadz obne pergmentz breff, som mig saadant haffuer confirmerit och viidere derudom fortrøstitt. Huorudoffuer ieg siden haffuer giordt en vsigelige stuoer vmage och bekaastning ydermere end thilforn forhaabendis, att naar E: K: M: kom thil regimentztidt, schulde da haffue en naadigste behag dertill, och lade mig och mine nyde dett gott att.

Men nu haffuer dett sig anderledis tildragitt en ieg haffde troffuit, huorudom ieg vil nu vformeldit lade videre omstendt. E: K: M: er vden tuiffvel bevist, att ieg er affschildt med huis ieg schulde haffue thil samme konstis vnderholding, och mig bleffuen opschreffuit, att E: K: M: war icke til sindz saadant att ville holde ved macht, foruden møgitt andit, som vformodendis er thilkommit, och (som ieg achter) vden min schyld och brøde er hendit. Och efftherdi ieg endelig aff den naade, som Gud giffuer, faaer att fuldkomme huis ieg engang medt saa stuoer alvorlighedt och y lang tidt fortagitt och dreffuit haffuer, huilchet och mange nationer vitterligt er, och stuorligen adtraader, och icke sielff haffuer y formue dett nochsom att bekaaste; thi ieg alrede er kommen saa thil achters, der ieg endskønt haffde de forrige forlæninger, att ieg alligvel nøddis thil att affhende alt mitt fæderne guodtz. Forseer ieg mig till, att E: K: M: naadigste anseer saadan min nødtorfftighedt och icke tager denne min vdreyße thil nogen vnaade, efftherdi att ieg aff diße och flere aarsager saa hoyeligen trengdis att søge anden middel och raadt, att dett som well er begyndt kand bliffue thilbørligen fuldkommit, och ieg mitt guode naffn och rycte y fremmede land kand vidt macht holde. Dog er ieg icke y den mening vddragen, att ieg aldelis ville forlade mitt fæderne land, mens ickon att vere vde om hielp och bistandt hoes andre herrer och potentater, om saa mugligt kunde vere, paa dett ieg icke schulde were E: K: M: eller rigett formøgitt thil besuering. Dersom dett kunde bliffue mig forgyntt, att ieg y Dannemarck maa min forthagne arbeytt forfølge, vil ieg dett icke forsige; thi ieg endnu som thilforn alsomhelst E: K: M for nogen andre potentater, och mitt egen fæderne rige thil ære och loufflig effthersagn, alt huis ieg aff yderste formue kunde vdrette, saa fremt dett kunde skee med lidelig vilkaar och vden min skade. Huis och icke, vil ieg dog, huor dett er forseet, att ieg anderstedz skal bliffue, stedtze vere E: K: M: med all loufflig effthersagn och vnderdanighedt aff min ringe formue villig. Settendis dett vnderdanigst til E: K: M: guode bethenckende, att ieg ingenlunde aff nogen lettferdighedt (helst paa denne alder, ieg er offuer halfftridiesintyffue aar, och icke med ringe huesgesindt beladen, huilche ieg med stuoer vgeleyenhedt nødis att giøre fremmede) drager aff mitt fæderne land og fra min guode slecht och venner, och kandt dett som endnu er paa Huæn aff mig giordt, nochsom vdviße, att icke thilforn haffde werit min acht och mening saaledes att ville drage derfra. Forhaabis derfore vnderdanigste, naar E: K: M: dette medt sine omstendighedt rett betrackter, att E: K: M: werer och bliffuer min naadigste herre och konningh, och mig och mine medt al kongelig gunst och naade tilbevogenn. Jeg wil althid findis E: K: M: vnderdanig, throff och villig aff min yderste formue, huor Gud almegtigste mig min leylighedt haffuer forsiunit. Denn samme guode Gudt, som raader for all wertzlige regimente, vnde E: K: M: vdi sin kongelige regieringh lycke, velsignelße, guode raad och anßlagh.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Most Mighty, high-born, most gracious Lord. After the humble tender of my willing and faithful service, I make known to Your Majesty most humbly that, since I had no opportunity to speak with Your Royal Majesty before I departed, and did not know whether it might be agreeable to Your Royal Majesty or not, I am therefore now moved to let Your Royal Majesty know in writing, as briefly as may be, what otherwise I had it in mind humbly to report by word of mouth. Now then, inasmuch as from my youth I have had a singular inclination thoroughly to learn and understand the laudable art of astronomy and afterwards to set it rightly upon its feet, and had on that account formerly intended to settle in Germany so as conveniently to accomplish it, Your Royal Majesty's lord father, of high and laudable memory, when His Majesty perceived this, most graciously desired and moved me to set about and do that same thing on Hven. Which thing I have also, in the two-and-twentieth year [now], carried out with the very greatest diligence, labour, and expense, presuming that I have thereby sufficiently shown that I would most gladly have done it to the honour of my own lord and king and of [my] fatherland, [and] to the utmost of my ability. And Your Royal Majesty's lord father had most graciously seen fit and promised that whatever I there set up for that art should, by a foundation [fundatz], be sufficiently provided for and perpetuated, together with several other good conditions which were most graciously assured to me. Which Your Royal Majesty's lady mother, my most gracious Queen, without doubt still calls to mind, and beforehand had it made known to the Council of Denmark, so that I therefore obtained the whole Council of the realm of Denmark's open parchment letter, which confirmed such [provision] to me and further gave me assurance thereof. Wherefore I have since taken upon myself an unspeakably great labour and expense, yet more than before, in the hope that, when Your Royal Majesty should come to the time of [your own] rule, Your Majesty would then have a most gracious pleasure therein, and would let me and mine enjoy the good of it.

But now it has fallen out otherwise than I had believed, the particulars whereof I will for the present leave further unmentioned. Your Royal Majesty is without doubt aware that I have been deprived of what I should have had for the maintenance of that same art, and [that] it was put to me in writing [opschreffuit] that Your Royal Majesty was not minded to wish to uphold such [provision] in force — besides much else that has come about unlooked-for, and (as I judge) has befallen without my fault or offence. And inasmuch as I must in the end, by the grace that God gives, complete what I have once undertaken and pursued with such great earnestness and over a long time — a thing which is also known to many nations, and greatly desired [by them] — and do not myself have it in [my] means sufficiently to defray it; for I am already come so far behind [in means] — even though I had the former fiefs — that I am nevertheless constrained to dispose of all my patrimonial estate: I trust that Your Royal Majesty will most graciously regard such my necessity, and will not take this my journeying-out [vdreyße] into any disfavour, since for these and several causes I was so highly pressed to seek other means and counsel, so that what is well begun may be duly completed, and I may keep my good name and repute abroad in full force. Yet I have not journeyed out with the intent that I would altogether forsake my fatherland, but only to be abroad [seeking] help and assistance from other lords and potentates, if so it might be possible, to the end that I should not be too much of a burden to Your Royal Majesty or to the realm. If it could be granted me that in Denmark I may pursue my undertaken work, I will not refuse it; for I, now as before, [would] most willingly [serve] Your Royal Majesty before any other potentates, and to the honour and laudable repute of my own fatherland's realm [do] all that I could accomplish to the utmost of my ability — provided it could be done on tolerable conditions and without my hurt. And if not, yet — wherever it is provided that I shall remain elsewhere — I will at all times be willing [to serve] Your Royal Majesty with all laudable repute and humility, according to my slender ability. Submitting it most humbly to Your Royal Majesty's good consideration, that I do by no means out of any levity (least of all at this [my] age — I am over fifty years old, and burdened with no small household [huesgesindt], which with great inconvenience I am constrained to make strangers [abroad]) depart from my fatherland and from my good kindred and friends; and that what is even yet on Hven, made by me, may sufficiently show that it had not formerly been my purpose and intent thus to wish to depart from there. I most humbly hope, therefore, that when Your Royal Majesty rightly considers this with all its circumstances, Your Royal Majesty will be and remain my most gracious Lord and King, and [will be] favourably disposed toward me and mine with all royal grace and favour. I will at all times be found humble, faithful, and willing toward Your Royal Majesty to the utmost of my ability, wherever God Almighty shall have provided me my occasion. May the same good God, who governs all worldly rule, grant Your Royal Majesty in your royal governance good fortune, blessing, good counsel, and good resolve.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601) to Christian IV of Denmark (r. 1588–1648), then aged 20 and reigning in his own right since the regency ended in 1596. This is Tycho's apologia written shortly after he had quit Hven and Copenhagen in the spring of 1597 — among the most consequential letters in his correspondence, justifying his departure from Denmark while leaving the door open to a return.
  • Place. No place is named in the dateline as transmitted; the body's deictic "paa Huaen … aff mig giordt" ("on Hven … made by me") treats Hven as remote. Tycho had withdrawn to Copenhagen (and soon Rostock); the witnesses are later copies (Afskrift), and Dreyer prints no place-line. The header therefore gives [n.p.].
  • Document type / witnesses. Two later manuscript copies: A = KB, Langebeks Ekscerpter no. 178; B = Rigsarkivet, Langebeks Diplomatarium — both eighteenth-century transcripts (Langebek), not the original. Shelfmarks reproduced as given.
  • Calendar. No explicit dateline survives in the transcribed text; the edition label is "1597. 10. juli" (10 July 1597), Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). As the body furnishes no internal date, the header follows the catalogue label and is marked [O.S.]; no in-letter date discrepancy arises (none is stated).
  • Chancery-Danish orthography (c. 1597). E: K: M: = Eders Kongelige Majestæt ("Your Royal Majesty"); E: M: = Eders Majestæt. Nest … formelding = Næst … formælding ("after the tendering [of service]"). troff = tro ("faithful"); affdrog = afdrog ("departed"); löfflig / loufflig = lovlig ("laudable, lawful"); thilgeneyenhedt = tilgenegenhed ("inclination"); bequemmeligen = bekvemmeligen ("conveniently"); anstille = anstille ("set about, arrange"); forlaeninger = forleninger ("fiefs, enfeoffments"); affhende = afhænde ("dispose of, alienate"); forgyntt = forundt ("granted, vouchsafed"); forsige = forsige ("refuse, decline"); tilbevogenn = tilbevågen ("favourably disposed"); anßlagh = anslag ("resolve, design"). Long-ß and w/v/u variation are chancery features.
  • fundatz — a foundation (endowment/charter), the Latin-derived chancery term for the endowed establishment Frederik II had promised would perpetually maintain the Uraniborg programme; kept as a technical term, glossed.
  • E: K: M: herre fader = Frederik II (r. 1559–88), who granted Tycho the fief of Hven in 1576. frue moder, min naadigste droning = the Queen Dowager Sophie of Mecklenburg (1557–1631), Christian IV's mother. Dannemarchis riges raadz obne pergmentz breff = the open letter (charter) of the Council of the Realm, on parchment, confirming the endowment.
  • paa anden och tyffvende aar — "in the two-and-twentieth year": Tycho counts from the 1576 grant, making 1597 roughly the twenty-second year of his work on Hven. Rendered as written; no silent arithmetic adjustment.
  • regimentztidt — "the time of [Christian's] rule" (i.e. his majority/personal reign); Tycho had hoped the young king's accession to full power would secure the endowment.
  • Age statement / numeral. "ieg er offuer halfftridiesintyffue aar" — halfftridiesintyffue = halvtredsindstyve, the Old Danish vigesimal numeral for fifty (halvtredje sinde tyve = "[the] half-third times twenty" = 2½ × 20 = 50), the source of modern Danish halvtreds "50." So "offuer halfftridiesintyffue aar" = "over fifty years [old]." Tycho, born 14 December 1546, was 50 in July 1597 — internally consistent. The translation reads "over fifty." The trap to avoid is reading the halv- element as "half of [the next ten]" to yield 55; that is wrong here — the figure is 50. (Numeral flagged for the vigesimal false-friend; the reading itself is secure.)
  • huesgesindt = husgesinde ("household, household retinue/servants"); "icke med ringe huesgesindt beladen" = "burdened with no small household," whom Tycho would have to uproot and "make strangers" (resettle abroad).
  • affschildt med huis ieg schulde haffue = "deprived of [afskilt med = parted/separated from] what I should have had" — i.e. the revenues/fiefs withdrawn. opschreffuit = opskrevet ("put to [me] in writing / notified in writing").
  • Negation audit. Six negations carry the letter's legal and rhetorical force and are reproduced exactly: (1) "angenem eller icke" = "agreeable … or not"; (2) "E: K: M: war icke til sindz … att ville holde ved macht" = "was not minded to wish to uphold [it]"; (3) "icke sielff haffuer y formue" = "do not myself have the means"; (4) "icke tager denne min vdreyße thil nogen vnaade" = "will not take this my departure into any disfavour"; (5) "er ieg icke y den mening vddragen, att ieg aldelis ville forlade mitt faederne land" = "I have not journeyed out with the intent that I would altogether forsake my fatherland"; (6) "paa dett ieg icke schulde were … formøgitt thil besuering" = "that I should not be too much of a burden"; plus "vil ieg dett icke forsige" = "I will not refuse it," and "att icke thilforn haffde werit min acht … saaledes att ville drage derfra" = "that it had not formerly been my intent thus to depart from there." The conditional "Dersom dett kunde bliffue mig forgyntt … vil ieg dett icke forsige" is genuinely concessive (a real offer to stay if the conditions are met); rendered as such, not sharpened into a demand.
  • vformeldit lade videre omstendt — "leave the further particulars unmentioned": uformeldt ("unmentioned, unreported"); omstendt = omstænder/omstændigheder ("circumstances, particulars"). Tycho pointedly declines to detail his grievances.
  • No Greek, no astronomical symbols, no verse occur in this letter; the only technical term is fundatz (above).
