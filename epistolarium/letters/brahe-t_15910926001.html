DEM DURCHLEUCHTIGSTEN/ HOCHGEBORNEN FÜRSTEN VND HERRN/ HERRN WILHELM, LANDTGRAUEN ZU HESSEN/ GRAUEN ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ DIETZ/ ZIEGENHAIN VND NIDDA: MEINEM GNEDIGEN HERRN.

DURCHLEUCHTIGSTER/ Hochgeborner Fürst. Gnedigster Herr/ Ewer F. G. kan ich dienstlich vnangezeigt nicht lassen/ wie daß ich derselben Schreiben von jrem Denischen Jungen/ dem Bilden meinem Vettern den 23. Septembris wol empfangen hab. In welchem Ew. F. G. abermals von dem Elendsthier (wovon ich ein mal in meinem Schreiben Zusage gethan) meldung thut. Was aber dasselbig Thier (da ichs noch im vorradt hette/ vnd willens war/ Ew. F. G. vberzuschicken) am Beinbruche fürn zusall bekommen hab/ vnd darüber gestorben sey/ solchs ist in meinem vorigen Schreiben gnugsam angezogen/ Welch Schreiben ich dem Edlen vnd Vesten Henrich Rammel/ meinem besondern gutem Freunde/ so baldt er mir Ew. F. G. Brieff (deß Datum vnderm 22. Maij stehet) hat zugeschickt/ widerumb vberliefern lassen. Darauff er mich folgendts allhie besucht/ vnd also solchen Brieff mit einem eigen Botten an Ew. F. G. abzufertigen verheissen. Er hat auch allhie das mal vnder andern mit mir von dero Theologorum (die Anno 1529. zu Marpurg zusammen gewesen) Vergleichung geredt/ vnd darneben ein Deutsches Scriptum (welchs Ew. F. G. jme vberschickt) gezeigt/ worin dieselbige Concordantien Articulweiß verfast/ vnd mit Ew. F. G. Handtschrifft allenthalben zu weiterer Versicherung vndergezeichnet seyndt. Vnd wiewol ich damals dieselbige mit den jenigen/ die in Historia Augustanæ Confessionis, Francofordiæ ad Mænum, per Matthiam Ritterum Ecclesiasten Anno 1578. editâ, et a Chytræo prius contextâ, fol. 643. et seq. commemorirt werden/ ob alles da bona fide, wie es sich gebürt/ Latine referirt vnd reddirt wirdt/ zu conferiren willens gewesen bin/ So ists doch (dieweyl gedachter Ramelius von hinnen nach Pommern notwendiger Gescheffte halben eylete/ vnd auch vmb anderer fürfelle willen) nachblieben. Es kan aber Ew. F. G. leichtlich mit dem jetztgedachten Lateinischen Exemplari ein Collation hierin instituiren lassen/ ob alles gebürlich vnd rechtmessig transferirt worden. Welchs ich auch gern thun wolte/ wan Ew. F. G. mir auch ein Abschrifft davon gnediglich communiciren, oder aber daß mir ermeldter Ramelius ein Copey von dem seinigen mitteile/ gestatten wolte. Dan ich vermercke wol auß der widerparteyischen Theologen zu vnsern zeiten außgangenen Scriptis, daß sie nicht allenthalben candide et conuenienter Aruersariorum sententias citiren vnd außlegen/ sondern daß ein grosse Philautia, ambitio, odium, vnd vincendi vindictæque cupiditas bey jnen mehrern teils vberhandt genommen/ Ita vt sub prætextu promotionis veritatis Euangelicæ, seipsos et sua vt plurimum quærant. Aber man mag sie in jrer Würde bleiben/ vnd jre Sachen selber verantworten lassen.

Anlangendt aber mein vorbemeldts Schreiben (in welchem ich alle gelegenheit vom Elendsthier vermeldet hab) schicke ich Ew. F. G. nu dasselb zu. Dan da obgedachter Ramelius auff dem Wege/ alß er von hinnen nach Deutschlandt reysete/ erfaren/ daß Ew. F. G. Junge allhie verhanden war/ hat er mir den Brieff wider zurück geschickt/ vnd darneben einen andern/ den er selbst an Ew. F. G. dirigirt, auff daß ich Ew. F. G. Jungen dieselbe/ vmb andere Vnkosten zu vermeyden/ mitgeben kündte/ welchs ich auch gethan hab/ dieweyl der Junge widerumb zu Ew. F. G. baldt zurück zeucht. Jch hab auch vnder der zeit/ die er hie im Lande verharret/ allenthalben bey meiner Freundtschafft nachforschen lassen/ ob jrgendts ein Elendsthier/ das zahm sey/ verhanden were/ aber niergendts ein solchs erfragen können/ sonst hette ich jetzunder Ew. F. G. eines mit demselbigen Jungen (der mir gnugsam zu verstehen gab/ was Ew. F. G. fur grosse Lust zu einem solchen Thier habe) vbergeschickt. Auff das aber Ew. F. G. gleichwol hierin mit dem ersten möchte gewilfart werden/ hab ich hinauff in Norwegen an Königlicher Maiestat allda Stadthalter/ den Edlen vnd Wolgebornen Axel Güldenstern/ welcher mein gar nahe Verwandter vnd sehr guter Freundt ist/ fleissig geschrieben vnd angelangt/ daß er mir auffs wenigst ein par derselbigen Thier/ die da jung weren/ mit erster gelegenheit herab schicken wolte/ dan dero in seinem Lehen vnd Gebiete etliche verhanden seyndt. Hab auch gleichfalls einem andern Edelman in Norwegen/ der mir nahe beschwägert ist/ geschrieben/ daß er mir auch ein par auß seinem Lehen (da man auch der Gattung nicht wenig findet) mit dem ersten wolte zustehen lassen. Bin darumb guter hoffnung/ ich werde auffs wenigste von dieser einem/ wo nicht von allen beyden/ dero Thieren etliche bekommen. Welchs/ so es nicht vorm Winter geschehen kan/ hoffe ich doch auffs Vorjar Ew. F. G. hierin nach jrem willen dienstlich zu seyn.

Was antrifft das ander Thier/ welchs Orix genandt wirdt/ hab ich das auch in meinem Deutschen Gesnero nachgesucht/ vnd ein kurtze Beschreibung davon am 68. Blat gefunden/ doch keine Verzeichnuß darbey/ So meldets auch nicht/ wie hoch vnd groß es seyn soll. Worauß ich vermercke/ daß Ew. F. G. Deutscher Gesnerus ein ander Buch vnd Exemplar sey/ dan welchs mir bey der Handt ist. Es muß ein gewaltig groß Thier seyn/ vnd wol bey zweyer Mans höhe erreichen/ wan es 23. Spann hoch ist. Von diesem weiß man in vnsern Landen/ vnd den jenigen/ die noch mehr gegen Norden gelegen seyndt/ bißhero gar nichts zu sagen/ dan es wirdt allda kaum gefunden. Ich bin wol newlich in erfarung kommen/ daß vorbemeldter Axel Güldenstern vor kurtzer zeit/ ein seltzames vnd zuvor nicht viel bekandtes Thier in Norwegen/ von den jenigen Leuten/ die vber die hohe vnd mit Schnee bedeckten Klippen auff besondern langen vnd breiten Höltzern vnder den Füssen fast gemacht/ gar schnell lauffen/ also/ daß sie ein Hirsch ereylen können/ soll auffgespürt haben. Aber was es für ein Thier sey/ vnd wie groß/ hab ich noch nicht gnugsam erfaren können. So baldt ich einige Wiffenschafft davon kriege/ wil ich Ew. F. G. solchs anzeigen.

Von den Obseruationibus Stellarum Fixarum, davon Ew. F. G. allhie auch meldung thut/ hab ich in meinem vorigen Schreiben anzeigung gethan/ daß dasselbig Buch/ darin sie begriffen werden/ noch nicht vollendts in Druck verfertigt sey. So baldt aber das geschicht/ soli Ew. F. G. dasselb zuzuschichen nicht vnderlassen werden.

Daß Ew. F. G. Mathematicus ROTHMANNVS, noch bißhero sich nicht hat eingestelt/ thut mich je lenger je mehr verwundern/ vnd es muß seltzame Vrsache haben/ daß er so lange außbleibt. Ewer F. G. wirdt wol Mittel finden/ daß sie jn widerumb zu sich alliciren könne/ wan sie schon einige Liberalitet, mehr alß sonst/ auff jn wagen solle/ auff daß er das vorgenommene Opus Fixarum Stellarum desto williger vnd bälder verfertige. Es mangelt jme am Ingenio vnd Wiffenschafft darzu nicht/ Aber mir hat wol an jme gedaucht/ daß er (wie Ew. F. G. auch zuvor von jm geschrieben hat) einen eigenwillischen Kopff haben solte/ worauff jme nur gelüstete seinen eigen Hut auffzusetzen. Wie man dan offt findet/ daß excellentia Ingenia auch jre sonderliche Gebrechen vnd Næuos haben/ die man dan/ so sie nicht anders zu corrigiren seyndt/ gedulten mag. Jch versehe mich zu jme/ er werde sich noch besinnen/ vnd sich zu Ew. F. G. wider einstellen/ vnd im fürhabenden Wercke keinen fleiß vnd mühe sparen/ damit es mit dem ersten an Tag möchte kommen.

Von den Papiermachern hab ich Ew. F. G. auch im vorigen Schreiben zuverstehen geben/ daß ich deren jetzunder keinen bedürffe/ dieweil ich selbs schon lengst Leute bekommen hab/ die mir die Arbeit verrichten. Thu mich gleichwol für den erbottenen gnedigen willen/ gegen Ew. F. G. dienstlich vnd zum höchsten bedancken: Gleichsfalls auch für die mitgeteilte newe Zeitung/ die mir gar angenem gewesen seyndt.

Was diese Länder antrifft/ weiß ich Ew. F. G. nichts newes widerumb zuzuschreiben/ dan daß Status Daniæ vnd Noruegiæ, noch (Gott Lob) gar friedig vnd richtig sey. Der Allmechtige Gott wölle weiter Gnade verleihen. Weiß E. F. G. dißmal weiters nicht zuzuschreiben/ Wo aber etwas fürfellt/ solches soll Ew. F. G. mit erster gelegenheit referirt werden.

Vnd wil Ew. F. G. in Gottes Allmechtigen gnadenreichen Schirm/ zu langwiriger Gesundtheit vnd friedlicher Landsregierung/ hiemit befohlen haben.
Datum Vraniburg, den 26. Septembris, Anno 1591.
Ewer F. G.
Dienstwilliger
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Serene, High-Born Prince and Lord, Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda: my gracious Lord.

Most Serene, High-Born Prince, most gracious Lord. I cannot dutifully leave Your Princely Grace unadvised that I duly received Your Grace's letter from your Danish page, Bille my kinsman, on 23 September. In it Your Princely Grace once more makes mention of the elk [Elendsthier] (of which I once made a promise in my letter). But that this animal — when I still had it in store and was minded to send it over to Your Princely Grace — suffered, by mishap, a broken leg and thereupon died, this was sufficiently set out in my previous letter; which letter I had delivered back to the Noble and Steadfast Henrik Ramel, my especially good friend, as soon as he had sent on to me Your Princely Grace's letter (which bears the date 22 May). Whereupon he afterwards visited me here, and so promised to dispatch that letter to Your Princely Grace by a courier of his own. He also, on that occasion, spoke with me, among other matters, about the reconciliation of those theologians (who came together at Marburg in the year 1529), and showed me besides a German document (which Your Princely Grace had sent over to him) in which those concords are set down article by article, and subscribed throughout, for further assurance, with Your Princely Grace's own hand. And although I was at that time minded to compare these with those [accords] that are recorded in the History of the Augsburg Confession — published at Frankfurt-on-Main by Matthias Ritter, churchman, in the year 1578, and previously composed by Chytraeus, fol. 643 and following — [to see] whether everything there is reported and rendered into Latin in good faith [bona fide], as is fitting, yet it was left undone (since the said Ramel was hastening hence to Pomerania on necessary business, and also on account of other occurrences). But Your Princely Grace can easily have a collation undertaken in this matter with the Latin copy just mentioned, [to see] whether everything has been translated fittingly and rightly. Which I too would gladly do, if Your Princely Grace would graciously communicate to me a transcript thereof, or else permit the said Ramel to share with me a copy of his own. For I do well perceive, from the writings put forth by the theologians of the opposing party in our times, that they do not everywhere cite and expound their adversaries' opinions candidly and fittingly [candide et convenienter Adversariorum sententias], but rather that a great self-love, ambition, hatred, and lust to conquer and to take revenge [Philautia, ambitio, odium, vincendi vindictaeque cupiditas] has for the most part got the upper hand among them — so that, under the pretext of promoting the truth of the Gospel, they for the most part seek themselves and their own [Ita ut sub praetextu promotionis veritatis Evangelicae, seipsos et sua ut plurimum quaerant]. But one may let them remain in their dignity and answer for their affairs themselves.

As concerns my aforesaid letter (in which I reported every circumstance about the elk), I now send the same to Your Princely Grace. For when the above-mentioned Ramel, on his way as he was travelling hence to Germany, learned that Your Princely Grace's page was present here, he sent the letter back to me, and besides it another which he himself had addressed to Your Princely Grace, so that I might give them to Your Princely Grace's page — to avoid other expenses — which I have indeed done, since the page is shortly returning again to Your Princely Grace. During the time he tarried here in the land, I also had inquiry made everywhere among my kinsfolk whether a tame elk were anywhere to be had, but nowhere could such a one be obtained; otherwise I would now have sent Your Princely Grace one by that same page (who gave me sufficiently to understand what great desire Your Princely Grace has for such an animal). But so that Your Princely Grace might nonetheless be obliged in this at the first [opportunity], I have diligently written to and applied to His Royal Majesty's governor up in Norway, the Noble and Well-Born Axel Gyldenstierne, who is my very near kinsman and very good friend, that he would send down to me at the first opportunity at least a pair of those animals that are young, since there are several to be had in his fief and territory. I have likewise written to another nobleman in Norway, who is closely related to me by marriage, that he too would let me have a pair from his fief (where also one finds not a few of the kind) at the first [opportunity]. I am therefore in good hope that I shall obtain some of those animals, at the least from one of these, if not from both. And should this not be able to happen before winter, I yet hope to be at Your Princely Grace's service in this, according to your wish, by next spring.

As concerns the other animal, which is called the oryx [Orix], I have looked it up too in my German Gessner, and found a short description of it on leaf 68, but no illustration alongside it; nor does it state how tall and large it is supposed to be. From which I perceive that Your Princely Grace's German Gessner is another book and copy than the one I have to hand. It must be a mighty large animal, and may well reach two men's height, if it is 23 spans tall. Of this [animal] one knows hitherto nothing at all to say in our lands, nor in those that lie still further to the north, for it is scarcely found there. I have indeed recently come to learn that the above-mentioned Axel Gyldenstierne is said, a short time ago, to have tracked down a strange and previously not much known animal in Norway, [reported] by those people who, over the high and snow-covered crags, run very swiftly upon particular long and broad boards almost fastened under their feet, so that they can overtake a stag. But what kind of animal it is, and how large, I have not yet been able to learn sufficiently. As soon as I get any knowledge of it, I will signify it to Your Princely Grace.

Concerning the Observations of the Fixed Stars [Observationibus Stellarum Fixarum], of which Your Princely Grace here also makes mention, I gave notice in my previous letter that the book in which they are contained is not yet entirely finished in print. But as soon as that is done, sending the same to Your Princely Grace shall not be neglected.

That Your Princely Grace's mathematician Rothmann has still not so far presented himself astonishes me more and more the longer [it goes on], and there must be some strange cause that he stays away so long. Your Princely Grace will surely find means by which you may entice him back to you again — even should you have to venture some liberality upon him, more than usual — so that he may complete the more willingly and the sooner the undertaken Work on the Fixed Stars [Opus Fixarum Stellarum]. He does not lack the talent and learning for it [Ingenio und Wissenschaft]; but it had indeed seemed to me of him that he (as Your Princely Grace too has previously written of him) was likely to have a self-willed head, set on nothing but putting on his own hat. As one often finds that excellent talents [excellentia Ingenia] also have their particular failings and blemishes [Naevos], which one may then bear with, if they are not otherwise to be corrected. I trust of him that he will yet bethink himself, and present himself to Your Princely Grace again, and spare no diligence and pains in the work in hand, so that it might come to light at the first.

Concerning the paper-makers, I have likewise given Your Princely Grace to understand in my previous letter that I have need of none of them at present, since I have long since myself obtained people who do the work for me. Nonetheless I dutifully and most highly thank Your Princely Grace for the gracious goodwill offered: likewise also for the new tidings imparted, which were very agreeable to me.

As concerns these lands, I know of nothing new to write back to Your Princely Grace, save that the state of Denmark and of Norway [Status Daniae und Norvegiae] is still (praise God) quite peaceful and in good order. May Almighty God grant further grace. I know nothing further to write to Your Princely Grace at this time; but should anything occur, it shall be reported to Your Princely Grace at the first opportunity.

And I herewith commend Your Princely Grace to the gracious protection of God Almighty, to long-lasting health and peaceful governance of your lands.
Given at Uraniborg, the 26th of September, in the year 1591.
Your Princely Grace's
obedient servant,
TYCHO BRAHE.

[Postscript:] Postscript. Since I perceive from Your Princely Grace's letter that Rothmann has still, in so long a time, not arrived, I rather think that he, too, will scarcely have sent to Your Princely Grace the description of my instruments which he drew up here. Therefore I would not refrain from charging my German amanuensis to draw up afresh a register thereof, so far as was possible to him in the haste — which Your Princely Grace will receive herewith by the aforesaid boy. When I myself once have better leisure, it is my intention to describe all my astronomical instruments more clearly and carefully, both as to their make and use. May Your Princely Grace receive this slight consignation [inventory-description] with gracious goodwill. Given as above.

Apparatus

  • Correspondents. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven, to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince whose Kassel observatory and mathematician Christoph Rothmann were Tycho's principal scholarly counterparts in the 1580s. The titulature (Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, Nidda) is the Landgrave's full chancery style and is preserved.
  • Language. Early New High German chancery prose (correctly tagged de), not mis-detected Danish. Tycho code-switches into Latin for technical and learned matter throughout: Latin nouns and tags (conferiren, transferirt, Collation, communiciren, Liberalität, Ingenio, Naevos, Status Daniae und Norvegiae) are kept in the source block; the longer Latin clauses are translated with the Latin retained in brackets on first occurrence.
  • Subject — the elk (Elendsthier). The whole exchange concerns the Landgrave's wish to stock his menagerie (Thiergarten) with a Scandinavian elk/moose (Alces alces). Wilhelm's earlier letter (in the same corpus, opening "…wir in unserm Thiergarten ein Elendsthier haben…") reports his own elk lacking a mate; Tycho's promised animal had died of a broken leg. Elendsthier (= Elen[d]tier, "elk") is rendered "elk" throughout; not to be confused with the false-friend Elend "misery."
  • Persons.
  • dem Bilden meinem Vettern — a page of the Bille family ("Bille, my kinsman"); the Bille and Brahe houses were closely allied (Tycho's mother was Beate Bille). Vetter here = "kinsman/cousin" in the broad period sense.
  • Henrich Rammel / Ramelius = Henrik Ramel (d. 1610), Pomeranian-born Danish privy councillor and diplomat, a friend of both courts; the conduit for the Hesse–Denmark correspondence. The honorific der Edle und Veste ("the Noble and Steadfast") is the standard address for the lesser nobility.
  • Axel Güldenstern = Axel Gyldenstierne (1542–1603), royal Statholder (governor) of Norway from 1588, Tycho's near kinsman — Tycho's grandmother was a Gyldenstierne — here the hoped-for source of young Norwegian elk.
  • Chytraeus = David Chytraeus (1530–1600), Rostock theologian and historian, original compiler of the history of the Augsburg Confession; Matthias Ritter (Ritterus), the churchman under whose name the 1578 Frankfurt edition appeared.
  • Theological matter. die Theologorum … die Anno 1529. zu Marpurg zusammen gewesen = the participants of the Marburg Colloquy of 1529 (Luther, Zwingli, et al., on the Eucharist); Wilhelm IV — son of Philip of Hesse, who had convened it — was forwarding a German record of the agreed articles for Tycho to collate against the printed Latin Historia Augustanae Confessionis. The reference fol. 643. et seq. is Tycho's own citation of that 1578 volume; preserved as given.
  • The oryx (Orix) and the "German Gessner". mein Deutscher Gesnerus = the German Thierbuch, the vernacular version of Conrad Gessner's Historia animalium. Tycho notes that the Orix (oryx) description sits on leaf 68 of his copy but with no woodcut and no stated dimensions, and infers the Landgrave owns a different printing/copy. Spann = a span (the hand-breadth measure, c. 20–23 cm); "23 spans" is reported as Tycho gives it, with his own hedge ("if it is 23 spans tall").
  • The skier passage (flagged). von den jenigen Leuten/ die vber die hohe vnd mit Schnee bedeckten Klippen auff besondern langen vnd breiten Höltzern vnder den Füssen fast gemacht/ gar schnell lauffen — an early description of skiing (Sámi/Norwegian skiers on long boards, fast enough to overtake a stag). The syntax is loose: fast is here the adverb "almost/well-nigh" qualifying gemacht ("almost fastened under their feet"), not modern fest "firmly" — though period fast/fest overlap and the sense could be "firmly fastened." Rendered "almost fastened" with the alternative noted; the looseness is Tycho's, not smoothed.
  • Astronomical content. Observationibus Stellarum Fixarum / Opus Fixarum Stellarum — Tycho's fixed-star catalogue, the still-unprinted star-catalogue volume (eventually part of the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata); rendered "Observations of the Fixed Stars" / "Work on the Fixed Stars," the Latin kept by the edition's convention Ew. F. G. Mathematicus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician, who had visited Hven in 1590 and then failed to return to Kassel — the absence Tycho here puzzles over. No instrument-terms or astronomical symbols occur in this letter.
  • Paper-makers. Wilhelm had evidently offered to send paper-makers for Tycho's Uraniborg paper-mill; Tycho declines, having already secured his own workers.
  • Formulae. Opening Ewer F. G. … dienstlich … nicht lassen and the address Durchleuchtigster, Hochgeborner Fürst, Gnedigster Herr are standard chancery courtesy to a prince; Ew. F. G. = Ewer Fürstlichen Gnaden → "Your Princely Grace" throughout. Subscription Ewer F. G. Dienstwilliger → "Your Princely Grace's obedient servant," by the edition's convention
  • Calendar / dateline. Datum Vraniburg, den 26. Septembris, Anno 1591 = "Given at Uraniborg, 26 September 1591" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The header date matches the dateline and the edition label exactly; no discrepancy. The received-date 23. Septembris and the Landgrave's letter-date 22. Maij are internal references, reproduced as written, not converted.
  • Negation check. The legally/logically load-bearing negatives are reproduced one-for-one: kan ich … nicht lassen ("I cannot leave [you] unadvised"); deren jetzunder keinen bedürffe ("have need of none of them at present"); niergendts ein solchs erfragen können ("nowhere could obtain such a one"); nicht allenthalben candide … citiren ("do not everywhere cite candidly"); soll … nicht vnderlassen werden ("shall not be neglected"). The double-negative courtesy formula (nicht … vnangezeigt … lassen = "cannot leave unadvised") is rendered as the affirmative it is.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. German closing postscript announcing the enclosed instrument-"Consignatio", restored from Dreyer VI, p. 246 (dropped by the working transcription); confirmed against the facing Latin (VI, p. 249).

Not collated against Dreyer — no page reference for this letter, so the grade is capped at Probable however sound the translation looks. the edition gives no page reference for this letter (one of the 49 letters with no Dreyer extract by the audit's rule), so audited against the transcribed original only and grade is capped at Probable regardless of outcome. Collated the published English against the German the transcribed original (with embedded Latin phrases) clause by clause: the address block and title; the elk (Elendsthier) narrative including the broken-leg/death detail and the Ramel courier chain; the Marburg 1529 theologians' reconciliation passage and its Latin quotations (Philautia, ambitio, odium, vincendi vindictaeque cupiditas; sub praetextu promotionis veritatis Evangelicae); the Frankfurt printing citation (Matthias Ritter, 1578, Chytraeus, fol. 643); the Axel Gyldenstierne elk-procurement passage (named twice, consistently); the oryx/Gessner passage including the 23-span height figure; the skiers-and-strange-animal passage; the fixed-stars book status; the Rothmann paragraph; the paper-makers paragraph; the closing and the 26 September 1591 date; and the postscript (which the transcribed original itself flags as restored from TBOO VI p.246 by the working transcription — unverifiable here since no page images were available, but the postscript's own English rendering matches its German exactly). No fabrication, omission, mistranslation, or hedge-loss found anywhere in the letter.
