The original

NOBILISSIMO ET AMPLISSIMO VIRO, VIRTUTE ET ERUDITIONE INPRIMIS CONSPICUO, DOMINO HENRICO RANZOVIO, DOMINO DE BREITENBERGA ET REGIO IN HOLSATIA AC DITHMARSIA GUBERNATORI, AMICO SUO PLURIMUM COLENDO. BREDENBERG. S

NON desinam, Nobilissime et praeclarissime vir, te crebrioribus literis compellare, vel potius inter graviores tuas, cum publicas tum privatas, occupationes interpellare, donec aliquid tandem responsi a te receperim. Ternas enim aut etiam quaternas per aestatem et non ita dudum ad te dedi literas, quibus nihil rescriptum est. Cuperem autem quam maxime, te operum nostrorum Mathematicorum maturationem et publicationem tuo auxilio atque consilio, quod et te lubenter facturum non diffido, promovere, idque ea potissimum ratione, ut de Chartopaeo idoneo mihi primo quoque tempore, cum omnia, quae ad opificium illud requiruntur, iam hîc in procinctu sint, prospiciatur. Si itaque CASPARUS ille, quem ab ERICO LANGIO accepi, in tua, uti mihi relatum est, laborat Moletrina, peroptarem illum ad me remitti, si modo absque tuo dispendio eius opera carere poteris. Neque enim illi amplius imputabitur, quod sex Ioachimicis a Procuratore meo Lubecensi, IOACHIMO BUCKIO, praeter voluntatem meam abreptis, clam se subduxerit. Id illi, te etiam uno vel altero verbo pro ipso, ut tutius huc accedat, intercedente, lubens condonabo atque in posterum tum salario tum alijs necessarijs illi sufficienter prospiciam. Persuade saltem, ut audacter et certo citoque huc veniat. Erit illud absque illius damno nec sine emolumento.

STURORUM in Svecia olim celebrium gentilitia insignia necdum depicta conquirere licuit, utut multos, tam Danos quam Svecos, hoc nomine solicitârim. Inter quos etiam fuit Doctorculus quidam Italus, apud Ducem CAROLUM in Svecia medicinam faciens. Qui cum praeterito vere me hîc invisisset, insignium illorum procurationem non invite in se recepit. At cum postea aestate elapsa semel ad me scripserit, nullam eorum mentionem fecit, sed saltem πάρεργα nonnulla, de quibus non tam curiose cum illo egeram, adducebat. Interim tamen cognovi a quibusdam Danis Nobilibus, STURORUM arma prae se ferre piscem quendam, eodem nomine Stör appellatum, fluvio cuidam incumbentem. Verum incerti erant, an veterum in Svecia STURORUM haec ipsa fuerint arma, anque aliud praeterea additum vel ademptum habuerint. Si quid certius posthac de his cognovero, te de ijs commonefaciam.

Adiunxi hîc terna Exemplaria meae effigiei nuper Amsterodami cupro expressae, sed citra voluntatem meam et praemonitionem nimijs monilibus onustae, nec omnia decenter, prout praedelineatum erat, imitantis; ut ob id aliam illic confieri curem. Vellem autem te obnixe rogatum, ut, si per alias tuas seriores curas licuerit, Epigramma quoddam argutum et acutum in hanc atque Operum meorum Astronomicorum, quibus praefigetur, celebrationem concinnare non graveris; idemque, ut ab alijs in Holsatia atque vicinis locis excellentibus Poëtis, quales illic nonnullos reperiri non dubito, praestetur, procurare. Si quid nostra Venula Pegasea in hac Hvenula Uraniburgica vicissim tibi et caeteris recinere sustinuerit, equidem id Musis caeteris Uraniae primaevae, imprimisque sorore Calliopea obsecundantibus efficere non supersedebo.

Scripsi etiam semel atque iterum filio tuo GERARDO de Privilegio Hispanico, pro Operum meorum indemnitate, intercedente Secretario Proregis Portugaliae, MATTHIA ab Otten, Holsato, Ulissiponae agente, obtinendo, ut literis hac hyeme illuc mittendis ipsum denuo ea de re adiunctis etiam meis admoneret. Verum multo iam interlapso tempore, nihil ab eo responsi recipio, planeque aures obturare atque oculos nostris claudere videtur. Forte iam aliquanto plus dissitus, ut Hvenam amplius intueri nequeat, tam minutulae Insulae nullam curam habet. At vastum est coelum, quod spectat et scrutatur. Nec me eius inhabitatorem, ex quo, cum vicini essemus, fraterno animo nos invicem complectebamur, adeo cito ob tam modicam disiunctionem animo et memoria prorsus eliminare decuit. Anne sic Quam procul ex oculis tam procul ibit amor? Spero tamen ipsum brevi tam ad se, quam mei recordationem rediturum, atque amici officio benevole functurum. Quod ut citius fiat, te monitore adeoque instigatore opus esse video, quod et ut facias atque caeteris meis desiderijs subvenire non detrectes, etiam atque etiam te per amicitiam nostram diu multumque sartam tectam perduraturam, rogatum habeo. Vale et felici atque diutina fruere senecta.

Iterum cum tota domo suaviter dege et vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 7. Xbris Anno 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most distinguished man, conspicuous above all for virtue and erudition, Master Henrik Rantzau, Lord of Breitenberg and royal Governor in Holstein and Dithmarschen, his much-to-be-honoured friend. At Breitenberg. Greeting.

I shall not cease, most noble and most renowned man, to accost you with more frequent letters — or rather to interrupt you amid your weightier occupations, both public and private — until I have at length received some answer from you. For three or even four times over the summer, and not so long ago, I have sent you letters, to which nothing has been written back. Yet I should wish most greatly to advance, by your aid and counsel — which I do not doubt you will gladly give — the hastening and publication of our mathematical works; and this chiefly for this reason, that a suitable papermaker [Chartopaeus] be provided for me at the very earliest time, now that all the things required for that craft are already in readiness here. If, therefore, that Caspar whom I took on from Erik Lange is working, as has been reported to me, in your mill [Moletrina], I should most earnestly wish him to be sent back to me — provided only that you can do without his service without loss to yourself. For it shall no longer be charged against him that he slipped away secretly when six joachimsthalers had been snatched from him, against my will, by my agent at Lübeck, Joachim Bucke. This I shall gladly forgive him — you too interceding for him with a word or two, so that he may come hither the more safely — and hereafter I shall provide for him sufficiently, both in salary and in other necessaries. Only persuade him to come hither boldly and surely and quickly. It will be without loss to him, and not without profit.

The hereditary armorial bearings of the Sture, once famous in Sweden, I have not yet been able to obtain depicted, although I have solicited many on this account, both Danes and Swedes. Among them was also a certain little Italian doctor [Doctorculus], practising medicine in Sweden at the court of Duke Charles. When he had visited me here last spring, he took upon himself, not unwillingly, the procuring of those arms. But when afterwards, the summer being past, he wrote to me once, he made no mention of them, but only adduced a few side-matters [πάρεργα], about which I had not dealt with him so attentively. Meanwhile, however, I learned from certain Danish noblemen that the arms of the Sture display a certain fish, called by the same name Stör [sturgeon], lying upon a river. But they were uncertain whether these very arms were those of the ancient Sture in Sweden, and whether they had besides anything else added or taken away. If hereafter I learn anything more certain about these matters, I shall apprise you of it.

I have here enclosed three copies of my portrait lately engraved in copper at Amsterdam — but, against my will and without my forewarning, loaded with excessive ornaments [necklaces], and not imitating everything fittingly as had been pre-drawn; so that on this account I am seeing to having another made there. And I should wish to ask you earnestly that, if your more serious other cares permit, you would not find it burdensome to compose some witty and pointed epigram in celebration of this [portrait] and of my astronomical works, before which it is to be set; and that you procure that the same be furnished by other excellent poets in Holstein and neighbouring places, of whom I do not doubt that not a few are to be found there. If our little Pegasean vein [Venula Pegasea] in this little Hven of Uraniborg [Hvenula Uraniburgica] shall in turn undertake to sing back something to you and the rest, I for my part shall not refrain from doing so, with the other Muses of primeval Urania complying, and above all her sister Calliope.

I have also written once and again to your son Gerhard about obtaining a Spanish privilege, for the protection of my works, through the intercession of the Secretary of the Viceroy of Portugal, Matthias von Otten, a Holsteiner, acting at Lisbon — that, by letters to be sent thither this winter, he should again remind him of that matter, with mine too enclosed. But, much time having now elapsed, I receive no answer from him, and he seems quite to stop his ears and to close his eyes to my affairs. Perhaps, being now somewhat more far removed, so that he can no longer behold Hven, he has no care for so very tiny an island. But vast is the heaven, which he beholds and scrutinizes. Nor was it fitting that he should so quickly cast me — its inhabitant, with whom, when we were neighbours, he embraced and was embraced in a brotherly spirit — so utterly out of mind and memory, on account of so slight a separation. Is it thus: the further from the eyes, the further love will go? Yet I hope that he will shortly return both to himself and to remembrance of me, and will kindly perform the office of a friend. That this may be done the sooner, I see there is need of you as monitor and indeed as instigator; and that you do this, and not refuse to come to the aid of my other desires, I ask you again and again, by our friendship — long since established, and to endure long and much, kept whole and sound. Farewell, and enjoy a happy and long old age.

Again, live pleasantly with your whole household, and farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 7 December, in the year 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on Hven. The subscription manu propria ("in his own hand") marks the letter as an autograph subscription.
  • Recipient. Henricus Ranzovius = Henrik Rantzau / Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), the great Holstein humanist, statesman, and Statthalter (royal Governor) of the Danish king in the duchies of Holstein and Dithmarschen, seated at Breitenberg (Lat. Breitenberga; the subscript address BREDENBERG). A leading patron of letters and of Tycho; the two corresponded extensively. Dominus de Breitenberga = "Lord of Breitenberg."
  • Place / address. Bredenberg (Breitenberg, Rantzau's seat in Holstein) names the destination; the letter is dated from Uraniborg (by this chapter's convention, Uraniburgum → Uraniborg, on Hven). Holsatia = Holstein; Dithmarsia = Dithmarschen.
  • The papermaker and the printing project. operum nostrorum Mathematicorum maturationem et publicationem — Tycho's drive to print his own works at Uraniborg (he had set up his own press and was establishing a paper-mill on Hven). Chartopaeus (Gk.-Lat. coinage, χάρτης + -ποιός) = papermaker; opificium illud = "that craft" (paper-making). Moletrina = "mill" (here Rantzau's paper-/grinding-mill). Caspar (taken on ab Erico Langio, from Erik Lange, the alchemist nobleman and Tycho's friend and brother-in-law-to-be) is the sought paper-worker. sex Ioachimicis = six joachimsthalers (silver Joachimsthaler coins). Procurator … Lubecensis = Tycho's agent/factor at Lübeck, Joachim Bucke.
  • The Sture arms. STURORUM … gentilitia insignia — the hereditary armorial bearings of the Sture, the great Swedish noble family. Doctorculus quidam Italus … apud Ducem Carolum — a "certain little Italian doctor" (diminutive, mildly contemptuous) at the court of Duke Charles of Södermanland (later Charles IX of Sweden). The pun: the Sture arms show a fish — German Stör, "sturgeon" — echoing the family name; fluvio … incumbentem = "lying upon a river." Tycho preserves the uncertainty (incerti erant, an …) over whether these are the authentic ancient arms; the hedge is kept (no silent sharpening).
  • Greek. πάρεργα — "side-matters, by-works, incidental trifles" (sing. πάρεργον); transcribed as printed and glossed. Here = matters incidental to (and distracting from) the business of the arms.
  • The portrait. meae effigiei nuper Amsterodami cupro expressae — Tycho's portrait engraved in copper at Amsterdam (cupro expressae = "struck/expressed in copper," a copper-plate engraving), to be prefixed to his astronomical works. nimijs monilibus onustae = "loaded with excessive necklaces/ornaments" — Tycho's complaint that the engraver over-ornamented him against the praedelineatum ("the pre-drawn [design]"). He asks Rantzau for a celebratory Epigramma and to procure others from Holstein poets.
  • The poetic flourish. Venula Pegasea … Hvenula Uraniburgica — "our little Pegasean vein [of poetry] in this little Hven of Uraniborg": Venula and Hvenula are playful diminutives (a small poetic "vein"; a "little Hven"), with a sound-play Venula / Hvenula. Musis … Uraniae primaevae … sorore Calliopea — the Muses of "primeval Urania" (Urania, Muse of astronomy) and her sister Calliope (Muse of epic/heroic poetry). The diminutives and wordplay are kept as wordplay, not flattened.
  • The Spanish privilege. filio tuo Gerardo = Rantzau's son Gerhard Rantzau (1558–1627). Privilegium Hispanicum … pro Operum meorum indemnitate — a Spanish printing-privilege (copyright protection, "for the indemnity of my works"). Secretario Proregis Portugaliae, Matthia ab Osten, Holsato, Ulissiponae agente = the Secretary of the Viceroy of Portugal (then under the Spanish crown), Matthias von der Osten, a Holsteiner, agente Ulissiponae = "acting at Lisbon" (Ulissipo / Olisipo, Lisbon).
  • The "vast heaven" passage. The conceit turns on Hven's smallness against the heaven the addressee "beholds and scrutinizes" (spectat et scrutatur) — i.e. astronomy itself dwarfs the island; Tycho reproaches the (unnamed) far-off correspondent for forgetting him. The referent of ipsum / eius inhabitatorem in this paragraph is the same person to whom Tycho complains of silence; he is the one now "more far removed" and unable to "behold Hven." (Read by Dreyer as continuing the matter of the unanswered letters; Tycho keeps the figure deliberately allusive.)
  • Sententia / verse. Anne sic Quam procul ex oculis tam procul ibit amor? — kept as a proverbial sententia and set off in the translation ("the further from the eyes, the further love will go?"); a variation on the quantum oculis, animo tam procul ibit amor topos (Propertius III.21.10), framed as a question. Rendered as a quoted maxim, not absorbed into the prose.
  • Formulae. S (after the address) = salutem [dicit], "(sends) greeting". Vale … Iterum … vale = "Farewell … Again … farewell". manu propria = "in his own hand."
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Uraniburgi 7. Xbris Anno 1590 = "Given at Uraniborg, 7 December 1590." Xbris = Decembris (the X- = Roman numeral ten / decem- abbreviation for December); expanded to "December." Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The as-written dateline (7 December 1590) agrees with the catalogue label; no discrepancy.
  • Correction. Secretary's surname corrected from 'Osten' to 'Otten' in the Latin (Dreyer TBOO VII p.301); the English correspondingly corrected from 'Matthias von der Osten' to 'Matthias von Otten', removing an unsourced 'der' not present in either Latin form of the name.
