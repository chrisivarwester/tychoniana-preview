The original

NOBILITATE NON TANTUM GENERIS, SED ET GENIJ ATQUE INGENIJ VIRO INPRIMIS CONSPICUO DOMINO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGVINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO INTIMO.

S. P.

DEDI ad te literas, Nobilissime et Præstantissime Domine OLIGERE, Cognate et Amice charissime, ac tuis respondi, priusquam Rostochio discederem; quas etiam tibi redditas, utut is, quem isthuc ablegavi, reversus nondum sit, nihil ambigo. Nunc vero, cum amanuensem meum cum Presbytero isthuc remeante ad vos mittere decrevissem, denuo te pro necessitudine nostra meis compellandum, et de statu meo brevibus edocendum censui. Scias itaque me ob luem Epidemicam Rostochij ingravescentem in Holsatiam a Nobilissimo viro, Domino HENRICO RANZOVIO literis perbenigne evocatum discessisse, qui aliquot Arcium suarum optione mihi data, quam vellem ex ijs, inhabitandam concessit, ac pluribus etiamnum beneficijs me sibi devincire non cessat. Elegi autem præ cæteris mihi oblatis Wandesburgum, dimidio saltem miliari Hamburgo remotum, eo quod egregia ibi commoditas suppeteret, et vicina civitas, unde in omnes ferme Europæ partes frequens internuncius offertur, necessaria omnia procurandi oportunitatem sufficientem concederet. Ubi studijs consuetis more solito gnaviter incumbo, et a præstantibus ac Eruditis hinc inde viris in maiore, quam ipsemet desidero, et unquam in Patria factum, pretio et honore habeor. Et si non consulto hîc in vicinijs Daniæ subsistere voluissem, conditiones iam antea fuissem nactus, quales Patria vix largiretur; quas etiam me, quam primum lubuerit, ex divina providentia assecuturum, nihil addubito; ut hanc rerum nostrarum mutationem neutiquam doleam, sed Deum ob id mihi isthæc evenire permisisse confidam, quo meliorem in Germania aut alibi locorum fortunam largiretur. Quod autem tam diu in propinquo moratus sum, hinc factum, ut responsum ad literas Illmi Ducis ULDARICI, meo nomine ad Sermum Regem nostrum perscriptas, quibus etiam meæ adiunctæ erant, expectarem. Neque enim longius discedendum, aut apud alios Magnates quidpiam tentandum duxi, antequam responsione accepta, qualis etiamnum esset in me et mea studia animus Regis, comperirem. Cum vero his ipsis diebus hanc adeptus sim, qualem ob quorundam in me malevolentiam recepturum prævidebam (licet non adeo asperam et immeritam) id quod tam diu premebam, iam efficere, nihil restat obstaculi. Daboque operam, favente Numine, ut nemini isthic sim amplius mea præsentia molestus.

Relatum mihi est te fratris mei AXILLI filiam SOPHIAM in uxorem ducturum. Quod equidem lubentissime inaudivi, atque in hoc coniugio omnia vobis quam faustissima et precor et auguror. Et licet meo iudicio nihil rectius facere potueris, quam ut uxore ducta te ab Aulicis compedibus liberares, cum alios tolerabiliores et minus ingratos futuros spes sit (siquidem per viam universæ carnis eundum est) videris nihilominus, ne per hanc SOPHIAM terrigenam sublimiorem quandam ac Philosophicam, Diam illam, et mentem a terrenis caducisque ad longe altiora tollentem, atque immortalitati consecrantem negligas. Solet enim in his fieri id quod præcinebat OVIDIUS: Successore novo pellitur omnis amor. At cum et illa, quatenus fert sexus, Philosophica futura sit, ac Amitam SOPHIAM, quoad datur, haud degenerans imitatura, sociam altioris alicuius, quam vulgus capit, SOPHIÆ potius quam obstaculum fore, præsagio et opto.

Plura rerum mearum, temporis angustia præpedito mihi nunc scribere non datur; ex sorore SOPHIA, tibi etiam amica, tum quoque communi Amico Domino Doctore NICOLAO CRAGIO cognosces. Quæ vero me concernentia, mi OLIGERE, nosti, fideliter (uti tibi confido) indica, a me tacite habenda: nec invidos osores ac detrectatores meos verearis. Quos et te haud extimescere, omnino mihi persuadeo. Ego iam pridem illorum malevolentiam superare et negligere didici. Vale mi optime OLIGERE, meque quod facis mutuo amare perge.

Dabantur Wandesburgo ex arce Ranzoviana die 7. Novembris Anni 97.
Tuus ex intimis
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To a man pre-eminently distinguished by nobility not only of birth, but of genius and intellect too, Master Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, [my] kinsman, relation by marriage, and most intimate friend.

Heartiest greeting.

I sent you a letter, most noble and most excellent Master Holger, dearest kinsman and friend, and answered yours, before I departed from Rostock; and that it was duly delivered to you I in no way doubt, even though the man whom I dispatched thither has not yet returned. But now, since I had resolved to send my amanuensis to you, together with the clergyman who is returning thither, I judged that for the sake of our close bond you should be addressed again by my [letter], and informed in brief of my situation. Know then that, on account of the epidemic pestilence growing worse at Rostock, I withdrew into Holstein, most kindly summoned by letter by that most noble man, Master Henrik Rantzau, who, having given me the choice of several of his castles to dwell in, whichever of them I should wish, does not even yet cease to bind me to himself by further kindnesses. I chose, however, in preference to the others offered me, Wandsbek, removed only half a mile from Hamburg, because there was excellent convenience to hand there, and the neighbouring city — whence frequent couriers go out to almost all parts of Europe — would grant sufficient opportunity of procuring everything needful. Here I diligently apply myself to my customary studies in my usual manner, and am held by distinguished and learned men on every side in greater esteem and honour than I myself desire, and than was ever done in my homeland. And if I had not deliberately wished to stay here in the neighbourhood of Denmark, I should already before now have obtained terms such as my homeland would scarcely bestow; and that I shall obtain these too, by divine providence, as soon as it shall please me, I in no way doubt — so that I by no means grieve at this change in my affairs, but trust that God has permitted these things to befall me for this very reason, that he might grant me a better fortune in Germany or elsewhere. But that I have lingered so long in the vicinity came about from this, that I was awaiting a reply to the letters of the Most Illustrious Duke Ulrich, written in my name to our Most Serene King, to which mine too were joined. For I judged that I should not depart further off, nor attempt anything with other magnates, before, the reply received, I had ascertained what the King's disposition still was toward me and my studies. But since in these very days I have obtained it — of the sort that, on account of the ill-will of certain men toward me, I foresaw I should receive (though not so harsh and undeserved [as that]) — there now remains no obstacle to my carrying out at once that which I had so long held back. And I shall take pains, with the favour of the Deity, that I be no longer a trouble to anyone there by my presence.

It has been reported to me that you are to take in marriage Sophie, the daughter of my brother Axel. Which indeed I heard with the greatest gladness, and I both pray and forebode all things most auspicious for you in this match. And although in my judgement you could do nothing more rightly than, having taken a wife, to free yourself from the courtly fetters — since there is hope that others will prove more tolerable and less ungrateful (seeing that one must go by the way of all flesh) — see nonetheless that, on account of this earth-born Sophie, you do not neglect that other, loftier and Philosophical Sophia, the divine one, who lifts the mind from things earthly and perishable to things far higher, and consecrates it to immortality. For in these matters it commonly happens as Ovid foretold: Every love is driven out by a new successor. But since she too, so far as her sex allows, is going to be Philosophical, and — not degenerating, as far as is granted — will imitate her aunt Sophie, I foresee and pray that she will rather be a companion of some loftier Sophia, beyond what the common crowd grasps, than an obstacle [to it].

More of my affairs it is not given me to write now, hindered as I am by shortness of time; you will learn it from [my] sister Sophie, your friend too, and also from our common friend Master Doctor Niels Krag. But the things that concern me which you know, my Holger, disclose faithfully (as I trust you to do), [those things] to be kept silent by me; and do not fear my envious haters and disparagers. Whom that you too do not dread, I am wholly persuaded. I myself learned long ago to overcome and disregard their ill-will. Farewell, my excellent Holger, and continue to love me mutually, as you do.

Given at Wandsbek, from the Rantzau castle, on the 7th day of November in the year [15]97.
Yours from the heart,
Tycho Brahe,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender & recipient. Tycho writes from exile to Holger Rosenkrantz (1574–1642), "the Learned," of Rosenholm, the theologian and statesman. Oligerus Rosaekrantzius is the Latinised form of Holger Rosenkrantz; "Oligere" is the vocative. He is addressed as consanguineus, affinis et amicus intimus — "kinsman, relation by marriage, and most intimate friend": the affinitas is the impending marriage announced in the letter itself.
  • Date & place. Dabantur Wandesburgo ex arce Ranzoviana die 7. Novembris Anni 97 = "Given at Wandsbek (Wandesburgum), from the Rantzau castle, the 7th day of November, year [15]97" (O.S.; Denmark and the Lutheran north kept the Julian calendar). The header date matches the dateline; the edition label ("1597. 7. november. Wandsburg") agrees. Wandsbek (now a district of Hamburg) was a seat of Henrik Rantzau; Tycho lodged there from autumn 1597 to spring 1598, where he completed the Mechanica and the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata proofs. He had left Hven in 1597 and passed through Rostock before being invited into Holstein.
  • Henrik Rantzau (Henricus Ranzovius, 1526–1598), the powerful royal governor (Statthalter) of the Danish king in the duchies of Schleswig-Holstein, humanist, astrologer and patron, who offered Tycho the choice of several of his castles. Rendered "Master Henrik Rantzau."
  • Illmi Ducis ULDARICI = Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg (Ulrich III, 1527–1603), maternal grandfather of King Christian IV, who interceded on Tycho's behalf. Illmi = Illustrissimi, "Most Illustrious"; Sermum Regem nostrum = Serenissimum, "our Most Serene King," i.e. Christian IV of Denmark (r. 1588/1596–1648), then newly crowned. Tycho does not name the king but identifies him as Rex noster.
  • The marriage. fratris mei AXILLI filiam SOPHIAM = "Sophie, daughter of my brother Axel (Axilis = Axel Brahe, 1550–1616)." This is Sophie Axelsdatter Brahe (1578–1646), Tycho's niece, whom Holger Rosenkrantz married in 1598. Tycho was thus already her uncle and was about to become uncle-by-marriage to Holger.
  • The Sophia wordplay. The letter turns on a sustained pun between three Sophies: (1) SOPHIA terrigena — the "earth-born Sophie," the bride; (2) Sophia Philosophica / Dia illa — capital-S Sophia, divine Wisdom (the philo-sophia a scholar weds in a wife's stead, cf. Wolf's letter of 1571); and (3) Amita SOPHIA — the bride's aunt Sophie, i.e. Sophie Brahe (1559–1643), Tycho's learned sister, herself a noted student of horticulture, chemistry and genealogy, named again at the close as soror SOPHIA ("[my] sister Sophie"). The pun is preserved by keeping "Sophie/Sophia" throughout and flagged here rather than flattened. terrigenam ("earth-born") is the deliberate antithesis to the heavenly Wisdom.
  • The Ovid tag. Successore novo pellitur omnis amor — "Every love is driven out by a new successor": Ovid, Remedia Amoris 462. Kept as the sententia it is; the verse is rendered as a single hexameter-sense line and italicised. Tycho cites it to warn that an earthly love can drive out the love of Wisdom.
  • Aulicis compedibus = "courtly fetters/shackles" — court service as bondage; the metaphor is kept.
  • per viam universae carnis eundum est = "one must go by the way of all flesh" — a Scriptural commonplace (cf. Joshua 23:14; 1 Kings 2:2), here a wry gloss on the necessity of marriage; rendered literally.
  • Niels Krag. communi Amico Domino Doctore NICOLAO CRAGIO = "our common friend Master Doctor Niels Krag" (Nicolaus Cragius, c. 1550–1602), the Danish humanist, historiographer royal and professor at Copenhagen; he also corresponded with Tycho directly (Dreyer VIII). Rendered "Master Doctor Niels Krag."
  • luem Epidemicam = "epidemic pestilence" — the plague then worsening at Rostock, the stated cause of Tycho's move into Holstein.
  • amanuensis kept as "amanuensis" (Tycho's secretary/scribe); Presbyter rendered "clergyman/priest."
  • dimidio … miliari = "half a [German] mile"; the period miliare is far longer than the English mile, so "half a mile" is the literal rendering and the magnitude is not converted.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = Salutem plurimam (dicit), "heartiest greeting"; Vale = "Farewell"; Tuus ex intimis = "Yours from the heart / from [my] innermost [self]"; manu propria = "in his own hand," signalling an autograph subscription beneath a secretary's fair copy.
  • Epistemic hedges preserved. Tycho's qualifiers are kept intact: nihil ambigo / nihil addubito ("I in no way doubt"), praesagio et opto ("I foresee and pray"), and especially the parenthesis (licet non adeo asperam et immeritam) — "(though not so harsh and undeserved [as that])," his guarded characterisation of the King's reply, which is left as the deliberately oblique statement it is rather than resolved into a flat verdict on Christian IV's letter.
  • Witness. The sole witness is a later copy (Afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium, Rigsarkivet, Copenhagen — the 18th-century Langebek transcripts of the Rosenholm family archive of Tycho's letters to Holger (the originals having since been largely lost), so the text descends through a copy, not an autograph.
