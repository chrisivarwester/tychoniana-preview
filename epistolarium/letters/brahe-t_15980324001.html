The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTIANUM LONGOMONTANUM.

DILECTE CHRISTIANE, scripsit ad me ante paucos dies IACOBUS MONAVIUS Vratislaviensis, Amicus meus integerrimus, te sub finem anni præteriti Vratislaviam venisse, et discessus mei causas e Dania per omnes circumstantias sibi retulisse, tum quoque te admodum de me solicitum fuisse, atque hac inprimis de causa inde abijsse Lipsiam. Quod sane mihi auditu pergratum fuit, dum te ita animatum agnosco. Ego tibi vicissim ex animo bene favere atque ad meliora quæque promovere non intermittam. Indicavit insuper ibidem MONAVIUS, quod te ad sororem WITICHIJ B. M. deduci curaverit, ubi omnes illius libros perlustraveris. Rogo itaque ut de ijs, qui et quales fuerint, præsertim manuscripti, et an eos vendere velit, qualique pretio, mihi significes. Vidisti proculdubio plagiarij mei impuri illius URSI maledicentissimum scriptum, in quo præter alia innumera convitia meo et meorum honori non parcit. Ego quidem refutatione illum indignum censeo, cum omnes modestiæ limites imo honestatis longe transcenderit; efficiam tamen ut non impune ferat. Cum vero tibi de plagio ipsius tum quoque omnibus circumstantijs procul dubio ex aliorum, qui tunc adfuerunt, relatu innotuerit, rogo in charta designare velis, quidnam hac de re a tuis apud me commilitonibus inaudivisti. Ego quidem nonnulla adhuc recordor, sed poterant aliqua tanto temporis intervallo, et inter tot alias curas mihi facile excidisse; tum quoque quid alij in Academijs, ubi interea fuisti, de hoc maledico scripto sentiant. Verum ut tecum plenius non tantum de hoc negotio, quod ex alto despicio, sed et de alijs quibusdam longe maioris momenti loqui queam, antequam ad ulteriora hinc peregrinationem suscipiam, atque plus forte disiungamur, cuperem quam maxime, ut meis sumptibus actutum huc Wandesburgum, dimidio saltem milliari Hamburgo remotum, te conferres, quo de necessarijs quibusdam tecum præsens agere queam. Neque enim omnia chartis committere commodum est. Si viaticum tibi ad manus non est, mutuo ab aliquo pete, vel oppignora quid, a me, qualecunque tandem sit, bona fide redimendum, aut aliquis pro te spondeat. Faxo ut illi sit indemne. Non est quod verearis, ne te hîc ultra voluntatem detineam, nam ipsemet non constitui hîc diu manere. Est nunc apud me IOHANNES MOLLERUS Electoris Brandenburgici Mathematicus, qui Hvenæ in coronatione (ut scis) nobis aderat. Is negotijs præest Astronomicis et studiosis meis, quos hîc non minus quam antea Uraniburgi haud paucos sustento. Vide iam, mi CHRISTIERNE, ne fallas exspectationem meam, sed quam primum te hîc sistas. Invenies me vicissim gratum, ut huius pro me suscepti itineris te neutiquam pœniteat. Sume tecum exemplar Ephemeridum novarum N. EVERHARDI e novis (ut iactitat) Tabulis supputatarum, quod illas in aliqua Academia facile nancisceris, et si quid aliud nosti, quod meis desiderijs conveniat. Hamburgi hic liber venalis non est. Mihi nihil præter solum eius libri titulum in catalogo hucusque videre licuit.

Vale, et confestim, prout tibi confido, ad nos advola.
Raptim Wandesburgi die 24. Martij Anni 1598.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

TYCHO BRAHE TO CHRISTIAN LONGOMONTANUS.

Beloved Christian, a few days ago Jacob Monau of Breslau, my most upright friend, wrote to me that towards the end of last year you had come to Breslau and recounted to him, in all its particulars, the causes of my departure from Denmark; and that you too had been greatly anxious on my account, and had gone off from there to Leipzig chiefly for this reason. This indeed was most welcome for me to hear, since I recognize you to be so disposed. I in turn shall not cease to wish you well from my heart and to advance you to all that is better. Monau also reported, in the same place, that he had had you conducted to the sister of Wittich, of blessed memory, where you went through all his books. I ask you therefore to let me know about these — which and of what kind they were, the manuscripts especially, and whether she is willing to sell them, and at what price. You have doubtless seen the most slanderous writing of that impure plagiarist of mine, Ursus, in which, besides countless other abuses, he does not spare my honour and that of my own people. I for my part judge him unworthy of a refutation, since he has far overstepped all bounds of modesty — indeed of decency; yet I shall see to it that he does not get off unpunished. But since his plagiarism, and likewise all the circumstances, have doubtless become known to you from the report of others who were then present, I ask you to be willing to set down on paper what you have heard about this matter from those fellow-students of yours who were with me. I myself still recall a number of things, but some could easily have slipped from me over so great an interval of time and amid so many other cares; and also [I ask] what others in the universities, where you have been in the meantime, think about this slanderous writing. But so that I may speak with you more fully not only of this business — which I look down upon from on high — but also of certain others of far greater moment, before I undertake my journey onward from here, and before perhaps we are more parted, I should very much wish that, at my expense, you would betake yourself at once here to Wandsbek, distant from Hamburg by but half a [German] mile, so that I may treat with you in person about certain necessary matters. For it is not convenient to commit everything to paper. If you have no travel-money at hand, borrow it from someone, or pawn something — to be redeemed by me in good faith, whatever it may finally be — or let someone go surety for you. I shall see to it that he is held harmless. There is no cause for you to fear that I shall detain you here beyond your wish, for I myself have not resolved to stay here long. There is now with me Johannes Müller, mathematician to the Elector of Brandenburg, who (as you know) was present with us at Hven at the coronation. He is in charge of the astronomical work and of my students, of whom I maintain here not a few, no less than before at Uraniborg. See now, my Christian, that you do not disappoint my expectation, but present yourself here as soon as possible. You will find me grateful in return, so that you shall by no means regret this journey undertaken for my sake. Bring with you a copy of the new ephemerides of N. Eberhart, computed from new Tables (as he boasts), for you will easily come by them in some university; and anything else you know of that suits my wishes. Here at Hamburg this book is not for sale. Up to now I have been able to see nothing of it but the bare title of the book in a catalogue.

Farewell, and at once — as I trust you — fly to us.
In haste, at Wandsbek, the 24th day of March, the year 1598.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from his temporary exile residence at Wandsbek castle, near Hamburg, in the year after he had left Hven (1597). His programme of instauratio astronomiae continued there with a reduced household.
  • Recipient. Christianus Longomontanus = Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (1562–1647), Tycho's most senior and longest-serving assistant, later professor of astronomy at Copenhagen. He had left Tycho's service in 1597 and was travelling through the German universities; the literature consulted (Christianson, On Tycho's Island) confirms he took his M.A. at Rostock in spring 1598 and then made this short summer visit to Wandsbek "to discuss the Ursus affair." Tycho addresses him familiarly as Dilecte Christiane and, in the close, mi Christierne (the Danish vocative form) — both rendered "Christian" to keep the single referent clear.
  • Iacobus Monavius Vratislaviensis = Jacob Monau (Monavius) of Breslau (Vratislavia = Breslau / Wrocław), 1546–1603, humanist and Tycho's correspondent. Per the literature consulted (On Tycho's Island), it was Monau who introduced Longomontanus to Wittich's sister and her late brother's library at Wrocław.
  • soror Witichij B. M. — the sister of Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586), the Wrocław mathematician whose visit to Hven in 1580 left a lasting imprint on Tycho's instruments and trigonometric methods (and whose later relations with Tycho turned into a priority dispute). B. M. = beatae memoriae, "of blessed memory" (Wittich was by then dead); rendered as such. Tycho's request that Longomontanus inventory the books — especially the manuscripts — and ascertain a sale price is the documented attempt to acquire Wittich's library.
  • plagiarius … Ursus = Nicolaus Reimers Bär, called Ursus ("the Bear"), c. 1551–1600, Imperial Mathematician, whom Tycho accused of stealing his geo-heliocentric world-system during a 1584 visit to Hven. The maledicentissimum scriptum is Ursus's De astronomicis hypothesibus (Prague, 1597), whose scurrilous attack on Tycho prompted the long Apologia / Astronomical Letters counter-campaign (and, eventually, Kepler's Apologia Tychonis contra Ursum). plagiarij mei … illius Ursi — "that plagiarist of mine, Ursus": mei marks Ursus as the one who plagiarized Tycho, not a possessive of fondness; rendered "that … plagiarist of mine."
  • efficiam tamen ut non impune ferat — "yet I shall see to it that he does not get off unpunished." A double construction (non … impune) carrying a single negation of impunity; reproduced exactly (one "not"). Tycho first says Ursus is beneath refutation, then immediately reverses to promise retaliation — the tension is the author's, preserved.
  • commilitones — literally "fellow-soldiers"; here the metaphor for Tycho's resident assistants/students at Hven who witnessed the Ursus visit. Rendered "fellow-students of yours who were with me."
  • Wandesburgum … dimidio saltem milliari Hamburgo remotum — Wandsbek, "distant from Hamburg by but half a [German] mile." The unit is the German Meile (≈ 7.5 km), far larger than the English mile; "[German]" is bracketed as an editorial clarification not in the source. saltem here = "at [most] only / merely" — Tycho stresses the shortness of the trip, not a minimum; rendered "but half a mile."
  • viaticum … oppignora … spondeat … Faxo ut illi sit indemne — a chain of finance offers: borrow, pawn (Tycho to redeem in good faith), or have a guarantor; Faxo (archaic future of facio) "I shall see to it" that the guarantor "is held harmless" (indemnis). The pronoun illi refers to the surety (aliquis), not the lender; rendered "that he is held harmless."
  • Iohannes Mollerus, Electoris Brandenburgici Mathematicus = Johannes Müller, mathematician to the Elector of Brandenburg, present at Hven at "the coronation" (Christian IV's coronation, 1596, which Tycho's circle attended). He now superintends Tycho's astronomical work and students at Wandsbek.
  • Hvena / Uraniburgum = the island of Hven and Uraniborg, Tycho's former observatory; he notes he still keeps "not a few" students at Wandsbek, "no less than before at Uraniborg."
  • Ephemerides novae N. Everhardi … e novis (ut iactitat) Tabulis supputatae = the new ephemerides of N. Eberhart (Nicolaus Everhardus / Eberhart), "computed from new Tables (as he boasts)." ut iactitat "as he boasts/brags" is Tycho's skeptical aside about the author's own claim for his Tables — the hedge is preserved, not smoothed into neutral report. Tycho has seen only the title in a sale catalogue, the book being unavailable at Hamburg.
  • Sacris Uraniae sign-off not present here; instead the close is the urgent ad nos advola ("fly to us"), a hyperbolic imperative kept as such.
  • manu propria — "in his own hand": the subscription marks the letter as autograph (though witness A is a later copy in Langebek's Diplomatarium).
  • Calendar / place. Raptim Wandesburgi die 24. Martij Anni 1598 = "In haste, at Wandsbek, 24 March 1598." Denmark and the Lutheran north were still on the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1598; the date is O.S. and matches the edition label. No in-letter date discrepancy.
