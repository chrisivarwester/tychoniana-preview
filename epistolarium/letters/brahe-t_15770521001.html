The original

MAGNIFICO DOMINO RECTORI ET CLARISS. VIRIS, REGIÆ ACADEMIÆ HAFNIENSIS PROFESSORIBUS VENERANDIS, DOMINIS ET AMICIS PERPETUO OBSERVANDIS.
S.

MAGNIFICE Domine Rector, clarissimique viri, Hafniensis Academiæ venerandi professores! Quod unanimi consensu, singulari vestro favore impellente, me nihil tale a vobis expectantem Rectoratus honoribus insignire et in Reipublicæ vestræ litterariæ per subsequens Annuum tempus Administratorem eligere non dubitaveritis, vobis omnibus et singulis reverenter habeo gratias. Ego vero præterquam quod me tanto officio digne sustinendo minime idoneum agnoscam, ideoque electionis huius rationem vestræ potius benevolentiæ et favori adscribendam censeam, quam quod revera tale aliquid a me præstari possit, gravibus insuper ijsque non paucis, hoc præsertim tempore, distrahor curis et negotijs, quo minus vestræ voluntati satisfacere et tam præclaro officio rite obeundo vacare possim. Inprimis vero ob Architecturæ in ædificiis constituendis occupationes, quibus in hac Insula, a veteri desolatione in habitabilem oram, Deo conatui nostro favente, revocanda, implicor. Quapropter reverenter et obnixe vestras Excellentias rogatas volo, ut, consideratis his negotijs, quibus hoc ipso tempore, quo vobis adesse oporteret, impedior, me a tanto munere obeundo, quod alias etiam nostris viribus impar est, excusatum habere velint. Imposterum vero, Dei nutu, liberato mihi ab hoc occupationum onere, si quid erit, quo mea tenuitas vestræ Academiæ et vobis in quocunque officij genere usui esse possit, lubens omnique conatu pro virium mensura præstare et volo et debeo. Omnemque meam operam, quacunque in re litterariæ Reipublicæ vestræque utilitati inservire possit, vobis perpetuo defero. Deus optimus et æternus, autor omnis scientiæ et veritatis, vos vestraque studia in suum honorem et publici boni utilitatem diu clementer conservet.

Valete.
Datæ in Insula Huena Maij die 21. Anno Christi 1577.
Mag. et Exc. V. amantiss.
Tycho Brahe.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the Magnificent Lord Rector and the most distinguished men, the venerable professors of the Royal University of Copenhagen, my lords and friends ever to be regarded — greeting.

Magnificent Lord Rector, and most distinguished men, venerable professors of the University of Copenhagen! Inasmuch as by unanimous consent, prompted by your singular favour, you did not hesitate to distinguish me — who expected no such thing from you — with the honours of the rectorship, and to elect me as Administrator of your literary commonwealth for the year following, I reverently give thanks to you, all and each. But I, for my part — besides that I acknowledge myself by no means fit to sustain so great an office worthily, and therefore judge that the reason for this election ought rather to be ascribed to your goodwill and favour than to any such thing being in truth performable by me — am moreover distracted, at this time especially, by grave cares and affairs, and those not few, so that I am the less able to satisfy your wish and to be free to discharge so illustrious an office duly. Above all [I am so distracted] on account of the occupations of architecture, in setting up buildings, in which I am entangled here on this Island — [the Island] to be recalled, from its old desolation, into a habitable shore, with God favouring our endeavour. Wherefore I would reverently and earnestly have Your Excellencies asked that, these affairs being considered — by which, at the very time when I ought to be present with you, I am hindered — they may be willing to hold me excused from discharging so great a charge, which besides is unequal to our powers. But hereafter, at God's nod, when I am freed from this burden of occupations, if there shall be anything wherein my slender means may be of use to your University and to you in any kind of service whatsoever, I both will and ought to perform it gladly and with every effort according to the measure of my strength. And I offer you in perpetuity all my labour, in whatever matter it may serve the advantage of the literary commonwealth and your own. May God, best and eternal, the author of all knowledge and truth, long and graciously preserve you and your studies, to His own honour and to the advantage of the public good.

Farewell.
Given on the Island of Hven, the 21st day of May, in the year of Christ 1577.
Most loving toward Your Magnificences and Excellencies,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / occasion. Tycho Brahe, writing from Hven (Lat. Huena), declines the rectorship of the University of Copenhagen to which the rector and professors had elected him for the coming academic year. He pleads unfitness (a courtesy topos) and, more concretely, that he is consumed by the building works on Hven — Frederik II had granted him the island in 1576, and in 1577 the construction of Uraniborg was under way. The letter is the formal recusatio of an honour.
  • Recipients. The collegium of the Regia Academia Hafniensis — the University of Copenhagen (founded 1479) — addressed through its sitting Rector (Magnificus Dominus Rector, the standard university style) and the body of professors.
  • Salutation / subscription formulae. S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting". Valete → "Farewell" (plural). Mag. et Exc. V. amantiss. = Magnificentiae et Excellentiae Vestrae amantissimus, "Most loving toward Your Magnificences and Excellencies" — the closing courtesy keyed to the collective addressees (Magnificentia the rector's style, Excellentia the professors'); rendered in the plural to match the body's vobis.
  • Reipublicae … litterariae … Administratorem — "Administrator of your literary commonwealth": the rectorship cast in the humanist trope of the university as a res publica litteraria (republic of letters); the rector being its annual Administrator. per subsequens Annuum tempus = "for the year following" (the one-year rectorial term).
  • Architecturae in aedificiis constituendis occupationes — "the occupations of architecture, in setting up buildings": Tycho's Hven construction works (Uraniborg, begun 1576–77). Rendered literally; Architectura kept as "architecture" (building-craft).
  • a veteri desolatione in habitabilem oram … revocanda — "to be recalled, from its old desolation, into a habitable shore": the island is the implied object (Insula … revocanda, gerundive of purpose/destiny). ora "shore, edge, region"; here the once-derelict island made habitable. The slight strain of oram ("shore/region") used of an island is the author's, kept rather than smoothed.
  • Deo conatui nostro favente — "with God favouring our endeavour": ablative absolute; conatui dative governed by favente. One of three pious framings (Deo … favente; Dei nutu; the closing Deus optimus et aeternus).
  • quod alias etiam nostris viribus impar est — "which besides is unequal to our powers": i.e. the office exceeds his strength even apart from the building works. impar … viribus, "unequal to [his] strength."
  • mea tenuitas — "my slender means": a self-deprecating abstract ("my slenderness/lowliness"), the conventional modesty formula; rendered "my slender means."
  • Dei nutu — "at God's nod / by God's will"; nutus the divine assent. Kept as "at God's nod."
  • Calendar / place. Maij die 21. Anno Christi 1577 … in Insula Huena = "21 May 1577, on the Island of Hven" (Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline agrees with the edition label (1577. 21. maj. Hven); no discrepancy. Huena = Hven (the island in the Øresund granted to Tycho in 1576; site of Uraniborg).
  • Orthography. Humanist -ij- for -ii- (negotijs, ijsque, officij) and Maij; autor for auctor; capitalised honorific abstracts (Rectoratus, Administratorem, Annuum). No Greek, no symbols, no astrological or technical-instrument vocabulary in this letter.
