The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD GELLIUM SASCERIDEM.

... Quod librum meum IO. ANTONIUS MAGINUS perlegerit, eumque sibi et alijs eruditis eiusdem professionis viris placuisse testatus sit, grata mente accipio. Pollicitationem autem nostram emendandorum motuum perturbasse illum, equidem non miror, ex quo tam ipse, quam nonnulli alij, hactenus tot tantosque labores (ubi nemo illo maiores, excepto COPERNICO et Reinholdino fundamento, quod tamen multimode locupletavit) in calculandis Tabulis coelestium motuum et Ephemeridibus, exantlârunt; cum nihilominus coelum ipsum non modo in minutijs, sed etiam in gradibus aliquot deviationem a Tabulis saepenumero ostendat. Hac in Sole quidem ipso caeterorum Planetarum chorago, circa Aequinoctium vernum, paulo plus dimidio gradu anticipando aberrant; ut nihil nunc dicam de caeteris Planetis, in quorum nullo non admodum evidens Coeli et Tabularum disconvenientia invenitur, ut suo tempore ex Observationum mearum acervo ob oculos ponam. Quid in aethereorum corporum Revolutionibus, quo ad numerorum denotationem ipsi coelo correspondentem, favente ipso coelestium Opifice, praestiterim, perspiciet doctissimus ille vir, quando opus nostrum Astronomiae instaurationis, quod multo iam tempore molimur, publici iuris factum fuerit. Illic etiam in secunda videlicet eius voluminis parte, Planetarum quinque gyrationes, cum eorum trium qui Solem ambientes Terram simul includunt, tum etiam reliquorum duorum, qui circa eumdem revoluti hanc non includunt, quo ad omnes singulorum proportiones, et particulares etiam inaequalitatum apparentium excusationes atque demonstrationes, luculenter cognoscet; et unâ circa Eccentricitates atque Apogaea, prout communiter appellantur (quae tamen ego secundum propriam Hypothesin aliter ordino atque tueor) unâ cum Orbium omnium symmetria atque commensuratione, voti compos reddetur. Sed ipse in suis recentioribus Theoricis, quas numeris Coperniceis rectius et competentius, quam antea in tabulis a se editis Astronomicis, applicuit, dum realitatem et soliditatem ipsorum Orbium, Aristotelea et veterum quorumdam imitatione, approbat (quae tamen revera nulla est) id pluribus conatur exequi, quod alias paucioribus requisitis possit praestari. Quin et alia quaedam inaequalitatis particularis insinuatio, quo ad quinque errantium siderum apparentias, adhuc in occulto subest, quam nec veteres Ptolemaicae, nec recentiores Coperniceae quocumque modo usurpatae excusant Hypotheses. Hanc tamen non minus quam caeteras (quae ab Epicyclis vel motu annuo Terrae cum commixtis Eccentricis et Aequantibus proveniunt) favente Numine, nostrae concinniore, uti spero, ratiocinatione expedient Hypotheses. Quantum ad obliquitatis Ecclipticae (de qua etiam certior fieri avet) mutationem attinet, ego eam hisce temporibus esse partium 23. M. 31½ exacte, pluribus et maximis, ijsdemque affabre e solido metallo elaboratis Instrumentis aliquot annorum reiteratis Observationibus, sedulo adinveni, nec sensibiliter minorem hactenus umquam fuisse existimo. Nam quod COPERNICUS et quidam eius Synchroni eam 3½ scrupulis angustiorem reddiderunt, id evenit, quod Refractionem ☉lis circa Brumale Solstitium, non uti oportuit, praecaverint; ut de Instrumentorum, quibus confisi sunt, in tam subtili rimatione, insufsicientia nihil dicam. Quin neque Poli altitudinem ex Sole per utrumque Solstitium, eandem ob causam, rite scrutati sunt; adeo ut cum ipsemet Fruenburgi, in loco habitationis Coperniceae, per proprium Instrumentum periculum de altitudine Polari accurate (uti scis) fieri curassem, deprehensum sit, ipsum 3¼ Minutis eamdem iusto minorem consignasse; quod non parum illi erroris in Apogaeo et Eccentricitate Solari, unâ cum toto eius curriculo competenter denotando invexit, coincidentibus et alijs quibusdam deviationum occasionibus, quas alibi a me reseratas nunc praetereo. An autem summa Eclipticae obliquitas in posterum aliquatenus accrescet vel coarctabitur, non adeo promptum est decernere. Nec enim Hypothesis Copernicea (utut admodum ingeniosa) circa Aequinoctium et huius obliquitatis mutationem, locum meretur. Verisimilius tamen duco, hanc Eclipticae ab Aequatore digressionem, subsequentibus saeculis paululum dilatatum iri. Quemadmodum Eccentricitatem Solis nunc etiam pedetentim augeri, non autem circa citimum decrementum, ut fert Copernicea ratiocinatio, versari, haud dubijs inditijs compertum habeo. Nec adeo mirum est, COPERNICUM hac in parte deceptum fuisse, cum ipsam quoque sui temporis Solis Eccentricitatem, unâ cum Apogaeo, minus dextre scrutatus sit, ob causas alio loco (ut dixi) a me manifestatas. Est enim circa haec tempora Eccentricitas Solis vera et indubia part. 2. Min. 9, de 60 totius Semidiametri Eccentrici 13 proxime scrupulis maior COPERNICI opinione; et Apogaeum versatur hoc anno paulo post medietatem sexti gradus ♋ ipsius numeratione 3½ Gradibus anterius (velut etiam in reliquorum Planetarum Apogaeis disponendis non paucis gradibus ab eo peccatum est, ut Eccentricitates horum nunc praeteream) quemadmodum idipsum exquisitis observationibus et certis demonstrationibus a me esse adinventum, ipsemet satis perspectum habes; prout etiam caput primum anterioris nostri Tomi recentiorum Coeli Phaenomenωn, haec et alia ad Solaris cursus accuratam restitutionem facientia, affatim patefacit atque demonstrat. Illic brevi MAGINUS ea quae in Solaris tramitis restitutione hactenus desiderantur extricata reperiet, quibus etiam postmodum in suarum Ephemeridum recognitione tuto, absque iniuria coelestium apparentiarum, uti poterit. De declinatione Zodiaci maxima, an unquam maior fuerit, quam a PTOLEMÆO et eius aliquot antecessoribus assignata est, etsi nonnihil certis de causis ambigam, malo tamen eorum denotationi acquiescere, nec sensibiliter maiorem antea fuisse statuere. An tamen in ipsis Minutijs illorum observatio rite constet, tam Instrumentorum, quibus usi sunt, in minimis animadversionibus fallacia, quam Parallaxeos atque refractionis Solaris neglectus, me non parum ancipitem reddunt. Affixarum Stellarum iuxta nostras diuturnas et sedulas observationes restitutarum Catalogum, quem unâ expetit MAGINUS, ex secundo capite eiusdem primi libri obtinebit. Illic enim circiter 500 Stellae in ipso minuto, et quo ad latitudinem et longitudinem proprijs Instrumentis desuper acceptae rectificantur, praemissa fundamentali restitutionis earumdem ratione ac Geometrica demonstratione. Consultius itaque erit, ut suarum Ephemeridum ad praelum revocationem eousque differat, donec hunc nostrum primum tomum nactus fuerit, quod spero instanti aestate futurum. Est enim iam ad finem Typographico labore ferme perductus, deficiente saltem decimo et ultimo capite, idque ob papyri impressoriae penuriam, quam tamen sufficienti copia brevi me habiturum arbitror, ex propria mea, quam in hac insula eius conficiendae gratia magnis impensis iam nuper construi curavi, moletrina, quae iam prorsus parata Chartopaeum idoneum, pro quo conquirendo meum in Germaniam amandavi Typographum, solummodo expectat. Rationem Trianguli non rectanguli, ex cognitis duobus lateribus cum angulo comprehenso, reliqua absque perpendiculari compendiosius quam communiter sit, venandi, ex quarto meo Dogmate Triangulorum planorum illi aperire poteris, uti ei satisfiat. Nec mihi displicet, quod illi Dogmatis sexti et noni Compendiorum nostrorum operationem patefeceris; siquidem, uti video, demonstrationes horum et similium ab alijs, qui se alienis ornant plumis, nunc divulgentur. Res enim ita se habet, prout refers, quod Plagiarius iste Ursinus (qui etiam mearum hypothesium inventionem pro sua inverecunde publicavit, utut eam fundamentaliter non intelligat, quod satis liquet ex Martis orbe falso ab eo ordinato, ut de caeteris nunc taceam) eas, quas pro suis venditavit, Triangulorum demonstrationes et compendiosas in numeros resolutiones, apud IUSTUM BYRGIUM Landgravianum Automatopaeum, qui eas a WITICHIO impetravit, nactus sit. Cum WITICHIO autem, quando hîc ante tuum ad me adventum aliquandiu commoratus est, de his et alijs ad rem Mathematicam facilius excolendam, liberaliter contuleram, qui hinc discedens postea apud Illustrissimum Principem Landgravium Hassiae pleraque eorum unâ cum quorundam Instrumentorum ex meis concinna fabrica, propalavit, quae nunc alij perfrictae frontis homines pro suis inventionibus sibi vendicare non erubescunt, adeo ut nulla fere ad primos inventores redundet gratia. Verumenimvero quaecumque de Triangulorum tam planorum quam sphaericorum, quatenus ad rem Astronomicam extruendam stabiliendamque conducunt, compendiosa tractatione hactenus comperta habeo, ea omnia in primam partem Astronomiae redintegrandae, tam secundum demonstrationes Geometricas, quam numerorum praxim, sufficienter digeram. Illinc MAGINUS atque alij varias easdemque faciles et abbreviatas Triangulorum explanandorum et in numeros resolvendorum inventiones percipient. Quae hactenus dicta sunt Clarissimi et doctissimi MAGINI ad te perscriptam Epistolam eiusque in ea ad me directas petitiones spectantia, poteris ad ipsum prima quaque occasione, responsionis loco per literas referre, aut etiam, si lubet, hanc Epistolae partem descriptam transmittere. Si ipsemet ad me aliquid literarum dederit, aut lucubrationum suarum miserit, ulterius cum illo lubens conferam, et de pluribus, sicubi opus fuerit, commonefaciam, atque ipsius desiderijs non gravatim obviabo ...

TYCHO BRAHE TO GELLIUS SASCERIDES.

… That Giovanni Antonio Magini has read my book through, and has testified that it pleased him and other learned men of the same profession, I receive with a grateful mind. That my promise to amend the [planetary] motions has unsettled him, for my part I do not wonder, since both he himself and several others have hitherto drained off so many and so great labours (in which no one [accomplished] more than he, save Copernicus and the Reinholdian foundation, which he nonetheless enriched in manifold ways) in computing Tables of the celestial motions and Ephemerides — and yet the heaven itself often shows a deviation from the Tables not only in minutes but even in several degrees. In the Sun itself, indeed — the choragus [leader of the dance] of the other planets — they err about the vernal equinox by anticipating [running ahead] a little more than half a degree; to say nothing now of the other planets, in none of which is there not found a very evident disagreement of Heaven and the Tables, as in due time I shall set before [men's] eyes from the heap of my observations. What I have achieved, by the favour of the celestial Maker himself, in the Revolutions of the ethereal bodies — as regards a numerical reckoning corresponding to the heaven itself — that most learned man will discern when our work of the restoration of astronomy [Astronomiae instauratio], which we have long been labouring at, shall have been made public. There too, namely in the second part of that volume, he will clearly come to know the gyrations of the five planets — both of those three which, encircling the Sun, at the same time enclose the Earth, and of the remaining two which, revolved about the same [Sun], do not enclose it — as to all the proportions of each, and the particular accountings and demonstrations also of the apparent inequalities; and, together with the symmetry and commensuration of all the orbs, he will, in one [exposition], regarding the eccentricities and apogees as they are commonly called (which, however, I arrange and maintain otherwise, according to my own hypothesis), be granted his wish. But he himself, in his more recent Theorics, which he has fitted to the Copernican numbers more rightly and aptly than before in the astronomical tables he had published, while he approves — in imitation of Aristotle and certain of the ancients — the reality and solidity of the orbs themselves (which in truth is none), attempts to carry out with more [machinery] what could otherwise be accomplished with fewer requisites. Moreover, a certain other intimation of particular inequality, as regards the appearances of the five wandering stars, still lies hidden in concealment, which neither the old Ptolemaic nor the more recent Copernican hypotheses, employed in any manner whatever, account for. Yet this one, no less than the others (which arise from epicycles, or from the annual motion of the Earth combined with eccentrics and equants), our hypotheses will, by the favour of the Deity, dispatch by a neater reasoning, as I hope. As regards the change of the obliquity of the ecliptic (about which too he is eager to be made more certain), I have for my part found out diligently — by repeated observations of several years, with many and very large instruments, skilfully wrought of solid metal — that in these times it is exactly 23 [degrees], 31½ minutes, and I judge that it has never hitherto been perceptibly smaller. For as to the fact that Copernicus and certain of his contemporaries made it narrower by 3½ scruples [minutes], that came about because they did not, as they ought, guard against the refraction of the ☉ Sun about the winter solstice; to say nothing of the insufficiency of the instruments they relied on in so subtle a probing. Nor, for the same reason, did they duly investigate the altitude of the Pole from the Sun through both solstices; so that, when I myself had had a careful trial of the polar altitude made at Frauenburg, in the place of Copernicus's dwelling, by my own instrument (as you know), it was discovered that he had set the same down 3¼ minutes smaller than is right — which brought upon him no small error in determining the [solar] apogee and the solar eccentricity, together with its whole course, properly, other occasions of deviation coinciding too, which, disclosed by me elsewhere, I now pass over. But whether the maximum obliquity of the ecliptic will in time hereafter increase or be contracted to some extent, is not so easy to decide. For the Copernican hypothesis (however highly ingenious) concerning the equinox and the change of this obliquity does not deserve [its] place. Yet I judge it more probable that this departure of the ecliptic from the equator will, in succeeding centuries, be widened a little. Just as I hold it ascertained by no doubtful tokens that the eccentricity of the Sun is now likewise being gradually increased, and is not engaged about a nearest decrement, as the Copernican reasoning would have it. Nor is it so much to be wondered at that Copernicus was deceived in this part, since he also investigated less skilfully the eccentricity of the Sun of his own time, together with the apogee, for the reasons disclosed by me (as I said) in another place. For about these times the true and undoubted eccentricity of the Sun is 2 parts, 9 minutes — of the 60 of the whole semidiameter of the eccentric — about 13 scruples greater than Copernicus's opinion; and the apogee this year stands a little past the middle of the sixth degree of ♋ Cancer, 3½ degrees earlier than his reckoning (just as in disposing the apogees of the other planets too he erred from [the truth] by not a few degrees, to pass over for now the eccentricities of these) — exactly as you yourself have well enough perceived that this very thing was found out by me through exquisite observations and sure demonstrations; just as the first chapter of the earlier Tome of our recent Phenomena of the Heaven [Coeli Phaenomena] also abundantly sets forth and demonstrates these and other matters bearing on the accurate restoration of the Sun's course. There Magini will shortly find disentangled those things which have hitherto been wanting in the restoration of the solar track, which afterwards he will be able to use safely, without injury to the celestial appearances, in the revision of his Ephemerides. As to the maximum declination of the zodiac — whether it was ever greater than was assigned by Ptolemy and several of his predecessors — although for certain reasons I am somewhat in doubt, I yet prefer to acquiesce in their determination, and to hold that it was not perceptibly greater before. Whether, however, their observation rightly holds good in the very minutes [of arc], both the fallibility of the instruments they used in the smallest discernments, and their neglect of parallax and of solar refraction, render me not a little of two minds. The Catalogue of the fixed stars, restored according to our long and diligent observations — which Magini desires together [with the rest] — he will obtain from the second chapter of the same first book. For there about 500 stars, taken from above with proper instruments as to both latitude and longitude, are rectified to the very minute, the fundamental method of their restoration and the geometrical demonstration being set out beforehand. It will therefore be more advisable that he defer the recalling of his Ephemerides to the press until he shall have obtained this first tome of ours, which I hope will be in the coming summer. For it has now been almost carried through to its end by the labour of the press, only the tenth and last chapter being wanting, and that on account of a scarcity of printing-paper — which, however, I think I shall shortly have in sufficient supply from my own paper-mill, which I lately had built at great expense on this island for the making of it, and which, now wholly ready, awaits only a suitable papermaker, to procure whom I have sent off my printer into Germany. The method of hunting out, for a non-right-angled triangle, from two known sides with the included angle, the remaining [parts] without [dropping] a perpendicular, more concisely than is commonly done, you will be able to disclose to him from my fourth Theorem of plane triangles, so that he may be satisfied. Nor does it displease me that you have disclosed to him the working of the sixth and ninth Theorems of our Compendia; since, as I see, the demonstrations of these and the like are now being divulged by others, who deck themselves in another's feathers. For the matter stands thus, just as you report — that that plagiarist Ursinus (who has also shamelessly published the invention of my hypotheses as his own, although he does not fundamentally understand it, which is clear enough from the orb of Mars falsely arranged by him, to say nothing now of the rest) obtained those demonstrations of triangles, and concise resolutions into numbers, which he peddled as his own, from Justus Byrgius [Jost Bürgi], the Landgrave's automaton-maker [clockmaker], who got them from Wittich. Now with Wittich, when he stayed here for some while before your coming to me, I had liberally conferred about these and other matters tending to the easier cultivation of the mathematical art; and he, departing hence, afterwards made most of them public at the court of the Most Illustrious Prince, the Landgrave of Hesse, together with the neat construction of certain instruments [taken] from mine — which now other men of brazen brow [shameless men] do not blush to claim for themselves as their own inventions, so that scarcely any thanks redound to the first discoverers. But truly, whatever I have hitherto found out, by concise treatment, concerning triangles both plane and spherical — in so far as they conduce to building up and establishing the science of astronomy — all of it I shall set out sufficiently in the first part of [my] Astronomy Restored, both according to geometrical demonstrations and to the practice of numbers. Thence Magini and others will gather various, and those easy and abbreviated, methods of explaining triangles and resolving them into numbers. The things that have been said thus far, bearing on the letter written out to you by the most renowned and learned Magini and his requests directed in it to me, you will be able to relate back to him by letter at the very first occasion, in the place of a reply; or also, if you please, to transmit this part of the letter copied out. If he himself shall have sent me any letter, or any of his lucubrations [scholarly works], I shall gladly confer with him further, and shall advise him about more matters wherever there shall be need, and shall not grudgingly meet his desires …

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe writing from Uraniborg (no place is named in the excerpt) to Gellius Sascerides (Gellius Sascerides / Gjellis Saksesen, 1562–1612), the Danish physician-astronomer who had studied on Hven, travelled to Italy in 1589–90, and acted there as Tycho's intermediary with the Italian astronomical community — chiefly Giovanni Antonio Magini of Bologna. (His later betrothal to Tycho's daughter Magdalene and its collapse is the famous "Gellius affair," not at issue here.)
  • Excerpt status. The text opens and closes with ellipses (…) in the edition: this is an extract, not the whole letter. No salutation, dateline, or subscription is transmitted in this portion.
  • Person identifications (first occurrence). Io. Antonius Maginus = Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), professor of mathematics at Bologna, author of widely-used planetary tables and ephemerides; Copernicus = Nicolaus Copernicus; Reinholdino fundamento = the work of Erasmus Reinhold, i.e. the Prutenic Tables (1551), the Copernican-numbered tables on which Magini's own were founded; Ptolemaeus = Ptolemy; Ursinus / Plagiarius iste Ursinus = Nicolaus Reimers Ursus (Nicolai Reimers Bär, "Ursus," d. 1600), the Dithmarschen autodidact and imperial mathematician whom Tycho accused of stealing his geoheliocentric world-system (the priority dispute that runs through the late letters); Witichio = Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586) of Wrocław, who visited Hven in 1580 and later Kassel; Iustus Byrgius Landgravianus Automatopaeus = Jost Bürgi (1552–1632), the Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel's celebrated clockmaker and instrument-/mechanism-maker; Illustrissimus Princeps Landgravius Hassiae = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel.
  • Place identifications. Fruenburgi = Frauenburg (Frombork, Ermland/Warmia), Copernicus's residence, where Tycho had a measurement of the polar altitude taken (referring to the 1584 mission of his assistant Elias Olsen Morsing); hac insula = the island of Hven; in Germaniam = Germany.
  • The world-system passage. Planetarum quinque … trium qui Solem ambientes Terram simul includunt … reliquorum duorum, qui circa eumdem revoluti hanc non includunt — a precise statement of Tycho's geoheliocentric model: the three superior planets (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars), whose solar orbits enclose the Earth, and the two inferior (Venus, Mercury), whose solar orbits do not. Hypothesis / Hypotheses kept (rendered "hypothesis/hypotheses"); Tycho's emphatic secundum propriam Hypothesin underlies the priority claim against Ursus. realitatem et soliditatem ipsorum Orbium … quae tamen revera nulla est — Tycho's rejection of solid celestial orbs (the comet-derived doctrine), set against Magini's Aristotelian retention of them; the hedge "in truth is none" is Tycho's and is preserved.
  • Technical terms (editorial glossary). motus → motion; Tabulae / Ephemerides → Tables / Ephemerides; Revolutiones → Revolutions; Eccentricitas / Eccentricus → eccentricity / eccentric; Apogaeum → apogee; Aequans → equant; Epicyclus → epicycle; Orbis / Sphaera → orb / sphere; Aequinoctium vernum → vernal equinox; Brumale Solstitium → winter solstice; Parallaxis → parallax; Refractio → refraction; obliquitas Eclipticae / declinatio Zodiaci maxima → obliquity of the ecliptic / maximum declination of the zodiac; Poli altitudo → altitude of the pole; restitutio / instauratio Astronomiae → restoration / renewal of astronomy. Astronomiae instaurationis opus / Astronomiae redintegrandae refer to Tycho's projected magnum opus (the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata, then in press on Hven, which the letter describes as nearly finished but lacking its tenth chapter for want of paper).
  • Greek. One Greek-derived term, Phaenomenωn, is printed with a Greek omega (ω) for the genitive ending in the edition — i.e. Coeli Phaenomenōn, "of the Phenomena of the Heaven"; the printed mixed-script form is retained in the Source block. Chorago (choragus, χορηγός) "leader of the chorus/dance," used metaphorically of the Sun as ruler of the planetary dance, is glossed in brackets.
  • Symbols. ☉lis = a contraction of Solis (genitive of Sol) using the Sun glyph ☉ + the ending -lis; rendered "of the ☉ Sun," glyph kept in the Source block. ♋ = the sign Cancer (named "Cancer," glyph kept), by this chapter's convention
  • Numerical values (verbatim, not modernised). Obliquity given as partium 23. M. 31½ = "23 [degrees], 31½ minutes"; Copernicus's value 3½ scrupulis angustiorem = "narrower by 3½ scruples [minutes of arc]" (scrupulum = minute of arc here); Frauenburg polar-altitude error 3¼ Minutis … iusto minorem; solar eccentricity part. 2. Min. 9, de 60 totius Semidiametri Eccentrici = "2 parts 9 minutes, of the 60 of the whole semidiameter of the eccentric" (i.e. eccentricity expressed in sixtieths of the eccentric's radius), 13 proxime scrupulis maior Copernici opinione; apogee paulo post medietatem sexti gradus ♋ … 3½ Gradibus anterius. These figures are reproduced exactly; no recomputation or correction is offered.
  • The plagiarism transmission-chain. Tycho's account: his triangle-theorems and prosthaphaeresis-style "concise resolutions into numbers" (compendious computational devices) passed Hven → Wittich → Bürgi at Kassel → Ursus, who published them (and Tycho's world-system) as his own. qui se alienis ornant plumis ("who deck themselves in another's feathers") and perfrictae frontis homines ("men of brazen brow") are Tycho's invective and are kept as such. The literature (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens; Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg) corroborates this transmission and the Wittich/Bürgi link, and records that Ursus, when accused, named Wittich and Bürgi as his source. Tycho's Dogmata (Dogma quartum / sextum / nonum) of plane triangles are rendered "Theorem(s)"; Compendia "Compendia."
  • Negation check. The passage turns on several load-bearing negatives, reproduced one-for-one: non miror ("I do not wonder"); non modo … sed etiam ("not only … but even"); in quorum nullo non … invenitur (double negative, "in none of which is there not found" = in every one there is found); quae tamen revera nulla est ("which in truth is none"); non autem circa citimum decrementum ("and is not engaged about a nearest decrement"); non erubescunt ("do not blush"); non gravatim ("not grudgingly"). The double negative nullo non is intentional litotes and is kept as such rather than flattened.
  • Calendar / dating. No dateline survives in the excerpt. The catalogue date is 1 October 1590 (label "1590 1. oktober"; Dreyer dates it October 1590, O.S.). Because the source text carries no dateline of its own, the header gives the catalogued date marked "[O.S., catalogued]" rather than asserting an as-written date the text does not substantiate. The witness is a copy Gellius Sascerides incorporated into his own letter to Magini of 1 February 1591 (Bologna, Archivio Malvezzi de' Medici) — consistent with an autumn-1590 original that Gellius forwarded onward, as the closing instruction ("transmit this part of the letter copied out") anticipates. Denmark used Old Style throughout; Magini in Bologna used New Style — a gap relevant to the courier chronology but not to any in-text date here.
  • Correction. 'Plagiarius iste Ursinus' (Dreyer, TBOO VII p.281): the epithet 'Ursinus' restored in the English (was flattened to the bare name 'Ursus'), preserving Tycho's derisive pun on Ursus's bear-name.
