The original

NOBILITATE PERILLUSTRI ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO, D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, ET ARCIS URANIBURG IN INSULA HELLESPONTI DANICI HUÆNA FUNDATORI.

CUM abs te nihil ad nos allatum sit post eas litteras, quas ad me scriptas D. IOANNI FRANCISCO SAGREDO Patritio Veneto commendasti, ut ad me perferendas curaret, quibus ego et statim respondi diligenter, eiusdem opera usus, ut ad te tuto transmitteretur, suspicari coepi suboffensum esse te, quod ego in tua causa procuranda lentior expectatione et voluntate tua fuissem. Sed, mi TYCHO, illud te reputare cum animo tuo velim, quae non in nostra, sed in aliorum manu sunt sita, ea non tam cito confici posse: his accedit occupatio domestica; solus sum meae familiae moderator, quam in officio continere, cui opportuna ac necessaria providere necesse est, ita, quod tempus aut studijs, aut amicorum negotijs obeundis dicatum fuit, id invito mihi domesticae curae eripiunt. Ac dum quidem studia in scholis publice exercentur, et tum mihi et publice et privatim auditoribus studiosisque meis opera danda est; cum autem illa intermittuntur, ne tum quidem otioso mihi esse licet, ultimis enim elapsis duobus annis a Serenissimo Duce Mantuae sum accersitus, ut per vacationum dies, Principibus suis filijs in Mathematica facultate instruendis vacarem. Neque vero sufficere ille sibi duxit omne istud aestivum tempus atque autumnale, quo solemus a publicis lectionibus feriari; sed et hoc, quod ad Quadragesimam usque ab ineunte fere Ianuario nobis vacuum datur, oportet me ab eodem accersitum, magno meo incommodo, Mantuae commorari. Cum igitur ad Aulam Imperatoriam ab ipso Serenissimo Duce sit destinatus Rerum suarum Procurator, Nobilissimus Comes LÆLIUS ARIVABENUS, percommodum mihi visum est, has ad te litteras ab eo perferendas mittere.

Quod itaque ad praeclarum tuum illud de Restitutionibus coelestium motuum opus spectat, vellem equidem incepisse fructum aliquem ex eo percipere. Solum enim habui cathalogum stellarum inerrantium, qui mihi pro thesauro est, itemque Tabulam illam motus Lunaris; putabamque posse me primo quoque tempore habere a te motum Solis correctum, itemque Motus Lunaris complementum, sed, ut video, frustra putabam. Dubitationes etiam mihi sese obijcientes in hac Lunari tua Tabula, cuius supputationes ad unguem congruere tuis observationibus non perspicio, dilui abs te volebam, docerique praeterea de arcubus visionum, stellarum tum errantium, tum inerrantium in eum usum, de quo ad te perscripsi. Adhaec operam meam ultro obtuli in supputandis aequationum Tabulis, neglectis scrupulis Proportionalibus, Argumenti aequationibus et excessibus, iuxta novas meas inventiones, quae ab ijs omnibus, a quibus perspectae sunt, utilissimae ac nobilissimae iudicantur, cum earum ope omnes supputationum labores facile compendiosiores fiant. Habeo et alias quasdam vias, ad eundem scopum attingendum planiores et compendiosiores, quas non tam cito alijs communicandas esse iustis de causis decrevi. Quae ne tu somnia esse putes, mitto ad te Tabulam tuam Lunarem a me novo modo elaboratam, unâ cum eius usu, quibus compendijs perspectis tu videris, num tum diligentia tum inventione PTOLEMÆUM, COPERNICUM et REINOLDUM etiam post me longe reliquerim. Quid enim mea Prostaphaereseων Planetarum Generali Tabula, quae omnibus Planetis communis est, neque viginti paginas excedit, compendiosius excogitari poterit? an non plurimi libentius et maiori compendio per eam, quam per Ephemerides Planetarum computum subducturi sunt? Neque vero tu mireris, velim, si denuo a me supputatas videris centri variationes in hac tua Tabula, et Anomaliae aequationes; hoc enim, ut nullo fere mihi labori fuit, ita magna cum animi mei voluptate feci, quae quidem Anomaliae aequatio prodit ex additione variationis centri et aequationis secundi Epicicli. Aequationes tamen argumenti, seu maioris Epicicli, cum adhaerente eis excessu denuo supputare nolui, licet cognovissem Apogaeas aequationes omnium minimas, quae in novilunijs et plenilunijs contingunt, colligendas esse, manente Luna in puncto G secundi Epicicli, proxima puncto D, dum centrum Eccentrici occupat punctum B parvi Circelli, atque etiam Aequationes omnium maximas, quae in Quadraturis accidunt, definiendas esse, manente Luna in F, et centro Eccentrici in H, Perigaeo parvi circelli, a quibus postea eliciendus esset excessus per subductionem singularum aequationum Apogaearum a singulis his Perigaei aequationibus. Nolui, inquam, eas de integro elaborare, cum eandem viam laboriosam, ne dicam parum accuratam, praesertim in ♂, ♀ et ☿ amplius ingredi non satis e re nostra iudicarem. Si itaque idoneum me putas, qui in aliorum Planetarum Tabulis, iuxta has meas inventiones, ulli tibi usui esse possim, nihil dubites meam implorare operam, nam hac in re, ut quod res est fatear, multo me habebis promptiorem, quam in altera illa mihi tradita Provincia, cuius magna pars in aliorum manu est, ut absque aliorum opera nihil praestare valeamus, qua in his supputationibus nequaquam egeo. Illud etiam addere volo, meas has rationes promptius et commodius subservituras esse variationibus Eccentricitatum, quam usitatam, nam possumus definire numerum multiplicandum sive numerum mysticum ei adiacentem, ad unum situm, notando differentiam, quae pro altero situ extremo consurgit, ut ex ea haberi possit congruus numerus multiplicandus, vel numerus mysticus ad optatum Eccentricitatis situm. Sed pluribus alias, nunc vale, eruditissime vir, meque amantissimum tui, ac praeter omnes studiosissimum observantissimumque redamare non graveris.

Datae Mantuae, in Aula Ducali, die 20 Februarij, anno 1601.
Perillustri Dominationi tuae addictissimus
IOANNES ANTONIUS MAGINUS, Patavinus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man most illustrious in nobility and most excellent in erudition, Lord Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup, and Founder of the castle of Uraniborg on the Island Hven in the Danish Hellespont.

Since nothing has been brought to us from you after that letter — which, written to me, you commended to Lord Giovanni Francesco Sagredo, a Venetian patrician, to see delivered to me, and to which I both at once replied carefully (using his help, that it might be safely transmitted to you) — I began to suspect you were somewhat offended that I had been slower in procuring your business than your expectation and wish. But, my Tycho, I would have you consider with your mind that what is placed not in our own hand but in others' cannot be accomplished so quickly. To this is added domestic occupation: I am the sole governor of my household, which it is necessary to keep in order and to provide with what is fitting and needful — so that what time was devoted to studies or to friends' business, the domestic cares snatch from me against my will. And while studies are publicly carried on in the schools, I must then give my labour both publicly and privately to my hearers and students; but when these are interrupted, not even then am I allowed to be at leisure — for these last two years past I have been summoned by the most serene Duke of Mantua to give my vacation days to instructing his princely sons in the Mathematical faculty. Nor did he think it enough for himself, all that summer and autumn time when we are wont to take holiday from the public lectures; but also this [period], which is given us free from about the beginning of January up to Lent, I must — summoned by him, to my great inconvenience — spend at Mantua. Since, then, the most serene Duke has appointed to the Imperial Court, as Procurator of his affairs, the most noble Count Lelio Arrivabene, it has seemed very convenient to me to send this letter to you, to be carried by him.

As, then, concerns that famous work of yours on the Restitutions of the celestial motions, I could indeed wish to have begun to perceive some fruit from it. For I have had only the catalogue of the fixed stars — which is to me as a treasure — and that Table of the Lunar motion; and I thought I could, at the first opportunity, have from you the corrected motion of the Sun, and likewise the complement of the Lunar motion; but, as I see, I thought in vain. The doubts, too, presenting themselves to me in this Lunar Table of yours — whose computations I do not perceive to agree to the nail with your observations — I wished to have resolved by you; and to be taught besides about the arcs of visions [arcus visionum] of the stars, both wandering and fixed, for that use of which I wrote to you. Moreover, I have freely offered my labour in computing Tables of equations — the proportional minutes, the Argument-equations, and the excesses being set aside — according to my new inventions, which by all who have examined them are judged most useful and most noble, since by their help all the labours of computation easily become more compendious. I have also certain other ways, plainer and more compendious, for reaching the same goal, which for good reasons I have resolved not to communicate to others so quickly. Lest you think these are dreams, I send you your Lunar Table, elaborated by me in a new way, together with its use — from which compendia, once examined, you may see whether, in diligence and in invention, I have left Ptolemy, Copernicus, and Reinhold far behind me as well. For what could be devised more compendious than my General Table of the Prosthaphaereses of the Planets, which is common to all the Planets, and does not exceed twenty pages? Will not very many more gladly, and with greater economy, draw their computation by it than through the Ephemerides of the Planets? Nor would I have you wonder if you see, computed anew by me, the variations of the centre in this Table of yours, and the equations of the Anomaly; for this — as it was almost no labour to me — I did with great delight of mind; which Anomaly-equation, indeed, comes out from the addition of the variation of the centre and the equation of the second Epicycle. The equations of the Argument, however, or of the greater Epicycle, with the excess attaching to them, I was unwilling to compute anew — although I had recognised that the apogee-equations, the smallest of all, which occur at new and full moons, are to be gathered with the Moon remaining at point G of the second Epicycle, next to point D, while the centre of the Eccentric occupies point B of the small circle; and also that the equations greatest of all, which happen at the Quadratures, are to be defined with the Moon remaining at F, and the centre of the Eccentric at H, the Perigee of the small circle — from which the excess would afterward have to be drawn out, by subtracting the several apogee-equations from these several perigee-equations. I was unwilling, I say, to work these out entire, since I judged it not enough to our purpose to enter further upon that same laborious — not to say, scarcely accurate — way, especially in ♂ [Mars], ♀ [Venus], and ☿ [Mercury]. If, then, you think me fit to be of any use to you in the Tables of the other Planets, according to these inventions of mine, do not at all doubt to implore my labour; for in this matter — to confess the thing as it is — you will have me far readier than in that other Province committed to me, a great part of which is in others' hands, so that without others' labour we can accomplish nothing — whereas in these computations I in no way need [them]. This too I will add: that these methods of mine will serve the variations of the Eccentricities more readily and conveniently than the usual [method]; for we can define the multiplier-number, or the "mystic number" adjacent to it, for one position, by noting the difference that arises for the other extreme position, so that from it the fitting multiplier-number, or mystic number, for the desired position of the Eccentricity, may be had. But of these things more another time. Now farewell, most learned man, and do not be unwilling to love in return one most loving of you, and beyond all most zealous and observant.

Given at Mantua, in the Ducal Court, the 20th day of February, the year 1601.
Most devoted to Your most illustrious Lordship,
Giovanni Antonio Magini, of Padua.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Giovanni Antonio Magini (Maginus) (1555–1617) — professor of mathematics/astronomy at Bologna (the man who beat Galileo to that chair), a major ephemerides-maker and table-computer — to Tycho. He signs "Patavinus" (of/from Padua). The letter survives in Magini's own printed Tabulae primi mobilis (no manuscript witness recorded).
  • Significance — Tychonic tables in Italian hands, and Magini's computational self-advertisement.
  • What Magini has of Tycho's work. Only the catalogue of fixed stars ("a treasure to me") and a Table of the Lunar motion; he is still waiting (in vain) for the corrected Solar motion and the "complement" of the Lunar motion — a window on how partially Tycho's results had circulated before publication.
  • A genuine objection. Magini reports the Lunar Table's "computations do not agree to the nail with [Tycho's] observations," and asks Tycho to resolve the doubts — and to teach him about the "arcs of visions" (visibility arcs) of planets and stars.
  • Magini's methods. He boasts of new compendious computation methods (omitting proportional minutes, argument-equations, excesses), a "General Table of Planetary Prosthaphaereses" under 20 pages common to all planets, claiming to have left Ptolemy, Copernicus, and Reinhold behind. He encloses Tycho's own Lunar Table re-worked his way.
  • The technical lunar passage is rendered literally: Magini recomputed the variations of the centre and the anomaly-equations of Tycho's multi-epicycle lunar model (the "second epicycle," the "small circle," lettered points G, D, B, F, H, apogee-/perigee-equations at syzygies and quadratures, the "excess"), but declined to redo the argument-equations as too laborious and "scarcely accurate," especially for Mars, Venus, Mercury. The lettered points refer to Tycho's/Magini's lunar diagram (not reproduced here); the geometry is translated as written, without reconstruction.
  • An offer. Magini volunteers to help compute the other planets' tables by his methods — frankly noting he is freer in pure computation than in "that other Province committed to me … a great part in others' hands" (likely his observational/instrument obligations).
  • The courier network. Tycho's earlier letter reached Magini via Giovanni Francesco Sagredo (the Venetian patrician — later immortalised as an interlocutor in Galileo's Dialogue); this reply travels via Count Lelio Arrivabene, the Duke of Mantua's (Vincenzo I Gonzaga's) agent to the Imperial court.
  • Persons named. Giovanni Antonio Magini; Tycho (addressee, "Founder of Uraniborg on Hven in the Danish Hellespont"); Giovanni Francesco Sagredo; the Duke of Mantua (Vincenzo I Gonzaga, unnamed) and his sons; Count Lelio Arrivabene; Ptolemy, Copernicus, Erasmus Reinhold. Places: Mantua (ducal court), Bologna/Padua (implied), Venice, the Imperial court.
  • Calendar / date. The printed source gives only "die Februarij, anno 1601" — no day-number; the catalogue label supplies 20 February 1601 [N.S.] (Italy = New Style). Header gives "[20] February 1601 [N.S.]"; the bracket flags the supplied day. (Mantua was New Style.)
  • §1 Dreyer collation. The day is not merely label-supplied: Dreyer (Opera Omnia VIII p. 401; running-head 10/20 Febr.) prints die 20 Februarij. Restored to header, Source, and translation; the bracket/“supplied” qualification is withdrawn.
  • Correction. Fixed a pronoun-flip mistranslation in the closing boast: Magini's Latin reads 'etiam post me longe reliquerim' ('I have left them far behind me too'), confirmed at Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.400, l.37. The published English had rendered this 'even after you', misattributing Magini's self-boast about surpassing Ptolemy, Copernicus, and Reinhold to Tycho instead of to Magini himself.
