The original

MAGNIFICO D:NO RECTORJ OC HEDERLIGE OC HØYLERDE PROFESSORIBUS Y KIØBENHAFFNS VNIUERSITET, MINE SYNDERLIGE GODE WENNER, GANDSKE WENLIGENN THILLSCHREFFUIT.

MAGNIFICE d:ne rector och hederlige och høylerde professores vdj Kiøbenhaffns vniversitet, synderlige gode venner. Nest min wenlig tacksigelse for allt beuiist gode, huilchet at forskylle ieg stedße vill findis aff formuenn willig, giffuendis eder der hoes gandske wenligen thilckiende, att eders schriffuelse er mig for nogle dage thillhende kommen, huor vdj y lod mig forstaa om eders thiener vid naffn Lauge møller vdj Klare mølle y Roskiilde, at hand haffuer ført nogenn klagemaall offuer en aff mine thiener vid naffn Peder møller, som ligger til Kongernis cappell sammesteds, huilche hans klagemaall schrifftligen forfattede y mig med samme eders breff tillkickedt haffuer. Derhoes begierendis, att ieg selff samme min thienner wilde straffe, som eders møllere y saa maade haffde foruretted, eller oc lade eder hannom tilltale for retten. Saa kand ieg eder her paa till itt wenligt suar icke forholle, att ieg strax schreff Mogens Bertellßønn, forstander y hospitalidt der j Kiøbenhaffnn, blant andet till, at handt vilde giffue eder paa mine wegne thilckiende, att ieg vilde haffue budt epther min møller och haalde hannom slige klagemaal forre, och høre huad hand der thil haffde at suare, siiden vilde ieg schriffue eder beskeen der paa igien, giffuendis ether selff att betencke, at ieg icke saa strax paa den andens klagemaall och beretning kunde beskylle och straffe min thiener aldelis vhørt oc foruden nøyagtig beuisning offuer hannom, quia (utj scitis) audienda etiam altera pars, et conuincenda antequam puniatur, et procedendum secundum leges receptas, si aliter causa transigi nequeat. Haffuer ieg derforre strax sendt bud epther for:ne min møller, oc ladet lest for hannom eders møllers schrifftlige klagemaall, och giffuid hannem respiit, ocsaa schrifftligen at suare der till, huilcke hans giensuar oc replication ieg sender ether her hoes. Dersom den icke siunis eder at kunde følge skell, och hans gienpart icke will lade sig nøye der met, kand ieg for min personn well liide, att de taler hiinn anden thill med loug och rett, oc at der bliffuer paa deris werne thing legittime citeret oc deciderit vdj sagen, thj y skulle ingelunde giøre mig imod der medt, att y lader forsuare ethers thiener och lader min thiltale, saa wiit y kunde giøre med loug och ret. Jeg will icke heller haffue wiidere der imod att siige eller minn thiener att handtheffue, end som billigt och ret kand verre. Huilchet ieg haffde at giffue ether thill it wenligt suar paa eders schriffuilße vdigien tilckiende. Tackendis ether der hoes gandske gierne, att y wilde lade mig slig leylighedt thillschriffue, før end y secundum strictum jus lod procedere der medt. Och will gierne slig och ald anden beuiist gode och welluillighet hoes eder alle och en huer vdj sehr, saa wiit mig mueligt kand werre, stedße findis gandske goduillig igien att forskylle, y huis maade ieg wedt ether thill aere och gode kandt werre, Och vill her med nu och alltiid haffue ether Gud allermegtigste befahledt.

Datum Vraniborg den 7. dag decembris a:o 94.
Tycho Brahe
manu pp:a.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the Magnificent Master Rector and the honourable and most learned Professors in the University of Copenhagen, my especial good friends, written quite amicably.

Magnificent Master Rector and honourable and most learned Professors in the University of Copenhagen, [my] especial good friends. Next [after] my friendly thanks for all kindness shown — which I shall ever be found willing to repay according to my ability — I make known to you herewith, quite amicably, that your letter reached my hands some days ago, in which you gave me to understand concerning your servant by name Lauge Møller [the miller] at Klare Mølle in Roskilde, that he has lodged a certain complaint against one of my servants by name Peder Møller [the miller], who belongs to the Kings' Chapel in that same place, which complaint of his, drawn up in writing, you have sent to me with that same letter of yours. Therewith requesting that I myself would punish that same servant of mine, who had in such manner wronged your miller, or else let you proceed against him at law. So I cannot withhold from you here, by way of a friendly reply, that I straightway wrote, among other matters, to Mogens Bertelsen, the warden at the hospital there in Copenhagen, [asking] that he would on my behalf give you to understand that I would have sent for my miller and put such complaints to him, and hear what he had to answer thereto, [and] thereafter I would write you an account of it in return — giving you yourself to consider that I could not so straightway, on the other [party's] complaint and report, accuse and punish my servant altogether unheard and without sufficient proof against him, quia (uti scitis) audienda etiam altera pars, et convincenda antequam puniatur, et procedendum secundum leges receptas, si aliter causa transigi nequeat [because, as you know, the other party too must be heard, and must be convicted before being punished, and one must proceed according to the received laws, if the case cannot be settled otherwise]. I have therefore straightway sent for my aforesaid miller, and had your miller's written complaint read out to him, and given him a respite [in which] to answer it, likewise in writing, which answer and reply of his I send you herewith. If it does not seem to you to be able to satisfy reason, and his counterpart [i.e. the opposing party] will not be content therewith, I can well bear, for my own person, that they address one another at law and in [the] court, and that there be lawful summons issued and the matter decided at their proper venue [thing]; for you shall by no means thereby act against me by your defending your servant and letting [proceedings be brought against] mine, so far as you can do with law and justice. Neither will I have anything further to say against it, nor [seek] to uphold my servant, beyond what may be fair and just. This [is what] I had to give you to understand, by way of a friendly reply to your letter in return. Thanking you withal quite gladly that you were willing to write to me of such occasion, before you proceeded therein secundum strictum jus [according to strict law]. And I will gladly be found ever quite well-disposed to repay such, and all other kindness and goodwill, with you all and with each one in particular, so far as may be possible for me, in whatever manner I may be able to [serve] your honour and good. And herewith [I] will now and always have you commended to God Almighty.

Given at Uraniborg, the 7th day of December, in the year [15]94.
Tycho Brahe,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on Hven, his observatory-estate; the subscription manu pp:a. = manu propria ("in his own hand"). The hand here is the chancery-Danish register of a magnate, not Tycho's Latin scholarly idiom.
  • Recipients. The Rector and Professors of the University of Copenhagen (Kiøbenhaffns vniversitet), addressed collectively in their corporate capacity as patrons of the Roskilde mill in dispute. The university held the Klare Mølle (Clare Mill) at Roskilde and figures here as the opposing servant's master.
  • The dispute. A milling-jurisdiction quarrel between two millers: Lauge Møller (Lauge the miller) at Klare Mølle in Roskilde, the university's tenant, and Peder Møller (Peder the miller), Tycho's man, who ligger til Kongernis cappell — "belongs to / is attached to the Kings' Chapel" (Kongernes Kapel, the royal burial chapel at Roskilde Cathedral, with whose mill-rights Tycho was associated). The surname Møller in both cases is the occupational designation "the miller," not a fixed family name; left untranslated in the body with the gloss "[the miller]" on first occurrence.
  • Mogens Bertelsen (Mogens Bertellßønn), forstander y hospitalidt — the warden (steward) of the hospital in Copenhagen, through whom Tycho relayed his initial reply; identified by office.
  • Latin legal maxim (the rhetorical and legal centre of the letter): quia (uti scitis) audienda etiam altera pars, et convincenda antequam puniatur, et procedendum secundum leges receptas, si aliter causa transigi nequeat. Rendered "because, as you know, the other party too must be heard, and must be convicted before being punished, and one must proceed according to the received laws, if the case cannot be settled otherwise." This is the audiatur et altera pars principle in gerundive form; the three gerundives (audienda … convincenda … procedendum) carry the force of obligation ("must be heard / must be convicted / one must proceed"). The closing conditional si aliter causa transigi nequeat — "if the case cannot be settled otherwise" — qualifies only the procedendum clause (recourse to formal law as a last resort, after amicable settlement fails); the deponent nequeat is a genuine negative ("cannot"), preserved.
  • secundum strictum jus — "according to strict law"; secundum leges receptas — "according to the received laws." Tycho contrasts the university's amicable forewarning with proceeding by strict right, and thanks them for the former.
  • Negation count and legal force. The body's substantive negations were tracked and reproduced one-for-one: (1) icke forholle — "[I] cannot withhold"; (2) ieg icke saa strax … kunde beskylle och straffe — "I could not so straightway accuse and punish"; (3) aldelis vhørt oc foruden nøyagtig beuisning — "altogether unheard and without sufficient proof" (two privatives, both kept); (4) icke siunis … at kunde følge skell — "does not seem … able to satisfy reason"; (5) icke will lade sig nøye — "will not be content"; (6) y skulle ingelunde giøre mig imod — "you shall by no means act against me"; (7) Jeg will icke heller haffue wiidere der imod att siige — "Neither will I have anything further to say against it"; (8) si aliter causa transigi nequeat — "if the case cannot be settled otherwise." No negation was added or dropped.
  • thing (werne thing, deris werne thing) — the thing, the local legal assembly/court of competent venue; werne = værne(-), "[their] proper/protective [venue]." Rendered "their proper venue (thing)."
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1594). thillschreffuit = tilskrevet ("written to"); synderlige = synderlige ("especial, particular"); beuiist gode = bevist gode ("kindness shown"); forskylle = forskylde ("repay, requite"); thilckiende … giffue = give til kende ("give to understand, make known"); thillhende kommen = til hænde kommen ("come to hand"); klagemaall = klagemål ("complaint, grievance"); foruretted = forurettet ("wronged"); tilltale for retten = "proceed against / arraign at law"; respiit = respit ("respite, time allowed"); giensuar oc replication = "answer and reply (rejoinder)"; skell = skel ("reason, equity, what is right"); gienpart = genpart (here "the counterpart / opposing party"); legittime citeret oc deciderit = a Danish–Latin code-switch, "lawfully summoned and decided"; billigt och ret = "fair and just"; befahledt = befalet ("commended").
  • Code-switching. The letter is Danish throughout with three Latin intrusions of rising formality: the address rubric (Magnifico D:no Rectori … Professoribus), the embedded legal maxim, and the tag secundum strictum jus / legittime … deciderit — all preserved by the edition's convention, the maxim translated in brackets after the Latin.
  • Calendar / place. Datum Vraniborg den 7. dag decembris a:o 94 = "Given at Uraniborg, the 7th day of December, [15]94" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The edition label ("1594. 7. december. Uraniborg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
