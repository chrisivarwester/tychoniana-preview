The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD ESCHILLUM BILLE].

MINN gandske wenlig hilsen nu och allthiid forsend met Vor Herre. Kierre Eske Bilde och kierre broder, Gud allmechtigste beuare dig nu och allthiid naadelligen och vell fran allt ont, och nest megen tacksigelße for all hulhet och gode, som du mig stedtze giort och beuist haffuer, huilket du skallt allthiid finde mig gandske godwillig aff formuen att forskylde y hues maade ieg kan giørre nogit, som dig till aerre och gode kan werre. Kierre Eske, bekom ieg din skriffuelße met min søn Tyge, som kom hid hiem till mig igien lidet epther nøtaarsdag, och formercker først derudaff, att diße gode willkord, som Vor Herre haffuer forsønid mig met her hoß rom. key. may., haffuer well behagit dig och mine andre wenner derudj landet. Althing er Gudskeloff y samme wesen epther som ieg y min skriffuelße da formeldit, och haabis, att det endnu kan bliffue bedre, Gud giffue widre løcke och benedidelße. Keyseren haffuer icke verrid till Prag vdj itt halff aar, men er wentendis did igien, naar paske er gangen. Her haffuer werrid so skrøfuellige en thiid met søgdom y forganget aar, saa hans may. tord icke bliffue vdj slotid till Prag, och ieg war epther hanß may. naadigst beuilling dragen hen paa itt andit aff hanß may. huß, ved naffn Girsitz, paa 6 eller 7 vgers thiid att giøre, och skreff dig till derfran y nouemb. maanid, huilket mit breff ieg forseer mig till, att du siden haffuer bekommit, horvdj ieg formener mig att haffue suarid till en part aff dett, som din skriffuelße nu formelder, som tillbage war. Jeg formercker, att min søn Tyge haffuer bekommit alle mine jnstrumenter der vd aff landit, och att die nu erre till Lybch eller Hamburgk, och forhaabis met første muelighed att bekomme dennom hidt op till mig formedelst Elbstromen, epthersom key. may. i forgangit aar haffuer naadigst skreffuet die herrer vdj Hamburgk till derom; die andre, som ieg haffde vd met mig, erre endnu mesten parten till Magdeburgk met en stor hoff aff mit andit gotz. Die herrer aff Magdeburgk fant paa itt juristisk vdloff, att die icke skulle lade føre det alltsammen hid for winttern paa deris bekostning, epthersom hanß may. haffde ocsesaa thuenne gange skreffuit dennom till derudom. Nu giorde die det gerne, dersom die motte, menß hans may. tog dett so hart till møstøcke, att die tillforn weirede dennom derudj, att han nu inted will haffue det giort aff dennom, menß haffuer skreffuit dommherrene y Magdeburgß kapitell till derom, huilke gierne wille epterkom hans may. naadigst begering, som die och goduilligen hauffue det tillbøid, førend die fick breffuit. Forhaabis derfor att bekomme det hid met det første paa deris bekaastning.

Die 7 hundrid thaller, som du haffde forskaffet till mig, bekom ieg icke førend nogit for forgangen jiull, saa lenge haffde die werrid paa weien eblant kiøbmennen.

See till, att du kanst faa stillid fru Citzelle Orne till fridß hueß hoffuidstollen bellanger; rentten tør du slet inted ved att giffue hinder, ieg vill før haffue en dom der paa, och formener ieg endnu, att hinder bør att stande mig till rette for die 5 hundrid thaller, som jomfru Jehanne och mig var imellom, eptherdj ieg er icke anderledis førmuit derudj, end met det breff, hun gaff mig, som du haffuer; haffde ieg vist, att Citzelle Orne icke wille lade sig nøye dermet, da [ha]fde[?] ieg well bekommit anden følist aff j. Jehanne derfor, ey heller haffuer ieg vist tillforn aff [den] herristing-dom, Citzelle haffuer forhuervid, lader mig icke heller nøye dermed; thi ieg formenner, att den ganger icke mig saa møgit an som medarffuene indbørdis, huilke derudi haffde beuilliget och samtøckt mig wuedendis, ey heller befindes y raadsentz contract nocsom met alle sin omstend der att werre indført; menß kan hun wisse nogen quitanze aff jomfru Oxe, Johanne vdj sehr, att hun quiterer hinder for die penge, mig var beuillid, kan ieg well lade mig nøye dermedt. Haffde det so werit, da haffde jomfru Jehanne well ladit mig vist dett y hindis leffuendis liff och tagid Citzillis hanßskrifft aff mig vdigien, och y andre moder stillid mig tillfridz, saa att ieg deraff kunde haffue betallt Citzille die 5 hundrid thaller, menß eptherdj bode hun och Frerich tagde stille dermed, och inted kreffuid mig dennom all den stund Frederich leffte, wiste ieg derfor icke andit end die lod dennom nøye medt att quitte samme sum emod Citzillis handskrifft. Jeg beder dig gierne, att du wille talle flittelligen met Axell Gyldenstiern herudom, huad gode raad han giffuer, att mig icke skall ske for kortt y nogen maade, och dersom ieg ingenlunde kune faa min opretning y de penge, som ieg war s[al.] Albritt skyldig vdj saa maade, om ieg icke da kunde erlange det hoß j. Jehannis arffuinge, saa at ieg kunde bekomme tillbørlig vederlag vdigien for saadan skade. Dersom dig ocsaa gott sønis att werre, daa kunde man tage wenner paa begge sider, som skilde Citzelle och mig ad herudom. Det breff, du formelder du haffde ladit min søn seet, som ieg skulle haffue skreffuet Citzelle till herom vnder min egen hon, motte ieg gierne wijde, horledis det løder och nor det er daterit, kandske det er sked førend ieg fick hinders breff aff j. Jehanne; om saa er, da er der got att suare till, ieg ved vell, att ieg ville hafft pengene aff j. Jehanne att bethalle Citzelle met, for hun manid mig nogid epther Gud haffde kaldit Albrit Ochse, och da haffuer ieg vden thuiffuel skreffuit hinder saadant itt breff till. Menß siden ieg fick det andit aff jomfru Jehanne att sette der imod, haffuer hun neppe bekommit saadan besken aff mig, menß ieg haffuer vden thuill skreffuid hinder det samme till, som ieg nu stor paa, dersom hun ellers wille lade saadanne breffue komme frem. Giør well och send mig en copi aff mit breff, som du formelder om, saa will ieg lade dig wijde wijdre besken derpaa, epthersom ieg mig kan erindre. Jeg haffde achtid mig did ind till herredagene nu vdj aar, menß ieg formerker aff din skriffuelße, att man er icke wiß paa, om der bliffuer nogen herredage vdj dette aar, eptherdj den siste stod saa langt vd paa aarid. Dertillmed kan ieg icke fo forloff aff røm. kay. may. y dette første aar att drage so langt her fran, eyheller er min handell endnu her nochsom anrettid, saa ieg haffuer en stor part vd paa sommeren att giørre dermed, menß dersom alltingest ganger well till, achter ieg, nest Gudz hielp, thill den anden herredag y det nestkommendis aar att komme did ind och thalle met mine slegt och wenner och vdrette mine bestillinger, epthersom behoff giøris. Forseer mig till, ad ieg da will bekomme forloff aff hans may. paa en otte eller 10 vgers thiid att giørre. Det, du sagde till min søn Tyge, haffuer ieg slet ingen fare for, thi om saa skede, horthill man dog ingen rett haffuer, bleff der vell den, som tog mig y forsuar, och lod mig ingen vrett vederfaris, eller ramte det paa en anden mode vdigien, om die wille giøre nogen geuald, och ieg achter att haffue saadan breffue met mig, naar Gud vill ieg kommer, att man vell skall betencke sig huad man giør. Haffuer nogen mig nogit till att thalle, da giør sig det for vpartiske dommer, saa skulle die vel bekomme suar. Jeg achter endnu att haffue nogen aff mine penge vdigien, som ieg sette derudi landit, och mente att skulle haffue hafft anden tack och vederlag derfor igien, end som sked er, menß allthing haffuer sin thiid, der tør engang bliffue raad til wraad.

Du skallt haffue tack, att du wille forskaffue die rentte-penge till Lantheholsten, huilket ieg forseer mig att werre sked. Och som du giør fortrøstning att wille fli mig mit hoffuid-breff vdigien, som stor vdj Lantheholsten, paa det ieg dermed kunde opkreffue och igien bekomme die penge, ieg haffuer stoendiß y Lanthemeckelburg, so giør du mig dermet en sønderlige thienist, och forlader mig wisselligen dertill, att du det vdrettennis vorder, thi mig ligger storligen macht derpaa. Ieg sender dig herhos epther din begering copier aff begge breffuene, bade horledis det, som ieg haffuer bekommit y Lanthemeckelburg och ocsaa det, som ieg haffuer vdgiffuit y Lantheholsten, løder, hort du kanst wijde dig epther ad rette. Och lad so strax sige pengene op vdj Lanthemeckelburg, paa det att ieg kan bekomme dennom vdj thiide, ieg achter met Gudz hielp att giørre mig bedre gaffn met dennom herudj landit, epthersom ieg thillforn haffuer skreffuit dig besken till derom. Och skreff ieg dig ocsaa siste gang till fra det bemelte keysernß hus Girtzis, vdj hues mode ieg mente, att du kunde bekomme samme sum penge att løße breffuid ind y Lantheholsten met, bode aff die, ieg haffuer stoendis hos Axell Gyldenstiern, som han haffuer wist atter igien loffuit, att ieg skall bekomme till omslagid, och ieg paa nø haffuer skreffuet hanom till derom, huilket breff ieg sender dig herhos, att du hannom det met første leylighed kan forskaffue. Kan du och tage dertill, huad du kanst fo for min gaar y Kiøbenhaffn och hues andit, du weest som mig kan tillkomme, och hues der bliffuer thilloffuers, som man icke so snart kan affsted komme, will ieg forrente y Lantheholsten, och giffue 6 aff hundrid, som nu der sedwonligt er; du forskaffuer mig vell nogen wisse forloffuerre, huilke ieg vill holde skadisløß vdj alle mode.

Som du skriffuer mig om min stott, du haffuer ladit ført offuer till Skonne, och icke enda viste, horledis den kunde bliffue sold, so vill ieg lade dig selffuer rode, bode for saadant och andit, thi ieg tuill inted paa, att du io berammer det gaffnligen och well.

Die heste, du haffuer forskaffid mig, skallt du haffue tack for, endog die erre vell døre, man bekomer bedre kiøb paa heste her vdj landit, menß ieg lader mig dog gierne nøye met hues du giør. Den ene, som du bekom aff Oluff Bilde till mig selff, behager mig vell, han so nogit illde vd dett første gang, han var kommen hid, och haffde forlorid sitt trau paa weien, menß han kommer sig nu igien bade paa hulden och trauwid. Jeg vill beholde den for mig sielff att riide paa vdi Prag och andenstes, saa ingen skall lettelligen bekomme [den] aff mig, som du ocsaa raader, och skalt du haffue megen tack for den smo[k]e lille kuschwogen, som du foraerid mig. Hueß foller der er paa Huaen igien, som mig tillkommer, kanst du giør aff huad dig selff løster, dog so ieg heller, att du forhandlid dennom hoß andre end hoß bønderne der paa landit. Anlangendis die fiske paa Huaen, som ieg wille haffue insaltid, beretter Tyge for mig, att han haffuer ladit indsaltid deraf ved 14 tønner; ieg haffde førment mange fler der att bekomme, mens du lader vell tillsee bode met det och andit, saa att allting ganger lidelligen och vell till, och att der bliffuer sett smo ley igien aff karper och anden smaa fisk y die største fiskewan och hor ellers behoff giøris.

Dauid Pedersønß regenskab och hues dett angaar kan ieg icke nu forskaffue dig, thi det er blant mit andit gotz, som foruaris till Magdeburg. Menß du fanger vell dog raad till hannom, epthersom du vdenad ved besken om hans handell.

Er ocsaa Diderich farrers quindis regenskab der hoß det andit, so att ieg ey heller kan sende dig det, du vest vell selff, horledis det var raderid och forfalskid, horudaff det er nochsom att forstaa, hor gott det andit var, naar hun haffuer giort klart for nogen laester rug och bøch, som hun y nogle aar haffde y hinders foruaring, och feillede alltid 4 eller 5 skepper paa huer pun, naar det kom till Huaen, so vill ieg først suare till hinders selffdictede regenskab. Hun kan wijsse hinders quitantze paa samme rug och buch, och hun wed well, att ieg giørde hinders man y hanß leffuendis liff icke ringe mangell derudj, som skede met all billighed, och ieg dog ingen oprettning haffuer siden bekom[met] vdigien.

Jeg ved presten paa Huaen slett inted till ville, enten met smeden, som ieg lod bøgge der for mine egne penge, eller y andre moder, thi han inted saadant haffuer forskylt aff mig; skulle han lønnis epther sin forskyldning, da skulle man anderledis handle met hannom, eyheller er han kommen did ing med min ville och samtøcke, som borde fornemlig att raade derfore. Haffuer Dauid Pedersøn giort der nogit vdj, da er det mig vuendis. Morten deyn siger dig vell beskening, horledis samme prest haffuer beted sig imod mig, siden han kom der, om han ellers vill rett vd dermed. Prestegaaren lod bønderne selff forfalle och stall bort en stor part deraff imod mit forbod, som Suen Trussen vell ved, om han ellers vill bestaa sanden, och ieg vntte presten en anden plaß, som ieg selff haffde kiøbt och hialp till att lade bøge, och skulle vell haffue bleffuit bedre fuldkommit, haffde ieg icke bleffuit foraarsagit att drage derfran. Att lagaarden forfaller och att sloted bliffuer ocsesaa brøstfeldig, ved ieg icke andit att suare dig till, end som ieg tillforn haffuer skreffuit, att ieg icke er tilsindz att giørre der nogen ødermere bekostning paa; det er alt formøgit, som sked er tillforn, haffde ieg die penge vdigien, da skulle die neppe bliffue mere so ilde sted.

Kierre Eske, som du formelder y dine postscriptis, siden du vast igen kommen till Kiøbenhaffn, om min gaar der y bøen, att du kanst icke bekomme offuer 15 hundrid thaller derfor aff die frower, so koster hand mig aff begøndelsen att lade bøgge offuer die 2 thussend foruden plaßen och die andre huß, der stod tillforn, och lod ieg forbedre och wijdre bekoste derpaa, nogit førend ieg drog aff landit, for thussend thaller y det minste, som Michel Wibe en stor part ved besken om. Den klocke, der henger y den lille spir, ieg lod giørre offuer karnappit, ville ieg, att du met det første lod nedertage och sent til Lybch til Jochum Buch, thi den hører till seieruerkid, som er i Lybch, so ieg vill dog icke, att hun skall bliffue der, menß ieg wed her anderledis att bringe den till nøtte. Som ieg och formercker, att itt aff mine seierwerch, som wiser minuterne, bleff ochsaa tillbage paa Huaen, huilket Tyge bereter, att du haffuer lont Jørgen Brockenhus att lade giørre itt andit epther, ville ieg ocsaa gierne haffue det igien, thi ieg haffuer det her ibehoff vdi min obseruation, om ieg haffde endskønt flere saadanne. Du kanst lade en seyermager packke det sammen vdj en lille firkantt kiste och sende det ocsesaa till Lybch till Jochum Buch, thi ieg tencker vell, att Jørgen Brockenhus haffuer nu vel brugt det saa møyet han haffuer ybehoff. Menß dersom han kan icke fo itt andit der giort, och ville gierne beholle det, daa maa du lade hannom haffue det, thi han er min gode wen och tør vndertiden sige sanningen for min skyld. Jeg so selff gierne, att han wille lade giøre sig nogen instrumenter och dermed obseruere nogit derwdj landit, epthersom han sagde till Tyge sig att wille giørre, och naar ieg for att wjde, att det er hans allffwor, will ieg skriffue hanom en instruction, huad huert aar y besøndellighed er att obseruere. Om die andre, som inted forstaar sig paa saadant, ville verre sposk derudoffuer, skall han inted paasse derpaa, eyheller suare dennom nogit dertill. Kunne han engang komme hid vd till mig, da wille ieg vell paa en kort thiid so rette hannom an derudj, att han skulle kende sig gott deraff, och siden dis lettere och løcckelliger gange om dermet, huilket du kanst sige hannom paa mine wegne, naar du kommer y taall met hannom. Jeg lader ocsaa paa attskillige steder vdj Tydsland, formedelst dennom, som haffuer løst dertill, rette saadant an. Och er der nogen aff die fornemme herrer vdj Prag, som allerede haffuer ladet giort sig nogen instrumenter epther de, som ieg haffuer hid met mig, och wille der lade obseruere dermedt, och haffuer keyserlige may. selff nogen vdi forraad och achter flere att lade giørre epther nogen aff mine, nor die alle ankommer, huilke hans may. will haffue paa slotid vdj Prag for sig selff och huem han dertill vdj sønderlighed will bruge, thi hans may. haffuer stor løst och behagellighed till saadant, och er och tillsindz strax epther paaske, att sende sin fornembste vrmacher hid till mig, som er en sønderlig kønstler, huilken hoß mig skall forfare allehonne, som der till hører, och siden giørre saadant for hanß may.

Kierre Eske och kierre broder, fortenck mig icke, att ieg saa thitt bemøder dig met min handell, ieg haffuer ingen anden derudj landit, som will haffue saa møgen wmag for mig; dersom ieg vdi nogen mode kan tienne dig vdigien, skall du finde mig mere end villig dertill. Och vill hermed nu och allthiid haffue dig Gud allmechtigste befallit, han beuar dig fran allt, dig kan werre skadelligt enten till saell eller liff. Giør well och sig din kierre fader och moder met alle andre voris gode wenner mange gode netter paa mine wegne.

Daterit vdj Lanthebemen paa det keyserlige slot Benatky den 22 dag martij anno 1600

[Tycho Brahe to Eske Bille.]

My altogether friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with Our Lord. Dear Eske Bille and dear brother, may God Almighty guard you now and always, graciously and well, from all evil; and next, with much thanksgiving for all the kindness and good that you have ever done and shown me — for which you shall always find me quite willing, to the best of my ability, to requite it in whatever way I can do anything that may be for your honour and good. Dear Eske, I received your letter by my son Tyge, who came home to me again a little after New Year's Day; and I note first from it that these good conditions which Our Lord has provided me with here, with the Roman Imperial Majesty, have well pleased you and my other friends in the country. Everything is, God be praised, in the same state as I reported in my letter then, and it is to be hoped that it may yet become better — God grant further fortune and blessing. The Emperor has not been at Prague for half a year, but is expected there again when Easter is past. There has been so sickly a time with disease in the past year that His Majesty durst not stay in the castle at Prague; and I had, by His Majesty's gracious permission, gone away to another of His Majesty's houses, by the name of Girsitz, for some six or seven weeks' stay, and wrote to you from there in the month of November — which letter of mine I expect you have since received, wherein I think I answered a part of what your letter now reports, which was [still] outstanding. I note that my son Tyge has got all my instruments out of the country, and that they are now at Lübeck or Hamburg, and I hope to get them up here to me at the first opportunity by the Elbe stream, since His Imperial Majesty in the past year graciously wrote to the lords of Hamburg about it. The others, which I had out with me, are still for the most part at Magdeburg, with a great heap of my other goods. The lords of Magdeburg hit upon a juridical evasion, that they should not have it all conveyed here at their expense before winter, although His Majesty had also twice written to them about it. Now they would gladly do it, if they might; but His Majesty took it so much amiss that they had earlier refused them in it, that he now will not have it done by them, but has written to the canons in the chapter of Magdeburg about it — who will gladly comply with His Majesty's gracious request, as they had also of their own goodwill offered it before they received the letter. I hope, therefore, to get it here at the first [chance], at their expense.

The 700 thalers that you had procured for me, I did not receive until somewhat before last Christmas — so long had they been on the way among the merchants.

See to it that you can get Lady Cecilie Urne satisfied as concerns the principal; the interest you must by no means venture to give her — I will first have a judgment upon it; and I still hold that she ought to answer to me for the 500 thalers that were between Mistress Johanne and me, since I am not otherwise secured in it than by the deed she gave me, which you hold. Had I known that Cecilie Urne would not content herself therewith, I should well have obtained other security from Mistress Johanne for it; nor had I known before of the district-court judgment that Cecilie has obtained — nor do I content myself with it, for I hold that it concerns not so much me as the co-heirs among themselves, who had therein consented and agreed without my knowledge; nor is it found to be entered in the town-council contract sufficiently, with all its circumstances. But if she can show any quittance from Mistress Oxe — Johanne in particular — that she acquits her [Cecilie] for the money that was granted to me, I can well content myself therewith. Had it been so, then Mistress Johanne would surely have let me know it in her lifetime, and taken Cecilie's bond back from me, and in other ways set me at ease, so that I could therewith have paid Cecilie the 500 thalers; but since both she and Frederik kept silent about it, and never demanded them of me all the while Frederik lived, I therefore knew nothing else than that they were content to acquit that same sum against Cecilie's bond. I earnestly ask you to speak diligently with Axel Gyldenstierne about this — what good counsel he gives, that I be not short-changed in any way; and if I could in no way get my settlement in the money I owed the late Albret in this manner, [I ask] whether I might not then obtain it from Mistress Johanne's heirs, so that I might get due compensation again for such loss. If it also seem good to you, then one might take friends [as arbitrators] on both sides, to settle the matter between Cecilie and me. The letter that you mention you had shown my son — which I am supposed to have written to Cecilie about this under my own hand — I should gladly know how it reads and when it is dated; perhaps it was done before I received her deed from Mistress Johanne. If so, then there is a good answer to it: I know well that I wished to have the money from Mistress Johanne to pay Cecilie with, for she dunned me somewhat after God had called Albret Oxe [to Him], and then I have doubtless written her such a letter. But since I received the other [deed] from Mistress Johanne to set against it, she has hardly received such word from me; rather I have doubtless written to her the same thing that I now insist on — if only she would let such letters come forth. Be so good as to send me a copy of my letter that you mention, and I will let you know further word on it, as I can recall. I had intended to go in to the Diet this year, but I gather from your letter that one is not sure whether there will be any Diet this year, since the last stood so late in the year. Moreover, I cannot get leave from the Roman Imperial Majesty this first year to travel so far from here; nor is my work here yet sufficiently set up, so that I have a great part of it [to do] out into the summer. But if all goes well, I intend, with God's help, to come in to the next Diet in the coming year, and to speak with my kindred and friends and dispatch my affairs, as need requires. I expect that I shall then get leave from His Majesty for some eight or ten weeks. What you said to my son Tyge, I have no fear of at all; for if it should so happen — to which no one has any right — there would surely be one who would take me into his protection and let no wrong befall me, or would requite it in another way, should they try to do any violence; and I intend to have such letters [of imperial protection] with me, when, God willing, I come, that one shall well think twice about what one does. If anyone has anything to charge against me, let him bring it before impartial judges, then they shall surely get an answer. I still intend to recover some of my money that I laid out in the country, having thought I should have had other thanks and recompense for it than has happened; but everything has its time — there may yet at last be a remedy for the mischief.

Thanks to you for procuring the interest-money to Holstein, which I trust has been done. And as you give assurance that you will get me back my principal-bond, which stands in Holstein, so that I might therewith call in and recover the money I have standing in Mecklenburg — you do me thereby a special service, and I rely confidently on you that it shall be carried out, for it greatly concerns me. I send you herewith, at your request, copies of both bonds — both how the one I received in Mecklenburg, and also the one I issued in Holstein, reads — so that you may know how to govern yourself accordingly. And so have the money called up at once in Mecklenburg, so that I may receive it in time; I intend, with God's help, to make better use of it here in this land, as I have written you word before. And I also wrote to you last time from the said Emperor's house Girtzis, in what manner I thought you could obtain the same sum of money to redeem the bond in Holstein — partly from what I have standing with Axel Gyldenstierne, which he has again promised that I shall receive by the term-market [Umschlag] — and I have written to him anew about it, which letter I send you herewith, that you may convey it to him at the first opportunity. You may also add to it whatever you can get for my house in Copenhagen, and whatever else you know may belong to me; and whatever is left over, which one cannot so soon dispose of, I will put out at interest in Holstein, and give 6 per hundred, as is now customary there; you will surely procure me some reliable sureties, whom I will hold harmless in every way.

As you write to me about my stud, which you have had carried over to Scania, and did not yet know how it could be sold — I will leave it to you yourself to decide, both for that and for other things, for I doubt not that you will arrange it profitably and well.

The horses you have procured for me, thanks to you for them, although they are quite dear — one gets a better bargain on horses here in this land — but I gladly content myself with what you do. The one you got from Oluf Bilde for myself pleases me well; he looked somewhat ill the first time he had come here, and had lost his trot on the way, but he is now recovering both in flesh and in his trot. I will keep him for myself, to ride on in Prague and elsewhere, so that no one shall easily get him from me, as you also advise; and many thanks to you for the fine little coach-wagon that you presented me. Whatever foals there are left on Hven that belong to me, you may do with as you yourself please — though I would rather you traded them to others than to the peasants there on the island. Concerning the fish on Hven that I wanted salted down, Tyge reports to me that he has had about 14 barrels of them salted; I had expected to get many more there, but you will surely see to both that and other matters, so that everything goes tolerably and well, and that small fry of carp and other little fish be set out again in the largest fishponds, and wherever else there is need.

David Pedersen's accounts, and what concerns them, I cannot now furnish you, for they are among my other goods that are kept at Magdeburg. But you will surely manage him, since you know by heart the account of his dealings.

Diderich the ferryman's wife's accounts are also there with the rest, so that I cannot send you those either. You well know yourself how it was erased and falsified — from which one may sufficiently understand how good the rest was — when she has rendered account for some lasts of rye and barley that she had in her keeping for some years, and there was always a shortfall of 4 or 5 bushels on each [measure] when it came to Hven; so I will first answer to her self-invented account. She can show her quittance for the same rye and barley; and she knows well that I charged her husband, in his lifetime, with no small deficiency in it — which was done with all fairness — and yet I have since received no settlement for it.

I owe the priest on Hven nothing at all, either with the smithy that I had built there at my own expense, or in any other way, for he has deserved no such thing from me. Were he to be rewarded according to his deserts, one should deal otherwise with him; nor did he come in there with my will and consent, which ought chiefly to have governed it. If David Pedersen has done anything in it, it is unknown to me. Morten the deacon will surely tell you the account of how that same priest has behaved toward me since he came there, if only he will out with it honestly. The peasants themselves let the parsonage fall into ruin, and stole away a great part of it against my prohibition, as Sven Trussen well knows, if only he will stand by the truth; and I granted the priest another plot, which I had myself bought and helped to have built, and which would surely have been better finished, had I not been compelled to depart thence. That the home-farm falls into decay, and that the castle too becomes dilapidated — I know nothing else to answer you than what I have written before: that I am not minded to make any further expense upon it. It is all too much that has been spent before; had I that money back, they would hardly be left so badly off.

Dear Eske, as you mention in your postscripts, since you had come back to Copenhagen, concerning my house there in the town — that you cannot get over 1,500 thalers for it from the ladies: it cost me, from the beginning, over 2,000 to have built, besides the plot and the other houses that stood there before; and I had it improved and spent further upon it, somewhat before I left the country, to the tune of a thousand thalers at the least, of which Mikkel Vibe knows a great part. The bell that hangs in the little spire I had made over the oriel — I would that you have it taken down at the first [opportunity] and sent to Lübeck to Jochim Buck, for it belongs to the clockwork that is in Lübeck; for I do not wish it to remain there, but I know how to put it to use here otherwise. As I also gather that one of my clocks, which shows the minutes, was likewise left behind on Hven — which Tyge reports you have lent to Jørgen Brockenhuus to have another made after it — I should also gladly have it back, for I have need of it here in my observation, even though I had several such. You can have a clockmaker pack it up in a little square chest and send it likewise to Lübeck to Jochim Buck, for I well think that Jørgen Brockenhuus has now used it as much as he needs. But if he cannot get another made there, and would gladly keep it, then you may let him have it, for he is my good friend and dares sometimes to tell the truth for my sake. I should myself be glad if he would have some instruments made for himself and observe somewhat therewith in the country, as he told Tyge he would do; and when I come to know that he is in earnest about it, I will write him an instruction [as to] what is in particular to be observed each year. If the others, who understand nothing of such things, would be mocking about it, he should pay no heed to it, nor answer them anything. Could he once come out here to me, I would well, in a short time, so set him up in it that he should profit well from it, and thereafter go about it the more easily and successfully — which you may tell him on my behalf, when you come into talk with him. I am also having such things set up in various places in Germany, through those who have a taste for it. And there are some of the noble lords in Prague who have already had instruments made for themselves after those I have here with me, and would observe therewith; and His Imperial Majesty himself has some in store, and intends to have more made after some of mine, when they all arrive — which His Majesty will have at the castle in Prague for himself and for whomever he will particularly use for it, for His Majesty has great delight and pleasure in such things; and he is also minded, right after Easter, to send his chief clockmaker here to me — who is a special artificer — who shall learn from me all that pertains to it, and afterwards make such things for His Majesty.

Dear Eske and dear brother, do not think ill of me that I so often trouble you with my affairs; I have no other in the country who will take so much pains for me. If I can in any way serve you in return, you shall find me more than willing for it. And herewith I commend you, now and always, to God Almighty; may He guard you from all that may be harmful to you, whether to your weal or your life. Be so good as to bid your dear father and mother, with all our other good friends, many good nights on my behalf.

Dated in the land of Bohemia, at the imperial castle Benátky, the 22nd day of March, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Edition of record. Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia vol. VIII (Epistolae). MS witness: A = Basel, Universitätsbibliothek, Autographensammlung Vol. 35, Fol. 56–63, marked Kladde — Tycho's own draft. (The heading [Tycho Brahe ad Eschillum Bille] is the editor's.)
  • Date / place. Dateline "den 22 dag martij anno 1600," from "det keyserlige slot Benatky" in "Lanthebemen" (the land of Bohemia); the catalogue label confirms New Style ("22. marts n.s."). It is the third of three letters of the same week — to Sophie Brahe (21 March, brahe-t_16000321001), to Niels Krag (24 March, brahe-t_16000324002), and to Eske Bille here (22 March) — all carrying the same core news (the plague, Rudolf II's favour, the instruments stranded in Germany behind the frozen Elbe, the Magdeburg refusal). This letter is the estate-and-property member of the trio.
  • Recipient. Eske Bille (Eschillus Bille), a Danish nobleman, Tycho's kinsman ("kierre broder") and the chief administrator of his Danish affairs after the emigration. Tycho calls him "the only one in the country who will take so much pains for me."
  • Biographical significance — the winding-up of Hven. This is among the most important documents of Tycho's liquidation of his Danish establishment:
  • The instruments leave Denmark. Son Tyge has shipped all the instruments out; some are at Lübeck/Hamburg, the rest (with "a great heap of my other goods") at Magdeburg, awaiting transport up the Elbe to Bohemia — the Magdeburg city authorities' "juridical evasion" and Rudolf II's angry redirection to the cathedral chapter match the account given to Krag (brahe-t_16000324002).
  • Uraniborg in decay. Tycho flatly records that "the home-farm falls into decay, and the castle [Uraniborg] too becomes dilapidated," and refuses to spend more — "it is all too much that has been spent before; had I that money back, they would hardly be left so badly off." A poignant primary witness to the abandonment of Uraniborg (1599–1600).
  • The clocks & the bell. The minute-showing clock (seierværk … som viser minuterne) left on Hven and lent to Jørgen Brockenhuus is recalled for Tycho's observations; the bell in the oriel spire is to be taken down and sent to Lübeck (to Jochim Buck) to rejoin its clockwork. Tycho's instrument-clocks are central to his observational programme.
  • The Copenhagen house & Danish capital. The town house (built for >2,000 thaler + c. 1,000 in improvements, per Mikkel Vibe) fetches only 1,500 from prospective lady-buyers; Tycho is calling in bonds in Holstein and Mecklenburg, drawing on money held by Axel Gyldenstierne (payable at the Kiel Umschlag term-market), and reinvesting surplus in Holstein at 6 %.
  • Diffusion of Tychonic instruments. Rudolf II keeps copies of Tycho's instruments at Prague castle and will have more made; Tycho is helping set up observing "in various places in Germany"; he encourages Brockenhuus as a Danish observer and offers a yearly observing-instruction. A vivid statement of the spread of Tycho's instrumental astronomy.
  • The Emperor's chief clockmaker. Rudolf II intends, "right after Easter," to send "his chief clockmaker, who is a special artificer," to learn the instrument-craft from Tycho — a notable datum on the technical exchange at the imperial court (the imperial Hofuhrmacher of this period; not named here, and not to be confidently equated with Jost Bürgi, whose Prague imperial appointment is documented somewhat later).
  • Persons named.
  • Tyge — Tycho Brahe the Younger (courier; oversaw the shipping of the instruments).
  • the Emperor / His Imperial Majesty — Rudolf II.
  • Cecilie Urne (fru Citzelle Orne), Mistress Johanne Oxe (jomfru Jehanne/Oxe), the late Albret Oxe (sal. Albrit Ochse), and Frederik (Frerich/Frederich) — the parties to a tangled debt-and-inheritance suit (a 500-thaler claim secured by a bond, a district-court judgment, co-heirs, a town-council contract, and a quittance). Their exact relationships are not spelled out in the letter; left as named.
  • Axel Gyldenstierne — Danish statesman (governor of Norway), Tycho's financial adviser and a holder of his funds.
  • Oluf Bilde — supplied a riding-horse; a kinsman of Eske Bille.
  • Mikkel Vibe (Michel Wibe) — Copenhagen official/merchant, witness to the cost of Tycho's town house.
  • Jochim Buck (Jochum Buch) of Lübeck — custodian of Tycho's clockwork.
  • Jørgen Brockenhuus — Danish nobleman and astronomy enthusiast, "my good friend [who] dares sometimes to tell the truth for my sake," borrower of the minute-clock.
  • David Pedersen (Dauid Pedersøn) — Tycho's steward/accountant (his accounts boxed up at Magdeburg).
  • Diderich the ferryman's wife — accused of falsified grain accounts on Hven.
  • Morten the deacon (Morten deyn) and Sven Truelsen (Suen Trussen) — Hven witnesses to the priest's conduct and the ruin of the parsonage.
  • the priest on Hven (unnamed) and the ladies (die frower) negotiating for the Copenhagen house.
  • Places. Benátky (Benatky); Prague; Girsitz / Girtzis — an imperial residence where Tycho stayed c. 6–7 weeks in Nov 1599 during the plague (a Bohemian crown estate near Benátky; precise identification uncertain); Lübeck, Hamburg, Magdeburg; Holstein (Lantheholsten) and Mecklenburg (Lanthemeckelburg), where Tycho's capital is bonded; the Kiel Umschlag (omslagid, the term-payment money-market); Copenhagen; Hven (Huaen, with Uraniborg); Scania (Skonne).
  • Reading cruxes. (1) "min stott … du haffuer ladit ført offuer till Skonne" — stott is ambiguous: most likely "stud" (a stod, a herd of breeding horses, consistent with the horse-and-foal matter that follows), though a "monument/column" (støtte) cannot be excluded; rendered "my stud" with this flag. (2) "[ha]fde[?]" in the Cecilie-Urne passage is an editor-conjectured reading. (3) "enten till saell eller liff" — saell read as sæl/sjæl, hence "weal" (or "soul"); rendered "weal."
  • Correction. The witness's surname was mistranslated as “Sven Truelsen”; both our source transcription and Dreyer's print (TBOO XIV p.188) consistently give “Sven Trussen”. Corrected.
