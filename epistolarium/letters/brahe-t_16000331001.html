The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD JACOBUM BORDINGUM].

Magnifice et excellentissime vir, amice inprimis honorande.

JNTELLEXI ex literis, quas per filium meum, circa initia hujus anni huc ad me revertentem, mihi scripsisti, posteriores meas ad jllustrissimum principem ducem Vldaricum, d:num meum clementissimum, tum quoque simul ad te datas, recte esse redditas, et de conditionibus, quas divino provisu apud sa:am cæs:am ma:tem, dominum nostrum clementissimum, adeptus sum, ipsius jll:mam cel:nem clementer et lubenti animo accepisse, atque tibi etiam hunc rerum mearum statum auditu fuisse perjucundum, quo nomine jll:mæ ejus cel:ni humillime gratias ago, et tibi quoque quam plurimas. Si quid postea mihi rescriptum est, quod futurum innuisti, quandoquidem tunc ob occupationum molem et filii festinum isthinc discessum fieri id nequibat, scias me eas literas non accepisse, uti etiam nullas a d:no Henrico Ramelio, affine et amico meo singulari, qui ad te suas missurum, cum mei in Dania emissarii essent, aiebat.

Cœterum quia tempus iam adest, quo interesse (uti vocant) pro decem millibus joachymicis, quos isthic jll:mis junioribus principibus mutuo dedi, numerandum sit, rogo peramanter, velis efficere, ut summam illam, quæ sexcentos adæquat, actutum obtineam. Et quia difficulter, nec etiam satis tuto in moneta argentea sive thaleris huc adferri poterit, optarem, ut in aureis Vngaricis solverentur, ita ut quilibet Vngaricus loco sesquialterius thaleri numeretur, sicque quadringenti tales aurei sexcentis illis joachymicis æquivalerent; talis enim est hujus monetæ aureæ in Dania valor, ita ut apud vos nihil aut admodum parum esse discriminis existimem. Poterint autem aurei illi commode pyxidi alicui includi et obsignari, sicque aurigæ Wittebergensi, ut eos bene conservet atque m. Jœstelio mathematico isthic professori, quamprimum Wittebergam venerit, tradat; is enim affinem quendam Dresdæ habet, qui postea totum illud ad me facile transmittet. Si Vngarici isthic tam cito in promptu non sint, poterint eorum loco mitti engelotti, quorum quilibet duobus joachymicis cum quadrante æquiparatur. Roseinobiles, uti etiam floreni Rhenani hic parum in usu sunt, ideoque eos nollem. Si nulla alia ratio supererit, istam monetam auream loco joachymicorum mihi debitorum exponendi, spero ill:mum principem ducem Vldaricum, pro ea qua ipsius cel:do erga me est clementissima affectione, facile sua authoritate et consilio effecturum (præsertim si ipsius cel:ni abs te hoc negotium meo nomine humillime expositum commendetur), ut desideriis meis hac in parte subveniatur. Si autem vobis aliqua alia commoditas perspecta esset, qua pecuniam illam in joachymicis jmperialibus hic certo et absque meo damno accipere queam, mihi id perinde erit, lubensque acquiescam. Chirographum interesse illud persolutum testans hisce inclusi, quod tuæ fidei committo. Neque enim dubito te, quem semper expertus sum mei studiosissimum, hanc curam, utut alias sis occupatissimus, non detrectaturum, quo absque diutina dilatione id, quicquid est, pecuniarum hic nancisci queam; sic me adhuc pluribus quam antea officiis, quibuscunque per me licuerit modis, vicissim compensandis, tibi reddideris quam devinctissimum.

Vale.
Dabantur ex arce cæsarea Benatica die ultimo martii stylo novo anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TYCHO BRAHE TO JACOB BORDING].

Magnificent and most excellent man, friend especially to be honoured.

I have understood, from the letter you wrote me by my son, returning hither to me about the beginning of this year, that my later [letters] to the most illustrious prince Duke Ulrich, my most clement lord, and at the same time to you, were duly delivered; and that, concerning the conditions which by divine provision I have obtained with the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement lord, his most illustrious Highness received [the news] graciously and with willing mind, and that to you too this state of my affairs was most pleasing to hear — on which account I give most humble thanks to his most illustrious Highness, and very many to you also. If anything was afterward written back to me — which you hinted would happen, though it could not then be done, because of the mass of occupations and my son's hasty departure from there — know that I have not received those letters; just as [I have received] none from Lord Henrik Ramel, my kinsman and especial friend, who said he would send his to you when my emissaries were in Denmark.

For the rest: since the time is now at hand when the interest (as they call it) for the ten thousand joachimsthalers which I lent there to the most illustrious younger princes is to be paid, I most lovingly ask that you would bring it about that I obtain at once that sum, which amounts to six hundred [thalers]. And because it can be brought hither with difficulty, and not safely enough, in silver money or thalers, I should wish that it be paid in Hungarian gold [ducats], such that each Hungarian [ducat] be reckoned in place of one and a half thalers, and so four hundred such gold pieces would equal those six hundred joachimsthalers — for such is the value of this gold money in Denmark, so that I think there is no difference, or very little, with you. Those gold pieces can conveniently be enclosed in some little box and sealed, and [given] thus to the Wittenberg coachman, that he keep them well and hand them to Master Jöstel, the mathematician [and] professor there, as soon as he comes to Wittenberg; for he has a certain kinsman at Dresden, who will then easily transmit the whole to me. If Hungarian [ducats] are not so quickly to be had there, engelots can be sent in their place, each of which is reckoned at two joachimsthalers and a quarter. Rose-nobles, like Rhenish florins, are little in use here, and therefore I should not want them. If no other way shall remain of laying out that gold money in place of the joachimsthalers owed me, I hope the most illustrious prince Duke Ulrich, by that most clement affection his Highness has toward me, will easily bring it about by his authority and counsel (especially if this business be humbly laid before and commended to his Highness by you in my name) that my wishes in this part be met. But if some other convenience be known to you, by which I may receive that money here in imperial joachimsthalers, surely and without my loss, it will be all the same to me, and I will gladly acquiesce. The receipt testifying that interest paid I have enclosed herewith, which I commit to your good faith. For I do not doubt that you — whom I have always found most devoted to me — will not refuse this care, however otherwise most occupied you be, so that without long delay I may obtain here that money, whatever it is; thus you will have bound me most closely to you, [the obligation] to be repaid in turn by more services than before, in whatever ways I shall be able.

Farewell.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the last day of March, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Jacob Bording, a leading man of Lübeck and Tycho's agent there (cf. the later Tycho→Bording letter, brahe-t_16010412001). The MS is an autograph draft (Kladde).
  • Position in the Mecklenburg debt-thread. This 31 March 1600 letter is the earliest of the surviving Bording letters and an early node of the whole 10,000-thaler affair (cf. brahe-t_16001115002 of 15 Nov 1600; the 1601 letters to Meier and Duke Ulrich). It first reports the good news of Tycho's imperial conditions (received gladly by Duke Ulrich and Bording — Tycho's letters had been delivered "by my son" returning early in 1600), then turns to the recurring task: collecting the 600-thaler semi-annual interest on the 10,000 joachimsthalers lent to the "younger princes of Mecklenburg."
  • The numismatic detail (notable for monetary history). Tycho specifies how he wants the 600 thalers paid, to ease safe transport to Bohemia:
  • Preferred: Hungarian gold ducats (aurei Ungarici) at 1½ thalers each, so 400 ducats = 600 joachimsthalers ("such is their value in Denmark").
  • Route: sealed in a little box, given to the Wittenberg coachman, handed to Master Jöstel (Melchior Jöstel, the Wittenberg mathematician; cf. brahe-t_16001008001), whose Dresden kinsman forwards the whole to Tycho.
  • Fallback: engelotti (English "angelots"/angels) at 2¼ thalers each; but rose-nobles and Rhenish florins "are little in use here," so he refuses them.
  • Failing all, Duke Ulrich's authority is to be invoked (via Bording), or the money taken "in imperial joachimsthalers" if safer. A receipt (chirographum) for the interest is enclosed.
  • Persons named. Jacob Bording; Duke Ulrich III of Mecklenburg; the "younger princes" (the late Duke Johann's sons; see brahe-t_16000920002); Henrik Ramel (Henricus Ramelius, Tycho's affinis and friend, a Pomeranian/Danish noble); Melchior Jöstel; Tycho's son (Tycho the Younger); Rudolf II (the imperial conditions).
  • Coin glossary (kept/glossed;). aurei Ungarici = Hungarian gold ducats; joachymici = joachimsthalers (the Joachimsthal silver thaler); engelotti = angelots / English angels (gold); roseinobiles = rose-nobles (English gold); floreni Rhenani = Rhenish florins/gulden.
  • Calendar. Dateline die ultimo Martii stylo novo anni 1600 — New Style (Prague/imperial). Header agrees with the label.
