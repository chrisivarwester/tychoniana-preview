The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS, VIRTUTE AC ERUDITIONE ILLUSTRI VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP ET URANIBURGO, FAUTORI SUO OMNI OBSERVANTIA COLENDO. S.

NISI quodammodo T. E. Generosissime Domine TYCHO mea erga T. E. debita observantia, et mihi tua in me benivolentia perspecta esset, vererer sane ne meum incusares officium, quod toto iam pene semestri nihil ad te literarum transmiserim. Id quod meae ingratitudini aut incuriae nequaquam ascribas velim. In ea siquidem locorum adyta hîc detrudimur, ad quae commeantibus hinc inde ad vos Tabellarijs haud ita crebro praebetur aditus. Scripsi tamen non ita dudum ad V. E. cum internuncio quodam Marpurgensi, qui Herbornae, assumpto Chartopaeo quodam, me convenerat. Is in Daniam, adhibito eiusdem Opificij comministro, se unâ confestim profecturum et te operam suam in Officina Chartacea aliquamdiu elocaturum spondebat. Verum trifurcifer iste Marpurgium rediens causa nescio qua clam se subduxit, meque ita praeter opinionem thaleris aliquot, quos pro commeatu isti nebuloni praenumeravi, callide emunxit. Sed patior hoc quieto quid est iacturae eo facilius, quod iam demum quid eiusmodi committendum sit agyrtis et vespertilionibus, meo qualicunque damno rectius sapiam. Caeterum CLAVIJ hunc in Sphaeram SACROBOSCI Commentarium ad te ablegavi ob tractatulum quendam de Nova Stella Anni 72 insertum. In quo multa, si recte memini, praesertim de generatione et situ eius Aethereo, idque inter Firmamenti Sidera, magnitudine primaeva apparenti etc. vestris non omnia dissona placitis adfert. Miror hominem Peripateticum a suae sectae Coriphaeo ARISTOTELE tam libere in plerisque his dissentire. Quo nomine CRAIGO illi Scoto iurato ARISTOTELIS mancipio, veritatis perduelli, longe, meo iudicio, anteponi merebitur. Vidi egomet non ita dudum Epistolas illius nonnullas ad te de negotio Cometico perscriptas. Inaudieram etiam a Conterraneis eius circumferri Capnuraniae istius nugamenta et cum doctis quibusdam communicari. Exoptant igitur mecum et expetunt quidam vestrarum laudum et lucubrationum studiosi, ut non tantum Apologiam Scoticis istis de Cometarum ortu, situ ac motu a vobis oppositam somnijs, sed etiam Lichnuraniam quandam, reliquas Capnuraniae illius tenebras ac densos fumos suis radijs discutientem, publici iuris faceretis. Sed quid ego noctuas Athenas? Catalogum vero hunc recentiorum auctorum addidi, si quos forte proximo mercato Francofurtensi ex ijs, qui hîc assignantur, novellis libris vobis per me coëmi expetiveritis. In quem usum, quas ex vestris pecunijs reliquas habeo, tum si quid ulterius sit erogandum, lubens impendam. Reperietis etiam his adiunctam terrae, ut vocant, sigillatae portiunculam quandam, quae tribus hinc circiter milliaribus ubertim effoditur. Quod vero OTTONI auctor et suasor fuerim, ut unâ ad te perscriberet, in meliorem partem interpreteris oro. Dudum siquidem a me petijt, ut sibi permitterem amoris et benevolentiae testificandae gratia, literas suas ad te daret. Id quod me illi tum concessurum spondebam ubi sine crassioribus mendis proprio Marte epistolam conficere valeret. Itaque iam aggressus est me suasore, et quidem felicius quam sperabam. Paucula a me limitata, nonnulla interpolata. Vides igitur non obscura ingenij hac praesertim aetate indicia. Quem in virum bonum ac doctum successu temporis pervasurum mihi persuadeo, si modo quemadmodum inceperit, ita in posterum inoffenso pede ad callem VIRTUTIS ac VERITATIS contenderit.

Vale praecipuum Patriae ornamentum, vita, ut dignus, quam diutissime, si quisquam, incolumi, meque tuo favore dignare.

Iterum quam felicissime vale.
Sigenae Nassoviorum 23. Octob. Anno 1594.
T. amplissimi nominis honoris
desiderij studioss.
CUNRADUS BERGENSIS.

Sciatis globum cœlestem secundum vestras observationes restitutum et affabre
solerterque elaboratum his nundinis prodijsse, magna sane multorum gratulatione.

To the man illustrious in nobility of birth, in virtue, and in learning, Master Tycho Brahe, lord of Knudstrup and of Uraniborg, his patron to be honoured with every mark of respect — greeting.

Were the respect I owe to Your Excellency, most noble Lord Tycho, and the goodwill of yours toward me which I have clearly perceived, not in some measure [assured], I should indeed fear that you might reproach my dutifulness, in that for almost a whole half-year now I have sent you no letter. This I would by no means have you ascribe to ingratitude or carelessness on my part. For we are thrust away here into such remote recesses of place that, to the couriers travelling to and fro to you, access is not so very often afforded. Yet I wrote to Your Excellency not so long ago by way of a certain Marburg messenger, who had met with me at Herborn, having taken on a certain paper-maker. He gave his word that he would set out together, at once, into Denmark — having engaged a fellow-workman of the same trade — and that he would hire out his labour to you for some while in [your] paper-mill. But that thrice-gallows-rogue, returning to Marburg, made off in secret for some reason I know not, and so, contrary to my expectation, slyly fleeced me of the several thalers which I had paid in advance to that scoundrel for his journey. But I bear this trifle of a loss the more calmly because at last I may know better — at whatever cost to myself — what is to be entrusted to mountebanks and bats [= shifty night-creatures]. For the rest, I have dispatched to you this Commentary of Clavius on the Sphere of Sacrobosco, on account of a certain little treatise inserted [in it] concerning the New Star of the year [15]72. In which, if I remember rightly, he brings forward much — especially concerning its generation and its aetherial situation, and that among the stars of the firmament, [and] its original [first-appearing] magnitude, etc. — not all of it discordant with your views. I marvel that a Peripatetic man dissents so freely from Aristotle, the Coryphaeus [chief] of his own sect, in most of these matters. On which score he will deserve, in my judgment, to be set far before that Craig, the Scot, a sworn bondman of Aristotle and a [public] enemy of the truth. I myself saw not long ago several letters of his written out to you about the cometary affair. I had also heard that the trifles of that Capnurania [smoke-of-the-sky] of his are being passed about by his countrymen and shared with certain learned men. And so certain men devoted to your praises and your lucubrations long for, and seek, together with me, that you would make public not only the Apologia set by you against those Scottish dreams about the rising, situation, and motion of comets, but also a certain Lichnurania [lamp-of-the-sky], scattering with its rays the remaining shadows and dense smokes of that Capnurania of his. But why [do I carry] owls to Athens? I have, moreover, added this catalogue of more recent authors, in case you should perhaps wish me to buy up for you at the next Frankfurt fair some of the new books out of those that are listed here. To which use I will gladly devote whatever I have left over from your monies, and then anything further that may have to be laid out. You will also find joined to these a little portion of what they call sealed earth [terra sigillata], which is dug up abundantly about three miles from here. As for my having been the prompter and persuader to Otto, that he should write to you at the same time, I beg you to interpret it in the better part. For he asked me some while ago to allow him to send his own letter to you, for the sake of attesting his love and goodwill. This I then gave my word I would grant him once he was able to compose a letter by his own effort without the grosser errors. And so he has now set to it at my urging, and indeed more happily than I had hoped. A few things [were] trimmed by me, some interpolated. You see, then, no obscure signs of talent, especially at this age. I am persuaded that with the passage of time he will turn out a good and learned man, if only, just as he has begun, he will so press on hereafter with unstumbling foot toward the path of VIRTUE and TRUTH.

Farewell, foremost ornament of our country; may you, as you deserve, live as long as may be — unharmed, if anyone may be so — and deem me worthy of your favour.

Again, fare you as happily as may be.
At Siegen of the Nassaus, 23 October, the year 1594.
Most devoted to the honour of your most ample name and to [your] regard,
Conrad of Bergen.

You should know that a celestial globe, restored according to your observations and skilfully and ingeniously worked, has come out at this [book] fair, to the no small congratulation indeed of many. But it was valued too dearly — namely, together with a terrestrial [globe] of equal size, at 22 thalers.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Cunradus Bergensis = Cort (Conrad) Aslakssøn (1564–1624), the Bergen-born Norwegian theologian and astronomer, at this date one of Tycho's pupils and assistants; he had left Hven and was acting abroad as Tycho's book-buyer and travelling tutor. He later became professor of theology at Copenhagen. The signature Latinises his given name (Cort/Conrad) and his birthplace (Bergen). This chapter's own letters confirm the role: Tycho's June-1598 letter to Grynaeus names "Otto Brahe, who together with his tutor Cort Aslakssøn had recently been resident at Basel."
  • Recipient titulature. Dominus de Knudstrup et Uraniburgo — "lord of Knudstrup [the Brahe family seat in Scania] and of Uraniborg [Tycho's observatory-residence on Hven]."
  • Place. Sigenae Nassoviorum = Siegen, the seat of the counts of Nassau-Siegen (Nassau-Dillenburg lands); Herborna = Herborn and Marpurgum = Marburg, both in/near Hesse — the region's Reformed academies (the Hohe Schule at Herborn; the university at Marburg), consistent with Aslakssøn's Reformed-territory itinerary.
  • John Craig (the controversy). Craigo illi Scoto … de negotio Cometico = John Craig (Johannes Craigius, d. 1620), the Scottish physician and Aristotelian with whom Tycho conducted a sustained epistolary controversy over comets (whether they are sublunary or supralunary) in 1589–90, continuing into 1592; Aslakssøn says he had seen several of Craig's letters to Tycho on the matter. The edition's letter-register lists "CRAIGIUS Scotus" / John Craig ↔ Tycho letters (1590–92). Aslakssøn's iurato Aristotelis mancipio, veritatis perduelli ("a sworn bondman of Aristotle, an enemy of the truth") is a partisan flourish on Tycho's behalf, kept as written.
  • Clavius / Sacrobosco. Clavii … in Sphaeram Sacrobosci Commentarium … de Nova Stella Anni 72 — Christoph Clavius's In Sphaeram Ioannis de Sacro Bosco commentarius, which from its expanded editions carries a treatment of the new star (nova) of 1572 (Tycho's De nova stella). Aslakssøn notes — with the hedge si recte memini ("if I remember rightly"), preserved — that the Jesuit Clavius, though a Peripateticus (Aristotelian), grants the nova an aetherial situation … among the stars of the firmament with a primary magnitude (so its location in the heavens, not the sublunary realm), positions "not all discordant" with Tycho's. Coriphaeus (Gk. κορυφαῖος, "chorus-leader, chief") kept as "Coryphaeus."
  • Greek-derived coinages (named cruxes). Two mock-learned neologisms, built on Greek and left untranslated in the body with bracketed glosses:
  • Capnurania (Gk. καπνός kapnós "smoke" + οὐρανία ouranía "of the heaven/sky") — literally "smoke-of-the-sky," Aslakssøn's contemptuous coinage for Craig's cometary doctrine (comets as terrestrial exhalations/smoke); rendered "[smoke-of-the-sky]." The precise title/sense Craig himself used (if any) is not recoverable from this letter; the coinage may be Aslakssøn's own jibe rather than a real title — flagged.
  • Lichnurania (Gk. λύχνος lýchnos "lamp" + οὐρανία) — "lamp-of-the-sky," the antithetical work Aslakssøn wishes Tycho would publish, "scattering with its rays" the smokes of the Capnurania; rendered "[lamp-of-the-sky]." A nonce-word coined to balance Capnurania; not an attested title.
  • Apologia. Apologiam … Scoticis istis … somnijs … oppositam — the defence (Apologia) Tycho is urged to publish against Craig's positions; per Nielsen's bibliography of Tycho's printed works, no such printed Apologia against Craig appeared (Tycho's comet polemic remained in correspondence and in the De mundi material). Rendered "Apologia."
  • Proverbs / allusions. quid ego noctuas Athenas? — "owls to Athens" (the Greek proverb γλαῦκ' Ἀθήναζε, "to carry owls to Athens" = to do something superfluous; the owl was Athena's bird and the city's emblem); the elliptical phrasing is kept. proprio Marte — "by his own effort/unaided" (idiom, "by one's own Mars"). inoffenso pede — "with unstumbling foot."
  • Material enclosure. terra … sigillata — terra sigillata ("sealed earth"), the medicinal clay sold in stamped cakes; Aslakssøn encloses a sample dug "about three miles" off. milliare = a (German) mile; left as "miles," the period unit not modernised.
  • Persons mentioned. Otto — the young Otto (Otte) Brahe whom Aslakssøn was tutoring (a kinsman in Tycho's orbit; the same Otto Brahe + Aslakssøn pairing recurs in Tycho's 1598 Grynaeus letter), here making his first attempt at a Latin letter to Tycho under Aslakssøn's supervision (paucula a me limitata, nonnulla interpolata — "a few things trimmed by me, some interpolated"). Chartopaeus / Officina Chartacea — a paper-maker and a paper-mill: the absconding messenger had promised to bring a paper-maker to work in Tycho's mill on Hven; trifurcifer ("thrice-gallows-rogue"), nebulo ("scoundrel"), agyrtae et vespertiliones ("mountebanks and bats") are Aslakssøn's invective for the cheat, kept in register.
  • thaleri — thalers (the silver coin), kept untranslated.
  • Closing formulae. T. … desiderij studiosiss. — Cunradus Bergensis rendered by this chapter's convention; Vale … Iterum … vale → "Farewell … Again, fare you …".
  • Calendar. 23. Octob. Anno 1594 — 23 October 1594, Old Style (Denmark and the Reformed German territories were still on the Julian calendar in 1594; the writer flags no New-Style equivalent). The dateline date agrees with the catalogue label ("1594. 23. oktober. Siegen"); no discrepancy.
  • What this is. A newsy paragraph preceding the letter's close — Cort Aslakssøn reporting fresh book-fair intelligence to Tycho: a celestial globe built on Tycho's own corrected star positions has just appeared for sale, admired for its workmanship but criticised as overpriced.
  • Independent confirmation — this is a known, cited episode. Adam Mosley's Bearing the Heavens reports, from Tycho's own correspondence, that "in October of 1594, Aslachus had informed him that the van Langren instruments were available at the book-fairs, to the combined pleasure and dismay of the masses... the dismay by their price: 22 thalers for the celestial globe along with its terrestrial partner" [Mosley, Bearing the Heavens (Cambridge, 2007)]. The date (October 1594), the price (22 thalers for the pair), and the informant ("Aslachus" = the Latinized Cort Aslakssøn) all match this letter precisely — this recovered segment is very likely the primary-source basis for Mosley's own footnote. Mosley further suggests this report may have inclined Tycho toward supporting Willem Blaeu's rival globe project instead.
  • The globe-maker identified. The globes in question are almost certainly the products of the van Langren family workshop (Arnold Florent van Langren and sons), Dutch globe- and instrument-makers who had obtained Tycho's revised star catalogue and were marketing celestial/terrestrial globe pairs at the Frankfurt fair in this period.
  • The correspondent. Cort Aslakssøn (Latinized Cunradus Aslacus/Aslachus Bergensis, i.e. "of Bergen"; here signing as "Cunradus Bergensis" — see the letter's own signature), a Norwegian-Danish theologian and philosopher who served among Tycho's assistants and later became a professor at Copenhagen [New Dictionary of Scientific Biography — "Tycho's assistant, Cort Aslakssøn"; Zeeberg, Urania Titani bibliography].
  • Correction. Latin corrected from 'apparent' to 'apparenti' per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.367.
