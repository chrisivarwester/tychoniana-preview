The original

HENRICUS BRUCÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

DANIEL RENTFLERUS, in bonis literis apprime versatus, eruditus mehercle iuvenis et optimis moribus excultus, Daniam petere voluit visendi gratia et te inprimis salutare, qui alter Atlas cœlum humeris ferre diceris. Age, mi TYCHO, persequere magna cum laude labores institutos et cœlum ipsum ad nos transfer, longitudinibus stellarum definitis, et, si quid in latitudinibus desideretur, idipsum posteritati prodens. Nam audio, te aliquid novi in his invenisse, adeo ut dubitari possit, an non ipsæ etiam mutentur latitudines. Est quidem audax facinus a tanto illustrium Mathematicorum consensu dissentire, qui tanto temporum intervallo distrahuntur. Sed si te sic rata docuit experientia, cur tibi non fidem adhibeamus potius quam illis, qui forte non tam subtiliter omnia rimati sunt? Longitudines autem stellarum non respondere calculo, nihil mirum, cum hactenus Hypotheses inventæ, quæ hanc apparentem motuum inæqualitatem ad æqualitatem reducant, non sint. Latitudines vero invariabiles servari omnes prodiderunt. Expecto tuam sententiam, et quid de tuis Cometis fiat, utrum prælo subiecti sint, an vero supprimere decreveris. Hunc vero iuvenem, quem ob eruditionem et modestiam omnes amant, nostra et sua causa et pro tua singulari erga homines literarum amantes benevolentia complectere. Tractatum de Crepusculis Sphæræ adiectum, utrum legeris, scire velim. Si quid habeas Geometricum laude dignum, ab aliquo recentor editum, eius autorem indicato et, si aliquot dies carere queas, ad nos mittito. Nos minus hoc tempore hisce studijs vacamus, alijs distracti. Tu quid agas, quid mediteris, fac intelligam. Et si quid ex Vulcani officina habes, quod in paralyticis et alijs contumacibus morbis nostra medicamenta respuentibus expertus es, id communices rogo.

Vale, Nobilissime doctissimeque TYCHO, et nos mutuo ama.
Rostochij 22. Maij 1587.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Heinrich Brucaeus to Tycho Brahe.

Daniel Rentflerus, most accomplished in good letters — a learned young man, by Hercules, and refined in the finest character — has wished to make for Denmark for the sake of seeing [it], and above all to greet you, who are said to be a second Atlas bearing heaven upon your shoulders. Come, my Tycho, carry through with great renown the labours you have undertaken, and transfer heaven itself to us, with the longitudes of the stars determined and — if anything be wanting in the latitudes — bringing that very thing forth to posterity [as well]. For I hear that you have found something new in these matters, so much so that it may be doubted whether the latitudes themselves are not also changing. It is, to be sure, a bold deed to dissent from so great a consensus of illustrious mathematicians, who are spread across so great an interval of time. But if settled experience has so taught you, why should we not give credence to you rather than to those who perhaps have not searched out everything so subtly? That the longitudes of the stars do not answer to calculation is no wonder, since up to now there have been no hypotheses devised that reduce this apparent inequality of the motions to equality. The latitudes, however, all have reported to be kept invariable. I await your opinion, and what is becoming of your comets — whether they have been put under the press, or whether you have rather resolved to suppress them. But do embrace this young man, whom all love for his erudition and modesty, both for our sake and for his own, and out of that singular benevolence of yours toward men who love letters. I should like to know whether you have read the enclosed treatise On the Twilights of the Sphere. If you have anything geometrical worthy of praise, lately published by someone, point out its author, and — if you can do without it for some few days — send it to us. We, at this time, have less leisure for these studies, being drawn off by others. What you are doing, what you are pondering — let me understand. And if you have anything from Vulcan's workshop which you have tried out on the paralytic and on other stubborn diseases that spurn our medicines, I beg you to share it.

Farewell, most noble and most learned Tycho, and love us in return.
At Rostock, 22 May 1587.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Hendrik van den Broecke, c. 1530/31–1593), professor of mathematics and then of medicine at the University of Rostock, a Bruges-born physician-mathematician and one of Tycho's senior scholarly correspondents. The dual mathematical/medical interest is on full display here: the letter moves from stellar coordinates to a request for a chemical remedy.
  • Daniel Rentflerus — Daniel Rentfler(us), the young scholar carrying the letter as a letter of introduction; warmly recommended for his learning and modesty. (The bearer-recommendation is the letter's nominal occasion.)
  • alter Atlas coelum humeris ferre — "a second Atlas bearing heaven on his shoulders," the mythological compliment to Tycho's astronomical labours; the classical image is kept.
  • Astronomy — the fixed-star question. The core technical matter is whether stellar latitudes (celestial latitude, latitudo) are constant. longitudinibus stellarum definitis … si quid in latitudinibus desideretur = "with the longitudes of the stars determined … if anything be wanting in the latitudes." Brucaeus reports a rumour that Tycho has found the latitudes themselves to be changing (an non ipsae etiam mutentur latitudines) — a genuinely "bold deed" against the consensus of earlier authorities who "reported the latitudes to be kept invariable" (Latitudines … invariabiles servari omnes prodiderunt). The longitude/latitude renderings follow the edition's convention
  • Hypotheses … apparentem motuum inaequalitatem ad aequalitatem reducant — "hypotheses that reduce this apparent inequality of the motions to equality": the standard astronomical sense of hypothesis as a geometrical model accounting for the non-uniform apparent motions; motus → "motion," inaequalitas/aequalitas kept as "inequality/equality" of the motions.
  • rata … experientia — "settled / ratified experience"; experientia rendered "experience" (the appeal to Tycho's observational basis), with rata as "settled." Brucaeus' epistemic hedges (forte "perhaps," dubitari possit "it may be doubted," audio "I hear") are preserved, not sharpened.
  • tuis Cometis … utrum praelo subiecti sint, an … supprimere decreveris — "your comets — whether they have been put under the press, or whether you have resolved to suppress them": a reference to Tycho's cometary work, in print preparation by this date (the De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, on the comet of 1577, was being printed at Uraniborg in the later 1580s). praelum = "press" (printing press); the question of publishing vs. withholding is kept as posed.
  • Tractatum de Crepusculis Sphaerae — "the treatise On the Twilights of the Sphere," an enclosed work concerning twilight (crepusculum → "twilight,") and the doctrine of the sphere. The exact title/author is not specified in the letter; rendered descriptively and not identified beyond the text (see crux).
  • ex Vulcani officina — "from Vulcan's workshop," a period circumlocution for the alchemical / chymical laboratory and its iatrochemical preparations. Brucaeus, as a physician, asks for a chemical remedy Tycho has tried on paralytici ("the paralytic") and other contumaces morbi ("stubborn diseases") that "spurn our [ordinary] medicines." Kept as the period metaphor, not modernised.
  • mehercle — "by Hercules," a colloquial classical asseveration, kept.
  • Salutation / subscription. Vale … et nos mutuo ama = "Farewell … and love us in return," the affectionate close. Nobilissime doctissimeque = "most noble and most learned," the vocative honorific addressed to Tycho.
  • Place. Rostochij = Rostock, seat of the university where Brucaeus taught.
  • Dateline / calendar. Rostochij 22. Maij 1587 = "At Rostock, 22 May 1587." The edition label ("1587. 22. maj. Rostock") agrees with the as-written dateline; no discrepancy. Style is Old Style (Julian): Lutheran Mecklenburg/Rostock had not adopted the Gregorian reform, so the date is O.S.
  • Textual note. recentor (in ab aliquo recentor editum) is anomalous; read as recenter ("lately, recently"), the reading adopted in the translation; the printed form is retained in the Source block by the edition's convention. idipsum and inprimis are written solid as printed.
