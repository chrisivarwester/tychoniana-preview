The original

PETRO IUNIO. TYCHO BRAHE. S.

DIUTURNUM illud tuum silentium, quod excusationis silentio praetereundum censes, sicque vel maxime excusas, id quidem, mi optime IUNI, argute facis, at nihilominus vel sic nimium te apud amicum, sponte sua nihil sinistrius de te suspicantem, purgas. Non enim addubito, licet Huaenulam nostram literis tuis diu et avide desideratis (alijs procul dubio et gravioribus impeditus occupationibus) invisere aliquamdiu supersederis, quin amorem tamen et memoriam nostri in animo illo tuo adeo humano et candido arcte foveris. Quod ut confidentius mihi persuadeam, non saltem literae hae tuae compotissimae, eximiae erga nos benevolentiae specimen referentes, et ea, quae D. Legati ad nos feliciter reversi, ex tuis de nobis amicis sermonibus insuper retulerunt, affatim evincunt, verum etiam ex insigni illo studio tuo, quo tam graviter votis nostris satisfecisti, ut et diploma honorificum plane et praeclarum Serenissimi ac potentissimi Regis vestri, et epigrammata, arguta sane illa et accurata, ab eius Maiestate in mei gratiam favorabiliter conscripta, mihi procurâris, non obscure liquet, quanti nos et studia nostra facias. Quam quidem ob rem ego vicissim de te honorifice, ut tua fert virtus et praelustris eruditio, sentire, gratamque tui memoriam in posterum, uti et hactenus feci, conservare non desinam, nec ullo officiorum genere, quatenus vires nostrae id sustinent, me a te vinci sinam. Gratias autem imprimis, quas possum maximas, serenissimo Regi vestro reverenter ago, quod Astronomica nostra scripta et diplomate Regio munere et carminibus adeo elegantibus exornare non sit dedignatus. Tibi quoque, quod haec tam illustria nostrorum operum elogia, ab ipsius Maiestate meo nomine demisse impetrare non sis gravatus, haud parum debeo. Quantum enim nostris laboribus hinc decoris et gratiae accessurum sit, non sum nescius. Itaque ut perpetua observantia tam egregia in me collata beneficia recolam, devinciri me tibi lubens agnosco. BUCHANANI vitam, si aliquando per otium describi curâris mihique miseris, non saltem rem gratam praestiteris, sed iamdudum datis sponsionibus te tandem exsolveris. Vale, vir praestantissime, et de voluntate ac officijs in te meis ita velim statuas, ut omnia tibi de me polliceri fidenter et libere possis, quae ab amico sincero et constanti proficisci soleant. Iterum vale, et amicos in Scotia ex me saluta, imprimis eruditissimum illum THOMAM CRAIGIUM, qui Uraniam nostram carminibus suis haud illepidis decorare voluit. Fratrem eius, quem meum Antagonistam vocas, attingere non audeo, cum prae arroganti philautia nimis mordax veritatisque invictae hostis existat. Quod enim Cometae in Aethere ipso versentur, non saltem in eo, qui anno 1577 apparuit (de quo inter nos controvertitur) sed quatuor insuper alijs postmodum visis adeo accurate observavi, Geometriceque demonstravi, ut contradicere praesumentes sensu communi destitui merito videantur.

Iamque tertium et pluries vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 11 Septemb. 1593.
Tuae Magnificentiae addictissimus
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Peter Young. Tycho Brahe. Greeting.

That long silence of yours, which you reckon should be passed over with a silence of excuse — and so make your very greatest excuse for — that, my excellent Young, you do cleverly; yet even so you clear yourself overmuch before a friend who, of his own accord, suspects nothing more unfavourable of you. For I do not doubt that, though you have for a while held off from visiting our little Hven with the letters of yours so long and eagerly desired (hindered, no doubt, by other and weightier occupations), you have nonetheless kept close in that mind of yours, so humane and candid, your love and remembrance of us. And that I may persuade myself of this the more confidently, not only do these most welcome letters of yours, bearing a token of your exceptional goodwill toward us, and the things which the Lord Ambassadors — happily returned to us — further reported from your friendly talk about us, prove it abundantly; but also, from that remarkable zeal of yours, by which you so substantially fulfilled our wishes — in that you procured for me both an honorific charter, plainly splendid, from your Most Serene and most mighty King, and epigrams, truly clever and exact, graciously composed by His Majesty in my favour — it is plainly clear how much you esteem us and our studies. For which reason I in turn shall not cease to think honourably of you, as your virtue and most illustrious learning require, and to keep, hereafter as I have done hitherto, a grateful remembrance of you; nor, in any kind of good office, so far as our strength bears it, shall I allow myself to be outdone by you. And first of all I give the greatest thanks I can, reverently, to your Most Serene King, in that he did not disdain to adorn our astronomical writings both with the royal gift of a charter and with verses so elegant. To you too I owe no little, in that you were not loath to obtain, humbly in my name, these so illustrious eulogies of our works from His Majesty himself. For I am not unaware how much grace and distinction will accrue to our labours from this. And so, that I may with perpetual observance reflect upon benefits so distinguished conferred upon me, I gladly acknowledge myself bound to you. The Life of Buchanan, if you should at some time at your leisure see to having it written out and send it to me, you would not only render a welcome service, but would at last discharge yourself of pledges given long ago. Farewell, most excellent sir, and concerning my goodwill and good offices toward you I would have you so judge that you may confidently and freely promise yourself everything from me that is wont to come from a sincere and steadfast friend. Again farewell, and greet for me my friends in Scotland, above all that most learned Thomas Craig, who was willing to adorn our Urania with his verses, by no means without charm. His brother, whom you call my Antagonist, I dare not touch upon, since out of arrogant self-love he is too biting, and an enemy of unconquerable truth. For that comets move in the aether itself — not only in the one that appeared in the year 1577 (about which there is dispute between us), but in four others besides, seen afterward — I have observed so accurately and demonstrated geometrically that those who presume to contradict it may deservedly be thought to be destitute of common sense.

And now a third time, and many times over, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 11 September 1593.
Most devoted to Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Petrus Iunius = Peter Young (1544–1628), Scottish humanist, royal preceptor (tutor to James VI together with George Buchanan) and diplomat; he had visited Uraniborg and corresponded with Tycho (cf. the 18 Aug. 1586 letter). The address-line PETRO IUNIO. TYCHO BRAHE. S. is, exceptionally, in this terse form rather than an extended honorific salutation; S. = salutem ("greeting"), by the edition's convention
  • Serenissimi … Regis vestri / ipsius Maiestate — the King of Scots, James VI (later James I of England), Buchanan's and Young's royal pupil. He had visited Hven in 1590 and here is credited with both an honorific charter (diploma honorificum) and epigrams (epigrammata) composed in Tycho's honour.
  • Huaenula nostra — "our little Hven," an affectionate diminutive for the island of Hven (Latin Huaena), site of Uraniborg; rendered "our little Hven."
  • D. Legati … reversi — "the Lord Ambassadors, happily returned"; the Scottish embassy/envoys who had carried news between the courts. D. (Domini) = "Lords/Masters".
  • Reading crux — i6.n mei gratiam. The witness as printed reads the garbled "i6.n mei gratiam"; this is a transcription artifact for _in mei gratiam_ ("in my favour / for my sake"), the reading required by sense and confirmed by the parallel phrase elsewhere in the literature consulted (nihil … in mei gratiam faciendum). The printed garble is retained in the Source block by the edition's convention; the translation follows the corrected reading.
  • Buchanani vitam — the Life of George Buchanan (1506–1582), the Scottish humanist, historian and poet, James VI's tutor; Tycho owned and displayed a portrait of Buchanan (a gift from Young in 1586) at Uraniborg. Tycho here duns Young for a promised biographical account (vita), recalling sponsiones ("pledges") long given.
  • Thomas Craigium — Thomas Craig (of Riccarton, c. 1538–1608), Scottish jurist and Latin poet, who had honoured Tycho's Urania (i.e. his astronomical work) with complimentary verses (carminibus … haud illepidis, "by no means without charm" — a litotes preserved).
  • Fratrem eius … meum Antagonistam — Craig's brother, John Craig (d. 1620), royal physician and mathematician, Tycho's cometary opponent. Young had evidently styled him Tycho's Antagonista ("Antagonist"). Tycho declines to engage him (attingere non audeo, "I dare not touch upon [him]"), charging him with arrogans philautia ("arrogant self-love"). The contest concerns the comets' location.
  • Astronomy / the cometary controversy. Cometae in Aethere ipso versentur = "comets move in the aether itself" — Tycho's claim that comets are supralunary bodies coursing through the celestial aether, not sublunary exhalations. He cites the comet of 1577 (qui anno 1577 apparuit) "about which there is dispute between us," plus four others observed since, as Geometrice ("geometrically") demonstrated by parallax. Cometa / aether kept as the period terms; no instrument-name occurs.
  • Uraniam nostram — "our Urania," i.e. Tycho's astronomy / astronomical œuvre, personified by the Muse Urania; kept as a flourish (cf. the Sacris Uraniae idiom,).
  • Subscription. Tuae Magnificentiae addictissimus = "Most devoted to Your Magnificence" — Magnificentia Tua ("Your Magnificence") is the honorific Tycho uses of Young throughout the letter (cf. de voluntate … in te meis); rendered consistently.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgi 11 Septemb. 1593 = "Given at Uraniborg, 11 September 1593" (Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The edition label "1593. 11. september. Uraniborg" agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Correction. Latin: 'i6.n' corrected to 'in', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p.355 (translation unaffected — already correctly rendered 'in my favour').
