The original

CLARISSIMO ET EXCELLENTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO IACOBO MONAVIO, PATRITIO VRATISLAVIENSI, ILLUSTRIS DUCIS SILESIAe CONSILIARIO DIGNISSIMO, AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO.

S. P.

QUAS circa Martij medietatem ad me dedisti literas, Excellentiss. Domine MONAVI, amice inprimis honorande, eas nuper accepi, cum autem non suppeteret certus tabellarius, responsionem hucusque distuli, quam etiam nunc accurtare cogor, siquidem plurimis alijs distrahar negotijs, nec tamen per internuntium hunc sponte sese offerentem occasionem datam negligere lubuit. De Typographo itaque vestro sic habe: Videtur is plus iusto poscere, cum Witebergae vel Lipsiae longe minori pretio mea excudi possint, ubi etiam id effici curabo, si propria mea typographia huc satis mature non advenerit.

Domino Doctori FISCHERO brevibus, prout haec otij penuria exigit, rescripsi, ut gratum ipsius in me studium mihi esse cognoscat. Si literas illas SCULTETUS noster ad eum non direxerit, sed ad te pervenerint, ut illi per oportunitatem mittantur, provideas quaeso.

Libros Witichianos me iam facilius obtenturum existimo, quam dum tenax et difficilis eius soror vixit. Non tamen omnes cuperem, sed solummodo eos, quos in apposita scheda ex relatione veteris mei domestici, M. CHRISTIERNI LONGOMONTANI, tibi noti, qui mecum nunc est, denotavi. Si tamen separatim inde nihil relicti pupilli tutores divendere voluerint, me de iusto omnium precio certiorem redde, aut, si fieri possit, solummodo eorum, quos consignavi. Sin vero omnino renuerint inde quippiam distrahere, da saltem operam, ut tua interposita fide eos, quos maxime vellem, per pauculas septimanas mutuo obtineam; restituam ad praefinitum tempus integre et inviolate.

Ad Dominum DANIELEM BUCRETIUM, quem memini me semel atque iterum olim in Insula nostra Uraniburgica invisisse, literas etiam dedi, de observationibus, si quae supersunt, ab haeredibus illius, qui aliquando apud vos fuisse dicitur, Astronomi impetrandis, quas velim illi tradi, et responsum mature urgeri.

Cum ante paucos dies hîc mecum fuisset Generosus Baro CAROLUS de WARTEBERG, catalogum librorum totius Bibliothecae optimae recordationis Domini ANDREAe DUDITHIJ mihi inspiciendum dedit, qui etiamnum penes me est, illi restituendus, cum instantibus diebus festis eum, prout ab eo rogatus promisi, in sua arce 7 miliaribus hinc distante D. V. invisero. Optarem itaque, ut interea ab haeredibus, sive per te ipsum, sive alium aliquem, expiscari velis, an aliquos ex ijs, qui mihi maxime arriserunt, videndi copia concedi possit, et an separatim nonnullos venundare velint; vix enim emptorem pro universa Bibliotheca invenient. Si id, quod desidero, fieri posse, a te intellexero, mittam designationem eorum, quos potissimum expeterem, quod et nunc fecissem, nisi otij penuria omnium plenariam perlustrationem impedivisset.

Caesar necdum Pilsna Pragam reversus est, neque certo constat, quam cito id fiet; speramus tamen statim a Pentecostes festo ipsius Maiestatem adventuram. Aderunt etiam tunc aliquot, uti aiunt, Germaniae Principum Legati. De rebus Ungaricis nihil nobis hîc innotuit, quod non pariter ad vos relatum existimem.

Anglici et Danici Legati nunc Embdae conveniunt, res magni momenti invicem tractaturi, de quibus si inter eos non convenerit, periculum est, ne dissensio illa bellum pariat, quod tamen Deus avertat, cui te tuaque omnia commendo, in quo etiam sanus vale et vive cum omnibus.

Dabantur Benaticae 16 Maij A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most excellent man, Lord Jacob Monau, patrician of Breslau, most worthy Councillor of the Illustrious Duke of Silesia, his friend especially to be honoured.

Heartiest greeting.

The letter you sent me about the middle of March, most excellent Lord Monau, friend especially to be honoured, I received recently; but since no sure courier was at hand, I have deferred my reply until now — which I am also now compelled to shorten, being distracted by very many other affairs, yet I did not wish to neglect the opportunity given by this messenger offering himself unbidden. Concerning your Printer, then, take it thus: he seems to demand more than is fair, since my [works] can be printed at Wittenberg or Leipzig at a far lower price — where I will also have it done, if my own printing-press does not arrive here early enough.

To Lord Doctor Fischer I have replied briefly, as this scarcity of leisure demands, that he may know his devotion toward me is welcome to me. If our Scultetus has not directed those letters to him, but they have come to you, please see that they be sent to him at an opportunity.

The Wittich books I think I shall now obtain more easily than while his tenacious and difficult sister was alive. Yet I would not want all of them, but only those which I have noted in the appended schedule, from the report of my old servant Master Christen Longomontanus — known to you, who is now with me. If, however, the guardians of the orphan left [behind] are unwilling to sell anything from it separately, inform me of the just price of the whole, or, if it can be done, only of those I have listed. But if they altogether refuse to sell anything from it, at least take pains that — your good faith being interposed — I may obtain on loan, for a few weeks, those I most want; I will restore them at the appointed time, whole and unharmed.

To Lord Daniel Bucretius — whom I remember to have visited me once and again, formerly, on our island of Uraniborg — I have also given a letter, about the observations, if any survive, to be obtained from the heirs of that Astronomer who is said once to have been among you; which letter I should wish delivered to him, and a reply pressed in good time.

When, a few days ago, the Generous Baron Karl von Warttenberg was here with me, he gave me to inspect the catalogue of the books of the whole Library of Lord Andreas Dudith of best memory — which is still with me, to be returned to him when, on the coming feast-days, I shall visit him (as, at his asking, I promised) in his castle, seven miles distant from here. I should wish, therefore, that meanwhile you would — through yourself or another — fish out from the heirs whether the chance of seeing some of those that most pleased me can be granted, and whether they are willing to sell some separately; for they will scarcely find a buyer for the whole Library. If I learn from you that what I desire can be done, I will send a list of those I should most especially seek — which I would have done even now, had not the scarcity of leisure prevented a full perusal of all.

The Emperor has not yet returned from Pilsen to Prague, nor is it certain how soon it will be; yet we hope His Majesty will come immediately after the feast of Pentecost. There will then be present too, as they say, some legates of the German Princes. Of Hungarian affairs nothing has become known to us here that I do not think has equally been reported to you. The English and Danish legates are now meeting at Emden, to treat together of matters of great moment; about which, if they do not agree, there is danger lest that dissension breed war — which may God avert, to whom I commend you and all yours; in whom also fare well and live, sound, with all [yours].

Given at Benátky, 16 May, the year 1600.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Jacob Monau (Monavius) of Breslau (cf. brahe-t_16000110001, of which this is the continuation). Tycho's principal Silesian book-agent.
- Significance — a major book-acquisition / provenance letter. Three threads:
1. The Wittich books (continuation of brahe-t_16000110001). Tycho expects to obtain them "more easily now that his tenacious and difficult sister [the sister-heir of the earlier letter] is dead." He wants not all, but only specific titles noted in an appended schedule drawn up from the report of Longomontanus (who had known Wittich's books at first hand and "is now with me"). The estate has passed to the guardians of an orphan (pupillus); Tycho offers to buy the whole, or his listed items, or — failing sale — to borrow the key books "for a few weeks," on Monau's surety, to be returned "whole and unharmed." A precise window onto the dispersal of Paul Wittich's library (incl. the hand-annotated Copernicus copies).
2. Daniel Bucretius & the dead Breslau astronomer. Tycho routes the request for the observations of the unnamed deceased Breslau astronomer (cf. brahe-t_16000110001) through Daniel Bucretius (Daniel Brouček/Bucretius, the Breslau mathematician who had twice visited Tycho on Hven, and would later help edit Tycho's works; cf. brahe-t_15980627001).
3. The library of Andreas Dudith (the notable new item). Baron Karl von Warttenberg had brought Tycho the catalogue of the whole library of Andreas Dudith (Andreas Dudithius, 1533–1589 — the Hungarian humanist, ex-bishop of Pécs, diplomat and astronomical correspondent, who died at Breslau; a recurring figure in the edition, e.g. brahe-t_15880817001). Tycho, about to visit Warttenberg "in his castle seven miles hence," asks Monau to sound out Dudith's heirs whether they will let him see, and sell separately, the books that "most pleased" him — "for they will scarcely find a buyer for the whole Library." Tycho will then send a target-list. A first-rate document of Tycho's late library-building and of the dispersal of Dudith's renowned collection.
- Printing. Monau's Breslau printer "demands more than is fair"; Tycho will print at Wittenberg or Leipzig instead, unless his own press reaches Bohemia in time (cf. the printing plans of brahe-t_16000110001, brahe-t_16010415002).
- News (spring 1600). Rudolf II still at Pilsen (plague-displaced), hoped back after Pentecost, with German princes' legates expected; Hungarian war-news; and the Anglo-Danish conference at Emden (1600 — the disputes over Sound dues / northern trade), which Tycho fears "may breed war."
- Persons named. Jacob Monau; Tobias Fischer (Doctor Fischer, the poet; cf. brahe-t_16000110001); Bartholomäus Scultetus (Scultetus noster, the Görlitz mathematician); Paul Wittich (his sister, the orphan-heir, the guardians); Christen Longomontanus; Daniel Bucretius; the unnamed dead Breslau astronomer; Karl von Warttenberg; Andreas Dudith (and his heirs); Rudolf II.
- Calendar. Dateline Benaticae 16 Maij A. 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
- Correction. Latin corrected from 'septimanae' to 'septimanas' (plural accusative, agreeing with 'pauculas'), per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII p.320.

1 defect found:
- text error (low). our transcribed original prints the noun as singular 'septimanae', disagreeing in number with its own governing adjective 'pauculas' (plural) and with the accusative 'per' construction; Dreyer's page correctly reads plural accusative 'septimanas'. A corrupted case-ending, not a meaning-changing error (the English translation 'a few weeks' is unaffected either way). Dreyer prints: per pauculas septimanas mutuo obtineam (TBOO VIII p.320).

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO VIII p.320 (page image, not the digitised text layer). No other part of the letter was touched.
