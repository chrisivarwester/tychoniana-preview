MAGNIFICO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO, DIGNITATE, PRUDENTIA ET DOCTRINA INPRIMIS CONSPICUO, DOMINO CHRISTIANO FRISIO DE BORREBY, SERENISSIMI REGIS NOSTRI CANCELLARIO ET A CONSILIJS INTIMO, AMICO SUO HONORANDO.

CUM ad dignitatem præcipuam in hoc regno Daniæ evectus sis, magnifice domine cancellarie, amice honorande, eqvidem ex te ipso absqve ullo monitore intelligere potes, quæ decus regni concernunt, tuarum esse partium hortatu et consilijs promovere. Qvod in disciplinarum liberalium liberali etiam sustentatione et conservatione non minima ex parte consistere pro tua prudentia non ignoras, præsertim cum ipsemet doctrinis humanioribus præclare imbutus sis. Astronomia autem inter omnes scientias locum principem merito obtinens, in qvantum et regnis et regibus gloriosa semper exstiterit, multæ rationes multaqve exempla attestantur, ut ob id a Manilio poeta, Augusto imperatori scribente, hoc elogio deprædicetur, qvod

Regales animos primum dignata movere est
Proxima tangentes cælo fastigia rerum.

Hanc igitur tam sublimem artem ex decreto et clementi voluntate Friderici IIdi, laudatissimæ memoriæ regis in hac insula Huenna summis viribus excolere adeoqve redintegrare jamdudum agressus sum, ingentibus ausis et laboribus, neqve sumptibus mediocribus, omnes nervos eo intendens, ut in honorem patriæ illa illustrior et absolutior quam uspiam antea prodiret. Qva in re quid hactenus non saltem qvoad instrumentorum idoneorum, qvalia forte alibi nullo ævo extiterunt, et ædificiorum huc spectantium exstructione, sed et in observationibus qvam plurimis, nulla fere serenitate omissa, coelitus deducendis atque ex his restituendis astrorum motibus effecerimus, judicent alij, qvotqvot Evropa habet vel vnqvam habitura est horum intelligentes et cupidos. Qvos confido non illubenter concessuros me omnes stellas fixas, visui commode patentes, tanta subtilitate et certitudine restituisse, qvanta a nullis veterum aut recentiorum effectum est, res sane ardua multisqve seculis frustra desiderata, sed et solis lunæque curricula in normam cælo ad amussim consonam a nobis esse redacta, omnium ætatum subsecuturæ experientur animadversiones. Qvin et in reliqvis planetis (qva in parte adhuc sedulo laboramus, circuituum tarditate in nonnullis cunctationem exigente) id ipsum exeqvi indies magnis conatibus enitimur; hypothesi jam antea scienter excogitata, quæ antecedentium omnium astronomorum speculationes, fatentibus id hujus seculi artificibus præcipuis, eludit. Constituerat vero dictus rex serenissimus, artis hujus regiæ culturam in hac insula perpetuare et fundatione liberali ejus ad posteros conservationi prospicere. Verum nimis immature (proh dolor) nobis ereptus, id ante supremum diem exeqvi non potuit. Retuli itaqve de eo statim post ipsius obitum ad regni senatum, attestans etiam serenissimam reginam et quosdam e senatoribus conscios, qvi illud ex rege, cum in vivis esset, inaudivissent. Qvare et universus senatus mox non inuite, literis in membrana exaratis (qvarum hic exemplar adjunxi) regium illud propositum confirmavit, et se, cum princeps administrationi rerum admoveretur, omni conatu et svasu elaboraturum, ut is artis hujus perpetuationi in hac insula clementer et liberaliter provideret, addixit. Qvin et tunc feudum qvoddam in Norvagia mihi concessum est, et alias meæ indemnitati, qvantum ea tempora patiebantur, consultum, spe facta, qvod in posterum omnia ex voto uberius evenirent. Præter omnem nihilominus meam expectationem accidit, quod statim ac serenissimus noster rex coronatus est, feudo illo citra ullam (uti confido) meam culpam privatus sim, adeo ut potissima portio eorum, qvæ astronomicæ restaurationis subsidia spectabant, mihi subtracta sit, non mediocri meo incommodo. Rem enim familiarem (omnia qvæ tum e regijs feudis tum bonis hæreditarijs acquirere licuit, in hoc opus expendendo) hactenus potius imminuatam quam auctam, nimis vere expertus sum, cum alijs hujus regni nobilibus, meis coæqualibus, contra evenerit, qvi et propria conservarunt et insuper e regijs rem fecerunt. Accedit et hoc, qvod his primordijs, ab artis hujus sustentatione adeo aversis, dubius reddar, qvid de ejus in hoc regno promotione et conservatione in posterum sperandum sit; anne illius exercitatio prorsus deserenda veniat, an vero alia ejus continuandæ media sint qværenda, qvorum hoc fortassis patriæ nostræ minus honorificum, illud certe exteri ægerrime paterentur, qvi vndeqvaque quotannis ad me datis literis serio hortantur, ut inchoatam telam pertexere non omittam, sed confectam orbi aliqvando communicem, qvod non mediocris copia literarum ex Italia, Gallia, Germania, Bohemia, Anglia et Scotia alijsqve regionibus profecta et apud me reservata ostendet. Qvin et ipsemet, qvi potiorem in his ætatis partem consumpsi, tot annorum exantlatos labores, tot vigilatas noctes tantosqve erogatos sumptus incassum factos reddi admodum invite ferrem. Qvin potius ne id fieret utqve expectationi regum qvorundam, principum atqve præstantissimorum hinc inde virorum satisfaciam, nec multis spem conceptam laudabilem illaudabiliter frustremur, omnino rationes ineundæ, quibus id, quod semel bene inchoatum est, totqve annis continuatum, aliqvando in publicam orbis utilitatem absolvatur, ubicunqve tandem locorum et qvibuscunqve medijs id commode exseqvi liceat. Qvæ cum ita se habeant et necessario nobis facienda sint, in tuum sinum deponenda, tibiqve hisce literis aliqvatenus ea exponenda duximus, qvo autoritate atqve prudentia, qva vales, huic incommodo subvenire et mature obviare velis, qvod etiam te libere et lubenter facturum mihi persvadeo siqvidem non difficulter prævideas, qvam honorifica huic regno artis tam sublimis conservatio existat, qvamqve minus apud alias nationes decorum eidem foret, si ejus instaurationem tam diu tantoqve molimine hic factitatam, cujus fama plurimas Europæ regiones jam penetravit, aut negligi aut aliorsum transferri necessum fuerit. Ut vero aliqvatenus perspicias, qvænam in hoc regno ad astronomiæ redintegrationem indefesso studio hactenus molitus sim, habes hic primum tomum epistolarum astronomicarum e nostris, qvi suo loco brevem instrumentorum a me ejus artis gratia elaboratorum descriptionem, vna cum ædificijs huc pertinentibus exhibet, adjunctis in fine qvorundam ex ijsdem organis jconibus, qvibus introspectis, utcunqve tibi constabit, arbitror, me non obiter, sed summis viribus imo etiam supra ipsas vires tanta perfecisse. Qvod etiam tibi ipsi, si aliqvando mihi in hac insula adesse haud gravatus fueris, opinione verius æstimabitur. Oblatus qvidem est idem liber una cum jconibus serenissimo regi meo nomine submisse, anteqvam Hafnia ultimo discederet, sed cum tunc in itineris Cimbrici præcinctu esset, neqve ad ea serio attendere, neqve de ijs, qvæ proponebantur, qvidpiam certi statuere ipsius majestati commodum erat. Rogo itaqve officiose, magnifice domine cancellarie, ut patriæ saltem causa hoc tam arduum negotium tibi cordi esse sinas, et de ijs, quæ artis adeo eximiæ apud nos conservationem et propagationem concernunt, serio cogites, regiqve nostro serenissimo opportunum in his des consilium. Hoc autem tempore nihil aliud expeto, qvam ut feudum in Norvagia mihi ademptum clementer restituatur, si non diutius, saltem in diem calend. maij, qvo feudorum apud nos commutatio fieri soleat, ut præfectus meus tunc mihi rationes reddituum (qvos alias nullos toto hoc anno inde accepi) reddere qveat. Interea serenissimus rex et secum et ex prudentissimi senatus consilio deliberatius statuat, qvid in posterum circa hæc fieri velit, cujus ego regiæ voluntati, in qvamcunqve partem res vergat, lubens acqviescam. Porro te etiam obnixe rogatum habeo, ut per hunc meum ministrum nonnulla mihi de antedictis rescribere, tuamqve sententiam aperire ne recusare velis, qvo mature, qvid facto opus sit, mecum statuere qveam. Exspectabit istic, donec id tibi per alias occupationes opportunum fuerit. Ego vicissim de te pro virili bene mereri, tuoqve honori et nomini officiose studere non intermittam. Vale, atqve hanc in scribendo prolixitatem, re ita exigente, candide et benevole accipe.

Iterum cum tota familia felix et incolumis vale, annumqve hunc ut auspicato ingrediaris absolvasqve, opto.
Dabantur Uraniburgo pridie calendarum januarij anni 1597.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and most noble man, conspicuous above all for rank, prudence, and learning, Master Christian Friis of Borreby, Chancellor and Privy Councillor of our most serene King, his friend to be honoured.

Since you have been raised to a foremost dignity in this kingdom of Denmark, magnificent lord chancellor, honoured friend, you can of course understand from your own self, without any prompter, that it falls to your part to advance by your exhortation and counsel those things which concern the honour of the realm. And in your prudence you are not unaware that this consists, in no small part, in the liberal maintenance and preservation of the liberal disciplines — especially since you yourself are excellently steeped in the more humane studies. Now astronomy, deservedly holding the chief place among all the sciences, how glorious it has always proved both to kingdoms and to kings, many reasons and many examples attest; so that on this account it is celebrated by the poet Manilius, writing to the emperor Augustus, with this encomium — that

It first deigned to move royal minds,
those that touch the summits of affairs nearest to the sky.

This art, then, so sublime, I long ago undertook, by the decree and gracious will of Frederick II, king of most laudable memory, to cultivate and indeed to renew on this island of Hven with my utmost strength — with vast ventures and labours, and with no moderate expense, straining every sinew to this end, that for the honour of my homeland it might come forth more illustrious and more complete than anywhere before. As to what I have accomplished therein hitherto — not only in the construction of suitable instruments (such as perhaps existed in no age elsewhere) and of the buildings pertaining to this work, but also in very many observations to be drawn down from the heavens, scarcely any clear sky being let slip, and in the restoration of the motions of the stars from these — let others judge, as many as Europe has, or ever will have, who understand and desire these matters. I am confident they will not unwillingly grant that I have restored all the fixed stars conveniently open to the sight with such precision and certainty as has been achieved by none of the ancients or moderns — an arduous matter indeed, and longed for in vain through many ages — and that the courses of the Sun and Moon too have been reduced by us to a rule exactly consonant with the heavens, as the observations of every age to follow will find. Moreover, in the remaining planets (in which part we still diligently labour, the slowness of the circuits in some of them demanding delay) we strive to carry out the very same thing day by day with great efforts; with a hypothesis already devised with full knowledge beforehand, which — as the foremost practitioners of this age confess — eludes the speculations of all preceding astronomers. Now the said most serene king had resolved to perpetuate the cultivation of this royal art on this island and, by a liberal endowment, to provide for its preservation to posterity. But, snatched from us all too untimely (alas!), he could not carry this out before his last day. I therefore reported the matter, immediately after his death, to the council of the realm, calling to witness also the most serene queen and certain of the councillors who were privy to it, who had heard it from the king while he was still living. Wherefore the whole council too, not unwillingly, soon confirmed that royal intention by letters engrossed on parchment (a copy of which I have here attached), and pledged that, when the prince should be brought to the administration of affairs, it would labour with every effort and persuasion that he should graciously and liberally provide for the perpetuation of this art on this island. And at that time, too, a certain fief in Norway was granted to me, and otherwise provision was made for my indemnity, so far as those times allowed, the hope being given that in future all things would turn out more abundantly according to my wish. Nevertheless, contrary to all my expectation it happened that, as soon as our most serene king was crowned, I was deprived of that fief through no fault of mine (as I trust), so that the most important portion of those things which served as supports for the restoration of astronomy has been withdrawn from me, to my no slight disadvantage. For I have learned all too truly that my estate (in spending upon this work everything that I was able to acquire both from the royal fiefs and from my hereditary goods) has hitherto been diminished rather than increased — whereas the contrary has befallen other nobles of this realm, my equals, who have both preserved their own and, besides, made their fortune from the king's. There is added this also: that from these first beginnings, so averse to the maintenance of this art, I am rendered doubtful what is to be hoped, in future, concerning its promotion and preservation in this kingdom — whether the practice of it must be abandoned altogether, or whether other means of continuing it must be sought; of which the former would perhaps be less honourable to our homeland, while the latter foreigners would certainly bear most grievously, who from every quarter, year by year, in letters sent to me earnestly exhort me not to leave off weaving to its end the web I have begun, but to share it, when finished, with the world at last — as a no slight store of letters, dispatched from Italy, France, Germany, Bohemia, England, and Scotland and other regions and kept by me, will show. And I myself, too, who have spent the better part of my life on these things, should very unwillingly bear that the labours endured through so many years, so many wakeful nights, and so great expenditures laid out, should be rendered made-in-vain. Rather, that this should not happen, and that I may satisfy the expectation of certain kings, princes, and most excellent men here and there, nor frustrate without praise the laudable hope conceived by many, means must by all means be entered upon whereby that which has once been well begun, and continued through so many years, may at last be brought to completion for the public benefit of the world — wherever in the end, and by whatever means, it may conveniently be carried out. Since these things are so, and must of necessity be done by us, I have judged that they should be laid in your bosom, and to some extent set out to you in this letter, that by the authority and prudence in which you are strong you may be willing to come to the aid of this difficulty and to forestall it in good time — which I persuade myself you will also do freely and gladly, since you may without difficulty foresee how honourable to this kingdom is the preservation of so sublime an art, and how less than seemly it would be for it in the eyes of other nations, if the renewal of it, so long and with such great effort wrought here — whose fame has already penetrated very many regions of Europe — should have to be either neglected or transferred elsewhere. But that you may to some extent perceive what I have hitherto, with untiring zeal, set in motion in this kingdom toward the renewal of astronomy, you have here the first volume of astronomical letters from among ours, which in its place presents a brief description of the instruments wrought by me for the sake of that art, together with the buildings pertaining hereto, with the figures of certain of those same instruments appended at the end; on inspecting which, I think it will be in some measure evident to you that I have accomplished such great things not in passing, but with my utmost strength — nay, even beyond those very strengths. Which will also be more truly estimated by you yourself, if you should at some time not be loath to be present with me on this island. The same book, together with the figures, was indeed offered submissively in my name to the most serene king, before he last departed from Copenhagen; but since he was then on the eve of the Cimbrian journey, it was convenient for his majesty neither to attend seriously to these matters, nor to settle anything certain concerning the things that were proposed. I therefore dutifully ask, magnificent lord chancellor, that for the homeland's sake at least you may let this so arduous business be at heart to you, and that you may seriously consider those things which concern the preservation and propagation of so excellent an art among us, and may give our most serene king timely counsel in these matters. At this time, however, I seek nothing else than that the fief taken from me in Norway be graciously restored — if not for longer, at least until the first of May, the day on which the changing of fiefs is customarily done among us — so that my steward may then be able to render me the accounts of the revenues (of which I have otherwise received none from there in this whole year). Meanwhile let the most serene king, both with himself and from the counsel of his most prudent council, determine more deliberately what he would wish to be done about these matters in future; in whose royal will, to whichever side the matter incline, I shall gladly acquiesce. Further, I also hold you earnestly asked that, through this my servant, you not be unwilling to write back to me something about the aforesaid matters and to disclose your opinion, so that I may in good time determine with you what needs to be done. He will wait there until it shall be convenient for you, amid your other occupations. I, in turn, shall not cease to deserve well of you to the best of my ability, and dutifully to study your honour and name. Farewell, and take this prolixity in writing — the matter so demanding — candidly and kindly.

Again, fare you well, happy and unharmed, together with your whole household; and I wish that you may both enter upon and complete this year under good auspices.
Given at Uraniborg, the day before the Kalends of January [31 December], of the year 1597.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe to Christian Friis of Borreby (Christian Friis til Borreby, 1556–1616), appointed Chancellor of the Realm (kongens kansler / Danmarks Riges kansler) by the young Christian IV soon after the king's coronation (29 August 1596), succeeding the late Niels Kaas. Corpus background: On Tycho's Island (Manderup Parsberg "held authority as royal chancellor but was about to hand it to Christian Friis of Borreby"); Dreyer, Tycho Brahe ("He had appointed Christian Friis of Borreby, Chancellor …"). The letter is Tycho's formal appeal to the new chancellor over the loss of his Norwegian fief and the imperilled royal support for his work — a key document of the crisis that ended in his 1597 departure from Denmark.
  • Place / date. Dabantur Uraniburgo pridie calendarum januarij anni 1597 = "Given at Uraniborg [on Hven], the day before the Kalends of January, of the year 1597." The day before the Kalends of January is 31 December; reckoned to the coming year's Kalends it is the last day of 1596 (Old Style — Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The header therefore reads 31 December 1596 [O.S.]; the edition identifier agrees (15961231). The Roman idiom is kept and glossed by this chapter's convention
  • Manilius verse. Regales animos primum dignata movere est / Proxima tangentes caelo fastigia rerum — Manilius, Astronomica I.41–42 (the proem, on how the heavenly art first stirred the minds of kings, who hold the summits of affairs nearest the sky). Tycho ascribes it to "Manilius … writing to the emperor Augustus" (the Astronomica was composed under Augustus/Tiberius). Kept as verse by this chapter's convention
  • The enclosed book. primum tomum epistolarum astronomicarum e nostris … adjunctis in fine qvorundam ex ijsdem organis jconibus = "the first volume of astronomical letters from among ours … with figures (icones) of certain of the instruments appended at the end" — Tycho's own Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I, Uraniborg 1596), whose appended icones depict his instruments. organis rendered "instruments" and icones "figures" per glossary.
  • Astronomical claims. redintegrare / restituendis astrorum motibus / astronomicae restaurationis / instaurationem — Tycho's instauratio astronomiae programme: rendered "renew / restore the motions of the stars / restoration of astronomy / renewal" per glossary. omnes stellas fixas … restituisse = the fixed-star catalogue; solis lunaeque curricula in normam … redacta = the solar and lunar theories reduced to a rule consonant with observation. hypothesi jam antea scienter excogitata, quae antecedentium omnium astronomorum speculationes … eludit = the Tychonic world-system ("a hypothesis … which eludes the speculations of all preceding astronomers"). The author's hedges are preserved: the fixed stars are restored visui commode patentes ("conveniently open to the sight"), the planets are still in progress (adhuc sedulo laboramus), and the chancellor's good will is hoped (uti confido, "as I trust"; mihi persvadeo, "I persuade myself"), not asserted.
  • The fief and the politics. feudum qvoddam in Norvagia = the Norwegian fief (Nordfjord) granted under Frederik II; coronatus est … feudo illo … privatus sim = Tycho's deprivation of it upon Christian IV's coronation (1596). literis in membrana exaratis = the council's confirmatory letters "engrossed on parchment." itineris Cimbrici = "the Cimbrian journey," i.e. the king's progress through Jutland/the Cimbrian peninsula (the Cimbri being the classicising name for the Jutes). calend. maij = "the Kalends of May" = 1 May, the customary fief-transfer term; kept and glossed.
  • Negations / conditional force (checked). The conditional appeal si non diutius, saltem in diem calend. maij is rendered exactly: "if not for longer, at least until the first of May" — a minimum request, not a renunciation. citra ullam … meam culpam = "through no fault of mine"; non mediocri meo incommodo / neqve sumptibus mediocribus = litotes ("no slight … / no moderate …"), preserved. The double-negative nec multis spem conceptam laudabilem illaudabiliter frustremur ("nor frustrate without praise the laudable hope …") is reproduced with both negatives intact.
  • Honorifics / formulae. Magnifico … domino … amico suo honorando / magnifice domine cancellarie, amice honorande — the magnate-and-councillor register, rendered "magnificent lord chancellor, honoured friend" (cf. glossary). Vale … Iterum … vale = "Farewell … Again, fare you well". Dabantur = "Given" (the issuing formula). proh dolor = "alas!" kept as an interjection.
  • Editorial note. Source spellings are as transcribed in the Dreyer base (qv- for qu-, -ij for -ii, Evropa, Huenna, jconibus); reproduced verbatim in the Source block, normalised silently only in the translation. No competing reading affects the sense; "[15]97 → 31 Dec 1596" is the only interpretive point, handled in the calendar note above.
