The original

TESTIMONIUM DOMINO LONGOMONTANO A TYCHONE DATUM.

EGO TYCHO BRAHE OTTONIDES hæreditarius de Knudstrup, et Arcis Uraniburgi in insula Daniæ Venusia Fundator et Præses, tenore præsentium testor, quod harum exhibitor CHRISTIANUS SEVERINI F. Lymvicus, mihi in Astronomicis exercitijs per octennium ferme fideliter navaverit operam, et in cæteris præterea se gesserit, uti virtutis et bonarum literarum amantem decere posset. Nunc vero cum a me missionem peteret, non invite dimittendum censui, suoque arbitrio relinquendum. Commendatum igitur volo omnibus cuiusque ordinis et status, ut illum sua humanitate et beneficentia complectantur. In cuius rei fidem sigillum meum apposui et manu mea propria subscripsi.

Actum Hafniæ Calendis Iunij Anno 1597.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

A testimonial given to Master Longomontanus by Tycho.

I, Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, hereditary lord of Knudstrup, and founder and director of the castle of Uraniborg on the Danish island of Venusia [Hven], by the tenor of these presents bear witness that the bearer of this [letter], Christian son of Søren [Severinus], of Longberg [Lymvicus], has for nearly eight years faithfully devoted his labour to me in astronomical pursuits, and has moreover conducted himself in all other respects as might befit a lover of virtue and of good letters. But now, since he sought his discharge from me, I judged that he was not to be let go unwillingly, and that he should be left to his own choice. I wish him therefore commended to all of whatever order and station, that they may embrace him with their kindness and beneficence. In faith whereof I have affixed my seal and have signed with my own hand.

Done at Copenhagen, on the Kalends of June [1 June], in the year 1597.
TYCHO BRAHE,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Document type. Not a letter in the ordinary sense but an open testimonial / letter of recommendation (testimonium) — hence no named addressee in the edition (recipient: empty); it is directed omnibus cuiusque ordinis et status, "to all of whatever order and station." Surviving only as a copy (Afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium at the Danish National Archives (Rigsarkivet).
  • Issuer. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), here styling himself in full: Ottonides ("son of Otte" — see the edition's convention, Otto / Ottonides = Otte Brahe, Tycho's father), haereditarius de Knudstrup ("hereditary lord of Knudstrup," the family seat in Scania), and Arcis Uraniburgi … Fundator et Praeses ("founder and director of the castle of Uraniborg"). Written at Copenhagen in June 1597, in the final months before Tycho left Denmark — Uraniborg's operations were by then winding down.
  • Subject / bearer. Christianus Severini F. Lymvicus = Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (1562–1647), later the leading Tychonic astronomer of the next generation and professor at Copenhagen, author of the Astronomia Danica (1622). The Latin gives his patronymic literally: Severini F[ilius] = "son of Søren (Severinus)." Lymvicus is the Latin toponym from Longberg / Lomborg in Jutland (whence the humanist surname Longomontanus, "of the long mountain"); rendered "of Longberg" with the printed form flagged. The testimonial documents his service "for nearly eight years" (per octennium ferme) — he had joined Tycho on Hven c. 1589.
  • Place. Hafniae = Copenhagen.
  • missionem peteret — "sought his discharge / leave to depart" (missio = an honourable release from service), not "a mission." Translated as "his discharge."
  • harum exhibitor — "the bearer of this [letter]" (lit. "the exhibitor of these [presents]"), the standard formula by which a testimonial identifies the person carrying it.
  • tenore praesentium … In cuius rei fidem … manu mea propria subscripsi — chancery/notarial formulae ("by the tenor of these presents … in faith whereof … I have signed with my own hand"); manu propria repeated as the subscription line, kept as the autograph attestation.
  • Calendar / dateline. Actum Hafniae Calendis Iunij Anno 1597 = "Done at Copenhagen, on the Kalends of June (1 June), 1597" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The Roman-calendar idiom Calendis Iunij is rendered with the idiom and glossed to the civil date by the edition's convention. The as-written dateline (1 June 1597) agrees with the edition label; Actum "Done" kept by the edition's convention
