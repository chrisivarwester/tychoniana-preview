The original

EXCELLENTISSIMO ET CLARISSIMO VIRO DOMINO IOACHIMO CAMERARIO, PHILOSOPHIÆ ET MEDICINÆ DOCTORI EXIMIO, EARUNDEMQUE IN INCLYTA NORIBERGENSIUM REPUBLICA EXCULTORI, ET CIVI, AMICO SUO INPRIMIS COLENDO.

CLARISSIME et eruditissime vir, amice summopere dilecte. Quas ad me circa Æquinoctium vernum huius anni dedisti, literulas accepi, et quod tardius atque brevius, quam expectabam, meis iam dudum ad te datis respondebas, te ob occupationum molem, qua distineris, amicitia etiam nostra candidius omnia interpretante, lubens excusatum habeo. Librum meum secundum recentiorum cœli Phaenomenωn tibi allatum, nec displicuisse, mihi auditu gratum est; quodque illum ad Illustrem et excellentiss. virum Dominum VINCENTIUM PINELLUM Patavium miseris, adhuc multo acceptius habeo. Si is, ut est Mathematum insigniter peritus, suum gravissimum atque eruditissimum de ijs, quæ inibi continentur, per literas communicârit iudicium, adhuc longe gratissimam rem tam ille quam tu mihi præstiteritis. Idque, ut mature fieret, exoptarem. Intellexi iam nuper ex literis cuiusdam Studiosi Dani, qui Patavij studijs incumbit, GELLII SASCERIDIS nomine, qui octennium mihi in studijs Astronomicis et Pyronomicis diligenter inservivit, prædicto Domino PINELLO tractatulum etiam quendam meum de huius anni Cometa forte tua quoque opera redditum esse, utpote ab Illustrissimo Principe Hassiæ Landgravio (ad quem ista breviter perscripseram) tibi transmissum. Quem adeo secretum et charum habeat, ut ne GELLIO quidem meo nedum alijs eum describere, nomini nostro (uti ait) favens, concedat. Quæ viri illius sincera et benevola erga me de facie illi ignotum voluntas inprimis omnem gratitudinem meretur. Si et de hac Cometæ illius descriptiuncula suam eruditam subiunxerit censuram, benefecerit. Tomum nostrum primum, qui iam typis meis pene absolutus est, restante saltem ultimo et decimo capite ob papyri impressoriæ defectum (cui tamen propria moletrina illi usui destinata et non parvis impensis in hac Insula iam nuper a me extructa sufficienter mox subveniam) futura æstate favente divino auxilio obtinebitis, unâque tertium de quatuor alijs minoribus Cometis postmodum conspectis, inter quos hic, de quo dixi, paulo ante vernum tempus huius anni prodiens, quartus et ultimus est. Habebitis etiam unâ (modo tam cito Typographico labore ad colophonem deduci poterit) Epistolarum Astronomicarum volumen, in quo plurimæ et variæ eruditissimorum virorum literæ mecum de re Astronomica et Cometometrica affatim conferentium unâ cum nostra ad singulas responsione continebuntur. Has ternorum librorum evulgationes Operi illi magno de perennium et Mundo coævorum syderum revolutionibus ad normam cœlestem ad amussim restituendis, quod longo iam tempore molior, quasi prodromi sive præludij loco præmittere animus est. Plæraque enim continebunt, quæ ingenti illi molimini atque structuræ viam sternent et nonnulla ponent fundamina. Quæ cum (vires atque industriam suppeditante Cœlestium Opifice) magnis laboribus exantlata fuerint, vos eorundem quoque reddam participes.

Sed est, mi CAMERARI, quod te nunc obnixe rogatum cuperem nomine eius, cuius antea mentionem feci, Studiosi, cui ob ingenium, diligentiam et morum integritatem, quodque multis (uti dixi) annis fidelem mihi præstitit operam, quam optime cupio. Scripsi itaque Domino IOACHIMO POMERIO vestræ civitatis Senatori primario, ut illi meo nomine 50 Coronatos confestim per Mercatores Venetijs negotiantes collybo sive literis cambij (ut vocant) transmitti procuret. Ego nulla intercedente mora, Lubecæ vel ubicunque in propinquo voluerit, renumerari unâ cum interesse non invite curabo. Sin vero Dominus POMERIUS se hac in parte (quod tamen non spero) difficilem præbuerit, da tu operam, amantissime CAMERARI, ut GELLIUS meus mature et quam citissime fieri poterit, 50 illos Coronatos Patavij, vel ubicunque in Italia agat, obtineat. Fiet illud absque omni tuo damno, egoque ad recompensandum et promptus et gratus inveniar. Admonui etiam eundem GELLIUM per literas, ut quotiescunque aliquid illi ad studiorum suorum sublevamen necessarium fuerit (tum etiam in sumptus Instrumenti cuiusdam Astronomici, quod illic ob quædam in Italia commodius quam hîc observationi patentia meis impensis fieri iniunxi) statim ad Dominum POMERIUM et te de eo audacter scribat, atque a vobis meo nomine expetat. Præbete igitur vos faciles obsecro in exponendo et subministrando illi, quicquid a vobis petierit. Scio enim satis, illum superflua non desideraturum. Ego vicissim bona et integra fide spondeo, id quicquid erit, quod in eius usum a vobis erogatum fuerit, primo quoque tempore citra omne dispendium, etiam cum lucro in vicinia, ubicunque vobis placuerit, persolutum iri. Da operam, charissime CAMERARI, ut omnia votis meis hac in parte quam optime respondeant. Feceris mihi officium ea in re multis nominibus acceptum.

Quin et aliud quiddam est, quo te molestare, amicitia nostra sic suadente, non intermittam. Si eximius ille Poëta MELISSUS adhuc vobiscum est, vel etiam alibi, per literas illum roga, ut carmen quoddam egregium in effigiem meam, unâ cum circumscriptis Insignibus, atque in Operis commendationem ex sua illa melliflua poëtica vena concinnare non gravetur, ita ut unum latus paginæ in 4to typis impressum adimplere possit. Et si quos excellentes in Italia et Gallia, tum etiam alibi in Germania vestra nosti Poëtas, illos eodem nomine in mei gratiam rogatos habe. Inveniar vicissim erga ipsos gratus. Poterit quilibet inventionem extruere, prout libuerit et spiritus feret poëticus, ut consideratis et expensis singulis, quæ maxime placuerint, seligam. Icona autem meam brevi (volente Deo) obtinebis per peculiarem quendam hominem, quem Noribergam cum Instrumento quodam e nostris, ut illic nonnulla cœlitus mihi denotet, mittere decrevi. Qua de re antea quoque, si recte memini, te per literas me aliquando facturum certiorem reddidi; et cum Domino POMERIO, quando hîc præsens aderat, oretenus collocutus sum, qui mihi operam suam, quo nuntius ille meus commodum observationi cœlesti atque a turba separatum locum, dum illic est, nanciscatur, non invite addixit. Qua in parte ut tuum quoque subministres unâ cum consilio auxilium, te etiam atque etiam rogatum habeo. Verum nolo te a tuis multiplicibus occupationibus ulterius avocare, unicum saltem amanter insuper petens, ut adiuncturum literarum fasciculum absque omni mora ad antenominatum Studiosum GELLIUM SASCERIDEM in Italiam Patavium tuto et certo perferri procurare non molestum ducas. Ego ad tibi vicissim, quacunque occasione dabitur, gratificandum perpetuo me polliceor quam paratissimum.

Vale cum uxore et tota familia quam diutissime felicissimeque.
Datæ Uraniburgi die 21. Octobris Anno 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most excellent and most renowned man, Master Joachim Camerarius, distinguished Doctor of Philosophy and of Medicine, and a cultivator of those same disciplines in the illustrious commonwealth of the Nurembergers, and a citizen [of it], his friend to be honoured above all.

Most renowned and most learned man, friend most exceedingly beloved. I have received the little letter you sent me around the spring equinox of this year; and that you were replying later and more briefly than I expected to those [letters] of mine long since sent to you, I gladly hold you excused on account of the mass of business by which you are kept occupied — our friendship, too, interpreting everything in the kinder light. That my second book of the more recent phenomena of the heavens [Phaenomenωn], having been brought to you, did not displease you, is welcome news to me to hear; and that you sent it on to the illustrious and most excellent man Master Vincenzo Pinelli at Padua, I take as much more welcome still. If he — being as he is signally skilled in the mathematical sciences — should communicate by letter his most weighty and most learned judgement concerning the things contained therein, both he and you will have rendered me a thing far more welcome yet. And I could wish that this be done in good time. I have lately understood from the letter of a certain Danish student who is engaged in his studies at Padua, by name Gellius Sascerides — who for eight years diligently served me in astronomical and pyronomic studies — that a certain little treatise of mine too, on this year's comet, was delivered to the aforesaid Master Pinelli, perhaps by your agency as well, having been transmitted to you by the Most Illustrious Prince the Landgrave of Hesse (to whom I had briefly written these things out). [Pinelli] holds it so secret and dear that he will not allow even my Gellius, let alone others, to copy it out — favouring our name (as he says). That man's sincere and benevolent disposition towards me — towards one unknown to him by face — deserves above all every gratitude. If he should append his learned assessment of that little description of the comet too, he will have done well. My first volume, which is now almost completed at my press — only the last and tenth chapter remaining, on account of a shortage of printing-paper (which, however, I shall presently relieve sufficiently by my own paper-mill destined for that use, and built at no small expense here on this island just lately by me) — you shall obtain next summer, God's help favouring it; and together with it the third [volume], concerning four other lesser comets observed thereafter, among which this one of which I have spoken, appearing a little before the spring season of this year, is the fourth and last. You will have together with these also (provided it can be brought so quickly to its colophon by the labour of the press) a volume of Astronomical Letters, in which will be contained very many and various letters of most learned men conferring with me fully on astronomical and comet-measuring matters, together with my own reply to each. It is my intention to send these publications of three books ahead, as it were in the place of a forerunner or prelude, before that great Work on the revolutions of the everlasting stars coeval with the World, to be restored exactly to the celestial standard — which I have now for a long time been labouring at. For they will contain most things which will pave the way for that vast undertaking and structure, and will lay down some foundations. And when these (the Maker of the Heavenly Things supplying strength and industry) shall have been drained dry through great labours, I shall make you sharers in them as well.

But there is something, my Camerarius, in which I should earnestly wish you now to be asked [for help], in the name of that student of whom I made mention before, for whom — on account of his talent, diligence, and integrity of character, and because (as I have said) for many years he rendered me faithful service — I wish as well as possible. I have therefore written to Master Joachim Pomarius, the foremost Senator of your city, that he procure 50 crowns to be transmitted to him at once in my name, through the merchants trading at Venice, by exchange or by letters of exchange (as they call them). I, with no delay intervening, shall not unwillingly see to its being repaid, together with the interest, at Lübeck or wherever nearby he may wish. But if Master Pomarius should in this part show himself difficult (which, however, I do not expect), do you take pains, most loving Camerarius, that my Gellius may in good time and as quickly as possible obtain those 50 crowns at Padua, or wherever he may be in Italy. It will be done without any loss to you, and I shall be found both ready and grateful to make recompense. I have also admonished this same Gellius by letter that, as often as anything be necessary to him for the relief of his studies (and also for the costs of a certain astronomical instrument which, on account of certain things being more conveniently open to observation in Italy than here, I have charged to be made there at my expense), he should at once write boldly about it to Master Pomarius and to you, and request it from you in my name. Show yourselves therefore accommodating, I beseech you, in laying out and supplying to him whatever he shall have requested of you. For I know well enough that he will not desire superfluous things. I in turn pledge in good and entire faith that whatever it shall be that is disbursed by you for his use will, at the very first opportunity, be paid back without any loss — indeed with profit — in your neighbourhood, wherever it shall please you. Take pains, dearest Camerarius, that everything may answer as well as possible to my wishes in this matter. You will have done me a service welcome on many counts therein.

Indeed there is yet another thing, with which — our friendship so urging — I shall not refrain from troubling you. If that distinguished poet Melissus is still with you, or even elsewhere, ask him by letter that he not think it a burden to compose, out of that mellifluous poetic vein of his, some excellent poem upon my portrait — together with the arms inscribed around it — and to the commendation of the Work, such that it may fill one side of a page printed in quarto. And if you know any excellent poets in Italy and France, and also elsewhere in your Germany, have them asked on the same account for my sake. I shall in turn be found grateful towards them. Anyone may construct his conceit just as he pleases and as the poetic spirit shall carry him, so that, the several pieces being considered and weighed, I may select those which shall most have pleased. My likeness, moreover, you shall shortly obtain (God willing) through a certain particular man whom I have resolved to send to Nuremberg with one of our instruments, that he may there note down for me some [phenomena] from the heavens. Of which matter also I earlier, if I rightly remember, gave you notice by letter that I would at some time do so; and with Master Pomarius, when he was here present, I spoke by word of mouth, who not unwillingly assigned me his assistance, so that that messenger of mine, while he is there, may obtain a place convenient for celestial observation and set apart from the crowd. In which part, that you too may supply your aid together with your counsel, I have you asked again and again. But I do not wish to call you away further from your manifold occupations, asking lovingly besides only this one thing: that you not think it a trouble to procure that the attached bundle of letters be conveyed without any delay, safely and surely, to the aforenamed student Gellius Sascerides into Italy, to Padua. I in turn promise myself ever most ready to gratify you, on whatever occasion shall be given.

Farewell, with your wife and your whole household, as long and as happily as may be.
Given at Uraniborg, on the 21st day of October, in the year 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on Hven. manu propria = "in his own hand" — the autograph subscription.
  • Recipient. Ioachimus Camerarius = Joachim Camerarius the Younger (1534–1598), physician, botanist and humanist of Nuremberg (Noribergensium Republica = "the commonwealth of the Nurembergers," i.e. the Free Imperial City of Nuremberg); son of the elder Joachim Camerarius. Philosophiae et Medicinae Doctori = "Doctor of Philosophy and of Medicine."
  • Persons named.
  • Vincentius Pinellus = Gian Vincenzo Pinelli (1535–1601), the great Paduan bibliophile and patron of learning whose house was a centre of scientific exchange; Mathematum insigniter peritus = "signally skilled in the mathematical sciences."
  • Gellius Sascerides = Gellius Sascerides (1562–1612), Tycho's former assistant on Hven, then a Danish student at Padua; later (1594) the broken betrothal to Tycho's daughter Magdalene soured the relationship. octennium … inservivit = "served me for eight years." Mosley (Bearing the Heavens) and Dreyer both treat this letter as the key evidence that Tycho was financing Sascerides' instrument-building and use in Italy.
  • Illustrissimus Princeps Hassiae Landgravius = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592).
  • Ioachimus Pomerius = Joachim Pomarius (latinised; the Nuremberg patrician/senator), here Senatori primario "the foremost Senator of your city."
  • Melissus = Paul Melissus Schede (1539–1602), the celebrated German Neo-Latin poet and Comes Palatinus. (He did in fact later address verses to Tycho; cf. his letter of 5 March 1600 from Heidelberg.)
  • Tycho's publishing programme (the works named).
  • Liber meus secundus recentiorum coeli Phaenomenωn = "my second book of the more recent phenomena of the heavens" = the De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588), Tycho's "second book" treating the comet of 1577. The word Phaenomenωn is written with a Greek omega (ω) in the genitive-plural ending — a learned graphic flourish on a Greek loan-word; kept as printed in the Source block and rendered "phenomena."
  • Tomus primus = "the first volume" = the planned Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (the long-gestating work on the new star of 1572 and the restored solar/stellar theory); here reported as all-but-printed at Tycho's own Uraniborg press, with only the tenth and last chapter held up by a paper shortage — hence the new paper-mill (moletrina) on Hven. The phrasing de … syderum revolutionibus … ad normam coelestem ad amussim restituendis ("the stars … to be restored exactly to the celestial standard") is Tycho's instauratio programme (the edition's convention: restitutio/instauratio → restoration/renewal).
  • tertium [volumen] de quatuor alijs minoribus Cometis = "the third [volume], on four other lesser comets" — the never-completed third cometary volume; hic … Cometa (this year's comet, paulo ante vernum tempus huius anni, "a little before the spring of this year") = the comet of 1590, the fourth and last of the four lesser comets.
  • Epistolarum Astronomicarum volumen = "a volume of Astronomical Letters" = the Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I, printed at Uraniborg 1596). Cometometrica = "comet-measuring [matters]" — Tycho's coinage; rendered literally and flagged.
  • Opus illud magnum … prodromi sive praeludij loco praemittere = "to send [these three works] ahead in the place of a forerunner or prelude before that great Work": Tycho casts the three publications as a prodromus to his comprehensive astronomy.
  • Technical / lexical notes.
  • studijs Astronomicis et Pyronomicis = "astronomical and pyronomic studies": pyronomia = the art/management of fire, i.e. the alchemical/distillatory (laboratory) work Tycho ran on Hven alongside astronomy. Rendered "pyronomic" and glossed; not modernised to "chemistry."
  • 50 Coronatos = "50 crowns" (gold scudi/coronati); collybo sive literis cambij (ut vocant) = "by exchange or by letters of exchange (as they call them)" — collybus (Greek κόλλυβος, money-changing) and the bill-of-exchange mechanism for remitting funds via Venice merchants; Tycho's own ut vocant hedge preserved. unâ cum interesse = "together with the interest."
  • Instrumentum … Astronomicum (the edition's convention: organum/instrumentum → instrument); ob quaedam in Italia commodius quam hîc observationi patentia = "on account of certain things being more conveniently open to observation in Italy than here" (lower latitude / southern stars).
  • Icona / effigiem meam … unâ cum circumscriptis Insignibus = "my likeness / my portrait … together with the arms [coat of arms] inscribed around it"; in 4to = "in quarto." Tycho is soliciting laudatory verse for an engraved author-portrait to accompany the Work. melliflua poëtica vena = "mellifluous poetic vein."
  • coelitus … denotet = "may note down [phenomena] from the heavens": the instrument is sent to Nuremberg so the messenger can make observations there; commodum … atque a turba separatum locum = "a place convenient [for observation] and set apart from the crowd."
  • Epistolary formulae. Superscription closes Amico suo inprimis colendo = "his friend to be honoured above all"; S. (in the address) = salutem, "greeting". Subscription Vale … quam diutissime felicissimeque = "Farewell … as long and as happily as may be"; Datae … die 21. Octobris = "Given … on the 21st day of October."
  • Calendar. Denmark was on the Julian (Old Style) calendar in 1590; the dateline die 21. Octobris Anno 1590 is therefore O.S. (= 31 October N.S.). The edition label ("1590. 21. oktober. Uraniborg") agrees with the source's own dateline; no discrepancy. No date is internally conflicting; the year-references huius anni are all 1590.
