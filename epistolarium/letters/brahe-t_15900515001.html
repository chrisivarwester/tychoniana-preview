The original

DEM VESTEN/ VNSERM LIEBEN BESONDERN/ TYCHONI BRAHE HERRN ZU KNUDSTRUP.
WILHELM VON GOTTES GNADEN/ LANDTGRAUE ZU HESSEN/ GRAUE ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ ETC.

VNSERN gnädigen Gruß zuvor/ Vester lieber Besonder/ daß wir euch nu in so langer zeit nicht geschrieben/ ist die Vrsach/ daß vnser Mathematicus CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS, ein zeitlang hero fast vnpaßlich gewesen/ zu dem auch numehr wir selbst/ tam mole ætatis, quam laborum atque curarum, dermassen gedruckt werden/ daß wir vns mit den oblectamentis Mathematicis nicht mehr/ wiewol zuvor beschehen/ oblectiren können. Nicht desto weniger aber/ dato otio, vnderlassen wir vnser Studium Mathematicum nicht so gar bleiben/ sondern hincken noch hernacher/ wie ein alt Weib am Stecken/ Befinden aber doch/ daß es leider mit vns so weit kommen ist/ daß was wir hiebevor magna alacritate selbst erfunden/ jetzo nicht mehr wol verstehen. O pietas, o prisca fides; quis talia fando, Temperet a lachrymis? Was aber vorgedachten vnsern Mathematicum betrifft/ ist derselbige der meynung/ wenn er nur ein wenig möcht wandern/ vnd die Lufft verendern/ daß es alßdenn mit jhm besser werden solle/ Welchs wir jhm aber hart widerrathen/ So hat er aber doch einen eignen Kopff/ den kaufft er alle Jar einen eignen Hut/ damit müssen wir jhn lassen geweren/ Aber leidt were vns/ wenn jhm einiger Schade widerfahren solte/ denn er ist ingeniosus, vnd ein feiner gelehrter Gesell. Begeren derhalben gnädiglich/ jhr wollet euch jhm von vnsert wegen lassen befohlen seyn/ vnd jhm ewere Instrumenta Mathematica sehen lassen/ Auch widerumb von jhm vernemen/ was vns vnser Vhrmacher für Instrumenta gemacht/ vnd noch zu machen vnderhanden hat. Deßgleichen vns auch hinwider zuentbieten/ wie es allenthalben/ so wol vmb euch alß auch ewern gnädigsten Herrn den jungen König/ vnd die gantze Regierung deß Königreichs Dennemarck/ jetzo stehet. Das seyndt wir hinwider gegen euch in Gnaden/ damit wir euch sonders wol gewogen/ zu erkennen geneigt.

Datum in vnser Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel/ am 15. Maij, Anno 1590.
WILHELM LANDTGRAFF ZU HESSEN.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Honourable, Our trusty and well-beloved, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup.
Wilhelm, by the grace of God, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, etc.

Our gracious greeting beforehand. Trusty and well-beloved: that we have now in so long a time not written to you, the cause is that our mathematician Christophorus Rothmannus [Christoph Rothmann] has for a while past been almost indisposed; to which is added that now we ourself are so weighed down, both by the burden of age and by [that] of labours and cares [tam mole aetatis, quam laborum atque curarum], that we can no longer take pleasure in those mathematical delights [oblectamentis Mathematicis] as we formerly did. Nonetheless, when leisure is given [dato otio], we do not let our mathematical study [Studium Mathematicum] lapse altogether, but limp along after it still, like an old woman on a stick — yet we find, alas, that it has come so far with us that what we formerly discovered ourself with great eagerness [magna alacritate] we now no longer well understand. O piety, o ancient faith! Who, telling of such things, could refrain from tears? But as concerns our aforesaid mathematician, the same is of the opinion that, if only he might travel a little and change the air, it would then go better with him; which, however, we have strongly advised him against. Yet he has a head of his own — for which he buys himself a hat of his own every year — and so we must let him have his way. But it would grieve us if any harm should befall him, for he is ingenious [ingeniosus] and a fine learned fellow. We therefore graciously desire that you will let him be commended to you for our sake, and let him see your mathematical instruments [Instrumenta Mathematica]; and likewise learn from him in turn what instruments [Instrumenta] our clockmaker has made for us, and has still in hand to make. Likewise [we desire] that you in turn inform us how things now stand everywhere, both as regards yourself and as regards your most gracious lord the young King, and the whole government of the Kingdom of Denmark. This in turn we are inclined to acknowledge toward you in [our] grace, being therewith especially well disposed to you.

Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on 15 May, in the year 1590.
Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Wilhelm von Gottes Gnaden, Landtgraue zu Hessen = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the "astronomer-prince" of Kassel, whose observatory and instrument workshop were Tycho's chief institutional counterpart and correspondent. Graue zu Catzenelnbogen = Count of Katzenelnbogen, a hereditary Hessian title. The princely register (Wir/euch, full titulature) is preserved by the edition's convention/.
  • Address line. Dem Vesten, vnserm lieben Besondern = "To the Honourable, Our trusty and well-beloved" (chancery honorific for a noble correspondent; vester = fester, "steadfast/honourable"; the edition's convention). Herrn zu Knudstrup — Tycho as Lord of Knudstrup (the Brahe family seat in Scania), his territorial style.
  • Christoph Rothmann. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician/astronomer at Kassel and a major correspondent of Tycho's. The letter is in effect a letter of introduction: Rothmann would shortly travel to Hven (he reached Uraniborg in August 1590), and Wilhelm here commends him to Tycho. The Landgrave records that he had advised Rothmann against the journey he proposed — a single, carefully counted negation (welchs wir jhm aber hart widerrathen, "which we strongly advised him against"), not a prohibition of the visit to Tycho.
  • Latin code-switches (preserved and bracketed in the translation by the edition's convention):
  • tam mole aetatis, quam laborum atque curarum — "both by the burden of age, and by [that] of labours and cares."
  • oblectamentis Mathematicis … oblectiren — "mathematical delights … take pleasure," a Latin-noun / Latin-stem-German-verb pun (oblectare).
  • dato otio — ablative absolute, "leisure being given / when leisure is given."
  • Studium Mathematicum, Instrumenta Mathematica, Instrumenta, ingeniosus, magna alacritate — technical/scholarly nouns and phrases code-switched into the German, the hallmark of this chancery register; Instrumenta rendered "instruments" by the edition's convention
  • Virgilian quotation. O pietas, o prisca fides; quis talia fando, / Temperet a lachrymis? — a conflation of two famous Virgilian tags: o pietas, o prisca fides (Aeneid VI.878, of Marcellus) and quis talia fando … temperet a lacrimis? (Aeneid II.6–8, Aeneas beginning the tale of Troy's fall). Kept as the lament-verse it is (the edition's convention, "keep sententiae and verse as verse") and set in italics. The Landgrave applies it to his own decayed mathematical powers.
  • Idiom. hincken noch hernacher, wie ein alt Weib am Stecken — "limp along after it still, like an old woman on a stick"; the self-deprecating simile is kept literal. einen eignen Kopff … kaufft er alle Jar einen eignen Hut — "a head of his own … buys himself a hat of his own every year," a proverbial play on eigner Kopf ("self-willed, headstrong") rendered literally so the wordplay survives; the sense is that Rothmann is obstinate and must be humoured.
  • The young King. ewern gnädigsten Herrn den jungen König = Christian IV of Denmark (b. 1577; not yet of age — the regency government, die gantze Regierung, then ruled), Tycho's sovereign after Frederik II's death in 1588.
  • Clockmaker. vnser Vhrmacher — "our clockmaker"; the Kassel court clockmaker (Jost Bürgi held this post and built astronomical instruments and clocks for Wilhelm); the letter asks Tycho to gather from Rothmann news of the instruments the clockmaker had built and had still in hand.
  • Calendar / dating — RESOLVED. The letter is dated 15 May 1590 (O.S.; Hesse-Kassel kept the Julian calendar until 1700). Dreyer's 1596 Epistolae print and Wilhelm's own reply both read 15. Maij, confirming the catalogue date; the working transcription's 25. Maij was the error, corrected in the header, Source, and translation.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: Dreyer (the 1596 Epistolae = Opera Omnia VI, p. 214) and Wilhelm's own reply both read 15 May (15. Maij), agreeing with the catalogue; the working transcription's 25. Maij is the error. Corrected to 15 May in header, Source, and translation.
