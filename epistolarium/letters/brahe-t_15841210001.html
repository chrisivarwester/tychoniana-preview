The original

HENRICUS BRUCÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.
S. P.

SCRIPSI per eundem, qui tuas literas ad me pertulit, Bibliopolam Francofurto ad nos redeuntem nihil eorum librorum, quorum in literis meministi, attulisse, daturum autem me operam, ut vel Lipsia aut Witeberga, quos nancisci potero, adferendos curem. Gnomotericam CLAVIJ, quam aestimat Ioachimicis prope 5, RANZOVIO vendidit. Laborabo, ut studium et diligentiam nostram in hisce libris conquirendis agnoscas. Intellige autem, duo illa volumina iunctim venire Joachimicis 16. Typographum nostrum MYLIANDRUM typorum nomine conveni; is respondet, ante D. Antonij festum non posse hisce typis carere, tum, si voles, velle aliquot libras vendere; arbitratur autem, libras 10 non sufficere ad unam columnam sive paginam excudendam. Aestimat singulas libras dimidio Thalero. Quid fieri velis, indicabis, antequam mare claudatur. VITUS ille, cuius meministi, mihi adfuit et respondit, velle fundere typos, quorum in tuis literis ad eum datis mentionem fecisti. Quaerenti mihi de precio respondit, unâ cum quadratis Ioachimicos prope 30 requiri.

In Eclipsi Lunae 7. Novembris observata nostrae observationes non plane cum tuis conveniunt. Initium non fuit observatum. Porro ubi tota obscurari visa est, deprehensa est Procyonis altitudo partium 26. Cum primum ex umbra inciperet emergere, altitudo eiusdem stellae partium erat 37. Ubi tota ex umbra emergere visa est, erat 41. Ex quibus tempora si deducantur, principium Eclipsis incidet in horam 11 6' 32 ", principium vero totius occultationis in h. 12 11' 44", medium [totius obscurationis in h. 1 1' 48", finis] totius obscurationis in h. 1 51' 52". Tota ex umbra emersit h. 2 55' 4". Ex quibus colligo MÆSTLINUM sesquihora ab hac observatione differre, ipsum vero COPERNICUM scrupulis solum sex, PURBACHIUM vero 30'. Si quid tamen ex superioribus observationibus potui colligere, propius a PURBACHIO fuit accessum in aliquot prioribus Eclipsibus. Et credo, me superiorem aliquam Eclipsin observasse, in qua proxime visus est MÆSTLINUS accedere. Verum, mi TYCHO, haec res eget, ut ego iudico, longe ante actorum temporum et observationum artificum excellentium collatione, et homine ingenij sagacis, qui queat aliorum inventis aliquid addere. Ego in PROCLO ad Solis usque motum progressus sum, in cuius consideratione, ut aliorum siderum, cum studium tuum et diligentia perspecta mihi sint, velim aequinoctium solstitiorumque tempora ex te intelligere. Tua enim industria cum habeas instrumenta exquisite elaborata et utendi ratio optime tibi constet, non dubito, tibi dies et horarum momenta, in quae incidunt, constare.

Bene vale, carissime TYCHO, et me mutuo amore prosequere.
Rostochij 1O. Decemb. 1584.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Heinrich Brucaeus to Tycho Brahe.
Heartiest greeting.

I wrote — by the same man who brought your letter to me — that the bookseller, returning to us from Frankfurt, had brought none of those books which you mentioned in your letter, but that I would take pains to see to it that those I can obtain be brought either from Leipzig or from Wittenberg. The Gnomonice of Clavius, which he reckons at nearly 5 Joachimsthalers, I sold to Rantzau. I shall labour so that you may recognize our zeal and diligence in seeking out these books. Understand, moreover, that those two volumes go together for 16 Joachimsthalers. I approached our printer Myliander on the matter of the types; he replies that before St Anthony's feast he cannot do without these types, [but] then, if you wish, he is willing to sell some pounds [of them]; he judges, however, that 10 pounds are not enough to print off a single column or page. He values each pound at half a thaler. You will indicate what you wish to be done before the sea is closed. That Vitus whom you mentioned was present with me and replied that he is willing to cast the types of which you made mention in your letter sent to him. When I asked him about the price, he replied that, together with the quadrats, nearly 30 Joachimsthalers are required.

In the eclipse of the Moon observed on 7 November our observations do not entirely agree with yours. The beginning was not observed. Further, when it appeared to be wholly darkened, the altitude of Procyon was found to be 26 degrees. When it first began to emerge from the shadow, the altitude of the same star was 37 degrees. When it appeared to emerge wholly from the shadow, it was 41. If the times are deduced from these, the beginning of the eclipse will fall at the hour 11h 6' 32", the beginning of total occultation at 12h 11' 44", the middle [of total obscuration at 1h 1' 48", the end] of total obscuration at 1h 51' 52". It emerged wholly from the shadow at 2h 55' 4". From these I gather that Maestlin differs from this observation by an hour and a half, but Copernicus himself by only six minutes [of arc / of time], and Peurbach by 30'. Yet if I have been able to gather anything from earlier observations, in several prior eclipses the approach was closer to Peurbach. And I believe that I observed some earlier eclipse in which Maestlin was seen to come nearest. But, my Tycho, this matter requires, as I judge, a collation of the times of long-past events and of the observations of excellent craftsmen, and a man of sagacious genius, who is able to add something to the discoveries of others. I have, in Proclus, advanced as far as the motion of the Sun, in the consideration of which — as of the other heavenly bodies — since your zeal and diligence are well known to me, I should like to learn from you the times of the equinox and of the solstices. For since by your industry you have instruments exquisitely wrought, and the manner of using them is perfectly settled for you, I do not doubt that the days, and the moments of the hours on which they fall, are settled for you.

Fare you well, dearest Tycho, and continue to attend me with mutual love.
Rostock, 10 December 1584.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Hendrik van den Broeck, 1530/31–1593), Flemish-born professor of medicine and mathematics at the University of Rostock, where Tycho had studied; a long-standing correspondent and book/instrument intermediary for him. The Vienna copy-book (cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66) preserves this among the epistolae; the letter is Dreyer, TBOO VII, p. 88.
  • Salutation. S. P. = salutem plurimam (dicit), "heartiest greeting". Bene vale = "Fare you well."
  • Book trade. Bibliopolam Francofurto … redeuntem — a bookseller returning from the Frankfurt book fair; Lipsia / Witeberga = Leipzig / Wittenberg. Ioachimici (sc. thaleri Ioachimici) = Joachimsthalers (the silver Joachimsthaler / "Joachim's-dale thaler"), here the unit of account; Thalerus = thaler. Ranzovio = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), the Holstein royal governor, humanist and astrology patron, a noted book-collector.
  • Gnomotericam Clavij — Christopher Clavius's work on dialling/gnomonics, i.e. the Gnomonices libri octo (Rome, 1581). The MS spelling Gnomotericam (for Gnomonicam) is retained in the Source block; rendered Gnomonice in translation, the slip flagged here rather than silently corrected.
  • Printing-type procurement. The letter's central business: Tycho's setting-up of the Uraniborg press. Typographum nostrum Myliandrum = the Rostock printer Stephan Myliander (Möllemann); typorum nomine conveni = "I approached … on the matter of the types." ante D. Antonij festum = "before St Anthony's feast" (St Anthony the Abbot, 17 January, O.S.). libras / libra here = pounds (weight) of type-metal, not coins or books (libras 10 non sufficere ad unam columnam … excudendam fixes the sense). VITUS ille = "that Vitus" (Veit), a type-founder named in Tycho's prior letter; velle fundere typos = "willing to cast the types." quadratis / quadratis = quadrats (the blank spacing-pieces of a type-font). Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, cites this exact passage (TBOO VII, 88.13–17) for Tycho's type procurement from Myliander; On Tycho's Island confirms that fonts were cast at Rostock with Brucaeus's help, with some type from Wittenberg.
  • antequam mare claudatur — "before the sea is closed," i.e. before winter ice/storms shut down Baltic shipping between Rostock and Hven: the practical deadline for the consignment.
  • Lunar eclipse of 7 November 1584. A citation-grade comparison of observed contact-times. partium 26 … 37 … 41 = degrees of altitudo (altitude) of Procyon (Procyonis), the reference star used to derive the times. principium Eclipsis … principium totius occultationis … finis totius obscurationis … Tota ex umbra emersit = the eclipse phases (first contact / beginning of totality / end of totality / last contact); times given in h[ora] … ' …" = hours, minutes, seconds.
  • Editorial restoration (Dreyer). In medium [totius obscurationis in h. 1 1' 48", finis] totius obscurationis the bracketed words are the editor's restoration of text lost by eye-skip between the two occurrences of totius obscurationis; kept as printed in the Source block and reproduced (in brackets) in the translation.
  • Authorities compared. Maestlinum = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631, then at Tübingen/Backnang; later Kepler's teacher); Copernicum = Nicolaus Copernicus (i.e. tables computed from De revolutionibus); Purbachium = Georg Peurbach (1423–1461, the Theoricae novae planetarum / Alfonsine tradition). Brucaeus measures each authority's predicted timing against his own observation.
  • Crux — scrupulis solum sex. scrupula in this period denotes either minutes of arc or minutes of time (scrupula temporis); since Brucaeus expresses Maestlin's discrepancy as sesquihora ("an hour and a half" — time) and Peurbach's as 30', the sex for Copernicus is most naturally minutes of time, but the bare scrupulis does not decide arc vs. time. Rendered "six minutes [of arc / of time]," the ambiguity flagged rather than resolved.
  • in Proclo … ad Solis usque motum — Brucaeus has read in Proclus (his Hypotyposis astronomicarum positionum, the late-antique exposition of Ptolemaic theory) as far as the motion of the Sun, and asks Tycho for his observed aequinoctium solstitiorumque tempora (times of the equinox and solstices) — a request that trades on Tycho's instrumenta exquisite elaborata ("exquisitely wrought instruments," this chapter's convention instrumenta).
  • Calendar / place. Rostochij … Decemb. 1584 = Rostock, December 1584 (O.S.; Denmark and the Lutheran north kept the Julian calendar). Day-figure reading: the MS prints the day as 1O. — a figure 1 followed by a letter-form O rather than a clear 10.; the intended day is 10 December, agreeing with the catalogue date "1584. 10. december" and with the scholarly citation (TBOO VII, 88). The printed 1O. is kept in the Source block; "10 December" adopted in the header and translation, the orthographic oddity noted here. 7. Novembris (the eclipse date) is likewise O.S.
  • Correction. Restored Dreyer's numeral "30" (the sum of Joachimsthalers required for the type-casting, together with the quadrats) and corrected "vendidij" to Dreyer's "vendidit" (TBOO VII p.88).

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VII pp. 88–89; defects the audit noted: text error (3) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO VII pp.88-90 read as page images; the Brucaeus-to-Tycho letter runs from p.88 through the first four lines of p.89 ('...Rostochij 10. Decemb. 1584.'); the remainder of pp.89-90 is a different letter (Tycho to Ranzovius, 8 Jan. 1585) and was not needed. Collated the full Latin text word for word against Dreyer, including the entire eclipse-observation paragraph (Procyon altitudes 26/37/41 degrees, the four timed phases 11h6'32"/12h11'44"/1h1'48"/1h51'52", total emersion 2h55'4", Maestlinus/Copernicus/Purbachius differences) — every figure there matches exactly. The two departures found are both in the opening paragraph about books and type; the dropped sum ('30') is a genuine lost fact and forces grade Possible.

2 defects found:
- text error (medium). Dreyer's page clearly prints the number '30' (the type-caster Vitus wants nearly 30 Joachimsthalers, together with the quadrats); the transcribed original drops the numeral entirely. The English translator noticed the gap and marked it with a bracketed hedge rather than fabricating a figure, but a scholar consulting this letter for the actual sum would still come away without it. Dreyer prints: quadratis Ioachimicos prope 30 requiri (TBOO VII p.88, line 21).
- text error (low). Dreyer prints the third-person 'vendidit'; the transcribed original has 'vendidij', which is neither Dreyer's word nor a normal long-i rendering of it. The sense (Brucaeus selling Clavius's Gnomonice to Rantzau) is unaffected either way since the whole letter is written in the first person, so the English is not misled, but the transcribed original itself departs from the page. Dreyer prints: RANZOVIO vendidit (TBOO VII p.88, line 9-10).

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.88-90. Page 88, lines 9-10 read "RANZOVIO vendidit"; lines 20-22 read "...unâ cum quadratis Ioachimicos prope 30 requiri." Both text error confirmed and fixed; the English's existing bracketed hedge for the missing numeral was replaced with the restored figure. No other defects found on these three pages.
