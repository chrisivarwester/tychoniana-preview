The original

CLARISSIMO DOCTISSIMOQUE VIRO, D. DOCTORI PAULO MATTHIÆ, SUPERINTENDENTI SELANDENSI ET NUNC RECTORI ACADEMIÆ HAFNIENSIS MAGNIFICO, AMICO PLURIMUM DILECTO.

CLARISSIME et doctissime d. doctor Paule, amice dilecte. Constitueram iam aliquamdiu Hafniam ire, vt cum domino cancellario cum de alijs, tum de Gellij negotio, de quo vltimo per m. Paulum Petrj, jllustrissimi ducis Vdalrici pædagogum, commonefierj curaui, verum, interim dum frigus asperius continue durat, veritus, id quod iam præ foribus est, fretum videlicet hoc congelatum iri, reditumque maturiorem interclusurum, profectionem hanc consulto in commodioris auræ statum distuli. Rogo autem peramanter, vt interea, dum ego absum, nihil Gellij nomine ob promotionem magisterij nondum susceptam, vel a te vel quoquam alio ad dominum cancellarium perferatur, quod Gellio meo vllo damno aut detrimento cedat. Jd enim si factum fuerit, cogor ego postmodum omnem rem apud dominum cancellarium excusare, atque ea referre, quæ lubentius omitterem. Suasu enim et consilio d. doct. Andreæ (bonæ memoriæ) id factum est, quod promotio illa dilata sit, quemadmodum ego illi paucis diebus ante obitum perscripsi, et meapte manu eius formalia verba, quæ mihi aliquando in aurem dixerat, in memoriam reuocaui; ignorabam enim illum lethaliter decumbere. Atque ipsæ literæ in ipsius archiuis adhuc reperientur; ne quis me post descessum eius hæc data opera excogitare suspicari queat. Pro tua itaque prudentia fac hac in parte id, quod omnibus consultius erit. Quam primum celi clementia opportunitatem concesserit dominum cancellarium accedendi, non diffido, quin ab ipso impetraturus sim, vt cum Gellio hac in parte certas ob caussas dispensetur, quodque tam vos quam ille sitis extra periculum futuri. Hæc tibi breuibus per literas nunc indicanda duxi, cum ipsemet tam cito atque vellem præsens adesse nequeam. Rogo, vt hæc in bonam partem, quemadmodum etiam scripta sunt, accipere velis, atque per presentem nuntium sententiam tuam hac de re vicissim significare.

Vale quam felicissime.
Datum Vraniburgi die 16 decembris anno 89.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu pp:a.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most learned man, Master Doctor Paulus Matthiae, Superintendent of Zealand and now Magnificent Rector of the University of Copenhagen, a friend most dearly beloved.

Most distinguished and most learned Master Doctor Poul, beloved friend. I had resolved for some while now to go to Copenhagen, to confer with the Lord Chancellor both about other matters and about the affair of Gellius — concerning which last [matter] I lately took care to remind [him] through Master Paulus Petri, tutor to the Most Illustrious Duke Ulrik; but meanwhile, while the rather harsh cold keeps on unbroken, fearing what is now at the very door — namely that this Sound will freeze over and cut off [my] timely return — I have deliberately put off this journey until a milder state of the air. And so I ask most affectionately that meanwhile, while I am away, nothing be conveyed in Gellius's name — on account of the master's degree not yet taken up — by you or by anyone else to the Lord Chancellor that might turn out to any loss or detriment of my [friend] Gellius. For if that should be done, I am afterwards compelled to make the whole matter excused before the Lord Chancellor, and to report things that I would more gladly leave unsaid. For it was by the urging and counsel of Doctor Andreas (of blessed memory) that this came about, that that promotion was deferred — just as I wrote out to him at length a few days before [his] death, and with my own hand recalled to memory his formal words, which he had once spoken to me in [my] ear; for I did not know that he lay mortally [ill]. And the very letter will still be found in his archives, lest anyone be able to suspect that I have, after his decease, thought up these things on purpose. Therefore, according to your prudence, do in this part that which will be the more advisable for all. As soon as the clemency of the sky grants opportunity of approaching the Lord Chancellor, I do not doubt that I shall obtain from him that Gellius be, in this part, dispensed for certain reasons, and that both you and he will be out of danger henceforth. These things I have judged should be set forth to you now briefly by letter, since I myself cannot be present in person as soon as I would wish. I ask that you be willing to take these things in good part, even as they are also written, and through the present messenger to signify to me in turn your opinion on this matter.

Fare you most happily.
Given at Uraniborg, the 16th day of December, in the year [15]89.
TYCHO BRAHE,
by his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Paulus Matthiae = Poul Madsen (Paulus Matthiæ, c. 1527–1590), theologian, Superintendent (Lutheran bishop) of Zealand and at this date Rector of the University of Copenhagen ("now … Magnificent Rector"). As both diocesan superintendent and university head he was the natural authority on a degree-promotion question.
  • Sender's subscription. manu pp:a = manu propria, "by his own hand" — Tycho signs the original autograph; the MS witness (A) is indeed the Originalbrev.
  • Gellius = Gellius Sascerides (Gellius Sascerides Cimber, 1562–1612), Tycho's former student, who had returned from Basel with an M.D. and was then seeking his magisterium (M.A.) at Copenhagen. He later (1593–95) became engaged to Tycho's eldest daughter Magdalene — the celebrated broken-betrothal scandal — but that is several years after this letter; here the concern is purely the timing of his academic promotion.
  • promotionem magisterij — "the promotion to the master's degree" (the conferral of the M.A.), rendered "the master's degree." promotio … dilata sit = "that promotion was deferred."
  • Lord Chancellor (dominus cancellarius). Unnamed in the letter, but in 1589 the office was held by Niels Kaas (1535–1594), Royal Chancellor of Denmark. He is referred to throughout only by title, and the translation keeps the title without inserting the name.
  • m. Paulus Petrj … jllustrissimi ducis Vdalrici paedagogum = "Master Paulus Petri, tutor (paedagogus) to the Most Illustrious Duke Ulrik" — i.e. Ulrik III, Duke of Mecklenburg (1527–1603), Frederik II's father-in-law (father of Queen Sophie). paedagogus here = a princely tutor/governor, rendered "tutor." Paulus Petri is not further identified.
  • d. doct. Andreae (bonae memoriae) — "Doctor Andreas, of blessed memory": a Copenhagen doctor (academic) who had recently died and who had advised that Gellius's promotion be deferred. The text makes clear he died shortly after Tycho wrote to him ("a few days before death," "I did not know that he lay mortally [ill]"). Identification crux: the period form Andreas could point to more than one Copenhagen academic of the day; the mathematician-physician Anders Krag is excluded, since he did not die until 1600. The man is therefore left identified only as the letter identifies him — a recently-deceased Doctor Andreas — rather than asserted to a particular individual.
  • bonae memoriae — the standard formula for a person lately deceased; rendered "of blessed memory."
  • fretum … congelatum iri — "this Sound will freeze over": fretum is the Øresund between Hven/Copenhagen and the mainland, whose freezing would strand Tycho. Rendered "this Sound." The winter-ice fear is the stated reason for postponing the trip.
  • celi clementia … aurae statum — astronomical/weather idiom: commodioris aurae statum "a milder state of the air," celi clementia "the clemency of the sky [= weather]"; kept literal, as the meteorological sense is the point (travel across the ice-threatened Sound).
  • data opera — adverbial, "on purpose / deliberately"; here "thought up these things on purpose" (i.e. fabricated them after the doctor's death). Distinct from the earlier consulto ("deliberately") describing the postponed journey; both kept.
  • dispensetur … certas ob caussas — "be dispensed for certain reasons": dispensatio in the academic/canonical sense = a formal exemption from a requirement (here, evidently, from whatever condition had stalled the M.A.). Rendered "be … dispensed," the impersonal passive preserved.
  • Epistolary formulae. Salutation amico plurimum dilecto → "a friend most dearly beloved"; closing Vale quam felicissime → "Fare you most happily" (by this chapter's convention, Vale = Farewell / Fare you well, here intensified). Datum … die 16 decembris anno 89 → "Given … the 16th day of December, in the year [15]89"; the elided century is supplied in brackets.
  • Calendar. The dateline reads die 16 decembris anno 89 (= 16 December [15]89, O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The header date agrees with the dateline; the edition label ("1589. 16. december. Uraniborg") agrees. No in-letter date discrepancy.
