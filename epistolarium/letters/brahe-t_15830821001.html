The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CAROLUM DANZÆUM.

MAGNIFICE Domine DANZÆE, Amice summa observantia colende, reddidit Architectus meus mihi literas a te missas, quibus percepi te, licet inter politica negotia iam multum temporis apud Cimbros consumseris, nondum tamen Huenensium memoriam obliterasse, pro qua benevola erga nos affectione tibi magnas habeo gratias. Nos licet absentes tui memoriam indies recolimus, optamusque ut brevi incolumis ad nos revertaris. Quod Sycophantæ quidam aulici te vexent, oportet te magno animo ferre, et finem expectare, quo videbis omnia in ipsorum retundi caput. Hoc genus hominum delectatur aliorum afflictione, quam indies astute machinatur, ut sibi tandem perniciem (debitam mercedem) vel Deo ultore parent. Sed tute hoc nosti melius. Salutem quam ERICUS LANGIUS mihi per te dixit, velim ut ipsi referas, quamvis nunc eo felicitatis pervenerit (uti ab architecto meo intelligo) ut vix indigeat auspicatiore voto. Multiplicationem enim farinæ adinvenisse ferunt ab unitate in ternarium novo Alchymiæ mysterio, quo lapidis illius auriferi omnem fermentationem et multiplicationem (quibus tot annis insudatum est) excellentiore invento illudit; quis enim ignorat panis perpetuo sufficientem copiam, quæ ex ista multiplicatione promanat, auro et argento præstantiorem esse, cum his carere possint homines, illo autem nequaquam. Caveat itaque sibi ab Alchymistarum invidia et prospiciat, ne arti struant insidias, fermentumque peregrinum admisceant. Diu multumque mecum cogitavi, in quem usum panis potius quam auri et argenti augmentationem investigaverit; tandem incidit mihi in mentem Draconis illius Iutici, qui olim bellum civile ipsorum Patriæ movit, feruntque ob penuriam alimentorum in tanta militantium copia bellum illud Troiæ decennium excessisse, quod alias citius finiri potuisset magno illorum commodo; veritus itaque ne vetus ille Draco reviviscat vel novus aliquando ex improviso exoriatur, Patriæ suæ hoc utilissimo invento providere voluit. Laudo ipsius prudentiam et Patriæ eius congratulor, quod talem sibi genuerit protectorem et in commeatus penuria provisorem incomparabilem. Is rectissime mihi exequutus videtur Philosophorum mentem, qui nos non nobis sed Patriæ nasci asseverabant. Ego per occasionem congratulatorium carmen in laudem tanti mysterij ipsi compingam, siquidem Draconis illius memoriam tantæque victoriæ celebrationem amplius referre non liceat, cum in profundum maris tota ipsius recordatio sit submersa. Sed forsan te a serijs detineo, dum tecum de his diutius iocor. Da operam ut ad nos ante hyemem revertaris, magno enim tui desiderio detinentur, ut multi alij, sic vel ego maxime, qui te tanto temporis locorumque intervallo tuaque exoptatissima conversatione admodum illibenter careo. Deus opti. max. te nobis incolumem quam citissime restituat, tuaque omnia negotia optato successu secundet.

Vale.
Datæ in ædibus Uraniæ 21. Augusti 1583.
T. T.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Charles de Dançay.

Magnificent Master Dançay, friend to be cherished with the utmost regard: my architect has delivered to me the letter you sent, from which I gathered that, although you have by now spent much time on political affairs among the Cimbri [Danes], you have nonetheless not yet blotted out the memory of the people of Hven — for which kindly affection towards us I owe you great thanks. We, though far off, daily call your memory to mind anew, and wish that you may soon return to us safe and sound. As for the fact that certain court flatterers vex you, you must bear it with a great spirit and await the end, in which you will see all of it beaten back upon their own heads. This kind of men delights in the affliction of others, which day by day they cunningly contrive, only that at last they may prepare destruction for themselves (their deserved wages) — or with God as avenger. But you yourself know this better [than I]. The greeting that Erik Lange sent me by your hand, I would have you carry back to him — although he has now reached such a pitch of good fortune (as I understand from my architect) that he scarcely needs any more auspicious wish. For they report that he has discovered the multiplication of flour, from unity into a threefold quantity, by a new mystery of alchemy, whereby, with this more excellent discovery, he makes a mockery of all the fermentation and multiplication of that gold-bearing stone [the philosophers' stone] over which men have sweated so many years; for who does not know that a perpetually sufficient supply of bread, such as flows from this multiplication, is more excellent than gold and silver, since men can do without these, but by no means without that? Let him therefore beware of the alchemists' envy, and take care that they lay no snares against the art, and mix in no foreign leaven. Long and hard did I ponder with myself to what use he should have investigated the augmentation of bread rather than of gold and silver; at length there came into my mind that Jutish Dragon, who once stirred up a civil war in their fatherland — and they say that, owing to a scarcity of provisions amid so great a host of fighting men, that war outran the ten-year war of Troy, though otherwise it might have been ended sooner, to their great advantage; and so, fearing lest that old Dragon should come back to life, or a new one some day arise unforeseen, he wished to provide for his fatherland by this most useful invention. I praise his prudence and congratulate his fatherland, that it has begotten for itself such a protector and, in a dearth of supplies, an incomparable provisioner. He seems to me to have carried out most rightly the mind of the Philosophers, who used to affirm that we are born not for ourselves but for our fatherland. As occasion serves, I shall compose for him a congratulatory poem in praise of so great a mystery — if indeed it be no longer permitted to recall the memory of that Dragon and the celebration of so great a victory, since the whole remembrance of it has been sunk into the deep of the sea. But perhaps I am keeping you from serious matters, while I jest with you too long about these things. See to it that you return to us before winter, for many others are held by a great longing for you, and so above all am even I, who most unwillingly do without you across so great an interval of time and place, and without your most-longed-for conversation. May God, Best and Greatest, restore you to us safe and sound as swiftly as may be, and prosper all your affairs with the success you wish.

Farewell.
Given in the House of Urania [Uraniborg], 21 August 1583.
Yours truly [Tuus Totus],
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / place. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven; in aedibus Uraniae ("in the House / dwelling of Urania") is Tycho's own learned name for the residence-observatory, rendered with the gloss.
  • Recipient. Carolus Danzaeus = Charles de Dançay (c. 1510–1589), the long-serving French resident ambassador to the Danish court, a humanist and friend of Tycho; here evidently absent from Hven on diplomatic business "among the Cimbri."
  • apud Cimbros — "among the Cimbri," a learned classicising name for the Danes / the Danish (Jutland) realm; kept and glossed, not modernised to "in Denmark."
  • Architectus meus — "my architect": the builder/master-craftsman in Tycho's service at Uraniborg, who carried the correspondence; rendered "my architect" and treated as the recurring bearer of news.
  • Sycophantae quidam aulici — "certain court flatterers / sycophants": sycophanta in this register = informer/flatterer/slanderer, not the modern narrow sense; rendered "court flatterers."
  • Ericus Langius = Erik Lange (1553–1613), Danish nobleman and devoted alchemist, Tycho's friend (and later, through Sophie Brahe, his prospective brother-in-law), whose lifelong and ruinous pursuit of the philosophers' stone is the target of the whole satirical passage.
  • The alchemical satire. The letter's core is sustained irony. lapis ille aurifer = the gold-bearing stone, i.e. the philosophers' stone; fermentatio and multiplicatio are technical alchemical operations (the "multiplication" or augmentation of the stone's power). Tycho's joke inverts the goal: Lange, he says, has discovered the multiplicatio farinae — the "multiplication of flour/bread" ab unitate in ternarium ("from unity into a threefold quantity," i.e. one part yielding three) — a feat better than gold-making, since men can live without gold but not without bread. The praise is mock-encomiastic throughout; fermentum peregrinum ("a foreign leaven") puns on alchemical ferment and the leaven of bread.
  • lapidis illius auriferi … fermentationem et multiplicationem (quibus tot annis insudatum est) — the parenthesis ("over which men have sweated so many years") is Tycho's own aside on the futility of the long alchemical labour; preserved as a parenthesis, the hedge not sharpened into a flat verdict.
  • Draco ille Iuticus — "that Jutish Dragon": an allusion (mock-heroic) to a legendary/historical episode of civil war in the Danish/Jutish past in which famine among the troops protracted the conflict beyond even the ten-year siege of Troy. The exact saga-referent is not specified in the letter; the comparison is rhetorical, and is left as Tycho frames it (see crux below). in profundum maris … submersa ("sunk into the deep of the sea") continues the mock-epic register: the memory of the dragon/victory is now lost.
  • congratulatorium carmen — Tycho proposes, per occasionem ("as occasion serves"), to write a congratulatory poem for Lange in praise of the supposed discovery — itself part of the joke; rendered as prose intention (no verse is given in the letter).
  • Philosophorum mentem, qui nos non nobis sed Patriae nasci asseverabant — "the mind of the Philosophers, who used to affirm that we are born not for ourselves but for our fatherland": a commonplace (ultimately Ciceronian/Platonic, non nobis solum nati sumus); kept as the sententious tag it is.
  • Subscription. T. T. = Tuus Totus ("Yours wholly / entirely truly"); expanded in brackets in the rendering. Vale = "Farewell".
  • Calendar / dating crux. The dateline reads 21. Augusti 1583 = 21 August 1583 (Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The edition label field, however, catalogues the letter as "1583. 21. maj" (21 May 1583). These disagree by three months. The header follows the as-written dateline (21 August) by the edition's convention, with the catalogue date appended in parentheses; the identifier (…15830821001) likewise encodes August, corroborating the dateline against the label. The discrepancy is recorded, not silently reconciled — the source text substantiates only 21 August.
  • MS witness. The witness is given only as a copy (Afskrift) in Tycho Brahe's private archive, Rigsarkivet, Copenhagen — i.e. a scribal copy, not an autograph original.
