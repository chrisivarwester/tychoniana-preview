The original

COPIA DESS BRIEFS, SO AN DEM HERRN ADMINISTRATORN ICH INN DER EIL ZU BRAMBSTADT GEGEBEN HABE.

DURCHLEUCHTIGER hochgeborner furst, gnedigster herr. Nachdem jch auß hochwichtigen vnd nodtwendigen vrsachen, so in die feder nicht alle woll zubringen, mich auß meinem lieben vaterlandt Dennemarck begeben musßen, vnndt hernacher bin innenworden, das die kön. maytt: zu Dennemarcken etc., mein gnedigster herr vndt könig, solche meine außreise, vielleicht durch etzliche meiner mißgönner angebenn, also ausgenommen, alß wen das jhr kön: maytt: zu verdruß geschehen sey. Welches ich mit aller warheidt sagen kan, daß keines weges meine meinung gewesen, sondern hette gern alda geblieben, vnndt meinem gnedigstem herrn vndt könig vndt lieben vaterlandt, hernacher wie zuuor, alles zu ehren vndt rumblicher nachsage gethan, was in meinem geringen vermögen sein köntte, wan solches mit guther ruhe vnd gelegenheidt, vndt ohne meinen schaden vnnd verlierung der zeit, hette geschehen mugen.

Vnd kan ein jeder leichtlich ermessen, daß ich in diesen meinem hohen alter, nun jch über die funfftzig jahr bin, mit fraw vndt seks kinder vnd anderem meinem haußgesinde beladen, mich nicht leichtferttiger weiß, vndt ohne hochdringenden vrsachen hab endtfrembdett. Dieweil es aber also vielleicht auß Gottes vorhengnuß hatt geschehen mußen, will ich mich auch mit diesem meinem zustandt gerne genugen lasßen, vnd anderßwo versuchen, was gluch der liebe Gott bescheren will, dan jch darumb keinen verlangen trage, nunmehr vor mein person in Dennemarck zu sein vndt gerestituirt zu werden, vnd wan das schon geschehen solte, so ist es mir doch sehr vngelegen, auf der jnsull Huen lenger zuwohnen vnd stets zu bleiben, wouon jch ahn einem andern ortt meine vrsachen vorzeichnet habe. Wolte deßwegen vnterthenigst E:F:G: gebetten haben, dieselben sich vnschwer erzeigen wollen, ahn jhr kön: maytt:, seinen vielgeliebten herrn sohn, ein schreiben von meinetwegen zuthun, daß jhr kön. maytt: mir hirin nichts zu vngnaden aufnehmen wolle, sondern mein gnedigster herr vndt könig sein vndt bleibenn; jch wil doch anderßwo, wo dan Gott mir meine gelegenheidt vorsehen hat, jhr königl. maytt: getrew erfunden werden, auch deroselbigen vnd meinem vielgeliebtenn vaterlandt zu ehren thun alles was mir immer muglich, welches vielleicht anderswo eben sowoll, wo nicht mehr, vndt viel beqvemer vnd ruhelicher als in Dennemarck geschehen kan. Wofern aber jhr kön. maytt: mit deroselben hochweisen reichs räthen noch des sins wurden, das dieselben die astronomische studia in Dennemarck vndt auf der jnsull Huen zu Vraniburgk, auß gnedigstem willen vnd begehren des hochlöblicher gedechtnuß königs Friderich zu dem brauch von mir gebauet vnd aufgerichtett, durch ersprießliche mittel, deroselben vnd dem reich zu ehren vnd hochgemeltem löblichen könig Friderich zur gedechtnuß, ferner vnterhalten wollen, wil ich gerne meinen außersten fleiß ahnwenden, das solches, wo schon nicht durch mich selbst, doch durch einen von den meinen (so ich datzu vnterwiesen hab, vnd ferner vnterweisen wil) ins werck gestellet werde, vnd das vielleicht, nicht mit geringerm fleiß, als wen ich selbst da were; will auch nechst denvier vberauss grossen vnd etzlichen anderen astronomischen jnstrumenten, die ich da gelasßen hab, noch mehr, nachdem es von nötten, da hinstellen lasßen, vnd alles ordiniren vnd vorschreiben, wie es mit den observationibus vnd dero gebrauch gehalten soll werden, auff das, was einmahl da so wohl angefangen vnd gecontinuirt ist, köntte stets bey macht erhaltten werden, jhr königl: mayttz:, meinem gnedigsten herrn vnd könig, vndt dem reiche zu ehren, auf das nicht anderswo solte gesaget werden, daß solche lobliche sachen gar zerstöret vnd zu nicht gemacht sein. Es wolle aber daneben jhr kön: maytt: mit notturfftichlicher vnterhaltung, dasselbe mit canonicien vndt geistlichen lehen, nach einhalt der algemeinen reichs räthe brieff in interregno gnedigst vorsehen, vnd mit einer bequemen fundation perpetuiren. Von diesem vnd was hier vndter zuuorstehen gelanget ahn E:F:G: mein vnterthenige bitte, dieselben j: kön: maytt: zu Dennemarck, meinem gnedigsten herrn vnd könig, dieses erinnern vnd vortragen lasßen wollen, wofern aber es j: kön: may: nicht gefiele, die astronomische sachen auf der insul weiter zu erhalten, daß alsdan mit sr. may: gnedigsten vnndt guten willen, jch die alda noch vberige jnstrumenta, vndt was mir sonsten zugehörig, möchte lasßen wecknehmen vnd anderswo zu nutz bringen. Daneben auch, wofern es j: maytt: nicht zu widern were, das jch einige compensation wieder vor alle meine grosse muhe vnd bekostung, die ich alda über die 20 jar ertragen hab, vnd vielmehr dazu außgelegt, als von hochlöblichen könig Friderich vnd denn reichs rath mir erstreckt vnd gegonnet worden, erlangen möchte. Welches in jhr kön: maytt: gnedigsten willen vndt wolgefallen jch vnterthenigst heimbstelle. Wnndt thue E:F:G: ich mich in vntherthenigkeit vorsehen, dieselbigen hierahn kein misßgefallen tragen werden, das E:F:G: ich hiemit vntertheniglich vnndt guther meinung ersuche, E:F:G: hinwiederumb mit aller vnterthenigkeit nach meinem geringen vormugen zudienen, wil ich stets williger dan willig gefunden werden. Thue E:F:G: sambt dero vielgeliebten gemahlin, jungen herrn vndt freulein in den gnaden schütz des Almechtigen getreulich zu gluchlicher vnd langwerender regierung entpfehlen.

Datum Bramstadt den 22. decembris anno 1597.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Copy of the letter that I gave in haste to the Lord Administrator at Bramstedt.

Most Serene, high-born Prince, most gracious Lord. Whereas for weighty and pressing reasons — which cannot all well be set down with the pen — I have had to remove myself from my dear fatherland Denmark, and have afterwards become aware that the Royal Majesty of Denmark, etc., my most gracious lord and king, has taken this departure of mine — informed of it, perhaps, by certain of those who begrudge me — in such a way as if it had been done to His Royal Majesty's displeasure. Which I can say with all truth was in no way my intention; rather I would gladly have remained there, and would have done for my most gracious lord and king and my dear fatherland, afterwards as before, everything to [their] honour and praiseworthy renown that might lie within my slender means, had this been able to come about with good peace and opportunity, and without harm to myself and loss of time.

And anyone can readily judge that I, in this my advanced age — now that I am past fifty years, burdened with a wife and six children and the rest of my household — have not estranged myself in any frivolous manner, nor without most urgent reasons. But since it has thus had to come about, perhaps by God's providence, I will gladly let myself be content with this my condition, and try elsewhere what fortune the dear God will grant; for I bear no desire on that account to be in Denmark any longer for my own person and to be restored [to my place]; and even should that come to pass, it is nonetheless very inconvenient for me to dwell any longer on the island of Hven and to remain there always — concerning which I have recorded my reasons in another place. I would therefore most humbly have entreated Your Princely Grace that you might be pleased to show yourself not unwilling to write a letter on my behalf to His Royal Majesty, your most beloved lord son, [asking] that His Royal Majesty would not take anything herein to my disgrace, but be and remain my most gracious lord and king; for I will nonetheless, elsewhere — wherever God has provided my opportunity — be found faithful to His Royal Majesty, and will do for the honour of the same and of my most beloved fatherland everything that may ever be possible to me, which perhaps can be done elsewhere just as well, if not more, and far more conveniently and peaceably than in Denmark. But if His Royal Majesty, with his most wise Councillors of the Realm, should still be of the mind that they wish to maintain further — by fruitful means, to the honour of the same and of the realm, and to the memory of the aforesaid praiseworthy King Frederik — the astronomical studies in Denmark and on the island of Hven at Uraniborg, built and set up by me for that use out of the most gracious will and desire of King Frederik of laudable memory: then I will gladly apply my utmost diligence that this be put into effect — if not by myself, then by one of my own people (whom I have instructed for it, and will instruct further) — and that, perhaps, with no less diligence than if I myself were there. I will also, besides the four exceedingly large and certain other astronomical instruments which I have left there, have yet more set up there as need requires, and will arrange and prescribe everything as to how the matter of the observations and their use is to be conducted, so that what has once been so well begun and continued there might always be kept in force, to the honour of His Royal Majesty, my most gracious lord and king, and of the realm — so that it not be said elsewhere that such praiseworthy things have been utterly destroyed and brought to nothing. But may His Royal Majesty besides this, with sufficient maintenance, most graciously provide for it with canonries and ecclesiastical benefices, according to the tenor of the letter of the general Councillors of the Realm in the interregnum, and perpetuate it with a suitable endowment. Concerning this, and what is to be understood thereunder, my humble request goes to Your Princely Grace that you would be pleased to have His Royal Majesty of Denmark, my most gracious lord and king, reminded of this and have it laid before him; but if it should not please His Royal Majesty to maintain the astronomical concerns on the island any longer, that then, with His Majesty's most gracious and good will, I might have the instruments still remaining there, and whatever else belongs to me, taken away and brought to use elsewhere. And besides, that — if it were not repugnant to His Majesty — I might obtain some compensation in return for all my great labour and expense, which I have borne there for over twenty years, and have laid out far more thereto than was extended and granted to me by the laudable King Frederik and the Council of the Realm. Which I most humbly leave to His Royal Majesty's most gracious will and pleasure. And I assure myself in humility of Your Princely Grace that you will bear no displeasure herein, [seeing] that I herewith humbly and in good meaning entreat Your Princely Grace; [and] to serve Your Princely Grace in return with all humility according to my slender ability, I will ever be found more ready than willing. I commit Your Princely Grace, together with your most beloved consort, young lords and ladies, faithfully to the gracious protection of the Almighty, unto a happy and long-enduring reign.

Given at Bramstedt, the 22nd of December, in the year 1597.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing in December 1597 from Bramstedt (Bad Bramstedt, in Holstein), some months into his self-imposed departure from Denmark. He had left Hven in spring 1597 and Copenhagen that summer, moving via Rostock to Heinrich Rantzau's estate at Wandsbek; the meeting with the margrave at Bramstedt was arranged by Rantzau. The "another place" where he says he has recorded his reasons (ahn einem andern ortt … vrsachen vorzeichnet) is his contemporaneous elegy Elegia ad Daniam and his other appeals.
  • Recipient. Der Herr Administrator / Durchleuchtiger hochgeborner Fürst = Joachim Friedrich (1546–1608), Margrave of Brandenburg, then Administrator of the Archbishopric of Magdeburg (the Protestant prince-administrator's title, hence "Administrator"). He had been in Denmark in late 1597 for the marriage of his daughter; on the return journey he met Tycho at Bramstedt on 22 December 1597. In January 1598, weeks after this letter, he succeeded his father as Elector of Brandenburg. The header's Herr Administrator and the body's Durchleuchtiger Fürst are the same person.
  • "your most beloved lord son" (seinen vielgeliebten herrn sohn). The "Royal Majesty of Denmark" is Christian IV (r. 1588–1648); Tycho addresses Joachim Friedrich as a kinsman able to intercede with the young king. The relationship is courtly/diplomatic, not literally paternal: Christian IV's queen would later be Joachim Friedrich's granddaughter, but in 1597 the deference reflects the older prince's standing. The phrase is rendered literally and the relationship clarified here rather than smoothed.
  • "past fifty years … wife and six children" (über die funfftzig jahr … fraw vndt seks kinder). Tycho was born December 1546, so genuinely just past fifty. Fraw = his common-law wife Kirsten Jørgensdatter. The number of children (seks) is as written; six are named in the household at this date.
  • König Friderich — Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), who in 1576 granted Tycho the island of Hven and underwrote Uraniborg; repeatedly invoked here ("of laudable / praiseworthy memory") as the founder whose legacy the establishment embodies.
  • Instruments. die vier vberauss grossen … astronomischen jnstrumenten = "the four exceedingly large astronomical instruments" left behind at Uraniborg/Stjerneborg (per glossary, organa → instruments; here Instrumenten / Instrumenta). observationibus (Latin ablative, code-switched) = "observations". gecontinuirt, gerestituirt, perpetuiren, fundation, compensation, canonicien, in interregno, ordiniren, vorschreiben are the letter's Latinate chancery vocabulary, kept in register.
  • canonicien vndt geistlichen lehen … in interregno — "canonries and ecclesiastical benefices … in the interregnum": Tycho proposes that the establishment be endowed from church prebends, citing a letter of the Councillors of the Realm issued during the interregnum (the regency between Frederik II's death in 1588 and Christian IV's majority in 1596). Rendered literally; Lehen here = ecclesiastical livings/prebends.
  • Negation / legal force. The pivotal clauses each turn on a single negation, reproduced one-for-one: (1) keines weges meine meinung gewesen — "in no way my intention" (the departure was not meant as an affront); (2) daß jhr kön. maytt: mir hirin nichts zu vngnaden aufnehmen wolle — the king should take nothing herein to Tycho's disgrace; (3) the two conditional wofern aber … nicht gefiele / nicht zu widern were clauses governing the fallback requests (removal of the instruments; compensation) — translated as conditionals ("but if it should not please …"; "if it were not repugnant …") without strengthening or weakening their hypothetical mood.
  • Hedges preserved. vielleicht durch etzliche meiner mißgönner ("perhaps by certain of those who begrudge me"); vielleicht auß Gottes vorhengnuß ("perhaps by God's providence"); welches vielleicht anderswo eben sowoll, wo nicht mehr ("which perhaps … just as well, if not more") — each vielleicht / conditional kept as a hedge, none sharpened to assertion.
  • Orthography (Early New High German chancery, c. 1597). jch / vnd / vnndt = ich / und; vrsachen = Ursachen; außreise = Ausreise; gluch / gluchlicher = Glück / glücklicher; funfftzig = fünfzig; seks = sechs; müsßen, lasßen, außgelegt with ß for ss; könig. maytt: / kön: maytt: = königliche Majestät; E:F:G: = Euer Fürstliche Gnaden ("Your Princely Grace"); j: kön: may: = Ihre königliche Majestät; sr. may: = Seiner Majestät. Brambstadt / Bramstadt = Bramstedt (Bad Bramstedt, Holstein). Vraniburgk = Uraniborg; Huen = Hven.
  • Honorifics. Vnsern gnedigen Gruß is absent (the letter opens directly with the princely address); the closing commendation in den gnaden schütz des Almechtigen … entpfehlen ("commit … to the gracious protection of the Almighty") is the standard chancery valediction, translated in full rather than dropped.
  • Calendar / place. Datum Bramstadt den 22. decembris anno 1597 = "Given at Bramstedt, 22 December 1597." Old Style: Tycho and the Holstein/Danish sphere kept the Julian calendar in 1597 (Denmark until 1700). The edition's catalogue label (22 December 1597, Bramstedt) agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. The header opening (… ICH INN DER EIL ZU BRAMBSTADT GEGEBEN HABE, "which I gave in haste at Bramstedt") is an archival rubric on the file copy (Langebek's Diplomatarium), not part of the letter proper; preserved by this chapter's convention
