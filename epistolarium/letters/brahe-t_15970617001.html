The original

ILLUSTRISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO, DOMINO MAURITIO, HASSIÆ ETC. LANDTGRAVIO, COMITI IN CATZENELNPOGEN, DIETZ, ZIGENHAIN et NIDDA ETC., DOMINO CLEMENTISSIMO.

ILLUSTRISSIME princeps, domine clementissime. Binas Celsit. Tuæ per civem quendam Cassellanum literas mihi clementer missas, calendis ipsis junij accepi. Cum autem tunc in procinctu essem itineris Germanici, quemadmodum etiam sequenti statim die Haffnia discessi, meque huc Rostochium contuli (ubi etiamnum paulisper, donec res meæ navigio adventarint, subsisto) nulla dabatur commoditas ijsdem respondendi et longe minus ea, quæ Germanico idiomate exaratis continebantur, exequendi. Jn negotio Erici Langij, amici et affinis mei dilecti, quod T. C. tam clementem sedulam dederit operam, ut, quo in statu res eius versentur, quantoque ære alieno laboret, nobis innotesceret, est cur C. T. submisse gratias plurimas habeam. Verum incommode, et præter opinionem accidit, quod hac æstate comitia regni (uti quotannis fieri solet) celebrata non sint, in quibus maxima pars nobilitatis Danicæ convenire assolet, cum quorum aliquibus, præsertim ijs, qui Erico consanguinitatis vel affinitatis iure coniuncti essent, de debitis eius exolvendis (si qua ratione id obtinere possem) fideliter agere animus erat. Quin etsi comitia illa more solito successissent, nimis tamen sero is, qui T. C. literas, una cum debitorum Erici rationibus attulit, isthuc accesserat. Neque enim ultra quatuordecim dies post ascensionis festum procerum et nobilium regni in urbe illa congressus prorogari solent. Extra vero id tempus, illi, qui Erico aut sanguine, aut alia ratione coniuncti sunt, ita dispersi hinc inde habitant, ut eorum voluntates experiri, et simul cum illis de hoc negotio agere minime oportunum sit. Accessit et hoc, quod mihi, qui hac de re cæteros solicitare constitueram, iam tum e patria in Germaniam discedendum erat, adornato inde a paschatis festo itinere, ut ne propria quidem omni ex parte satis exequi, nedum aliena curare licuerit. Quare T:am C:m, quod in Erici rebus ob prædicta impedimenta nihil tunc temporis præstari potuerit, clementer excusatum habituram confido, et ut creditores eius adhuc aliquandiu, donec melior detur ratio atque occasio illis satisfaciendi, cum ipso contenti esse velint, sua authoritate et clementia effecturam.

Meæ autem in Germaniam transmigrationis rationes nimis longum foret T. C. hic exponere. Neque etiam tempus et calamus id nunc patiuntur. Id saltem brevibus indicabo, quod, cum intellexissem eos qui nunc fatis apud nos præsunt politicis, minus Uraniæ nostræ arduis laboribus favere, et eorum sufficientem sustentationem suis clancularijs consilijs avertere, nec ijs quæ a Friderico secundo laudatissimæ memoriæ circa hæc (approbantibus id post ipsius obitum omnibus regni senatoribus, literis confirmantibus) decreta erant, satis acquiescere, neque regem nostrum serenissimum alias optima indole et natura præditum ita de his, uti decuit, admonere voluisse: existimavi, mature talibus difficultatibus subveniendum, ne omnia, quæ tot iam annis in astronomiæ instaurationem magno conamine, nec parvis sumptibus molitus sum, interirent. Quare huc in Germaniam sponte secessi, ut alias commoditates ea ipsa continuandi absolvendique quærerem, quo omnia in utilitatem reip. literariæ ad posteritatem sarcta tecta transmitti possent. Qua in parte si T. C. nostris desiderijs consilio auxilioque subvenire non degravata fuerit, præstiterit rem, non saltem mihi, sed toti astronomico studio, quod parens tuus laudatissimæ memoriæ tanto amore prosequutus est, quo etiam ipsemet non modicum afficeris, percommodam, et cum eorum, qui nunc supersunt, tum etiam posterorum, grata memoria laudeque celebrandam. Et si T. C. hac de re quidpiam clementer ad me scribere, literasque illas ad producem Holsatiæ dominum Henricum Ranzovium dirigere voluerit, ille eas facile isthuc, ubicunque locorum me versari noverit, perferri curabit. Porro icones instrumentorum meorum, quas transmiseram T:æ C:ni, esse redditas lubens cognovi, et pro opere canonis triangulorum (cuius quidem antea quaterna exemplaria coëmeram) vicissim mihi donato gratias submisse ago. Interim vero non possum ex literis T. C. aut aliunde percipere, utrum liber epistolarum astronomicarum, quem T:æ C:i, quod paternas mecum communicatas literas contineret, dedicandum censui (quem etiam ob id non ingratum futurum, mihi persuadebam) ad C:nis Tuæ manus pervenerit, nec ne. Quod si exemplar a me transmissum intercidit, dabo operam, ut aliud per occasionem oblatam eidem substituatur: nisi forte e nundinis Francofurtensibus ultimis, ubi aliquot eiusdem libri exemplaria prostabant, eius copia facta sit. Sed desino T:m C:m pluribus nunc detinere, cum et ipsemet in hoc peregrino loco inter alia plurima negotia atque interpellationes vix hæc ipsa scribendi otium nactus sim. Vbi maiori tranquillitati, favore numinis, restitutus fuero, plura etiam et accuratiora dare non intermittam. Interea hæc festinanti calamo, et, citra commoditatem effusa T. C. clementer accipiat, ac diu feliciterque valeat.

Dabantur Rostochio die 17. junij anni 1597.
Tuæ Cels, addictiss.
Tycho Brahe
manu propria.

To the Most Illustrious Prince and Lord, Lord Moritz, Landgrave of Hesse etc., Count in Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain and Nidda etc., his most gracious Lord.

Most Illustrious Prince, most gracious Lord. The two letters of Your Highness, graciously sent to me by a certain citizen of Kassel, I received on the very Kalends of June [1 June]. But since I was then on the point of setting out on a journey into Germany — as indeed I departed from Copenhagen the very next day, and betook myself here to Rostock (where I am still tarrying a little while, until my effects arrive by ship) — there was no opportunity of replying to them, and far less of carrying out the things that were contained in those written in the German tongue. In the matter of Erik Lange, my beloved friend and kinsman by marriage, that Your Highness has given such gracious and diligent effort, that there might be made known to us in what state his affairs stand and with how great a debt he labours, there is reason why I should most humbly render Your Highness very many thanks. But it has fallen out inconveniently, and beyond expectation, that this summer the Diet of the realm (as is wont to be held each year) has not been held — at which the greatest part of the Danish nobility is accustomed to assemble; with some of whom, especially those who were joined to Erik by right of consanguinity or affinity, it was my intention to deal faithfully concerning the discharge of his debts (if by any means I could obtain it). Indeed, even had that Diet proceeded in the wonted manner, yet too late had he arrived there who brought Your Highness's letters together with the accounts of Erik's debts. For the gatherings of the grandees and nobles of the realm in that city are not wont to be prolonged beyond fourteen days after the feast of the Ascension. But outside that time, those who are joined to Erik either by blood or by some other tie dwell so scattered here and there that to sound out their wills, and at the same time to treat with them of this business, is by no means convenient. This too was added, that I myself — who had resolved to solicit the rest in this matter — had even then to depart out of my homeland into Germany, the journey having been prepared from the feast of Easter onward, so that I was not even permitted to attend to my own affairs adequately in every part, much less to see to another's. Wherefore I trust that Your Highness will graciously hold me excused that nothing could be accomplished at that time in Erik's affairs on account of the aforesaid hindrances; and that, by your authority and clemency, you will bring it about that his creditors may be willing to rest content with him yet a little while longer, until a better means and occasion of satisfying them be given.

But the reasons for my migration into Germany it would be too long to set forth here to Your Highness. Nor indeed do the time and the pen now permit it. This at least I shall indicate in brief: that, when I had understood that those who now preside over political destinies among us favoured but little the arduous labours of our Urania, and were diverting their adequate maintenance by their clandestine counsels, and were not sufficiently abiding by those things which had been decreed concerning these matters by Frederick II, of most praiseworthy memory (with all the senators of the realm approving it after his death, and confirming it by letters), nor had been willing duly to admonish, as was fitting, our most serene King — who is otherwise endowed with an excellent disposition and nature — about these things: I judged that such difficulties must be met betimes, lest all those things should perish which now for so many years I have laboured at, with great effort and at no small expense, towards the restoration of astronomy. Wherefore I have withdrawn hither into Germany of my own accord, that I might seek other facilities for continuing and completing those very labours, whereby everything might be transmitted, whole and entire, to posterity for the benefit of the republic of letters. In which part, if Your Highness will not be too burdened to assist our desires with counsel and aid, you will have rendered a service most opportune — not to me alone, but to the whole study of astronomy, which your father of most praiseworthy memory pursued with so great a love, and by which you yourself too are affected in no small degree — and one to be celebrated with the grateful remembrance and praise both of those who now survive and also of those to come. And if Your Highness will graciously be willing to write anything to me on this matter, and to direct those letters to the governor [producem] of Holstein, Lord Heinrich Rantzau, he will readily see to it that they are carried thither, wherever in the world he may know me to be staying. Further, I was glad to learn that the engravings of my instruments, which I had sent to Your Highness, were duly delivered; and for the work of the canon of triangles given me in return (of which I had indeed earlier bought four copies), I humbly render thanks. In the meantime, however, I cannot perceive from Your Highness's letters or from elsewhere whether the book of astronomical letters — which I judged should be dedicated to Your Highness, because it contained your father's letters shared with me (and which for that reason too I persuaded myself would not be unwelcome) — has come into Your Highness's hands or not. But if the copy sent by me has gone astray, I shall take care that another be substituted for it as occasion offers: unless perhaps a supply of it was had from the last Frankfurt fair, where several copies of the same book were on sale. But I cease now to detain Your Highness with more, since I myself too, in this foreign place, amid very many other affairs and interruptions, have scarcely found leisure to write these very lines. When I shall have been restored to greater tranquillity, by the favour of the deity, I shall not fail to send still more and more accurate things. In the meantime, may Your Highness graciously receive these things, poured out with a hurrying pen and without due care, and may you fare well long and happily.

Given at Rostock, on the 17th day of June in the year 1597.
Most devoted to Your Highness,
Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender / context. Tycho Brahe, writing from Rostock in the first weeks of his self-imposed exile from Denmark. Having lost the favour of the regency government following Frederick II's death (1588) and the accession of the young Christian IV, Tycho had abandoned Hven in spring 1597, sailing from Copenhagen early in June; the letter is written during his stopover at Rostock while awaiting his effects by ship. He would go on to Wandsbek (the guest of Heinrich Rantzau), then to Prague.
  • Recipient. Mauritius, Hassiae Landtgravius = Moritz "the Learned," Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632), who had succeeded his father Wilhelm IV in 1592. The full address-style enumerates the subsidiary Hessian comital titles: Catzenelnpogen = Katzenelnbogen, Dietz = Diez, Zigenhain = Ziegenhain, Nidda.
  • Erici Langij, amici et affinis mei dilecti — Erik Lange (1553–1613), Danish nobleman and alchemist, betrothed to Tycho's sister Sophie Brahe in 1590 (in Danish law a binding tie — hence affinis, "kinsman by marriage"). Ruined by his pursuit of chrysopoeia, Lange had fled his creditors and was then on the Continent in Moritz's orbit; Moritz had written to Tycho reporting on the state of Lange's debts, which Tycho here acknowledges and to which he replies that the failure of the annual Danish comitia (Diet) had frustrated his plan to lobby Lange's noble kinsmen for repayment.
  • producem Holsatiae dominum Henricum Ranzovium — Heinrich Rantzau (Henricus Ranzovius, 1526–1598), the great Holstein humanist, royal Statthalter (governor; produx/produx here = lieutenant-governor) of the Danish duchies. Tycho names him as the relay-point for return correspondence; Rantzau was his host at Wandsbek shortly after.
  • liber epistolarum astronomicarum … dedicandum censui — Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (Uraniborg, 1596), the printed correspondence with Wilhelm IV and Christoph Rothmann, which Tycho dedicated to Moritz precisely because it contained his late father's letters ("paternas mecum communicatas literas"). The literature confirms the dedication-to-Moritz and its rationale (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens; the dedication leaf (:)4r). Tycho is here anxiously inquiring whether the presentation copy reached Moritz, offering a replacement, and noting copies were on sale at the most recent Frankfurt book fair (nundinae Francofurtenses).
  • icones instrumentorum meorum — "the engravings of my instruments": Tycho had sent Moritz a set of the instrument plates (the icones, later gathered in the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica, 1598). Icones rendered "engravings/figures" per the instrument register.
  • opere canonis triangulorum — "the work of the canon of triangles," i.e. a printed table of trigonometric functions (canon triangulorum = a canon/table for the solution of triangles), sent to Tycho by Moritz in return; Tycho notes he had already bought four copies. canon kept and glossed.
  • Astronomy. astronomiae instaurationem = "the restoration of astronomy" — Tycho's programmatic phrase (instauratio astronomiae), rendered by the edition's convention Uraniae nostrae … arduis laboribus = "the arduous labours of our Urania" (the Muse of astronomy, standing metonymically for Tycho's astronomical enterprise at Uraniborg).
  • Negation note (legal/political force). The long central sentence carries a chain of complaints against the regency, governed by cum intellexissem and strung on a minus … nec … neque structure; the three negative limbs are reproduced exactly: (1) they favoured but little (minus … favere) Tycho's work and were diverting (avertere) its funding; (2) they were not sufficiently abiding (nec … satis acquiescere) by Frederick II's decrees; (3) they had not been willing to admonish (neque … admonere voluisse) the young King. No negation has been added or dropped. The conditional si T. C. … non degravata fuerit ("if Your Highness will not be too burdened") is a single litotes-style negative — a courteous request, not a prohibition — and is rendered as such. clancularijs consilijs = "by their clandestine/secret counsels," kept at full pejorative force (no softening to "private").
  • sarcta tecta — a fixed legal-administrative idiom ("in good repair, whole and entire," lit. of buildings kept sarta tecta, "patched and roofed"); rendered "whole and entire."
  • Calendar. calendis ipsis junij = "on the very Kalends of June" (= 1 June); Roman-calendar idiom kept and glossed by the edition's convention. The dateline Dabantur Rostochio die 17. junij anni 1597 = "Given at Rostock, 17 June 1597" agrees with the catalogue label; O.S. (Denmark and the Lutheran German principalities were still on the Julian calendar in 1597). post ascensionis festum / a paschatis festo — movable feasts (Ascension; Easter), kept as named feasts, not converted to civil dates.
  • Subscription. Tuae Cels. addictiss. = "Most devoted to Your Highness" (Celsitudini Tuae addictissimus, by the edition's convention); manu propria = "with his own hand" (the original is an autograph letter, per the MS witness).
