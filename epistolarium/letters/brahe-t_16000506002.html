The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD RUDOLPHUM CORRADUCIUM.

ILLUSTRIS ET GENEROSE VIR, DOMINE OBSERVANDISSIME.

CUM rumor hîc nuper increbuisset, Sacram Cæsam Matem, Dominum nostrum Clementissimum, Pragam sub initia modo elapsæ hebdomadæ redituram, et unâ cum ea vestram Illem Domnem ibi affuturam, mox eo excurrens nihil magis in votis habui quam vestram Illem Domnem coram intueri et officiosissime compellare, prout magno eius rei desiderio diu flagravi. Et licet spe hac nunc frustratus sim, id tamen propediem meliori successu futurum confido. Interim domesticum illum meum, qui prius Pilsnæ fuit, isthuc ad vestram Illem Domnem denuo ablego. Et quoniam FRANCISCUS TENGNAGLIUS noster, qui meo nomine vestram Illem Domnem ibi convenit, mihi, cum in procinctu esset sui isthinc in Patriam abitus, per literas fiduciam fecit de nonnullis, quæ Sacra Cæsa Matas, intercessione Illustris Dominationis vestræ, mihi Clementissime indulsit, quorum tamen necdum, ob alias intervenientes remoras, compos factus sum: idcirco Illem Domem vestram, qua possum reverentia rogatam habeo, velit, quatenus per alias suas gravissimas occupationes licet, ut ea executioni committantur, efficere non degravari, inprimis vero quod Prædium illud hîc in veteri Bennaticka mihi Clementissime ab ipsius Cæsa Mate deputatum (illud enim necdum, licet anni tempus eius culturam urgeat, accepi) si itaque Illris Domo vestra ad Capitaneum Brandeisensem hac de re pauculis scribere atque ei ex Clementissimo Sacræ Cæsæ Matis mandato serio iniungere, ut Prædium mihi hoc absque diuturniore dilatione aut apud alios solicitatione tradat, non dubito, quin is sit obtemperaturus: tum quoque ut vestra Illris Domio de ædificijs his Benaticensibus pro Instrumentis meis parandis, et si quæ sunt alia huc facientia, quatenus fieri debeant vel secus, ut eorum structura diu iam neglecta compleatur, provisionem [com]petentem facere velit. Omnia enim hîc nimis lente et difficulter procedunt, nec habeo commoda loca, in quibus organa mea Astronomica, ubi huc Magdæburgo et Hamburgo adlata fuerint, reponam, quo ab iniuria aëris et pluviarum tuta sint. Sed de his atque alijs, quæ necessitas cogit me ad vestræ Illris Domis opem et promotionem confugere, is, quem nunc denuo mitto, plenius [per] oportunitatem exponet. Nolo itaque Illrem Domem vestram alijs quam plurimis gravissimis Reipub. curis distentam hisce remorari.

Deus Opt. Max. eam quam diutissime feliciter valere et florere faciat.
Datæ ex Arce Benaticka die 6 Maij Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Rudolf Coraduz.

Illustrious and generous man, most-to-be-revered lord.

When a rumour lately grew strong here that the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most Clement Lord, would return to Prague about the beginning of the week just past, and that your Illustrious Lordship would be there together with him, I soon ran thither, having nothing more in my wishes than to behold your Illustrious Lordship in person and address you most dutifully — as I have long burned with great desire for that. And although I am now frustrated of this hope, I trust it will shortly come about with better success. Meanwhile I send that servant of mine, who was earlier at Pilsen, again thither to your Illustrious Lordship. And since our Franz Tengnagel, who waited on your Illustrious Lordship there in my name, gave me assurance by letter — when he was on the point of his departure thence for his homeland — concerning certain things which the Sacred Imperial Majesty, by your Illustrious Lordship's intercession, most clemently granted me (of which, however, I have not yet been made possessor, on account of other intervening delays): I therefore have your Illustrious Lordship asked, with what reverence I can, that — so far as your other most weighty occupations allow — you would not find it a burden to bring it about that these be put into execution. Above all, as regards that estate here in old Benátky most clemently assigned to me by the Imperial Majesty (for I have not yet received it, though the season of the year presses for its cultivation): if your Illustrious Lordship would therefore write a few words to the Captain of Brandeis about this matter, and earnestly enjoin him, by the most clement mandate of the Sacred Imperial Majesty, to hand over this estate to me without longer delay or soliciting among others, I doubt not he would obey. Then too, that your Illustrious Lordship would make competent provision concerning these buildings at Benátky for the preparing of my instruments — and whatever else conduces hither, how far they ought to be done or otherwise — so that their construction, now long neglected, may be completed. For everything here proceeds too slowly and with difficulty, nor have I convenient places in which to store my astronomical instruments, when they shall have been brought hither from Magdeburg and Hamburg, so that they be safe from the injury of weather and rains. But of these matters and others, in which necessity compels me to take refuge in your Illustrious Lordship's aid and furtherance, the man whom I now send again will more fully explain at opportunity. I do not wish, therefore, to detain with these [lines] your Illustrious Lordship, stretched as you are by so many most weighty cares of the commonwealth.

May God, best and greatest, make you fare happily and flourish for the longest time.
Given from the castle of Benátky, the 6th of May, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender & purpose. Tycho Brahe at Benátky — pressing, through the highest channel, for the practical fulfilment of the Emperor's promises: the assigned estate and the observatory buildings. The letter is a key document for the troubled material history of the Benátky establishment (where Kepler had just been working).
  • Recipient. Rudolphus Corraducius = Rudolf Coraduz (Coraduz), imperial vice-chancellor under Rudolf II — one of Tycho's two principal court patrons (the Coraduz/Barvitius axis; cf. brahe-t_16000813001). Addressed as Illustris Dominatio Vestra, "your Illustrious Lordship."
  • The two asks.
  • The estate (Praedium) in "old Benátky" (vetus Bennaticka, Staré Benátky), granted by the Emperor but not yet handed over — "though the season of the year presses for its cultivation"; Tycho asks Coraduz to order the Captain of Brandeis to deliver it.
  • Provision for the Benátky buildings to house and set up Tycho's instruments (organa Astronomica,), whose construction is "long neglected"; Tycho has nowhere safe to store the instruments once they arrive from Magdeburg and Hamburg (the consignment of his effects out of Germany — cf. the Magdeburg-goods letter brahe-t_16000926001). "Everything here proceeds too slowly and with difficulty."
  • Persons named.
  • Franciscus Tengnaglius noster = Franz Tengnagel, who had interceded with Coraduz in Tycho's name before departing for Westphalia (cf. brahe-t_16000306002).
  • Capitaneus Brandeisensis = the Captain of Brandeis (Brandýs), the local officer who must convey the estate.
  • Sacra Caesarea Maiestas = Rudolf II.
  • Places. Benaticka arx / vetus Bennaticka = Benátky / old Benátky; Pilsna = Plzeň (the court's refuge); Magdeburgum, Hamburgum = Magdeburg and Hamburg (whence the instruments were coming).
  • Epistolary formulae. Datae (epistolary) → "Given"; Deus Opt. Max. … florere faciat → the closing benediction.
  • Calendar & header/label note. The dateline reads die 6 Maij Anno 1600; the edition's catalogue label gives 5 May (n.s.). The header therefore leads with the as-written 6 May and records the catalogue's 5 May as (catalogued 5 May) by this chapter's convention, rather than adopting it silently. New Style (Imperial service), not converted.
