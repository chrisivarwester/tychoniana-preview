The original

SERENISSIMO AC POTENTISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO. DOMINO FERDINANDO, MAGNO HETRURIÆ, FLORENTIÆ, SENORUM AC PISARUM DUCI, DOMINO SUO CLEMENTISSIMO AD MANUS PROPRIAS.

SERENISSIME et Potentissime Princeps, Domine Clementissime. Quod Serenissima Celsitudo Tua mihi, licet de facie ignoto et tam longe dissito, adeo clementer et benevole superiori æstate scribere non dedignata fuerit, humillima et obsequentissima mente eidem gratias ago quas possum maximas. Accepi eas literas, cum primum Pragam sub initia Iulij, a Sa Cæsareâ Maiestate, Domino meo Clementissimo, isthuc e Germania vocatus, et benignissime susceptus advenissem. Quæ sane non parvâ me affecerunt voluptate, quod inde perciperem tantum Principem, inter Europæos paucissimis cedentem, non saltem me immeritum Clementissimo suo favore prosequi, sed et Divinæ illius Astronomiæ (cui plurimis abhinc Annis, arduo et indefesso studio, nec modicis impensis operam navavi) mysterijs solerter et iucunde affici, prout hæc sublimis et Heroica Scientia, inde ab initio Regibus et Principibus, Heroicisque Viris, in sublimi rerum mundanarum fastigio collocatis, semper acceptissima, atque ab ijs in præcipuo honore habita fuit. Quâ etiam de caussâ Augustissimus Imperator RUDOLPHUS II me unâ cum Uraniâ nostrâ, Patriâ, quod ibi debite conservari et perpetuari nequiret, sponte excedentem huc in Bohemiam (uti dictum) ubi nunc in Arce quâdam Cæsareanâ, itinere saltem dimidij diei Pragâ remotâ, eius fundamina et culturam denuo instauro, arcessiri voluit, atque huic Regali Studio, Regaliter quoque et liberaliter prospicere non degravatus est. Libros quos Serenissimæ Tuæ Celsitudini humillime misi, eidem gratos fuisse, mihi plurimum congratulor.

Quoniam vero ex prædictis Serenissimæ Celsitudinis Tuæ literis percepi, illam plura ex meis in re Astronomicâ Operibus Clementissimo animo requirere, nullatenus intermittam, quin per primam quamque oportunitatem, ipsius optimæ voluntati, quâ possum reverentiâ, morem geram; et præstitissem id iam antea quam lubentissime, si supellex mea libraria, quæ Magdæburgi in Germania etiamnum reservatur, huc allata fuisset, quod tamen instanti vere, ubi Albis fluvius hoc hyberno tempore congelatus, denuo erit navigabilis, futurum spero. Sic enim a Cæsareâ Maiestate Magdæburgensibus per literas iniunctum est. Ex quo insuper Serenissima Celsitudo Tua Nobilem et Eruditum Adolescentem, FRANCISCUM TENGNAGLIUM, meum aliquandiu domesticum, qui dictos libros Serenissimæ Tuæ Celsitudini submisse a me obtulit, ex aliquali nostrâ humillimâ commendatione Clementissimo favore non indignum censuit, sed ita sibi acceptum habuit, ut nuper huc ad me reversus Serenissimæ Celsitudinis Tuæ Clementissimam et singularem erga se benignitatem satis deprædicare nequiverit, id equidem tanti beneficij loco reputo, ac si a Tuâ Serenissimâ Celsitudine mihi ipsi præstitum foret. Et quia vel inde Serenissimæ Celsitudinis Tuæ Clementissimum erga me animum haud obscure intelligam, non verebor Serenissimam Tuam Celsitudinem in alio quodam haud dissimili negotio eo audentius demisse compellare, præsertim cum Ipsamet in suis ad me Clementissimis literis benignissima sua officia mihi ultro offerre dignetur. Est autem eiuscemodi.

Cuperem quam maxime non saltem in Italia, ubi minor est quam hîc Sphæræ Mundanæ inclinatio, sed et in Ægypto apud Alexandriam, siquidem ibi adhuc rectior est, nonnulla in Astrorum Observationibus, quæ hîc non ita patent, perficere. Quamobrem filium meum natu maiorem ex me ipso denominatum, adolescentem 18 Annorum, quem inde ab ipsa pueritia ad hæc exercitia assuefeci, adiuncto illi pro comite quodam ex meis studiosis, qui usque in tertium Annum in rebus Astronomicis factitandis fideliter et sedulo mihi inservivit, harumque non vulgarem peritiam sibi interea comparavit, favente Divino Numine, instanti vere, vel quamprimum fieri poterit, ad Smam Tuam Celnem in Hetruriam mittere, tradito illi uno vel altero Instrumento ex meis, quod portatile sit (si modo id commode effici queat) unâ cum instructione, quomodo plura, prout opus fuerit, isthic paranda sint, et quænam ijs cœlitus denotanda. Daboque simul operam, ut is aliquos librorum meorum a S. T. Celne clementissime desideraturum secum adferat. Deinde studiosus ille, relicto in Smæ Tuæ Celnis Aulico servitio (modo S. T. Celni sic placuerit) filio meo, in Ægyptum navigabit, atque ea quæ isthic ulterius observari poterunt, iuxta meam præscriptionem pariter designabit. Qua in re Inclyta Venetiarum Respublica, quando aliquam eo navem ablegat, competentem promotionem (uti etiam spem aliqualem prædicto TENGNAGLIO, hac de re, dum ibi esset, meo nomine solicitanti fecit) potissimum vero Tuæ Serenissimæ Celsitudinis authoritate et intercessione allecta, non est (uti spero) denegatura. Si itaque Sma Celdo Tua filium meum unâ cum adiuncto ipsi comite, per annuum tempus aut circiter in Aula sua apud se retinere, sibique commendatum habere, pro singulari suo et Clementissimo erga me favore, non aversetur, quibus possum precibus demisse oro, ut de sua Clementissima hac in parte voluntate me per literas certiorem reddere non degravetur, aut etiam id Generosissimo et Magnificentissimo suo apud Sam Cæsam Matem Legato, qui Pragæ est, significare. Fecerit sic Serenissima Celsitudo Tua non saltem mihi humillimo suo servitori, ad omnia obsequia pro virili præstanda paratissimo, sed et ipsi Cæsareæ Maiestati, cui hæc studia cordi sunt, rem (uti non dubito) inprimis acceptam et Serenissimæ Tuæ Celsitudini (modo ipsius laudibus aliquid accedere possit) gloriosam et apud omnem posteritatem commendabilem.

Sed desino Serenissimam Tuam Celsitudinem, gravissimis Ditionum suarum rebus gubernandis intentam, ulterius interpellare. Deus Opt. Max. eam quam diutissime sospitem et florentem conservet.

Dabantur ex Arce Cæsareanâ Benaticâ Die 24 Ianuarij An. 1600.
Serenissimæ Celsitudinis Vestræ humillimus servitor
TYCHO BRAHE, manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most serene and most powerful Prince and Lord, Lord Ferdinando, Grand Duke of Tuscany, Florence, Siena and Pisa, his most clement Lord — into his own hands.

Most serene and most powerful Prince, most clement Lord. That Your most serene Highness did not disdain to write to me — though unknown by face and so far distant — so clemently and benevolently last summer, I give You, with most humble and obedient mind, the greatest thanks I can. I received that letter when I had first come to Prague, about the beginning of July, called hither from Germany by the Sacred Imperial Majesty, my most clement Lord, and most kindly received. It affected me indeed with no small delight, that I perceived thence so great a Prince — yielding to very few among Europeans — not only to pursue me, undeserving, with His most clement favour, but also to be skilfully and pleasantly taken with the mysteries of that divine Astronomy (on which, for very many years now, with arduous and untiring study, and at no modest expense, I have bestowed my labour) — as this sublime and Heroic Science has, from the beginning, always been most acceptable to Kings and Princes and Heroic Men set on the lofty summit of worldly things, and held by them in special honour. For which cause also the most august Emperor Rudolf II willed that I be summoned hither into Bohemia — withdrawing of my own accord, together with our Urania, from my fatherland, because she could not there be duly preserved and perpetuated — where now, in a certain imperial Castle, a journey of but half a day distant from Prague [Benátky], I again restore her foundations and cultivation; and He has not thought it beneath Him to provide for this Royal Study, royally too and liberally. The books which I most humbly sent to Your most serene Highness, that they were welcome to You, I greatly rejoice.

But since from Your most serene Highness's said letter I perceived that You, with most clement mind, desire more of my Works in Astronomical matters, I will by no means fail, at the first opportunity, to comply with Your excellent wish, with what reverence I can; and I would have done it most gladly already, had my library-stock — which is still kept at Magdeburg in Germany — been brought hither; which, however, I hope will come about this coming spring, when the river Elbe, frozen in this winter season, shall again be navigable (for so it has been enjoined upon the Magdeburgers by the Imperial Majesty, by letter). Moreover, since Your most serene Highness judged the noble and learned youth Franz Tengnagel — for some while a member of my household, who humbly presented those books to Your most serene Highness from me — not unworthy, on some humble commendation of ours, of Your most clement favour, but held him so acceptable that, lately returned hither to me, he could not sufficiently proclaim Your Highness's most clement and singular kindness toward him: I count this in the place of so great a benefit, as if it had been done to me myself by Your most serene Highness. And because even thence I clearly understand Your Highness's most clement disposition toward me, I will not fear the more boldly and humbly to address Your most serene Highness on another, not dissimilar, business — especially since You Yourself, in Your most clement letter to me, deign of Your own accord to offer me Your most kindly services. It is of this kind.

I should most greatly wish to perfect certain observations of the stars — which here are not so open [to view] — not only in Italy (where the inclination of the celestial Sphere is less than here), but also in Egypt, at Alexandria, since there it is yet more upright [rectior]. Wherefore my elder son, named after myself, a youth of 18 years, whom from his very boyhood I have habituated to these exercises — adding to him as companion a certain one of my students, who has faithfully and diligently served me, even into a third year, in carrying out astronomical work, and has meanwhile acquired no common skill in these things — I purpose, God favouring, this coming spring, or as soon as it can be done, to send to Your most serene Highness into Tuscany, having given him one or two of my Instruments, such as is portable (if only it can conveniently be made so), together with instruction how more are to be prepared there as need requires, and what is to be marked by them from the heavens. And I will at the same time take care that he bring with him some of my books, which Your Highness will most clemently desire. Then that student — my son being left in Your most serene Highness's court service (if so it please Your Highness) — will sail to Egypt, and will likewise mark down what can there be further observed, according to my prescription. In which matter the renowned Republic of Venice, when it sends some ship thither, will not (as I hope) refuse competent furtherance — as it gave some hope, too, to the aforesaid Tengnagel, soliciting on this matter in my name while he was there — but above all drawn by Your most serene Highness's authority and intercession. If, then, Your most serene Highness, of Your singular and most clement favour toward me, would not be averse to keep my son, together with the companion adjoined to him, for a year's time or thereabouts in Your Court with You, and to hold him commended, I humbly beg, with what prayers I can, that You would not think it a burden to inform me by letter of Your most clement will in this part, or even to signify it to Your most noble and magnificent Legate at the Imperial court, who is at Prague. So would Your most serene Highness do a thing — as I do not doubt — especially welcome not only to me, Your most humble servant, most ready to render all obedience to the utmost, but also to the Imperial Majesty Himself, to whom these studies are dear; and glorious to Your most serene Highness (if anything can be added to Your praises), and commendable to all posterity.

But I cease to interrupt Your most serene Highness further, intent on the weightiest affairs of governing Your Dominions. May God, Best and Greatest, preserve You safe and flourishing as long as possible.

Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 24th day of January, the year 1600.
Your most serene Highness's most humble servant,
Tycho Brahe, written in my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Ferdinando I de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany (Florence, Siena, Pisa) — the same correspondent as brahe-t_16010430001 (30 April 1601). This is the earlier of the pair (Jan 1600 vs Apr 1601).
  • Significance — the Alexandria/Egypt observation plan and the bid to place the son at the Medici court (the back-story to the letter of 30 April 1601).
  • The low-latitude observation project. Tycho's striking ambition: to make observations impossible at Bohemian latitude by sending observers south — to Italy, "where the inclination of the celestial Sphere is less," and above all to Alexandria in Egypt, "where it is yet more upright" (nearer the sphaera recta of the equator, where the southern stars and the zodiac ride higher — the classic site of Ptolemaic astronomy). A rare glimpse of Tycho planning a southern expedition to extend his star-work.
  • The personnel. His elder son, Tycho Brahe the Younger ("named after myself," aged 18), trained "from boyhood," with a student-companion of three years' astronomical service, to carry one or two portable instruments plus instructions; the son to remain in the Medici court, the student to sail on to Egypt.
  • The Venice connection. Tycho counts on the Republic of Venice to ship the student to Egypt — hope already raised to Tengnagel when he solicited there — especially with the Grand Duke's backing.
  • Tengnagel's Florence embassy. Confirms Franz Tengnagel carried Tycho's gift books to Ferdinando and was warmly received — the diplomatic groundwork.
  • Aftermath cross-reference. The plan did not unfold as designed: by April 1601 (brahe-t_16010430001) the son had gone to Italy with the Persian embassy instead, was left at Siena, and the Capuchin calumny had made Rome dangerous — with Tycho then seeking to place him at Henri IV's court. Read the letter of 24 January 1600 and that of 30 April 1601 as the plan and its derailment.
  • Other details. Tycho's library is still stored at Magdeburg, awaiting the spring thaw of the Elbe (the Emperor having ordered the Magdeburgers to release it) — which is why he cannot yet send the Grand Duke more books. He writes from Benátky, "half a day from Prague," restoring "Urania's foundations."
  • Persons & places named. Ferdinando I de' Medici; Rudolf II; Franz Tengnagel (the book-bearing emissary); Tycho Brahe the Younger (the 18-year-old son); the unnamed student-companion (3 years' service, bound for Egypt); the Republic of Venice; the Tuscan Legate at Prague (unnamed). Places: Benátky, Prague, Germany/Magdeburg, the Elbe, Italy/Tuscany, Egypt/Alexandria, Venice.
  • Calendar. Die 24 Ianuarij An. 1600 at Benátky (imperial reckoning) → 24 January 1600 [N.S.]; header and label agree.
