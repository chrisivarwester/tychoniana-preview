The original

EXEMPLAR LITERARVM ILLVSTRISSIMI PRINCIPIS WILHELMI HASSIÆ LANDTGRAVII AD NOBILISSIMVM DOMINVM HENRICVM RANTZOVIVM, DE COMETA ANNI 85, QUÆ EX PARTE OCCASIONEM PRÆBUERUNT ALIQUOT SUBSEQUENTIBUS.
WILHELM VON GOTTES GNADEN LANDTGRAUE ZU HESSEN/ GRAUE ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ DIETZ/ ZIGENHAYN VND NIDDA.

VNSERN gnedigen gruß zuuor/ Vester lieber besonder/ wir haben ewer schreiben de dato Segenberg/ den 22 verschienes Monats Septemb. neben dem vns zugeschickten Buch empfangen/ Thun vns deßwegen gegen euch gnediglich bedancken/ vnd wollen dasselbige zu seiner zeit mit lust verlesen. Darneben wollen wir euch auch nicht verhalten/ das vff angeben Pauli Wittichij/ wir vnsere Instrumenta Mathematica dermassen verbessert/ das/ da wir zuuor kaum 2 Min. scharff/ wir jetzo ½ ja ¼ einer min. obseruiren können/ Haben vns derhalben vff die Art Quadrantem Horizontalem, deßgleichen ein Sextantem, ad obseruandas distantias Stellarum inter se, lassen zurichten/ jedes von gutem Messing vnd bicubital. Halten auch drey Gesellen Astronomiæ et Obseruationum peritos ad iustificanda loca Stellarum Fixarum, Wiewol vnsere vorige Obseruationes, die wir selbst in otio vor angehung vnser Regierung nostris oculis genommen/ vnd dauon jhr als wir nicht anders wissen/ Copey gehabt/ nit vber 4 oder minut. vffs meiste ab hisce discrepiren werden/ hoffen auch bald damit zu endt zukommen/ wann wir nur klar Wetter circa nouilunium hetten/ welches vns diß gantze Jahr vber gemangelt. Nuhn has vns vnser Mathematicus CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS zugeschrieben/ dz er einen kleinen Cometen vff den 8 huius, Abends vmb 9 vhr in 23 grad. 15 minut. piscium, cum Latitudine merid. 14 grad. 0 min. Vnd vff den 9 eiusdem in 25 grad. 50 min. piscium cum Latitud. merid. 13 grad. et 6 min. obseruiret, Denselbigen haben wir gestern den 19 zu Abend allhier doch mit keinen Instrumenten, sondern allein per lineas rectas vff vnserm Globo obseruiret, vnd jhn im 21 gr. Arietis, cum Declinatione latitud. 4 gr. merid. gefunden. Er ist klein/ vnd vmb vnd vmb hero voller Haer/ das wir achten/ er sey ex genere Cometarum, so man vnsers versehens Circæos nennet/ vnd tempore maximarum coniunctionum zuerscheinen pflegen/ Was er nun bedeuten wird/ sonderlich dieweil er gar Saturninus, vnd Saturno coniunctus, gibt die zeit/ vnd möchten wol wissen/ ob er auch bey euch gesehen werde/ Mögen auch wol leiden/ das jhr TYCHONEM BRAHEN mit vormeldung vnsers gnedigen grusses/ Apparitionem et Obseruationem huius Cometæ, zuwissen thut/ mit begeren/ das er vns zuwissen thue/ wie er jhn Obseruiret habe/ Denn weil er so klein/ halten wirs dafür/ er werde von weinigen obseruirt werden/ Das wollen wir vns vorsehen/ vnd seind euch mit Gnaden geneigt/

Datum Rottenburgk am 20 Octobris, Anno 85.

English translation machine translation, Probable

A copy of a letter of the Most Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, to the Most Noble Lord Heinrich Rantzau, concerning the comet of the year [15]85, which letters in part gave occasion to several of those that follow.
Wilhelm by the grace of God Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Catzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain and Nidda.

Our gracious greeting beforehand. Our trusty and well-beloved: we have received your letter dated Segeberg, the 22nd of the past month of September, together with the book sent to us, and we therefore thank you graciously for it, and intend to read it through with pleasure in due time. Besides this we will also not conceal from you that, upon the instruction of Paul Wittich, we have so improved our mathematical instruments [Instrumenta Mathematica] that, whereas before we could scarcely observe [to] 2 minutes precisely, we can now observe [to] half — indeed a quarter — of a minute. We have therefore had set up after that fashion a horizontal quadrant [Quadrantem Horizontalem], likewise a sextant [Sextantem], for observing the distances of the stars from one another [ad observandas distantias Stellarum inter se], each of good brass and two cubits [in size]. We also keep three journeymen skilled in astronomy and in observations [Astronomiae et Observationum peritos] for verifying the places of the fixed stars [ad iustificanda loca Stellarum Fixarum] — although our earlier observations, which we ourselves took at leisure with our own eyes [nostris oculis] before the commencement of our reign, and of which, so far as we know, you have had a copy, will at most differ from these by no more than 4 minutes [or so]; and we hope soon to come to an end with it, if only we had clear weather around the new moon [circa novilunium], which has been lacking to us this whole year through. Now our mathematician Christoph Rothmann has written to us that he observed a small comet on the 8th of this month, in the evening at about 9 o'clock, in 23 degrees 15 minutes of Pisces, with southern latitude [cum Latitudine merid.] 14 degrees 0 minutes; and on the 9th of the same [month] in 25 degrees 50 minutes of Pisces, with southern latitude 13 degrees and 6 minutes. This same [comet] we observed here yesterday, the 19th, in the evening — yet with no instruments, but only by straight lines [per lineas rectas] upon our globe [Globo] — and found it in 21 degrees of Aries, with a southern declination of latitude [cum Declinatione latitud.] of 4 degrees. It is small, and all about it full of hair [hairy], so that we judge it to be of the kind of comets [ex genere Cometarum] that, as we suppose, are called Circaei, and that are wont to appear at the time of the great conjunctions [tempore maximarum coniunctionum]. What it will now signify — especially since it is wholly Saturnine [Saturninus], and conjoined with Saturn [Saturno coniunctus] — time will tell; and we should well like to know whether it has been seen with you also. We can also well allow that you make known to Tycho Brahe, with the conveyance of our gracious greeting, the appearance and observation of this comet [Apparitionem et Observationem huius Cometae], with the desire that he make known to us how he has observed it; for, since it is so small, we hold it [likely] that it will be observed by few. This we shall await, and we are inclined to you with [our] grace.

Given at Rotenburg, the 20th of October, in the year [15]85.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the observing prince of Kassel, builder of one of the two great pre-telescopic observatories (with Tycho's on Hven). The titulature names his subsidiary fiefs: Catzenelnbogen (Katzenelnbogen), Dietz (Diez), Ziegenhain, Nidda.
  • Recipient. Henricus Rantzovius = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), royal governor (Statthalter) of the Danish duchies of Schleswig-Holstein, humanist patron and astrological writer, and a hub of scientific correspondence linking Tycho, Kassel, and the German learned world. His seat at Segeberg (Segenberg) is the place of his own letter here acknowledged.
  • Document type and frame. The piece is not a letter to or from Tycho but a copy (Exemplar) of Wilhelm's letter to Rantzau, which Tycho printed as a prefatory document in his Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596) because, as the Latin rubric says, it "in part gave occasion" to the correspondence that follows. The rubric and the full titulature are in Latin, the body in chancery German with constant code-switching into Latin for technical nouns — preserved by the edition's convention
  • Language note. The body is German (Early New High German / chancery: vff = auf, dz = daß, jhn = ihn, zuuor = zuvor, vmb = um, weinigen = wenigen), not Danish despite the de heuristic tag — verified by reading. The opening VNSERN gnedigen gruß zuuor / Vester lieber besonder is the standard princely address formula, rendered by the edition's convention ("Our gracious greeting beforehand … Our trusty and well-beloved").
  • Paul Wittich. Pauli Wittichij = Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586), the Wrocław mathematician whose visit to Kassel in 1584 transmitted the instrument-improvement techniques (sights, transversal/diagonal graduation) he had learned at Hven — the reciprocal-influence figure between Tycho and Rothmann. The claim that Wittich's "instruction" raised Kassel's precision from c. 2′ to ¼′ is Wilhelm's own (not silently sharpened).
  • Instruments. Quadrantem Horizontalem → horizontal quadrant; Sextantem … ad observandas distantias Stellarum inter se → sextant, for observing the distances of the stars from one another; bicubital → two cubits [in size] (about 1 metre on a side). Globo → globe (a celestial globe, on which Wilhelm read off the comet's place graphically, per lineas rectas, "by straight lines," lacking instruments that evening).
  • Astronomical terms. loca Stellarum Fixarum → places of the fixed stars; iustificanda → verifying (here in the sense of correcting/confirming the catalogue places); circa novilunium → around the new moon; Declinatione → declination (but note the source's own redundant phrase Declinatione latitud., lit. "declination of latitude," flagged below). Positions are given in zodiacal longitude (degrees within a sign) with latitude; merid. = meridionalis, southern.
  • Astrology. Saturninus … Saturno coniunctus → Saturnine … conjoined with Saturn: the comet is read as Saturn-natured and in conjunction with Saturn, hence ominous; tempore maximarum coniunctionum → at the time of the great(est) conjunctions (the Saturn–Jupiter great-conjunction doctrine). Pisces and Aries are named in English by the edition's convention (the source writes the sign-names in Latin, piscium / Arietis; no glyphs appear).
  • Circaeos — Circaei, a comet-class name. Wilhelm flags his own uncertainty (vnsers versehens, "as we suppose"), preserved as a hedge. The term is not in the standard Aristotelian/Ptolemaic comet taxonomies in this spelling; it appears to render a category of "hairy"/bearded comets (cf. the description vmb vnd vmb hero voller Haer, "all about full of hair"). Left untranslated and flagged as a lexical crux rather than forced to a modern class.
  • Comet of 1585. The object is the comet of October 1585, independently observed at Kassel (by Rothmann, 8–9 Oct.) and noted here by Wilhelm (19 Oct., by globe). The request that Rantzau forward the appearance and observation to Tycho Brahe — and ask how Tycho observed it — is the occasion of the ensuing Tycho–Kassel exchange.
  • Calendar. Both dates — Rantzau's letter of 22 September (Segeberg) and Wilhelm's of 20 October (Rotenburg) — are Old Style; Hesse, like Denmark, retained the Julian calendar until 1700. Datum Rottenburgk am 20 Octobris, Anno 85 = "Given at Rotenburg (Rotenburg an der Fulda), 20 October [15]85" (O.S.). The internal dates (comet seen "on the 8th … and on the 9th," and "yesterday, the 19th") are consistent with the 20 October dateline and with the catalogue label; no discrepancy. Per the edition's convention no date is converted.
  • Negation check. The source carries two consequential negations, both reproduced and neither added: (1) wir … wollen euch auch nicht verhalten → "we will also not conceal from you" (a courtesy litotes = "we wish you to know"); (2) werden … nit vber 4 … minut. … discrepiren → "will differ by no more than 4 minutes" — the precision boast, where the negation governs the magnitude and must not be dropped or doubled.
