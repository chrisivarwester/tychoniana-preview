The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM

S. P.

REVERENDE et Praestantissime Vir, Amice honorande, quas cum filio meo huc revertente mihi rescripsisti literas, circa huius labentis Anni initia accepi, et pro Amicâ atque candidâ congratulatione de conditionibus, quas Deus Opt. Max. apud hunc Augustissimum Imperatorem mihi et meis studijs prospexit, proque benevolo tuo voto, quo precaris, ut omnia inposterum feliciter stabiliantur et adaugeantur, maximas quas possum tibi habeo gratias. Elucet hinc sincerus ille tuus affectus, quo me diu multumque prosecutus es; invenies me pari perpetuo erga te tuosque animo. Et certe sic est, uti scribis, agnosco Divini Numinis singularem providentiam et benignitatem, quâ me a difficultatibus, quibus in Patriâ obnoxius fui, Clementer ereptum in portu tranquilliore hîc locavit. Et sane non possum satis depraedicare huius optimi Imperatoris non saltem Caesareum, sed et Paternum (si sic loqui liceret) erga me animum; qui vel inde perspicitur, quod licet ipsius Matas ob Epidemicos morbos, qui gravissime Bohemiam hoc antecedente Anno afflixerunt, Pragâ abfuerit, et in civitate Pilsna hinc 15 miliaribus distante sese continuerit, durante adhuc infectione illâ, atque in hoc quoque loco, quem inhabitandum mihi concessit, totâque viciniâ saeviente, tamen proprio motu de me et meis sollicita fuerit, adeo ut aliquoties huc, quomodo valeremus, expiscatum miserit, simul Clementissime offerendo, si in quamcunque aliam ipsius Matis Arcem, ubi tutius degere foret, secedere vellem, id mihi licere, liberum etiam relinquendo, si Viennam Austriae me conferre luberet, ubi tempestive de commodâ habitatione caeterisque necessarijs mihi prospicere vellet. Atqui ego, actis humilime gratijs, me hîc potius in statione meâ mansurum et Divinae protectioni ac voluntati omnia commissurum respondi. Agoque eidem Deo misericordi protectori toto pectore gratias, quod me totamque domum meam, in tantis periculis, quibus undique cingebamur, incolumes conservârit, oroque, ut inposterum suâ gratiâ nobis adesse dignetur.

Recte a te factum, quod Historiolam nostrae in Bohemiam migrationis, et eorum, quae postea mihi hîc evenerunt, quam OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO, communi amico, ob eam quae nobis intercedit amicitiam intimam, ut de consanguinitate et affinitate nihil dicam, paulo luculentius retuleram, resignaveris atque perlegeris. Cumque inde statum mearum rerum sufficienter perceperis, in quo etiam Dei benignitate nunc persistit atque inposterum, eodem favente, plenius augeri poterit, non duco operaeprecium quidpiam de eodem hîc repetere. Expecto brevi supellectilem meam et Instrumenta Magdaeburgi relicta, tum quoque ea, quae Hamburgi sunt, quae per meos emissarios e Dania ante hyemem eo transferri curavi; scripsit enim Imperator ad utriusque Civitatis Magistratum, ut in sui gratiam illa omnia huc per Albim advehi faciant: cumque Magdaeburgenses Senatores subterfugia quaedam quaererent, ipsius Matas ad Capitulum eiusdem loci hoc nomine alias dedit literas, quod se lubentissime ipsius Caeae Matis voluntati obtemperaturum promisit; interea structurae pro Instrumentis hîc debito modo disponendis parantur.

Quod M. CHRISTIERNO LONGOMONTANO hortator fuisti, ut me accederet, idque ei persuasisti, pergratum mihi est; dabo operam, ut ipsum huius itineris non poeniteat, liberumque ei relinquam vel hîc apud me vel Pragae esse, quam diu voluerit.

De coeteris, quae scribis, bene inter nos convenit, prout aequum et oportunum fuit. Audio apud vos esse Suecum quendam Astronomicis studijs addictum, nomine ........ qui etiam Observationibus Coelestibus delectatur; si efficere poteris, ut is huc veniat, erit id mihi acceptum et ipsi non inutile; iuvabo eius conatus, quantum potero.

Audio Regnum Suecicum illud, continuis bellis et tumultuationibus iam diu conquassatum, in potestatem CAROLI Ducis venisse. Deus flectat eius cor, ut hac victoriâ et potentiâ moderate utatur, atque ijs, quos pro adversarijs habet, clementer parcat.

Nova, quae ex his oris communicem, vix aliqua, quae momentum habeant, in promptu sunt. Bellum Ungaricum magnâ vi continuatur et in futuram aestatem utrinque apparatum. Dicunt Turcam longe maiori exercitu adfuturum quam unquam antea; noster Imperator, licet a Principibus Imperij maiori ex parte nullas speret suppetias, quantacunque tamen poterit militari manu ei resistere eiusque vires et minas infringere, pro eo quem Deus largitus fuerit successu, constituit; ideoque equites et pedites varijs e locis conscribit, ut iusto exercitu tam atroci hosti obviare queat et suos defendere. Deus Opt. Max. omnia in bonum finem dirigat; cui etiam te tuosque commendo.

Vale.
Dabantur ex Arce Caesarea Benatica Die 29 Martij Anni 1600 stylo novo.

TYCHO BRAHE TO AN ANONYMOUS [PERSON]

Heartiest greeting.

Reverend and most excellent Man, friend to be honoured: the letter you wrote me back, with my son returning hither, I received about the beginning of this passing year; and for your friendly and candid congratulation on the conditions which God Best and Greatest has provided for me and my studies with this most august Emperor, and for your benevolent wish, by which you pray that all may henceforth be happily established and increased, I give you the greatest thanks I can. Hence shines forth that sincere affection of yours, with which you have long and greatly attended me; you will find me of like perpetual mind toward you and yours. And certainly it is so, as you write: I acknowledge the singular providence and kindness of the Divine Power, which clemently snatched me from the difficulties to which I was liable in my fatherland, and placed me here in a more tranquil harbour. And indeed I cannot sufficiently proclaim this best Emperor's not only imperial, but also paternal (if it were permitted so to speak) disposition toward me — seen even from this, that although His Majesty, on account of the epidemic diseases which most gravely afflicted Bohemia this past year, was absent from Prague and kept himself in the city of Pilsen, 15 miles distant (that infection still lasting, and raging in this place too, which he granted me to inhabit, and in the whole neighbourhood), yet of his own motion he was solicitous about me and mine, several times sending hither to inquire how we fared — at the same time most clemently offering that, if I wished to withdraw to any other castle of His Majesty's, where it were safer to live, it should be permitted me, and leaving it free, too, if I should please, to betake myself to Vienna of Austria, where he would in time provide me a convenient dwelling and the other necessaries. But I, having most humbly given thanks, answered that I would rather remain here in my station, and commit everything to Divine protection and will. And I give thanks, with my whole heart, to the same merciful God my protector, that he preserved me and my whole house unharmed amid such great dangers, by which we were surrounded on all sides; and I pray that he deign henceforth to be present to us by his grace.

Rightly was it done by you, that you unsealed and read through the little history of our migration into Bohemia, and of what afterward befell me here, which I had related a little more fully to Holger Rosenkrantz, our common friend, on account of the intimate friendship between us (to say nothing of kinship and relationship). And since from it you have sufficiently perceived the state of my affairs — in which, by God's kindness, it now persists, and henceforth can be more fully increased — I think it not worthwhile to repeat any of it here. I expect soon my furniture and the Instruments left at Magdeburg, and also those at Hamburg, which I had transferred thither from Denmark by my emissaries before winter; for the Emperor wrote to the Magistrate of both cities that, for his sake, they should have all conveyed hither by the Elbe; and when the Magdeburg Senators sought certain subterfuges, His Majesty on that account gave another letter to the Chapter of the same place, which promised it would most gladly comply with the Imperial Majesty's will. Meanwhile structures are being prepared for arranging the Instruments here in due manner.

That you were an exhorter to Master Christen Longomontanus, that he should come to me, and persuaded him of it, is most welcome to me; I will take pains that he not regret this journey, and will leave it free to him to be either here with me or at Prague, as long as he wishes.

Concerning the rest you write, it is well agreed between us, as was fair and fitting. I hear that there is among you a certain Swede devoted to astronomical studies, by name …… , who also delights in celestial observations; if you can bring it about that he come hither, it will be welcome to me, and not useless to him — I will aid his endeavours as much as I can.

I hear that the Swedish kingdom — long shaken by continual wars and tumults — has come into the power of Duke Charles. May God bend his heart, that he use this victory and power moderately, and clemently spare those he holds as adversaries.

News from these parts worth weight is scarcely at hand. The Hungarian war is continued with great force, and preparation [is made] on both sides for the coming summer. They say the Turk will be present with a far greater army than ever before; our Emperor, though for the most part hoping no aid from the Princes of the Empire, has nonetheless resolved to resist him with whatever military force he can, and to break his strength and threats, according to the success God shall grant; and therefore he levies horse and foot from various places, that with a proper army he may meet so atrocious an enemy and defend his own. May God Best and Greatest direct all to a good end; to whom I also commend you and yours.

Farewell.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 29th day of March, the year 1600, New Style.

Apparatus

  • Recipient (unnamed; flagged). Headed Ad Anonymum — addressed to a "Reverend and most excellent" friend (a cleric or learned divine) who: had congratulated Tycho on his imperial conditions; is a "common friend" of Tycho and Holger Rosenkrantz, to whom Holger had shown Tycho's migration-narrative; had urged Longomontanus to come to Tycho; and lives near "a Swede devoted to astronomy." On the evidence, an unidentified north-German / Rostock-area reverend of Tycho's circle (parallel in content to the Chytraeus reply, brahe-t_16000328002, but a distinct person). Not identified — left so per the no-fabrication rule.
  • Significance. A counterpart of the same-period letters on Tycho's imperial establishment:
  • The Emperor's "paternal" care. As in the family letters (brahe-t_16000321004, brahe-t_16000321002) and the Coraduz letter (brahe-t_16000308001), Tycho dwells on Rudolf II's personal solicitude during the plague — repeated inquiries after his safety, and the standing offer to relocate him to any safer castle, or to Vienna — which Tycho declined to "remain in my station."
  • The migration-narrative. The recipient had read the autobiographical Historiola of Tycho's migration into Bohemia that Tycho sent Holger Rosenkrantz (the same narrative Chytraeus and Sturtz also sought; cf. brahe-t_16010415002, brahe-t_16000328002).
  • The instrument-transport. The Magdeburg/Hamburg saga: the Emperor's letters to both cities to ship the instruments up the Elbe; the Magdeburg Senate's "subterfuges" answered by the Emperor's further letter to the Cathedral Chapter (cf. brahe-t_16000404001, brahe-t_16000321005).
  • Longomontanus — the recipient had persuaded him to come to Tycho; Tycho will make him welcome.
  • Recruiting a Swedish observer. Tycho asks the recipient to send to him a Swede skilled in astronomy and observation (name a lacuna in the witness) — a glimpse of Tycho recruiting observational staff for Bohemia.
  • News: Charles of Sweden's conquest (with the hope he spare his adversaries; cf. brahe-t_16000328002, brahe-t_15991203001); the Hungarian war and the Turk's great army; the Emperor levying troops despite the princes' withheld aid.
  • Persons named. the unnamed reverend (recipient); Holger Rosenkrantz; Christen Longomontanus; the unnamed Swedish astronomer; Charles of Sweden; Rudolf II; Tycho's son (Tycho the Younger).
  • Textual flags. The recipient's name is lost (heading Ad Anonymum), and the Swede's name is a lacuna in the source (nomine ……); both left unfilled. Calendar: dateline Die 29 Martij Anni 1600 stylo novo; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Restored 'hinc 15 miliaribus distante' (Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.287) where our transcription had dropped the numeral; the translator's bracketed '[some]' is now replaced by the recovered figure. Restored 'Turcam' for the non-word 'Turam' (Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.288); the English 'Turk' was already correct and needed no change.
