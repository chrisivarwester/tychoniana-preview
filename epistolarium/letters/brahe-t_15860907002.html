The original

NOBILISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, EQVITI DANO, DOMINO A KNVDSTRVP, MATHEMATICO NOSTRO SECVLO PRÆSTANTISSIMO, ETC. DOMINO AC FAUTORI SUO VNICE COLENDO, ETC. S. P.

NON dubito, Nobilissime Domine, Mathematicorum præstantissime, quin in recenti adhuc habeas memoria, quæ in proximis nundinis Francofordiensibus mandato Illustrissimi Principis nostri transmittendo aliquot Obseruationes ad T. Excell. perscripserim, et vt a T. Excell., etiam atque etiam petierim, vt his nostris Literis respondere dignaretur. At cum nullum adhuc a T. Excell. viderimus responsum, valde dubitat Illustriss. Princeps noster, num eæ ad manus tuas peruenerint. Mandauerat enim Illustriss. ipsius Celsitudo, vt non tantum suæ Celsitudinis clementem salutationem, verumetiam ingens tuæ responsionis videndæ desiderium per ministrum tuum tibi significarem. Quapropter cum nullus Dies intercedat, quin de T. Excell. responsione ab Illustrissimo principe nostro interroger, eiusque intermissionem semper a disiunctione locorum excusare cogar, peto a T. Excell. adhuc maiorem in modum, ne responsionem in Salutem et promotionem Astronomiæ diutius intermittat. Ipse quidem, quibuscunque possum rationibus, Astronomiam Illustrissimo Principi nostro etiam atque etiam commendare non desino: dummodo T. Excell. mihi in subsidium veniat, atque currentem et bene admonitum acrioribus calcaribus instiget. Acer et ad palmæ per se cursurus honores: Si tamen horteris, fortius ibit equus. De Altitudinibus Stellarum Meridianis a me per Quadrantem obseruatis, inuenio Quadranti non iuste secundum Horizontis planum a Wittichio et Horologiario nostro perpendicula esse applicata, sed ostendi ab eo Altitudines Stellarum iusto et quidem integro minuto maiores, quam reuera sint: Quod licet toties Illustriss. Principi nostro ab eo tempore, quo hîc Mathematicum ago, dixerim, credere tamen noluit, donec nuper defectionem tam Horologiario, quam Illustriss. ipsius Cels. ad oculum ostenderem. Nec dubito, quin tu quoque eundem errorem in nostris Obseruationibus iamdudum animaduerteris. Iteratis igitur Obseruationibus inuenio correctis perpendiculis Eleuationem Poli huius loci ex Stella Polari 51 grad. 19 min. vno scilicet minuto minorem. Ad summam, non dubito, quin pro tuo erga Astronomiam singulari Amore Nobilissimam hanc Scientiam etiam atque etiam apud Illustrissimum Principem nostrum sis commendaturus. Scis hanc Artem omnino Regiam esse, ac Regum ac Principum fauorem desiderare. Plura scriberem, præsertim de impuro illo nebulone Nicolao Raymaro Vrso Dithmarso, qui superiori Hyeme apud T. Excell. Typographicam Literarum collectionem et ordinationem, vt opinor, exercuit, quomodo scilicet hic te conuicijs proscindere non desierit, et quomodo te defenderim: nisi angustia temporis me impediret. Cum nuper in præsentia Illustrissimi Principis JOANNIS CASIMIRI Ducis Palatini, etc. Itemque, Inclyti Domini a Donaw, tui honorificam, vt par erat, facerem mentionem: dici vix potest, quomodo Dominus a Donaw te commendârit, adeo vt me iam pridem tui amore flagrantem iam totum inflammârit.

His igitur bene et feliciter vale, optime TYCHO, et de Astronomia bene mereri ne desine.

Cassellis 7 Calend. Septembris, Anno 86.

T. T.
CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS Bernburgensis.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe, Danish knight, Lord of Knudstrup, the most outstanding mathematician of our age, etc., his lord and patron singularly to be honoured, etc. — heartiest greeting.

I do not doubt, most noble Lord, most outstanding of mathematicians, that you still hold in fresh memory how, at the last Frankfurt fair, by the command of our Most Illustrious Prince, I wrote out to Your Excellency several observations to be conveyed [to you], and how again and again I begged of Your Excellency that he would deign to answer this letter of ours. But since we have as yet seen no reply from Your Excellency, our Most Illustrious Prince greatly doubts whether they reached your hands. For his Most Illustrious Highness had charged me to signify to you, through your servant, not only his Highness's gracious greeting, but also a vast desire to see your reply. Wherefore, since not a day passes but I am questioned by our Most Illustrious Prince concerning Your Excellency's reply, and am always forced to excuse the delay of it by the separation of our places, I ask of Your Excellency yet more earnestly that he not put off any longer a reply for the welfare and advancement of astronomy. For my own part, by whatever arguments I can, I do not cease to commend astronomy again and again to our Most Illustrious Prince — provided that Your Excellency come to my aid, and goad on with sharper spurs one who is already running and well-admonished. The keen [horse] will of itself run on to the honours of the palm; yet if you urge him, the horse will go the more strongly. As to the meridian altitudes of the stars observed by me with the quadrant, I find that the plumb-lines were not rightly applied to the quadrant along the plane of the horizon by Wittich and our clock-maker, but that it [the quadrant] showed the altitudes of the stars greater by a full minute, indeed an entire minute, than they truly are: which, although I told our Most Illustrious Prince so many times from the time when I have been acting as mathematician here, he was nevertheless unwilling to believe, until lately I showed the fault to the clock-maker and to his Most Illustrious Highness before their very eyes. Nor do I doubt that you too have long since noticed the same error in our observations. Therefore, with the observations repeated and the plumb-lines corrected, I find the elevation of the pole of this place, from the Pole Star, to be 51 degrees 19 minutes — that is, less by one minute. In sum, I do not doubt that, out of your singular love toward astronomy, you will commend this most noble science again and again to our Most Illustrious Prince. You know that this art is altogether a royal one, and craves the favour of kings and princes. I would write more — especially about that foul scoundrel Nicolaus Raymarus Ursus Dithmarsus, who last winter, as I suppose, practised the typographical collection and arrangement of letters [i.e. typesetting] at Your Excellency's [house], [and about] how indeed he has not ceased to slash at you here with insults, and how I defended you — were not the shortness of time hindering me. When lately, in the presence of the Most Illustrious Prince Johann Casimir, Duke Palatine, etc., and likewise of the renowned Lord von Dohna, I made honourable mention of you, as was fitting, it can scarcely be told how the Lord von Dohna commended you, so that he wholly inflamed me, already long since burning with love of you.

With this, then, farewell — well and happily — best Tycho, and do not cease to deserve well of astronomy.

Kassel, the 7th [day before] the Kalends of September [26 August], in the year [15]86.

Yours truly,
Christoph Rothmann of Bernburg.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Christophorus Rothmannus Bernburgensis = Christoph Rothmann (c. 1550/60 – c. 1600), of Bernburg in Anhalt; mathematician and astronomer to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel. This is one of the Rothmann–Tycho letters Tycho himself published in the 1596 Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I) printed at Uraniborg — the edition of record for this letter.
  • Recipient titulature. Equiti Dano, Domino a Knudstrup = "Danish knight, Lord of Knudstrup" (Tycho's family seat in Scania). Mathematico nostro seculo praestantissimo = "the most outstanding mathematician of our age." S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting," the edition's convention).
  • "Illustrissimus Princeps noster." Throughout, "our Most Illustrious Prince" / "his Highness" = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (Rothmann's employer), not named in the body. Celsitudo = "Highness" by the edition's convention
  • "the last Frankfurt fair." proximis nundinis Francofordiensibus — the Frankfurt book fair (spring or autumn), the standard conduit for scholarly correspondence and observations between the Kassel and Hven circles.
  • Equestrian proverb. currentem … acrioribus calcaribus instiget … Si tamen horteris, fortius ibit equus — "spur on one already running … yet if you urge [him], the horse will go the more strongly": a classical commonplace (cf. currentem incitare, "to spur the willing horse"; Ovid, Ex Ponto II.6.38 fortius ut moveant, calcar habere volunt). Rothmann casts himself as the eager horse needing only Tycho's encouragement. Kept as the running metaphor; ad palmae … honores = "to the honours of the palm [of victory]."
  • Instrument / observation. Altitudines Stellarum Meridianae … per Quadrantem = "meridian altitudes of the stars … with the quadrant". perpendicula = the plumb-lines by which the quadrant's vertical was set; mis-set "along the plane of the horizon," they introduced a systematic error of a full arc-minute. Eleuatio Poli … ex Stella Polari = "elevation of the pole … from the Pole Star," i.e. the observed latitude of Kassel, corrected to 51° 19′ (one minute less than before). ad oculum = "before their very eyes / demonstrably." Horologiarius rendered "clock-maker" (the court instrument-mechanic).
  • "Wittich." a Wittichio = Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586), the Silesian mathematician who had visited both Hven (1580) and Kassel; here blamed (with the clock-maker) for the faulty plumb-line setting. Tycho's later quarrel over priority for the geo-heliocentric arrangement involved Wittich and Ursus; Rothmann names him neutrally as the prior installer.
  • "Ursus." Nicolaus Raymarus Ursus Dithmarsus = Nicolaus Reimers Bär (Ursus), of Dithmarschen — the future Imperial mathematician and Tycho's bitterest priority-rival. impuro illo nebulone = "that foul scoundrel": Rothmann's invective. The clause Typographicam Literarum collectionem et ordinationem … exercuit literally "practised the typographical collection and arrangement of letters" = worked as a typesetter/compositor during his stay at Hven the previous winter (1584/85) — a pointed status-jab. te conuicijs proscindere = "to slash at you with insults." This passage is an early datable trace of the Tycho–Ursus feud, from Rothmann's pro-Tycho side.
  • Other persons. Joannes Casimirus Dux Palatinus = Johann Casimir of the Palatinate-Simmern (1543–1592), Count Palatine, administrator of the Electoral Palatinate. Dominus a Donaw / a Donaw = a Lord (Burgrave) von Dohna, of the prominent Prussian-Silesian noble house (the precise individual is not specified in the letter; likely one of the Burgraves von Dohna active at the Reformed German courts). Both are reported praising Tycho.
  • Royal-art topos. hanc Artem omnino Regiam esse, ac Regum ac Principum fauorem desiderare = "this art is altogether a royal one, and craves the favour of kings and princes" — the standard plea that astronomy needs princely patronage, here urging Tycho to keep the Landgrave engaged.
  • Sign-off formula. His igitur bene et feliciter vale — vale is rendered with the controlled formula "farewell"; the adverbial pair bene et feliciter ("well and happily") qualifying it is retained alongside, set off with dashes, so that the fixed vale gloss is preserved without dropping Rothmann's doubled adverb.
  • Subscription. T. T. = Tui tibi / Tuus totus ("Yours truly / wholly yours"); expanded conventionally.
  • Printed-edition markers. The working transcription carries scattered superscript letter-anchors (rendered as a stray "A" in several places, including a spurious "A" splitting the opening S. P. | NON dubito) — these are footnote/marginal-note sigla of the 1596 printed Epistolae, not part of Rothmann's prose; they are silently removed from both the cleaned source block and the translation.
  • Dateline — calendar crux (flagged, not reconciled). The letter is subscribed Cassellis 7 Calend. Septembris, Anno 86. By the Roman idiom (inclusive counting, with 1 September = Kalendis Septembris), "7 Kalends of September" = 26 August 1586 (the edition's convention, Roman-calendar idiom glossed to civil date). The edition's metadata and identifier, however, label the letter "7 September 1586" (whence identifier …090700). The two do not agree; by the audit's rule the dateline is reproduced as written and the discrepancy recorded, not silently corrected. The header gives the literal Roman reading with its civil equivalent; the identifier-date (7 Sept.) is noted as the editorial/catalogue date. All O.S. (Hesse-Kassel and Denmark kept the Julian calendar).
  • Place. Cassellis = Kassel.
