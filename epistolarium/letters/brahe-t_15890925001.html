The original

TILL TYGE BRAHE NOGEN WOX ANRØRENDIS VT SEQUITUR

Christian 4. etc.

WOR gunst tilfornn. Wiid, att thenne breffuißer Thomes Loeding haffuer wnderdanigst tilkiende giiffuit, huorledis for nogen tiid siiden wdj a:o 87 er st[r]andit ett schiib paa Anholt, som haffuer tilhørt nogen kiøbmend aff Engelland, huor wdj haffuer werrit eblant andit guodtz noget wox, aff huilcket en part schal werre strandit och bierget wed Wardbierg, en part wed Laugholm, och ett støcke ther for Hueen schal werre indkommen, huilcket du til thig schal haffue anammit, och fornemme Thometz Loeding paa for:ne engelsche kiøbmends wegne haffuer wnderdanigst werrit begierendis, hannom samme wox motte betalis och fornøyes. Tha epterdj att alt huis wrag, som saaledis her vdj riigit nogen stedts kand strande och indkomme for Wor och cronens grund, tilkommer Oß och cronen allenne, bede Wij thig och wille, attu thig ther om emoed Wor elsk:e tilforornede regierings raad erklerer, epter att Wij slige høighet wille Oß allenne forbeholdit haffue, hannom ochsaa samme wox, som du wdj saa maade haffuer ladet anamme, betaler och fornøyer, att hand sig wiidere for Oß icke schal haffue sig at beklage.

Ther met etc.
Actum Haffniæ 25 septem: a:o 1589.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Tyge Brahe, concerning certain wax, as follows:

Christian IV, etc.

Our favour beforehand. Know that this bearer-of-the-letter, Thomes Loeding, has most humbly made known how, some time since, in the year [15]87, a ship was wrecked off Anholt which had belonged to certain merchants of England, in which there was, among other goods, some wax — of which one part is said to have been wrecked and salvaged near Varberg, one part near Laholm, and a piece [of which] is said to have come in there off Hven, which you are said to have taken in to yourself; and, as we understand, Thomes Loeding, on behalf of the aforesaid English merchants, has most humbly been requesting that the same wax be paid for and made good to him. Then, inasmuch as all such wreck as may in this manner be stranded and come in anywhere here within the realm upon our and the crown's ground pertains to us and the crown alone, we ask and will of you that you make declaration of yourself concerning this before our beloved appointed Council of the Regency — since we will have reserved such prerogative to ourselves alone — [and] that you also pay and make good to him the same wax, which you have in such manner caused to be taken in, so that he shall not have [cause] to complain of you to us any further.

Therewith, etc.
Done at Copenhagen, 25 September, in the year 1589.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Christian IV of Denmark (1577–1648), at this date eleven years old and still a minor; he had been elected king-designate in 1580 and acclaimed in 1584, but did not assume personal rule until 1596. Letters issued in his name during 1588–96 are acts of the regency government (the regeringsråd), which the letter itself names — hence the king's command to Tycho to "declare himself" before the Council of the Regency. The young king succeeded Frederik II (Tycho's patron, d. 4 April 1588), whose own chancery dealings with Tycho over instruments are the subject of the sibling pilot letter (brahe-t_15841211001).
  • Recipient. Tyge (Tycho) Brahe, then resident on Hven (Hveen), where he held the island in fief from the crown; the wreck-and-salvage jurisdiction at issue turns on that tenancy.
  • Bearer / claimant. Thomes Loeding (also Thometz Loeding in the genitive) — the bearer of the letter, acting paa … engelsche kiøbmends wegne ("on behalf of the English merchants") who owned the wrecked cargo. Identity otherwise undetermined; the spelling suggests an agent or factor rather than one of the principals (named only collectively as "certain merchants of England").
  • Document type. A chancery-register entry (Sjællandske Tegnelser), as in the 1584 Frederik II letter. The Latin rubric "TILL TYGE BRAHE NOGEN WOX ANRØRENDIS VT SEQUITUR" ("To Tyge Brahe, concerning certain wax, as follows") heads the Danish body — a code-switch preserved by this chapter's convention The closing Actum Haffniae ("Done at Copenhagen") is likewise the standard Latin chancery formula.
  • Places. Anholt — the island in the Kattegat (notorious for wrecks). Wardbierg = Varberg and Laugholm = Laholm, both on the then-Danish coast of Halland (now Sweden). Hueen = Hven, Tycho's island. Haffniae = Copenhagen (Latin Hafnia,).
  • Legal substance (wrag / regalian salvage right). alt huis wrag … tilkommer Oß och cronen allenne = "all such wreck … pertains to us and the crown alone": the king asserts the crown's regalian right of wreck (ius naufragii / strandret) over goods cast up on crown ground. slige høighet … Oß allenne forbeholdit haffue — høighet here is the sovereign prerogative / regalian right ("such prerogative … reserved to ourselves alone"), not "highness" as a style of address. The command's force is twofold and is read as conjoined (the two infinitive clauses erklerer … betaler och fornøyer both governed by bede Wij thig och wille, attu …): Tycho is (1) to render account of the matter before the Regency Council, and (2) to pay and satisfy the claimant for the wax — i.e. the crown takes the salvage right but directs Tycho to compensate the owner so the claim is closed.
  • Negation. The source carries exactly one negation in the operative clause: att hand sig wiidere for Oß icke schal haffue sig at beklage = "so that he shall not have [cause] to complain of you to us any further." Reproduced as a single negation; the purpose-clause is the consequence of Tycho's compliance, not a prohibition laid on the claimant.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1589). WOR gunst tilfornn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula,); Wiid = Vid ("know," imperative); breffuißer = brevviser ("bearer of the letter"); wnderdanigst = underdanigst ("most humbly"); tilkiende giiffuit = tilkendegivet ("made known"); st[r]andit — the register copy reads standit; Dreyer prints st[r]andit, restoring the elided r (so too in the source block, where the bracketed r is editorial); eblant = iblandt ("among"); guodtz = gods ("goods"); bierget = bjerget ("salvaged"); anammit / anamme = annammet / annamme ("taken in, received"); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); begierendis = begærendes ("requesting"); motte = måtte ("might"); fornøyes / fornøyer = fornøjes / fornøjer ("be satisfied / make good, satisfy"); epterdj = efterdi ("inasmuch as"); wrag = vrag ("wreck, flotsam"); riigit = riget ("the realm"); tilkommer = tilkommer ("pertains/belongs to"); Wor elsk:e tilforornede regierings raad = Vor elskelige tilforordnede regerings råd ("our beloved appointed Council of the Regency"); erklerer = erklærer ("declare/explain [yourself]"); slige = slige ("such"); forbeholdit = forbeholdt ("reserved"); wiidere = videre ("further"); beklage = beklage ("complain").
  • Calendar. Actum Haffniae 25 septem: a:o 1589 = "Done at Copenhagen, 25 September, in the year 1589" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700,). The in-letter date a:o 87 ("in the year [15]87") refers to the wreck, two years before the letter; it is not a dating discrepancy with the dateline. The edition label ("1589. 25. september. København") agrees with the dateline.
