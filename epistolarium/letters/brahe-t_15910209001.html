The original

AN MATHEMATICUM CHRISTOPHORUM ROTHMANN.

WILHELM, von Gotteß gnaden landtgrauen zu Hessen, graue zu Catzenelenbogen.

Lieber getrewer. Wir haben dein schreibenn, darin du Vnß dein itzo lang wehrende vndt beschwehrliche leibs schwacheit zu erkennen geben, benebenn dem vberschickten abriß des gebewes, so Tycho Brahe pro observationibus siderum vffrichten laßen, wie auch die eigentliche vndt in faden vberschickte mensur gedachter abriß wohlgelieffert entpfangen. Daß du nun mit so vielfältigen leibs kranckheiten zu gleich beladen vndt angegriffen worden, deßwegen tragen wir ein gnediges mitleiden mit dir, vndt mochten dir nichts liebers gonnen alß deine wohlfarigkeit vndt gesundheit, vndt haltenß dafur es werden sich nun mehr ingruente illa temporum mutatione, auch die humores anders vndt sich also deine sachen zur beßerung vndt gesundheit anlaßen vndt schicken. Wollen gleichwohl nicht hoffen, daß du dir solche deine kranckheiten mit freßen vndt sauffen oder sonst vnordentlichen leben verursacht, vndt also selbst praesentis tui mali faber gewesen seiest.

Wie eß dir in Denmarck ergangen, mogten Wir dich dauon gern mundtlich höoren reden, jn masen wir dan auch gern sehen, daß du dich bey Vnß in deinen vorigen dienst wieder einstellen woltest.

Deß stern oder stern observation[s] buch (welchs du tabellas oder catalogos stellarum nennest) wolten Wir dir deßelben copey gern zuschicken, wenn Wir nur iemandt bekommen konten, ders recht vndt ohn mangell abschreiben konte; in masen wir dann die versehung thun laßen, daß einer der nicht allein vleisig, sondern auch correct schreiben kan, sich der subscription trewlich vndernehme, wollen Wir dir sie alßdan verwahrlich zuschicken.

Waß dan des Tychonis Brahae Instrumenta belanget, verstheen wir, daß sie zwar groß; von was materien sie aber seyen, sehen wir nicht allenthalben. Dan wie Vnß von vielen gesagt ist, so sollen sie von pappen vndt derogleichen materien gemacht sein, do Vnß nicht bedunckt, daß sie konnen bestendig sein. Beuehlen dir derhalben nachmalß, daß du Vnß deinen bericht, nemblich, ob du sie vor just vndt bestendig haltest, vffs ehist zukommen lasest. Wolten Wir dir nicht verhalten vndt seindt dir mit gnaden wohl gewogen.

Signatum Caßell am 9t. feb: ao: etc. 91.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the mathematician Christoph Rothmann.

Wilhelm, by the grace of God Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen.

Trusty and well-beloved. We have duly received your letter, in which you make known to Us your now long-continuing and grievous bodily infirmity, together with the sketch you sent over of the building which Tycho Brahe has had erected for the observation of the stars [pro observationibus siderum], as also the exact measurements of the said sketch, sent over [marked out] in thread. That you have now been at one and the same time burdened and assailed with so manifold bodily ailments — for this We bear gracious compassion towards you, and could wish you nothing more dearly than your well-being and health; and We hold it for [certain] that now, with that change of the seasons coming on [ingruente illa temporum mutatione], the humours [humores] too will turn otherwise, and that thus your affairs will set themselves and tend toward improvement and health. We will nonetheless not suppose that you have brought such ailments of yours upon yourself by gorging and guzzling or by otherwise disorderly living, and so have yourself been [a cause] of your present ills [praesentis tui mali faber].

How matters went for you in Denmark, We would gladly hear you tell of by word of mouth; even as We should also gladly see that you would present yourself with Us again in your former service.

The star-book, or book of star-observations (which you call tables or catalogues of the stars [tabellas oder catalogos stellarum]) — We would gladly send you a copy of it, if only We could obtain someone who could copy it out correctly and without fault; accordingly, when We shall have made provision that one who can write not only diligently but also correctly should faithfully undertake the transcription [subscription], We will then send it to you under safe keeping.

As then concerns Tycho Brahe's instruments [Instrumenta], We understand that they are indeed large; but of what materials they may be, We do not in every respect see. For, as has been told Us by many, they are said to be made of pasteboard and suchlike materials, wherein it does not seem to Us that they can be durable. We therefore charge you once more that you let your report reach Us at the very soonest — namely, whether you hold them to be accurate and durable. This We would not withhold from you, and are well disposed towards you with [Our] grace.

Given at Kassel on the 9th of February, year etc. [15]91.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Wilhelm, von Gotteß gnaden landtgrauen zu Hessen, graue zu Catzenelenbogen = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), "Wilhelm the Wise," himself a serious observational astronomer and the patron of the Kassel observatory; graue zu Catzenelenbogen = Count of Katzenelnbogen, a component of the Hessian titulature. The princely first-person Wir / Vnß (the royal "We") and the chancery form of address Lieber getrewer ("Trusty and well-beloved," by the edition's convention) are preserved.
  • Recipient. Christophorus Rothmann = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician at Kassel and Tycho's chief correspondent there; he had visited Hven in 1590 (at Wilhelm's request, conveyed in the Landgrave's letter of 15 May 1590) to inspect Tycho's instruments, fell seriously ill, and never returned to Kassel service — the background to both the offer of re-employment and the inquiry into his health here. The letter answers Rothmann's own report.
  • The sketch and the thread-measurement. abriß des gebewes … die eigentliche vndt in faden vberschickte mensur — Rothmann had sent the Landgrave a drawing of Tycho's observatory building (Uraniborg / Stjerneborg) together with its true dimensions conveyed in faden, i.e. marked out in [a length of] thread (a knotted/measured cord standing in for a scale) — a notable detail of how architectural measure was transmitted by post.
  • The "instruments of pasteboard" report (the substantive crux of the letter). sie sollen von pappen vndt derogleichen materien gemacht sein … nicht bedunckt, daß sie konnen bestendig sein — the Landgrave reports the rumour, "told Us by many," that Tycho's instruments are made of pappen and like materials, and doubts such instruments "can be durable/stable" (bestendig = both physically stable and dimensionally constant). Pappen here = pasteboard / cardboard / papier-mâché, not "paper" in the sheet sense; rendered "pasteboard." Tycho's instruments were of course of metal and seasoned wood, and Rothmann's report would correct the rumour — this passage is the historically famous expression of the rivalrous Kassel skepticism about the Hven instruments. bestendig is rendered "durable" in its two occurrences and the doublet just vndt bestendig as "accurate and durable" (just = exact/accurate); the double sense of bestendig is flagged rather than collapsed.
  • The star-catalogue. Deß stern oder stern observation[s] buch (welchs du tabellas oder catalogos stellarum nennest) — the Landgrave's own catalogue of fixed-star observations (the Kassel star catalogue), which Rothmann terms with the Latin tabellas oder catalogos stellarum ("tables or catalogues of the stars"); Wilhelm offers to have a fair copy made and sent. subscription here is used loosely for the act of transcription / copying out (he wants a scribe who writes correct, i.e. accurately); rendered "transcription."
  • Latin code-switching. Typical of Kassel chancery German to a learned correspondent. pro observationibus siderum = "for the observation of the stars"; ingruente illa temporum mutatione = "with that change of the seasons [/of the times] coming on" (temporum read as the seasons, given the medical context); humores = the bodily humours (humoral medicine, the edition's convention register); praesentis tui malis = "of your present ills" (the grammar of the MS draft is loose — an ablative/genitive slip; aber in malis aber gewesen reads as Urheber, "author/cause," so the sense is "a cause of your present ills," given in brackets); Instrumenta, tabellas, catalogos stellarum — see above. Retained Latin is italicised and glossed on first use by the edition's convention.
  • Reading note. stern observation[s] buch: the source draft has observation buch; the genitive -s is supplied in brackets. höoren (for hören) is the draft's spelling, kept verbatim in the source block.
  • Calendar / place. Signatum Caßell am 9t. feb: ao: etc. 91 = "Given at Kassel, 9 February, year etc. [15]91" — Old Style (Hesse-Kassel, like Denmark, kept the Julian calendar; it did not adopt the Gregorian reform in this period). The dateline date agrees with the edition label (1591. 9. februar. Kassel); no discrepancy. Signatum ("signed/given") is the chancery dating verb, parallel to the Danish Actum.
  • MS witness note. The edition records the witness folder as "Landgraf Moritz (!) Correspondenz" — the editorial (!) (sic) flags that the draft is filed under Landgrave Moritz's correspondence series at Marburg though it is Wilhelm IV's letter (Moritz succeeded Wilhelm in 1592); reproduced as given.
  • Correction. Latin: 'malis aber' corrected to 'mali faber' per Dreyer, Opera Omnia XIV, p. 55; the English's bracketed Latin citation updated to match.

1 defect found:
- text error (low). The transcribed original corrupts Dreyer's Latin tag 'mali faber' (architect/maker of your own ill, echoing the classical 'faber est suae quisque fortunae') into the nonsensical 'malis aber', dropping the word 'faber' and mis-splitting 'mali'. The published English's bracketed gloss '[praesentis tui malis]' reproduces the corrupted source rather than Dreyer's actual reading, though the English prose itself ('a cause of your present ills') still conveys roughly the right sense despite the corrupted citation. Dreyer prints: vndt alfo felbft præfentis tui mali faber gewefen feieft. (TBOO XIV p.55, line 15).

Repaired. Verified the single defect by direct visual inspection of the Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.54-56 page images. 'præfentis tui mali faber' is unambiguous on p.55. The English prose itself needed no substantive change (it already conveyed the sense), but its bracketed Latin quotation was updated for consistency with the corrected Latin. No other divergence from Dreyer was found in this letter.
