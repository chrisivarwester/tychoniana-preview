The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD JACOBUM BORDINGUM].
Magnifice et clarissime vir, d:ne et amice honorande.

SCRIPSI Mag:iæ T:æ non ita dudum nonnulla de statu mearum rerum et simul eam rogaui, ut ab ampliss:o senatu Lubecensi sua intercessione et authoritate impetrare non degrauaretur, quo ea, quæ etiamnum ex mea supellectile isthic residua sunt, eorum iussu et provisione Hamburgum per flu[u]iolum interlabentem, mature deportentur, idque in gratiam s:æ cæs:æ m:tis, d:ni nostri clementissimi, cuj hoc officium gratiose placiturum. Spero itaque M:am T:am id ipsum executioni aut iam mandasse aut breuj mandaturam, quare nihil ulterius hac de re nunc repetam, ne videar de M:æ T:æ erga me beneuolentia, diu multumque mihi satis perspecta, quidpiam hæsitare.

Verum aliud quid maioris momentj nunc addam. Repetij, vtj scit M: T:a, totam pecuniæ summam ab jllustrissimorum juniorum principum Megapolitanorum quæstore, quam illis mutuo dederam et quidem legitimo tempore, prout literæ contractus habent, repetij. Verum is nunc prætendens ærarij vacuitatem summam promissam ac interesse ad tempus calendarum aprilis præfinitarum numerare tergiuersatur, aliter quam ab jll:mo duce Vldarico, d:no meo clementiss., constitutum illique iniunctum est. Cumque hæc procrastinatio mihi sit intolerabilis, siquidem in vltimis huius regni comitiis mense februario proxime elapso celebratis quibusdam præcipuis viris decem illa millia joachimicorum addixerim, atque inde plus commodj sperem quam prius obtinui, oportet utique me promissis stare et fidem datam redimere, nec etiam mihi integrum est interesse illo diutius carere, satis graue fuit, quod alterius anni usum fructum nunc primum per dd. Gödelmannum receperim. Scripsi itaque ad jllustrissimum ducem Vldaricum hac de re submisse et diligenter, vt ipsius cel:do dignetur sua authoritate efficere, quo mihi eueniat, id quod æquitas postulat. Quocirca M:am T:am magnopere rogatam habeo, ut apud ipsius c:nem negotium hoc promouere velit. Si quid fuerit quod apud s:am cæs:am, m:tem, d:num nostrum clementiss:m, in hac aula ipsius cel:tudini gratum præstare potuero, offero omni debita reuerentia et obsequio meam opellam quam fidelissime, quam etiam eodem modo inclytæ ciuitati Lubecensi officiosissime defero, quod et vtrisque T:a M:a meo nomine annuntiare velit. Quin et si qua in re M:æ T:æ vsui vnquam esse potuero, utatur me tanquam amico integro et constante, eius desiderijs, quantum in ipso est, non defuturo.

Jamque valeat M:a Tua cum tota sua domo quam optime et diutissime.
Dabantur Pragæ ex ædibus quondam Curtianis, nunc nouo Vraniburgo Rudolphæo die 12 aprilis anno 1601 stylo nouo.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TYCHO BRAHE TO JACOB BORDING].
Magnificent and most renowned man, my lord and friend to be honoured.

I wrote to Your Magnificence not so long ago some things about the state of my affairs, and at the same time asked you not to find it burdensome to obtain, by your intercession and authority, from the most ample Senate of Lübeck, that those things which still remain there of my household goods may, by their order and provision, be conveyed in good time to Hamburg by the little river that flows between — and this for the sake of His Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, to whom this service will be graciously pleasing. I hope, therefore, that Your Magnificence has either already given this very thing to execution, or will shortly; wherefore I shall now repeat nothing further on the matter, lest I seem to harbour any doubt about Your Magnificence's benevolence toward me, long and amply well known to me.

But I will now add something of greater moment. I have called in, as Your Magnificence knows, the whole sum of money from the treasurer of the most illustrious younger Princes of Mecklenburg — the sum which I had given them on loan, and that at the lawful time, as the letters of contract provide; I have called it in. But he now, pleading the emptiness of the treasury, shuffles over paying out the promised sum and the interest at the appointed term of the Kalends of April [1 April], otherwise than was determined by the most illustrious Duke Ulrich, my most clement Lord, and enjoined upon him. And since this procrastination is intolerable to me — seeing that at the last Diet of this kingdom [Bohemia], held this past February, I have assigned those ten thousand joachimsthalers to certain leading men, and from that I hope for more advantage than I obtained before — I must by all means stand by my promises and redeem the faith I have pledged; nor is it open to me to be without that interest any longer. It was grievous enough that I have only now received, through Dr. Gödelmann, the usufruct [the interest] of the other year. I have therefore written to the most illustrious Duke Ulrich about this matter, humbly and earnestly, that His Highness may deign by his authority to bring it about that there come to me what fairness requires. Wherefore I earnestly entreat Your Magnificence to be willing to advance this business with His Highness. If there be anything I can perform, pleasing to His Highness, with His Sacred Imperial Majesty — our most clement Lord — in this Court, I offer my poor service most faithfully, with all due reverence and obedience; the same I likewise most dutifully tender to the renowned city of Lübeck — which Your Magnificence will be willing to announce to both in my name. Nay, if ever I can be of use to Your Magnificence in any matter, use me as a whole and constant friend, who will not fail your wishes, so far as in him lies.

And now may Your Magnificence fare as well and as long as possible, with all your house.
Given at Prague, from the house formerly Curtius's — now the new Rudolphine Uraniborg — the 12th day of April, in the year 1601, New Style.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Jacob Bording, a leading man of Lübeck (jurist/magistrate of the Hanseatic city) able to act "with the most ample Senate of Lübeck" on Tycho's behalf — addressed throughout in the high civic register Magnificentia Tua ("Your Magnificence"). The witness is an autograph letter draft (Brevudkast) in the Basel Autographensammlung.
  • First business — the goods at Lübeck. Tycho asks Bording to procure from the Senate of Lübeck an order to ship the remainder of his supellex (household goods/effects) still lying there onward to Hamburg per fluviolum interlabentem — "by the little river that runs between" (the Lübeck–Hamburg water route, the Stecknitz/Trave–Elbe connection) — framing the favour as pleasing to His Imperial Majesty (Rudolf II). This tracks the slow westward removal of Tycho's possessions from the Baltic toward his new imperial seat.
  • Second business — the Mecklenburg loan (the principal matter). This letter belongs to the 10,000-thaler Mecklenburg-debt thread: Tycho had lent 10,000 joachimsthalers to the younger princes of Mecklenburg (illustrissimi juniores principes Megapolitani; Megapolitanus = Mecklenburg) and had called the principal + interest due at the lawful term — the Kalends of April (1 April) 1601 — as fixed by Duke Ulrich. The dukes' quaestor (treasurer) now stalls, pleading an empty treasury. Tycho's urgency is concrete: at the Bohemian Diet of February 1601 he had already assigned those 10,000 thalers to certain magnates (on better terms than before) and must keep his word. Cf. the parallel chain — Tycho→Andreas Meier, 27 May 1601 (brahe-t_16010527001) and 18 July (brahe-t_16010718001); Tycho→Duke Ulrich, 4 June 1601 (brahe-t_16010604001); and the ducal letters of 24 July (brahe-t_16010724001) and 9 August (brahe-t_16010809001).
  • Persons named.
  • Duke Vldaricus = Ulrich III, Duke of Mecklenburg-Güstrow (1527–1603), Tycho's dominus clementissimus in the matter, who had ordered payment.
  • dd. Gödelmannus = Johann Georg Gödelmann (1559–1611), jurist and Mecklenburg councillor/diplomat (later in imperial service), through whom Tycho had at last received the previous year's interest. (dd. = dominum doctorem.)
  • quaestor of the younger princes — the ducal treasurer, unnamed here (the rent-master Andreas Meier of Wismar in the companion letters).
  • Place / self-styling. Pragae ex aedibus quondam Curtianis, nunc novo Uraniburgo Rudolphaeo — Prague, from the Curtius house (the aedes Curtianae, Tycho's Prague residence) which he renames "the new Rudolphine Uraniborg," transplanting the name of his lost Hven seat onto his imperial quarters.
  • Calendar. Dateline explicitly stylo novo — New Style (Gregorian), correct for Tycho's imperial-service dating. Reproduced, not converted.
  • Abbreviations. The draft is heavily contracted in chancery style — M:ae T:ae (Magnificentiae Tuae), s:ae caes:ae m:tis (sacrae caesareae maiestatis), jll:mo (illustrissimo), cel:do / cel:tudini (celsitudo / celsitudini), dd. (dominum doctorem), a:o etc. — all silently expanded in translation; the source block keeps them as written.
