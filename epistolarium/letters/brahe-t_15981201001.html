The original

POSTSCRIPTA THADDÆI HAGECIJ AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

CUM hasce ad te literas obsignassem, daturus eas tuo tabellario, misi adhuc meum puerum hodie, qui est 1. Decembr. cum hacce schedula ad D. CORRADUCIUM, quam ad te mitto, ex qua intelliges voluntatem Cæsaris. Est sigillum Dni CORRADUCIJ et manus. Multum gavisus sum, me tandem ante hunc meum abitum hoc responsum et resolutionem Cæsaris accepisse. Hisce quod addam amplius non est necesse.

EXEMPLUM LITERARUM DOMINI D. THADEI AD AMPLISSIMUM PROCANCELLARIUM IMPERIJ
RUDOLPHUM
CORRADUCIUM.Magce D. et Patrone observandiss. Hucusque noster Dominus
TYCHO omnia egit ex consilio tuæ Magtiæ et voluntate clementi Cæsaris. Venit cum
uxore, liberis et familia ac 12 equis longo et difficili
itinere, sumptibus magnis, Dresdam usque. Et nulli alij Principi, nulli nationi

efficere velit, ut responsum a Cæsare accipiat et negotia sua dirigere
stabilireque possit, ac ne ita dubius cum magno suarum rerum dispendio hæreat.
Literas meas ad BARVITIUM reverenter peto
prima occasione mitti curet, eumque etiam sua schedula

Postscript of Thaddaeus Hagecius to Tycho Brahe.

When I had sealed these letters to you, intending to give them to your courier, I have today — which is 1 December — sent my boy besides, with this note to Master Coraduz, which I send on to you, from which you will understand the will of the Emperor. It bears Master Coraduz's seal and hand. I have rejoiced greatly that at last, before this my departure, I have received this reply and resolution of the Emperor. To these things there is no need that I add anything more.

Copy of a letter of Master Thaddeus Hagecius to the Most Ample Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, Rudolph Corraducius.

Magnificent Lord and most honoured Patron. Thus far our Lord Tycho has done everything on the counsel and advice of Your Magnificence and by the gracious will of the Emperor. He has come, with his wife, children and household, and twelve horses, by a long and difficult journey and at great expense, as far as Dresden. And to no other Prince, to no [other] nation, has he wished to devote his service, but to the Emperor alone. He asks, therefore — and I intercede [on his behalf] — that you be pleased to bring it about that he receive an answer from the Emperor, and be able to direct and settle his affairs, and not be left hanging thus in doubt, to the great detriment of his estate. I respectfully beg that you see to it that my letter to Barvitius be sent at the first opportunity, and that you also remind him by your own note to lay the matter before the Emperor, and see to it that the final resolution be sent to Dresden. You will not repent, I know, my Lord, of this labour, and Master Tycho, once he has become known to you face to face, will prove exceedingly grateful.

Farewell. From our house, 1 December, in the year 1598. Ready to serve Your Magnificence, Thaddeus.

Rudolph Corraducius to Thaddeus Hagecius.

What Master Barvitius, by the Emperor's order, has written back to me concerning our Lord Tycho, is this: 'Of Tycho's arrival the Emperor has learned with the greatest pleasure; he wishes him to remain for a time at Dresden, or Prague, or elsewhere nearby, until the court is gathered again in some fixed place; where he may then be conveniently and suitably settled, he graciously has in mind.' Thus far Master Barvitius — which words I have thought it right to make known to Your Magnificence in this [letter] of mine. Meanwhile I ask that you greet Master Tycho most dutifully in my words, and promise him my service, and most diligently excuse me for not writing to him myself, on account of my immense preoccupations.

Rudolph Corraducius, with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Thaddaeus Hagecius (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku, 1525–1600), imperial physician at Prague, astronomer, and Tycho's long-standing friend and chief broker at the Rudolfine court; the principal channel by which Tycho's reception into imperial service was negotiated.
  • D. Corraducius = Rudolf Coraduz (Coraduz / Coraduz), Imperial Vice-Chancellor (Reichsvizekanzler; Procancellarius in the companion letter brahe-t_15980530001). The enclosed note conveyed "the will of the Emperor" — Rudolf II's favourable resolution on Tycho's appointment — under Coraduz's seal and hand.
  • Document type. A postscriptum — a postscript written after the main letter had been sealed, hence its separate transmission.
  • ⚠ Date. The body fixes the day explicitly: hodie, qui est 1. Decembr. = 1 December 1598; the identifier (…12 01…) agrees. The edition catalogues the piece under 1 November; the one-month discrepancy is reproduced, not reconciled — the header follows the as-written 1 December. New Style (Prague, imperial Catholic).
  • What the recovered text is. The postscript already published for this letter (Hagecius to Tycho, 1 December 1598) tells Tycho he is forwarding a note from "D. Coraducius" bearing on "the will of the Emperor," but breaks off before quoting it. The recovered segment is that enclosure in full: fair copies of two letters — (1) Hagecius's own appeal to the imperial Vice-Chancellor on Tycho's behalf, and (2) the Vice-Chancellor's reply, itself quoting the Emperor's secretary verbatim.
  • The three courtiers named are Tycho's patronage brokers at Rudolf II's court in the winter of 1598–99: Thaddaeus Hagecius (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku), the Emperor's physician and Tycho's chief broker in Prague; Rudolph Corraducius (Rudolf von Coraduz), Vice-Chancellor of the Empire; and Johannes Barvitius, Rudolf II's confidential secretary and closest adviser — Christianson (On Tycho's Island, and Measure of the Heavens) names all three as the inner circle Hagecius mobilised to secure Tycho's imperial appointment.
  • Concrete biographical detail not in the letter proper. Hagecius's covering letter to Corraducius specifies that Tycho arrived at Dresden "with his wife, children and household, and twelve horses" (cum uxore, liberis et familia ac 12 equis), by a long and costly journey — a precise snapshot of the size and expense of the 1597–98 exodus from Denmark that the published tail does not give.
  • This is the actual textual source of "the will of the Emperor" referenced in the already-published tail. Corraducius quotes Barvitius's report of Rudolf II's own instruction (set off with guillemets in the source): the Emperor is glad of Tycho's arrival and wants him to wait at Dresden, Prague, or nearby until the peripatetic imperial court settles in one place, at which point he will be suitably provided for — the direct documentary basis for the extended limbo Tycho experienced before his Benátky/Prague settlement.
  • Hagecius stresses Tycho's exclusive loyalty to the Emperor ("nulli alij Principi, nulli nationi suam operam voluit devovere nisi uni Cæsari") — a rhetorical move consistent with the delicate diplomatic position Tycho was in, having left Denmark and needing to be seen as uncompromisingly committed to Habsburg service rather than shopping his services elsewhere.
