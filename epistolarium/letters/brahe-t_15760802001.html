The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS, DOCTRINA ET VIRTUTE PRÆSTANTISSIMO DN. TYCHONI BRAHE OTTONIDI, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO SUMMA OBSERVANTIA COLENDO. S.

BENEVOLENTIÆ tuæ erga me singularis, generose Domine, testimonium sunt non solum tui sermones suavissimi, quibus hîc liberaliter sum institutus, sed etiam liber tuus de Stella Nova editus, quem mihi dono mittere non dubitasti. Quare quamprimum eum a D. WOLFIO, Compatre meo charissimo, accepi, prima quacunque occasione oblata ad te gratitudinis ergo scribendum duxi, præsertim cum id meus in te amor et singularis observantia, qua te semper sum prosecutus, flagitaret, ne aut spacio temporis aut locorum intervallis diminuta esse videretur. Dominationi igitur tuæ primum de salvo atque incolumi in patriam reditu gratulor, eamque incolumitatem tibi perpetuam esse precor. Deinde pro libro tibi gratias, quas possum, maximas ago. Etsi autem ea non est mei ingenij facultas, ut parem gratiam referre possim, tamen affirmo, me omne meum studium, omnem meam observantiam cultum honorem denique tibi dicasse, meque tuarum maximarum virtutum, quas semper sum admiratus, memoriam perpetuo conservaturum esse. Porro te ignorare nolo, mi Domine TYCHO, SCHISLERUM γνωμονοποιὸν nostrum confecisse Globum ex cupro fabrefactum secundum omnes dimensiones perfectæ, veluti tuum Automaton, rotunditatis, cuius diameter quinquepedalis. Globus autem tali arte est fabrefactus, ut in 8 partes dissolvi et rursus in integrum restitui possit, necessitate exigente, quem in Hispaniam unâ cum Astrolabio quantitatis eiusdem per MARCUM FUGGERUM est misurus. Cum itaque viderem Globum, de tuis commodis cogitans quærebam, num etiam maiorem conficere posset? qui respondit ita, seque ad tale corpus conficiendum motum esse ex eo, quod audivisset D. tuam hîc artificem quæsivisse, qui id præstaret, seque posse æque conficere eiusmodi corpus, cuius diameter esset 8 vel 10 pedum, et ea arte, ut in 40 circiter partes resolutus facilime vasique inclusus, commodissime vel per mare vel per terras transportari possit. Cum autem ulterius quærerem, quantum cuperet pro tali corpore conficiendo, me ad D. tuam hac de re scripturum, respondit, non minus quam 1000 aureos, circulis exceptis et pede. Quare cum hîc Globum viderim ligneum, qui tamen propter ligni naturam suis ex æquo partibus non respondere videretur, hac de re certiorem facere volui, ut si forte eiusmodi corpus globosum D. tua habere cuperet, quod et ad durationem et perfectionem magis accederet, sciret, hîc esse Artificem, qui hoc se præstare posse diceret. Proinde si qua in re mea opera uti volueris, efficiam ut te nec sumptuum pœnitere queat, nec ego cura, opera et studio tibi defuisse videar, modo cum aliquo agas, qui artifici dimidium sumptuum tuo nomine exponat. In ceteris neque diligentia neque fide unquam deero.

Novi quod ad te scriberem non habebam, nisi forte scire velis, hoc ipso die post obitum PEUTINGERI in Duumvirum, quem nos vulgo Stadtpfleger nominamus, MARCUM FUGGERUM electum esse cum omnium civium admiratione.

Hisce vale feliciter, et me, quod facis, amare perge.
Augustæ Rheticæ postridie Cal. Augusti 1576.
D. T. deditissimus Iohan. Maior.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most excellent in nobility of birth, in learning, and in virtue, Master Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, Lord of Knudstrup, his lord and friend to be honoured with the utmost regard. Greeting.

Tokens of your singular goodwill toward me, noble Lord, are not only your most delightful conversations, in which I was here generously instructed, but also your published book on the New Star, which you did not hesitate to send me as a gift. And so, as soon as I received it from Master Wolf, my dearest co-godfather, I judged that I should write to you to express my gratitude at whatever first occasion offered itself — especially since my love for you and the singular regard with which I have always attended you demanded it, lest [that regard] should seem to have been lessened either by length of time or by the distances of place. I therefore congratulate Your Lordship first of all on your safe and unharmed return to your homeland, and I pray that that safety may be perpetual for you. Next, for the book I give you the greatest thanks I can. And although it is not within the power of my talent to render an equal thanks, yet I affirm that I have dedicated to you all my zeal, all my regard, my devotion, and in short my honour, and that I shall preserve forever the memory of your very great virtues, which I have always admired. Furthermore, I would not have you ignorant, my Lord Tycho, that Schissler, our gnomon-maker [γνωμονοποιός], has completed a Globe fashioned out of copper, perfect in all its dimensions of roundness — like your own Automaton — whose diameter is five feet. And the Globe has been fashioned with such art that it can be taken apart into 8 pieces and again restored to a whole, as need requires; this he is about to send into Spain, together with an Astrolabe of the same size, by way of Marcus Fugger. And so, when I saw the Globe, thinking of your advantage, I asked whether he could also make a larger one. He answered yes, and [said] that he had been moved to make such a body from the fact that he had heard Your Lordship had sought here for a craftsman who might furnish it, and that he could equally make a body of the kind whose diameter would be 8 or 10 feet, and [made] with such art that, taken apart into about 40 pieces and easily packed in a chest, it might most conveniently be transported either by sea or by land. But when I asked further how much he would want for making such a body — [saying] that I would write to Your Lordship about this matter — he answered, not less than 1000 gold pieces, the circles and the foot [stand] excepted. And so, since I have seen here a wooden Globe which nevertheless, owing to the nature of wood, did not seem to correspond evenly in its parts, I wished to inform you of this matter, so that, if perhaps Your Lordship should wish to have a spherical body of this kind — one which would come nearer both to durability and to perfection — you might know that there is here a Craftsman who said he could furnish this. Accordingly, if in any matter you should wish to use my service, I shall see to it that you can neither regret the expense, nor [that] I shall seem to have failed you in care, effort, and zeal — provided you deal with someone who, in your name, lays out half the expense to the craftsman. In all else I shall never be wanting either in diligence or in fidelity.

News that I might write to you I had none, unless perhaps you should wish to know that on this very day, after the death of Peutinger, Marcus Fugger has been elected as Duumvir — whom we commonly call Stadtpfleger — to the admiration of all the citizens.

With these things, fare happily, and continue, as you do, to love me.
At Augsburg of the Rhaetians, the day after the Kalends of August [2 August] 1576.
Most devoted to Your Lordship, Johan Maior.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iohan. Maior = Johannes Major (Johann Mair), an Augsburg friend and regular correspondent of Tycho's from his Augsburg years; named alongside Hieronymus Wolf and the Hainzel brothers among Tycho's Augsburg circle (Christianson, On Tycho's Island). He signs himself D. T. deditissimus ("most devoted to Your Lordship").
  • Ottonidi … Domino de Knudstrup — "son of Otte, Lord of Knudstrup": the Greek-style patronymic for Otte Brahe (Tycho's father) plus Tycho's principal Scanian seat, Knudstrup, kept as in.
  • liber tuus de Stella Nova — Tycho's De nova stella (Copenhagen, 1573), his book on the 1572 supernova in Cassiopeia, here acknowledged as a presentation gift.
  • D. Wolfio, Compatre meo — Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist (cf. the 1571 Wolf→Brahe letter, same circle); compater is the spiritual-kinship tie "co-godfather" (godparent to the same child / fellow sponsor), rendered literally rather than reduced to "friend."
  • Greek. γνωμονοποιὸν (gnōmonopoión, accusative of γνωμονοποιός) = "maker of gnomons / dials," here a learned epithet for the instrument-maker; transcribed as printed (final-sigma not normalised in the inflected form) and translated "gnomon-maker." gnomon is kept by the edition's convention
  • Instrument-maker. Schislerum = Christoph(er) Schissler the elder (active in Augsburg from 1546), the celebrated maker of fine astronomical and geometrical instruments. Scholarship notes that this very letter is the principal evidence on the question of who built the shell of Tycho's Globus Magnus Orichalcicus: Major reports Schissler offering to make Tycho a globe, which Mosley (Bearing the Heavens, citing TBOO VII, 36.5–26) reads as evidence that Schissler was not in fact the maker of Tycho's existing instrument. The letter is reported, not adjudicated, here.
  • veluti tuum Automaton — "like your own Automaton": rendered literally. The referent is uncertain — most plausibly Tycho's own great globe / a mechanical celestial device of his — and is left unglossed rather than guessed.
  • Globum … in 8 partes dissolvi … in 40 circiter partes resolutus … vasique inclusus — the demountable copper globe (5-foot diameter) dismantles into 8 parts; the proposed larger one (8–10-foot diameter) into about 40 parts, packable in a chest for transport by sea or land.
  • Marcum Fuggerum — Marcus Fugger (1529–1597), of the Augsburg banking dynasty; the shipping agent for the Spain consignment and, per the closing news, newly elected Duumvir / Stadtpfleger (city magistrate) of Augsburg.
  • Peutingeri — the Peutinger whose death created the vacancy Fugger filled; of the prominent Augsburg Peutinger family (the antiquary Konrad Peutinger's descendants). Stadtpfleger (the German civic title) is kept and glossed by Major's own Latin equivalent Duumvir.
  • 1000 aureos, circulis exceptis et pede — "1000 gold pieces, the circles and the foot excepted": the price quoted excludes the mounted graduated circles (circuli) and the stand/base (pes). aurei rendered "gold pieces" (gold coins, likely gulden/florins) rather than over-specified.
  • Astrolabio quantitatis eiusdem — "an Astrolabe of the same size," i.e. matching the 5-foot globe, the pair destined for Spain.
  • modo cum aliquo agas, qui artifici dimidium sumptuum tuo nomine exponat — the one conditional clause: Major's offer of service is conditioned on Tycho's dealing through an agent who will advance the craftsman half the cost in Tycho's name. Rendered as a single positive proviso (one modo-clause, no negation).
  • Dateline / calendar. Augustae Rheticae postridie Cal. Augusti 1576 = "at Augsburg [Augusta Rhaetica], the day after the Kalends of August, 1576" (O.S.). postridie Kalendas Augusti = the day after 1 August = 2 August; this agrees with the edition label ("2. august"). Dating crux flagged: Mosley cites the letter as "1 August 1576" (Bearing the Heavens), i.e. the Kalends itself; the discrepancy is the standard ambiguity over whether postridie Cal. is reckoned as 1 or 2 August. The Roman idiom is reproduced and glossed to 2 August per the literal reading; the date is not silently reconciled to Mosley's 1 August.
  • MS witness. The edition gives the witness as Vienna, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66 — the identical shelfmark recorded for the 1571 Wolf→Brahe letter; this may be a edition carry-over rather than a verified folio for this letter. Recorded as given, not relied on.
