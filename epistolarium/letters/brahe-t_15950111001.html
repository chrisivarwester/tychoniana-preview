The original

[AD RECTOREM ET PROFESSORES UNIVERSITATIS HAUNIENSIS].

DETTE erre mine suar till den vidisse, som magnificus dominus rector loedt mig y gaardt offuerantuor[de]. Effterdj ieg lod selff formedelst doctor Niell[s] Krag och Mogens Berttellßen, som vaare vdtßende aff mig, tilbyde den, som sagen ahngaar, der de fem breffue vaare lest for hannem, att der som hand haffde nogett der imodt att sige, waar ieg erbødig att suare der thill y magnifici domini rectoris och nogle aff professorum deris neruerellße, entten privatim eller publice, och der som hand nogitt sliigt wille, schulle hand giernne bekomme en vdtskrifft aff alle breffuene paa min egen bekostning. Huilckedt bleff hannom thidre end engang ahnbødit, alligeuell at hand icke selff wille schriffue paa breffuene, att de waare leste for hannom, och hand da in continenti intedt sligt, som hannem godtuilligen waar offereret, wille begerre och annamme, mens lod dennum beholle samme breffue, saa at de mig om afftenen bleffue igien offuerantuordede. Siunis mig derforre, att ieg icke nu haffuer y behoff att giffue hannom nogen copier aff samme breffue, wden saa waar, at hand tilbødt siig att wille billigen replicere nogit der wdj, som macht paalaahe, och beuiße det anderledis, och aff sig selff, waar der hos begierrendis, at got folck maatte sige och kiende der paa, da er ieg till sindtz gerne at lade hannem faa dem alle vdtschreffne, och att hannem tilstedis att haffue tilbørliig tiid och respitt at betencke sig der paa, och føre der imodt, hues hannom ßelff lyster. Och vill ieg da gerne lade mig nøye met hues magnificus dominus rector och professores her y vniuersitetett rettuißelliigenn kiende och affsiige derpaa, beßynderliigen huad de fornemste puncther och hoffuidt sagen y sig selff ahngaar, som er, om karlen haffuer holdit sitt løffte, ordt och breffue och derhos andre got folckis ordt och breffue, paa hans wegne och met hans willie och widtschab vdtgiffne, saa erligen och oprechteligen, som dett sig burde, och om ieg, eller nogen paa mine wegne, haffuer giffuett hannem nogen skellig aarsage att handle der imodt. Dersom hand sligtt aff sig selff er begierrendis (huorudj ieg dog, om mueligt vaar, hannem gierne wille haffue forschaanit), schall hand (som sagt er) met det første bekomme sandtferdiige copier aff alle de fem breffue, som bleffue leste for hannem, och hues andre hand paaesker. Ellers beßuer ieg mig nu paa ny hannem sligt at tilbyde eller lade wederfaris, paa det mine och min kierre daathers skriffuellßer icke schulle bliffue wrangeligen y min affuerelße vdtydett och mißbrugtt. Dog dersom magnificus dominus rector met sine assessoribus et collegis wille och kunde met rette kende, at mig bør sliigtt, hues copierne aff breffuene belanger hannem at lade faa, nu at effterlade, vahnßeett at de erre hannum tilforn forgebs tilbødne, och alligeuell at sagen icke bliffuer legitime conoscerit och vrdelit, och de ville giffue mig den deris sententz beschreffuen, da er ieg erbødig at wille rette mig dereffter, och giøre siden dervdj, hues billigt kand werre, och som handelen y sig selff wdtkreffuer. Hues det andett belanger, at ieg schulle icke lade lesße for got folck samme breffue, dett achter ieg, at ingen kand eller bør mig formene, eller anderledis billigen begerre, ey heller at komme wiidt de forulempninger, ieg haffuer ladett hannem adtuare att haffue y fordrag, och icke io at maa siige, hues hand kan bestaa met, mens at mig bør endelligen sligt at giøre, paa dett gottfolck kunde komme y en rett grund om sagsens leyliighedt, som dennem tilforn waar vbewist. Helst effterdj hand och hans ahnhang haffuer nu en gantsche maanidt omkring her y byen och anderstedtz giffuett allehonde forre, till at smycke hans sag met, och att wende all skylden paa mig och mine, anderledis end ieg formener, att dett y sandheedt schall befindis, saa att mange gott folck waare derudtoffuer kommen y en wrang mening och mißforstandt, før de en part finge leyligheden der om rett at wide. Syntis derforre mig at werre aldelis fornøden, att ieg lodtt lesße samme skriffter for nogle fornemme gott folck, och berettede dennom derhos leyligheeden wdj all sandtheedt, icke for ieg achtede at lade hannem wederfaris derudtoffuer nogett onde,mens alleniste mig och mine till en sandtferdig och fornødenn vndtschyldtning. Huilcken min betenckning, met hues anden rettigheedt wdj denne sag nu eder hereffter som fornøden kandt werre att tale paa, och mig giffues billig aarsage till, ieg will aldelis haffue mig forbehollen.

Actum Kiøbenhaffn den 11 ianvarii anno 1595.
Tyghe Brahe
met egen hd.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TO THE RECTOR AND PROFESSORS OF THE UNIVERSITY OF COPENHAGEN].

THIS is my answer to the attestation [vidisse] which the Magnificent Lord Rector had delivered over to me yesterday. Inasmuch as I myself, through Doctor Niels Krag and Mogens Bertelsen, who had been sent out by me, caused an offer to be made to the man whom the matter concerns — when the five letters had been read out to him — that, if he had anything to say against them, I was ready to answer to it in the presence of the Magnificent Lord Rector and some of the professors, whether privately or publicly; and that if he wished anything of the kind, he should gladly receive a transcript of all the letters at my own expense. Which was offered to him more than once; yet [seeing] that he would not himself write on the letters that they had been read out to him, and that he then would not, in continenti, request and accept any such thing as was offered him of [my] good will, but let them keep these same letters — so that in the evening they were delivered back to me — it therefore seems to me that I am not now under obligation to give him any copies of these same letters, unless it were that he offered himself willing to reply in proper form to something therein that bore weight, and to prove it otherwise; and, of his own accord, were besides desirous that good people might pronounce and adjudge upon it: [in that case] I am minded gladly to let him have them all transcribed, and that he be allowed to have due time and respite to consider himself upon it, and to bring against it whatever he himself pleases. And I will then gladly let myself be content with whatever the Magnificent Lord Rector and the professors here at the university shall justly adjudge and pronounce thereon — particularly as concerns the foremost points and the principal matter itself, which is: whether the fellow has kept his promise, word and letters, and likewise other good people's words and letters issued on his behalf and with his will and knowledge, as honestly and uprightly as it ought to have been; and whether I, or anyone on my behalf, have given him any reasonable cause to act against it. If he is desirous of such a thing of his own accord (wherein, however, were it possible, I would gladly have spared him), he shall (as has been said) at the first receive truthful copies of all the five letters that were read out to him, and of whatever others he demands. Otherwise it is grievous to me now anew to offer or let befall him such a thing, lest my own and my dear daughter's writings should, in my absence, be wrongfully interpreted and misused. Yet if the Magnificent Lord Rector together with his assessors and colleagues should be willing and able rightly to judge that it behoves me now to forgo such a thing — as touches the letting him have the copies of the letters — notwithstanding that they were previously offered him in vain, and notwithstanding that the case is not [thereby] legitime examined and adjudged, and [if] they will give me their sentence in writing, then I am ready to be willing to conform myself thereto, and thereafter to do therein whatever may be equitable and as the affair itself requires. As concerns the other point — that I should not let these same letters be read out to good people — that I hold that no one can or ought to forbid me, nor otherwise equitably to require [of me], nor [to forbid me] to touch upon those injuries which I have had him warned to bear in patience, and indeed [to forbid me] to say what he can answer for; but rather that it behoves me by all means to do such a thing, so that good people might come to a true ground concerning the circumstances of the case, which were previously unknown to them. The more so, inasmuch as he and his following have now for a whole month round about, here in the town and elsewhere, given out all manner of things to dress up his cause withal, and to turn all the blame upon me and mine, otherwise than I am persuaded it shall in truth be found — so that many good people had thereupon fallen into a wrong opinion and misunderstanding, before some part [of them] got rightly to know the circumstances thereof. It therefore seemed to me to be altogether needful that I should let these same writings be read out before some eminent good people, and report to them therewith the circumstances in all truth — not because I intended to let any evil befall him thereby, but only for a truthful and needful exculpation of myself and mine. Which my purpose, together with whatever other right in this case it may hereafter be needful for me to plead before you, and to which I am given equitable cause, I will altogether keep reserved to myself.

Done at Copenhagen, the 11th of January, the year 1595.
Tyge Brahe
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender / occasion. Tycho Brahe, writing from the mainland (the letter is dated at Copenhagen) to the Consistory of the University of Copenhagen — the rector and professors. The document is a formal pleading in the Gellius Sascerides affair: the broken betrothal between Tycho's eldest daughter Magdalene Brahe and the physician Gellius Sascerides (Jell(e) Sascerides, M.D. Basel). After negotiations collapsed in the last months of 1594, the University Consistory intervened in January 1595 to mediate; the affair was never resolved by marriage (Gellius married elsewhere in 1599; Magdalene followed her father to Prague and never married). See On Tycho's Island and Dreyer, Tycho Brahe.
  • "den, som sagen ahngaar" / "karlen" / "hand" — "the man whom the matter concerns," "the fellow," "he": throughout, the unnamed adversary is Gellius Sascerides. Tycho never names him; the contemptuous karlen ("the fellow / the churl") is preserved as "the fellow."
  • "min kierre daather" — "my dear daughter," i.e. Magdalene Brahe; her skriffuellßer ("writings," letters in the betrothal correspondence) are among the documents Tycho will not surrender for fear of misuse.
  • Emissaries. doctor Niels Krag = Niels Krag (c. 1550–1602), humanist, historian and professor at Copenhagen (later royal historiographer). Mogens Berttellßen = Mogens Bertelsen Dallin, who acted as spokesman in the negotiations (named in On Tycho's Island as "Mogens Bertelsen Dallin … spokesman for Dr. Gellius" in the broader affair; here he is sent by Tycho). Both were "sent out by me" to put the offer to Gellius.
  • Latin rubric & code-switches. The heading [AD RECTOREM ET PROFESSORES UNIVERSITATIS HAUNIENSIS] ("To the Rector and Professors of the University of Copenhagen") is the editorial/chancery Latin rubric over a Danish body; preserved by the edition's convention Latin technical terms studding the Danish are kept and glossed: vidisse = a notarised attestation / certified statement (the Consistory's written record, "the vidisse"); magnificus dominus rector = "the Magnificent Lord Rector"; professores / professorum = "the professors"; privatim … publice = "privately … publicly"; in continenti = "then and there, on the spot"; replicere = to reply / rejoin (in the legal sense); assessoribus et collegis = "[his] assessors and colleagues"; legitime conoscerit och vrdelit = "lawfully examined and adjudged" (conoscerit < Lat. cognoscere, to take judicial cognizance of; vrdelit = Da. ordelet/dømt, "adjudged"); sententz = the sentence / judgment (in writing).
  • Negation count (load-bearing — verified against the source). The dense clause on the second offer reproduces exactly the source's negatives: "he would not himself write … that they had been read out to him, and … would not … request and accept any such thing." The penultimate long sentence stacks three coordinated prohibitions Tycho denies anyone may impose: "no one can or ought to forbid me, nor otherwise equitably to require, nor [to forbid me] to touch upon those injuries … and [nor to forbid me] to say what he can answer for." The Danish here is elliptical — "och icke io at maa siige, hues hand kan bestaa met" — and the governing verb (formene, "forbid") must be carried forward from the head of the period; the bracketed "[to forbid me]" supplies it. This is the principal crux (see Confidence).
  • "hues hand kan bestaa met" — literally "what he can answer for / stand by." Parallel uses of bestaa in Dreyer, Opera Omnia XIV (pp. 70 bestaa oc bekiende = acknowledge/confess; 109 ieg faar well adt bestaa det = stand by/answer for) confirm the core sense "answer for / acknowledge," rendered accordingly (revised from the earlier "be charged with"); the secondary shading "what he can put up with" is flagged. The governing verb formene ("forbid") is overt at the head of the period (Dreyer XIV p. 86: ingen kand eller bør mig formene), so the supplied "[to forbid me]" across the ellipsis is grammatically secure — confirming the (a)-crux structure.
  • "haffue y fordrag" — "to bear in patience / forbear," i.e. the injuries (forulempninger) Tycho says he had Gellius warned to endure; adtuare = "to warn / admonish" (Da. advare).
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1595, German-influenced). erre = ere ("are"); suar = svar ("answer"); offuerantuor[de] = overantvorde ("deliver over"; the MS contracts the ending, expanded in brackets); effterdj = eftersom/efterdi ("inasmuch as"); formedelst = "by means of, through"; vdtßende = udsendte ("sent out"); hannem/hannom = ham ("him"); thidre end engang = tiere end engang ("more than once"); in continenti (Latin) kept; dennum/dennem = dennem ("them"); offuerantuordede = overantvordede ("delivered over"); wden saa waar = uden så var ("unless it were"); billigen / billigt = "equitably / equitable, reasonable"; macht paalaahe = magt pålå ("bore weight, mattered"); got folck / gottfolck = "good people" (respectable persons, here as informal arbiters/witnesses); respitt = "respite" (a Latin/French loan); rettuißelliigenn = retfærdeligen ("justly"); affsiige = afsige ("pronounce [judgment]"); hoffuidt sagen = hovedsagen ("the principal matter"); løffte = løfte ("promise"); widtschab = vidskab/viden ("knowledge"); oprechteligen = oprigteligen ("uprightly"); skellig aarsage = skellig årsag ("reasonable cause"); paaesker = påæsker ("demands"); beßuer ieg mig = besværer jeg mig ("it is grievous / burdensome to me"); affuerelße = fraværelse ("absence"); wrangeligen … vdtydett = vrangeligen udtydet ("wrongfully interpreted"); vahnßeett = uanset ("notwithstanding"); forgebs = forgæves ("in vain"); forulempninger = "injuries, wrongs"; ahnhang = anhang ("following, faction"; a Ger. loan); en gantsche maanidt = en ganske måned ("a whole month"); giffuett allehonde forre = givet allehånde fore ("given out / put about all manner of things"); smycke = smykke ("to adorn, dress up"); wrang mening = vrang mening ("wrong opinion"); vbewist = ubevidst/ubekendt ("unknown"); betenckning = "purpose, consideration, opinion"; forbehollen = forbeholdt ("reserved").
  • Calendar / place / subscription. Actum Kiøbenhaffn den 11 ianvarii anno 1595 = "Done at Copenhagen, 11 January 1595" (O.S.; Actum "Done/Given" kept by the edition's convention). The edition label ("1595. 11. januar. Uraniborg") names Uraniborg as place, but the source's own dateline reads Copenhagen (Kiøbenhaffn); the header follows the as-written dateline by the edition's convention The discrepancy is consistent with the affair playing out before the Copenhagen Consistory: the catalogue may record Tycho's seat (Uraniborg) rather than the place of subscription. met egen hd. = med egen hånd ("with [my] own hand"), the autograph subscription formula.
