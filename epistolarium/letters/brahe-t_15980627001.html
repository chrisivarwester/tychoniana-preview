The original

NOBILITATE, SAPIENTIA, PIETATE ET VIRTUTE PRAESTANTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO PATRONO ET AMICO VETERI SUMMA OBSERVANTIA COLENDO.

CLARISSIME vir ac frater in Domino dilecte, CLAUDIUS MULERUS tuus domesticus tradidit mihi literas tuas humanissime scriptas, ex quibus intelligo te memorem esse veteris nostræ consuetudinis atque amicitiæ, id quod mihi pergratum et periucundum fuit. Rogo Deum optim. max. ut te quam diutissime sanum et incolumem cum tota familiâ conservet. Etsi autem per valetudinem non licet prolixius scribere, tamen animus meus, donec vixero, tibi adhærebit sincerus. Quod autem salutes me nomine MONAVIJ præstantissimi viri HERMANNI et DANIELIS BUCRETIJ, est mihi pergratum, precorque illis vicissim et animi et corporis constantem valetudinem, et rogo te clarissime vir, ut in tuis ad eos literis vicissim salutem dicas meo nomine. Porro quod MONAVIUS petat copiam quarundam literarum PHILIPPI ad me scriptarum, non possum ipsius voto satisfacere. Nam et ipsæ li[teræ] perierunt et fuerunt scriptæ de privatis negotijs et politicis non publicandis etc. Quare rogo, ut boni consulas, quod non possim in hac re tuæ petitioni satisfacere, alias promptissimus ad omnia præstanda, quæ tibi intellexero fore grata. Vale vir clarissime cum familia tua et domesticis omnibus. Deus te conservet, amen.

Roschildiæ 27. Iunij Anno 1598.
NICOLAUS HEMMINGIUS,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man most excellent in nobility, wisdom, piety, and virtue, Master Tycho Brahe, his lord, patron, and old friend, to be honoured with the utmost respect.

Most distinguished man and brother beloved in the Lord, Claude Muler, your domestic, delivered to me your most kindly-written letter, from which I understand that you are mindful of our old acquaintance and friendship — which was most pleasing and most delightful to me. I pray God Best and Greatest to preserve you, sound and unharmed, as long as possible, with your whole household. And although, by reason of [my] health, I am not permitted to write at greater length, yet my mind, as long as I shall live, will cleave to you, sincere. But that you greet me in the name of Monau, [and] of the most excellent men Hermann and Daniel Bucretius, is most pleasing to me; and I pray for them in turn constant health of mind and body, and I ask you, most distinguished man, that in your letters to them you, in turn, say a greeting in my name. Further, as to Monau's seeking a copy of certain letters of Philip [Melanchthon] written to me — I cannot satisfy his wish. For both the letters themselves have perished, and they were written about private and political matters, not to be published, etc. Wherefore I ask that you take it in good part that I cannot, in this matter, satisfy your request — being otherwise most ready to perform everything which I shall understand will be pleasing to you. Farewell, most distinguished man, with your family and all your household. May God preserve you, amen.

Roskilde, 27 June, in the year 1598.
Niels Hemmingsen,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Niels Hemmingsen (Nicolaus Hemmingius, 1513–1600), the most eminent Danish Lutheran theologian of his age and a pupil of Melanchthon; by 1598 very old (he died 1600) and living in retirement at Roskilde (he had been removed from his Copenhagen chair in 1579 on a charge of Calvinist leanings). He addresses Tycho as frater in Domino dilecte ("brother beloved in the Lord") and "old friend."
  • Bearer. Claudius Mulerus, tuus domesticus = Claude Muler (Mulerius), a French member of Tycho's household, who carried the letter (he reappears as the would-be courier in brahe-t_16000919001).
  • The Melanchthon letters. Monavius = Jacob Monau (Monavius, 1546–1603), the Breslau (Wrocław) humanist — which explains the Wrocław provenance of the witness. Monau had asked, through Tycho, for copies of certain letters of Philip Melanchthon (Philippi) to Hemmingsen; Hemmingsen declines: they have perished, and concerned private and political matters not to be published. (Hemmingsen does not sharpen or apologise beyond this; the refusal is preserved as written.)
  • Hermann and Daniel Bucretius — two further men whom Tycho had greeted on Monau's behalf; flagged: read as a pair (likely brothers) sharing the surname Bucretius, but not securely re-identified; rendered "Hermann and Daniel Bucretius."
  • Calendar. Roschildiae 27. Iunij 1598 = 27 June 1598; Denmark (Roskilde) = Julian / Old Style. Header agrees with the label.
