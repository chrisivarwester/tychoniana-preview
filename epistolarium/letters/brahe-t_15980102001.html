The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD IMPERATOREM RUDOLPHUM SECUNDUM.

AUGUSTISSIME Imperator RUDOLPHE Secunde. Cum de tuae Imperatoriae Maiestatis erga liberalia studia, praesertim in his principaliora, Mathematica et Astronomica, clementi favore, diu multumque inaudiverim, ipsaque tua Caesarea Maiestas non tantum potentissimorum et laudatissimorum maiorum suorum ALPHONSI Regis Arragonum etc. MATTHIAe HUNNIADIS, HENRICI illius secundi ob indefessam rerum coelestium contemplationem Astronomi dicti, CAROLI quinti, FERDINANDI ac Augustissimi Parentis MAXIMILIANI secundi aliorumque, studijs his impense addictorum, quos numerare longum foret, celebratissimo exemplo, sed et sua ipsiusmet vere Heroica et Caesarea inclinatione, quantum Regnorum ac Reip. gubernatio ferunt, ijsdem his laudabiliter vacet exercitijs: omnino mihi polliceor, Caesaream tuam Maiestatem clementi animo accepturam, me viarum intercapedine alijsque difficultatibus, quo minus Caesaream tuam Maiestatem ipse cito adeo, ac praeoptarim alias, accedere liceat, praepeditum, hasce literas arduum licet negotium concernentes summisse exhibere non vereri ipsi. Cuius meae fidentiae caussam hanc primam habeo et praecipuam Imperatoriae tuae Maiestatis clementissimum erga me eaque, quae multis abhinc annis tracto studia, favorem, iampridem mihi vel ex solo eo singulari diplomate perspectum, quod Caesarea tua Maiestas tanquam in epistola ad me directo sermone clementer mihi impertiri, non sine harum artium praeclaro elogio, dignata est. Caesarea itaque tua Maiestas praesentibus hisce clementer scire dignetur, me, evidentibus ijsque haud levibus de caussis mutato Patrio solo, in Germaniam tuae Caesareae Maiestati subditam cum Astronomica mea supellectile, adeoque tota familia, nuper concessisse, ut est rerum humanarum omnium quaedam subinde vicissitudo. Ubi, favente coelestium Opifice, Astronomicos meos labores per 21 Annos in patria mea dulcissima Dania exantlatos continuare ac ad propositum scopum dirigere et absolvere decrevi, impetrata primum hîc alicubi et constituta rebus coelestibus sat commoda, constanti et inviolabili sede, unde fundamentalis earundem tractatio aeviternum ad posteros etiam in Imperij ipsius decus (uti confido) non contemnendum transmitti possit. Quocirca ad tuam Imperatoriam Maiestatem omnium primo mihi concedendum, eiusque clementissimam voluntatem quam humillime explorandam censui, an non illustre hoc Astronomicum negotium solide et solenniter institutum in aliqua suarum ditionum porro continuare atque ad optatum finem, Dei beneficio, perducere daretur. Sunt mihi ad hoc Opus in promptu Instrumenta varia, eaque maxima et affabre elaborata, qualia et quanta vix uspiam invenire datur. Sunt plurimorum annorum exactissimae Observationes veterum inventis non cedentes. Sunt multa alia huc conducentia, et non pauca, imo praecipua iam confecta. Ut vero Caesarea tua Maiestas specimen aliquod eorum habeat, mitto cum filio meo Organorum, quae hactenus in usu habui, quotquot tam cito exscindi curare licuit, delineationem, apposita earundem succincta explicatione. Offero quoque humiliter Canonicam Affixarum Stellarum expositionem, quotquot in nostro climate utcunque visuntur, numero millenarum, quas omnes intra Annos 20 praeteritos summa et indefessa diligentia meis Organis coelitus quam scrupulosissime demensus sum, cum sine harum, potissimum Zodiaco vicinarum, exacta verificatione Planetarum viae et motiones ad amussim non percipiantur. Qua etiam in parte (quoad errantia videlicet sidera) quid hactenus praestiterimus, quidque in posterum superaddendum restet, prior ille, de quo dixi, liber suo loco pandit. Plura meorum de Astronomia Operum tua Caesarea Maiestas, cui me meaque studia omnia humiliter offero et commendata cupio, brevi, aspirante Universitatis authore, obtinebit. Interea hasce primitias alijs gravioribus viam sternentes non aversetur. De caetero Caesarea tua Maiestas per Dominum Procancellarium Imperij aut aliquem e suis Consiliarijs intimis, referente Domino Doctore THADDÆO HAGGECIO, clementer intelligat, qui a filio meo praesente singula uberius cognoscat. Utque Caesarea tua Maiestas, hoc intermediante, sibi humillime meo nomine proposita clementer excipiat, et responsum, quandocunque eidem commodum fuerit, impertiri dignetur, qua possum submissione precor.

Deus Coelestium et Terrestrium author tuam Caesaream Maiestatem felici Imperij et Regnorum gubernatione quam diutissime florentem et incolumem tueatur.

Datae Wandelburgo ex Arce Ranzoviana prope Hamburgum sita, die 2 Ianuarij Anni 1598.

TYCHO BRAHE TO EMPEROR RUDOLF THE SECOND.

Most August Emperor Rudolf the Second. Since I have for a long while and much heard tell of Your Imperial Majesty's clement favour toward the liberal studies — and, foremost among these, toward the mathematical and astronomical [studies] — and [since] Your Imperial Majesty Himself, not only by the most celebrated example of His most powerful and most praised ancestors — Alfonso, King of Aragon, etc., Matthias Hunyadi, that Henry the Second who for his unwearied contemplation of the things of the heavens was called the Astronomer, Charles the Fifth, Ferdinand, and that most August Parent Maximilian the Second, and others, who were earnestly devoted to these studies, whom it would be long to number — but also by His own truly heroic and imperial inclination, devotes Himself in laudable fashion to these same pursuits, so far as the government of His kingdoms and of the commonwealth allow: I altogether promise myself that Your Imperial Majesty will receive with clement spirit [the fact] that I, hindered by the long stretch of the roads and by other difficulties from being able to approach Your Imperial Majesty as soon as, and as I should otherwise have most preferred, do not fear to present humbly this letter, though it concerns an arduous matter. The first and chief cause of this confidence of mine I hold to be Your Imperial Majesty's most clement favour toward me and toward those studies which I have pursued for many years now — long since made plain to me even from that single, extraordinary diploma which Your Imperial Majesty deigned clemently to grant me, addressing me as it were in a letter in direct speech, and not without splendid praise of these arts. May Your Imperial Majesty therefore deign clemently to know by these present [letters] that I, for evident and by no means slight causes, having changed my native soil, have lately withdrawn into Germany, subject to Your Imperial Majesty, with my astronomical equipment and indeed with my whole household — as there is from time to time a certain vicissitude in all human affairs. There, the Maker of the heavenly things favouring [me], I have resolved to continue the astronomical labours which I toiled through over 21 years in my most sweet homeland Denmark, and to direct them to their proposed goal and bring them to completion — once there has first been obtained and established somewhere here a settled and inviolable seat, sufficiently suited to the heavenly matters, whence the fundamental treatment of those same [matters] may be transmitted to posterity for all time, and (as I trust) to no contemptible adornment of the Empire itself. Wherefore I judged that I must first of all be granted access to Your Imperial Majesty, and most humbly explore His most clement will [as to] whether it might not be granted [me] to continue this illustrious astronomical undertaking — solidly and solemnly established — onward in some one of His dominions, and to bring it, by God's beneficence, to the wished-for end. I have ready to hand for this Work instruments various, and those very great and skilfully wrought, such, and so great, as one is scarcely granted to find anywhere. There are most exact observations of very many years, not yielding to the discoveries of the ancients. There are many other things conducing hereto, and not a few — indeed the chief ones — already completed. But that Your Imperial Majesty may have some specimen of these, I send with my son a delineation of the instruments which I have hitherto had in use — as many as could be engraved so quickly — with a concise explanation of the same appended. I also humbly offer a canonical exposition of the fixed stars — as many as are in any way seen in our clime, to the number of a thousand — all of which, within the 20 years past, I have measured out from the heavens by my instruments, with the utmost and unwearied diligence, as scrupulously as could be; since without an exact verification of these — especially of those near the zodiac — the courses and motions of the planets are not perceived to the line. In which part too (with respect, that is, to the wandering stars) what I have so far accomplished, and what remains to be superadded hereafter, that former book of which I spoke sets forth in its place. More of my works on astronomy Your Imperial Majesty — to whom I humbly offer myself and all my studies, and wish them commended — shall shortly obtain, the Author of the Universe favouring [it]. Meanwhile let Him not turn away from these first-fruits, which pave the way for others weightier. For the rest, may Your Imperial Majesty be clemently informed through the Lord Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, or some one of His privy councillors — Lord Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius reporting [the matter] — who may learn each particular more fully from my son, [who is] present. And I pray, with what submission I can, that Your Imperial Majesty, through this intermediary, may clemently take up the [matters] most humbly proposed in my name, and deign to grant a response whenever it shall be convenient to Him.

May God, the Author of the heavenly and the earthly things, keep Your Imperial Majesty, by a fortunate government of the Empire and the kingdoms, flourishing and unharmed for the very longest time.

Given at Wandsbek, from the Castle of Rantzau situated near Hamburg, on the 2nd day of January, in the year 1598.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), then in self-imposed exile from Denmark; Rudolf II (1552–1612), Holy Roman Emperor (r. 1576–1612), residing at Prague — to whose service Tycho was angling. This is Tycho's formal overture to imperial patronage, carried by his eldest son. It is the prose counterpart of the dedication of the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (dated 31 December 1597 at Wandsbek), and the delineation of the instruments it announces is the engraved instrument-catalogue of that very work.
  • Place. Wandesburgo ex Arce Ranzoviana prope Hamburgum = Wandsbek (now part of Hamburg), at the castle of Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), the Danish royal governor (Statthalter) of Holstein and humanist patron, who hosted Tycho in 1597–98. Tycho had left Hven in 1597 ("changed my native soil").
  • The son. cum filio meo … a filio meo praesente — "with my son … from my son, present": Tycho Brahe the Younger (b. 1581), here acting as his father's envoy to court (though the diplomatic mission to Prague is more usually associated with Tycho's assistant Franz Tengnagel; the letter names only filius meus). Rendered literally as "my son," not resolved to a named agent.
  • The ancestors named. A roll of imperial/royal patrons of astronomy: Alphonsus Rex Arragonum = Alfonso X of Castile (1221–1284), patron of the Alfonsine Tables — Tycho's "etc." and "of Aragon" loosely subsume the Iberian crowns (Alfonso X was King of Castile and León; the slip is the source's, kept). Matthias Hunniades = Matthias Corvinus (Hunyadi), King of Hungary (1443–1490), patron of Regiomontanus. Henricus ille secundus … Astronomi dicti = a Henry "the Astronomer"; the epithet was applied to several Henries (e.g., Henry III of Castile "el Doliente" is sometimes confused; medieval tradition styled an Emperor/king "Henry the Astronomer") — Tycho's exact referent is not securely fixed and is left as the text gives it (crux, see below). Carolus quintus = Charles V (1500–1558); Ferdinandus = Ferdinand I (1503–1564); Maximilianus secundus = Maximilian II (1527–1576), Rudolf's father (Augustissimus Parens).
  • Instruments / observations. Organa / Instrumenta → instruments; Organorum … delineatio = "a delineation [drawn representation/engraving] of the instruments" — the plates of the Mechanica. Canonica Affixarum Stellarum expositio … numero millenarum = a canonical exposition of the fixed stars, to the number of a thousand — Tycho's 1,000-star catalogue (Stellarum inerrantium restitutio), measured meis Organis coelitus ("from the heavens by my instruments"). ad amussim = "to the line / exactly" (a builder's-rule idiom), here rendered "to the line."
  • prior ille … liber — "that former book of which I spoke": i.e. the instrument-volume (Mechanica) just mentioned, which in its place treats the planets (errantia sidera, "the wandering stars").
  • primitias … alijs gravioribus viam sternentes — "these first-fruits, which pave the way for others weightier": the Mechanica + star-catalogue as forerunners of Tycho's larger projected works (the planetary theory / Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata).
  • Court officials. Dominus Procancellarius Imperij = the Imperial Vice-Chancellor; Consiliarij intimi = privy councillors. Thaddaeus Haggecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius, 1525–1600), Bohemian physician-astronomer and imperial counsellor at Prague, Tycho's long-standing correspondent and his key intermediary at Rudolf's court.
  • singulari diplomate … in epistola ad me directo sermone — "that single, extraordinary diploma … addressing me as it were in a letter in direct speech": an earlier personal mark of imperial favour shown to Tycho; rendered without sharpening the simile (tanquam … directo sermone).
  • aspirante Universitatis authore / Deus Coelestium et Terrestrium author — "the Author of the Universe favouring [it]" / "God, the Author of the heavenly and the earthly things": Tycho's pious framing; aspirante = "breathing favour upon," the standard Deo aspirante hedge, kept as a conditional ("favouring").
  • Calendar. die 2 Ianuarij Anni 1598 = 2 January 1598. Denmark and the Empire's Protestant correspondents used the Julian (Old Style) calendar; the dateline is taken at face value as O.S. The catalogue label ("1598. 2. januar. Wandsburg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. No style marker (n.s.) is present.
  • Hedges preserved. (uti confido) "as I trust"; favente coelestium Opifice / Dei beneficio / aspirante … authore — Tycho's success-conditions are kept as conditionals, not flattened into assertions; vix uspiam invenire datur "one is scarcely granted to find anywhere" (not "nowhere"); non contemnendum "no contemptible" (litotes kept).
  • Correction. Restored 'Wandelburgo' (Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.20) for the one-letter departure 'Wandesburgo' in our transcription of the closing dateline; the English 'Wandsbek' was unaffected.
