The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANDREAM SEVERINI VELLEIUM.
S.

PERIUCUNDUM mihi auditu fuerat, amantissime D. Magister ANDREA, quod nuper Hafniæ ex epistola tua Magnifico Domino DANZÆO inscripta intellexeram te brevi nos invisurum, modo hyems iter tuum non impediverit, vel potius gentis in retractandis pollicitis consuetudo. Cur vero nunc has ad te mittam, fraudulenta discessio cuiusdam servi me impulit, quod se in hunc modum habet: ob turpiter et inhoneste commissa facinora cum ancilla mea, quæ claves ad penu meum conservabat, cum qua ipse et libidinem et furta exercuit, unâ cum hoc altero, quem nunc secum abduxit, quæ etiam eo nomine cum infamia a me dimissa est. Hæc tamen omnia hactenus dissimulavi, parcens ipsius famæ, ob ætatem, artem et Ripenses amicos, adeo ut nemo me ea rescivisse de ipso intellexerit, sed saltem in alios corrivales ipsius tota culpa redundârit, nulla ipsius facta mentione, prout nostra postulat amicitia, quia est res malo exemplari propter alios meos opifices et servos, impune citra omnem causam et necessitatem se subducere. Ubi ipsum recepero, si resipuerit et culpam deprecatus fuerit, lenius cum illo agam quam promeritus est, præsertim te pro illo per literas intercedente. Omnem itaque move lapidem, charissime M. ANDREA, ut certo mihi restituatur, quod sane non difficulter efficere poteris, et da operam ne intelligam te minus sincere meas partes in hoc negocio agere, ne labefactetur amicitia nostra, quæ propemodum vacillare nonnihil mihi videtur ex literis Domino DANZÆO a te perscriptis, ubi vehementiæ et acerbitatis ob nescio quos versus me insimulas. Callide quidem satis pro teipso, ut apud æmulos per tralationem a suspicione consensus te liberes, sed pro tuo TYCHONE minus candide et fortasse etiam pro intimo tuo proprio sensu et iudicio minus vere. Vale et da operam, ut in hoc negocio tuam erga me fidem experiar. Saluta METTAM cum utroque parente ceterisque amicis nostris. Iterum vale et admonitionem hanc meam ut libere prolatam patienter et sincere accipe.

Iterum terque quaterque vale et ante hyemem, si per tempestates licuerit, nos invise.
Datæ Uraniburgi die 11. Nov. Anno 1585.
TYCHO BRAHE
mea manu.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Anders Sørensen Vedel.
Greeting.

It had been most delightful for me to hear, most loving Master Anders, what I lately learned at Copenhagen from your letter addressed to the Magnificent Master Dançay — that you would shortly visit us, provided the winter does not hinder your journey, or rather the nation's habit of going back on what it has promised. But the reason why I now send these [lines] to you is that the fraudulent departure of a certain servant has driven me [to it], the matter standing thus: on account of deeds shamefully and dishonourably committed with a maidservant of mine — she who kept the keys to my storeroom, with whom he practised both lust and thefts, together with this other [man] whom he has now carried off with him — she too has on that account been dismissed by me in disgrace. Yet all this I have hitherto dissembled, sparing his reputation on account of his age, his trade, and his Ribe friends, so that no one has perceived that I had found this out about him, but rather the whole blame has overflowed onto certain other rivals of his, with no mention made of his deed — as our friendship requires — because it is a matter of bad example, on account of my other workmen and servants, that he should withdraw himself with impunity, without any cause or necessity. When I have got him back, if he comes to his senses and begs pardon for his fault, I will deal more leniently with him than he has deserved, especially with you interceding for him by letter. So move every stone, dearest Master Anders, that he may surely be restored to me — which indeed you will be able to bring about without difficulty — and take care that I do not perceive you to be playing my part in this affair less than sincerely, lest our friendship be undermined, which seems to me to be on the point of wavering somewhat, from the letter written out by you to Master Dançay, where you charge me with vehemence and harshness on account of some verses or other. Cunningly enough, to be sure, on your own behalf — so that among [our] rivals you might, by shifting [it onto me], free yourself from the suspicion of complicity — but on behalf of your own Tycho less candidly, and perhaps also, as regards your own innermost feeling and judgement, less truthfully. Farewell, and take care that in this affair I may put your good faith toward me to the test. Greet Mette, with both her parents and the rest of our friends. Again farewell, and receive this admonition of mine, as freely uttered, patiently and sincerely.

Again, and thrice and four times, farewell; and before winter, if the storms allow, visit us.
Given at Uraniborg, the 11th day of November, in the year 1585.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in my own hand.

[Postscript:] Of other matters and private affairs I shall perhaps treat more conveniently on another occasion, or even more safely face to face.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Andreas Severini Vellejus = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), royal historiographer, translator of Saxo Grammaticus (1575), and Tycho's old friend; based at Ribe (hence the Ripenses amici, "Ribe friends," below). Addressed throughout as Magister / M. Andrea (academic "Master," this chapter's convention).
  • Salutation. S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting". Subscription mea manu = "in my own hand."
  • Dançay. Magnifico Domino Danzaeo / Domino Danzaeo = Charles de Dançay (Carolus Danzaeus, d. 1589), French royal ambassador (legatus regis Galliarum) resident at the Danish court, a friend and patron of Tycho; the Opera Omnia index lists him under Leg. Gal. (legatus Galliae) and records his role at Uraniborg's foundation-stone laying. Vedel's letters reached and were copied at Copenhagen, where Tycho read of his intended visit.
  • Mette. Mettam = Mette, Vedel's wife (Mette Hansdatter), greeted "with both her parents." Kept as the Danish given name.
  • Place / calendar. Datae Uraniburgi die 11. Nov. Anno 1585 = "Given at Uraniborg (on Hven), the 11th day of November, 1585" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label; header date as written.
  • The runaway-servant matter. fraudulenta discessio cuiusdam servi = "the fraudulent departure of a certain servant." Tycho writes that this servus had carried off with him "this other [man]" (hoc altero, quem nunc secum abduxit) and had been entangled with a ancilla ("maidservant") who held the storeroom keys; she has been dismissed cum infamia ("in disgrace"). Tycho asks Vedel to omnem move lapidem ("move every stone," = leave no stone unturned, the proverbial omnem movere lapidem) to have the man restored to him (restituatur) — i.e. returned, as an absconding servant under early-modern master-servant obligation.
  • Negation control. The source carries the principal negations non impediverit ("does not hinder"), nulla ipsius facta mentione ("no mention made of his deed"), ne intelligam te minus sincere … agere ("that I do not perceive you to be acting less than sincerely"), and ne labefactetur amicitia ("lest our friendship be undermined"). The doubled litotes minus candide … minus vere ("less candidly … less truthfully") is preserved as understatement, not flattened to "dishonestly/falsely."
  • The quarrel over verses. ob nescio quos versus me insimulas … vehementiae et acerbitatis = Vedel had, in his letter to Dançay, accused Tycho of "vehemence and harshness" on account of some verses or other (Tycho's dismissive nescio quos, "I know not what / some … or other," is kept as a hedge). per tralationem … a suspicione consensus te liberes — tralatio (= translatio) here in its rhetorical/figurative sense of transferring or shifting [the blame] onto Tycho so as to clear himself of "the suspicion of complicity" (consensus) before their aemuli ("rivals"). Rendered "by shifting [it onto me]"; the bracketed object is editorial.
  • Idioms / register. iter tuum non impediverit, vel potius gentis in retractandis pollicitis consuetudo — a barbed aside, "or rather the nation's habit of going back on what it has promised" (gens = the [Danish] people/nation; retractare pollicita = to withdraw promises); kept as written. Iterum terque quaterque vale echoes the Virgilian terque quaterque beati ("thrice and four times"), an intensifying farewell-flourish; rendered literally.
  • Editorial insertions. Bracketed words — "[lines]," "[man]," "[other man]," "[it onto me]," "[our]," "[to it]," "[yet]" — supply grammatically implied objects/subjects with no separate word in the Latin, by this chapter's convention
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (after "mea manu"), restored from Dreyer VII, p. 98 (dropped by the working transcription).
