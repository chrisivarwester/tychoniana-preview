The original

CLARISSIMO DOCTISSIMOQUE VIRO M. BARTHOLOMÆO SCULTETO, GORLICENSI, RERUM MATHEMATICARUM PERITISSIMO, VETERI AMICO PLURIMUM COLENDO.

PER Silesium quendam Studiosum Vratislaviensem (ni fallor) tibi scripsi, mense Maio proxime elapso, amantissime clarissimeque SCULTETE, librumque nostrum secundum, opusculum, quod de recentioribus ætherei Mundi Phænomenis emisimus, unâ transmisi. Nec dubito te eum interea accepisse, perspexisseque diligenter. Rogo itaque, velis mihi prima occasione tuam de ijs, quæ in eo continentur, perscribere sententiam. Nec est, cur te moveat, quod a tuis placitis tantopere in plurimis dissentiam, et fretus libertate Philosophica, quid in his veritati consentaneum sit, ingenue proferam. Scis enim, me nullo tua insectandi aut cavillandi animo id facere, et ut candide interpreteris omnia, amicitiæ nostræ diuturnum vinculum postulat. Ego tibi vicissim liberum de meis relinquo iudicium, ut ingenue proferas, quicquid invicta veritatis Mathematicæ certitudo exigat. Plurimum itaque tuas desidero literas, quibus meis desiderijs in his satisfiat, quas ut mature ante instantem hyemem accipiam, operam des: idque commodissime fiet, si eas miseris vel Rostochium ad D. HENRICUM BRUCÆUM Medicum, vel Lubecam, ad D. PETRUM MEMMIUM, etiam Medicum, aut illuc quoque ad FRANCISC. KNOCKERT, Patritium et Senatorem urbis, vel Nauclerum quendam, ALBERT GRUBE nomine, habitantem in Angelo Lubecensi. Quocunque horum in procurandis literis usus fueris, spero me eas ad civem quendam Hafniensem, in ædibus meis habitantem, DESIDERIUM FULLONEM nomine, delatas, facile et brevi obtenturum. Vale, et rescriptionem matura. Rogo etiam, ut Magnifico Nobilissimoque Viro ANDREÆ DUDITIO, si vobis in propinquo est, meo nomine officiose salutem dicere vel perscribere non intermittas. Optarem vero quam maxime illius de libro nostro, sibi etiam transmisso, iudicium. Quod si per te ab ipso etiam, ante hyemis impedimenta, literis consignatum impetravero, feceris mihi rem longe gratissimam. Iterum felicissime vale.

Datæ Uraniburgi die 17. Augusti Anno 1588.
Tui amantissimus
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned Master Bartholomaeus Scultetus of Görlitz, most skilled in mathematical matters, his old friend much to be honoured.

By way of a certain Silesian student of Breslau (if I am not mistaken) I wrote to you in the month of May just past, most loving and most renowned Scultetus, and along with it I sent our second book — the little work which we have issued concerning the more recent phenomena of the aethereal world. Nor do I doubt that you have meanwhile received it and examined it diligently. I ask you, therefore, that you be willing to write out for me at the first opportunity your judgement of the things contained in it. Nor is there any reason why it should disturb you that I dissent so greatly from your opinions in very many points, and that, relying on philosophical liberty, I set forth frankly what in these matters accords with the truth. For you know that I do this with no intention of attacking or carping at you; and the long-standing bond of our friendship requires that you interpret everything candidly. I, in turn, leave you free judgement concerning my own [views], that you may set forth frankly whatever the unconquered certainty of mathematical truth demands. I greatly desire, then, your letters, by which my longings in these matters may be satisfied; and may you take pains that I receive them in good time before the approaching winter. This will most conveniently be done if you send them either to Rostock, to Master Henricus Brucaeus, physician, or to Lübeck, to Master Petrus Memmius, likewise a physician, or thither too to Franciscus Knockert, patrician and senator of the city, or to a certain shipmaster, Albert Grube by name, dwelling in the [house of the] Angel at Lübeck. By whichever of these you make use of in procuring [the delivery of] the letters, I hope, once they are delivered to a certain citizen of Copenhagen dwelling in my house, Desiderius Fullo by name, easily and soon to obtain them. Farewell, and hasten your reply. I ask too that you not fail, in my name, to give or write out a dutiful greeting to the magnificent and most noble Master Andreas Dudith, if he is near to you. I would wish most of all for his judgement of our book, which has also been sent to him. And if through you I obtain it from him too, set down in a letter before the obstacles of winter, you will have done me a thing by far most welcome. Again, most happily, farewell.

Given at Uraniborg, the 17th day of August, the year 1588.
Most loving toward you,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe writing from Uraniborg on Hven to Bartholomäus Scultetus (Bartholomäus Schultz, 1540–1614), the Görlitz mathematician, cartographer, and astronomer, later burgomaster of that city. Tycho had received instruction from Scultetus in his student years (Thoren 1990, 17); the address-line VETERI AMICO ("old friend") marks the long acquaintance. GORLICENSI = "of Görlitz" (Lat. Gorlicium), in Upper Lusatia — whence the edition title Lusatica.
  • librum nostrum secundum … de recentioribus aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis — Tycho's second book, the De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (Uraniborg, 1588), on the comet of 1577; his "first" being De nova stella (1573). opusculum ("little work") is Tycho's habitual modest diminutive for it. Title-phrase rendered literally rather than supplied with the printed Latin title.
  • libertate Philosophica — "philosophical liberty," the period topos of free inquiry; Tycho frames his disagreement with Scultetus as the licensed candour of philosophers, not personal attack (nullo tua insectandi aut cavillandi animo).
  • invicta veritatis Mathematicae certitudo — "the unconquered certainty of mathematical truth"; invicta kept as "unconquered" (not softened to "unassailable").
  • Forwarding network. The chain of intermediaries for routing the reply: Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (1531–1593), professor of medicine at Rostock and a Tycho correspondent (glossary); Petrus Memmius = Peter Memmius, physician at Lübeck; Franciscus Knockert = Franz Knockert, patrician and city senator of Lübeck; Albert Grube, a shipmaster (Nauclerus) lodging in Angelo Lubecensi — "at the Angel in Lübeck," i.e. a house/inn at the sign of the Angel, rendered "in the [house of the] Angel." Vratislaviensem = "of Breslau" (Wrocław), the Silesian student who had carried the May letter.
  • Desiderius Fullo — Tycho's Copenhagen go-between, civem … Hafniensem, in aedibus meis habitantem ("a citizen of Copenhagen dwelling in my house"). The Latinised name (Desiderius = Desiderius/Désiré; Fullo lit. "fuller") masks a vernacular original not identified here; left as the Latin form and flagged.
  • Magnifico Nobilissimoque Viro Andreae Duditio — Andreas Dudith (András Dudith / Andreas Dudithius, 1533–1589), the Hungarian humanist, former bishop and Imperial diplomat, then living in Breslau (Wrocław) — hence Tycho's si vobis in propinquo est ("if he is near to you," Görlitz and Breslau both being in the region). Tycho had sent Dudith the De mundi and solicits his iudicium through Scultetus.
  • Greek / symbols. None in this letter.
  • Dateline. Datae Uraniburgi die 17. Augusti Anno 1588 = "Given at Uraniborg, 17 August 1588" (O.S.; Denmark held the Julian calendar). The header date matches the dateline; the edition label ("1588. 17. august. Uraniborg") agrees — no discrepancy.
  • Edition of record. Singularia historico-literaria Lusatica (the Lusatian historical-literary miscellany in which the letter was printed); no manuscript witness is recorded in the edition.
