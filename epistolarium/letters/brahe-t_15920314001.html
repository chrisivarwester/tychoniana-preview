The original

TYGE BRAHE FICK BREFF NOGETT SMERLLE ANRØRENDIS VT SEQUITUR.

Sophia.

WOR gunst thilfornn. Wider, att høgborne første och herre, her Wllrich, herre och første thill Meckelborg, Vor kierre herre fader, haffuer weed hanns schriffuellse aff Oß werritt begierrenndis, att Wi naadigst wille Hanns f: Naade forschaffe enn prøffue aff thennd jord, som kallis smerle, som glasbrenndere allthiid leyer, och nu nyligenn paa Huenn schulle verre funndenn, thi bede Wi ether och begierre, attj Oß wille vnnde och schiche enn ottinng fuld aff for:ne smerlle, huilched Wi sidenn Hanns Kierlighet wille thillsennde. Sammeledis giffue Wi ether och naadigst att wide, att Wi iche synnderligtt mere haffue aff thennd brennd antimonium, som i Oß senniste gaffue och thillschichede, bedenndis ether ther forre och begierre, attj Oß enndnu nogett aff for:ne brenntt antimonium ville giffue, huorudinnden i giør Oß enn synderlig thienniste och vellgefallenn.

Befallendis etc.
Actum Frederichsborg thennd 14. martij a:o 92.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tyge Brahe received a letter concerning some smerle, as follows:

Sophie.

Our favour beforehand. Know that the high-born prince and lord, Lord Ulrich, lord and prince of Mecklenburg, our dear lord father, has by his letter requested of us that we would graciously procure for His Princely Grace a sample of that earth which is called smerle [a clay], which glassmakers always use, and which is now lately said to have been found on Hven. We therefore ask and desire of you that you would grant and send to us a ottingr full [a one-eighth measure] of the aforesaid smerle, which we will then send on to His Lordship. Likewise we also graciously give you to know that we have nothing much left of that burnt antimony which you last gave and sent to us, asking and desiring of you therefore that you would give us yet some [more] of the aforesaid burnt antimony, wherein you do us a singular service and pleasure.

Commending [you] etc.
Done at Frederiksborg, the 14th of March, year [15]92.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Sophie of Mecklenburg (1557–1631), queen of Denmark, consort of Frederik II (d. 1588) and by 1592 queen dowager during the minority of her son Christian IV. She shared Tycho's interest in chemistry/alchemy and natural philosophy and visited Uraniborg; here she addresses him as a supplier of materials. The plain signature Sophia (without the Wij … etc. formula of a reigning king's letter) is the dowager-queen register.
  • Document type. A chancery-register entry (Dronning Sophies tegnelser). The Latin rubric "…NOGETT SMERLLE ANRØRENDIS ut sequitur" ("concerning some smerle, as follows") heads the Danish body — a code-switch preserved by the edition's convention
  • Person named. her Wllrich, herre och første thill Meckelborg, Vor kierre herre fader = Ulrich III, Duke of Mecklenburg-Güstrow (1527–1603), Sophie's father; "His Princely Grace" (Hanns f: Naade = Hans fyrstelige Naade) and "His Lordship" (Hanns Kierlighet = Hans Kærlighed, the chancery courtesy-title "His Affection/Lordship") both refer to him.
  • Place. Frederichsborg = Frederiksborg Castle (Hillerød, Zealand), a royal residence.
  • smerle — kept untranslated, glossed "[a clay]." The text defines it as thennd jord, som kallis smerle, som glasbrenndere allthiid leyer = "the earth called smerle, which glassmakers always use." This is the clay/earth used by glassmakers (Danish smærgel/smerle; the same Germanic root as English smear, and cognate with the abrasive Schmergel/emery family). The note that it had "now lately been found on Hven" ties the request to Tycho's own island. Not modernised, since the precise mineral is not certain from the word alone.
  • glasbrenndere = "glass-burners," i.e. glassmakers / glass-blowers (literally those who fire/melt glass).
  • enn ottinng fuld — "a ottingr full," i.e. an ottingr-measure full. The otting (literally "an eighth") was an Old Danish measure of capacity (one-eighth of a larger unit, e.g. of a tønde/barrel); rendered "a one-eighth measure" with the term flagged, as the exact volume intended is not specified in the letter.
  • brennd / brenntt antimonium = "burnt antimony" (antimonium calcinatum / crocus metallorum), the calcined antimony preparation of period chemistry/medicine and metallurgy. The Latin technical noun antimonium is embedded in the Danish (a code-switch typical of the chancery's chemical vocabulary), and Sophie reports she has nearly used up the previous batch Tycho supplied — evidence of an ongoing material exchange between the queen and Uraniborg's laboratory.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1592). WOR gunst thilfornn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula, the edition's convention); Wider = Vider ("Know ye"); haffuer … begierrenndis werritt = har … begærende været ("has been requesting"); attj = at I ("that you"); vnnde = unde ("grant"); schiche = skikke ("send, dispatch"); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); huilched = hvilket ("which"); sidenn = siden ("then, afterwards"); Sammeledis = Sammeledes ("likewise"); iche synnderligtt mere = ikke synderligt mere ("not much more / nothing much left"); senniste = seneste ("last, most recently"); huorudinnden = hvorudinden ("wherein"); synderlig thienniste och vellgefallenn = synderlig tjeneste og velbehag ("singular service and pleasure"); Befallendis = Befalendes ("commending [you to God]," the abbreviated closing formula).
  • Negation count. The body contains a single negation — iche synnderligtt mere ("not much more"), reporting that the queen's stock of burnt antimony is nearly exhausted. Both clauses of the letter are positive requests; no command or condition is reversed.
  • Calendar / place. Actum Frederichsborg thennd 14. martij a:o 92 = "Done at Frederiksborg, the 14th of March, [15]92" (O.S.); Actum "Done/Given" kept by the edition's convention The dateline (14 March 1592) agrees with the catalogue label; no discrepancy.
