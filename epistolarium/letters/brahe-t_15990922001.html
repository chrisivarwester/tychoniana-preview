The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTIANUM LONGOMONTANUM.

S.

FILIUS TYCHO, quem nunc cum MULÆO in Daniam pro adferendis residuis instrumentis (sic volente Cæsare) mitto ..... te socium tertium ijs adiungere, atque in expediendis pro me rebus adiutor esse poteris, eos in Daniam, inque insulam isthic desertam comitari et cum ijs, Deo volente, revertentibus me eodem curru accedere, nam et sic rem gratam præstiteris, utpote cum sciam te in instrumentis maioribus componendis et conservandis, atque in ijs, quæ huc mechanice efficienda sunt, solertem esse, eaque rite præordinare posse, tum quoque urgere, ut sine temporis iactura omnia mature et recte conficiantur. Scripsit iussu Cæsaris magnif. Dominus BARVICTIUS supremus Imperij Secretarius, Cæsaris intimus, imo apud ipsum factotum, uti loquuntur, ad Dominum HENRICUM RAMELIUM, ut Astronomicarum rerum, quascunque ibi residuas habuero, evectionem promoveat, et ego pariter ad aulæ Magistrum hac de re literas dedi etc. .........

IOHANNEM MULLERUM Mathematicum Electoris Brandeburgici brevi huc venturum expecto, nam cum sponte mihi suam operam, Wittebergam ante meum discessum isthinc transiens, obtulerit, si modo cum Principis sui bona venia id fieri possit, impetravi, ut Cæsarea Maiestas hoc nomine ad Electorem Brandeburgicum scriberet (sic enim inter MULLERUM et me convenit) quarum etiam literarum exemplar inter alia apud filium videbis. Est is, præter ingenium disciplinarum Mathematicarum capax, in Mechanicis huc facientibus non infeliciter versatus. Aderit etiam forte mihi Landgravianus olim Mathematicus CHRISTOPHORUS ROTHMANNUS, quem obijsse existimavimus, sed ex nuperis eius ad ROLLENHAGIUM literis ipsius manum cognovi, et adhuc in vivis esse perlubenter intellexi, ut sic dupliciter mentita sit sine omni pudore Ursina ista et Dithmarsica Bestia in suo famoso scripto, cum de me, quod ob facinus aliquod non leve, absque omni dubio perpetratum, e Dania eruptionem fecerim, vanissimo et impudentissimo ore fingat, sic illum nescio quibus morbis (pudet enim referre) infectum et confectum, qui tamen adhuc vivit et mediocriter bene habet, futiliter blaterat. Scripsi hisce diebus eidem ROTHMANNO, atque eum ad me, si per valetudinem liceret, invitavi. Quin et M. DAVID FABRICIUS Œst-Frisius, qui apprime his disciplinis delectatur, quem etiam tu, cum Wandesburgi apud me esset, novisti, tam ut mihi a privatis concionibus sit, quam ut se collaboratorem, prout a me instructus fuit, horum studiorum præbeat, ut existimo, adveniet. Duos quoque studiosos in triangulis et calculo versatos, sic per IOSTELIUM nostrum expetentes, Witteberga acciri curo, habiturus postmodum plures, prout opus fuerit. Accedent forte ultro et alij. Nam scholam Astronomicam, multorumque in eadem operas, favente cœlestium Opifice, Cæsare clementissime subsidia largiente, de novo erigere constitui; cuius ususfructus ad posteritatem omnem, uti spero, perseverabit, in Dei fabricæ cœlestis authoris gloriam, et Cæsaris nunquam intermorituram memoriam et laudabilem famam; atque ne nos etiam frustra extitisse talentumque divinitus concessum abscondidisse ac tempus preciosissimum inutiliter, ut nimis multi, quod dolendum est, faciunt, contrivisse videamur. Exoptarem primo quoque tempore quaterniones pauculos, qui in Tomo Progymnasmatum nostrorum primo desiderantur, labore typographico compleri, siquidem ea, quæ incuria Hamburgensis typographi perdidit, usui, scis, esse nequeunt, nec Wittebergæ ea recudere ob causam tibi notam placuerit. Inveniemus facile rationem, ubi simul fuerimus, qua obstaculis, quoad nunc fieri potest, succurratur, ut liber is brevi in lucem prodire possit. Cogita enim quanto non solum meo damno tot exemplaria tam diu mecum reservo, sed et quam aliorum desiderio minus, dum publicatio differtur, satisfacio. Poterit etiam mox subsequi prima pars secundi Tomi, quæ de Cometa Anni 77 nunc cum Apologis huc pertinentibus luculenter agit, donec altera pars reliquorum Cometarum a nobis observatorum descriptiones complectens (quarum fundamenta in promptu sunt) subiungatur. Eclipsis Solis in Iulio huius Anni circa ortum visa meis instrumentis, Pragæ quoque in turricula veteris Collegij observata est per IOHANNEM meum Hamburgensem, adstantibus alijs, deprehensusque est Sol, cum elevaretur 2 gradibus, deficientibus saltem totidem minutis, quasi unum digitum de limbo versus Septentrionem inclinato, interventu ☾ amisisse, et postea successive rotunditatem acquisivisse consuetam. Elevatur autem Pragæ polus, ut ipsemet, dum ibi morarer, tam ex Sole quam stellis observavi, partibus quinquaginta m. 4½ , sed hîc Benachiæ est partium 5 m. 16, in tantum sumus hîc Praga septentrionaliores, nonnihil tamen ad ortum versus Silesiam, ita ut Gorlicium duorum saltem dierum itinere hinc removeatur, dispositi, et axis cœlestium revolutionum 6 pene gradibus hîc minus elevata, pauloque rectiorem præbens sphæram, quam in Danico nostro Uraniburgo, ad observationes cœlestes aliquanto magis conducit.

Cæterum de fera ista Dithmarsica nimis effera et bruta, ut aliqua subiungam, licet indigna sit, cuius recordetur, scias, istam ante aliquot septimanas, prout nuper rescivi, Praga se subduxisse, sive male sibi conscia, et quod iustas pœnas per leges formidaret, sive quod aliud sinu suo latenter, more suo, ruminans. Sed investiganda tamen suo tempore per otium atque in ius protrahenda et punienda, quod etiam optimi quique Pragæ suadent. Verum de isto calumniatore et vitiatore impudentissimo hîc satis. Cætera ipsemet apud iudicem experietur. Restat, ut scribendi finem faciam, cum plura non vacet, sum enim scribendis non saltem in Daniam sed et hinc inde ad notos et amicos plurimis literis (cumuli partem residuam filius ostendet) quam occupatissimus, ideoque et hæc festinanti calamo ad te exarata boni consulas atque desiderijs nostris tuoque commodo morem gere. Videbis hîc locum amœnissimum, in edito et saluberrimo aëre situm.

Vale.
Dabantur Venetijs Bohemorum, sic enim arcis huius nomen vernacula gentis lingua interpretatur, ipso die Æquinoctij autumnalis 99.
Tui amantiss.
TYCHO BRAHE, manu propria.

TYCHO BRAHE TO CHRISTIAN LONGOMONTANUS.

Greeting.

My son Tycho, whom I now send with Mule into Denmark to fetch the remaining instruments (the Emperor so wishing) … [lacuna] you can join yourself to them as a third companion, and be a helper in expediting my affairs — accompany them into Denmark, and to the deserted island [Hven] there, and, with them returning (God willing), come to me on the same wagon; for thus too you will do a welcome thing, since I know you are skilful in assembling and preserving the larger instruments, and in the things that are to be made mechanically here, and can duly pre-arrange them, and also urge that everything be done maturely and rightly, without loss of time. By the Emperor's order, the magnificent Lord Barvitius, supreme Secretary of the Empire, the Emperor's intimate — nay, his factotum, as they say — has written to Lord Henrik Ramel to promote the transport of whatever astronomical things I have remaining there; and I likewise have written to the Court-Master about this, etc. ………

I expect Johann Müller, Mathematician of the Elector of Brandenburg, to come hither shortly; for since he of his own accord offered me his labour (passing through Wittenberg before my departure thence), provided it could be done with his Prince's good leave, I obtained that the Imperial Majesty should write on this account to the Elector of Brandenburg (for so it was agreed between Müller and me) — a copy of which letter, among others, you will see with my son. He is, besides a talent capable of the Mathematical disciplines, not unhappily versed in the Mechanics conducing hither. There will perhaps also be with me the former Landgravian Mathematician, Christoph Rothmann — whom we had thought dead; but from his recent letter to Rollenhagen I recognised his hand, and most gladly learned that he is still among the living — so that that Ursine and Dithmarsian Beast lied doubly, without all shame, in his infamous writing: when, with most empty and most shameless mouth, he feigns of me that I made my breaking-forth from Denmark on account of some grave crime, undoubtedly perpetrated; [and] so futilely blathers that Rothmann was infected and consumed by I know not what diseases (for it shames me to report it) — who yet lives and is moderately well. I have written these days to that same Rothmann, and invited him to me, if his health should allow. Moreover M. David Fabricius the East-Frisian, who is exceedingly delighted with these disciplines — whom you too knew, when he was with me at Wandsbek — will come, as I think, both to be my [chaplain] for private sermons, and to show himself a collaborator in these studies, as he was instructed by me. Two students, too, versed in triangles and calculation — requesting it so through our Jöstel — I am having summoned from Wittenberg, to have more afterward as need requires. Perhaps others too will come of their own accord. For I have resolved to erect anew an Astronomical school, and the labours of many in it — the heavenly Maker favouring, the Emperor most clemently bestowing subsidies — whose usufruct will, I hope, endure to all posterity, to the glory of God the author of the heavenly fabric, and to the never-dying memory and praiseworthy fame of the Emperor; and lest we too should seem to have existed in vain, and to have hidden the talent divinely granted, and to have worn away most precious time uselessly, as too many do (which is to be lamented). I could wish, at the first opportunity, that the few quaternions wanting in the first Volume of our Progymnasmata be completed by the printer's labour — since those which the carelessness of the Hamburg printer destroyed cannot, you know, be used, nor would it be advisable to reprint them at Wittenberg, for the reason known to you. We shall easily find a way, when we are together, by which the obstacles may, so far as can now be done, be relieved, that this book may shortly come to light. For consider with how much loss — not only mine — I keep so many copies so long with me, but also how little I satisfy others' desire, while publication is deferred. The first part of the second Volume can also soon follow, which now treats clearly of the Comet of the year [15]77, with the Apologias pertaining hither, until the other part — comprising the descriptions of the remaining Comets observed by us (whose foundations are at hand) — be subjoined.

The Solar eclipse seen in July of this year about sunrise with my instruments was observed also at Prague, in the little turret of the old College, by my Johann the Hamburger, others standing by; and the Sun was found, when it was elevated 2 degrees (lacking at least as many minutes [of arc]), to have lost about one digit from the limb, inclined toward the North, by the intervention of the Moon [☾], and afterward gradually to have regained its accustomed roundness. The pole is elevated at Prague — as I myself, while I tarried there, observed both from the Sun and from the stars — 50° 4½′; but here at Benátky it is 50° 16′ [source corrupt; see note] — by so much are we here more northerly than Prague, yet somewhat to the east, toward Silesia (so that Görlitz is removed from here by a journey of but two days); and the axis of the celestial revolutions, here elevated almost 6 degrees less [than at Uraniborg], and offering a somewhat more upright sphere than at our Danish Uraniborg, conduces somewhat more to celestial observations.

For the rest, concerning that too-savage and brutish Dithmarsian beast — that I may subjoin something, though it be unworthy to be remembered — know that, some weeks ago (as I lately learned), he withdrew himself from Prague — whether ill-conscious to himself, and because he dreaded the just penalties by the laws, or because, in his manner, ruminating secretly something else in his bosom. But it is to be investigated, nonetheless, in time, at leisure, and dragged into court and punished — which all the best men at Prague also advise. But of that most shameless calumniator and corrupter, enough here. The rest he will himself experience before the judge. It remains that I make an end of writing, since there is no leisure for more — for I am most busy writing very many letters, not only into Denmark, but here and there to acquaintances and friends (my son will show the remaining part of the heap); therefore take in good part these too, scratched to you with hurrying pen, and comply with my wishes and your own convenience. You will see here a most pleasant place, set in a high and most healthy air.

Farewell.
Given at the Venice of the Bohemians — for so the name of this castle is interpreted in the people's vernacular tongue [Benátky] — on the very day of the autumnal Equinox, [15]99.
Most loving of you,
Tycho Brahe, in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (Christianus Longomontanus), Tycho's chief assistant — here urged to join the instrument-fetching party to Denmark/Hven. (Cf. brahe-t_15980925001, where he is Tycho's envoy.)
  • Significance — the founding of the Benátky "astronomical school" and the recruitment of the team (a keystone document).
  • The instrument transport from Hven. Tycho's son and Mule go to Denmark to fetch "the remaining instruments" from "the deserted island" (Hven), with imperial backing: Barvitius (the supreme Imperial Secretary, "the Emperor's factotum") has written to Henrik Ramel to promote it; Longomontanus is asked to go as a third, for his skill with the great instruments.
  • The new staff (named recruitment). (1) Johann Müller, mathematician of the Elector of Brandenburg (Joachim Friedrich), secured via an imperial letter to the Elector. (2) Christoph Rothmann — the former Hesse-Kassel mathematician and Tycho's great correspondent — discovered to be alive (his hand recognised in a letter to Rollenhagen), invited; this doubly disproves Ursus, who had libelled both that Tycho "fled Denmark for a crime" and that Rothmann had died of disease. (3) David Fabricius, the East-Frisian pastor-astronomer (whom Longomontanus knew at Wandsbek), to serve as chaplain and collaborator. (4) Two Wittenberg students "versed in triangles and calculation," via Melchior Jöstel. The grand aim: "to erect anew an Astronomical school" with imperial subsidy, "whose usufruct will endure to all posterity."
  • The Progymnasmata printing troubles. Vol. I lacks "a few quaternions" that the Hamburg printer's carelessness destroyed (unusable; reprinting at Wittenberg inadvisable "for the reason known to you"); Tycho frets over the many undistributed copies. Vol. II part 1 (the Comet of 1577, with the Apologias) can soon follow, then the other comets.
  • The July 1599 solar eclipse. Observed at sunrise with Tycho's instruments and at Prague (the old College turret) by "my Johann the Hamburger": the Sun at 2° altitude lost about one digit from its northern limb, then regained roundness.
  • Geography/latitude. Tycho's own determinations: Prague pole-height 50° 4½′, Benátky 50° 16′ — and Benátky's pole "almost 6° lower than Uraniborg" (55°54′), giving a "more upright sphere" better for observation (the same rectior sphaera rationale as the Egypt plan, brahe-t_16000124001). Görlitz c. 2 days east.
  • Ursus's flight. Ursus (Nicolaus Reimers Bär) had fled Prague "some weeks ago," fearing legal penalties — to be pursued at law ("the rest he will experience before the judge"), as Prague's "best men" advise. (Cf. the Ursus thread, brahe-t_16000401001 etc.)
  • Persons & places named. Longomontanus; Tycho Brahe the Younger; Claus Mule; Barvitius; Henrik Ramel; Johann Müller (Brandenburg mathematician — possibly the same as "Johann the Hamburger" who observed the eclipse at Prague; flagged); Christoph Rothmann; Georg Rollenhagen; David Fabricius; Melchior Jöstel; two unnamed Wittenberg students; the Elector of Brandenburg; Ursus. Places: Benátky ("Bohemian Venice"), Prague, Hven, Denmark, Wittenberg, Hamburg, Wandsbek, Görlitz, Silesia, Uraniborg.
  • Notable points. The dateline pun: "Venice of the Bohemians" = Benátky (Czech Benátky = "Venice"). The autumnal-equinox dating ("ipso die Aequinoctij autumnalis 99") = 22 September 1599 [N.S.] (label agrees). The printed source is the older epistolary collection (Epistolae ab anno 1588), not Dreyer's vol. VIII.
  • Textual notes (flagged). A lacuna in the opening sentence (after "the Emperor so wishing"). The Benátky latitude is printed "partium 5 m. 16" — a copy corruption for 50° 16′ (the leading "0" dropped; c. 11′ north of Prague's 50° 4½′, and ≈ 5°38′ < Uraniborg, matching "almost 6 degrees"); read as 50° 16′ and flagged.
