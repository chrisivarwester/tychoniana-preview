The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MELCHIOREM IŒSTELIUM.

S. P.

ERUDITISSIME et Amantissime Domine Magister IŒSTELI, accepi quidem literas tuas cum filio paulo post huius Anni initia huc reverso; verum nihil ad eas hactenus respondi, partim ob penuriam tabellariorum, partim quod sperassem te desiderijs nostris, prout ibidem pollicitus es, brevi satisfacturum: at cum nuper Pragam (uti audio) venerit Dominus Doctor IESSENIUS, communis noster Amicus, et tuas huc miserit, percipio ex ijs te necdum in dispositione circuituum ♀ris et ☿ij calculoque huic congruente satis esse resolutum, utut non contemnendam in ijs locaveris operam. Ne igitur res nimis diu protrahatur, cum dispendio Libri 2di Epistolarum, in quo nonnulla ex ijs conveniente loco, uti scis, inserere animus est, te peramanter rogatum velim, ut saltem ea, quæ in tribus superioribus Planetis explorata habes, iuxta triplices illas Hypotheses mox consignes et dilucide explicata mihi mature mittas. Si unâ adiunxeris quæcunque in ♀ et ☿ varie tentâsti, et quousque in ijs progressus sis, qualesque inveneris remoras, et quid dubij potissimum in ijs sit, rescribam eo promptius, quid faciundum restet, et quomodo illis difficultatibus obviandum; neque operæ pretium censeo, ut ex meis observatis dimensiones circulorum ♀ris et ☿ij motuumque rationes perquiras; id enim non mediocris est negotij, a meipso D. V. elaborandum; sed solummodo ut numeris Coperniceis, quamvis errori obnoxijs, quamproxime conveniant, tum quoque Alphonsinis, secundum diversimodas tamen assumptiones, aplicentur. Omnem itaque move lapidem, mi M. IŒSTELI, ut, quamprimum fieri poterit, id, quicquid erit, obtineam, nec expectationem nostram diutius remoreris quæso. Miror M. ŒSTWALDUM tam diu in unius Anni Ephemeride Luminarium supputandâ hærere; quorsum nobis usui erit, ubi magna pars huius Anni præterierit? Si paratam habet, mittat actutum; mercedem addictam illi solvas, quam per affinem tuum Dresdensem recipies. Concederem illi plura pro me calculanda, non absque suo commodo, modo non ita tardus esset, et certo ad præstitutum tempus rem absolvere vellet. Si uxoratus non esset, vocarem illum huc, ut apud me calculo vacaret, constituto illi annuo salario. Cumque nullos habeat liberos, posset, si ei sic luberet, æque commode Pragæ atque Wittebergæ degere, et ibi etiam ex privatis prælectionibus victum et amyctum acquirere, ubi meo quoque subsidio non destituetur, et efficiam, ut isthic in veteri collegio commodum habeat locum. Quæ illi ex me proponere poteris, atque ipsius expiscari sententiam, mihi per primam occasionem indicandam. Ubi intellexero hanc conditionem non displicere, providebo ei de viatico necessario. Tu vero, quantum in te est, ipsum adhorteris, ut obsecundet.

Literas adiunctas Domino Doctori WOLFGANGO FRANTZIO trade; et Poëtam TAUBMANNUM diligenter ex me saluta. Scripsit is semel atque iterum mihi, se tale carmen in mei gratiam denuo concinnaturum, ut experiar eum non esse raucâ voce præpeditum. At, quantum video, non saltem rauxedine laborat, sed et plane mutus est; nisi forte hisce temporibus occupatur in conscribendo carmine de Sanctis Angelis, qui alias omnis sorditiei et avaritiæ expertes sunt, quo eos Altari adstantes et donaria pecuniarum numerantes atque consignantes, vel invitos et talia fastidientes introducat, sicque hyperbolice poëtiset et quibusdam placentia dicat.

Quæ in ultima Eclipsi Solis observasti, ex te scire cupio; brevi percipies alias rationes in motu ☾æ et Luminarium diametris, quibus omnia accuratius quam antea apparentijs consentiant.

Vale.
Dabantur properanter ex Arce CæsarisBenatica Die 27 Martij Anni 1600 stylo novo.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO MELCHIOR JÖSTEL.

Heartiest greeting.

Most learned and most loving Lord Master Jöstel, I did indeed receive your letter, with my son returned hither a little after the beginning of this year; but I have hitherto replied nothing to it, partly for the scarcity of couriers, partly because I had hoped you would soon satisfy my wishes, as you promised therein. But since lately (as I hear) Lord Doctor Jessenius, our common friend, has come to Prague and sent your [letters] hither, I perceive from them that you are not yet sufficiently resolved on the disposition of the orbits of Venus and Mercury, and the calculation congruent with it — although you have placed no contemptible labour on them. Lest, therefore, the matter be drawn out too long, to the detriment of the second Book of the Letters (into which it is my mind to insert some of these things in a fitting place, as you know), I should lovingly ask you that you would at least soon set down what you have explored in the three superior Planets, according to those triple Hypotheses, and send it me in good time, clearly explained. If you adjoin therewith whatever you have variously attempted in Venus and Mercury — how far you have progressed, what hindrances you have met, and what doubt especially is in them — I will write back the more promptly what remains to be done, and how those difficulties are to be met. Nor do I think it worth your labour to investigate, from my observations, the dimensions of the orbits of Venus and Mercury and the ratios of their motions — for that is no small task, to be worked out by myself, God willing — but only [your task is] that they be made to agree as closely as possible with the Copernican numbers (though liable to error), and likewise be applied to the Alphonsine [numbers], according, however, to various assumptions. Move every stone, therefore, my Master Jöstel, that I may obtain it — whatever it be — as soon as can be, and do not, I pray, delay our expectation any longer. I wonder that Master Oswald sticks so long at computing the Ephemeris of the Luminaries for a single year; to what use will it be to us, when a great part of this year will have passed? If he has it ready, let him send it at once; pay him the assigned fee, which you will receive through your Dresden kinsman. I would grant him more to calculate for me — not without his own profit — if only he were not so slow, and would surely finish the work at the set time. Were he not married, I would call him hither, to be free for calculation with me, an annual salary being settled on him; and since he has no children, he could, if it so pleased him, live as conveniently at Prague as at Wittenberg, and there too earn food and clothing from private lectures, where he will also not lack my support, and I will see that he have a convenient place in the old college there. These things you can propose to him from me, and fish out his opinion, to be told me at the first opportunity. When I understand that this offer does not displease, I will provide him the necessary travel-money. But do you, as far as in you lies, urge him to comply.

Hand the enclosed letter to Lord Doctor Wolfgang Franz; and diligently greet the Poet Taubmann on my behalf. He wrote to me once and again that he would compose another such poem in my honour, that I might find he is not hindered by a hoarse voice. But, as far as I see, he not only suffers from hoarseness, but is plainly mute — unless perhaps he is at these times occupied in writing a poem on the Holy Angels (who are otherwise free of all sordidness and avarice), in which he might bring them in, standing at the Altar and counting and recording the money-offerings, even unwilling and disdaining such things, and so poetize hyperbolically and say things pleasing to certain people.

What you observed in the last Solar Eclipse, I desire to know from you; soon you will perceive other methods in the motion of the Moon and the diameters of the Luminaries, by which everything agrees with the appearances more accurately than before.

Farewell.
Given in haste from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 27th day of March, the year 1600, New Style.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Melchior Jöstel (1559–1611), Wittenberg professor of mathematics and medicine (cf. brahe-t_16001008001, brahe-t_16001009001), here employed as Tycho's computational collaborator on the planetary theory. Same date as the Wolfgang-Franz letter (brahe-t_16000327002): both belong to Tycho's late-March 1600 correspondence with the Wittenberg circle, routed through Jessenius.
  • Significance — the planetary theory for the second Epistolae book (technical content; this chapter's convention). Tycho presses Jöstel for results he means to insert into the second Book of the Astronomical Letters:
  • The three superior planets (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars) "according to those triple Hypotheses" — to be set down and clearly explained;
  • Venus and Mercury: Jöstel is "not yet resolved on the disposition of [their] orbits and the congruent calculation"; Tycho wants his attempts, progress, hindrances and doubts.
  • Division of labour: Jöstel is not to derive the orbital dimensions and motion-ratios from Tycho's observations ("no small task, to be worked out by myself"), but only to make the parameters agree as closely as possible with the Copernican numbers ("though liable to error") and apply them to the Alphonsine tables "according to various assumptions." A clear window onto how Tycho farmed out planetary computation while reserving the observational reduction to himself. Omnem move lapidem — "leave no stone unturned."
  • Master Oswald — the ephemeris-computer (resolves a prior open item). M. Oeswaldus = Master Oswald, the Wittenberg calculator (the M. Oswaldus flagged in brahe-t_16001008001): Tycho chafes that he "sticks so long at computing the Ephemeris of the Luminaries [Sun and Moon] for a single year" (useless if late), to be paid via Jöstel's Dresden kinsman. Notably, Tycho offers to bring Oswald to Prague on an annual salary to compute for him — "were he not married … since he has no children … as conveniently at Prague as at Wittenberg," with private lectures, Tycho's support, and "a place in the old college." A vivid glimpse of Tycho recruiting computational staff for the imperial establishment.
  • Wolfgang Franz & the Taubmann satire. Tycho encloses a letter for Wolfgang Franz (Doctor; cf. brahe-t_16000327002, brahe-t_16001008001 — faculty still unspecified here, the theologian-vs-jurist question still open) and greets the poet Friedrich Taubmann, who had promised fresh verses "in my honour." Tycho's barbed joke: Taubmann is not merely "hoarse" but "plainly mute" (i.e. has sent nothing) — unless he is busy with "a poem on the Holy Angels … counting and recording the money-offerings at the Altar … and so poetizing hyperbolically and saying things pleasing to certain people" — a sardonic dig at venal flattery-verse.
  • Observational items. Tycho asks for Jöstel's data on the last Solar Eclipse, and previews "other methods in the motion of the Moon and the diameters of the Luminaries" giving better agreement with the appearances — the lunar-theory refinement that recurs across the literature consulted (cf. brahe-t_15981231001, brahe-t_16000420002).
  • Glyphs (kept;). ♀ = Venus, ☿ = Mercury, ☾ = Moon (names in English; glyphs retained in the source block).
  • Persons named. Melchior Jöstel; Jessenius; Master Oswald; Wolfgang Franz; Friedrich Taubmann; Tycho's son (Tycho the Younger); Copernicus and the Alphonsine tables (as computational standards).
  • Calendar. Dateline die 27 Martij Anni 1600 stylo novo; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
