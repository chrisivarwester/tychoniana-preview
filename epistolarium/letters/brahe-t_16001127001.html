The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

LITERAS tuas, Vir Nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile Princeps, quas quidem die 14 huius mensis Pragæ ad me dedisti, ex Ingolstadio ad me transmissas, hac ipsa horâ probe accepi. D. CHRISTOPHORUS VARSEVICIUS, uti optassem, ad me non venit. M. IOAN. KEPLERUM male valere doleo, et æque contristor, conditione commodâ eum iam carere. Si apud Cæsaream Maiestatem, quod abominor, ei non sit permanendum, ipse quoque dispiciam, anne forte alibi res suas instituere queat.

Tabulas BLANCHINI, PRUGNERI et BEURBACHIJ, quas petis, curabo, si modo extent, ut habeas. IOANNIS ANGELI Astrolabium et Planum cum IACOBO ANGELO de Cometis ob defectum tabellariorum mitto hisce iunctum, teque quam fœlicissime florere ex animo opto.

Datæ Monachij die 27 Nov. A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

Your letter, most noble man and easily prince of all mathematicians — which indeed you sent me at Prague on the 14th of this month, [and which was] transmitted to me from Ingolstadt — I have duly received this very hour. Master Christoph Warszewicki did not come to me, as I would have wished. I grieve that Master Johann Kepler is in poor health, and I am equally saddened that he now lacks a comfortable position. If he is not to remain with the Imperial Majesty — a thing which I abhor [to think] — I too will look about whether perhaps he may be able to set up his affairs elsewhere.

The Tables of Blanchinus, Prugner, and Peurbach, which you seek, I will see to it — if only they are to be had — that you have them. The Astrolabium and Planum of Johann Angelus, together with Jacobus Angelus On Comets, I send joined herewith, owing to a lack of couriers; and I wish from my heart that you flourish most happily.

Given at Munich, the 27th day of November, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (see the Munich series).
  • Kepler. A valuable external witness to Kepler's precarious situation in late 1600: newly arrived in Tycho's circle, male valere ("in poor health") and conditione commoda … carere (lacking a secure post). Herwart — Kepler's own correspondent — finds it detestable (quod abominor) that Kepler might not be kept on at the Imperial court, and offers to help place him elsewhere. (Kepler would secure the imperial mathematicianship only after Tycho's death in October 1601.)
  • Christophorus Varsevicius = Krzysztof Warszewicki (1543–1603), Polish diplomat and writer, who had been expected to call on Herwart.
  • Ingolstadium = Ingolstadt (the routing point of Tycho's letter); Monachium = Munich.
  • Books. Tabulae Blanchini, Prugneri et Beurbachij = the tables of Giovanni Bianchini, Nicolaus Prugner, and Georg Peurbach (the same set requested in brahe-t_16000923001). Ioannis Angeli Astrolabium et Planum = the Astrolabium (and Planum — the planispheric/astrolabe treatise) of Johann Engel (Johannes Angelus) (c. 1463–1512); Iacobo Angelo de Cometis = a comet-tract under the name Jacobus Angelus (identification flagged: the "Angelus" comet-piece, not securely distinguished here from the Johann Engel material).
  • Calendar. Munich (Catholic Bavaria) = New Style; die 27 Nov. 1600. Header agrees with the label. (Tycho's incoming letter is dated "14th of this month" at Prague — also N.S.)
