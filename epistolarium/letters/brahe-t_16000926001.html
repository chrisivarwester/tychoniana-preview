The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD DECANUM CAPITULI MAGDEBURGENSIS].

REUERENDISSIME et nobilissime domine decane. Do:ie honorandissime, advenerunt res meæ, quæ Magdeburgi repositæ erant, hisce præteritis diebus Leutmeritzium quousque Albis versus Bohemiam est nauigabilis. Quod autem in gratiam sac:æ cæs:æ maiestatis, domini nostri clementissimi, Dominatio Vestra vna cum cæteris reuerendissimi capituli Magdeburgensis collegis eas tam prompte et liberaliter proprijs sumtibus huc procurare non degrauati estis, id ipsius maiestas clementissimo animo acceptissimum habuit, prout ab ipsius maiestate, cui hoc vestrum obsequentissimum officium ante paucos dies, cum in horto prope arcem illi subiectissime adessem, commendabam, non saltem ipsemet cognoui sed et heri à magf:co d:no Baruitio, supremo secretario et ipsius ma:ti a consilijs intimo, percepi, qui etiam hac de re se ad vestri reuerendissimi capituli vicecancellarium Chilianum Stösserum scripsisse mihi insinuauit. Ego vero pro mea persona hanc vestram benignissimam voluntatem, quod meis desiderijs tam libere et libenter annueritis, et supellectilem, qua diu non sine incommodo destitutus sum, ad me direxeritis, gratias vestris impensis et habeo et debeo immortales; cumque ad compensandum imparem me agnoscam, id saltem polliceri possum me perpetuo grata mente hoc singulare reuerendissimarum Dn:m Vestrarum in me collatum beneficium recoliturum, et apud optimos quosque (quod et alias facere soleo) deprædicaturum, imo per oportunitatem id ipsum publice testaturum. Et si quid unquam fuerit in quo mea opella Vestris reuerendissimis Dom:bus, quod acceptum fuerit, vicissim præstare possit, re ipsa, quantum in me est, ostensurus sum, me huius singularis beneuolentiæ haud immemorem esse. Quod omnibus et singulis reuerendissimi capituli vestri membris officiosissime significari velim. Devs opt. max. Vestram reuerendissimam Dominationem vna cum honorandissimis cæteris capitularibus quam diutissime florentem et incolumem conseruet.

Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum die 26. septembris stylo nouo anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[Tycho Brahe to the Dean of the Chapter of Magdeburg.]

Most reverend and most noble Lord Dean, most honoured Sir: my belongings, which had been stored at Magdeburg, arrived in these last few days at Leitmeritz, the point to which the Elbe is navigable toward Bohemia. That Your Lordship, together with the other colleagues of the most reverend chapter of Magdeburg, did not find it a burden to procure and forward them hither so promptly and liberally, at your own expense, for the favour of His Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord — this His Majesty received with most gracious mind as most welcome; as I learned not only from His Majesty himself (to whom I commended this most obliging service of yours a few days ago, when I attended him most humbly in the garden near the castle), but also yesterday from the Magnificent Lord Barvitius, supreme secretary and privy councillor to His Majesty, who intimated to me that he had himself written about this matter to the vice-chancellor of your most reverend chapter, Kilian Stösser. For my own part, indeed, for this your most kindly disposition — that you assented so freely and willingly to my wishes, and directed to me the furnishings of which I have long, and not without inconvenience, been deprived — I both render and owe you undying thanks for your outlay; and since I acknowledge myself unequal to repaying it, this at least I can promise: that I shall ever, with grateful mind, cherish this singular benefit conferred upon me by Your most reverend Lordships, and shall proclaim it before all the best men (as I am wont to do on other occasions), nay shall, when occasion offers, publicly attest the very same. And if ever there be anything in which my poor service may in turn render Your most reverend Lordships something that shall be acceptable, I shall show by the deed itself, so far as in me lies, that I am by no means unmindful of this singular goodwill — which I would wish to be most dutifully signified to each and every member of your most reverend chapter. May God, best and greatest, preserve Your most reverend Lordship, together with the rest of the most honoured chapter-members, flourishing and unharmed for the longest time.

Given at Prague in Bohemia, the 26th day of September, New Style, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, now at Prague (he had moved from Benátky to the city in the late summer of 1600). The letter concerns the long-distance transport of his household goods — part of the effects shipped out of Denmark and across Germany after his 1597 departure from Hven.
  • Recipient. The Dean of the Cathedral Chapter of Magdeburg (decanus capituli Magdeburgensis), addressed by office, not by name; the goods had been in storage (repositae) at Magdeburg, a natural staging-point on the river route, and the chapter forwarded them downstream at imperial request.
  • Persons named.
  • Magnificus Dominus Barvitius = Johann Anton Barvitius (Barwitz) (d. 1620), Latin secretary and privy councillor (a consilijs intimo) to Rudolf II — one of Tycho's principal channels to the Emperor at Prague (he recurs across the late correspondence as Coraduz/Barvitius).
  • Chilianus Stösserus = Kilian Stösser, vice-chancellor of the Magdeburg chapter, Barvitius's correspondent in the matter.
  • Sacra Caesarea Maiestas, dominus noster clementissimus = Emperor Rudolf II.
  • Places. Leutmeritzium = Leitmeritz / Litoměřice on the Elbe (Albis), here named as the upstream limit of navigation toward Bohemia. Praga Bohemorum = Prague.
  • Witness note. The Basel manuscript is catalogued as a Kladde — a draft / retained office copy, not the dispatched original; hence the bracketed editorial heading [Tycho Brahe ad decanum capituli Magdeburgensis] supplies the address the fair copy would have borne.
  • Register & formulae. A formal letter of thanks in high curial register: cascading superlatives (reverendissime, honorandissime, clementissimo animo, immortales), the humility topos (ad compensandum imparem me agnoscam), and the closing benediction Deus optimus maximus … quam diutissime florentem et incolumem conservet. Dabantur (epistolary "they were given," i.e. dispatched) rendered "Given."
  • Calendar. Dateline die 26. Septembris stylo novo — explicitly New Style (Gregorian), as expected of Tycho's Prague correspondence; reproduced, not converted.
