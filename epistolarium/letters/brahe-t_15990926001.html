The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD DANIELEM CRAMERUM.

S.

RESCRIPSI tibi, clarissime et eruditissime Domine Doctor CRAMERE, paulo antequam Witeberga discederem, quas te accepisse reor. De meis autem rebus, postquam inde abiens in Bohemiam ex clementissimo mandato et evocatione augustissimi Imperatoris me contulerim, quia nimis longum foret hîc sigillatim omnia enarrare, iussi describi exemplum nuperarum ad OLIGERUM ROSÆKRANTZIUM nostrum a me datarum, quae historiolam quandam eorum, quae mihi hîc evenerunt, continent, cuius te facio participem. Inde per circumstantias omnia hauries; cum enim tam arcta sit inter OLIGERUM et te amicitia, is, quod descriptum suarum literarum tibi communicem (secretum tamen habendum, nec nisi secretis amicis ostendendum) minime aegre laturus est. Quare de meis negotijs nihil hîc addo, solummodo Deo immortali gratias ago. Agasque tu et omnes pij probique mecum, quod omnia bene et ex voto successerint.

Caeterum est aliud te non minima ex parte concernens, quod te non caelandum duxi. Cum Witebergae essem, accesserat eo Danus quidam studiosus mihi familiariter notus, BARTOLDUS IVARI MULÆUS dictus, qui cum Barone quodam Svetico STENONE BIELCHE meo consanguineo accesserat, cumque per aliquot dies ibi mecum conversarentur, accidit, ut STENO ille DUNCANI Scoti mentionem faceret, ambo enim illi Helmstadij studiorum caussa, unde etiam venerant, aliquamdiu fuere. Ego mox subijciens plagium eius in me eidem Baroni, praesente BARTHOLDO illo, aperui, cumque miraretur et quasi subhaesitaret, produxi tuas litteras ad OLIGERUM ROSÆKRANTZIUM prolixe ea de re scriptas ijsque legendas dedi. Nescio vero quî factum sit, quod, dum in alterum diem eas secum servaret Danicus ille studiosus, sibiipsi exemplum inde tacite exaravit, quod, cum Helmstadium venisset, Scotulo isti monstravit. Quomodo autem is acceperit, ex ipsius BARTOLDI literis, quas ante paucos dies nactus sum, verbotenus referam. »Deinde quoad Doctoris CRAMERI literas, eas DUNCANO exhibui, reticens tamen unde perceperim. Ibi homo coepit impietatem miselli asini (sic enim criminabatur) adeo culpare, ut eundem ingratissimum erga praeceptorem cuculum vocitaret, simulque se Magnif. tuae excusans, meque ad ordinem lectionum, quem hisce adiunxi, remittens, ubi honestissimam vestri mentionem fecisse gloriabatur«. Haec ille. Quae vero insinuat de ordine praelectionum impresso unâque transmisso, e quibus isthic verbis DUNCANUS se purgare praesumit, ea sub titulo Philosophicarum praelectionum sic habent: »DUNCANUS LIDDELIUS Scotus D. publice proponit doctrinam sinuum et triangulorum, qua absoluta, theorias Planetarum, secundum hypothesin PTOLEMÆI et COPERNICI et illam mundani systematis hypothesin, quam describit TYCHO BRAHE lib. 2 de aethereis Phaenomenis.« Ista iste, egregia sane et honestissima de me mentio, imo vulpeculae potius dolus; cum enim sibi conscius sit, se meas hypotheses sibi apud alios clam venditasse, et sic plagium commisisse, cum publice non audeat idem facere, neque meas esse fateri vult, ne contradicat ijs, quae prius commentus est, ideoque generaliter et astute loquendo, dicit saltem eas hypotheses, quas ego libro illo secundo describo; potest enim aliquis etiam ab alio inventa describere, nec ob id sua esse. Eadem vafritie usus est in thematibus quibusdam anno 95 Helmstadij editis, ubi inter Parerga Mathematica (sic enim vocat et recte, nam vere Parerga tractat, et de re inperspecta nugatur) propositione secunda sic habet: »Cum tres discrepantes hypotheses sint, quibus apparentiae coelestes solvuntur et explicantur, quarum una est PTOLEMÆI, altera COPERNICI, tertia est illa, cuius meminit TYCHO BRAHE lib. de Cometa anni 77, dubitatur, an aliqua ex his omnibus diversa dari possit, deinde quaenam harum apparentijs magis congruat.« Vide quam sibi similis et hîc et istic, idque ob eandem, quam dixi, caussam; o! astutule et nasutule Scotule. At non satis recordatur, uti oportebat mendacem memorem esse. In intimatione, quam Decanus Facultatis Medicae in Academia Iulia FRANCISCUS PARCOVIUS, sub quo LIDDELIUS in Doctorem promovit, ... haec verba, se conscio et forte etiam praescribente, inserta esse, publiceque edita et affixa, quae postquam LIDDELIJ mentionem, utinam tam veram quam pomposam, fecisset, sic proloquitur: »Quod enim ad Mathesin attinet, non solum in veterum hypothesi bene versatus est, sed et recentiorem laboriosam et operosam COPERNICI hypothesin optime novit«, in qua quicquid consecutus sum, ipsi debere me ingenue fateor. Quin et aliam novam hypothesin, quam publice et privatim docuit, licet alterius nomine usurpetur, adinvenit, cuius rei multi eius discipuli clarissimum ei dant testimonium. Sed addo descriptum totius intimationis; misissem impressum exemplar, sed cum unicum saltem habeam, eo carere nequeo. Annon ibi satis aperte proditur DUNCANUM mea inventa sibi arrogare? Neque enim excusare se potest, quod se inscio haec a suo promotore ascripta sint; vel si sic, cur non inficiatus est atque se invito factum protestatus? cur se sic alienis plumis ornari cornicula ista passa est? Sed dispiciemus suo tempore Deo volente de his latius. Interea huius pusilli Scoti arrogantiam et insolentiam ex alto despiciemus. Si in viros cordatos et eruditos atque ipso longe meliores scurriliter debacchari et mea de caussa eos misellos asinos ingratissimosque cuculos vocitare, quod veritatem quaesiti non subticeant, perrexerit, efficiam ut publice plagij accusatus et convictus secundum ius luat, id est infamis fiat, nec amplius irritet crabrones. Tu interim pro te, quae vera et iusta nosti, meditare atque consigna, et si lubuerit, in uno vel altero quaternione in publicum ede, tui meique defensionis gratia, ut habeat iste biliosus Scotulus, quod ipsum mordeat atque remordeat, si possit, donec illi occludatur os. Si Doctor NICOLAUS CRAGIUS, in Dania Historicus, quem nosti, velit aperte fateri, quae hac de re novit, quod eum etiam libenter facturum spero, non longa erit disputatio, an LIDDELIUS istas hypotheses a me didicerit, an vero ego ab illo. Assidebat enim mensae in arce olim Uraniburgica idem Doctor, cum hasce hypotheses LIDDELIO, cui nunquam tale quid antea, ne per somnium quidem, in mentem venerat, aperui. Quin et ipsi Doctori NICOLAO, ob sinceram inter nos amicitiam, iam diu antea designationem earundem hypothesium in patenti charta, prout a me sedulo expetierat, concesseram, uti ipsemet non invite fatebitur, et quod omnium maxime rem arguit, ipsae Liddelianae ad me datae literae ipsiusque manu scriptae, fraudem et plagium eius testabuntur. de istulo nunc satis. Tu quid tui sit officij, facile absque me monitore nosti.

Carmina quaedam in mei gratiam a varijs conscripta addam, si ea denuo exarare tempus permiserit, sin minus, habebis alias. Interea tu etiam aliqua ulterius pro tua arguta vena meditare, et Caesaris in me atque atque ea quae tracto studia clementissimam benignitatem collauda.

Vale atque brevi rescribe.
Dabantur ex arce Caesarea Benachia sive Venetijs Bohemorum Anno 99 die 26. Septembris stylo renovato.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 187; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Alter plagiarius et Mastiges meus impurissima fera Ditmarsica ante paucas hebdomadas Praga se subduxit, deserta uxore, quam nuper duxerat, uti perhibent, meretricula, sive quod male sibi conscius esset, et poenas secundum leges metueret, quas etiam vix effugiet, sive quod alibi latitando nova ruminetur mendacia novasque calumnias, pro stabiliendis prioribus, uti fieri solet, sicque decuplo pluribus opus erit, sed obstruetur et illi, Deo volente, os, ita ut mallet facta infecta esse.

TYCHO BRAHE TO DANIEL CRAMER.

Greeting.

I wrote back to you, most renowned and learned Lord Doctor Cramer, a little before I left Wittenberg, which I suppose you received. But of my affairs — after, departing thence, I betook myself into Bohemia by the most clement mandate and summons of the most august Emperor — since it would be too long to narrate everything severally here, I have had a copy made of my recent [letter] to our Holger Rosenkrantz, which contains a certain little history of what has befallen me here, of which I make you partaker. Thence you will draw everything through its circumstances; for since the friendship between Holger and you is so close, he will by no means take it ill that I share a copy of his letter with you (yet to be held secret, and shown to none but secret friends). Wherefore of my affairs I add nothing here, only I give thanks to immortal God. And do you, and all the pious and upright, give thanks with me, that all has succeeded well and according to my prayer.

But there is another thing, concerning you in no small part, which I judged should not be hidden from you. When I was at Wittenberg, there had come thither a certain Danish student familiarly known to me, called Bartold Ivari Mule, who had come with a certain Swedish Baron, Sten Bielke, my kinsman; and when they conversed with me there for some days, it happened that Sten made mention of Duncan the Scot — for both of them had been a while at Helmstedt for study's sake, whence too they had come. I, soon adding [the matter], opened to that Baron, in Bartold's presence, [Duncan's] plagiarism against me; and when he wondered and as it were hesitated, I produced your letter to Holger Rosenkrantz, written at length on this matter, and gave it them to read. But I know not how it came about that, while that Danish student kept it overnight, he silently made a copy for himself, which, when he came to Helmstedt, he showed to that little Scot. How [Duncan] received it, I will report word for word from Bartold's own letter, which I got a few days ago: "Then as for Doctor Cramer's letter, I showed it to Duncan, concealing, however, whence I got it. There the man began so to blame the impiety of the 'wretched little ass' (for so he slandered [Cramer]) that he called him 'a most ungrateful cuckoo toward his preceptor'; and at the same time, excusing himself to Your Magnificence, and referring me to the order of lectures which I append, where he boasted he had made most honourable mention of you." So far he.

What he intimates about the printed lecture-order, sent along — by which words Duncan presumes to clear himself — runs thus, under the title of Philosophical lectures: "Duncan Liddel the Scot, D[octor], publicly proposes the doctrine of sines and triangles; which finished, the theories of the Planets, according to the hypothesis of Ptolemy and Copernicus, and that hypothesis of the world-system which Tycho Brahe describes in book 2 of the aethereal Phenomena." This [is] his — a fine and most honourable mention of me, forsooth; nay, rather a little fox's trick. For since he is conscious that he secretly peddled my hypotheses to others as his own, and so committed plagiarism, [and] since he dares not do the same publicly, nor will he admit they are mine (lest he contradict what he earlier fabricated), therefore, speaking generally and cunningly, he says only "those hypotheses which I describe in that second book" — for one can also describe another's inventions, without their being on that account his own. He used the same craft in certain theses published at Helmstedt in [15]95, where among "Mathematical Parerga" (so he calls them, and rightly, for he truly handles by-works, and trifles about a matter not grasped), in the second proposition he has: "Since there are three discrepant hypotheses by which the celestial appearances are solved and explained — one of Ptolemy, another of Copernicus, the third that which Tycho Brahe mentions in his book on the Comet of '77 — it is doubted whether any other, different from all these, can be given; and then which of these agrees more with the appearances." See how like himself both here and there, and for the same cause I have said; O cunning and sharp-nosed little Scot! But he does not remember enough that a liar ought to have a good memory. In the announcement which Franz Parcovius, Dean of the Medical Faculty in the Academia Julia [Helmstedt], under whom Liddel was promoted to Doctor, [posted] — these words, with [Liddel's] knowledge, and perhaps even at his dictation, were inserted, and publicly published and posted; which, after making mention of Liddel (would it were as true as it is pompous), speaks thus: "For as to Mathematics, he is not only well versed in the ancients' hypothesis, but knows excellently the more recent, laborious, and elaborate hypothesis of Copernicus" — in which "whatever I have attained, I frankly confess I owe to him. Moreover he discovered another new hypothesis, which he taught publicly and privately, though it is usurped under another's name; of which thing many of his disciples give him the clearest testimony." But I add a copy of the whole announcement; I would have sent the printed copy, but since I have only one, I cannot do without it. Is it not there openly betrayed that Duncan arrogates my inventions to himself? For he cannot excuse himself that these things were ascribed by his promoter without his knowledge; or, if so, why did he not deny it, and protest it was done against his will? Why did that little crow let itself be so adorned with others' feathers? But we shall look into these things more amply in time, God willing. Meanwhile we shall look down from on high upon this little Scot's arrogance and insolence. If he goes on scurrilously raving against sensible and learned men far better than himself, and on my account calling them "wretched asses" and "most ungrateful cuckoos" — because they do not keep silent the truth they are asked — I will bring it about that, publicly accused and convicted of plagiarism, he pay according to the law — that is, become infamous — and no longer provoke the hornets. You, meanwhile, meditate and set down for yourself what you know to be true and just; and if you please, publish it, in a quaternion or two, in defence of yourself and me, that this bilious little Scot may have something to bite and bite back at, if he can — until his mouth is shut.

If Doctor Nicolaus Cragius [Niels Krag], the Historian in Denmark, whom you know, will openly confess what he knows of this matter — which I hope he will gladly do — there will be no long dispute whether Liddel learned those hypotheses from me, or I from him. For that same Doctor was sitting at table, in the once-Uraniborg castle, when I opened these hypotheses to Liddel — to whom never anything of the sort had before come into mind, not even in a dream. Moreover, to Doctor Niels himself, for the sincere friendship between us, I had long before granted a designation of these same hypotheses on an open sheet, as he had diligently sought from me — as he himself will not unwillingly confess; and, what most of all proves the matter, Liddel's own letters to me, written in his own hand, will testify to his fraud and plagiarism. Of the little fellow, enough now. You easily know your own duty without my prompting.

Some poems written for me by various [hands] I will add, if time permit me to copy them out anew; if not, you shall have them another time. Meanwhile do you too meditate something further, by your sharp vein, and praise the Emperor's most clement kindness toward me and the studies I pursue.

Farewell, and write back shortly.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, or the Venice of the Bohemians, the year [15]99, the 26th day of September, by the renewed [New] style.

[Postscript:] My other plagiarist and tormentor [Mastiges], that most impure Dithmarsian beast [Ursus], a few weeks ago withdrew himself from Prague, having deserted the wife — a little harlot, as they report — whom he had lately married: whether because he was ill-conscious to himself, and feared the penalties according to the laws (which he will scarcely escape), or because, lurking elsewhere, he is ruminating new lies and new calumnies to prop up the former ones (as is wont to happen), and so ten times more will be needed — but his mouth too will be stopped, God willing, so that he would rather his deeds were undone.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Daniel Cramer (1568–1637), the Pomeranian theologian-poet and Tycho's polemical ally (cf. brahe-t_16000401001), here enlisted against Liddel as later against Craig/Ursus.
  • Significance — the Duncan Liddel plagiarism dossier (the corpus's fullest statement of that dispute). This letter assembles Tycho's whole case against Duncan Liddel (1561–1613), the Scottish mathematician-physician at Helmstedt (Academia Julia), for claiming the Tychonic geo-heliocentric world-system as his own. (It fixes the "Duncan" of brahe-t_16000401001 — with Niels Krag's testimony — as Duncan Liddel.) The evidence Tycho marshals:
  • The Bartold Mule episode. At Wittenberg, the Danish student Bartold Ivari Mule, travelling with Sten Bielke (a Swedish baron, Tycho's kinsman), heard Tycho's charge and read Cramer's letter to Holger Rosenkrantz; he secretly copied it and showed it to Liddel at Helmstedt — whose contemptuous reaction (calling Cramer "a wretched ass," "an ungrateful cuckoo") Tycho quotes from Mule's letter.
  • Liddel's evasive "honourable mention." His printed lecture-catalogue ("the planetary theories according to Ptolemy, Copernicus, and that hypothesis which Tycho Brahe describes in book 2 of the aethereal Phenomena") and his 1595 Helmstedt theses ("Mathematical Parerga," prop. 2) both name Tycho's system only generally — a "fox's trick," Tycho says, to avoid admitting he had peddled it as his own.
  • The damning promotion-notice. The doctoral announcement by Dean Franz Parcovius, "with Liddel's knowledge and perhaps dictation," claims Liddel "discovered another new hypothesis … though it is usurped under another's name" — i.e. publicly crediting Liddel with the Tychonic system. Tycho: "Is it not openly betrayed that Duncan arrogates my inventions?"
  • The witnesses. Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius), the Danish royal historian, sat at Tycho's Uraniborg table when Tycho revealed the hypotheses to Liddel — "to whom never had such a thing come into mind, not even in a dream"; Tycho had also given Krag a diagram of the system on an open sheet. And Liddel's own letters will prove the plagiarism. (Krag's verbatim testimony is quoted in brahe-t_16000401001.)
  • The threat and the commission. Tycho threatens to have Liddel "publicly accused and convicted of plagiarism … become infamous"; he asks Cramer to publish a defence (a quaternion or two).
  • Cross-references. The master document of the Duncan Liddel priority dispute — pair with brahe-t_16000401001 (the Niels Krag testimony) and route alongside the parallel Ursus dispute (brahe-t_16000911001 etc.). "Book 2 of the aethereal Phenomena" / "book on the Comet of '77" = Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588), where the geo-heliocentric system was first published.
  • Persons & places named. Daniel Cramer; Holger Rosenkrantz (the shared letter); Bartold Ivari Mule; Sten Bielke; Duncan Liddel; Franz Parcovius (Helmstedt medical dean); Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius); Ptolemy, Copernicus; Rudolf II. Places: Benátky ("Venice of the Bohemians"), Wittenberg, Helmstedt (Academia Julia), Hven/Uraniborg, Denmark.
  • Place/calendar. Source dateline ex arce Caesarea Benachia sive Venetijs Bohemorum … die 26. Septembris stylo renovato → Benátky, 26 September 1599 [N.S.]. The catalogue label's "Wittenberg" is a location error (contradicted by the dateline); led with the source and flagged for review of the catalogue date.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript reporting Ursus had fled Prague, restored from Dreyer VIII p.187 (appended to the Tycho→Cramer letter of 16/26 Sept 1599; dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. Postscript closing idiom corrected from 'facta insecta esse' to 'facta infecta esse' per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p.187 (the classical tag 'facta infecta' = deeds made undone); the English translation was already correct and required no change.

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Collated the whole letter, including the postscript marked 'restored from Dreyer', visually against Dreyer TBOO VIII pp. 184-187, read within the supplied 183-188 extract (p.183 is the tail of a prior Tycho-to-Longomontanus letter, p.188 the continuation of the next Tycho-to-Scaliger letter, both checked to confirm the boundary). Verified the whole Bartold Ivari Mule / Sten Bielke / Duncan Liddel plagiarism narrative word for word, including the two embedded quotations (Bartold's letter, and Duncan's 1595 Helmstedt 'Parerga Mathematica' theses) and the Franciscus Parcovius encomium, plus the names Niels Krag/Cragius, Franciscus Parcovius, Helmstedt/Helmstadium — all match Dreyer exactly. Gave particular scrutiny to the embedded first-person clause on p.186, 'in qua quicquid consecutus sum, ipsi debere me ingenue fateor', since an abrupt person-shift there is exactly the shape of the process's known pronoun-flip defect (a self-boast turned into a claim about Tycho): the transcribed original matches Dreyer verbatim at this point, and the English preserves the same ambiguous person-shift already present in the Latin (Parcovius's encomium itself lurches from third person to an embedded first-person avowal and back) rather than introducing a new inversion — so this is not counted as a translator-introduced defect. Confirmed the restored postscript (the Ursus/'Mastiges' paragraph) is genuinely present on Dreyer p.187 and correctly reinstated. One text error found, in the postscript's closing words ('insecta' for Dreyer's 'infecta'); does not affect the English, which already carries the correct sense. No fabrication, omission, or sense-changing mistranslation found.

1 defect found:
- text error (low). Our source Latin misprints the classical idiom 'facta infecta' (deeds made undone — from the Roman-comedy tag 'quod factum infectum fieri nequit') as the nonsensical 'facta insecta'; Dreyer p.187 reads 'infecta'. The published English already renders the correct sense ('his deeds were undone'), so the translation itself is not at fault — only our stored the transcribed original is corrupted. Dreyer prints: ita ut mallet facta infecta eße.

Repaired. Verified the single text error against the Dreyer page image (TBOO VIII p.187, postscript, lines 8-10), read visually in the extract PDF. The plate clearly shows 'facta infecta eße', confirming the audit's claim. Only the Latin postscript was touched; nothing else in the letter was altered.
