The original

IOANNES CRAIGUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

NOBILISSIME et clarissime vir, amice colende. Non occurrit hoc tempore, quod scriptione dignum videatur, sed cum detur occasio, non prætermittendam puto. Et ne meræ omnino sint literæ, quamvis hoc fere nihili sit instar, quod prioribus cœperam de cœlestium Hypothesi ad te scribere, paucis repetam. In tua Hypothesi non alienum videtur, quod Terra luminarium et Sol cæterorum Planetarum sit centrum, sed tamen absurdum implicat, quod Mars acronychus terris propior fiat Sole; concinnior fortasse erit, si punctum Terræ vicinius quam Sol statuatur centrum, aut si id nolis, ut duplici epicyclo augeatur orbis Planetæ superioris, quemadmodum ad me suam ea de re cantationem scripsit M. DUNCANUS LIDDELIUS; quæ quidem Hypothesis nihil absurdi habebit; nam non solum hoc tuum vitabitur, sed etiam orbes Planetis asscribi poterunt. Sunt et plures modi, quibus ista supponi possunt, et ipse aliquando exponam, quid hac de re sentiam, plenius, omnino enim incumbendum statuo, ut Physicis et Mathematicis pariter satisfiat. Audio, clarissime vir, te in Chymicis præclare versatum esse, rogo te, ut mihi aliqua ex secretioribus et utilioribus communices. Habet ROLLANDUS in suis Centurijs curarum, ut sic loquar, aliquas mirabiles compositiones, ut aquæ benedictæ, balsami sulphuris, spiritus vitæ, extractæ esulæ, quæ si tibi nota sunt, fac me earum participem. Habeo extractum esulæ communi modo per spiritum vini; sed est alia quædam singularis ratio in istis extractionibus; sic si tibi aliquid constet de distillatis aquis purgantibus, quæ iam apud aliquos in usu sunt. Fuit apud nos hisce diebus PETRUS PAYSEN Danus, vir in linguis egregie versatus, et in pluribus disciplinis bene institutus, et in Mathematicis quoque bene doctus; multas regiones peragravit, et multa vidit, ei trado literas meas, ut ad tuam amicitiam ipsi aditus fiat, qua non dubito quin dignus futurus sit.

Bene et feliciter vale.
Datæ Edenburgi 9. Iulij.
Salutem precor nostratibus amicis.
Tuus
IOAN. CRAIGUS, Doctor.

English translation machine translation, Secure

John Craig to Tycho Brahe.

Most noble and most renowned Sir, friend to be honoured. Nothing presents itself at this time that seems worth the writing, yet since the occasion offers, I think it ought not to be passed by. And so that the letter may not be wholly empty — although this is next to nothing — I will briefly take up again what I had begun to write to you in earlier [letters] concerning the Hypothesis of the heavens. In your Hypothesis it does not seem out of place that the Earth should be the centre of the luminaries, and the Sun the centre of the remaining Planets; but it nonetheless involves an absurdity, namely that Mars, [when] acronychal, comes to be nearer the Earth than the Sun [is]. It will perhaps be more elegant if a point nearer the Earth than the Sun be set down as the centre; or, if you do not want that, [more elegant] that the orb of a superior Planet be augmented by a double epicycle — just as Master Duncan Liddel wrote me his little tune on this matter; which Hypothesis, indeed, will have nothing absurd in it, for not only will this [difficulty] of yours be avoided, but orbs too will be able to be assigned to the Planets. There are also several ways in which these things can be supposed, and I myself will one day set out more fully what I think on this matter; for I hold that one must by all means strive that satisfaction be given to the Physicists and the Mathematicians alike. I hear, most renowned Sir, that you are excellently versed in chymical matters; I ask you to share with me some of the more secret and more useful ones. Rolland has in his Centuries of cures, so to speak, certain wonderful preparations — such as of blessed water, of balsam of sulphur, of spirit of life, of extract of spurge — which, if they are known to you, make me a sharer in them. I have extract of spurge made in the common way, by spirit of wine; but there is a certain other, singular method in these extractions; likewise [I should be glad] if anything be established to you concerning the distilled purging waters which are now in use among some. There was with us in these [past] days Peter Paysen, a Dane, a man excellently versed in tongues and well grounded in several disciplines, and well learned in Mathematics too; he has travelled through many regions and seen many things. To him I entrust my letter, so that access to your friendship may be opened for him — of which I do not doubt that he will prove worthy.

Fare you well and happily.
Given at Edinburgh, 9 July.
I beg [you to convey] greeting to our friends.
Yours,
John Craig, Doctor.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Ioannes Craigus = John Craig (d. c.1620), Scottish physician and mathematician, educated on the Continent (Frankfurt an der Oder, where Duncan Liddel studied under him; later Königsberg), and from the 1580s royal physician at the court of James VI in Edinburgh. He entered a protracted epistolary dispute with Tycho over the geo-heliocentric world-system (treated in Adam Mosley, "Tycho Brahe and John Craig: The Dynamic of a Dispute," 2002); this letter is an early, still-courteous instalment of that exchange. The subscription Doctor marks his medical doctorate.
  • The astronomical objection (the substance of the letter). Craig grants the structure of Tycho's Hypothesis — Terra luminarium et Sol caeterorum Planetarum centrum, "the Earth the centre of the luminaries [Sun and Moon], the Sun the centre of the remaining planets," i.e. the geo-heliocentric arrangement. His objection is that Mars acronychus terris propior fiat Sole: that Mars at opposition (acronychal — rising at sunset, hence diametrically opposite the Sun) is brought nearer the Earth than the Sun is. This is the celebrated consequence of Tycho's system that the Martian orb must intersect the solar orb — the very point that made Tycho abandon the solid celestial spheres. Craig reads it as an absurdum; the hedge non alienum … sed tamen absurdum implicat is preserved (he concedes the merit, then flags the flaw).
  • Craig's proposed remedies. (1) si punctum Terrae vicinius quam Sol statuatur centrum — set the planets' centre at a point nearer the Earth than the Sun; (2) failing that, duplici epicyclo augeatur orbis Planetae superioris — augment a superior planet's orb with a double epicycle (here = a second, additional epicycle), so that solid orbs (orbes) can still be assigned to the planets without intersection. Craig's programme — ut Physicis et Mathematicis pariter satisfiat, "that satisfaction be given to the Physicists and the Mathematicians alike" — is to save both the physical reality of solid spheres and the geometry; this is the crux of his disagreement with Tycho, who held the spheres to be fluid/non-existent.
  • Duncan Liddel. M. Duncanus Liddelius = Duncan Liddel (1561–1613), Scottish mathematician and physician, then teaching at Frankfurt an der Oder / Rostock (later Helmstedt; he endowed a mathematics chair at Aberdeen in 1613). M. = Magister, "Master" (academic). cantationem … scripsit — literally "wrote his song/chant"; here a faintly belittling figure for Liddel's set-piece on the double-epicycle device. Kept as the metaphor "little tune," with the diminutive force flagged rather than smoothed to a neutral "account."
  • Chymica / alchemy. Chymicis … versatum — Craig solicits Tycho's chemical (Paracelsian-iatrochemical) secrets. Rollandus … in suis Centurijs curarum = the Centuriae curationum / Centuries of cures, a medical-recipe collection (most plausibly the Curationum … centuriae genre associated with the period's iatrochemists; the precise "Rolland" is not securely fixed — see crux). The named preparations: aqua benedicta "blessed water" (a period purgative/emetic compound — not holy water in the devotional sense); balsamum sulphuris "balsam of sulphur" (sulphur dissolved in oil, an iatrochemical remedy); spiritus vitae "spirit of life"; extractum esulae "extract of spurge" (esula = Euphorbia esula, a drastic purgative), which Craig says he prepares communi modo per spiritum vini, "in the common way, by spirit of wine," but suspects a singularis ratio, a special method. distillatae aquae purgantes = "distilled purging waters." Period terms retained and glossed by the edition's convention; none modernised.
  • Peter Paysen. Petrus Paysen Danus — Peter Paysen (Peder Paysen), a Dane, here described as a learned traveller skilled in languages and mathematics, carrying this letter as a letter of introduction to Tycho. Not otherwise identified here; the name is given as written.
  • Dateline. Datae Edenburgi 9. Iulij = "Given at Edinburgh, 9 July." The source dateline carries no year; the edition label supplies 1589, consistent with the Tycho–Craig exchange and with the Berlin-copy witness. The header therefore reads "9 July 1589," but note that the year is editorial/catalogue-supplied, not present in the dateline. O.S.: a Scottish correspondent in 1589 wrote Old Style (Scotland did not adopt the Gregorian calendar until 1600/1752); the 9 July is Julian.
  • Edition / witness. Printed in the Commercium litterarium clarorum virorum (the edition of record per the source); MS witness A = a handwritten copy bound in a copy of Tycho's astronomical writings, now in Berlin (shelfmark not specified in the source).
  • Formulae. Bene et feliciter vale → "Fare you well and happily". Tuus … Doctor subscription rendered in full.
