The original

GENEROSO ATQUE MAGNIFICO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, IN KNUDTSTRUPP ET URANIBURG, ET DOMINO SUO, FAUTORI AC PROMOTORI SUMME HONORANDO.

Salutem plurimam D.

PRÆTER omnem spem et expectationem meam accidit, Generose Domine TYCHO, fautor ac promotor honorandissime, ut hîc diutius moras trahere cogar. Nihilominus tamen ex voto et animi sententia mihi hactenus omnia successerunt, et uti spero amodo etiam successura esse. Tuas a Consobrino tuo CLAUDIO BILLEO cum Idea sive typo Structuræ novæ Benachiensis 8 Idus Ianuarij sartas tectasque accepi, quibus properante hinc Posta confestim rescribo. Ideam adhuc hodie Magnifico Domino BARVITIO, Cæsareæ Maiestati humilime ad exhibendum, offeram, cæteraque sedulo et diligenter, quæ in memoriale assignata reperi, exequar. Cæsarea Maiestas cum quotidie tui ad D. BARVITIUM fecisset mentionem, deque te Cæsareo favore valde sollicita esset, quinam valeres, quidnam rerum ageres, an nihil ei transmitteres, Dominus BARVITIUS, me quidem inscio, tua Progymnasmata Astronomiæ ad ipsius Maiestatem detulit. Dominus BARVITIUS ad me aiebat: »Ich muste nur Ihrer Röm. Kay. Mts. etwas weisenn von dem Herrn TYCHONE, damit von Ihr Kay. Mts. ich nit so offters gefraget werde, weil ich dan sobaldt nichts darauf zu andtwortenn weis, und zue fridenn bleibe.«. Quæ ipsius Cæsarea Maiestas actutum aperuit et per integrum diem vultu hilari et clementissimo, imo paterno erga te quem gerit animo, clementissime perlustravit, uti coram plenius id tibi aperiam; per festinationem enim Postæ commode hoc fieri nunc nequit. Annuatim a Cæsarea Maiestate clementissime ex ferarum genere tres tibi sunt deputatæ, et aliquot Züber (uti in Bohemia vocant) piscium, de qua re ex mandato ipsius Cæs. Maiestatis a Domino BARVITIO ad Dominum Capitaneum Brandeisensem iam scriptum est, nec non de Prædio, ut aliquod eligat, et per primam occasionem, quid ille sentiat, Dominum BARVITIUM certiorem faciat, quas literas mecum dante Deo afferam. Pecunias antea promissas post diuturnam moram etiam nunc obtinui. Magnificus Dominus CORRADUCIUS te officiose et peramanter resalutat omniaque felicia precatur; dicta salute et congratulatione ad me dicebat: »Pro congratulatione hac quam mihi nuncias, et salute quam nomine Domini TYCHONIS BRAHE dicis, gratias tibi ago maximas; vicissim eum, cui mea studia offero, peramice salutabis. Ego illum virum tanto amore prosequor, itaque ipsi faveo, licet de facie mihi ignotus sit, ut nîl supra, non modo ob insignem ac præclaram quam prositetur artem Astronomicam, sed et ob generis splendorem, dan er eines tapfferenn Geschlechts und Herkommens, auch vornehmer Man, dessen gleichenn izo in toto terrarum Orbe nit zu findenn, utinam ut quam primum copia mihi eum videndi et cum ipso loquendi se offerat; sed id brevi fore puto.« Dominus TYPOTIUS ante biduum huc Pilsnam Lipsiâ appulit, qui etiam te salva impertit salute.

Iamque diutissime ac felicissime cum tota Domo vale viveque annos Platonicos.
Dabantur calamo celeri Pilsnæ ipso die quo mihi tuæ cum modella sunt redditæ, Anno assertæ salutis 1600.
Tæ Mæ addictiss.
DANIEL FELS,
manu propria.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 233; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Quod meæ superioribus diebus tardius quam debebant tibi sint redditæ, culpa mihi non est danda, sed id vel Postæ, per quam rescripseram actutum, vel Domino Doctori HELLERO, ad quem meas direxeram, imputandum. Cum viaticum mihi desit, nec hîc mutuo accipere possum nummos, scripsi D. HELLERO, ut per Postam 8 Ioachimicos mihi, bonâ ac fide et gratia a te restituendos, transmittere velit. Iterum vale measque festinationi ignosce, meque incolumem tu incolumis expecta. Progymnasmata tua Astron. a Cæs. Maiestate nondum recepi, quotidie adhuc ipsius Cæs. Maiestas ea perlustrat.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the generous and magnificent man, Lord Tycho Brahe, in Knudstrup and Uraniborg, and his Lord, patron and promoter most highly to be honoured.

Heartiest greeting.

Beyond all my hope and expectation it has fallen out, generous Lord Tycho, most honoured patron and promoter, that I am compelled to draw out my stay here longer. Nevertheless all things have so far succeeded for me according to my wish and mind's intent, and, as I hope, will succeed henceforth too. Your [letter], with the design or model of the new Benátky structure, I received safe and sound from your cousin Claus Bille on the 6th of January, to which — as the post hastens from here — I reply at once. The design I shall today offer to the Magnificent Lord Barvitius, to be humbly laid before the Imperial Majesty, and I shall carefully and diligently carry out the rest which I found set down in the memorandum. Since the Imperial Majesty daily made mention of you to Lord Barvitius, and was very solicitous about you, with imperial favour — how you fared, what you were doing, whether you were sending him nothing — Lord Barvitius, without my knowledge indeed, brought your Progymnasmata of Astronomy to His Majesty. Lord Barvitius said to me: "I simply had to show His Roman Imperial Majesty something of Lord Tycho, so that I should not be asked by His Imperial Majesty so often — since I then know of nothing to answer thereto at once — and may be left in peace." Which [book] His Imperial Majesty at once opened, and for a whole day, with a cheerful and most clement countenance — indeed with the paternal disposition he bears toward you — most graciously perused; as I shall more fully disclose to you in person, for through the haste of the post this cannot now be conveniently done. Annually there have been most graciously assigned to you by the Imperial Majesty three [head] of the wild-game kind, and several Züber (as they call them in Bohemia) of fish — concerning which, by the Imperial Majesty's mandate, Lord Barvitius has already written to the Lord Captain of Brandeis; and also concerning an estate, that you may choose one, and at the first opportunity inform Lord Barvitius what you think of it — which letters, God granting, I shall bring with me. The money promised before, after a long delay, I have now likewise obtained. The Magnificent Lord Coraduz returns your greeting dutifully and most lovingly, and prays every happiness for you; the greeting and congratulation delivered, he said to me: "For this congratulation which you bring me, and the greeting you say in the name of Lord Tycho Brahe, I give you the greatest thanks; in turn you will greet most amicably him to whom I offer my devotion. I pursue that man with such great love, and so favour him — although he is unknown to me by face — that nothing could be beyond it: not only for the distinguished and illustrious astronomical art which he professes, but also for the splendour of his lineage, for he is of a valiant family and descent, and a distinguished man besides, whose like is not now to be found in all the world. Would that the chance of seeing him and speaking with him might offer itself as soon as may be — but that, I think, will be soon." Lord Typotius arrived here at Pilsen from Leipzig two days ago, who also bestows on you a safe greeting.

And now, most long and most happily, fare and live, with all your House, the Platonic years.
Given with swift pen at Pilsen, on the very day on which your [letter], with the model, was delivered to me, in the year of asserted salvation 1600.
Most devoted to Your Magnificence,
Daniel Fels,
with his own hand.

[Postscript:] That my [earlier letters] were delivered to you these last days later than they ought to have been, the blame is not to be laid on me, but is to be imputed either to the Post, by which I had at once written back, or to Lord Doctor Heller, to whom I had directed mine. Since I lack travel-money, and cannot here borrow coin, I have written to Doctor Heller to be willing to send me by the Post 8 Joachimsthalers — to be repaid by you in good faith and favour. Again farewell, and pardon my haste, and, sound yourself, await me sound. Your Progymnasmata of Astronomy I have not yet received back from the Imperial Majesty: the Imperial Majesty still peruses it daily.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Daniel Fels, Tycho's familiaris and on-the-spot agent at the imperial court (then at Pilsen), who managed Tycho's court business and carried letters (cf. brahe-t_16000306002, where Tycho calls him "my familiar Daniel Fels, who has stayed for some while at Pilsen with you").
  • Significance — Tycho's court-business at Pilsen, January 1600. A detailed dispatch on the negotiations that secured Tycho's imperial establishment:
  • The Benátky building plans. Fels has received from Tycho's cousin Claus Bille (Claudius Billeus, consobrinus) Tycho's letter with the "design or model of the new Benátky structure" (Tycho's projected observatory/works at Benátky), to be laid before the Emperor via Barvitius, plus a memoriale (memorandum) of further requests.
  • The Progymnasmata before the Emperor. Dominus Barvitius = Johann Anton Barvitius, Rudolf II's Latin secretary, who — pressed daily by the Emperor for news of Tycho — had on his own initiative laid Tycho's _Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata_ before His Majesty, who "for a whole day … most graciously perused" it "with the paternal disposition he bears toward you."
  • The court provisions & estate. The Emperor has granted Tycho an annual allowance of three head of game and several Züber (tubs) of fish (the German measure, glossed by Fels), arranged through the Captain of Brandeis (Brandýs nad Labem), the royal estate near Benátky; and Tycho is invited to choose an estate (praedium). The previously promised money has at last been paid.
  • The Coraduz greeting. Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor; cf. brahe-t_16000324001) returns Tycho's greeting with an effusive testimonial.
  • German code-switches (kept and flagged; the edition's convention). Two passages of direct speech break from Latin into German:
  • Barvitius: "Ich muste nur Ihrer Röm. Kay. Mts. etwas weisen von dem Herrn Tychone, damit … ich nit so offters gefraget werde … und zufrieden bleibe." ("I simply had to show His Imperial Majesty something of Lord Tycho, so as not to be asked so often … and be left in peace.")
  • Coraduz (macaronic, mid-sentence): "…sed et ob generis splendorem, dan er eines tapferen Geschlechts und Herkommens, auch vornehmer Mann, dessen gleichen jetzt in toto terrarum orbe nicht zu finden…" ("…but also for the splendour of his lineage, for he is of a valiant family and descent, and a distinguished man besides, whose like is not now to be found in the whole world…"). The Latin↔German switch within Coraduz's own quoted words is preserved.
  • Persons named. Daniel Fels; Claus Bille (Tycho's cousin); Johann Anton Barvitius (imperial secretary); Rudolf Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor); the Captain of Brandeis; Dominus Typotius = Jacobus Typotius (Jacques Typoets, 1540–1601), the imperial historiographer, just arrived from Leipzig.
  • Flourishes / dating. vive annos Platonicos = "live the Platonic years" (the Great Year — a hyperbolic long-life wish). 8 Idus Ianuarij = the 6th of January (the Roman-calendar idiom, glossed); the dateline repeats "the very day your letter … was delivered." Anno assertae salutis 1600 = "in the year of asserted salvation 1600"; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with label.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (the delivery-delay apology, the request to Dr Heller for 8 Joachimsthalers, "the Emperor still peruses the Progymnasmata daily"), restored from Dreyer VIII p.233 (dropped by the working transcription).
