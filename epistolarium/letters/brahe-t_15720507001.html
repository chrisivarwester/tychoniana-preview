The original

VIRO NOBILI TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO ET AMICO OBSERVANDISSIMO.
VERNAS SALUTES, CHEIROS ET MELISSAS.

RECEPI literas tuas, amicissime TYCHO, quas magna cum voluptate legi. Obsecro, dum illas funderes, quis te Olympicus radius tinxit, ut me quoque eadem tinctura figeres? An te sacrum Veneris sulphur, an Bacchi aethereus Mercurius, an dium illud Apollinis sal ab antiquis celebratum afflavit? Non sint mihi Urania et Vulcanus propitij, si isto extemporaneo flumine meum delectaveris plurimum. Non semper praemeditata aut digesta diu felicius cadunt. Extemporanea et impetuosa esse illa necesse est, quae momento solidum penetrantia, penetrata in sui naturam convertunt: ut patres nos nostri et fulmina utriusque Meteorologiae docuerunt. Ubi Soli, quem colo, sacrificabo, supplex ipse precabor, saepius ut tali te calore percellat. Macte sis atque hac mentis sublimitate, paucis cognita, cresce, donec te Iovis armiger ales in solium magni referat Iovis, unde ruinas labantis possis informes cernere mundi. Quod si fieri debet, utique fugiendus Gheburah et malum Aquilo: Auster bonus quaerendus est et vitalis Oriens, ubi vitam, donum et talentum, quod nobis concreditum est, recepimus. Hic Auster Oriensque nec locorum astringitur miserijs, nec certam ad plagam mundi sensibilis declinat: Ubique sunt: intra, extra omnia: in antiqua Iliade, in thesauris Universi, in abyssis animorum: imo intrate; hoc multis quaesitum Latium et Saturnium regnum. Non igitur versus Austrum sensibilem quaerenda illa Olympicae civitatis civilitas: nisi quatenus rigida Septentrio, tantum non tuleris, uti etiam forte non semper feret splendorem, tantumque donum, vel te ipso (quod saepius inaudivi) vate atque interprete. Neque tamen hic, in quo spiramus, Aquilo solus formidatam Barbariem alit. Etenim in extremo Oriente Austroque animas Boreae penetrabile frigus etiam nonnunquam adurit, et latus hoc mundi, quod nebulae malusque Iupiter urget, amoena quoque temperie perfundi dubium non est: illis tamen solis cognita, qui supra temporis et locorum Horizontem lumen intuentur. Profanum itaque sit voluptatis hortum temporum locorumve carceribus includere. Rident hasce exuvias maculis purgatae Lunae et nitentes Berilli. Nosce te ipsum et omnia noveris. In hanc dioptram respicientem nunquam te turbida fallet imago. Hactenus silentia: nunc verbum unum. Venit hisce diebus ad nos Coloniâ imperterritus ille Danus Wiburgensis, cuius apud te saepenumero mentio facta est, Graecus, Arabs, Galenicus, Paracelsicus, Theologaster, Philosophus, Medicus, Syrupiaster, Chymista, et plus, si velles: interim tamen ex his omnibus nihil. Hic Wiburgiam invisurus diu non visit senem parentem, heri profectus est, rediturus ad nos brevi. Conditionem philosophicam captat, aptam Spagiricis pulveribus et sudore Chymico. Est mirabilis Apothecarius: tibi, modo frugi esse velit, non displicebit. Si animus tibi est talem aliquem servitio adiungere, procul dubio in patria et extra patriam utilis tibi esse poterit. Ubi hominem ipse videris, fronte tecum loquetur. Interea si ipse brevi huc non concesseris, de hoc ipso negotio rescribe verbum unum. Sed audi, Nymphas sylvestres violasse te Nympharum Amatores narrant: evulsis sacris quercubus et late minitantibus alnis, vitaque privatis arboribus: quoniam hae dolent, quod morte sua (quam illis te intulisse toties ajebas) alijs sepultis digestisque rebus, vitam calore non posse excitare per te prohibeantur. Certe ut in Austrum vel Occidentem ad nos perferantur ratibus, in medio Aquilone non deerunt Aquilones.

Vale; et si coronam vernam expetis, Nymphas placa.
Hafniae 7. Maij Anno 1572.
T.
Pratensis

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the noble Tycho Brahe, his most esteemed lord and friend.
Vernal greetings — of hands and of bees [CHEIROS ET MELISSAS].

I have received your letter, dearest Tycho, which I read with great delight. I beg you — while you were pouring it out, what Olympian ray steeped you, that you should fix me too with the same tincture? Was it the sacred sulphur of Venus, or the ethereal Mercury of Bacchus, or that divine salt of Apollo celebrated by the ancients, that breathed upon you? May Urania and Vulcan be no longer favourable to me, if you have not delighted mine exceedingly with that extemporaneous flood. Things long premeditated or digested do not always fall out more happily. Those things must of necessity be extemporaneous and impetuous which, penetrating a solid body in a moment, once penetrated convert it into their own nature — as our fathers, and the thunderbolts of both Meteorologies, have taught us. When I sacrifice to the Sun, whom I worship, I shall as a suppliant pray that he may smite you more often with such heat. Be blessed, and with this sublimity of mind — known to few — grow, until the winged armour-bearer of Jove bears you back into the throne of great Jove, whence you may discern the shapeless ruins of the tottering world. But if this must come to pass, then by all means Gheburah and the evil North Wind are to be shunned: the good South Wind is to be sought, and the life-giving East, where we received life, that gift and talent which has been entrusted to us. This South and East are neither bound by the miseries of places, nor do they decline toward any fixed quarter of the sensible world: they are everywhere — within, without, in all things: in the ancient Iliad, in the treasuries of the Universe, in the depths of souls; nay, enter in — this is the Latium and the realm of Saturn sought by many. Not, therefore, toward the sensible South is that civility of the Olympian city to be sought — except in so far as you cannot bear the rigid North, just as perhaps it too will not always bear the splendour and so great a gift, even with yourself (as I have often heard tell) as seer and interpreter. And yet not here alone, where we breathe, does the North Wind nourish the dreaded Barbarism. For even in the farthest East and South the penetrating cold of Boreas now and then scorches souls; and that this side of the world, which mists and an evil Jupiter beset, is also bathed in a pleasant temperateness, there is no doubt — yet known to those alone who gaze upon the light above the Horizon of time and place. Let it therefore be a profanation to shut up the garden of delight within the prisons of times or places. At these cast-off skins the Moon, cleansed of her spots, and the gleaming Beryls, smile. Know thyself, and thou shalt know all things. To one who looks back into this lens [dioptra] no turbid image shall ever deceive him. So much for silences: now one word. There came to us these past days from Cologne that undaunted Dane of Viborg, of whom mention has often been made in your presence — a Greek, an Arab, a Galenist, a Paracelsian, a sham theologian, a philosopher, a physician, a syrup-monger, a chymist, and more, if you would; yet meanwhile, out of all these, nothing. He, [meaning] to visit Viborg, has long not visited his aged parent; he set out yesterday, [meaning] to return to us shortly. He is angling for a philosophical post, suited to spagyric powders and chymical sweat. He is a marvellous apothecary: he will not displease you — provided he be willing to be honest. If you have a mind to attach some such man to your service, beyond doubt he could be useful to you both at home and abroad. When you see the man yourself, he will speak with you face to face [from his very brow]. Meanwhile, if you yourself do not come hither shortly, write back one word about this very matter. But hear this: the Lovers of the Nymphs relate that you have done violence to the woodland Nymphs — the sacred oaks torn up, and the alders that threaten far and wide, and the trees robbed of life; for these grieve that by their own death (which you so often used to say you had inflicted on them) they are hindered, by you, from being able to rouse life by heat, once other things have been buried and digested. Certainly, that they may be carried over to us by rafts into the South or the West, in the very midst of the North there will be no lack of North Winds.

Farewell; and if you long for a vernal crown, appease the Nymphs.
At Copenhagen, 7 May, the year 1572.
Thine,
Pratensis.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Pratensis = Johannes Pratensis (Hans Philipsen, 1543–1576), Tycho's physician-friend and intimate, then rising at the University of Copenhagen (professor of medicine from 1571). The May 1572 exchange between the two introduced the subject of Tycho's De nova stella (1573): the new star of November 1572 was found later that year, and the published volume carried Latin poems by friends including Pratensis. This is the affectionate, ostentatiously learned reply to a lost letter of Tycho's that Pratensis judges blazing with inspiration.
  • Salutation. VERNAS SALUTES, CHEIROS ET MELISSAS — "Vernal greetings, cheiros and melissas." The two nouns are transliterated Greek: χείρ / χεῖρας ("hand[s]") and μέλισσα / μελίσσας ("bee[s]"). The phrase is a precious humanist conceit — "greetings of hands and of bees," i.e. greetings as busy/sweet as bees in spring — kept in Greek-derived form and glossed rather than smoothed. Flagged crux: the exact construction (salutes governing two Greek accusatives) is deliberately mannered; the gloss preserves the playfulness without over-determining it.
  • Hermetic-alchemical imagery (sustained). The letter is saturated with the language of the prima materia and the alchemical tria prima: sulphur — Mercurius — sal ("sulphur, Mercury, salt," the three principles, here divinised as "of Venus / of Bacchus / of Apollo"); tinxit … tinctura ("steeped … tincture," the alchemical tinctura that transmutes); Vulcanus (fire/the alchemist's furnace) paired with Urania (astronomy); Spagiricis pulveribus et sudore Chymico ("spagyric powders and chymical sweat" — spagyric, the Paracelsian art of separation-and-recombination, kept as period term). The conceit of the extemporaneous as that which "penetrates a solid body in a moment and converts it into its own nature" is alchemical-transmutational imagery applied to literary inspiration; rendered literally and flagged, not paraphrased away.
  • fulmina utriusque Meteorologiae — "the thunderbolts of both Meteorologies," i.e. the doctrine of lightning in both the natural-philosophical (Aristotelian Meteorologica) and a second (likely Scriptural/divine, or Paracelsian) sense; the precise pairing is left open and flagged.
  • Gheburah — Geburah (Hebrew גְּבוּרָה, "Severity / Might"), the fifth Sephirah of the Kabbalistic Tree of Life, associated with judgment, Mars, and the harsh north. Flagged crux: Pratensis pairs Gheburah with malum Aquilo ("the evil North Wind") against Auster bonus / vitalis Oriens ("the good South / life-giving East") — a Christian-Kabbalistic cosmography in which the rigorous North is to be fled for the merciful South/East. The Hebrew term is kept untranslated in the body and identified here; its force is allusive and not fully recoverable, hence the Possible grade below.
  • Cardinal-direction allegory. Aquilo / Boreas / Septentrio (North Wind / north) stand for harshness, "Barbarism," and the unwelcoming Danish climate (literal and figurative); Auster (South Wind) and Oriens (East) for life, warmth, and grace. Pratensis insists these are not fixed geographical quarters but inward and everywhere ("within, without, in all things … in the depths of souls") — a Neoplatonic-Hermetic interiorising of the compass, kept literal.
  • Iovis armiger ales … in solium magni Iovis — "the winged armour-bearer of Jove [the eagle] … into the throne of great Jove": the eagle of Jupiter bearing the soul aloft (Ganymede / apotheosis imagery), from which Tycho might "discern the shapeless ruins of the tottering world" — a contemplative ascent above the sublunary realm.
  • Latium et Saturnium regnum — "the Latium and realm of Saturn," i.e. the Golden Age (Saturn's reign in Latium, Aeneid VIII), here the inward paradise "sought by many."
  • maculis purgatae Lunae et nitentes Berilli — "the Moon cleansed of her spots, and the gleaming Beryls": the purified Moon and beryl (a scrying-stone) as emblems of the cleansed, all-reflecting soul. Beryllus as a speculum/scrying medium is a Hermetic commonplace; kept literal.
  • Nosce te ipsum — "Know thyself" (the Delphic γνῶθι σεαυτόν, in Latin); dioptram = dioptra (a sighting-tube / lens), here a metaphorical optical glass of self-knowledge in which "no turbid image shall deceive." Kept as dioptra and glossed "lens"; cf. the optical sense, not Tycho's later instruments.
  • imperterritus ille Danus Wiburgensis — "that undaunted Dane of Viborg": an unnamed itinerant physician-alchemist from Viborg (Jutland), arrived via Cologne (Colonia), whom Pratensis recommends to Tycho with heavily ironic praise — Graecus, Arabs, Galenicus, Paracelsicus, Theologaster, Philosophus, Medicus, Syrupiaster, Chymista ("a Greek, an Arab, a Galenist, a Paracelsian, a sham theologian, a philosopher, a physician, a syrup-monger, a chymist") — "yet out of all these, nothing." Theologaster and Syrupiaster are pejorative coinages (sham-theologian, syrup-quack), preserved. The man "is angling for a philosophical post" suited to alchemical work; Pratensis sounds Tycho on hiring him. The figure is unidentified; flagged.
  • fronte tecum loquetur — "he will speak with you from his [own] brow / face to face," i.e. you will read the man in his very countenance; idiom kept and glossed.
  • The Nymphs / felling-of-trees passage. Nymphas sylvestres violasse te … evulsis sacris quercubus et late minitantibus alnis — "you have done violence to the woodland Nymphs, the sacred oaks torn up and the wide-threatening alders [felled]." A playful, semi-mythological reproach: Tycho is charged (by the "Lovers of the Nymphs") with clearing timber — and the conceit is alchemical, for the felled trees "grieve" that, killed by Tycho (morte sua, quam illis te intulisse toties ajebas), they cannot now "rouse life by heat" (i.e. serve as fuel/fire for his furnace) "once other things have been buried and digested." Flagged crux: the passage fuses a literal estate-clearing (Tycho cutting wood, on Knudstrup land or for fuel) with an alchemical pun on death/putrefaction/heat (sepultis digestisque rebus … vitam calore … excitare) and an Aquilones-wordplay ("in the midst of the North there will be no lack of North Winds [Aquilones]", the wind that drives the rafts). The double sense — sylvan complaint and chymical allegory — is rendered as literally as the syntax allows and not resolved into one reading; this is the principal driver of the overall grade.
  • coronam vernam … Nymphas placa — "if you long for a vernal crown, appease the Nymphs": a closing flourish returning to the spring/Nymph conceit of the salutation.
  • Calendar / place. Hafniae 7. Maij Anno 1572 = "At Copenhagen (Hafnia), 7 May 1572" (O.S.; Denmark was on the Julian calendar). The subscription T. abbreviates Tuus ("Thine"). No in-letter date discrepancy.
  • Correction. Latin corrected 'Olympiae' to 'Olympicae' (Dreyer VII p.7) and removed the duplicated pronoun in 'profectus is est' to 'profectus est' (Dreyer VII p.8); English unaffected in both cases.
