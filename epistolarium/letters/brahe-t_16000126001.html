The original

ILLUSTRI ET GENEROSO DOMINO, DOMINO IOHANNI FRIDERICO HOFFMANNO, LIBERO BARONI IN GRUENPUECHEL ET STRECHAW, PRÆFECTO PROVINCIÆ DUCATUS STYRIÆ ET ARCHIDUCATUS AUSTRIÆ MARSCALCHO UBIQUE HÆREDITARIO BURGRAVIO IN STEYER NEC NON SAC. CÆS. MAIESTATIS AULÆ CONSILIARIO ET SERENISSIMI ARCHIDUCIS MAXIMILIANI CUBICULARIO PRIMARIO, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO.

ILLUSTRIS ET GENEROSE VIR, DOMINE ET AMICE HONORANDISSIME.

MAGNO equidem gaudio affectus sum, cum heri vesperi ex meis Domesticis huc Pragâ revertentibus percepissem Illustr. Generos. Tuam ex Austriâ nuper salvam et incolumem redijsse. Quo nomine non saltem Illustri Generositati Tuæ, sed et mihi ipsi quamplurimum congratulor, siquidem de Generositatis Tuæ erga me et ea, quæ tracto studia Astronomica, sincera et benevola affectione vel ex pauculo, quem superiori æstate invicem habuimus congressu, ita mihi optima quæque polliceor, ut in tanti viri, qui non saltem Genere clarus sit, sed et doctrinis liberalibus insigniter excultus, familiaritatem incidisse, non postremæ felicitatis loco ducam. Cumque nunc Pragam Nobilem et Eruditum adolescentem FRANCISCUM TENGNAGLIUM, mihi domesticum, qui ante paucas hebdomadas ex Italia huc reversus est, unâ cum filio meo natu maiore ex me denominato certis de caussis amandem, non potui intermittere, quin hasce pro felici reditu candido animo Illust. G. T. congratulantes unâ mitterem, atque ijs simul iniungerem, ut Tuam Illust. Generos. captata oportunitate accedentes eam ex me officiosissime salutarent. Pergratum vero est, quod Illustris Dominatio Tua Excellentem Mathematicum Dominum Μ. IOHANNEM KEPLERUM in suo Comitatu huc ad nos in Bohemiam adduxerit, qua etiam in parte non obscure elucet Illust. G. T. erga sublimia illa et a vulgi captu remota Mathematum studia eorumque alumnos singularis et nunquam satis laudatus favor atque benignitas ab omnibus hisce excellentioribus Scientijs addictis merito semper deprædicanda. Intercessit mihi cum illo viro, postquam Daniâ emigravi, nonnulla per Litteras eademque pergrata familiaritas, quæ ex mutua præsentia, quam brevi futuram mihi persuadeo, augeri poterit. Quicquid vero voluptatis et commodi ex hoc nostro congressu promanare poterit, Illustri Dominationi Tuæ præcipua ex parte acceptum ferendum grata mente agnosco. Pluribus Illust. Generos. T. multiplicibus alias pariterque gravissimis occupationibus distentam remorari nolo. Cætera, quæ hîc omitto, ij, quos ablego, si Illust. Domni Tuæ eos admittere commodum fuerit, supplebunt atque ex me referent; quibus ut benignam aurem pro solita sua humanitate præbere non dedignetur, qua possum reverentia, oro et obsecro. Valeat Tua Illust. Generositas quam felicissime et diutissime, huncque noviter inchoatum Annum Iubilæum plurimosque alios insequentes incolumis et florens transigat.

Dabantur ex Arce Cæsariana Benatica die 26 Ianuarij Anni labentis Epochæ Christianæ Dionysiacæ 1600, quam duobus deficere IOSEPHUS SCALIGER probare nititur.
Iterum et sæpius T. Illust. Gen. quam optime valeat, meque et meos sibi commendatos habeat.
Illustri Generositati Tuæ
addictiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious and generous Lord, Lord Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, Free Baron in Grünbüchel and Strechau, Prefect of the Province of the Duchy of Styria, hereditary Marshal everywhere of the Archduchy of Austria, Burgrave in Steyr, and also Aulic Councillor of the Sacred Imperial Majesty and First Chamberlain of the Most Serene Archduke Maximilian, his Lord and friend especially to be honoured.

Illustrious and generous Man, Lord and most honoured friend.

I was indeed filled with great joy when, yesterday evening, from my servants returning hither from Prague, I learned that Your Illustrious Generosity had lately returned safe and sound from Austria. On which account I congratulate not only Your Illustrious Generosity, but also myself, most heartily; since, concerning Your Generosity's sincere and benevolent affection toward me and the astronomical studies I pursue, I promise myself every best thing — even from the brief meeting we had together last summer — so that I count it no slight happiness to have fallen into the friendship of so great a man, who is not only illustrious by lineage, but also remarkably cultivated in the liberal learnings. And since I am now dispatching to Prague, for certain reasons, the noble and learned young man Franz Tengnagel, of my household — who a few weeks ago returned hither from Italy — together with my elder son, named after me, I could not omit to send along with them these lines congratulating Your Illustrious Generosity, with a candid heart, on your happy return, and at the same time to charge them that, approaching Your Illustrious Generosity at a fitting opportunity, they greet you most dutifully on my behalf. It is very welcome indeed that Your Illustrious Lordship has brought the excellent Mathematician Master Johannes Kepler, in your retinue, hither to us into Bohemia — in which respect, too, there shines forth not obscurely Your Illustrious Generosity's singular and never sufficiently praised favour and kindness toward those sublime mathematical studies, remote from the common grasp, and toward their nurslings — a favour ever deservedly to be proclaimed by all who are devoted to these more excellent Sciences. There has passed between me and that man, since I emigrated from Denmark, a certain and most pleasing familiarity by letters, which will be able to be increased by our mutual presence — which I am persuaded will be soon. And whatever of pleasure and profit may flow from this meeting of ours, I acknowledge, with grateful mind, that it is to be credited in chief part to Your Illustrious Lordship. I do not wish to detain Your Illustrious Generosity, taken up with manifold and likewise most weighty occupations, with more words. The rest, which I omit here, those whom I send will supply — if it be convenient for Your Illustrious Lordship to admit them — and will report on my behalf; to whom that you would not disdain to lend a kindly ear, after your wonted humanity, I beg and beseech with what reverence I can. May Your Illustrious Generosity fare most happily and most long, and pass this newly begun Jubilee Year, and very many others to follow, safe and flourishing.

Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 26th day of January of the passing year of the Christian Dionysian Epoch 1600 — which Joseph Scaliger strives to prove deficient by two [years].
Again and often, may Your Illustrious Generosity fare as well as possible, and hold me and mine commended to you.
Most devoted to Your Illustrious Generosity,
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Significance — the documentary record of Kepler's arrival to Tycho. This letter, the same-day companion of Tycho's first invitation to Kepler (brahe-t_16000126002, Benátky, 26 January 1600), thanks Hoffmann for bringing Kepler to Bohemia in his retinue. The two letters interlock: the "servants returning hither from Prague yesterday evening" who reported Hoffmann's return are the same who brought Kepler's letter in the Kepler-invitation; and both letters announce the dispatch of Franz Tengnagel and Tycho's elder son to Prague (in whose carriage Tycho invites Kepler to ride back to Benátky). Together they fix the convergence of Tycho and Kepler in the opening weeks of 1600.
  • Recipient. Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, Freiherr von Grünbüchel und Strechau (1555–1608) — Landeshauptmann (Prefect) of the Duchy of Styria, hereditary Marshal of the Archduchy of Austria, Burgrave in Steyr, Imperial Aulic Councillor, and First Chamberlain of Archduke Maximilian; a Protestant magnate and patron of learning at whose Prague house Kepler first lodged. The full titulature is reproduced.
  • Kepler. Excellens Mathematicus M. Johannes Keplerus, Illustrium Styriae Ordinum Mathematicus = Johannes Kepler, then mathematician to the Estates of Styria (the post he was losing as the Counter-Reformation drove him from Graz). Tycho records that he and Kepler had a "most pleasing familiarity by letters since I emigrated from Denmark," soon to be increased "by mutual presence" — the prelude to their collaboration. The whole favour of the meeting Tycho credits "in chief part" to Hoffmann.
  • The intermediaries. Franciscus Tengnaglius = Franz Tengnagel, "a few weeks ago returned from Italy" (cf. brahe-t_15991104001, brahe-t_16000504001/002); filius meus natu maior ex me denominatus = Tycho's elder son, Tycho Brahe the Younger. Both are sent to Prague to carry this letter and transact "certain business," and to greet Hoffmann.
  • The summer-1599 meeting. ex pauculo … superiori aestate … congressu — Tycho and Hoffmann had briefly met the previous summer (1599); Tycho builds on that acquaintance.
  • Persons named. Hoffmann; Johannes Kepler; Franz Tengnagel; Tycho the Younger; Archduke Maximilian (in the titulature); Joseph Scaliger (in the dateline jest).
  • Calendar / chronology jest. Dateline die 26 Ianuarij Anni labentis Epochae Christianae Dionysiacae 1600, quam duobus deficere Iosephus Scaliger probare nititur — "of the passing year of the Christian Dionysian Epoch 1600 [the Anno Domini reckoning of Dionysius Exiguus], which Joseph Scaliger strives to prove deficient by two [years]." The same Scaliger chronology jest Tycho makes in brahe-t_16000327002 (where the year is "really 1602"). New Style (imperial). Annus Jubilaeus = the Holy/Jubilee Year 1600. Header date matches the label.
