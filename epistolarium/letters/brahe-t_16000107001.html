The original

S. CLARISSIME ET ERUDITISSIME DOMINE BARTHOLOMÆE SCULTETE VETERI AMICITIA MIHI IUNCTISSIME.

ACCESSUM meum in Bohemiam, quod a Caesâ Mate hîc Clementissime receptus sim, tibi iam antea innotuisse, ex Domino Doctore SCHWALMIO communi nostro amico, paulo postquam Pragam adveneram, percepi. Rediens enim ille ex Silesiâ attulit schedam quandam memorialem tuâ manu exaratam, in qua Progymnasmatum meorum tomum primum tecum communicari rogabas: ac responsum ad meas, quas idem tibi tradidit, nullum reportavit, uti neque ad illas, quas Wittebergâ ad te dedi, rescribere voluisti, traditâ saltem tabellario chartulâ, quae probaret, eum meas exhibuisse. Quî vero fiat, quod in respondendo tam difficilis sis, equidem ignoro. Si curae tam publicae quam privatae impedimento sunt, scias me non minoribus distineri, et tamen haud intermittere, quin amicis scribam. Sed si in posterum hanc desidiam emendâris, erit veniae locus.

Progymnasmata desiderata mittere necdum potui, quia primus quaternio nondum impressus est, uti neque Conclusio; et deficiunt alias in primo capite sex quaterniones de motu ☾ae, quos Hamburgi quidem typis imprimi mandavi; sed in absentiâ meâ adeo depravate id factum est, ut eos recudere oporteat. Postquam vero in Bohemiam concessi, nihil eiuscemodi hucusque praestare licuit, partim ob pestem Pragae aliquamdiu grassantem, quodque isthic typographias numeris et figuris Astronomicis aptas non habeant, partim quod propriam meam Magdaeburgi unâ cum quibusdam ex meis Instrumentis atque supellectile domesticâ, interea dum ego in Bohemiam tendo, relictam ante hyemem me obtenturum frustra crediderim. Mandârat siquidem Caesa Matas per literas Magistratui eius loci, ut res meas omnes isthic conservatas mature huc in Bohemiam per Albim transvehi procuraret. Verum dum isthic moras nectunt, et nescio quae subterfugia quaerunt, superveniens hyemis asperitas id ante ver instans fieri posse vetuit. Sicque tam illud opus, quam alia a me inchoata et necdum typis plane consummata absolvere, postquam huc veni, non est concessum. Interim tamen in hac Arce Caesaris Benaticâ structurae pro Instrumentis meis Astronomicis ordine et sigillatim disponendis iussu eius Matis confectae sunt, in quarum aliquas ea, quae mecum attuli, iam disposita sunt, donec coetera, quibus relicta sunt convenientia loca, advenerint. Laboratorium etiam Spagyricum pro Pyronomicis meis exercitijs, quibus unâ delector, paratum est; satisque commode in hac Arce, quam tribus alijs pariter oblatis praetuli, cum meis habito; et de necessarijs a Caesâ Mate mihi liberaliter, quantum haec ferunt difficilia tempora, provisum. Absum autem hîc a vestro Gorlitzio vix plus itinere duorum dierum, aestivo praesertim tempore, ut facilior et crebrior (modo sic volueris) literarum inter nos commutatio fieri possit. Et si me aliquando hîc invisere tibi grave non fuerit, advenies non tam exoptatissimus hospes, quam Amicus antiquus nunquam antiquandus, sed perpetuo mihi futurus charissimus.

Quoniam vero intellexi Gorlitzij apud vos esse excultas Typographias, quae stylorum formâ et varietate praepolleant, simulque moletrinas papyro conficiendae idoneas ibi reperiri, cuperem a te resciscere, quali precio quaternio impressus in formâ papyri maiusculâ, quam Regalem vocant, ecudatur, idque in quarto, ita ut munda et nitida sit papyrus, typi vero partim ex cursivâ maiusculâ, partim minori et antiquâ mediocri, incidentibus nonnunquam quibusdam tabulis et figuris Geometricis, ita ut Exemplaria sint 1500, et quot quaterniones singulis hebdomadibus absolvi queant, quidque correctori idoneo numerandum. Ut vero mentem meam circa haec rectius percipias, apposui unum vel alterum quaternionem, formam papyri atque impressionis rationem ostendentem. Coetera per temetipsum facile capies atque dispones. Da itaque operam, amantissime SCULTETE, ut votis meis hac in parte respondeat eventus, idque mature resciscam, quo Exemplaria unâ cum pecuniâ necessariâ, quam tuae fidei committam, isthuc tempestive transmittere queam. Quae illuc primo quoque tempore labore typographico compleri averem, haec ferme sunt. In volumine primo Progymnasmatum instaurationis Astronomicae dimidium circiter Alphabetum hinc inde desideratur; ideoque quam citissime fieri queat, convenientibus locis supplendum. In libro illo de Cometâ Anni 77, quem dudum a me obtinuisti, subiunxi postea Apologetica quaedam scripta, quae Hamburgi quidem impressa sunt; sed refutatio cuiusdam Eruditi in Scotiâ viri, qui contra eiusdem libri praecipua contenta nonnulla inferre praesumpsit, necdum addita, quae forte quaterniones 16 adimplere poterit. Praeterea librum secundum Epistolarum Astronomicarum e nostris, quem paulo antequam Daniâ excessi, typis proprijs excudi inchoâram. Sed solummodo absoluto dimidio Alphabeto mihi inde in Germaniam emigrandum erat, sicque opera illa ob varias intercidentes irrequietudines intermissa est. Restant duo circiter Alphabeta plus minus typis pertexenda, idque totum minori illâ antiquâ, qualem primus Epistolarum tomus iam antea editus (quem proculdubio habes) exhibet.

Hos inquam libros iam antea typis nostris inceptos isthic apud vos ad Colophonem opere Typographico quam celerrime diduci posse, ut hoc Anno Iubilaeo publici iuris fierent, plurimum exoptarem. Fac igitur me de singulis huc pertinentibus per primam quamque occasionem (siquidem proprijs typis id nunc fieri nequeat) certiorem, et cum typographis vestris atque papyri confectoribus, prout aequum fuerit, transige, et quid ad minimum exigant, significa. Non poterunt mihi facile in hoc utroque negocio imponere, cum ipsemet utrumque, tam quoad typographicas impensas, quam papyri praeparationem in Insulâ olim Uraniburgicâ diu multumque proprijs sumptibus exercere curârim. Confido autem, sic persuadente veteri inter nos necessitudine, te desiderijs meis hac in parte non defuturum. Feceris sic non saltem mihi rem gratam et amicitiâ nostrâ haud indignam, sed et Divinae illius Astronomiae cultoribus inprimis acceptam nec (uti spero) inutilem. Cumque tu non postremum inter eos locum obtineas, te sponte tuâ rem omnem promoturum, nihil addubito. Sicque eo citius et promptius Exemplaria Operum meorum, quae requiris, nanciscêre.

Vale et actutum rescribe, meque de singulis diligenter admone. Opto tibi cum omnibus tuis hunc praesentem Annum Iubilaeum multosque alios instantes auspicatos et salutares. Iterumque vale. Dabantur ex Arce Benaticâ 5 milliaribus Pragâ versus Borrhapelioten remotâ Anno Epochae Christianae Dionysiacae 1600, quem tamen duobus Annis deficere IOSEPHUS SCALIGER in renovato Opere de Emendatione temporum (uti prius unico) contendit, Ianuarij die 7° stylo renovato. Tu, si potes (uti forte potes, siquidem in similibus diligenter versatus) hanc litem dirime.

Greeting. Most renowned and most learned Lord Bartholomäus Scultetus, most closely joined to me by old friendship.

That I had come into Bohemia, and was here most clemently received by the Imperial Majesty, had — I perceived, a little after I reached Prague — already become known to you, through Doctor Schwalm, our common friend. For he, returning from Silesia, brought a certain memorial-note written in your hand, in which you asked that the first volume of my Progymnasmata be shared with you; but he brought back no answer to mine, which he had delivered to you — just as you were unwilling to write back to those I sent you from Wittenberg, [or] at least to give the courier a slip proving that he had presented mine. Why it comes about that you are so difficult in replying, I do not know. If public and private cares are a hindrance, know that I am held by no smaller ones, and yet do not fail to write to my friends. But if hereafter you mend this slackness, there will be room for pardon.

The Progymnasmata you desire I have not yet been able to send, because the first quaternion is not yet printed, nor the Conclusion; and there are also wanting, in the first chapter, six quaternions on the motion of the Moon [☾], which I had ordered printed at Hamburg — but in my absence it was done so corruptly that they must be reprinted. After I withdrew into Bohemia, I have hitherto been able to do nothing of the kind — partly on account of the plague raging some while at Prague, and because there they have no printing-houses fit for astronomical numerals and figures; partly because I had vainly believed I should obtain, before winter, my own [press], left at Magdeburg together with some of my Instruments and household goods while I made for Bohemia. For the Imperial Majesty had by letter ordered the Magistrate of that place to see all my things kept there transported in good time hither into Bohemia by the Elbe; but while they weave delays there, and seek I know not what subterfuges, the oncoming harshness of winter forbade it could be done before the coming spring. And so neither that work, nor others begun by me and not yet quite finished in print, has it been granted me to complete since I came hither.

Meanwhile, however, in this imperial Castle of Benátky, structures have been made, by His Majesty's order, for arranging my Astronomical Instruments in order and severally; in some of which what I brought with me is already placed, until the rest — for which fit places are left — shall arrive. A Spagyric [alchemical] Laboratory, too, for my Pyronomic exercises, in which I likewise delight, has been prepared; and I dwell comfortably enough, with my [household], in this Castle — which I preferred to three others likewise offered; and I have been liberally provided by the Imperial Majesty with necessaries, so far as these difficult times allow. I am here scarcely more than a two-days' journey from your Görlitz (especially in summer-time), so that an easier and more frequent exchange of letters between us can be made (if only you so wish). And if it be not a burden to you to visit me here sometime, you will come not so much a most longed-for guest as an old Friend, never to grow old [to me], but to be forever most dear.

But since I have learned that at Görlitz, with you, there are excellent printing-houses, pre-eminent in the form and variety of their types, and likewise paper-mills fit for making paper: I should wish to learn from you at what price a quaternion is printed in the larger paper-format which they call Royal — and that in quarto — so that the paper be clean and bright, the types partly of the larger cursive, partly of the smaller and middling antique, with certain Geometric tables and figures sometimes falling in; so that there be 1500 copies; and how many quaternions can be finished each week; and what is to be paid a fit corrector. That you may the more rightly grasp my mind about these, I have appended one or two quaternions, showing the paper-format and the manner of printing. The rest you will easily take in and arrange by yourself. Take care, then, most loving Scultetus, that the outcome answer my wishes in this part, and that I learn it in good time, that I may seasonably send thither the copies, together with the necessary money, which I will commit to your trust.

What I should wish to have completed there, at the first opportunity, by the printer's labour, are about these: In the first volume of the Progymnasmata of the Astronomical Restoration, about half an alphabet [of gatherings] is wanting here and there, and so is to be supplied, in fit places, as quickly as can be. In that book on the Comet of the year [15]77 — which you obtained from me long ago — I afterward subjoined certain Apologetic writings, which were indeed printed at Hamburg; but the refutation of a certain Erudite man in Scotland, who presumed to bring some things against the chief contents of that book, is not yet added — which may perhaps fill 16 quaternions. Besides, the second volume of our Astronomical Letters, which a little before I left Denmark I had begun to print with my own types — but, only half an alphabet finished, I had to emigrate thence into Germany, and so that work was interrupted by various intervening unrests. There remain about two alphabets, more or less, to be woven out in type, and that whole [in] the smaller antique [type], such as the first volume of the Letters, already published (which you doubtless have), exhibits.

These books, I say — already begun in our types — I should most greatly wish could be drawn out there with you, by the printer's work, to the Colophon as swiftly as possible, that they might be made public in this Jubilee Year [1600]. Make me, then, informed of every particular pertaining hither, by the first occasion (since it cannot now be done with my own types); and transact with your printers and papermakers, as shall be fair, and signify what they demand at the least. They will not easily be able to impose on me in either business, since I myself took care to practise both — as much the printing costs as the preparation of paper — long and much, at my own expense, on the once-Uraniborgian Island. And I trust — the old bond between us so persuading — that you will not fail my wishes in this part. So you will do a thing not only welcome to me, and not unworthy of our friendship, but especially acceptable, and (as I hope) not useless, to the cultivators of that divine Astronomy. And since you hold no last place among them, I in no way doubt that you will of your own accord promote the whole matter. And so the more quickly and readily will you obtain the copies of my Works which you require.

Farewell, and write back at once, and admonish me diligently of every particular. I wish you, with all yours, this present Jubilee Year, and many others to come, auspicious and wholesome. And again farewell. Given from the Castle of Benátky, 5 miles from Prague toward the north-east [Borrhapeliotes], in the year of the Christian Dionysian Era 1600 — which, however, Joseph Scaliger, in his renewed work On the Emendation of Times, contends to be deficient by two years (as before by one) — the 7th day of January, by the renewed [New] style. You, if you can (as perhaps you can, being diligently versed in such things), settle this dispute.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Bartholomäus Scultetus (1540–1614), the Görlitz mathematician, cartographer, and burgomaster — Tycho's old friend (cf. the chronology-jest at brahe-t_16000327002, where he appears with the chronologers).
  • Significance — Tycho's publication program and the bid to print at Görlitz (a key bibliographical document).
  • The state of the Progymnasmata. Vol. I still lacks its first quaternion, the Conclusion, and six quaternions on the lunar motion — the latter printed corruptly at Hamburg in Tycho's absence and needing reprinting (cf. brahe-t_15990922001). "About half an alphabet of gatherings" is wanting throughout.
  • The Görlitz printing proposal. Tycho asks Scultetus to price, at the Görlitz presses and paper-mills, a quaternion in "Royal" large paper, quarto, 1500 copies, with mixed type (large cursive + smaller antique) and geometrical figures — enclosing sample quaternions. He notes he cannot be cheated, having run both press and papermaking himself at Uraniborg.
  • Three works to finish: (1) the Progymnasmata vol. I; (2) the Comet-of-1577 book, to which must still be added the refutation of "a certain Erudite man in Scotland" — i.e. John Craig (the comet dispute; c. 16 quaternions); (3) the Astronomical Letters vol. II, begun on Tycho's own press in Denmark (only half an alphabet done), c. 2 alphabets remaining — all to be "made public in this Jubilee Year 1600."
  • The Benátky establishment. Purpose-built housings for the instruments by imperial order (some already installed); a Spagyric (alchemical) laboratory for Tycho's "Pyronomic exercises" — a vivid record of Tycho's chemistry continuing at Benátky; the castle chosen over three others; c. 2 days from Görlitz.
  • The Magdeburg-press delay (cf. brahe-t_16000405001, brahe-t_16000124001): his own press and goods stuck at Magdeburg, the city "weaving delays," winter blocking the Elbe.
  • The Scaliger chronology dispute. The dateline again raises Scaliger's De emendatione temporum claim that the era is "deficient by two years (as before by one)" — and challenges the chronologist Scultetus to "settle this dispute." (Same controversy as brahe-t_16000327002.)
  • Persons & places named. Bartholomäus Scultetus; Dr. Schwalm (common friend); John Craig ("a certain Erudite man in Scotland"); Joseph Scaliger (De emendatione temporum); Rudolf II. Places: Benátky ("5 miles NE of Prague"), Prague, Görlitz, Hamburg, Magdeburg, the Elbe, Silesia, Wittenberg, Uraniborg.
  • Notable terms. Spagyricum laboratorium / Pyronomica exercitia — alchemical/chemical (fire-based) work. Borrhapeliotes — the north-east (Greek wind-name; Benátky lies NE of Prague). Alphabetum — a printer's run of gatherings (A–Z); "half an alphabet" ≈ 11–12 gatherings.
  • Calendar. Ianuarij die 7° stylo renovato → 7 January 1600 [N.S.] (identifier 16000107001 agrees; the label carries only "Benatky"). The "Christian Dionysian Era" formula and the Scaliger two-year jest are Tycho's chronological flourish.
  • Correction. Latin: 'exaratum' corrected to 'exaratam' (TBOO VIII p.234), restoring grammatical agreement with the feminine accusative 'schedam'.
