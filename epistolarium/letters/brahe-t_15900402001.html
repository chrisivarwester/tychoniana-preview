The original

NOBILISSIMO ET AMPLISSIMO VIRO, VIRTUTE ET ERUDITIONE IMPRIMIS CONSPICUO, DOMINO HENRICO RANZOVIO, DOMINO DE BREITENBERGA ET REGIO IN HOLSATIA AC DITHMARSIA GUBERNATORI ETC. AMICO SUO PLURIMUM COLENDO.

SERIUS ego quidem, Nobilissime mi RANZOVI, tuis iam respondeo literis, non tam mea culpa, quam quod de Insignibus Sturorum certior interea temporis fieri nequiverim. Licet enim diligentissime hincinde per sororem quoque meam SOPHIAM a Domina GURUILDA, relicta D. LAGONIS BRAHE vidua, id expiscari attentârim, votis tamen nostris nondum est satisfactum, ut et adiunctæ indicabunt literæ. Accessit et hoc, quod septimanis præteritis hospitum, qui me invisebant, multitudine sim præpeditus, quo minus, uti volebam, tuæ petitioni satisfacere vacârit. Ne vero adhuc te sine responso diutius detineam, non amplius rescriptione abstinendum duxi, licet nihil, quod ad Insignia illa attinet, adhuc compertum habeam. Ubi tamen aut ex Svecia vel aliunde rem ipsam cognovero, curabo, ut resciscas. Quod ad figuras libris præfigendas attinet, eas Buxo solummodo insculptas expeto, neque enim Typographus meus cupreus aptare novit. Typi WOLFFIANI meis non correspondent, præterea eos carius æstimat atque Vitebergenses, ubi aptiores veneunt 20 floren. in centenar. ut de ijs iam plura agere non sit necessum. Et ut rem ipsam dicam, non admodum mihi stylo nunc est opus, donec Chartopæum denuo acquisivero. Is enim, quem ad me misisti, Rostochium pro socio et veteramentis conquirendis ablegatus, acceptis præter voluntatem meam a D. BRUCÆO sex Ioachimicis, Wismariam, illic se veteramenta collecturum simulans, abijt, posteaque veterator iste se subduxit. Idem quoque mihi accidit cum priore, qui a IOACHIMO BUCK Lubecensi pecuniam nactus nusquam deinceps comparuit. Scripsi lamdudum tibi de ijs, quæ a me tum temporis, cum Calendarium Ranzovianum misisti, expetebas, sed colligo ex literis tuis, meas ad manus tuas non pervenisse. Ubi interea deliteant, ignoro. Plura per commodum otium scribam et conabor, quantum in me est, ut Insignium istorum Sturianorum delineatione potiaris. Vale, meaque, uti facis, promovere stude. Dabo vicissim operam, ut quæ tibi accepta cognovero, ubi potuero, fiant.

Iterum vale.
Datæ Uraniburgo 2. Aprilis Anno 90.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most distinguished man, conspicuous above all for virtue and erudition, Master Henrik Rantzau, lord of Breitenburg and royal governor in Holstein and Dithmarschen, etc., his friend much to be honoured.

Rather late, indeed, my most noble Rantzau, do I now reply to your letter — not so much through my own fault as because in the meantime I have been unable to gain surer knowledge concerning the arms of the Stures. For although I have most diligently attempted to fish that out from here and there — through my sister Sophia too, from the Lady Gørvel, the widow left by Master Lage Brahe — our wishes have nonetheless not yet been satisfied, as the enclosed letter[s] will also show. There was added this too, that in the past weeks I have been hindered by the multitude of guests who were visiting me, so that I had less leisure than I wished to satisfy your request. But lest I keep you still longer without a reply, I judged that I ought not to abstain from writing any further, although as yet I have learned nothing that bears upon those arms. Whenever, however, I shall have learned the matter itself, whether from Sweden or from elsewhere, I shall see to it that you are informed. As regards the figures to be set at the front of the books, I want them engraved in boxwood only, for my typographer does not know how to fit copper [plates]. The Wolffian types do not match mine; besides, he prices them more dearly than the Wittenberg ones, where more suitable ones are sold at 20 florins the hundredweight — so that there is no need to deal further with these matters now. And to speak the matter plainly, I have no great need of a graving-tool [stylus] at present, until I shall have acquired a paper-maker again. For the one whom you sent to me, having been dispatched to Rostock to seek out a partner and old materials, and having received — against my will — six Joachim-dollars from Master Brucaeus, went off to Wismar, pretending that he would collect old materials there, and afterwards that old rogue stole away. The same thing also befell me with the earlier one, who, having got money from Joachim Buck of Lübeck, never afterwards appeared anywhere. I wrote to you long ago about those things which you were requesting from me at the time when you sent the Rantzovian Calendar, but I gather from your letter that mine did not reach your hands. Where they lie hidden meanwhile, I do not know. I shall write more at convenient leisure, and shall endeavour, so far as in me lies, that you may obtain a drawing of those arms of the Stures. Farewell, and continue, as you do, to advance my interests. I in turn shall give my effort that whatever I learn is welcome to you may, when I can, be brought about.

Again, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 2 April, the year [15]90.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

[Postscript:] The letters which I have enclosed for the most illustrious Prince the Landgrave — if you would see to it that they are sent to Kassel, and the other one toward Prague — you will do a most welcome thing. And then likewise those that are to be directed to Lübeck and Hamburg.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe at Uraniborg → Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich (Henrik) Rantzau (1526–1598), lord of Breitenburg and royal Statthalter (governor / viceroy) of Schleswig-Holstein and Dithmarschen on behalf of the Danish crown — the great humanist patron, book-collector and astrological enthusiast with whom Tycho corresponded extensively. The address-formula's full titulature (Domino de Breitenberga et regio … gubernatori) is reproduced in full by the edition's convention/.
  • Insignia Sturorum / Sturianorum — "the arms of the Stures," the heraldic coat of arms of the Swedish noble Sture family, on which Rantzau (an avid genealogist and armorial collector) had asked Tycho to procure information. Rendered "arms"; the heraldic, not military, sense. Tycho's answer is throughout a hedged non-result: he has nihil … adhuc compertum ("learned nothing as yet"), preserved as such — no sharpening to a positive finding.
  • soror mea Sophia = Sophie (Sophia) Brahe (1559–1643), Tycho's learned younger sister, here acting as his intermediary in the genealogical inquiry.
  • Domina Guruilda, relicta D. Lagonis Brahe vidua = the Lady Gørvel — Gørvel Faddersdatter Sparre — described as the widow left by Master Lage Brahe. Relicta … vidua is pleonastic in the Latin ("the widow left behind by"); rendered "the widow left by Master Lage Brahe" without trimming the doubling.
  • expiscari — literally "to fish out," kept as the live metaphor ("fish that out").
  • adiunctae … literae — "the enclosed letter[s]." Latin literae is formally plural but regularly denotes a single letter; number left as the Latin gives it, with the alternative flagged here rather than silently singularised.
  • Printing matter. figuras libris praefigendas … Buxo … insculptas = woodcut figures (illustrations/plates) to be set at the front of books, "engraved in boxwood only" — i.e. woodblocks, buxus being the standard fine engraving-wood. Typographus meus cupreus aptare novit: Tycho's own printer at Uraniborg "does not know how to fit copper [plates]" — i.e. cannot execute copperplate (intaglio) engraving. aptare = "to fit/mount," kept literal.
  • Typi Wolffiani — "the Wolffian types," i.e. printing type from a founder/supplier named Wolff (most plausibly the Hamburg/Lübeck-area trade Rantzau had access to); identification not secured, so the epithet is kept as an adjective and flagged. eos carius aestimat atque Vitebergenses = "he prices them more dearly than the Wittenberg ones." 20 floren. in centenar. = "20 florins the hundredweight (per centenarius)," a price-by-weight for cast type; kept literal.
  • stylo … non est opus … donec Chartopaeum … acquisivero — stylus here is the engraver's/graving tool (the means of producing the figurae), not handwriting style; rendered "graving-tool [stylus]" with the Latin flagged, since the bracketing of cause (no engraver) and effect (no paper-maker) shows Tycho means the physical apparatus of book-production. Chartopaeus (Gk. χαρτοποιός) = paper-maker; the Uraniborg paper-mill's workman.
  • The two absconding paper-makers. Is … Rostochium pro socio et veteramentis conquirendis ablegatus … sex Ioachimicis … Wismariam … abijt … veterator iste se subduxit: the paper-maker Rantzau had sent, dispatched to Rostock to recruit a partner and gather veteramenta (old materials — i.e. rags/old paper-stock for pulp), pocketed six Joachim-dollars (Ioachimici = Joachimsthaler) from Brucaeus against Tycho's will, decamped to Wismar, and absconded. veterator iste ("that old rogue") puns on veteramenta; the wordplay (old-stock / old-hand-at-trickery) is noted, the insult rendered "that old rogue." D. Brucaeo = Heinrich Brucaeus (1531–1593), professor of medicine and mathematics at Rostock, Tycho's correspondent there. Idem … cum priore, qui a Ioachimo Buck Lubecensi pecuniam nactus: the same had happened with an earlier paper-maker, who took money from Joachim Buck of Lübeck and vanished.
  • Calendarium Ranzovianum — the Rantzovian Calendar, Rantzau's own Calendarium (an astrological-computational almanac he compiled and sent to Tycho). Kept as a title.
  • manu propria — "in his own hand," Tycho's autograph subscription; the witness is a chancery/archival register copy at Vienna (ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.25; see the collation note below — the 9737 siglum belongs to the preceding Craig letter), so the manu propria describes the original, not the surviving copy.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgo 2. Aprilis Anno 90 = "Given at Uraniborg, 2 April [15]90" (O.S.; Denmark kept Old Style until 1700). The dateline (2 April 1590) agrees with the edition label; no discrepancy.
  • Greeting / closing formulae. No opening salutem; the letter opens directly after the address. Vale … Iterum vale = "Farewell … Again, farewell". meaque … promovere stude = "continue … to advance my interests," the reciprocal-patronage register kept.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: MS-witness corrected to cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.25 (Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 243, assigns this letter to 10686²⁵; the 9737 siglum belongs to the preceding Craig letter).
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (after "manu propria"), restored from Dreyer VII, p. 243 (dropped by the working transcription).
