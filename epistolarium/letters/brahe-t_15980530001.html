The original

THADDÆUS HAGECIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

NOBILISSIME Domine TYCHO et amice observande, scripsi tibi propemodum ante mensem integrum, et cum hucusque nihil rescripseris, nisi forte tardiusculæ ad te pervenerunt meæ, te ipsum exspectabam, exspectoque etiamnum. Dominus Procancellarius CORADUCIUS diebus hisce me allocutus est, et dixit te iam Cæsari de meliori nota commendasse, modo ipse venias, et te Cæsari præsentes; in hoc solo cardo rei vertitur.

Aliud, quod scribam, non habeo, nisi ut quamprimum ad nos advoles.

B. V.
Pragæ penult. Maij novo Stylo 1598.
T. THAD.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Thaddaeus Hagecius to Tycho Brahe.

Most noble Master Tycho and esteemed friend, I wrote to you almost a full month ago, and since up to now you have written nothing back — unless perhaps my letters reached you somewhat late — I was awaiting your own self, and await you even now. Master Vice-Chancellor Coraduz addressed me in these days, and said that he had already commended you to the Emperor in the most favourable terms, provided only you come yourself and present yourself to the Emperor: on this alone the hinge of the matter turns.

I have nothing else to write, except that you fly to us as soon as possible.

Fare well.
Prague, the penultimate [day] of May, New Style, 1598.
Yours, Thaddaeus.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Thaddaeus Hagecius (see brahe-t_15981201001) — pressing Tycho to come to Prague in person, the decisive step toward his imperial appointment.
  • Procancellarius Coraducius = Rudolf Coraduz, Imperial Vice-Chancellor. de meliori nota commendasse — "to commend in better/the most favourable terms" (nota = the "mark" or note of recommendation). Flagged: the accusative-infinitive te … commendasse is grammatically open — it could read "that you had [already] commended yourself to the Emperor most favourably." The sense adopted (Coraduz as the one who commended Tycho) is the one the context demands, since Tycho had only to appear; rendered accordingly.
  • cardo rei vertitur — "the hinge of the matter turns," i.e. everything depends on this one point.
  • B. V. = Bene Vale, "Fare well." T. THAD. = Tuus Thaddaeus, "Yours, Thaddaeus."
  • Calendar. penult. Maij novo Stylo = the penultimate day of May, New Style = 30 May 1598; the novo Stylo is explicit (Prague, imperial). Header agrees with the edition label.
