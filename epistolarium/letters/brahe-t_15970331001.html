The original

NOBILISSIMO ET AMPLISSIMO VIRO, VIRTUTE ET ERUDITIONE INPRIMIS CONSPICUO DOMINO HENRICO RANTZOVIO DE BREDENBERGA, PRÆSIDI SEGEBERGENSI ET PRODUCI HOLSATIÆ ET DITMARSIÆ, AMICO ET AFFINI SUO PLURIMUM COLENDO.
S.

NONNULLAS dedi literas ad te ante aliquot septimanas, Nobilissime ac amplissime Domine HENRICE RANZOVI, affinis et amice honorande, inter quas quædam erant tibi aperte tradendæ, quædam vero secreto, ut seorsim solus eas legeres. Confido eas te accepisse, verum responsum ad illas usque huc nondum accepi ullum, quod per hunc tabellarium avide expeto expectoque, si forte aliqua ratione id neglectum est. Vale, et rem de qua tibi scripsi serio agi persuasum habe.

Iterumque vale.
Dabantur Hafniæ 31. Martij Anno 97.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most worthy man, eminent above all for virtue and erudition, Master Heinrich Rantzau of Breitenburg, Governor of Segeberg and Lord-Lieutenant of Holstein and Dithmarschen, his kinsman and friend much to be honoured — greeting.

I sent you several letters some weeks ago, most noble and most worthy Master Heinrich Rantzau, my honoured kinsman and friend, among which some were to be delivered to you openly, but some in secret, so that you alone might read them apart. I am confident that you received them, yet up to now I have received no reply at all to them — which I eagerly seek and await by this courier, in case by some chance it has been neglected. Farewell, and be assured that the matter about which I wrote to you is being pursued in earnest.

And again, farewell.
Given at Copenhagen, 31 March, the year [15]97.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Henricus Rantzovius de Bredenberga = Heinrich Rantzau (Henrik Rantzau, 1526–1598), the great Holstein humanist, statesman, and bibliophile; royal governor (Statthalter) of the Danish king in the duchies. He was a long-standing patron, correspondent, and connection of Tycho's, and is here addressed as affinis — "kinsman / connection by marriage" (the Brahe and Rantzau families were allied; affinis denotes relationship by marriage rather than blood). de Bredenberga = of Breitenburg, his castle and seat on the Stör in Holstein.
  • Titulature. PRAeSIDI SEGEBERGENSI = "Governor of Segeberg" (Rantzau's administrative seat at Segeberg in Holstein). PRODUCI HOLSATIAe ET DITMARSIAe — produci is the dative of produx, a rare/post-classical title here equivalent to provincial governor or lord-lieutenant; rendered "Lord-Lieutenant of Holstein and Dithmarschen." The form is unusual and is flagged rather than normalised to the more common prorex or praeses.
  • Address spelling. The superscription has RANTZOVIO; the body has RANZOVI (vocative, without the medial t). Both spellings of the name are reproduced as printed; no emendation is adopted.
  • aperte … secreto — "openly … in secret": the earlier batch of letters contained both an open portion and a confidential portion meant for Rantzau alone (seorsim solus). The "matter" (rem) pursued serio ("in earnest") is not named in this note — the substance lies in those prior letters, not here.
  • tabellarium — "courier / letter-carrier"; Tycho asks for a reply to be returned by the same bearer of the present note.
  • Salutation / subscription. S. = salutem ("greeting," this chapter's convention). Vale … Iterumque vale = "Farewell … And again, farewell". manu propria = "in his own hand," the autograph subscription formula.
  • Calendar / place. Dabantur Hafniae 31. Martij Anno 97 = "Given at Copenhagen [Hafnia], 31 March, the year [15]97" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label; Anno 97 is the abbreviated year, expanded to [15]97. Dabantur ("were given," epistolary plural/imperfect) rendered "Given."
  • Context (1597). The letter belongs to the months just before Tycho's departure from Denmark: he left Hven in 1597 and quit the kingdom that year, his royal support withdrawn after Frederik II's death. The unanswered "secret" letters and the matter "pursued in earnest" likely touch his deteriorating position and search for new patronage, for which Rantzau was a natural intermediary — but the note itself does not specify this, and the inference is not pressed into the translation.
