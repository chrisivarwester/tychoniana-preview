The original

CLARISSIMO ERUDITISSIMOQUE VIRO D. DOCTORI THADDÆO HAGECIO AB HAYK, MEDICO ET MATHEMATICO EXCELLENTISSIMO, AMICO SUO SINCERE DILECTO.
PRAG.

RARIORES ad te dare literas internunciorum cogit penuria, eruditissime charissimeque D. D. THADDÆE, et locorum quibus disiungimur intercapedo vota nostra plurimum impedit. Nactus itaque iam tandem aliqualem occasionem literas mittendi per Studiosum quendam Silesium me unâ cum quibusdam alijs hîc invisentem, non intermittendum duxi, quin brevibus ad te scriberem. Librum tuum de Nova Stella et Cometis misi iamdudum Francofurtum ad eundem hominem, de quo mihi indicâras, nec dubito te interea temporis eum recepisse. Quod si illum edere adhuc animus est, suaderem ut ea, quae in Cometa anni 77 minus considerate a te proposita sunt, vel supprimas vel emendes. Meum de hoc eodem Cometa iudicium percipies ex eo quem tibi nunc mitto libro, in quo secundus tomus operis nostri de recentioribus aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis continetur. Primus est de Stella Nova exquisitiori diligentia quam antea considerata, quem typis absolvere Gallici privilegij defectus, quod Caesariano adiungere constitui, hactenus impedivit. Nunc vero quoniam ante paucos dies idipsum adeptus sum, nihil obstabit nisi otij oportunitas, quominus eius etiam libri evulgationem adornem. Tertius est de tribus alijs Cometis, qui postmodum nobis illuxerunt et in alto aethere curriculum suum non minus quam ille, de quo liber modo transmissus agit, absolverunt. Quod autem in hoc ipso libro, ubi tua placita in eodem Cometa sub disquisitionem mathematicam voco, a te multoties dissentiri me res ipsa cogat, non est quod sinistrius interpreteris; scis enim me solo veritatis amore et libertate Philosophica idipsum agere, nulloque tua insectandi elevandique animo id a me fieri. In priori libro de Stella Nova tum etiam in tertio, ubi de Cometa anni 80 agitur, oportuniorem invenio commoditatem tecum magna ex parte sentiendi, tuaque dignis encomijs extollendi. Si quos habes Italicos, Hispanicos vel Gallicos authores, qui de Nova Stella aliquid commentati sunt, rogo ut eorum scripta Latinitate, si fieri possit, donata, mecum communices, unâque si quos alios Cometarum praecedentibus aliquot annis visorum descriptores peculiares nec ubique obvios in promtu habueris. Librum illum de Cometa anni 1531 ab APIANO seorsim editum nondum vidi, quemadmodum neque IOHANNIS PENAe Galli scriptum, quod PRÆTORIUS in descriptione huius Cometae anni 77 citat. Si horum me participem reddideris, meis votis plurimum subveneris. Memini me librum quendam Astrologicum a te dono accepisse, cum Ratisbonae simul essemus, qui Planetarum influentias peculiari quadam ratione per accessum ad Terram vel ab ea recessum proponeret; eum in patriam reversus nuspiam invenire potui. Si eius aliquod exemplar denuo per te obtinuere, diligentius illud inposterum conservabo, evolvamque accuratius; existimo enim quaedam in eo a veritate non dissentanea contineri, si dextre considerentur, et cum caeteris requisitis debite conferantur.

Ubi totum opus, de quo supra dixi, typis meis excusum fuerit, tibi illud transmittam. Interea hanc secundam partem per otium evolve, mihique tuum de ea iudicium ingenue significa. Invenies in eo novam orbium coelestium dispositionem, a me non ita dudum adinventam, quae quo penitius fuerit considerata, eo magis tibi placebit. Ego hanc non solum apparentijs consonam, sed etiam in rei veritate ita se habere non dubito, quod Ptolomaicae et Copernianae assumtiones nequaquam praestant. Sed de his nunc plura scribere temporis penuria et epistolae leges prohibent. Tu itaque, optime THADDÆE, quam felicissime vale et ingravescentis aetatis difficultates forti animo tolera, meque per occasionem quamcunque oblatam literis revisere non intermitte.

Iterum vale.

Datae Uraniburgi die 3. Maij Anno 1588.
Tuus
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned man, Master Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius of Hájek, most excellent physician and mathematician, his sincerely beloved friend.
Prague.

A scarcity of message-bearers compels me to send you letters too rarely, most learned and most dear Master Doctor Thaddaeus, and the distance of the places that separate us greatly hinders our wishes. Having therefore at last obtained some opportunity of sending a letter, by way of a certain Silesian student who is visiting me here together with some others, I thought it not to be neglected, but that I should write to you briefly. Your book on the New Star and on Comets I sent long ago to Frankfurt, to the same man of whom you had told me, and I do not doubt that you have received it in the meantime. But if you still have a mind to publish it, I would advise that those things which you proposed somewhat less carefully concerning the comet of the year [15]77 you either suppress or amend. My own judgement on this same comet you will gather from the book I now send you, in which is contained the second volume of our work on the more recent phenomena of the aethereal World. The first [volume] is on the New Star, examined with more exquisite diligence than before; and the lack of the French privilege — which I have resolved to add to the Imperial one — has hitherto hindered me from completing it in print. But now, since a few days ago I obtained that very thing, nothing will stand in the way, save the opportunity of leisure, of my preparing the publication of that book also. The third [volume] is on three other comets, which afterwards shone forth to us and ran their course in the high aether no less than that one which the book just sent treats of. But as for the fact that in this very book — where I summon your opinions on that same comet under a mathematical examination — the matter itself compels me to dissent from you on many points, there is no reason for you to interpret it amiss; for you know that I do this from love of truth alone and from philosophical liberty, and that it is done by me with no mind of attacking or belittling you. In the earlier book on the New Star, and also in the third, where the comet of the year [15]80 is treated, I find a more convenient occasion of agreeing with you for the most part, and of extolling your work with deserved praises. If you have any Italian, Spanish, or French authors who have written anything concerning the New Star, I ask that you share their writings with me, rendered into Latin if it can be done; and likewise if you have ready at hand any other particular describers of the comets seen in some preceding years, not everywhere to be come by. That book on the comet of the year 1531, published separately by Apian, I have not yet seen, just as neither [have I seen] the writing of the Frenchman Johannes Pena, which Praetorius cites in his description of this comet of the year [15]77. If you make me a partaker of these, you will very greatly assist my wishes. I remember that I received from you, as a gift, a certain astrological book, when we were together at Regensburg, which set forth the influences of the planets in a certain peculiar way, through their approach to the Earth or their recession from it; on returning to my homeland I could nowhere find it. If I might obtain some copy of it again through you, I shall hereafter keep it more carefully, and unfold it more accurately; for I judge that certain things are contained in it not at variance with the truth, if they be rightly considered and duly compared with the other requisites.

When the whole work of which I have spoken above shall have been printed at my press, I will send it to you. Meanwhile, unfold this second part at your leisure, and frankly signify to me your judgement of it. You will find in it a new arrangement of the celestial orbs, devised by me not so long ago, which the more deeply it is considered, the more it will please you. I do not doubt that this is not only consonant with the appearances, but also that it is so in the very truth of the matter — which the Ptolemaic and Copernican assumptions by no means achieve. But to write more of these things now, the scarcity of time and the laws of a letter forbid. Do you therefore, best Thaddaeus, fare most happily, and bear the difficulties of advancing age with a brave spirit, and do not neglect to revisit me with letters on whatever occasion is offered.

Again, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, the 3rd day of May, in the year 1588.
Yours,
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven, to Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayk = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius) (1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer, and imperial physician to Rudolf II at Prague; he and Tycho had observed the 1572 nova and the comets independently and corresponded for decades. The address-superscription (Prag. = Prague, his residence) is preserved.
  • Studiosum quendam Silesium — "a certain Silesian student," the unnamed bearer of the letter; not further identified.
  • The work announced. secundus tomus operis nostri de recentioribus aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis = "the second volume of our work on the more recent phenomena of the aethereal World" — i.e. Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis liber secundus (Uraniborg, 1588), the great treatise on the comet of 1577 in which the geo-heliocentric world-system is first published (see below). The primus … tomus (first volume), on the 1572 Stella Nova, is the projected but then-unprinted volume that eventually became the posthumous Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (1602); the tertius (third volume), on three further comets (those of 1580, 1582, 1585, etc.), was never completed.
  • Stella Nova / Nova Stella — the new star (nova) of 1572 in Cassiopeia. Cometa anni 77 / anni 80 — the comets of 1577 and 1580. Tycho dates by Old-Style civil years; no calendar conversion is implied.
  • novam orbium coelestium dispositionem … a me non ita dudum adinventam — "a new arrangement of the celestial orbs, devised by me not so long ago": Tycho's announcement of his own world-system (the Sun and Moon circling the Earth, the five planets circling the Sun). He claims it not merely apparentijs consonam ("consonant with the appearances") but true in rei veritate ("in the very truth of the matter"), surpassing what the Ptolomaicae et Copernianae assumtiones ("the Ptolemaic and Copernican assumptions") achieve. orbium coelestium rendered "celestial orbs" per glossary (orbis → orb).
  • Privileges. Gallici privilegij … Caesariano adiungere — the French [printing] privilege, which Tycho means to add to the Imperial one (Caesarianum = the privilege granted by the Emperor). He reports having secured the French privilege "a few days ago"; its earlier lack had delayed the printing of the first volume. ("Privilege" here = an exclusive printing licence, not modernised.)
  • Other authors. APIANO = Peter Apian (Petrus Apianus) (1495–1552), whose tract on the comet of 1531 Tycho has not seen. IOHANNIS PENAe Galli = Jean Pena (Johannes Pena) (c. 1528–1558), the French royal mathematician (author of the preface to his Euclid Optica). PRAeTORIUS = Johannes Praetorius (Richter) (1537–1616), professor at Altdorf, cited as referencing Pena in his account of the 1577 comet. Identifications on first occurrence by the edition's convention.
  • librum quendam Astrologicum … cum Ratisbonae simul essemus — "a certain astrological book … when we were together at Regensburg (Ratisbona)": Tycho and Hagecius had met at Regensburg, evidently at the imperial diet/coronation of 1575. The book, treating Planetarum influentias … per accessum ad Terram vel ab ea recessum ("the influences of the planets … through their approach to the Earth or recession from it"), is unidentified; Tycho wishes to recover a copy.
  • Technical / astrological vocabulary. Phaenomena → "phenomena"; Planetarum influentias → "the influences of the planets" (astrological sense kept, not modernised); disquisitionem mathematicam → "mathematical examination." No Greek, planet glyphs, or zodiac glyphs occur in this letter.
  • Epistolary formulae. Opening superscription and Iterum vale ("Again, farewell,") preserved; quam felicissime vale → "fare most happily." libertate Philosophica → "philosophical liberty" — Tycho's standard defence of frank scientific disagreement; the hedge that his criticism carries nullo … insectandi elevandique animo ("no mind of attacking or belittling") is preserved, not softened.
  • Dateline. Datae Uraniburgi die 3. Maij Anno 1588 = "Given at Uraniborg, the 3rd day of May, the year 1588" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The source prints the word Datae broken across a line ("D / atae"); reproduced as printed in the Source block, read as the single word Datae in translation. The header date (3 May 1588) matches both the dateline and the catalogue label; no discrepancy.
  • Correction. Latin corrected 'atae Uraniburgi' to 'Datae Uraniburgi' (Dreyer VII p.121); the closing formula had dropped its initial letter in transmission. English unaffected.
