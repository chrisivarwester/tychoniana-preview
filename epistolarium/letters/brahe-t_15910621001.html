The original

MAGNIFICO DOMINO RECTORI, HEDERLIG OC HØGLERDT MAND, DOCTOR ANDERS CHRISTENSØN, MEDICINAe PROFESSORI VDI KIØBENHAFFNS VNIUERSITET, MIN SYNDERLIG GODE WEN, GANDSKE WENLIGEN TILSCHREFFUIT.

MIN gandske wenlig helsen nu oc altidt forsendt med Wor Herre. Magnifice d:ne rector, oc kiere doctorAndres, synderlig gode wen. Nest min wenlig tacksigelse for alt beuist gode, huilcket ieg aff formuen wil findis willig at forskylde, er min gandske wenlig bøn til eder, at i will wel giøre, oc lade vocire oc fordre for eder, oc andre flere aff eders collegis, hederlige oc høglerde professoribus, eders collegam, hederlig oc høglerdt mand doctor Nielß Crage, oc der till medt ocsaa m. Nielß Colding, om hand er nogen stedts at finde der vdi byen, oc lade dennom i min fogits, wed naffn Christen Judes neruerelse, huilcken ieg der paa fuldmact giffuit haffuer, effter som hand paa mine wegne eskendis worder, bestaa oc bekiende, alt hues de wide sig i sandhed at haffue tildraget den tidt, min tiener Rasmus Persøn, som nu er vdi Gunsøgaard, haffde vdsendt vdi forgangne sommer to mend aff Roschild til mig, borgemester Christen Skyt, oc en anden, wed naffn Peder Swerdtfeger, effterdi de war da her tilstede hoß mig, at de derfor enten samptligen, eller huer i seer for sig, widne och offentlich vdi alle eders neruerelse bekiende oc bestaa saa widt som de wide, at denne handell anrørendis er, oc kunde medt en gode conscientz for Gud oc dennom sielff wide oc bestaa at were i sandhed tilgangen, oc ydermere for øffuerigheden, der som behoff giøris, bekiender.

Oc er her till lougligen steffnt oc kaldit for:ne Rasmus Persen, ocsaasaa at tage forbemelte mend medt sig, om de haffuer noget heri at sige, som i kunde forfare aff den steffning oc kaldsbreff, min fogitt haffuer ladet leßt for hannom, paa huilcken hand oc sielff paaskreffuit haffuer. Oc møder ocsaa der hoß mine kaldtsmend, som haffuer her offuer werit, oc wille bestaa det i eders paahør, enten Rasmus møder sielff, heller oc hans fuldmectige medt de andre to, eller oc icke. Er der for min gandske wenlige bøn til eder, at i wille well giøre, oc lade mig widerfaris hervdj hues billigt oc ret er, ac landzens skick oc brug medbringer. Oc at ieg kand faa alltingst nu strax beskreffuit, oc vnder vniuersitetens segel bekrefftigit. Forseendis mig till eder oc de andre eders collegis, at i hervdi giørendis worder, saa at ieg icke skal haffue mig der vdoffuer at beklage, men tacke eder for eders wmage oc gode wilge vdi alle maade, huilcken ieg oc saa altidt will findis, saa widt mig mugligt kand were, at forskylle gandske godtwillig oc tilgnegt.

Oc will hermedt nu oc altidt haffue eder Gud almectigste befalendis.
Datum Vraniburg den 21. junij anno 1591.
Tyghe Brahe
Egen hand.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the Magnificent Lord Rector, the honourable and most learned man, Doctor Anders Christensen, Professor of Medicine in the University of Copenhagen, my especially good friend — written in all friendliness.

My wholly friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with Our Lord. Magnificent Lord Rector, and dear Doctor Anders, especially good friend. Next after my friendly thanks for all the kindness shown [me] — which I will be found willing to requite according to my means — my wholly friendly request to you is that you would be so good as to summon and call before you, and before several others of your colleagues, the honourable and most learned professors, your colleague, the honourable and most learned man Doctor Niels Krag, and besides him also Master Niels Colding, if he is anywhere to be found there in the town, and to let them, in the presence of my bailiff, by name Christen Jude — to whom I have given full authority [in the matter], according as he is called upon on my behalf — affirm and acknowledge all that they know in truth to have taken place at the time when my servant Rasmus Persøn, who is now at Gunsøgaard, had sent out last summer two men from Roskilde to me, burgomaster Christen Skyt, and another, by name Peder Sverdfeger, inasmuch as they were then present here with me; that they therefore, either jointly or each one separately for himself, witness and publicly, in the presence of you all, acknowledge and affirm so far as they know what concerns this affair, and [what] they can with a good conscience before God and to themselves know and affirm to have truly come to pass, and further acknowledge it before the authorities, where need be.

And the aforesaid Rasmus Persen has herewith been lawfully summoned and cited, [and] also to take the aforementioned men with him, if they have anything to say herein, as you may learn from the summons and writ of citation which my bailiff has had read out to him, [and] upon which he has himself also endorsed [his name]. And [there] appear also there my summoners [witnesses to the citation], who have been over here, and will affirm it in your hearing — whether Rasmus appears himself, or else his attorney with the other two, or not [at all]. Therefore my wholly friendly request to you is that you would be so good as to let me obtain herein what is fair and right, and as the custom and usage of the land brings with it. And that I may get the whole [matter] now at once set down in writing, and confirmed under the University's seal. Trusting to you and the others, your colleagues, that you will act herein in such a way that I shall not have cause to complain of it, but to thank you for your trouble and good will in every way — which I too shall always be found, so far as may be possible for me, to requite, wholly willingly and devotedly.

And herewith I will, now and always, commend you to God Almighty.
Given at Uraniborg, the 21st of June, in the year 1591.
Tyge Brahe
[In] his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Doctor Anders Christensen (Andreas Christiernus / Christierni, 1551–1606), professor of medicine at the University of Copenhagen and, in 1591, its Rector (hence Magnifico Domino Rectori). The letter is addressed to him in that office, asking the consistory (university court) to take sworn testimony.
  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven. The subscription Egen hand ("[in] his own hand") marks the original as an autograph — consistent with witness A being the Originalbrev.
  • Document type. A private letter of legal business, not a chancery register copy: Tycho asks the rector and his colleagues to receive and minute sworn witness-statements under vniuersitetens segel ("under the University's seal"). The matter is a lawsuit between Tycho and his former servant.
  • The Latin editorial regest (Dreyer, Opera Omnia XIV, p. 70, no. 87) identifies the parties: the suit is between Tycho and Erasmus Petri e vico Gundsömagle (= Rasmus Pedersen / Rasmus Persøn of the village of Gundsømagle), and the witnesses sought are doctor Nicolaus Kragius and magister Nicolaus Koldingus. This confirms the persons named in the Danish body.
  • Persons named.
  • Doctor Nielß Crage = Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, c. 1550–1602), humanist, historian and professor at Copenhagen; here a collega of the rector.
  • m. Nielß Colding = Master Niels Colding (Nicolaus Koldingus); the prefix m. = Magister (academic Master, the edition's convention).
  • min fogit … Christen Jude = Christen Jude, Tycho's foged (bailiff/steward) on Hven, given fuldmagt (power of attorney) to act on his behalf; the variant Judes (gen.) of the working transcription matches Dreyer (Iudes).
  • min tiener Rasmus Persøn … nu … vdi Gunsøgaard = Rasmus Pedersen, Tycho's former tiener (servant/retainer), now at Gundsømagle (a village NW of Roskilde, Zealand); the adverse party.
  • borgemester Christen Skyt = Christen Skyt, borgemester (burgomaster) of Roskilde; a witness.
  • Peder Swerdtfeger = Peder Sverdfeger ("Sword-cutler," likely an occupational by-name, not relabelled); the second Roskilde witness.
  • Code-switches (Latin within Danish), preserved by the edition's convention The address-rubric is Latin (MAGNIFICO DOMINO RECTORI … MEDICINAe PROFESSORI …); the body switches into Latin for the academic and legal vocabulary it has no native term for: vocire (← vocare, "to summon/call"), collegis / collegam ("colleagues / colleague"), professoribus ("professors"), conscientz (← conscientia, "conscience"), and the closing Datum ("Given"). These are kept in the Source block and rendered in the Translation.
  • Reading note (working transcription vs. Dreyer). Where the working transcription reads oc fordre ("and demand/cite"), the Dreyer digitised text layer (p. 70, l. 28) reads oc farcire — an evident misreading of a Latin-form verb (citare?) in the same summoning series as vocire. The sense ("summon and cite") is unaffected; the cleaner working reading fordre is followed and Dreyer is recorded. Dreyer's line 39 likewise reads saa widt forn de wide where the working text reads saa widt som de wide ("so far as they know"); the working reading is adopted, the forn/som divergence being an digitised text layer artifact of the long-s.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1591). gandske wenlig(en) = ganske venlig(en) ("wholly friendly"); helsen = hilsen ("greeting"); beuist gode = bevist gode ("kindness shown"); forskylde / forskylle = forskylde ("requite, deserve in return"); eskendis worder = "is called upon / required"; bestaa oc bekiende = "affirm and acknowledge"; lougligen steffnt oc kaldit = lovligen stævnet og kaldt ("lawfully summoned and cited"); kaldsbreff = "writ of citation"; kaldtsmend = "summoners" (here the citation-witnesses); fuldmectige = "attorney / proxy"; billigt oc ret = "fair and right"; landzens skick oc brug = "the custom and usage of the land"; for:ne / forme / forbemelte = fornævnte / forbemeldte ("aforesaid / aforementioned," standard chancery abbreviations); tilgnegt = tilgnegen / tilgiven ("devoted").
  • Negation check. One negation in the body: eller oc icke ("or not [at all]," closing the threefold alternative of how Rasmus may respond to the citation — in person, by proxy, or not at all). It is reproduced once; the polite formula icke skal haffue mig der vdoffuer at beklage ("[that] I shall not have cause to complain of it") contains a second icke, also reproduced. No conditional in the letter inverts a command; the legal force is a private party's bøn (request) to the university court, not an order.
  • Calendar / place. Datum Vraniburg den 21. junij anno 1591 = "Given at Uraniborg, 21 June 1591" (O.S.; Denmark kept Old Style until 1700). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1591. 21. juni. Uraniborg) and with the Dreyer regest (1591, 21. junii) — no date discrepancy.
  • Correction. Corrected the English translation's place-name: Tycho's tenant Rasmus Persøn is said to reside "vdi Gunsøgaard" (Dreyer, TBOO XIV p.56), not at Gundsømagle as previously rendered.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia XIV pp. 55–57; defects the audit noted: mistranslation (1).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.55-58 read as page images. The target letter (no. 87, 1591, 21 junii, Tycho to Rector Anders Christensen) runs on pp.56-57; p.55 holds the end of a Rothmann/Wilhelm letter and p.58 the start of an unrelated 1591 court judgment, both correctly excluded from the transcribed original. Collated the full Danish letter word for word against Dreyer's print: all matches exactly (names Christen Jude, Niels Krag, Niels Colding, Christen Skyt, Peder Sverdfeger, Christiern Urup; the date 21 junii 1591) except the one place-name substitution flagged above, which the transcribed original itself gets right (Gunsøgaard) but the English changes.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). The letter itself names the tenant's current residence as Gunsøgaard, a farm; the English silently substitutes Gundsømagle, the village named only in Dreyer's separate Latin case-summary for the opposing party Erasmus Petri (letter no. 86, above this one). This conflates two distinct place-names belonging to two different people in the same land dispute. At: “my servant Rasmus Persøn, who is now at Gundsømagle”.

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.55-58 (this letter, no. 87, on pp.56-57). Page 56 line 34-35 clearly reads "min tiener Rasmus Perføn, fom nu er vdi Gunføgaard" — confirming the audit's claim that the source and Dreyer both name Gunsøgaard, and that "Gundsømagle" in the prior English was an error. The Latin/Danish the transcribed original already correctly reads "Gunsøgaard" and needed no change; only the English translation was corrected. No other defects found on these pages.
