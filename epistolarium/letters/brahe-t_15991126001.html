The original

ERLLIIG OCH VELBYERDIG MAND THYGGE BRAHE THILL VRANIBORIGH, MINN KIERRE SØNN, GANDSCHE VENLIGEN THILL SKREFFUENN.

KIERRE Thiige och kierre sønn, Gud allmegthiste werre nu och allthid huoß dig och alle dine och beuarre dig naadeligen och vell fra alltt dett, som vntt er. Kierre sønn, haffuer jeg dig paa thet allervenligste bethackitt for din guode skriffuelße, som du skriffuer mig thill, kierre sønn, och villtt wide, huor jeg nu er thill passe, saa er dett ennnu, Gud verre loffuett, gott mett mig. Kierre sønn, saa er ditt breff mig møgitt kiertt att hørre, att du, Gud verre loffuett, er nu kommen thill roulighed, och huis ditt breff er annlangindis, er mig møgitt kiertt, att dig och dine lider alltt vell, Gud verre loffuett; thj Gud vill ingen haffue forgett, som hannom frøckther. Och som din sønn haffuer ochsaa berett for migh, att din leilighed gaar dig løckelig och vell annd, Gud verre loffuet, saa vill jeg, kierre sønn, icke lenger ophollde dig mett denne minn korthe skrifuellße. Du skalltt alle miine dauge finde mig for din hulle maader och will her mett haffue dig Gud allmegthiste beffallendis, dig och alle dine, mett alltt løcke och vellfardt.

Aff Billisgaardt then 26 nouembris anno 99.
Beatte Bille

English translation machine translation, Secure

Honourable and well-born man, Tyge Brahe to Uraniborg, my dear son, written to in all friendliness.

Dear Tyge and dear son, may God Almighty be now and always with you and all yours, and keep you graciously and well from all that is evil. Dear son, I have most kindly thanked you for your good writing, which you write to me; dear son, and [you] would know how I now fare — so it is still, God be praised, well with me. Dear son, so is your letter very dear to me to hear: that you have, God be praised, now come to peace [a settled rest]; and as concerns your letter, it is very dear to me that you and yours are all faring well, God be praised — for God will have none forgotten who fear Him. And as your son has also reported to me, that your situation goes happily and well for you, God be praised — so I will, dear son, not detain you longer with this my short writing. You shall, all my days, find me [to be] your devoted mother; and [I] will herewith have you commended to God Almighty — you and all yours — with all fortune and welfare.

From Billesgård, the 26th of November, in the year [15]99.
Beate Bille.

Apparatus

  • Sender — Tycho's mother. Beate Bille (1526–1605), widow of Otte Brahe and mother of Tycho; writing from Billesgård (her estate in Skåne). A tender maternal letter: she rejoices that Tycho has "come to peace" (kommen til rolighed) and that, as his son had also told her, his "situation goes happily and well" — i.e. she has heard of his secure new position under the Emperor. She signs herself din hulde moder, "your devoted mother."
  • The grandson as messenger. din søn ("your son") who "reported to me" that Tycho's affairs prosper = one of Tycho's sons (most likely Tycho the Younger, then travelling between Bohemia and Denmark; cf. brahe-t_16000329003, …0414002), evidently the bearer of news to his grandmother.
  • Orthography. Late-16th-c. Danish: erlliig (ærlig), velbyerdig (velbyrdig), thill skreffuenn (tilskrevet), allmegthiste (almægtigste), beuarre (bevare), skriffuelße (skrivelse), thill passe (til pas, faring/in health), roulighed (rolighed, peace/quiet), annlangindis (anlangendes, concerning), frøckther (frygter, fears), leilighed (situation), løckelig (lykkelig), hulle maader = hulde moder (devoted mother), beffallendis (befalende, commending). Reading-verified as Danish.
  • Calendar. Billisgaardt then 26 nouembris anno 99 = 26 November 1599; Denmark (Skåne) kept the Julian / Old Style calendar. Header agrees with the label.
