The original

NON SALTEM GENERIS NOBILITATE SED ETIAM INSIGNI DOCTRINA ILLUSTRI VIRO OLIGERO ROSENKRANTZIO, DOMINO DE ROSENHOLM, AMICO, CONSANGVINEO ET AFFINI SUO INTEGERRIMO.

PRÆPROPERUS Patruelis tui hinc ad vos discessus, exoptatissime mi OLIGERE, fecit, ut tibi, prout alias voluissem, tunc non scripserim; concredidi nihilominus illi nonnulla oretenus tibi secreto referenda, quod et præstitum esse, modo tecum fuit, nullus dubito. Cæterum nunc nactus commodiorem oportunitatem, has subsequi volui. Neque tamen eorum, quæ ille viva voce (uti mihi persuadeo) tibi exposuit, quidpiam hîc repetam. Intercessionem Cæsaream pro illo sæpe et diligenter, interea dum abfuit, ursi, nec quidem unquam mihi denegata est a Domino CORRADUCIO, Vicecancellario, sed cum tam multa simul inciderint, gravissime Rempublicam concernentia, semper excusavit se, quod non haberet oportunitatem eiuscemodi particularia proponendi, antequam principaliora et magni momenti negotia expedita essent, et sancte affirmavit, nulla sua negligentia, sed necessitate sic efflagitante, id retardari. Rogavit itaque, ut hanc dilationem patienter ferrem, se nihilominus omnia in meliori forma effectui daturum. Nunc vero ante biduum mihi referri fecit, impetratam iam esse intercessionem illam, atque in secreto consilio, ut scribatur, conclusum. Quare se confestim, ut concipiatur et ad mundum exaretur, effecturum; unde adhuc pauculos dies exspectare oportebit, antequam voti compotes reddemur. Quin et postea aliquot interlabi poterunt, priusquam Cæsarea Maitas sua manu subscripserit, adeo lente procedunt hîc omnia, præsertim hoc tempore, ob periculum a Turca imminens, propter occupatum Canisium et denuo ferme amissam Transylvaniam. In me tamen nulla erit mora, quin, ut negotium hoc mature absolvatur, operam dem; postea tibi exemplum mittam, originale autem mecum reservabo, donec FRIDERICUS redierit, sic enim fieri voluit, si res nimis diu protraherentur.

Habito nunc in ædibus a priore Procancellario Domino IACOBO CURTIO Laudatæ Mem. exstructis et a viduâ relictâ liberalitate Cæsareæ Maitis pro 10000 Ioachimicis in meos usus coëmtis, quæ tamen duplo pluris exædificatori constiterunt. Accepique hîc exoptatam commoditatem pro studijs, observationibus et domesticis rebus debite administrandis; amœnissimus hortus ædes undique circumdat, Muro circumseptus. Constitui vero, favente Numine, in his ipsis ædibus Nuptias filiæ ELIZABETÆ cum Nobili viro FRANCISCO TEGNAGLIO, quem nosti, celebrare aliquo oportuno tempore inter Pascha et Pentecosten; quo nomine ad præcipuos quosdam et maxime singulares in Dania et Holsatia amicos Literas Nuptiales dedi, non quod eos interfuturos sperem, sed saltem ut conscij sint. Aliquos tamen ex intimis propriâ Personâ adventuros, aut saltem quempiam suo loco missuros confido. Cumque te inter sinceriores et integerrimos præcipuo loco reputem, quibus possum verbis, te rogatum velim, ut assumpta coniuge tuâ charissimâ, meâ ex fratre Nepte, tuâ præsentiâ hanc solennitatem augere et condecorare non degraveris. Iter non admodum longinquum, viæ tunc erunt faciles, et sumptus non multum excedent domesticos. Licebit sic in via veteres tuos amicos Wittebergenses iucunde revisere, et hîc aulam Imperatoriam variaque visu digna cognoscere, quæ tibi voluptati et forte etiam aliquando commodo erunt. Adsunt insuper in propinquo binæ thermæ, quas si sanitatis caussa ingredi lubuerit, minimo impendio id unâ fieri poterit. Interea sobolem tenellulam facile et tuto alicui Heroinæ e consangvineis commendabis. Adveniet etiam (uti spero) Soror mea SOPHIA, et ubi adveneritis, tum demum diem certum nuptialem præfiniam, ut et hîc Illustres et præcipui viri, amici et noti invitentur. Rogavi FRIDERICUM, ut serio tecum hac de re ageret, daretque operam, quo persuaderet, licet ego de tua in me fide et constantiâ nihil addubitem. Quare pluribus id contendere nolo. Sed te avide et certo expecto, utque queas nobis salvus adesse. Vale, saluta coniugem et matrem tuam ex me officiosissime.

Datum Pragæ in novo Uraniburgo Cæsareo die 5. Aprilis Anno 1601, Stylo novo.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.

Filius meus TYCHO abijt cum Legatis Persicis per Italiam,
Galliam et Hispaniam transiturus, munitus
salvo conductu Imperatoris. Habes hîc
Effigies duorum præcipuorum ex Legatis, et Cæsaris responsum.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man illustrious not only by nobility of birth but also by distinguished learning, Holger Rosenkrantz, Lord of Rosenholm, his most upright friend, kinsman, and relative.

The over-hasty departure hence to you of your cousin, my most longed-for Holger, made it that I did not then write to you as I should otherwise have wished; nevertheless I entrusted to him some things to be related to you secretly by word of mouth, which (as he was just now with you) I do not doubt was done. But now, having got a more convenient opportunity, I wished these [lines] to follow — yet I will not repeat here anything he set out to you, as I am persuaded, by living voice. The imperial intercession for him I urged often and diligently while he was away, nor was it ever denied me by Lord Coraduz, the Vice-Chancellor; but since so many things befell at once, most gravely concerning the Commonwealth, he always excused himself that he had no opportunity of proposing such particulars before the more principal and weighty matters were dispatched, and solemnly affirmed that it was delayed by no negligence of his, but by pressing necessity. He asked, therefore, that I bear the delay patiently, [saying] he would nonetheless bring everything to effect in a better form. But now, two days ago, he had it reported to me that that intercession has now been obtained, and resolved in the privy council that it be put in writing; wherefore he will at once have it drafted and fair-copied — whence it will be needful to wait yet a few days before we are made masters of our wish. Nay, and afterward several [days] may slip by before the Imperial Majesty has signed it with his own hand — so slowly do all things proceed here, especially at this time, on account of the danger threatening from the Turk, with Kanizsa occupied and Transylvania again nearly lost. Yet in me there will be no delay; I will see that this business is completed in good time; afterward I will send you a copy, but keep the original with me until Frederik returns — for so he wished it done, should the matter be drawn out too long.

I now dwell in the house built by the former Vice-Chancellor Lord Jacob Curtius, of praised memory, and bought from his widow, by the Imperial Majesty's liberality, for 10,000 joachimsthalers for my use — which, however, cost the builder double the price; and I have here a most desired convenience for duly conducting my studies, observations, and domestic affairs, a most pleasant garden surrounding the house on all sides, enclosed by a wall. And I have resolved, God favouring, to celebrate in this very house the wedding of my daughter Elisabeth with the noble man Franz Tengnagel, whom you know, at some convenient time between Easter and Pentecost; on which account I have sent Wedding-Letters to certain chief and most especial friends in Denmark and Holstein — not that I hope they will attend, but only that they may be aware; yet I trust some of my intimates will come in person, or at least send someone in their place. And since I reckon you in a chief place among the more sincere and upright, with what words I can I would ask that, taking your dearest wife — my niece by my brother — you would not find it burdensome to augment and adorn this solemnity by your presence. The journey is not very long, the roads will then be easy, and the costs will not much exceed those at home. You will thus be able, on the way, pleasantly to revisit your old Wittenberg friends, and here to see the imperial court and various things worth seeing, which will be a pleasure — and perhaps sometime an advantage — to you. There are, moreover, two thermal baths nearby, which, if you wish to take for your health, can be done at the same time at very little expense. Meanwhile you will easily and safely commend your tender little child to some noble lady of [your] kin. My sister Sophie, too, will come (as I hope); and when you have [all] come, then at last I will fix the certain wedding-day, that here too illustrious and chief men, friends and acquaintances, may be invited. I have asked Frederik to deal seriously with you on this, and take pains to persuade you — though I doubt nothing of your faith and constancy toward me. Wherefore I will not press it further. But I eagerly and surely await you, [praying] that you may be able to be safely with us. Farewell; greet your wife and your mother from me most dutifully.

Given at Prague, in the new imperial Uraniborg, the 5th day of April, the year 1601, New Style.
Your Tycho Brahe.

My son Tycho has departed with the Persian Legates, about to pass through Italy, France, and Spain, furnished with the Emperor's safe-conduct. Here you have the Portraits of two of the chief among the Legates, and the Emperor's reply.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" of Rosenholm — Tycho's consanguineus et affinis (cf. brahe-t_15980628001, brahe-t_15981231002). The MS is a later Langebek afskrift.
- Significance — the wedding invitation, and the imperial intercession for Frederik Rosenkrantz. Two intertwined matters:
1. The imperial intercession for Frederik Rosenkrantz. Frederik (Holger's patruelis/cousin — the disgraced-then-rehabilitated nobleman now in imperial service; cf. brahe-t_16000926002) had come and gone (carrying secret oral messages). Tycho had repeatedly pressed Coraduz for an imperial letter of intercession on Frederik's behalf (toward his rehabilitation in Denmark); it was always granted in principle but delayed by graver state business, and is now obtained and ordered drafted in the privy council — awaiting only Rudolf II's signature, slow "on account of the danger from the Turk, with Kanizsa occupied and Transylvania nearly lost." Tycho will keep the original until Frederik returns.
2. The wedding of Elisabeth Brahe and Franz Tengnagel (the central occasion). Tycho announces he will celebrate the wedding of his daughter Elisabeth with Franz Tengnagel "in this very house [the Curtius house], between Easter and Pentecost [1601]," having sent Wedding-Letters to chief friends in Denmark and Holstein. He warmly invites Holger and his wife — "my niece by my brother" (Sophie Brahe, daughter of Tycho's brother Axel Brahe — not to be confused with Tycho's sister Sophie, named just below) — promising easy roads, the sights of the imperial court, and the nearby thermal baths; his sister Sophie is also expected, after whose arrival the firm wedding-day will be set. (Parallel to the same-period invitation to Duke Ulrich, brahe-t_16010415003.)
- The Curtius house (cross-ref). The "new imperial Uraniborg": Jacob Curtius's house, bought from his widow by Rudolf II for 10,000 joachimsthalers (though it had cost the builder double), with its walled garden — consonant with brahe-t_16010415002, brahe-t_16010412001, brahe-t_16010410001.
- Persons named. Holger Rosenkrantz and his wife Sophie Brahe (Tycho's niece, Axel Brahe's daughter) and mother; Frederik Rosenkrantz (Holger's cousin, the intercession's beneficiary); Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor); Jacob Curtius (and his widow); Elisabeth Brahe and Franz Tengnagel (the bridal couple); Sophie Brahe (Tycho's sister); Rudolf II.
- Calendar. Dateline die 5. Aprilis Anno 1601, Stylo novo; New Style (Prague). Header agrees with the label.
- What this is. A striking piece of enclosed news and physical enclosures — engraved or drawn portraits ("Effigies") of two leading members of a Persian embassy, plus a copy of Emperor Rudolf II's official reply to that embassy — sent along with this letter, which the published tail confirms is Tycho's invitation to Holger Rosenkrantz for the wedding of his daughter Elisabeth Brahe to Franz Tengnagel.
- Tycho the Younger. "My son Tycho" is Tycho Tygesen Brahe. Christianson's On Tycho's Island records that he travelled to Italy in 1601 to pursue university studies [Christianson, On Tycho's Island (Cambridge, 2000)] — but says nothing there about a Persian embassy. This recovered passage supplies a genuinely new specific detail absent from that standard secondary account: that he travelled in company with a Persian legation, under an imperial safe-conduct, continuing on through France and Spain as well as Italy.
- This exact letter, cross-referenced. Dreyer's biography independently identifies this 5 April 1601 letter to Holger Rosenkrantz as the Latin companion to a Danish-language wedding-invitation Tycho sent the same correspondent the previous day, both concerning the Tengnagel–Elisabeth Brahe marriage [Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (Edinburgh, 1890)]; Thoren's Lord of Uraniborg prints a translation of the companion Danish letter (his "Letter 7," 25 March/4 April 1601) describing Rosenkrantz there as Tycho's "kinsman by birth, brother-in-law" [Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (Cambridge, 1990)].
- The Persian legation — not further identified in the works consulted. No source searched named the specific embassy passing through Prague at this date; contextually this falls in the period of diplomatic contacts between Shah Abbas I of Persia and European courts (the historically well-known Husain Ali Beg mission reached several European capitals around 1600–1601), but this is inference from the general period, not an identification confirmed in the literature, and is flagged accordingly rather than asserted.
