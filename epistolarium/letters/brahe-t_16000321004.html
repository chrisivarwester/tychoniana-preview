The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD BEATAM BILLE].

MIN gandske sønlig och wenlig hilsen nu och althiid forsendt met Vor Herre. Kierre moder, Gud almechtigste werre nu och althiid hoß eder och beware eder naadelligen och well fran alt det, eder kan werre skadelligt enten thill saell eller liff, och nest megen thacksigelße for allt bewist gode, for huilcket y althiid skulle finde mig willig och thienstachtig y alle die maade, ieg kan giørre nogit, som eder thill aere och gode kan werre, som ieg eder ocsaa stedtze plichtig er. Giffuer ieg eder gandske wenligen thillkiende, att eders gode skriffuellse er mig thillhende kommen met min søn Tyge, som kom hid thill mig ygien nogit epther nøttaarsthag y dette aar, och war det mig megit kiert deraff att fornemme, att eder Gudtskeloff lider well. Den samme gode Gudt vnde mig allthiid samme thidenner thill eder att spørge. Och tacker ieg eder derhos gandske gierne for det møgit gode, y bewiste samme min søn den stun, han var hoß eder, och for die penge, y foraerit hannom; hand lod sig derfor giørre itt smugt guldarmbond, som han will forware och berre for eders skyldt den stund han leffuer. Kierre moder, giffuer ieg ocsaa eder wenligen thillkiende, att ieg och mine lider aff Gudtz naade well, och haffuer den samme gode Gud naadelligen och well befrid oss alle fra den store fare, som war her y forgangen sommer och en god part vd paa wintteren intill emod jiulle thiid, met møgen pestelenße och blodsott, och det offuer dette helle kongerige Behemen so vell som y de ner om krind liggendis lanskab, horer och ødremehre end y nogen aff die forgangene aar, so att røm. key. may. motte wige fra Prag, hor hanss may. helst gid werid, hen wdj en anden bøy wedt naffn Pilsen, thi mille der fra ligendis, hor hans maytt. endnu er och kommer neppe thill Prag wdigien føren epther paaske. Samme farlige søgdommer haffde och thagit megit offuerhand y dette herschaff, som ligger her thill Benateck slott, hor ieg bor och holder huß, saa att der wed die thuthussend er affgangen, baade her vdj kiøbstaden, som ligger oppe paa en stor backe nehr vedt slottid, och y de omliggendis landßbøyer, som hid thill hørrer. Och der hanß key. may. fick att wijde, att her war saadan fare paaferre, haffuer hanß may. nogle gange hafft bodt och naadigst ladit forfarit, horledis det haffde sig met mig och mine, thi hanß may. haffde aff sin egen gunst och tillneienhed sønderlig omhu for mig och dennom, och lod mig thuende gange naadigst thillbøde, att dersom ieg wille drage nogen andenstedtz hen y hanß may. land, hor dett war sickerrer end her, om ieg endskønt willde drage bort thill Wien, som er hans may. fornembste hoffuidstad vdj Osterich, hor die forige keysere pleide mesten parten att holde huß, da skulde det stande mig offuen for, och hans maytt. wille der naadigst forskaffe mig god plaß och leylighedt. Men ieg lodt hans maytt. vnderdanigst betaecke, siigendis att ieg helderwille bliffue her och befalle Gud allthingest, huilket hans may. ocsaa lod sig naadigst befalle. Och war so naadigst aff mig begerendis, att ieg wille sende hannom nogen aff den medecin, som ieg brugte emod samme søgdom, och en beskriffning, horledis den skullde beredis, huilket ieg och strax giorde, huilket hanß may. well behagid, efftersom hanß may. ellers er mig met all keiserlige øndist och naade megit well bewogen, so att ieg icke nochsom kan thacke Vor Herre derfor, som haffuer giort mine willkor anderlediß och bedre end die thillforn ware y mitt fernelandt. Och will nu icke widløfftiger schriffue eder thill derom, eptherdj y det thillforn, der min søn var derinde y landit, haffuer en god part deraff fornummit.

Allthingest er endnu vdj samme wesen, och haabis nest Guds hielp, att det enda hereffter kan bliffue bedre, hortill den allmechtigste Gud giffue løcke och benedidellße. Den samme gode Gud vill ieg ocsaa haffue eder till languarendis sundhed, løckke och we[l]fard troligen befalid.

Daterit paa det keyserlige slot Benatick den 21 dag martij epther denne regning, som er den 11 hoß eder, anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TYCHO BRAHE TO BEATE BILLE].

My wholly filial and friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with [the blessing of] Our Lord. Dear mother, may God Almighty be now and always with you, and preserve you graciously and well from all that may harm you, whether in soul or body; and next [after] much thanks for all the kindness shown, for which you shall always find me willing and serviceable in every way I can do anything that may be to your honour and good, as I am ever bound to you. I give you quite amicably to know that your good letter has come to my hand with my son Tyge, who came back to me somewhat after New Year's Day this year; and it was very dear to me to perceive from it that you, God be praised, fare well. May the same good God grant me always to hear the same tidings of you. And I thank you herewith quite gladly for the much kindness you showed my same son while he was with you, and for the money you gave him: he had a pretty gold bracelet made from it, which he will keep and wear for your sake as long as he lives. Dear mother, I also give you amicably to know that I and mine fare, by God's grace, well; and the same good God has graciously delivered us all from the great danger that was here last summer, and a good part into the winter, until toward Christmas-time — with much pestilence and dysentery — and that over this whole kingdom of Bohemia, as well as in the neighbouring lands around, [raging] worse and more than in any past year, so that the Roman Imperial Majesty had to withdraw from Prague (where His Majesty would most gladly have been) to another town named Pilsen, ten miles thence — where His Majesty still is, and will scarcely come to Prague again before Easter. The same dangerous diseases had also greatly taken hold in this lordship, which belongs to Benátky castle, where I live and keep house, so that some two thousand have died — both here in the market-town (which lies up on a great hill near the castle) and in the surrounding villages belonging hereto. And when His Imperial Majesty got to know there was such danger afoot here, His Majesty several times sent a messenger and graciously had it asked how it stood with me and mine — for His Majesty had, of his own favour and inclination, special care for me and them — and twice graciously offered that, if I would move somewhere else in His Majesty's land where it was safer than here — even if I would move away to Vienna, His Majesty's chief capital in Austria, where the former emperors mostly kept house — then it should be at my choice, and His Majesty would there graciously provide me good place and accommodation. But I had His Majesty humbly thanked, saying I would rather stay here and commit everything to God — which His Majesty also graciously accepted. And [His Majesty] was so graciously desirous of me that I would send him some of the medicine I used against the same disease, and a description of how it should be prepared — which I did at once, and which pleased His Majesty well, since His Majesty is otherwise very well disposed toward me with all imperial favour and grace, so that I cannot sufficiently thank Our Lord, who has made my circumstances otherwise and better than they were before in my fatherland. And I will not now write to you more at length about it, since you have before — when my son was there in the country — perceived a good part of it.

Everything is still in the same state, and it is hoped, with God's help, that it may yet grow better hereafter — to which may God Almighty give fortune and blessing. To the same good God I will also faithfully commend you, for long-lasting health, fortune, and welfare.

Dated at the imperial castle Benátky, the 21st day of March by this reckoning, which is the 11th with you, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Beate Bille (1526–1605), Tycho's mother — a noblewoman of the powerful Bille family (and herself a learned woman). The MS is an autograph draft (Kladde). A tender, wholly personal letter (Tycho signs himself in the "filial" register, sønlig hilsen).
  • Significance — a personal/biographical letter of the plague winter 1599–1600.
  • Family warmth. Tycho thanks his mother for kindnesses and money she gave his son Tyge (Tycho the Younger, returned from the Denmark errand "after New Year's"), from which the grandson had a gold bracelet made, "to keep and wear for your sake as long as he lives."
  • The plague (1599). Tycho describes the pestilence and dysentery that ravaged "this whole kingdom of Bohemia" through the summer and into winter — c. 2,000 dead in the Benátky lordship alone (the hill-top market-town and its villages) — which drove Rudolf II from Prague to Pilsen (Plzeň), "ten miles thence," not to return before Easter (cf. brahe-t_16000324001, brahe-t_16000516001).
  • The Emperor's personal care (a vivid datum). Rudolf II twice sent to ask after Tycho's safety and twice offered to relocate him anywhere safer in his lands — even to Vienna, the old imperial capital — providing place and means. Tycho declined, "saying I would rather stay here and commit everything to God"; and at the Emperor's request sent him his own plague-medicine with a recipe — a striking glimpse of the personal bond between Tycho and Rudolf II.
  • Persons named. Beate Bille (Tycho's mother); his son Tyge (Tycho the Younger); Rudolf II.
  • Calendar (this chapter's convention — a model case). The dateline explicitly gives both styles: "den 21 dag Martij efter denne regning, som er den 11 hos eder" — "21 March by this [New Style] reckoning, which is the 11th [Old Style] with you [in Denmark]." Header marked [N.S.], the O.S. equivalent (11 March) preserved exactly by this chapter's convention — Tycho himself bridging the calendars for his Danish mother.
  • Places. Pilsen = Plzeň; Benateck = Benátky; Wien … i Østerrig = Vienna in Austria.
  • Correction. Danish text corrected 'pange' to 'penge' (Dreyer XIV p.175); English unaffected.
