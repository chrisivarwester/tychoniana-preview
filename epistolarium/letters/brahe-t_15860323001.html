The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD HENRICUM RANZOVIUM.

QUOD prioribus tuis litteris, Nobilissime et Eruditissime Domine RANZOVI, nunc quam vel tu expectâras vel ipse proposueram, serius respondeam, effecit Hyemis asperitas, quæ insulam hanc undique glacie circumsæptam, quo minus hinc multo tempore solveretur, præpedivit, dumque interea alijs rebus occupor, facile fit, ut per incuriam mora hæc mihi vel invito obrepuerit, quam ut candide interpreteris etiam atque etiam te rogatum volo. Quantum ad Landgravij petitionem attinet de observationibus Cometæ præterito anno visi, quas a me ipsius Celsitudo desiderat (velut ab exemplari litterarum tibi inscriptarum mihi transmisso cognovi) existimo me ipsius desiderijs pro virili satisfecisse. Dedi enim hoc nomine circa initium huius mensis ad ipsius Celsitudinem litteras et unâ adiunxi compendiosam quandam eius Cometæ ex observationibus nostris descriptionem. Habebam autem primum in animo hæc tibi mittere, ut per commodam opportunitatem tuo iussu D. Landgravio adferrentur. Verum cum interea negotium quoddam in nundinis Francfordensibus expediendum mihi occurreret, quod certum aliquem et proprium nuntium requirebat, non ignarus Cassellas, ubi Principis Landgravij aula est, in ipso itinere versus Francofurtum constitui, uni ex meis domesticis, qui ad nundinas illas meo nomine profecturus erat, ea etiam, quæ D. Landgravio erant perferenda, commisi; verum ut tu etiam conscius reddaris eorum, quæ Principi illi a me communicata sunt, curavi tum epistolam, tum etiam ea, quæ de Cometa illo conscripseram, denuo exarari eorumque singulorum exemplar tibi inspiciendum cognoscendumque mitto. Addidi etiam Diarium quoddam Astrologicum et Metheorologicum huius anni, a quodam meo studiosorum elaboratum et sex alia exemplaria. Motuum ... Solis a nobis restitutum, cuius in litteris ad D. Landgravium datis fit mentio, siquidem is in hoc Diario continetur, non ... iudicium opus esse peculiari carta denotare.

Quantum vero ad posteriores tuas litteras attinet, quas ante triduum accepi, unâ cum D. Decani Coloniensis litteris et quibusdam picturis urbium et aliarum rerum per cupri cælaturam expressis, cuperem equidem ipsius D. Decani votis in mittendis plurium urbium, quas expetit, delineationibus, adiectis etiam rerum illic gestarum annalibus, satisfacere, sed neminem hîc habere possum, qui eas apprime delineare novit. Nec mihi circa rerum Astronomicarum penitiorem inquisitionem gnaviter occupato otium conceditur ad diversa negotia evagandi. Rescribam autem ipsemet Domino Decano, quam primum per otium licuerit, nunc enim præpeditus sum quodam libello Regi nostro conscribendo, quem intra Paschatis festum me absoluturum promisi. Quod si quid interea ad ipsum litterarum dederis, rogo ut moram hanc meam in rescribendo apud ipsum excusare velis; dabo operam, ut prima quaque occasione ipsius expectationi satisfiat. Vale. Datæ Uraniburgi 23. Martij Anno 1586.

TYCHO BRAHE TO HEINRICH RANTZAU.

That I reply to your earlier letter, most noble and most learned Master Rantzau, later now than either you had expected or I myself had proposed, the harshness of the winter has brought about, which — hemming this island in on every side with ice — hindered any passing from here for a long time; and while in the meantime I am taken up with other matters, it easily comes about that through inattention this delay crept up on me even against my will. I ask you again and again to interpret it charitably. As far as concerns the Landgrave's request about the observations of the comet seen this past year, which His Highness desires of me (as I learned from a copy, sent to me, of the letter addressed to you), I judge that I have, to the best of my ability, satisfied his wishes. For on this account, about the beginning of this month, I sent a letter to His Highness, and along with it I added a certain compendious description of that comet drawn from our observations. Now I had at first in mind to send these to you, so that, at a convenient opportunity and at your bidding, they might be conveyed to the Lord Landgrave. But since in the meantime a certain piece of business to be dispatched at the Frankfurt fair came up for me, which required some sure and particular messenger, [and] knowing that Kassel, where the court of the Prince Landgrave is, lies right on the route toward Frankfurt, I entrusted to one of my household, who was about to set out to that fair in my name, those things too that were to be delivered to the Lord Landgrave; but, so that you also may be made privy to the things that have been communicated by me to that Prince, I had both the letter and also the things I had written about that comet copied out anew, and I am sending you a copy of each of them to inspect and learn. I have added too a certain astrological and meteorological diary [almanac] of this year, worked up by one of my students, and six other copies. [...] of the motions of the Sun restored by us, of which mention is made in the letter sent to the Lord Landgrave — since it is contained in this diary — [it is] not [...] there be need to note [it] [judgement?] on a separate sheet.

But as far as concerns your later letter, which I received three days ago, together with the letter of the Lord Dean of Cologne and certain pictures of cities and of other things rendered by copper engraving: I should indeed wish to satisfy the wishes of the Lord Dean himself in sending the drawings of the several cities which he seeks, with the annals of the things done there added as well; but I can have no one here who knows how to draw them aptly. Nor, busily occupied as I am about the deeper investigation of astronomical matters, is leisure granted me to range off into diverse affairs. But I shall myself write back to the Lord Dean as soon as leisure permits; for now I am held up by a certain little book to be written for our King, which I have promised to finish within the feast of Easter. But if in the meantime you should send him any letter, I ask that you be willing to excuse this delay of mine in writing back to him; I shall take pains that at the very first occasion his expectation be satisfied. Farewell. Given at Uraniborg, 23 March, the year 1586.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on the island of Hven.
  • Recipient. Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), royal governor (Statthalter) of the Danish king in the duchies of Schleswig-Holstein, seated at Breitenburg/Wandsbek; humanist, astrologer, and prolific patron and middleman of learned correspondence. He routinely served as an intermediary between Tycho and the Hesse-Kassel court, which this letter exemplifies.
  • Landgravius / Princeps Landgravius / Celsitudo — Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince of Kassel; Celsitudo = "His Highness". His aula (court) is at Kassel (Cassellae;).
  • Cometae praeterito anno visi — "the comet seen this past year": the comet of 1585 (observed at Kassel by Rothmann and at Uraniborg by Tycho). Tycho's "compendious description … from our observations" is the report he had just sent to the Landgrave at the start of March 1586.
  • D. Decanus Coloniensis — "the Lord Dean of Cologne," not further specified in the letter; left as given. He had sent, via Rantzau, copper-engraved city views (picturae urbium … per cupri caelaturam expressae) and sought reciprocal city-drawings with local annals — a topographical-antiquarian exchange of the kind Rantzau brokered. Identification left open (no internal evidence names him).
  • nundinae Francfordenses — the Frankfurt (book) fair, the great clearing-house through which Tycho's household agent travelled, conveniently by way of Kassel.
  • Diarium … Astrologicum et Metheorologicum … a quodam meo studiosorum elaboratum — "an astrological and meteorological diary [almanac] of this year, worked up by one of my students": a calendar/almanac produced under Tycho's roof; studiosus = a student/assistant of Tycho's. Metheorologicum is the source spelling (for meteorologicum), retained.
  • Motuum … Solis a nobis restitutum — "the [...] of the motions of the Sun restored by us": Tycho's restored solar theory (motus Solis restitutus; cf. restitutio / instauratio astronomiae, the edition's convention), to which his own almanac's solar positions were computed. Textual crux: the working transcription carries two ellipses ("Motuum... Solis" and "non... iudicium opus esse peculiari carta denotare") that are not resolvable from the local material — no Dreyer Opera Omnia vol. VII page image was available to collate against. The clause is left lacunose in the translation, with the gaps marked "[...]"; the closing sense is, with low confidence, "[it is] not [necessary] … that there be need to note [it] on a separate sheet" — i.e. the solar tables need not be set out separately because they are already contained in the diary. The reading of iudicium ("judgement," but here perhaps "computation/forecast") within the gap is uncertain. Not smoothed; see Confidence.
  • libellus Regi nostro conscribendus — "a little book to be written for our King" (Frederik II of Denmark, the edition's convention), promised within Easter. A short presentation work, unspecified here.
  • Allusion. Hyemis asperitas … glacie circumsaeptam — the winter ice-bound isolation of Hven, given as the literal cause of the postal delay; not a topos but a recurrent material fact of correspondence from Uraniborg.
  • Calendar. Datae Uraniburgi 23. Martij Anno 1586 = "Given at Uraniborg, 23 March 1586." Denmark was on the Julian calendar (Old Style); dated O.S. The edition label gives only the place (Uraniborg); the as-written dateline 23 March 1586 governs the header. No in-letter date discrepancy.
  • Formula. Vale = "Farewell".
