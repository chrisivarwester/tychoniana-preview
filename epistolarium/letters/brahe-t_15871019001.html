The original

HENRICO RANZOVIO PRODUCI CIMBRICO TYCHO BRAHE.
S. D.

QUOD nihil hac tota æstate ad te literarum dederim, nobilissime eruditissimeque Domine HENRICE RANZOVI, amice inprimis colende, neque oblivione ulla tui, cuius memoriam cum filio GERHARDO, amico et vicino meo dilecto, sæpius recolo, neque negligentia voluntaria factum existimes. Verum cum in dies expectarem ab illustrissimo Domino GULIELMO Landgravio Hassiæ litteras, quibus meis secundo ad ipsum datis per proprium tabellarium respondere pollicitus est, ut earum exemplar tibi unâ cum prioribus, quibus invicem de rebus Astronomicis ex voto egimus, inspiciendum mitterem, accidit ut diutius quam sperabam rescriptio illa procrastinata sit adeo, ut iam primum circa medium Octobris responsum a prædicto illustrissimo Principe ad Literas in Ianuario ipsi perscriptas acceperim. Contigit vero commodum, quod his ipsis diebus quendam ex meis domesticis Lubecam ablegaverim, ut papyrum impressoriam, quam operi cuidam, quod nunc typis meis elaboratur, sufficientem desidero, illic mature procuret, et quædam alia negotia, quæ in mandatis habet, unâ meo nomine expediat. Idcirco non inscius, Segebergam unius saltem diei itinere illinc abesse, nolui ipsum tam prope sedes, in quibus moraris, accedere, quin aliquid a me tibi literarum adferret meoque nomine officiose salutem diceret. Dedi itaque huic in mandatis, ut te Segebergæ conveniret hasque literas cum adiunctis tibi tradere non intermitteret. Invenies in his illustrissimi Domini Landgravij et ipsius Mathematici ROTHMANNI responsionem ad priores meas, quarum (ni fallor) tibi exemplar prius misi. Adiunxi etiam ea, quæ eidem illustrissimo Principi et dicto ipsius Mathematico postea de rebus Astronomicis luculenter rescripsi, et ab ipso Principe denuo remissum responsum, quod modo accepi, unâ addidi. Literæ Mathematici, quæ adiunctæ erant, non tam cito ob prolixitatem et copiam transcribi poterant, eo quod nuncius meus ob venti oportunitatem abitum festinaret. Sed alio tempore, si ita tibi placuerit, eas facile obtinebis. Addidi etiam festinanter quædam, quæ ex tempore mihi in mentem venerunt, ad hasce ultimas Landgravianas literas per nuncij et otij oportunitatem respondenda, et literarum notis certos paragraphos designavi, quibus responsiones eisdem literis adaptatæ quadrant. Hinc quid de affixarum restitutionibus utrinque sentiamus, facile percipies et non sine voluptate magnam congruentiam animadvertes. Non enim me latet, quanto desiderio rerum Astronomicarum afficiaris, et quam egregie de his meritus sis. Tuum erit, mecum, si quid vel ab antedicto Principe vel ex propria Bibliotheca aut aliunde habes, quod ad Astronomicam, qua utrinque delectamur, materiam illustrandam facit, vicissim communicare. Vale, et huic nuncio meo in ijs, quæ illi expedienda iniunxi, tuo consilio authoritateque, ubi opus fuerit, ne deesse velis.

Iterum feliciter cum nobilissima uxore et sobole non degenere totaque domo vale.
Datæ Uraniburgi 19. Octobris Anno 1587.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Heinrich Rantzau, Governor of the Cimbrian land.
Sends greeting.

That I have sent you no letter this whole summer, most noble and most learned Master Heinrich Rantzau, my friend especially to be honoured, you must not suppose to have come about either from any forgetfulness of you — whose memory I often recall together with your son Gerhard, my dear friend and neighbour — or from any wilful negligence. Rather, since I was daily awaiting letters from the most illustrious Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse — to which, after I had written to him a second time, he had promised to reply by his own courier — so that I might send you a copy of them, along with the earlier ones in which we dealt with one another on astronomical matters to our mutual wish, it came about that that reply was put off longer than I had hoped, so much so that it was only now, around the middle of October, that I received the answer of the aforesaid most illustrious Prince to the letter written out to him in January. But it has fallen out conveniently that in these very days I have dispatched one of my household to Lübeck, that he might procure there in good time printing paper — which I find sufficient for a certain work that is now being produced at my press — and might at the same time, in my name, settle certain other affairs which he has in charge. Therefore, not unaware that Segeberg is at least one day's journey distant from there, I was unwilling that he should pass so near the seat where you reside without his bringing you some letter from me and dutifully conveying a greeting in my name. And so I gave him charge to meet with you at Segeberg and not to fail to deliver to you this letter together with its enclosures. In them you will find the reply of the most illustrious Lord Landgrave and of his own Mathematician Rothmann to my earlier ones, of which (unless I am mistaken) I sent you a copy before. I have added also those things which I afterwards wrote back at length on astronomical matters to that same most illustrious Prince and to his said Mathematician, and I have added together with them the answer sent back again by the Prince himself, which I have just received. The Mathematician's letters, which were enclosed, could not be transcribed so quickly on account of their prolixity and abundance, since my messenger was hastening his departure because of the favourable wind. But at another time, if it so please you, you will easily obtain them. I have also hastily added certain things which occurred to me on the spur of the moment, to be answered to these last letters of the Landgrave as the opportunity of messenger and leisure allowed; and I have marked off particular paragraphs with letters of the alphabet, by which the replies fitted to those same letters correspond. From this you will easily perceive what each of us holds concerning the restorations of the fixed [stars], and you will note, not without pleasure, the great agreement. For it is not hidden from me with how great a longing you are moved toward astronomical matters, and how excellently you have deserved well of them. It will be for you in turn to share with me, if you have anything either from the aforesaid Prince, or from your own library, or from elsewhere, which serves to illuminate the astronomical subject in which we both delight. Farewell; and to this my messenger, in the matters which I have enjoined upon him to settle, do not be unwilling to lend your counsel and authority, where there shall be need.

Again, happily, farewell — with your most noble wife and your offspring that does not fall short of its stock, and your whole house.
Given at Uraniborg, 19 October, the year 1587.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe writes from Uraniborg on Hven. The addressee, Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), the Danish king's Statthalter (royal governor) of the duchies of Schleswig-Holstein, residing chiefly at Segeberg; one of the great humanist patrons and bibliophiles of the north, an astrological author, and a frequent intermediary in Tycho's correspondence.
  • Address-line title. Produci Cimbrico — "Governor of the Cimbrian land," i.e. of the Cimbrian peninsula (Jutland / the duchies). Produx (a late/Neo-Latin form, dative produci) here = a leading/governing magistrate, rendered "Governor"; the antiquarian epithet Cimbricus ("of the Cimbri") is the humanist style for Jutland-Holstein. Kept and glossed rather than modernised to "Statthalter."
  • S. D. = salutem dicit, "sends greeting".
  • Gerhard Rantzau (filio GERHARDO) — Heinrich's son Gerhard Rantzau (1558–1627), here named as Tycho's "friend and neighbour" (amico et vicino meo dilecto).
  • Wilhelm IV (illustrissimo Domino GULIELMO Landgravio Hassiae) = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, the astronomer-prince; his court mathematician Rothmann = Christoph Rothmann. The letter is a cover-note forwarding the Brahe–Hesse astronomical correspondence to Rantzau.
  • Calendar reference. responsum … ad Literas in Ianuario ipsi perscriptas — Tycho's letter "written out in January" is the long Tycho→Rothmann letter of 20 January 1587 (identifier brahe-t_15870120001); not converted, given as written.
  • papyrum impressoriam … operi cuidam, quod nunc typis meis elaboratur — "printing paper … for a certain work now being produced at my press": Tycho's own Uraniborg press; the "certain work" is most plausibly the volume that became the Epistolae astronomicae (printed at Uraniborg, the 1596 Liber I) and/or De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, then in progress — identification not asserted in the text, so left general.
  • Astronomical crux — affixarum restitutionibus. Literally "the restorations of the fixed [stars]," i.e. the re-determination / re-establishment of the fixed-star positions (the catalogue of stellae affixae, the fixed stars). restitutio is rendered "restoration" per the editorial glossary (, restitutio/instauratio → restoration/renewal); affixarum (fem. gen. pl.) stands for stellarum affixarum (the fixed stars), the noun stellarum understood — bracketed "[stars]" supplied. The phrase denotes the agreement between Tycho and Rothmann on their respective fixed-star coordinate restorations. Flagged because affixarum is elliptical and "restorations of the fixed stars" is a technical-procedural sense, not a generic "restoration."
  • ex propria Bibliotheca — "from your own library": a pointed compliment to Rantzau, whose library at Breitenburg was among the most celebrated private collections of the age.
  • Marking convention. literarum notis certos paragraphos designavi — "I have marked off particular paragraphs with letters of the alphabet": Tycho keyed his replies to numbered/lettered sections of the Landgrave's letters so the correspondence could be read in register. literarum notis = "with letters [of the alphabet] as marks," rendered accordingly.
  • Subscription / dateline. sobole non degenere — "offspring not degenerate," i.e. children who do not fall short of (do not disgrace) their lineage; rendered "offspring that does not fall short of its stock," the double negative (non degenere) preserved. Datae Uraniburgi 19. Octobris Anno 1587 = "Given at Uraniborg, 19 October 1587" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The catalogue label ("1587. 19. oktober. Uraniborg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Negations (verified count). Source negations rendered one-for-one: neque oblivione … neque negligentia … existimes ("neither … nor …"); non intermitteret ("not fail"); non inscius ("not unaware"); nolui … quin … adferret ("I was unwilling that he should pass … without bringing," litotes preserved); ni fallor ("unless I am mistaken"); non … me latet ("it is not hidden from me"); ne deesse velis ("do not be unwilling to lend"); non … sine voluptate ("not without pleasure"); non degenere ("not degenerate"). No negation added or dropped.
