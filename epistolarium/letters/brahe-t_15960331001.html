The original

[AD RECTOREM UNIVERSITATIS HAUNIENSIS].

MAGNIFICE domine rector. Eftersom ieg talde med eder y gaar, og waar venligen begierendis, att mig motte bliffue førdrett mett nogen endelig beskeen, och icke lenger opholden, mett hvis mig burde at haffue beskreffuit aff eders consistorio, och att ieg derhos maatte faae dett att see, førend dett bleff beseglet, eftersom mig anderstedz vederfaret er, saa well eder selff mett eders assessoribus som mig till beste, och widere uleylighed att forekomme. Jeg begier dog intet andet, end huis billigt och rett er och sandhed gemeß, eftersom eders acta udviser och eder alle witterligt kand vaere. Paa dett første gaff j mig temmelig beskeden, formenendis, att dett skulde bliffue snart fuldendt. Mens om dett andet kunde j ingen rigtig fortrøstning giøre mig, førend j havde talet och raadslaget met eders assessoribus derom. Thi er ieg endnu aff eder och de andre eders collegis ett gott och endeligt suar udi begge stycker paa thet wenligste och i en god mening begierendis, saa att ieg kunde faa att wide mett thette mitt bud, huoreffter ieg haffuer mig att rette. Thet forskylder ieg gierne igien, huor mig mugeligt kand were. Ombedendis derhos, att der motte bliffue tegnet paa denne skrifft, at den er laest for eder.

Actum 31. martii 1596.
Thyge Brahe
met egen haand.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TO THE RECTOR OF THE UNIVERSITY OF COPENHAGEN].

Magnificent lord rector. Inasmuch as I spoke with you yesterday, and was kindly requesting that I might be furthered [helped forward] with some final statement, and no longer held off, with that which I ought to have set down in writing for me by your consistory; and that I might moreover get to see it before it was sealed, as has befallen me elsewhere — both for your own and your assessors' best [interest] as well as mine, and to forestall further inconvenience. Yet I ask nothing else than what is fair and right and conformable to the truth, as your records [acta] show and as can be known to you all. At first you gave me a fair enough answer, supposing that it should soon be brought to completion. But concerning the other [matter] you could give me no proper assurance until you had spoken and taken counsel with your assessors about it. Therefore I am still, of you and the others, your colleagues, requesting a good and final answer on both points, in the friendliest way and in good intent, so that I may get to know by this my messenger how I am to govern myself. This I gladly requite in return, in whatever way may be possible for me. Requesting moreover that there might be marked upon this writing that it has been read out before you.

Done, 31 March 1596.
Tyge Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender / hand. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), signing Thyge Brahe met egen haand — "Tyge Brahe, with his own hand," i.e. an autograph subscription (the Latin formula's Danish equivalent of manu propria). Tycho's name appears in its Danish vernacular form Thyge (not the Latin Tycho) in the subscription.
  • Recipient. Addressed by office only — Magnifice domine rector / Rector Universitatis Hauniensis, the rector of the University of Copenhagen (the rector magnificus) and his consistory. The text names no individual; the present translation therefore identifies the addressee by office, not by name. The rector of Copenhagen in spring 1596 is not asserted here on the strength of the letter alone.
  • Context. One of a pair of letters Tycho addressed to the rector on the same day, 31 March 1596 (the companion piece is brahe-t_15960331002, opening Efterdi jeg formercker, att j mett eders assessoribus icke wilde…). Tycho is pressing the university consistory for a formal, sealed, written attestation (nogen endelig beskeen … beskreffuit aff eders consistorio) on two matters (begge stycker) — an administrative dispute, consonant with the deterioration of Tycho's standing at Copenhagen in the years 1596–97 that preceded his departure from Denmark in 1597. The substance of the two "points" is not specified within the letter and is not reconstructed here.
  • Latin code-switches (a learned chancery hand). Magnifice domine rector — "Magnificent lord rector," the formal academic salutation; consistorio — the university consistory (governing council); assessoribus / collegis — its assessors / colleagues (the professors sitting on the consistory); acta — the consistory's records / acts; Actum — "Done / Given" (the dating formula, the edition's convention); met egen haand glosses the manu propria subscription. The body is otherwise Danish.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1596). waar … begierendis = var … begærende ("was requesting"); førdrett = fordret/befordret ("furthered, helped forward"); opholden = opholdt ("held off, delayed"); hvis = relative hvad ("that which"); beskreffuit = beskrevet ("set down in writing"); anderstedz vederfaret = andetsteds vederfaret ("befallen [me] elsewhere"); till beste = til bedste ("for the best [interest]"); uleylighed = ulejlighed ("inconvenience"); billigt = "fair, equitable"; sandhed gemeß = sandhed gemæss ("conformable to the truth" — gemæss a German-loan postposition, "in accordance with"); temmelig beskeden = temmelig besked ("a fair enough answer/information" — beskeden here is the noun besked, not the adjective "modest"); fortrøstning = "assurance, reassurance"; raadslaget = rådslået ("taken counsel, deliberated"); stycker = stykker ("points, items, matters"); huoreffter ieg haffuer mig att rette = hvorefter jeg har mig at rette ("by which I am to govern/regulate myself"); forskylder … igien = forskylder igen ("requite, repay in return"); bud = "messenger" (the bearer of the letter); tegnet paa denne skrifft = tegnet på denne skrift ("marked / noted upon this writing"); laest = læst ("read [out]").
  • "beskeen / beskeden / besked." The two near-identical forms are the same noun besked ("a [final] statement, ruling, answer, information"): endelig beskeen = "a final statement/ruling"; temmelig beskeden = "a fair enough answer." Rendered consistently as "statement / answer"; not the modern adjective beskeden ("modest").
  • Negations. The body contains two negations — icke lenger opholden ("no longer held off") and intet andet, end ("nothing else than") — and the reported refusal kunde j ingen rigtig fortrøstning giøre mig ("you could give me no proper assurance"). All three are reproduced; none added or dropped.
  • Calendar / place. Actum 31. martii 1596 = "Done, 31 March 1596" (Julian / O.S.; Denmark kept Old Style until 1700). No place of writing is given (the edition's label records the place as s.l. = sine loco, "without place"); the header accordingly states "[no place given]." The date in the source's own dateline (31 March 1596) agrees with the catalogue label, so no date discrepancy arises.
