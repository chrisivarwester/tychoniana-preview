The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTIANUM LONGOMONTANUM.

S.

QUAS Wandesburgo ad me dedisti literas, eruditissime et amantissime M. CHRISTIERNE, eas hîc Wittebergæ accepi penultimo die huius anni. Refers ab initio in ijs de incuria Typographi Hamburgensis, qua quaternionem ibi relictum non saltem non emendatiorem edidit, sed adhuc magis quam antea corrupit, ut et in priori negligens fuit; sed quid faciendum cum talibus hominibus, qui nec fidem servant, nec quæ profitentur, satis callent? Constitui igitur duos illos quaterniones de motu Lunæ hîc, volente Deo, terse et emendate imprimi curare, ubi et reliquos quatuor Lunam concernentes, quos nunc sub prælo habeo. Ne igitur sis de priori iactura solicitus, et ferendum est, quod averti non potest. Quod vero excusas moram tuam aliquantulam isthic Wandesburgi, apud familiam meam muliebrem, tantum abest ut hoc ægre feram, quin potius tibi gratias habeam, quod id mihi et illis officij præstare volueris, ut non saltem Magdeburgo eos isthuc deduxeris, sed et aliquamdiu cum illis manseris, ne nimis solitariæ essent, donec ANDREAS minister reverteretur. Quoniam vero existimo te res tuas domi ordinasse, ut in Germaniam denuo cogites, aut nunc forte eo reversus sis, cuperem ut me de statu rerum tuarum certiorem redderes. Intellexisti ex ijs, quas ad te dedi, quam primum huc veni, me Wittebergæ hibernare, volente Numine, constituisse, et quæ sint causæ cur sic faciam. Si igitur res tuæ ullo modo id ferrent, ut me accedere posses, feceris mihi rem apprime gratam, nec tibi ipsi infrugiferam. Cum enim et antea a te intellexerim, et hæ tuæ literæ non obscure insinuent, quod in Academiam aliquam exteram ascendere cupias, ubi secundum conditiones atque ætatem honeste vivere possis, poterit id sane aliquantisper non incommode fieri, donec divina providentia res meæ quietum et certum locum in portu exoptato et firmo, concedente uti spero Cæsare, obtinuerint. Tunc melius tibi et tuis commodis prospicere non omittam, interim etiam de liberali sustentatione meis sumptibus tibi providebo, nec quicquam tibi deesse patiar, quod oportuna vivendi ratio exigat. Atque ubi præsens fueris (neque enim de tua erga me voluntate aut obsequio quidquam dubito) plenius de ijs, quæ tibi usui esse poterint, inter nos agemus. Gratum mihi inprimis est, quod de Apologia Antagonistæ mei Scotici refutanda serio cogites, vellem id mature fieri, ut Tomum illum de Cometa anni 77. sic concludere possim, atque interea dum hîc sum, typis absolvere, unâ cum alijs meis operibus antea inceptis, necdum prorsus ad Colophonem deductis. Tu vero mi dilectiss. Domine CHRISTIANE, te in his quam primum resolve; ego tibi et tuis commodis, quantum in me est, nullatenus sum defuturus, sed omni benevolentia et ad meliora semper promovendi amore et animi propensitate prosequar, idque re ipsa potius quam verbis, quam primum se occasio obtulerit, ostendam.

Vale.
Dabantur Witebergæ ex ædibus ...... (ubi nunc cum Domino D. IESSENIO ... conversor) ultimo die anni completi.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO CHRISTEN LONGOMONTANUS.

Greeting.

The letter you sent me from Wandsbek, most learned and most loving Master Christen, I received here at Wittenberg on the penultimate day of this year. You report, at the beginning of it, on the carelessness of the Hamburg printer, by which the gathering left there he not only did not bring out more correct, but corrupted even more than before — as he was negligent in the former one too. But what is to be done with such men, who neither keep faith, nor are skilled enough in what they profess? I have therefore resolved to have those two gatherings on the motion of the Moon printed here, God willing, cleanly and correctly — where [I have] also the remaining four concerning the Moon, which I now have under the press. So do not be anxious about the former loss; and what cannot be averted must be borne. But that you excuse your somewhat long stay there at Wandsbek, with my womenfolk — so far am I from taking this ill that I rather give you thanks, that you were willing to render this service to me and to them: in that you not only conducted them thither from Magdeburg, but also stayed with them a while, lest they be too solitary, until Andreas the servant returned. And since I judge that you have set your affairs in order at home, so as to think of [coming to] Germany again — or perhaps have now returned thither — I should wish you to inform me of the state of your affairs. You understood, from those [letters] I sent you as soon as I came hither, that I had resolved to winter at Wittenberg, God willing, and what the reasons are why I do so. If, therefore, your affairs would in any way allow that you could come to me, you will have done me a thing exceedingly welcome, and not unfruitful for yourself. For since I have understood before from you, and these your letters not obscurely hint, that you desire to advance to some foreign Academy [university], where, according to your circumstances and age, you may live honourably — this can indeed be done conveniently enough for a while, until, by divine providence, my affairs obtain a quiet and certain place in the wished-for and firm harbour, the Emperor granting it, as I hope. Then I shall not omit to provide better for you and your interests; and meanwhile I shall provide for your liberal maintenance at my own expense, nor shall I let anything be wanting to you that a fitting manner of life requires. And when you are present (for I doubt nothing of your goodwill or compliance toward me) we shall treat more fully between us of the things that may be of use to you. It is especially welcome to me that you are seriously thinking of refuting the Apologia of my Scottish antagonist; I should wish it done in good time, so that I may thus conclude that Tome on the Comet of the year [15]77, and meanwhile, while I am here, finish it in print, together with my other works begun earlier, not yet quite brought to the colophon. But do you, my most beloved Lord Christen, resolve yourself in these matters as soon as possible; I, for my part, will in no way fail you and your interests, so far as in me lies, but will pursue you with all benevolence, and with a love and inclination of mind ever to promote you to better things — and this I will show by the deed itself rather than by words, as soon as occasion offers.

Farewell.
Given at Wittenberg, from the house …… (where I now dwell with Lord Doctor Jessenius …) on the last day of the completed year.
TYCHO BRAHE,
with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (Christian Severin, 1562–1647), Tycho's senior assistant and the most gifted of his familia — here addressed M. Christierne / Domine Christiane, "Master Christen." After years at Hven he had left Tycho's service to settle his affairs in Denmark; this letter presses him to rejoin Tycho in Germany. (One of a close sequence of Tycho→Longomontanus letters from the Wittenberg winter; cf. brahe-t_15990111001, two weeks later.)
  • The Hamburg printer and the lunar theory (printing history). Longomontanus had reported the incompetence of the Hamburg printer, who botched a quaternio (gathering) of the lunar-theory text — corrupting it further instead of correcting it. Tycho resolves to reprint those two gatherings de motu Lunae at Wittenberg, where he already has "the remaining four concerning the Moon … under the press." This is the printing of the lunar theory for the Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata (the great work then dragging toward completion); "do not be anxious about the former loss — what cannot be averted must be borne."
  • Longomontanus's service to Tycho's household. Tycho warmly thanks him for escorting his womenfolk (familia muliebris — wife Kirsten and the daughters) from Magdeburg to Wandsbek and staying with them "lest they be too solitary, until Andreas the servant returned" — a humane domestic glimpse of the exile years.
  • The offer of patronage. Tycho, wintering at Wittenberg and awaiting (as he hopes) a settled "harbour" under the Emperor, urges Longomontanus to come to him: he may meanwhile "advance to some foreign Academy" suited to his age and station, while Tycho maintains him liberally at his own expense and promises to "provide better" once his own affairs are secure. The "wished-for and firm harbour … the Emperor granting it" anticipates the move to Bohemia.
  • The Craig controversy (cometary polemic). Apologia Antagonistae mei Scotici = the Apologia of John Craig (Joannes Craigius), the Scottish mathematician who had disputed claims in Tycho's "Tome on the Comet of 1577" — i.e. the second volume of the Progymnasmata-complex, _De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis_ (on the comet of 1577). Tycho is delighted that Longomontanus will write the refutation, so that he can conclude and print that Tome while at Wittenberg, "together with my other works … not yet brought to the colophon." (The Craig exchange runs through the 1589–90 correspondence, e.g. brahe-t_15900125001.)
  • Lodging with Jessenius. cum Domino D. Jessenio … conversor — Tycho records that at Wittenberg he is living with Johannes Jessenius (the anatomist), the same friend who recurs in brahe-t_16000327002 and brahe-t_16001008001.
  • Persons named. Longomontanus; the Hamburg printer (unnamed); Tycho's womenfolk; Andreas the servant; John Craig (the "Scottish antagonist"); Johannes Jessenius; the Emperor (Rudolf II).
  • Lacunae (flagged). The dateline is defective in the witness (a Langebek afskrift): the house-name is lost (ex aedibus ……) and there is a gap in the parenthesis (cum Domino D. Jessenio … conversor); marked with ellipses, not conjectured.
  • Calendar / date. ultimo die anni completi = the last day of the completed year = 31 December 1598; received by Tycho penultimo die huius anni (30 December). Wittenberg/Saxony kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar. The edition label gives only the place ("Wittenberg") with no date; the date is supplied from the dateline and identifier (15981231001) — noted.
