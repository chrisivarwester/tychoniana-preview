The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD LUDOLPHUM RIDDERSHUSIUM.

S. P.

CLARISSIME et Eruditissime Vir, Amice mihi plurimum semper faciende. Literæ tuæ paulo post solstitium hybernum superioris Anni ad me datæ præterlapso nuperrimo huius Æquinoctio mihi primum redditæ sunt, quibus si per omnia nunc ita luculenter respondere, uti desiderares, non concedatur, tribuas id occupationum mearum moli; quæ tamen otium utcunque admittit, brevibus exposita, sic habe.

De Directionum administratione et rationibus, tum quoque distributione Cœli, quæ antea significavi, satis per se clara sunt, nec, etiamsi quam maxime vellem, ea exemplis illustrandi nunc suppetit oportunitas. Possunt vero secundum Eclipticam pariter Directiones fieri, non dissimiliter, iuxta motum ☉lis diurnum, numerandæ, nam et has subinde pondus habere animadverti. Debent autem Directiones institui ad Horizontem eius loci, in quo quis natus est, non autem ubi postea habitat; quia quilibet suum secum portat Cœlum suasque influentias, quocunque iverit. Octogenaria Thematum distributio ad hoc conducit, ut robur efficaciæ Planetarum rectius pateat, siquidem dimidius Angulus Rectus sit inter Meridianum et Horizontem, post horum duorum limites, magni sunt roboris. Significata autem ex duodenaria Divisione potius consueto Astrologorum more petenda.

Transitus ♄ni, quia tardigradus est, utplurimum vim habet, ♂tis autera minus. Tunc vero stimulantur effectus, cum in idem tempus similes profectiones coincidunt; impediuntur vero, si contrariæ; et Directiones, quæ generaliorem habent significationem, hîc etiam augendo vel minuendo plurimum possunt. Si eodem tempore inciderint adversæ et prosperæ Directiones, seipsas fere elidunt, et effectum boni malique imminuunt. Directiones, quæ exitum naturalem vel violentem portendunt, variæ se habent, nec ad certum aliquod genus restringi possunt; et oportet multa alia simul considerare, nec Stoicam necessitatem introducere. Geniturarum influxus fausti vel infelices vario modo mutari et transplantari possunt; ut non nimium ijs sit indulgendum. Omnes autem rationes, quibus hoc efficitur, nimis longum foret enarrare. Neque etiam in omnes homines Astra æqualiter influunt, sed quidam se reddunt eorum viribus magis obnoxios quam alij. Verum Deus, Author Universi, omnia pro suo nutu moderatur. Atque hæc ad quæstiones tuas pro tempore respondisse sufficiat.

Ad tempora et eventus Nativitatum, quas misisti, attendere et in eas disquirere nunc non vacat; imo etiamsi vacaret, non multum temporis talibus curis libenter tribuo, gravioribus et maioris momenti studijs potius addictus. Restitutionem motus ☾ris nunc communicare non possum, nondum enim typis edita est, uti neque in cœteris Planetis desiderijs tuis morem gerere. Conservantur adhuc libri mei et res, pro maiori parte, Magdæburgi; ubi advenerint, et oportunius otium nactus fuero, dabo operam, ut plenius in talibus gratificari queam. Literas adiunctas ad Dominum DAVIDEM FABRICIUM absque omni interposita morâ per certum hominem dirige; eius enim interest, ut eas cito nanciscatur.

Iamque vale, quodque pluribus nunc tecum non agam, candide excusatum habe.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsareâ Benaticâ, 5 milliaribus Pragâ distante, Die 20 Aprilis Anni 1600, stylo recognito.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO LUDOLF RIDDERSHAUSEN.

Heartiest greeting.

Most renowned and most learned Man, a friend always to be highly esteemed by me. Your letter, sent to me a little after the winter solstice of last year, was first delivered to me only at the most recent equinox just past; to which, if it be not now granted me to reply so fully in every respect as you would wish, attribute it to the mass of my occupations. Yet what my leisure does, in any case, allow — set out briefly — take thus.

Concerning the management and methods of Directions, and also the distribution of the Heaven, which I indicated before, [they] are clear enough in themselves, nor — even if I most greatly wished it — is there now opportunity to illustrate them with examples. Directions can indeed equally be made according to the Ecliptic, to be reckoned not differently, by the Sun's diurnal motion — for I have observed that these too sometimes have weight. But Directions ought to be set up to the Horizon of the place in which a person was born, not where he afterward dwells; because each carries his own Heaven with him, and his own influences, wherever he goes. The eight-fold distribution of the Figures serves this end, that the strength of the Planets' efficacy may more rightly appear, since a half right-angle [45°] lies between the Meridian and the Horizon; beyond the bounds of these two, [the planets] are of great strength. But the significations are rather to be sought from the twelve-fold Division [the twelve houses], after the customary manner of the Astrologers.

The transit of Saturn, because it is slow-moving, has for the most part [great] force; that of Mars less. The effects are stimulated when similar profections coincide at the same time, but are impeded if contrary; and Directions, which have a more general signification, can here too do much, by increasing or diminishing. If at the same time adverse and prosperous Directions fall together, they almost cancel each other out, and lessen the effect of good and of ill. Directions which portend a natural or a violent death vary, and cannot be restricted to any fixed kind; and one must consider many other things at once, and not introduce Stoic necessity. The auspicious or unlucky influxes of genitures can be altered and transplanted in various ways, so that they are not to be indulged too far. But to relate all the methods by which this is effected would be too long. Nor do the Stars influence all men equally, but some render themselves more subject to their forces than others. Yet God, the Author of the Universe, governs all things according to his will. And let it suffice to have answered these things to your questions, for the time.

To attend to the times and outcomes of the Nativities you sent, and to inquire into them, there is now no leisure; indeed, even were there leisure, I do not willingly give much time to such cares, being rather devoted to weightier studies of greater moment. The restitution of the Moon's motion I cannot now share, for it is not yet published in print — nor [can I] gratify your wishes in the other Planets. My books and things are still kept, for the greater part, at Magdeburg; when they shall have arrived, and I have obtained more suitable leisure, I will take pains to gratify [you] more fully in such matters. The enclosed letter to Lord David Fabricius, direct without any interposed delay, by a sure man; for it is in his interest to obtain it quickly.

And now farewell; and that I do not deal with you at more length now, hold me candidly excused.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, distant 5 miles from Prague, the 20th day of April, the year 1600, in the recognised [New] style.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Ludolf Riddershausen (Ludolphus Riddershusius), a learned correspondent who had submitted astrological questions and nativities to Tycho. (No prior letter to/from him in the edition; precise biography to firm up.) His letter, sent "a little after the winter solstice" of 1599, reached Tycho only at the spring equinox of 1600.
  • Significance — Tycho's fullest brief statement on predictive-astrology technique. A compact exposition of his views (the edition's convention, Astrology), kept on the controlled terms:
  • Directions (Directiones) and the distribution / division of the heaven (house systems). Directions may also be reckoned along the Ecliptic by the Sun's diurnal motion; they must be cast to the Horizon of the birthplace, "because each carries his own Heaven with him … wherever he goes."
  • Two house schemes contrasted: the eight-fold distribution (quadrants halved at the 45° half-right-angle between Meridian and Horizon) best shows "the strength of the Planets' efficacy," while the significations are sought from the customary twelve-fold division (the twelve houses).
  • Transits & profections: Saturn's transit (slow) is potent, Mars's less; effects are reinforced when profections agree, cancelled when they conflict; concurrent adverse and prosperous Directions "almost cancel each other out."
  • Death-directions vary and resist fixed rules; "one must consider many things at once, and not introduce Stoic necessity."
  • Free-will caveat (doctrinally important): the influxes of genitures "can be altered and transplanted in various ways … not to be indulged too far"; the Stars do not influence all men equally; and "God, the Author of the Universe, governs all things according to his will." Tycho also distances himself from the practice — he declines to work up Riddershausen's nativities, "being rather devoted to weightier studies" (cf. the same posture in brahe-t_16000120001).
  • Glyphs & terms (kept; the edition's convention/). ☉ = Sun, ♄ = Saturn, ♂ = Mars, ☾ = Moon (names in English, glyphs retained in the source block). Directiones, profectiones, genitura, thema per the controlled list.
  • Textual flag — Octogenaria. Octogenaria Thematum distributio literally = an "eighty-fold" distribution, but the context (the 45° half-right-angle, contrasted with the duodenaria twelve-fold division) requires the eight-fold / octant (octotopos) house system in which each quadrant is bisected. Rendered "eight-fold distribution" by sense; the literal Octogenaria is flagged as a probable form/print issue and noted here, not silently emended.
  • Other matter. The lunar restitution is "not yet published in print" (cf. brahe-t_15981231001); Tycho's books are still at Magdeburg. He asks Riddershausen to forward an enclosed letter to David Fabricius (1564–1617, the Frisian pastor-astronomer, discoverer of Mira Ceti; the recurring "David Fabricius" thread) "without delay."
  • Calendar. Dateline Die 20 Aprilis Anni 1600, stylo recognito — stylo recognito = the "recognised" (New/Gregorian) style; imperial. The note 5 milliaribus Pragâ distante fixes Benátky "5 miles from Prague." Header agrees with the label.
