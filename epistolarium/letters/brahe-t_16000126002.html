The original

CLARISSIMO ET ERUDITISSIMO VIRO DOMINO Μ. IOHANNI KEPLERO ILLUSTRIUM STYRIÆ ORDINUM MATHEMATICO EXIMIO, AMICO SUO PLURIMI FACIENDO.

REVERSI heri vesperi sero ad me domestici illi mei, quos Pragam miseram, tuas, Eccme et amantissime Domine KEPLERE, ex insperato attulere humanissimas literas, quibus lectis, te Pragæ nunc esse, perlubenter comperi. Invite tamen ex ijsdem colligebam, te necdum meas, quas ultimis Tuis affatim respondentes ante mensem circiter Viennam, ut inde per Dominum Doctorem BLOTIUM tibi, ubicunque invenireris, mitterentur, direxi. Quæ si interea tibi traditæ fuissent, hac reiterata protestatione et excusatione in re ista, quæ tot verba non meretur, supersedisses, atque actutum citra diuturniorem deliberationem et cunctationem huc (siquidem in vicinia es) advolasses. Quod ut etiamnum facias, te etiam atque etiam rogo. Poteris in eodem vehiculo comitari Nobilem et eruditum adolescentem FRANCISCUM TENGNAGLIUM domesticum nostrum, et filium meum TYCHONEM natu maiorem, quos Pragam ob negocia quædam peculiaria, meo nomine isthic expedienda, nunc ablego. Illi enim intra paucos dies, volente Deo, huc revertentur, et locum pro te in curru facile invenient.

De meo isthuc adventu necdum certi quid concludere possum, antequam Cæsaream Maiestatem Dominum nostrum Clementissimum eo reversum esse intellexero. Neque enim quid alias isthic magnopere agam, habeo, nec libenter accedo, nisi a Cæsarea Maiestate clementissime vocer: siquidem id citra temporis, cuius, tanquam thesauri irrecuperabilis, sum parcissimus, aliqualem iacturam, et studiorum, quæ præ manibus habeo, remoram fieri nequeat.

Explorandi etiam hisce noctibus acronychij situs Martis et Iovis, tum quoque Mercurij, quem post occasum Solis discrete apparentem ter proxime elapsis crepusculis observavimus, tum quoque ad Venerem Eoam, quæ etiam nunc interdiu unâ cum Sole, adeoque in ipso Meridiano existens, spectatur, et observationi, prout aliquot quoque annis præteritis a nobis factum, patet, advertendum. Taceo quod Saturnus non longe a □ ☉ mane iuxta Meridianum apparens, pro Parallaxi orbis (uti vocat COPERNICUS) et Latitudine sua (siquidem appropinquet ad limitem maxime Boreum) rimanda, non inconvenientem exhibeat occasionem, donec adventante Aprili plane pernox redditus, Apogæum et Eccentricitatem, unâque ipsam Latitudinem maximam, prout hæc a nobis iam antea explorata sunt, comprobet. Accedit et hoc, quod in proxime futuro plenilunio Luna Eclipsin, licet minutulam, iuxta ipsum occasum paulo ante ortum Solis patietur, cui etiam (ut prioribus sedulo designatis adijci queat, atque restitutio motus Luminarium a nobis prius facta, ulterius examinari) attendendum, modo serenitas oportuna id permiserit.

Vides itaque, mi KEPLERE, quot et quanta exercitia nostra Astronomica ingerant obstacula, nobis tamen haud ingrata (siquidem ijs distineri volupe est) quo minus Pragam tam cito, uti opinatus es, excurrere queam. Tuum itaque erit prævenire, meque hîc mature (uti dictum) invisere. Advenies non tam hospes quam amicus gratissimus, nostrarumque in cœlestibus contemplationum, per ea quæ nunc ad manus habeo, instrumenta spectator et socius acceptissimus. Tunc, volente Numine, coram de pluribus. Vale et Illustrem ac Generosum Dominum Baronem IOHANNEM FRIDERICUM HOFFMANNUM, cui etiam nonnulla congratulationis caussa pro felici reditu scripsi, ex me officiosissime resaluta.

Dabantur properanter ex arce Cæsareana Benatica die 26 Ianuarij 1600.
Tui Amantissimus
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned man, Master Johannes Kepler, distinguished mathematician to the Illustrious Estates of Styria, his friend much to be esteemed.

When those servants of mine whom I had sent to Prague returned to me late yesterday evening, they brought, unexpectedly, your most courteous letter, most excellent and most beloved Master Kepler; and on reading it I learned with great pleasure that you are now in Prague. Yet from the same letter I gathered, against my wish, that you had not yet [received] mine — which, fully answering your last, I directed to Vienna about a month ago, so that from there, through Master Doctor Blotius, they might be sent on to you wherever you should be found. Had these meanwhile been delivered to you, you would have spared yourself this repeated protestation and apology in a matter that does not deserve so many words, and would forthwith, without longer deliberation and delay, have flown hither (since you are in the neighbourhood). And that you would do this even now, I ask you again and again. You will be able to travel in the same carriage with the noble and learned young man Franz Tengnagel, a member of my household, and with my elder son Tycho, whom I am now dispatching to Prague on account of certain particular business of mine to be transacted there. For within a few days, God willing, they will return hither, and will readily find a place for you in the carriage.

Concerning my own coming thither, I can as yet conclude nothing for certain, until I have learned that His Imperial Majesty, our Most Clement Lord, has returned there. For I have nothing else of much moment to do there, nor do I willingly go, unless I be most graciously summoned by His Imperial Majesty — seeing that this could not be done without some loss of time (of which, as of an irrecoverable treasure, I am most sparing) and without delay to the studies I have in hand.

[There is] also [reason] to investigate, on these [coming] nights, the acronychal position of Mars and of Jupiter; then too of Mercury, which we observed clearly visible after sunset, in the last three twilights just past; and likewise [we must] attend to Venus as Morning Star, which even now is to be seen by day together with the Sun — and indeed, being on the very meridian, is open to observation, as has also been done by us in several years past. I say nothing of how Saturn — appearing in the morning near the meridian, not far from quadrature with the Sun [□ ☉] — affords a not inconvenient occasion for investigating the parallax of the orb (as Copernicus calls it) and its [own] latitude (since it is approaching the most northerly limit); until, with April coming on, being rendered fully visible all night long, it may confirm its apogee and eccentricity, and together its greatest latitude itself, as these have already before been investigated by us. There is this besides, that at the full moon next to come the Moon will undergo an eclipse — a slight one, to be sure — close to setting [and] a little before sunrise; to which also [we must] attend (so that it may be added to those carefully recorded before, and so that the restoration of the motion of the luminaries, made earlier by us, may be examined further), provided that suitable clear weather permit it.

You see therefore, my dear Kepler, how many and how great are the obstacles our astronomical labours throw in the way — labours not unwelcome to us, however (since to be held fast by them is a pleasure) — to my being able to run over to Prague as soon as you supposed. It will be your part, then, to come first, and to visit me here in good time (as has been said). You will come not so much as a guest as a most welcome friend, and as a most acceptable spectator and partner of our contemplations of the heavens, by means of the instruments I now have at hand. Then, God willing, [we shall speak] face to face of more matters. Farewell; and on my behalf most dutifully return the greeting of the Illustrious and Noble Lord Baron Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, to whom too I have written some words by way of congratulation on his happy return.

Given in haste from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 26th day of January 1600.
Your most loving
TYCHO BRAHE,
I have written [this] with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, then established at the imperial castle of Benátky nad Jizerou (north-east of Prague), having entered the service of Emperor Rudolf II in 1599. This is the letter that summons the young Kepler to join him — one of the most consequential letters in the history of astronomy, written some four weeks before their first meeting (4 February 1600).
  • Recipient. M. Iohanni Keplero … Styriae Ordinum Mathematico = Johannes Kepler, then district mathematician (Landschaftsmathematiker) to the Protestant Estates of Styria at Graz, recently displaced by the Counter-Reformation expulsion of Protestants and so "in the neighbourhood." M. = Magister (academic "Master," the edition's convention).
  • Place. arce Caesareana Benatica = the Imperial castle of Benátky nad Jizerou (Latin Benatecum; the edition's convention).
  • Persons named.
  • Dominus Doctor Blotius = Hugo Blotius (Hugues Blotz), imperial librarian (Hofbibliothek) at Vienna, used here as a relay for correspondence.
  • Franciscus Tengnaglius = Franz Gansneb Tengnagel von Camp (c. 1576–1622), Tycho's noble Westphalian assistant, later his son-in-law and a literary executor of the Tychonic legacy.
  • filium meum Tychonem natu maiorem = Tyge (Tycho) Brahe the Younger, Tycho's elder son.
  • Caesarea Maiestas Dominus noster Clementissimus = Emperor Rudolf II, Tycho's imperial patron.
  • Baro Iohannes Fridericus Hoffmannus = Baron Johann Friedrich Hoffmann von Grünbüchel und Strechau, an imperial councillor and patron of Kepler's, in whose company Kepler had travelled and at whose Prague house he was lodging.
  • Astronomy — observational programme (this is the technical heart of the letter; the edition's convention).
  • acronychij situs Martis et Iovis = the acronychal position of Mars and Jupiter — i.e. their rising at sunset, near opposition to the Sun (the configuration most diagnostic for orbital elements). The Mars-opposition observations of exactly this season are the data Kepler would inherit and exploit. Acronychal (Greek akrónychos, "at nightfall") is kept as the technical term.
  • Mercurij … post occasum Solis discrete apparentem = Mercury seen as a distinct ("discrete") evening object after sunset, at crepusculis = the twilights.
  • Venerem Eoam = Venus as Morning Star (Eous, "of the dawn / eastern"); the rendering keeps the epithet's force. The daytime/meridian visibility noted is Venus near greatest brilliancy.
  • □ ☉ = the aspect glyph for quadrature (square) with the Sun; named in the translation, glyphs kept in the source block.
  • Parallaxi orbis (uti vocat Copernicus) = the parallax of the orb — Copernicus's own term (parallaxis orbis, the great-orbit / annual parallax: the apparent displacement of a superior planet arising from the Earth's annual motion). Tycho cites the Copernican label without endorsing Copernican cosmology; the hedge "as Copernicus calls it" is preserved, not flattened into "annual parallax."
  • Apogaeum et Eccentricitatem … Latitudinem maximam = apogee, eccentricity, and greatest latitude — the orbital elements of Saturn to be confirmed when it becomes pernox (visible the whole night long) in April.
  • plenilunio Luna Eclipsin … minutulam … patietur = at the coming full moon the Moon will suffer a slight (lunar) eclipse near setting, just before sunrise. (The lunar eclipse of 9/10 February 1600 N.S. was indeed of small magnitude.)
  • restitutio motus Luminarium = the restoration of the motion of the luminaries (Sun and Moon) — Tycho's programme term (restitutio; the edition's convention), the renewal of the solar and lunar theories he had already worked out and now wishes to test further.
  • instrumenta … spectator et socius = Tycho invites Kepler to be spectator and partner among the instruments (organa/instrumenta,) he has at Benátky.
  • Epistolary formulae. Vale → "Farewell"; Tui Amantissimus → "Your most loving"; manu propria scripsi → "I have written [this] with my own hand" (autograph attestation). Dabantur … Benatica (epistolary "they were given," i.e. dispatched) rendered "Given … from Benátky."
  • Calendar (the edition's convention — the chosen pilot crux). The dateline reads die 26 Ianuarij 1600; the edition label marks the letter n.s. (New Style / Gregorian). This is correct and significant: by 1600 Tycho, now in Catholic Imperial service at Benátky, has shifted from the Old-Style (Julian) dating of his Danish and Hessen correspondence to the New Style used in Rudolfine lands — the very 10-day Julian/Gregorian gap flagged in §3. The date is reproduced as written and not converted; the equivalent Old-Style date would be 16 January 1600, but no conversion is performed in the text. The lunar eclipse mentioned (the next full moon) falls 9/10 February 1600 N.S., consistent with the New-Style dating of the letter.
