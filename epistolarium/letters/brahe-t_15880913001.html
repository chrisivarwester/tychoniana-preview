The original

AMPLISSIMO NOBILISSIMOQUE VIRO DOMINO HENRICO RANZOVIO, DOMINO IN BREDENBERGA, ARCIS SEGEBERGAe PRÆSIDI ET REGIO IN HOLSATIA VICARIO, AMICO SUO INPRIMIS COLENDO.

ACCEPI intra paucas hebdomadas ter vel quater a te literas, Nobilissime amplissimeque vir, quibus singulis tuam erga me benevolentiam, et in procuranda papyro impressoria, nostrae officinae typographicae idonea, studium ac diligentiam affatim declaras. Quo nomine et pro typo etiam Sacelli, quod in honorem optimi et nunquam satis laudati Regis nostri, pijssimae memoriae, extrui curasti (quem unâ recepi) est quod gratias tibi habeam maximas. Laudo vero plurimum tuam in hoc erga Regem, optime de omnibus meritum, piam et perpetuam memoriam. Sciotericum horizontale, quod antea promiseram, iam mitto. Accepisses id citius cum GERARDO filio, si is me ante suum ad te abitum, ut sperabam, invisisset. Est autem horarium illud satis diligenter elaboratum, neque aliquid latet vitij, quo minus omnia ad amussim indicabit, quae carmina septena in frontispicio addita pollicentur, modo exquisite ad planum Horizontis, in columna (quod satius est) lapidea firmetur, sicque in ea disponatur, ut linea Meridiei, quae est horae duodecimae, exquisite Meridiano coelesti substernatur, quod qua ratione compendiose et certius quam per Compassum effici potest, exhibitori harum ostendi, qui tibi, modo satis indicationem nostram memoriâ retinuerit, omnia aperiet. Rogo peramanter, velis hoc munusculum boni consulere. Quod ad papyrum attinet, scias eam, quam ultimo misisti, de qua risas 3 ½ (deficiente a dimidia balla, quam te mittere significasti, risa 1 ½) accepi, esse satis idoneam et apprime oportunam meo operi, si modo de hac in aliqua copia obtinere possemus; desidero enim ad minimum praeter eam, quam aliunde conquiro, ballas adhuc plus minus decem, quas, si tua authoritate et procuratione ante hyemem iusto pretio acquisivero, nihil mihi gratius evenire poterit. Et licet haec ipsa, quam ultimo a te accepi, paululum mea priore maior sit, tamen id non tam interest, quam si minor foret; tunc enim esset prorsus inutilis, at nunc extraeminentia illa perexigua facile inter ligandum libros vel alias antea praecidetur. Utinam saltem de tali papyro in ea, qua dixi, copia mature et certo nancisci queam. Dubia illa, quae Clarissimus vir D. CASPAR PEUCERUS et alter ille non vulgaris, ut apparet (licet nomen eius mihi prius non innotuerat) Mathematicus D. GEORGIUS RULLENHAGIUS in mea dispositione revolutionum coelestium tibi per literas indicârunt, admodum facile sunt resolubilia. Nam quantum ad PEUCERUM attinet, nondum satis animadvertebat is, quod quando Sol, qui est centrum circuituum reliquorum Planetarum, deferretur in alteram partem, quae in delineatione inter octavam Sphaeram et orbem ♄ni otiosa videtur, tunc orbes imaginarios errantium Stellarum complere illud spatium eodem modo quo superius, quod nunc in figura vacuum apparet, et sic circumcirca eundo, ob centri annuam cum Sole concomitantiam, requiritur necessario tantum intercapedinis inter octavam Sphaeram et extimum ♄ni orbem, qua parte terris proximus fit, relinqui, ut ascensus et descensus Planetarum, in tota illa annua circumgyratione, capacitatem sine impedimento competentem, invenire possit, idque spatij orbis Solaris diametrum adaequat. Alterius haesitatio adhuc minori negotio resolvitur; id enim nunquam fieri potest, quod RULLENHAGIUS veretur, ut corpora Solis et ♂is concurrant, nam licet orbes fictitij eorum se invicem in delineatione transeant, tamen cum Sol ad illum locum pervenerit, ubi intersectio illa mutua notata apparet, Martius iste orbis Solarem longe alio in loco transibit, et stella ipsa ♂is tantundem a Sole distabit, quantum prius et alias semper, siquidem eum perpetuo in suo circuitu pro centro respicit. Impossibile enim est circumferentiam circuli centro uniri. Habes itaque utraque illorum dubia sufficienter explanata. Cuperem plurimum, ut mecum etiam communicares libros illos Genesium et Praeceptorum, quorum RULLENHAGIUS in principio suarum ad te literarum mentionem facit; ubi eas introspexero perlegeroque, statim remittam, neque alios eorum reddam participes. Quia vero ipsemet luculenter PEUCERO scripsi, et non solum dubitationem eius illi enodavi, sed etiam occasionem inventionis huius novae hypotheseos, unâ cum quibusdam alijs ipsi exposui, mitto earundem literarum ad te exemplar, ut plenius ex ijs, quae ad hanc rem faciant, cognoscere queas. Reddidi etiam illum compotem exemplarium quorundam literarum Illustris. Principis GIUILIELMI Landgravij Hassiae et ipsius Celsis Mathematici, ad me de materia Astronomica datarum, unâ cum mea ad singulas responsione, ut inde plurima ad Astronomicum negotium rite excolendum impense facientia uberius cognoscat. Quod si quae sunt literae inter has, quarum transcriptionem a me prius non obtinuisti (quod procul dubio in ijs, quae ultimo inter nos cis et ultra missae sunt, contingit) licebit utique aperto fasciculo eos inspicere perlegereque, aut etiam transcribi, si tibi ita visum fuerit, curare, modo postea rursus eodem modo componantur, tuoque sigillo denuo muniantur. Valeat tua Generositas cum uxore et liberis totaque domo quam felicissime. Nec dubito quin nervos omnes intensurus sis, ut papyrum illam mature et satis copiosam obtineam, dabo operam, ut in nominis tui, alias per se clari, celebratione, siquidem aliud praestare nequeam, me gratissimum et tui studiosissimum invenias.

Iterum et terque quaterque vale.
Datae Uraniburgi die 13. Septembris Anno 1588.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, pp. 126–127; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Quia vero expositio et declaratio mearum hypothesium e sola delineatione non a quovis ita facile percipitur, quoddam Mechanicum Organum confieri ex orichalco curavi, quo circuitus Planetarum respectu Solis, et ipsius Solis ac Lunae circa Terram manibus circumagi expeditiusque intelligi possunt, per quod etiam dubia illa tum PEUCERI tum etiam RULLENHAGIJ facillime extricantur, quemadmodum harum exhibitor (modo caput iuridicum sacra Uraniae sibi diligenter satis capere et memoria retinere queat, quod optarem quidem, sperare autem vix possum) tibi plenius ostendet; mitto enim tibi per eundem eiusdem Aequatorij Ideam, quam ut boni consulas rogo. Si GERARDUS filius tuus huc venisset, per unam saltem vel alteram horam, antequam iter suum ad te direxit, utique tum haec, tum alia ipsi melius et perfectius tibi referenda (cum sit ingeniosulus) declarassem. Petij ter vel quater idipsum serio ab eo per literas, verum is semper negotia (nescio quae) praetendebat. At nihilominus tantum illi suppetebat otij, ut ijsdem diebus nuptijs Landskronensibus, nihil ad se pertinentibus, interesse potuerit, et Huena exquisite in medio directi itineris a Croneburgo Landscroniam versus aut econtra iacet, ita ut vel in abitu aut reditu me sine ulla molestia tuo nomine invisere potuisset, modo voluntas promta non defuisset, quod me tibi per literas indicaturum, OSWALDO, ut illi idipsum significaret, iniunxi. Id igitur nunc fecisse oportuit. Iterum vale. Tui Hodierni. Tycho Brahe. / Mitto adhuc bina Exemplaria libri nostri secundi de aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis, et puto te iam septena accepisse, quod ideo refero, ut in memoria retineatur, quibusnam singula communicata sint, ut illi ijdem reliquas etiam operis partes aliquando adipiscantur.

To the most worthy and noble gentleman, Master Heinrich Rantzau, lord in Breitenburg, governor of the castle of Segeberg and royal viceroy in Holstein, his friend to be honoured above all.

Within these few weeks I have received from you three or four letters, most noble and worthy sir, in each of which you abundantly declare your goodwill towards me and your zeal and diligence in procuring printing-paper suitable for our typographical workshop. On that account, and also for the engraving [typus] of the chapel which you had built in honour of our best and never sufficiently praised King, of most pious memory (which I received together [with the letters]), I have cause to render you the greatest thanks. Indeed I greatly commend in this your pious and perpetual remembrance towards the King, who deserved most well of all. The horizontal sundial [sciotericum horizontale] which I had earlier promised I am now sending. You would have received it sooner by way of your son Gerhard, had he, before his departure to you, visited me as I had hoped. Now that timepiece is wrought with sufficient care, nor does any defect lie hidden in it that would keep it from indicating everything exactly [ad amussim], as the seven verses added on its face promise — provided only that it be fixed precisely to the plane of the horizon, upon a stone column (which is better), and so arranged upon it that the line of midday, which is that of the twelfth hour, be laid exactly under the celestial meridian; by what method this can be effected concisely and more surely than by a compass [Compassus], I have shown to the bearer of this letter, who will explain everything to you, provided he has retained our instruction well enough in memory. I most affectionately ask that you would be willing to take this little gift in good part. As for the paper: know that the [paper] which you last sent — of which I received 3½ reams [risae] (the half-bale [balla] short, which you indicated you were sending, being 1½ reams) — is sufficiently suitable and exceedingly well-fitted to my work, if only we could obtain some quantity of this kind; for I need, at the least, besides that which I am procuring from elsewhere, still about ten bales more, and if by your authority and agency I shall acquire these before winter at a fair price, nothing more welcome could befall me. And although this very paper which I last received from you is a little larger than my earlier [stock], yet that matters not so much as if it were smaller; for then it would be wholly useless, whereas now that very slight overhang [extraeminentia], being exceedingly small, will easily be trimmed off beforehand in the binding of the books or otherwise. Would that I might at least obtain such paper, in the quantity I have said, in good time and for certain. Those doubts which the most distinguished gentleman Master Caspar Peucer, and that other mathematician — no common one, as it appears (though his name had not previously become known to me) — Master Georg Rollenhagen indicated to you by letter concerning my arrangement of the celestial revolutions, are very easily resolved. For as far as Peucer is concerned, he had not yet sufficiently observed that, when the Sun — which is the centre of the circuits of the remaining planets — should be carried into the other part, which in the diagram [delineatio] appears idle [otiosa] between the eighth sphere and the orb of Saturn [♄], then the imaginary orbs of the wandering stars fill that space in the same way as above [in the part] which now appears empty in the figure; and so, going round about, on account of the centre's yearly accompanying of the Sun, it is necessarily required that only so much interval be left between the eighth sphere and the outermost orb of Saturn [♄], in that part where it becomes nearest to the Earth, as will allow the ascent and descent of the planets, in that whole yearly revolution, to find a fitting capacity without impediment; and that [interval of] space equals the diameter of the Sun's orb. The other man's hesitation is resolved with even less trouble; for that which Rollenhagen fears can never happen — namely, that the bodies of the Sun and of Mars [♂] should come together — for although their fictitious orbs cross one another in the diagram, yet when the Sun shall have arrived at that place where that mutual intersection appears marked, that orb of Mars will pass [through] the Sun's [orb] at a place far otherwise [located], and the body of Mars [♂] itself will be just as far distant from the Sun as before and at all other times, since indeed it perpetually regards the Sun as the centre in its circuit. For it is impossible that the circumference of a circle be united with its centre. You have therefore both of their doubts sufficiently explained. I should very much wish that you would also share with me those books of Genitures and of Precepts of which Rollenhagen makes mention at the beginning of his letters to you; once I have examined and read them through, I shall return them at once, nor make any others sharers of them. But because I myself have written clearly to Peucer, and have not only unravelled his doubt for him but have also set out for him the occasion of the discovery of this new hypothesis, together with certain other matters, I am sending you a copy of that same letter, that you may more fully learn from it the things that bear upon this matter. I have also put him [Peucer] in possession of copies of certain letters of the Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, and of His Highness's mathematician, written to me on an astronomical subject, together with my reply to each, so that from them he may more amply learn the very many things that bear earnestly upon the duly cultivating of the astronomical enterprise. And if there are any letters among these whose transcription you did not previously obtain from me (which without doubt occurs among those which were last sent between us, hither and thither), it will indeed be permitted to open the packet, to inspect and read them through, or even to have them transcribed, if it shall so seem good to you — provided that afterwards they be put together again in the same manner and secured anew with your seal. May Your Generosity, with your wife and children and your whole household, fare most happily. Nor do I doubt that you will strain every sinew so that I may obtain that paper in good time and in sufficient plenty; I shall take pains that, in the celebration of your name (otherwise illustrious of itself) — since I can render nothing else — you may find me most grateful and most devoted to you.

Again, and thrice and four times over, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, the 13th day of September, in the year 1588.

[Postscript:] But because the exposition and explanation of my hypotheses is not so easily grasped by everyone from a drawing alone, I have had a certain Mechanical Instrument [Organum] made of brass, by which the circuits of the Planets with respect to the Sun, and of the Sun itself and the Moon about the Earth, can be turned round by hand and the more readily understood; by which too those doubts both of Peucer and of Rollenhagen are most easily disentangled, as the bearer of this letter will more fully show you (provided only that his lawyer's head can take in the mysteries of Urania carefully enough and hold them in memory — which I should indeed wish, but can scarcely hope). For I send you by the same man a design of the same Equatorium, which I ask that you take in good part. Had your son Gerhard come hither, even for one hour or two, before he directed his journey to you, I should assuredly have made these things, and others to be reported to you, clearer and more perfectly to him (since he is rather clever). I asked the same of him seriously three or four times by letter, but he was always pleading affairs (I know not what). And yet he had leisure enough to spare to be present in those same days at the Landskrona wedding, which in no way concerned him; and Hven lies precisely in the middle of the direct route from Kronborg towards Landskrona or the reverse, so that either in going or returning he could have visited me in your name without any trouble, had only a ready will not been wanting — which I had enjoined upon Oswald to signify to him, that I would indicate it to you by letter. This therefore it behoved [me] now to have done. Again, farewell. Yours of today. Tycho Brahe. / I send besides two copies of our second book on the recent Phenomena of the Aethereal World, and I think you have by now received seven, which I mention for this reason: that it be kept in memory to whom the several copies have been communicated, so that those same persons may at some time obtain the remaining parts of the work also.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe to Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598; Latin Henricus Ranzovius), the great Holstein humanist, royal governor (Statthalter / regius vicarius) of the duchies under the Danish crown, lord of Breitenburg (Bredenberga) and Segeberg, owner of a celebrated library and his own printing connections. He was Tycho's most important patron-correspondent in the Empire and a key supplier of paper for the Uraniborg press; the salutation's full titulature is preserved by the edition's convention
  • Date / occasion. Datae Uraniburgi die 13. Septembris Anno 1588 — Uraniborg, 13 September 1588 (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline matches the catalogue label exactly; no discrepancy. The letter falls in the year Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (vol. II of his projected trilogy, printed at Uraniborg) appeared, presenting the geocentric-heliocentric world-system whose geometry is defended below.
  • typus Sacelli — "the engraving of the chapel." typus here is a printed/engraved representation (an image, not movable type); Rantzau had had built (and engraved) a memorial chapel honouring the late King Frederik II of Denmark (d. 11 April 1588), the "best and never sufficiently praised King, of most pious memory," Tycho's patron. Translated "engraving."
  • Sciotericum horizontale / horarium — a horizontal sundial (Gk. skiothērikon, shadow-instrument); horarium "timepiece." Rendered "horizontal sundial," consistent with instrument register. carmina septena in frontispicio — "seven verses on its face/frontispiece," an inscribed motto-poem on the dial-plate.
  • ad amussim — "exactly, to the line" (lit. by the carpenter's rule/plumb-line); idiom kept and glossed.
  • per Compassum — "by a compass." Here, in the context of orienting the dial's noon-line to the celestial meridian, this is a magnetic compass (for alignment), not a pair of dividers; Tycho says his shown method is surer than relying on the (magnetically-deviating) compass. Distinguished from the drawing-compass crux of the 1584 Frederik II letter; the referent here is reasonably secure from context.
  • Paper units. risa = ream (Ital./Lat. risma); balla = bale (a large bundle of reams). Tycho reports receiving 3½ reams, the consignment being 1½ reams short of the half-bale Rantzau had announced, and asks for about ten more bales for the press. Units kept in the source and glossed; the slightly larger sheet-size is welcome because the overhang (extraeminentia) can be trimmed in binding, whereas an undersized sheet would be useless.
  • Persons (objections to the world-system). D. CASPAR PEUCERUS = Caspar Peucer (1525–1602), Melanchthon's son-in-law, Wittenberg physician-astronomer. D. GEORGIUS RULLENHAGIUS = Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), Magdeburg rector, poet (Froschmeuseler) and amateur mathematician; Tycho notes his name was previously unknown to him. Both had relayed objections, through Rantzau, to Tycho's dispositio revolutionum coelestium (his diagram of the world-system in De mundi).
  • The geometry of the world-system (technical core). Two objections are answered:
  • Peucer's: the region of the diagram "between the eighth sphere and the orb of Saturn" looks idle/empty (otiosa … vacuum). Tycho replies that because the Sun is the moving centre of the planets' circuits and travels yearly round the Earth, the planets' imaginary orbs sweep through that apparently-empty space too as the Sun moves to the opposite side; the gap that must be left between Saturn's outermost orb (at its nearest approach to Earth) and the eighth (fixed-star) sphere equals the diameter of the Sun's orb — exactly enough room for the planets' annual ascent and descent.
  • Rollenhagen's: he feared the bodies of the Sun and Mars would collide (corpora Solis et ♂is concurrant) where their circles cross in the figure. Tycho answers that the orbs are fictitious / geometrical (orbes fictitij … in delineatione), not solid; when the Sun reaches the marked intersection, Mars's orb crosses the Sun's orb elsewhere and Mars's body stays as far from the Sun as ever, since Mars always keeps the Sun as the centre of its circuit — "for it is impossible that the circumference of a circle be united with its centre." This reading is corroborated by Dreyer (Tycho Brahe) and Mosley (Bearing the Heavens) and Christianson (On Tycho's Island / Lord of Uraniborg), who record precisely this Rollenhagen "collision" worry and Tycho's no-solid-spheres rebuttal.
  • Glyphs. ♄ = Saturn, ♂ = Mars — named in English in the translation, the glyph kept in the source block by the edition's convention
  • libri Genesium et Praeceptorum — "books of Genitures [nativity-figures] and of Precepts," astrological works Rollenhagen had cited; Genesis = geniture/nativity by the edition's convention Tycho asks to borrow them.
  • Illustris. Principis GIUILIELMI Landgravij Hassiae et ipsius Celsis Mathematici — Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel and his court mathematician (Christoph Rothmann); Tycho is circulating the Kassel–Uraniborg astronomical correspondence. GIUILIELMI is the source's spelling of Gulielmi/Wilhelmi, kept as printed.
  • Gerardo filio — Gerhard Rantzau (1558–1627), Heinrich's son and eventual successor as royal governor of the duchies.
  • Subscription. Iterum et terque quaterque vale — "Again, and thrice and four times over, farewell" (a Virgilian terque quaterque flourish), kept literally.
  • Edition / witness. Edition of record: Dreyer, Opera Omnia (vol. VII, Epistolae). MS witness as given in the edition: A = Vienna, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 9737, f. (folio number not supplied in the source header).
  • §1 Dreyer collation. TWO closing postscripts (the brass equatorium Mechanicum Organum sent via the bearer + the Gerhard/Kronborg–Landskrona logistics; then two more copies of the De mundi aetherei book), restored from Dreyer VII pp.126–127 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. English corrected against Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII p.126: the sentence 'Reddidi etiam illum compotem... cognoscat' is grammatically 3rd person, continuing the Peucer clauses of the prior sentence, not addressed to Rantzau (who is 'te'/'tu' throughout, including the next sentence's 'obtinuisti'). The published translation's bracketed gloss '[Rantzau]' misattributed custody of the Landgrave Wilhelm/Rothmann correspondence copies; corrected to '[Peucer]'.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (high). The Latin's illum (accusative 'him') and the 3rd-person subjunctive cognoscat ('that HE may learn') continue the immediately preceding sentence, which is entirely about Caspar Peucer (Quia vero ipsemet luculenter PEUCERO scripsi... illi enodavi... ipsi exposui) - not the letter's addressee Rantzau, who is addressed throughout in the 2nd person (te/tu), including the very next sentence's obtinuisti ('you have not obtained'). The published English glosses illum as '[Rantzau]', inverting the custody: it was Peucer, not Rantzau, whom Tycho made possessor of the Landgrave-Wilhelm correspondence copies so that Peucer could learn more. This matches the process's known failure mode of misattributing custody of a correspondence archive. At: “I have also put him [Rantzau] in possession of copies of certain letters of the Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of”.

Repaired. Verified against the Dreyer page image, TBOO VII p.126 (letter spans pp.124-128; pages 124-127 examined, encompassing the full defect passage). The sentence in question uses the 3rd-person accusative 'illum' and 3rd-person subjunctive 'cognoscat', sandwiched between two clearly 2nd-person sentences addressed to Rantzau ('cognoscere queas' before it, 'obtinuisti' after it) and immediately following three consecutive 3rd-person references to Peucer ('PEUCERO scripsi', 'illi enodavi', 'ipsi exposui'). This confirms Peucer, not Rantzau, is the antecedent of 'illum'/'cognoscat', and the correction is grammatically secure. The Latin text itself is left untouched as instructed for a mistranslation defect (unflagged minor transcription variants, e.g. 'Illustris.'/'GIUILIELMI' vs Dreyer's 'Illustriss.'/'GUILIELMI', were noted but not touched, being out of scope for this defect).
