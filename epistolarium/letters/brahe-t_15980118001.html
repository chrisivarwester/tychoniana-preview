The original

MAGNIFICO ET PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, NOBILI DANO, DOMINO ET FAUTORI SUO OBSERVANDISSIMO. HambURGK.
S. P. D.

CUM nuper ex Italia per utramque Germaniam in Angliam venissem, Generose et præstantissime Domine TYCHO, accepissemque a quodam meo amico Domino MATTHÆO ROYDON, Anglo, Mathematum, ut mihi videbatur, scientissimo, te ex Dania atque Uraniburgo tuo in Germaniam concessisse, atque adeo cum Lare prope Francofurtum ad Mœnum consedisse, gaudebam simul et dolebam. Gaudebam equidem, tum quod meæ, quam alioqui in Daniam ad te suscepturus eram, profectionis compendium facerem; tum etiam quia sperabam te Hassiæ nostræ, cum in proximo eius esses, futurum aliquando inquilinum. Id quod quamvis optare verius, quam sperare potuerim, quippe qui non ignorabam te Daniæ Regnum patriam tuam cum Hassiæ vix commutaturum, nihilominus quo facilius illud crederem, fecit Illustrissimi Principis MAURITIJ Domini mei clementissimini in viros præclaros amor ac munificentia singularis, utpote et quæ tibi cum laudatissimæ memoriæ parente eius diu intercessit familiaritas. Dolebam tamen, quod adversi aliquid in Dania tibi evenisse referrent, tuæque abitionis ex Dania alij alias recensebant causas. Siquidem fuerunt hîc in Anglia, qui tibi cum PETRO SEVERINO Medico litem fuisse nescio quam asseverarent, teque indignitatis adversarij puditum esse, et ideo inde discessisse. Alij Cancellarium Regium feudum aliquod tibi a FRIDERICO II felicis memoriæ concessum, sibi arrogasse, aliaque ad Astronomiæ studium exædificandum adminicula, ab eodem Rege et Regni Proceribus tibi nuncupata, nunc fuisse adempta caussabantur. Hoc verisimilius et mihi et illis visum est, illud de medico plane incredibile. Quotusquisque enim est, qui ignorat, te ex præclarissima prosapia ibidem prognatum, et summæ apud Regem auctoritatis virum tam levi molimine non posse ex patria moveri, ut taceam e patria emoveri. Verum cum in Angliam etiam delatum sit, SEVERINUM illum apud hunc novellum Cancellarium multum valere, adeo ut is ferme totus ab illo dependeat, atque obscurum non sit, Doctorem illum (ut referunt) diutina tui invidia laborasse, fortassis hoc, quod per semetipsum non potuit exequi, per hunc Cancellarium, cui Rex plurimum tribuere dicitur, effectum dedisse.

Hæc, Illustrissime et præstantissime Vir, quamvis ad me nihil spectant, tibi significo, ut etiam externorum de tuo Uraniburgo inopinato discessu iudicium ac sollicitudinem cognoscas. Cæterum paucis abhinc diebus, cum de loco ubi hæreres sollicitus, eos qui ex Germania recens venirent, interrogarem, intelligo te non prope Francofurtum, ut ille existimabat, sed iuxta Hamburgum sedes posuisse. Quare rogatus ab amicis Anglis, qui ad te abituri erant, has ad te depropero, ut cognoscam, an inibi sis mansurus, an vero alio iturus. Qua in re illis rem gratam facturum me non dubito, tibi etiam non iniucundum futurum illud arbitratus sum, si intelligeres etiam in Anglia esse, quos amor tui sollicite tangeret. Quamobrem plane confido, te has meas literas, quamvis ab homine tibi hactenus ignoto et tua familiaritate indigno profectæ sint, æqui bonique consulturum, et ad illas prima data occasione responsurum.

Faxit Deus, ut te aliquando, Vir Præstantissime, in Hassia videamus, exinde enim magnum decus ac nomen accessurum Patriæ meæ existimarem. Sin minus id superis visum sit, efficiam, ut te aliquando, volente Deo, ubicunque Germaniæ etiam sis, invisam, coramque venerer. Vale et meæ impudentiæ ignosce, vir longe præstantissime. Salutem porro accipe a multis in Anglia tibi etiam forte ignotis, nominis tamen tui observantissimis.

LondiniAngliæ, anno 1598, 15. Calend. Februarij.
T. Magnif. addictissimus
HENRICUS ELLENBERGER,
Hassus. M.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and most excellent man, Master Tycho Brahe, noble Dane, his lord and patron most to be esteemed. [At] Hamburg.
Sends [him] heartiest greeting.

When I had lately come out of Italy, through both Germanies [i.e. Upper and Lower Germany], into England, generous and most excellent Master Tycho, and had learned from a certain friend of mine, Master Matthew Roydon, an Englishman most learned (as it seemed to me) in the mathematical sciences, that you had withdrawn from Denmark and from your Uraniborg into Germany, and had even settled with your household near Frankfurt-am-Main, I rejoiced and grieved at once. I rejoiced, to be sure, both because I should thereby spare myself the journey I was otherwise about to undertake into Denmark to you, and also because I hoped that you would one day become a resident of our Hesse, since you would be in its near neighbourhood. And although I could wish for this more truly than I could hope for it — for I was not unaware that you would scarcely exchange the Kingdom of Denmark, your native land, for Hesse — nevertheless what made me believe it the more readily was the singular love and munificence of the Most Illustrious Prince Maurice, my most clement lord, toward distinguished men, and likewise the long-standing familiarity that subsisted between you and his parent of most praised memory. Yet I grieved, because they reported that something adverse had befallen you in Denmark, and different men gave different accounts of your departure from Denmark. For there were here in England those who asserted that you had had some quarrel or other — I know not what — with Peter Severinus the physician, and that you had been ashamed of your adversary's unworthiness, and had therefore departed thence. Others alleged that the Royal Chancellor had arrogated to himself a certain fief granted to you by Frederick II of happy memory, and that the other aids for building up the study of astronomy, assigned to you by that same King and by the Magnates of the Realm, had now been taken away. This seemed the more likely both to me and to them; that other [story] about the physician, plainly incredible. For how few there are who do not know that you, sprung there from a most distinguished lineage and a man of the highest authority with the King, cannot by so slight a contrivance be moved out of your homeland — to say nothing of being driven out of your homeland. But since it has also been brought to England that that Severinus prevails greatly with this new Chancellor, so much so that the latter depends almost wholly upon him, and since it is not obscure that that Doctor (as they report) has long laboured under an envy of you, perhaps he has accomplished, through this Chancellor — to whom the King is said to grant very much — that which he could not carry out by himself.

These things, Most Illustrious and most excellent man, although they in no way concern me, I make known to you, so that you may also learn the judgement and the anxious concern of foreigners regarding your unlooked-for departure from your Uraniborg. For the rest, a few days ago, when, anxious about the place where you might be lodging, I was questioning those who had recently come from Germany, I understand that you have placed your seat not near Frankfurt, as he supposed, but next to Hamburg. Wherefore, being asked by my English friends who were about to set out to you, I hasten this [letter] to you, that I may learn whether you are going to remain there, or rather to go elsewhere. In which matter I doubt not that I shall be doing them a welcome service; and I judged that it would also be not unpleasing to you, were you to understand that there are even in England those whom love of you anxiously touches. For which reason I fully trust that you will take this my letter in good part — although it has proceeded from a man hitherto unknown to you and unworthy of your familiarity — and will answer it at the first opportunity given.

God grant that we may one day see you, Most excellent man, in Hesse; for from that I should reckon that great honour and renown would accrue to my native land. But if it be less than this in the eyes of those above, I shall bring it about that one day, God willing, wherever in Germany you may be, I shall visit you and revere you in person. Farewell, and forgive my boldness, man by far most excellent. Receive, further, a greeting from many in England, perhaps likewise unknown to you, yet most respectful of your name.

At London in England, in the year 1598, on the 15th [day before the] Kalends of February.
Your Magnificence's most devoted,
Henricus Ellenberger,
a Hessian. M[aster].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent / sender. The edition header gives the sender as "Heinrich Ellenberger," matching the subscription Henricus Ellenberger, Hassus. M. — Henricus (Heinrich) Ellenberger, a Hessian (Hassus) holding the degree Magister (M.), writing from London as a young scholar previously unknown to Tycho (he calls himself homine tibi hactenus ignoto, "a man hitherto unknown to you"). He presents himself as a subject and well-wisher of the Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel.
  • Recipient / address. TYCHONI BRAHE, NOBILI DANO … HambURGK — Tycho, then in the Hamburg area during the early exile (Wandsbek/Wandsburg, just outside Hamburg, where he had settled in 1597–98 after leaving Hven). The address-line town Hamburg corrects the rumour, repeated in the body, that he was near Frankfurt.
  • S. P. D. = salutem plurimam dicit, "sends heartiest greeting".
  • Matthew Roydon. MATTHAeO ROYDON, Anglo = Matthew Roydon (d. c. 1622), the English poet and member of the Chapman–Raleigh literary-scientific circle, here Ellenberger's informant on Tycho's movements. The Dreyer Opera Omnia index (vol. XV) records the name at VIII 21, 462, locating this letter in Dreyer VIII.
  • Maurice of Hesse. Illustrissimi Principis MAURITIJ Domini mei clementissimi = Moritz ("the Learned"), Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (r. 1592–1627), Ellenberger's sovereign. laudatissimae memoriae parente eius ("his parent of most praised memory") = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (d. 1592), Tycho's astronomical correspondent — hence the "long-standing familiarity" Ellenberger invokes as grounds for hoping Tycho would settle in Hesse.
  • Peter Severinus. PETRO SEVERINO Medico = Peder Sørensen / Petrus Severinus (1542–1602), royal physician (Medicus) at the Danish court and a leading Paracelsian. The letter relays — and Ellenberger himself rejects as plane incredibile ("plainly incredible") — an English rumour of a lis ("quarrel/lawsuit") between Tycho and Severinus; he then floats the alternative that Severinus may have worked against Tycho indirectly, per hunc Cancellarium, through the new Chancellor. No silent sharpening: the whole Severinus narrative is reported speech, governed by asseverarent … referrent … ut referunt … fortassis ("they asserted … they reported … as they report … perhaps"); the hedges are preserved and the rumour is not stated as fact.
  • The Chancellor and the fief. Cancellarium Regium … feudum … a FRIDERICO II felicis memoriae concessum — the Royal Chancellor of Denmark (hunc novellum Cancellarium, "this new Chancellor": Christian Friis of Borreby, appointed 1596) is rumoured to have seized a fief granted to Tycho by Frederick II ("of happy memory," d. 1588) and to have withdrawn the other supports (adminicula) for astronomy assigned by the King and the realm's magnates. This — the loss of Crown patronage under the new regime of Christian IV — Ellenberger and his informants find verisimilius ("the more likely") explanation for Tycho's departure.
  • per utramque Germaniam — "through both Germanies," i.e. Upper and Lower Germany (the customary early-modern division), not two distinct countries; rendered literally with the gloss.
  • Persons sending greetings. Salutem … a multis in Anglia … nominis tui observantissimis — anonymous English admirers of Tycho's name; left unnamed in the source.
  • Dateline / calendar. LondiniAngliae, anno 1598, 15. Calend. Februarij = "At London in England, 1598, the 15th day before the Kalends of February." Reckoned inclusively (Kalends of February = 1 February as day 1), 15 Kal. Feb. = 18 January. This agrees with the edition's catalogue label "1598. 18. januar." England and Denmark both still kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar in 1598; the date is therefore O.S. with no conversion required, and none is applied. Anno 1598: by the Julian historical year used here, with no Annunciation-style year-start complication arising in the text.
  • T. Magnif. addictissimus = Tuae Magnificentiae addictissimus, "most devoted to Your Magnificence," the subscription formula matched in register to the honorific patterns.
  • Orthography. Printed PRAeSTANTISSIMO, MATTHAeO, clementissimini (a printer's slip for clementissimi), LondiniAngliae (set without space) are reproduced as printed in the Source block; clementissimini is read as clementissimi in the translation, the printed form retained above by this chapter's convention
