The original

PRIUILEGIUM TYCHONI BRAHÆ CONCESSUM COLDINGÆ VII MARTIJ ANNO 1594.

VNIUERSIS et singulis tenore præsentium notum facimus: Cum generosus ac fidelis nobis dilectus Tycho Brahæ de Knudtstrup ex antiqua et præclara huius regni nostri nobili Brahorum familia originem ducens post varios diuturnosque in studio Astronomico, cui inde ab ineunte ætate fuit addictissimus, exantlatos labores fructus eorundem nonnullos haud asspernendos, nunc in communem reipublicæ literariæ vtilitatem in publicum edere decreuerit: Astronomicæ videlicet instaurationis progymnasmata quædam luculenta, integro quod adornat operi præmittenda, ac vereri se nobis demisse ostenderit ne idem opus, postquam publicatum fuerit, alij temere non exiguo ipsius detrimento æmulentur, et propterea nobis quam humilime supplicauerit, vt pro solita nostra clementia indemnem ipsum præstemus. Visum est nobis pro fauore nostro erga eum singulari hoc illi diplomate nostro regio prouidere, quo serio ac seuere mandamus cautumque esse volumus, ne qui omnino in regnis ducatibus atque prouincijs nostris cuiuscunque status aut conditionis existant ullos libros ab ipso Tychone Brahæ siue proprijs siue alienis typis editos, præsertim eos, qui rem Philosophicam atque Astronomicam concernunt, vel recudere vel alibi impressos aut recusos adducere vel uenundare, siue totaliter siue partialiter, sine ipsius hæredumue eius consensu, publice uel occulte præsumant, sub pæna decem marcarum auri puri pro dimidia fisco nostro regio, reliqua uero parte ipsi Tychoni Brahæ siue eius hæredibus (præter librorum omnium amissionem, quos ipsis uel per se uel suos sibi vendicare, inque potestatem suam redigere fas esto) nulla sperata remissione applicandarum. In cuius rei ac voluntatis nostræ fidem et testimonium hoc diploma regio nostro sigillo munitum, manu propria subscripsimus ac a regni nostri senatoribus nunc præsentibus subscribendum mandauimus.

Actum in regia nostra Coldingæ.

English translation machine translation, Secure

A privilege granted to Tycho Brahe at Kolding, 7 March in the year 1594.

To each and all we make known by the tenor of these presents: Whereas the well-born Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, faithful and beloved of us, who draws his origin from the ancient and illustrious noble family of the Brahes of this our realm, after various and long-continued labours exhausted in the study of astronomy — to which from his earliest years he has been most devoted — has now resolved to publish, for the common benefit of the republic of letters, certain not-to-be-despised fruits of those same labours: namely certain brilliant preliminary exercises [progymnasmata] of the restoration of astronomy [astronomicae instaurationis], to be set before the whole work that he is preparing; and whereas he has humbly shown us that he fears lest, after that work has been published, others should rashly emulate [it] to his no slight detriment, and has therefore most humbly besought us that, of our accustomed clemency, we keep him from harm: it has seemed good to us, in our singular favour toward him, to provide for him by this our royal diploma, whereby we earnestly and strictly command, and will it to be enacted, that none whatsoever within our kingdoms, duchies, and provinces, of whatever estate or condition they be, shall presume — publicly or covertly — either to reprint any books published by Tycho Brahe himself, whether from his own presses or from others', and especially those that concern philosophical and astronomical matters, or to bring in [copies] printed or reprinted elsewhere, or to put [them] up for sale, whether wholly or in part, without the consent of him or of his heirs; under penalty of ten marks of pure gold — one half to be applied to our royal treasury, the other half to Tycho Brahe himself or to his heirs (besides the forfeiture of all the books, which they, whether by themselves or through their own [agents], shall be entitled to claim for themselves and to bring into their own power) — with no remission to be hoped for. In faith and testimony of which matter and of our will, we have subscribed this diploma, fortified with our royal seal, with our own hand, and have commanded it to be subscribed by the senators of our realm now present.

Done at our royal [residence of] Kolding.

Apparatus

  • Issuer / document type. A royal printing privilege (privilegium / diploma regium) issued in the name of Christian IV of Denmark (r. 1588–1648), then in his minority under the regency council; the edition names "Christian 4." as sender. It is a one-party legal instrument (a grant), not an epistolary exchange — hence the "recipient" is properly the realm at large, with Tycho Brahe as the beneficiary; the header records this. The text is cast throughout in the royal "we" (facimus, mandamus, subscripsimus).
  • Beneficiary. Tycho Brahae de Knudtstrup = Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup (the family seat in Scania), here styled generosus ac fidelis nobis dilectus ("well-born, faithful and beloved of us"), the standard chancery honorific for a noble subject.
  • The work protected. Astronomicae instaurationis progymnasmata = the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata ("preliminary exercises [toward] the restoration of astronomy"), Tycho's great work on the new star of 1572 and on solar/lunar theory, then in press at Uraniborg and not completed until after his death (1602). progymnasmata (Greek προγυμνάσματα, "preliminary exercises/drills") is Tycho's own title-word, kept and glossed. instauratio (astronomiae) → "restoration (of astronomy)" by the edition's convention — Tycho's programmatic term; the genitive here (instaurationis) modifies the progymnasmata. integro... operi praemittenda — "to be set before the whole work," i.e. these exercises form the opening volume of a larger projected corpus.
  • siue proprijs siue alienis typis editos — "published whether from his own presses or from others'." A pointed clause: Tycho ran his own press at Uraniborg (proprijs typis), and the privilege covers both his self-printed books and any printed for him elsewhere.
  • The penalty clause. sub paena decem marcarum auri puri = "under penalty of ten marks of pure gold," split pro dimidia... reliqua uero parte (half to the royal fiscus / treasury, half to Tycho or his heirs) — the conventional fine-sharing formula. praeter librorum omnium amissionem... quos... sibi vendicare... fas esto = "besides the forfeiture of all the [offending] books, which [the heirs] shall be entitled (fas esto) to claim and seize." The parenthesis is a grant of right to Tycho's side, not a further penalty; rendered as such.
  • Negations (checked). The operative prohibition carries the structure ne qui... ullos libros... vel recudere vel... adducere vel uenundare... sine... consensu... praesumant — one governing negation (ne... praesumant, "that none presume"), with sine ipsius... consensu ("without his consent") as the condition. Three negation-bearing elements total (ne qui, ullos, sine consensu); all three reproduced, none added. The force is: no one may do any of the three acts (reprint / import / sell) without consent. nulla sperata remissione ("with no remission to be hoped for") closes the clause and is preserved.
  • Reading note (transcription vs. Dreyer page image). The working transcription reads supplicauerit (3rd-sg., agreeing with Tycho as subject of the Cum... clause); the Dreyer digitised text layer shows supplicauere, but Dreyer's printed text is heavily mangled by long-s/ligature digitised text layer errors in this passage (e.g. Typhone for Tychone, Angulari for singulari, exibant for existant, recuses for recusos). The singular supplicauerit is grammatically required by the surrounding singular verbs (decreuerit... ostenderit) and is followed here; the variant is noted, not silently corrected.
  • Place / dateline. The date and place stand only in the rubric heading: COLDINGAe VII MARTIJ ANNO 1594 = "at Kolding [Coldingae], 7 March in the year 1594" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The body's closing Actum in regia nostra Coldingae ("Done at our royal [seat at] Kolding") repeats the place without a date. The edition label ("1594. 7. marts. Kolding") agrees with the rubric; no discrepancy. Kolding (Koldinghus) was a principal royal residence in Jutland. The line Ex Archivo Regni Daniae: Latina 1586–99, fol. 289 f. in the Dreyer page image is the editor's source-note, not part of the document.
  • reipublicae literariae = "the republic of letters," the humanist commonwealth of learning; kept as the period idiom.
