The original

HIERONYMUS WOLFIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

DUO Dani studiosi hâc transeuntes, qui se tua familiaritate aliquando usuros sperant, petierunt a me, ut sibi literulas ad Excellentiam tuam darem, quo se mecum fuisse probare possent. Etsi autem nuper nescio quid scripseram, neque nunc quicquam scriptione magnopere dignum habebam, tamen quia, mi nobilissime et doctissime D. TYCHO, libenter etiam absens tecum colloquor, morem illis gessi. In observatione proximæ eclipseos nostrates Astrologi omnes, alij plus, alij minus, a vero initio aberrarunt. Exquisitiora abs te expectamus. Nova quæ passim feruntur, e popularibus tuis cognosces. Augustana plæraque antiquum obtinent. Consul HAINZELIUS tibi familiarissimus superiori anno elocavit filiam, eius frater duobus filijs locupletes puellas uxores dedit. Nos Scholastici etsi rerum nostrarum satagentes, asininam fortunam æqui bonique facimus; tuæque Excellentiæ cœleste illud et beatum insulæ Uraniæ, vel potius fortunatæ, otium gratulamur.

Vale.
Augustæ Vind. pridie Idus Martias Anno 1580.
T. Excellentiæ studiosiss.
Hieron. Wolfius.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Hieronymus Wolf to Tycho Brahe.

Two Danish students passing this way, who hope to enjoy your acquaintance one day, asked me to give them a little letter to Your Excellency, by which they might prove that they had been with me. And although I had lately written something or other [to you], and had nothing now much worth the writing, yet — since, my most noble and most learned Master Tycho, I gladly converse with you even when absent — I obliged them. In the observation of the recent eclipse all our astrologers, some more, some less, strayed from the true beginning [of it]. From you we expect more exact things. The news that is bandied about everywhere you will learn from your own countrymen. Most things at Augsburg keep to their old course. Consul Hainzel, your most intimate friend, married off a daughter last year; his brother gave wealthy girls in marriage to two sons. We scholastics, though busied about our own affairs, take our asinine fortune in good part; and we congratulate Your Excellency on that heavenly and blessed leisure of the isle of Urania — or rather, of the fortunate isle.

Farewell.
At Augsburg, the day before the Ides of March [14 March], in the year 1580.
Most devoted to Your Excellency,
Hieronymus Wolf.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Hieronymus Wolfius = Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and Greek scholar (the "father of Byzantine studies"), then directing the city's library and school, and an early mentor-friend of Tycho's from his Augsburg years (1569–70). This is among the last of his letters to Tycho; Wolf died on 8 October 1580, some months after writing it.
  • Recipient/place. Tycho Brahe, by 1580 established on Hven at Uraniborg; Augusta Vindelicorum = Augsburg.
  • Duo Dani studiosi — "two Danish students": travelling Danes carrying the letter as a token of introduction (a commonplace of the humanist letter of recommendation). They are not named.
  • Consul Hainzelius … eius frater — "Consul Hainzel … his brother": the Hainzel brothers of Augsburg, patrician patrons of astronomy with whom Tycho had collaborated (with Paul Hainzel he built the great 19-foot Göppingen quadrant in 1569). The title Consul denotes the Augsburg burgomaster's office; the source names neither brother individually, so which of the two (Johann Baptist or Paul) is the Consul and which the frater is left as the text leaves it, rather than assigned. The brother is said to have married off two sons to wealthy girls.
  • Astronomy. in observatione proximae eclipseos … a vero initio aberrarunt — "in the observation of the recent eclipse … they strayed from the true beginning": the Augsburg astrologers misjudged the moment of first contact; Wolf defers to Tycho for exquisitiora, "more exact" determinations. The eclipse is not named in the letter; a total lunar eclipse fell on 31 January 1580 (O.S.), which a mid-March letter could naturally call "recent," but the identification is inferential and is not asserted by the text.
  • Astrologi — rendered "astrologers" in the period's undifferentiated sense (astronomer-astrologers); no separate technical term is used.
  • insulae Uraniae, vel potius fortunatae — "the isle of Urania, or rather the fortunate isle": a learned pun. Insula Uraniae names Hven after Urania, muse of astronomy (the conceit behind Uraniborg); Wolf glosses it as the insula fortunata — the "Fortunate Isle" of classical geography — playing on Tycho's blessed otium (leisure) there. The wordplay is kept rather than flattened.
  • Nos Scholastici … asininam fortunam aequi bonique facimus — "We scholastics … take our asinine fortune in good part": aequi bonique facere is the idiom "to take in good part, put up with"; asinina fortuna ("an ass's lot") is Wolf's self-deprecating figure for the scholar's drudgery (cf. the ergastulum of his 1571 letter). Kept as metaphor.
  • Augustana plaeraque antiquum obtinent — "most things at Augsburg keep to their old course," i.e. little has changed; plaeraque = pleraque.
  • Orthography. Humanist Latin: hâc (with circumflex, "this way"); literulas (diminutive, "a little letter"); consonantal j in alij, filijs, popularibus tuis; plaeraque for pleraque. Reproduced as printed in the Source block.
  • Salutation/subscription. T. Excellentiae studiosiss. = Tuae Excellentiae studiosissimus, "Most devoted to Your Excellency".
  • Calendar / dating crux. Dateline: pridie Idus Martias Anno 1580 = the day before the Ides of March = 14 March 1580 (the Ides of March falling on the 15th), O.S. The edition catalogues the letter as "1580. 14. januar" (14 January). The two disagree on the month (March vs January) while sharing the day (14th) and year (1580); the disagreement is plausibly a Martias/Januarias transcription or cataloguing slip. Per this chapter's convention the as-written dateline governs the header — hence 14 March 1580, with the catalogue date appended and the discrepancy flagged here rather than reconciled. No date is asserted that the source text does not substantiate.
