The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS, DOCTRINA AC VIRTUTE PRAESTANTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO HÆREDITARIO IN KNUDSTORFF, ASTRONOMO EXCELLENTISSIMO, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO. PRAGAe.

S. P.

NOBILISSIME et clarissime vir, Domine et amice inprimis colende. Binas abs te accepi literas hoc mense Septembri; priores die 8 mihi reddidit M. CHRISTIERNUS, qui ad tres septimanas Witebergae substiterat, posteriores tabellarius Praga a D. GODELMANNO huc ablegatus heri. Scis autem stylo nos Iuliano hîc uti. Utraeque negocium pecuniarium tuum mihi commendarunt, de quo et ad affinem meum Dn. Cancellarium D. BORDINGUM scripseras, quem Lubecae tum agentem ego monui, ut negocium istud suo loco promoveret. Venit is ante paucos dies Lubeca Gustrovium ab Illustriss. Principe UDALRICO vocatus, postquam Cels. eius in venatione tum circa Grabovium existenti nunciatum fuisset de obitu nepotis ex fratre Ducis SIGISMUNDI AUGUSTI, qui hydrope alijsque morbis diu conflictatus die 5 Septemb. Ivenaci pie decessit ex hac vita. Funus ei fiet die 10 Octob. Suerini, ubi parentis IOH. ALBERTI et fratris IOHANNIS aliorumque ex ea linea defunctorum corpora quiescunt. Eadem occasione Dn. Cancellarius Quaestorem Iuniorum Principum, qui et Ducis UDALRICI iam Quaestor est, in aula reperit, et effecit, ut hodie mihi per ministrum suum in meis aedibus 600 illos thaleros tibi debitos numeraverit, recepta apocha tua, quam interim apud me servaveram. Hanc ego pecuniam usque ad adventum D. GODELMANNI, quem huc brevi accessurum tibique in receptione pecuniae gratificaturum scribis, penes me retinebo. Ut autem in auream monetam commutem, haudquaquam fieri potest. Rarissime enim hîc vel minimum aureum cernimus, omni auro vel in Belgium vel in superiorem Germaniam, ubi maiori precio quam hîc censetur, hinc translato. Id tamen faciam, si potero, ut thaleros novos Belgicos, quorum multi summae huic intersunt, in alios Imperiales, uti vocant, commutem; hos enim gratiores istic esse arbitramur quam illos.

Porro quod ad continuationem Chronici D. CHYTRÆI p. m. qui postridie Iohannis nobis valedixit, attinet, neminem hoc quidem in loco eam ad rem magis idoneum esse iudico quam D. CHRISTOPHORUM STURTZIUM, qui historiarum lectioni in hac Academia magna cum laude praeest, ingenioque, memoria et facundia excellit. Quamprimum igitur Concilium Academiae convenerit, mentionem huius negocij et tuae commonefactionis inijciam, nec dubito, quin Domini Professores, quorum plerosque aetate, doctrina et prudentia graves esse non ignoras, id constituent, quod et Academiae nostrae et nomini magni illius CHYTRÆI honorificum et publice utile erit. Adveniente vero D. GODELMANNO, de eo, quod ad te privatim spectat, postremae Chronici parti inserendo, unâ cum D. CLINGIO, cum haeredibus D. CHYTRÆI libenter et diligenter agam, et si quae alia a me proficisci poterunt grata tibi officia, in illis promptum me semper reperies, donec vixero. Senesco enim iam, et post mensem, si Deus voluerit, annum 70 aetatis meae completurus sum. Facio tamen adhuc mediocriter vocationis meae in Ecclesia et Academia, per Dei gratiam, officia. M. CHRISTIERNUS paucis hîc diebus mansit, festinans in Daniam. Ego sane hunc virum nobiscum vivere et iuventutem nostram literariam in studijs Astronomicis, quae post obitum praestantissimi D. BRUCÆI segnius hîc tractata et culta sunt, erudire optarem. Tuas res honesto et incolumi adhuc statu esse, ac velle S. Caes. Mai. ut Pragae sibi adsis propius, gaudeo, et tibi tuisque deinceps etiam fausta feliciaque omnia precor.

Rumor isthuc delatus de dissidio inter Regem Daniae et nobilitatem suborto vanus est. Rex hoc tempore in Holsatia est cum coniuge, et genethliacis pompis ac convivio Gottorpij interfuturus dicitur, ubi soror eius IOHANNI ADOLPHO Holsatiae duci nupta filium peperit, quo et BUGISLAUS Pomeraniae dux legatum D. HENR. CAMERARIUM misit. Venationibus se Rex in Iutia oblectavit, magno cervorum numero caeso. Extruit passim munitiones Halmstadij et Overscherae in Sueciae finibus, quo cives ex vicinis oppidulis Rundeby et alijs migrare iussit, item Krempae in Holsatia. CAROLUS dux Sueciam clam nunc totam tenet pacifice, regiam vero coronam sibi a subditis oblatam nondum acceptare voluit, supplicio capitis 4 regni proceres captivos, ERICUM SPARRE Cancellarium, GUSTAVUM et STENONEM BANNER, et TURONEM BIELKEN, a concilio ordinum regni damnatos, affecit. Sunt et alij in Finlandia interfecti. Iam cum valido exercitu in Livonia est, omniaque Suecis hactenus subdita circa Revaliam cum hac ipsa urbe tenet, et Regi Poloniae bellum facturus existimatur, qui per Capitaneum suum GEORGIUM FARNSBECH exercitum circa Rigam conscripsit, qua de re certiora navibus nostris Riga redeuntibus cognoscemus. Cum Lubecensibus inducias pepigit, qui per legatos suos iam de pace cum eo agunt. Nuper duo Lubecensium Consules intra 24 horas obierunt, nec admodum bene inter Senatum et cives eius urbis convenit ob turbas Suecicas, quibus improvide Senatus inconsultis civibus se et urbem immiscuit, unde magna civibus in mari damna illata a CAROLO fuerunt.

In hisce regionibus, Dei beneficio, tranquillitas est, ac messem habuimus serenam et uberrimam. In Dania quoque segetes feliciter provenerunt, sed quia serior illic messis est, aër agricolis non adeo benignus fuit.

Postremo commendo tibi affinem meum ABRAHAMUM STOPIUM, qui istic aliquamdiu apud sororem et affinem vixit, eiusque negocium, quod haud dubie tibi exposuit, in quo si commodare ipsi apud Dn. Vicecancellarium vel alios proceres poteris, mihi quoque rem gratam feceris.

Bene et diu cum omnibus tuis vale.
Dabantur Rostochij 19 Septemb. stylo veteri Anno 1600.
Nobil. et Excel. T. studiosiss.
LUCAS BACMEISTERUS D.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man most excellent in nobility of birth, learning, and virtue, Lord Tycho Brahe, hereditary Lord of Knudstrup, most excellent Astronomer, his Lord and friend especially to be honoured. At Prague.

Heartiest greeting.

Most noble and renowned man, Lord and friend especially to be revered. I have received two letters from you this month of September: the earlier, M. Christiernus [Longomontanus] — who had tarried about three weeks at Wittenberg — delivered me on the 8th; the latter, a courier sent hither from Prague by Lord Gödelmann, yesterday. You know that we use the Julian style here. Both commended to me your money-business, about which you had also written to my kinsman, the Lord Chancellor Dr. Bording, whom — then acting at Lübeck — I admonished to promote that business in its place. He came a few days ago from Lübeck to Güstrow, summoned by the most illustrious Prince Ulrich, after His Highness — then hunting near Grabow — had been told of the death of his brother's son, Duke Sigismund August, who, long afflicted with dropsy and other diseases, piously departed this life at Ivenack on 5 September. His funeral will be on 10 October at Schwerin, where rest the bodies of his father Johann Albert, his brother Johann, and others deceased of that line. On the same occasion the Lord Chancellor found at court the Treasurer of the Younger Princes (who is now also Duke Ulrich's Treasurer), and brought it about that today, through his minister, he paid me at my house those 600 thalers owed to you, my receiving your receipt [apocha], which I had kept by me meanwhile. This money I will retain with me until the arrival of Lord Gödelmann, who, you write, will come hither shortly and assist you in receiving the money. To exchange it into gold coin cannot be done at all: very rarely here do we see even the smallest gold piece, all gold being carried hence either into the Low Countries or into upper Germany, where it is rated at a higher price than here. Yet I will do this, if I can: exchange the new Belgian thalers (many of which are in this sum) into others, "Imperial" as they call them; for we judge these more welcome there than those.

Further, as to the continuation of the Chronicle of Dr. Chytraeus (of pious memory, who bade us farewell the day after [the feast of St.] John [= 25 June]): I judge no one in this place fitter for the task than Dr. Christoph Sturtz, who presides over the lecture in history at this Academy with great praise, and excels in talent, memory, and eloquence. As soon, then, as the Council of the Academy meets, I will bring forward mention of this business and of your reminder; nor do I doubt that the Lord Professors — most of whom, as you know, are weighty in age, learning, and prudence — will resolve what will be both honourable to our Academy and to the name of that great Chytraeus, and publicly useful. And when Lord Gödelmann arrives, about that which privately concerns you, to be inserted in the last part of the Chronicle, I will gladly and diligently deal — together with Dr. Clingius — with Chytraeus's heirs; and if any other services welcome to you can proceed from me, you will always find me ready in them, as long as I live. For I am now growing old, and after a month, God willing, I shall complete the 70th year of my age; yet I still discharge moderately, by God's grace, the duties of my calling in the Church and the Academy. M. Christiernus stayed here a few days, hastening into Denmark. I should indeed wish this man to live among us, and to instruct our literary youth in the Astronomical studies — which, since the death of the most excellent Dr. Brucaeus, have been more sluggishly handled and cultivated here. That your affairs are still in an honourable and safe state, and that the Sacred Imperial Majesty wishes you to be nearer to him at Prague, I rejoice; and I pray all things auspicious and happy for you and yours henceforth too.

The rumour carried thither, of a dissension arisen between the King of Denmark and his nobility, is empty. The King is at this time in Holstein with his consort, and is said to be about to attend the birth-festivities and banquet at Gottorp, where his sister, married to Johann Adolf, Duke of Holstein, has borne a son — to which Bogislaw, Duke of Pomerania, sent as legate Dr. Heinrich Camerarius. The King has delighted himself with hunting in Jutland, a great number of stags being killed. He builds fortifications here and there — at Halmstad and Överskär on the Swedish borders (whither he has ordered the townsmen from the neighbouring little towns, Rundeby and others, to migrate), likewise at Krempe in Holstein. Duke Charles now secretly holds all Sweden peacefully, but has not yet been willing to accept the royal crown offered him by his subjects; he has inflicted capital punishment on four captive nobles of the realm, condemned by the council of the estates: Erik Sparre the Chancellor, Gustav and Sten Banér, and Ture Bielke [the Linköping Bloodbath]; and others have been killed in Finland. Now he is with a strong army in Livonia, and holds all that was hitherto subject to the Swedes around Reval, with that city itself, and is thought about to make war on the King of Poland — who, through his captain Georg Farensbach, has levied an army around Riga; whereof we shall learn more certainly from our ships returning from Riga. With the Lübeckers he has struck a truce; they now treat with him for peace through their legates. Lately two of the Lübeck consuls died within 24 hours; nor is it well agreed between the Senate and the citizens of that city, over the Swedish troubles, into which the Senate has imprudently entangled itself and the city without consulting the citizens — whence great damages at sea have been inflicted on the citizens by Charles.

In these regions, by God's benefit, there is tranquillity, and we have had a serene and most abundant harvest. In Denmark too the crops have come up happily, but since the harvest is later there, the weather has not been so kind to the farmers.

Lastly, I commend to you my kinsman Abraham Stopius, who lived there a while with his sister and kinsman, and his business (which he doubtless explained to you), in which, if you can accommodate him with the Lord Vice-Chancellor or other nobles, you will do me too a welcome thing.

Fare well, long, with all yours.
Given at Rostock, 19 September, Old Style, the year 1600.
Of Your Nobility and Excellency the most zealous,
Lucas Bacmeister, Doctor.

The financial-intermediary "Bacmeister" of the corpus is the Elder; the identification at brahe-t_16010415001 should be revised.
  • Significance — a dense Baltic news-letter, with the Linköping Bloodbath.
  • The Mecklenburg money. The 600 thalers of interest paid (via Chancellor Bording and the Treasurer of the Younger Princes, on the occasion of Duke Sigismund August of Mecklenburg's death and funeral arrangements at Schwerin); Bacmeister holds the sum for Gödelmann's arrival, and — unable to get gold (all exported) — will swap the new Belgian thalers into Imperial thalers. (Tycho's side: brahe-t_16010415001.)
  • Chytraeus's Chronicle. Confirms David Chytraeus died 25 June 1600 ("the day after St. John's"; cf. brahe-t_16000808001). Collated the full letter, opening address through signature, against TBOO VIII pp.377-379 page images. Every proper name, sum, and place-name in the dense political/dynastic report checks out and is translated faithfully: the 600 thalers, the 24-hour deaths of the two Lübeck consuls, Bacmeister's 70th year, and the string of place-names (Güstrow, Grabow, Schwerin, Gottorp, Jutland, Halmstad, Överskär, Krempe, Livonia, Reval, Riga) and persons (Bording, Ulrich of Mecklenburg, Johann Adolf of Holstein, Bogislaw of Pomerania, Heinrich Camerarius, Sturtz, Clingius, Brucaeus, the four executed Swedish nobles Sparre/Banér×2/Bielke, Georg Farensbach, Abraham Stopius) all match Dreyer. Three text error deviations were found in our Latin source layer against Dreyer (a dropped day-number, a vernacular word substituted for the Latin, a grammatical-case corruption); none originates in the translator's English, but the dropped date is a real, citable fact lost from the source layer, so this is graded down despite the translation itself being sound.

3 defects found:
- text error (medium). The transcribed original drops the day-number '5' before 'Septemb.'; Dreyer gives the death of Duke Sigismund August's nephew as 5 September. The translator handled the gap responsibly with a bracketed placeholder rather than inventing a date, but the source layer itself is corrupted and the recoverable fact (the day) is missing from it. Dreyer prints: qui hydrope alijfque morbis diu conflictatus die 5 Septemb. Ivenaci pie deceßit ex hac vita. (TBOO VIII p.377, l.20-21).
- text error (low). The transcribed original substitutes the vernacular word 'Denmark' where Dreyer prints the Latin genitive 'Daniae'. The English translation is unaffected (the referent is unambiguous either way), but the Latin source text has been corrupted. Dreyer prints: Rumor ifthuc delatus de dißidio inter Regem Daniae et nobilitatem fuborto vanus eft. (TBOO VIII p.378, l.21).
- text error (low). The transcribed original has the ungrammatical nominative/genitive 'tres septimanae' where Dreyer prints the correct accusative-of-duration 'tres septimanas'. No effect on the (correctly rendered) English. Dreyer prints: qui ad tres feptimanas Witebergae fubftiterat (TBOO VIII p.377, l.11-12).

Repaired. Collated all three defects directly against the Dreyer page images (TBOO VIII pp.376-379): p.377 confirms both 'tres septimanas' (l.11-12) and 'die 5 Septemb.' (l.20-21), and p.378 confirms 'Regem Daniae' (l.21). All three are genuine Latin transcription corruptions in our source text, now corrected; only the missing day-number had any bearing on the English, which has been restored accordingly. No remaining crux.
