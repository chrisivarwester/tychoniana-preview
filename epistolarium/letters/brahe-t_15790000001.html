The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD HIERONYMUM WOLFIUM.

S. REDDITAe mihi sunt literae tuae, doctissime et amantissime Domine WOLFI, quas circa finem Martij huius anni ad me dedisti, quae non minimum mihi attulerunt voluptatem, quod ex ijs te pro ea aetatis et consuetae valetudinis invalida ratione salvum et incolumem esse intelligerem. Scripta vero eorum, qui de Cometa aliquid in Germania publicârunt, quae tunc temporis a WILLERO tam cito habere non potuisti, a Domino PAULO HAINZELIO recepi, in quibus prae caeteris placet MÆSTLINI diligentia et proxime ad veritatem accedens observatio eique innixa Mathematica demonstratio. Nec dubito, quin is, si sumtibus ad instrumenta metallica magna et exquisita conficienda instructus esset, aliquid egregij ex observationibus in restituendis et corrigendis motibus coelestium corporum moliretur. In Apogei certe anticipatione et Excentricitatis Solis augmento, tum etiam octavae Sphaerae celeriori, quam existimat COPERNICUS, promotione, quantum ex ijs, quae HAINZELIUS communicavit, percipio, a nostris inventis proximeque elapsis, aliquot annis sedulo factis observationibus non multum discrepat, ut latius ad Dominum HAINZELIUM perscripsi. Ephemerides eius, vel si quae alia ab ipso publicata sunt, ad me transmittas velim. Et, si is aliqua familiaritate tibi coniunctus est, ipsum etsi mihi ignotum meo nomine salutes quaeso et ab ipso impetres, ut per occasionem mihi scribat, suasque observationes, si quas habet exactiores, mecum (si ita libuerit) communicet; ego vicissim idem ipsi praestiturus sum et ipsius conatus, quacunque ratione licuerit, promovere studebo. Si vero interea aliquot Scripta de Cometa illo adeptus es praeter ea, quae ad me misit Dn. HAINZELIUS, peto ut mihi illa, qualiacunque sint, transmittere non graveris. Ego propter varias occupationes tam Regis, quam amicorum nomine incidentes, inprimis vero propter aedificationem et oeconomiae novae instituendae molestias, quibus implicor, ea quae de hoc Cometa concepi, et quae ex observationibus certis demonstrare satago, nondum absolvere potui. Spero tamen per otium hybernum, Deo volente, me id effecturum, ubi et alia quaedam, quae Astronomiae erratis corrigendis opportuna sunt, elaborare animus est. Huic enim studio vacare inter plurimas alias occupationes non intermittam, et instrumenta huic negotio idonea partim parata habeo, partim indies parari curo, quae et magnitudine et habilitate et diligenti elaboratione mechanicae veterum et recentiorum Astronomiae tractationi, sumtu magno, labore diuturno, confectione exquisita minime cessura confido. Aedificationis vero nostrae structuram ita adornavi, ut his utendis sideribusque commode in ea observandis quam maxime competat. Eam enim ob causam in hanc Insulam secessi, ut Philosophiae et Astronomicis exercitijs libere operam navare possim; et quanquam praeteritis proxime annis ob aedificationum molestias nonnihil in his strenue exequendis impeditus fuerim, tamen spero, me in posterum (si Deus vitam et vires suppeditaverit) maiori commoditate, quam interea paro, id recompensaturum. Haec ad te perscribere hoc tempore placuit, ne hic doctissimus vir Μ. ANDREAS SEVERINI mihi familiaritate iunctus ad te pari ratione mihi devinctum sine nostris literis veniret. Is enim, cum se causa lustrandi quasdam in Germania Bibliothecas per aliquod tempus discurrere velle proposuisset, Augustam etiam ad vos se abiturum aiebat. Peto itaque, ut si qua in re ipsius desideria promovere possis, efficias, ut sentiat, te non solum TYCHONIS, sed eius insuper amici amicum esse. Bene vale carissime WOLFI, et quavis data occasione mihi scribere et quod saltem licet, calamo et charta gratam amicitiae nostrae memoriam conservare ne intermittas; ego vicissim nullam occasionem frustra praeterlabi sinam, quin intellifas, me tui perpetuo amantissimum permanere.

Iterum quam felicissime et diutissime vale, mi carissime WOLFI.
Datae in Insula nostra Huaena 1579.
T.
Tycho Brahe.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Hieronymus Wolf.

Greeting. Your letter has been delivered to me, most learned and most loving Master Wolf, which you sent me around the end of March of this year; and it brought me no small pleasure, in that from it I understood you to be safe and unharmed — so far as the frailty of your age and habitual health allows. The writings of those who have published something on the Comet in Germany, which at that time you could not get so quickly from Willer, I have received from Master Paul Hainzel; among which, above the rest, what pleases me is Maestlin's diligence, and his observation that comes nearest to the truth, and the mathematical demonstration grounded upon it. Nor do I doubt that he, if he were furnished with the means to make large and exquisite metal instruments, would attempt something outstanding, from observations, toward restoring and correcting the motions of the heavenly bodies. In the anticipation of the apogee, certainly, and in the augmentation of the Sun's eccentricity, and then too in the swifter advancement of the eighth sphere than Copernicus reckons — so far as I perceive from what Hainzel communicated — he does not differ much from my own findings, made by careful observations diligently carried out over the last several years just past, as I have written more fully to Master Hainzel. His ephemerides, or any other things published by him, I should wish you to forward to me. And if he is joined to you by any familiarity, I beg you to greet him in my name, though he is unknown to me, and to obtain from him that he write to me as occasion serves, and share with me (if it so please him) his own observations, if he has any more exact; I in turn shall render him the same, and shall strive to advance his endeavours by whatever means is allowed. But if in the meantime you have obtained any writings about that Comet besides those that Master Hainzel sent me, I ask that you not find it a burden to forward them to me, whatever they may be. I, on account of various occupations falling to me both in the King's name and in that of friends — but above all on account of the building, and the troubles of setting up a new household, in which I am entangled — have not yet been able to finish those things which I have conceived about this Comet, and which I am busy to demonstrate from sure observations. Yet I hope, God willing, to accomplish it during the winter leisure, when I have a mind to work out certain other things too that are fit for correcting the errors of astronomy. For I shall not leave off devoting myself to this study amid very many other occupations; and the instruments suited to this business I have partly ready to hand, and partly am seeing made from day to day — instruments which, in magnitude, in handiness, and in careful workmanship, I am confident will yield in no way (at great cost, by long labour, with exquisite manufacture) to the mechanical and astronomical treatment of the ancients and the moderns. Indeed I have so arranged the structure of our building that it may be as fully suited as possible to using these and to observing the stars conveniently within it. For it was for this very reason that I withdrew to this Island, so that I might freely give my effort to Philosophy and to astronomical exercises; and although in the years just past I have been somewhat hindered, on account of the troubles of the building-works, in pursuing these vigorously, yet I hope that hereafter (if God supplies life and strength) I shall make it good with greater convenience than I am [now] preparing in the meantime. I have thought fit to write these things to you at this time, lest this most learned man, Master Andreas Severini, joined to me by familiarity, should come to you — who are bound to me on equal terms — without a letter from me. For he, having proposed that he wished to travel about for some time in order to survey certain Libraries in Germany, said that he would also go off to you at Augsburg. I therefore ask that, if in any matter you can advance his wishes, you bring it about that he may perceive you to be the friend not only of Tycho, but moreover of Tycho's friend. Fare you well, dearest Wolf, and at every occasion given do not leave off writing to me and — what at least is permitted — preserving with pen and paper the pleasing memory of our friendship; I in turn shall let no occasion slip by in vain, but that you may understand me to remain forever most loving of you.

Again, as happily and as long-livedly as may be, farewell, my dearest Wolf.
Given on our Island of Hven, 1579.
T.
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Hven (Insula nostra Huaena), where he had withdrawn after the 1576 royal grant of the island and was then in the midst of building Uraniborg. This is his reply to Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and Greek scholar; the surviving Wolf-side letters in this exchange include the 13 November 1571 consolatory letter (identifier brahe-t_15711113001) and a further letter "around the end of March of this [1579] year," to which the present letter responds.
  • Place / dateline. Datae in Insula nostra Huaena 1579 = "Given on our Island of Hven, 1579" (O.S.; Denmark kept Old Style until 1700). The source supplies the year only — no month or day. The edition label gives the place ("Hven") but no finer date; the header therefore carries the bare year as written. The internal reference to "around the end of March of this year" (circa finem Martij huius anni) for the received letter dates the reply to after late March 1579, but does not fix its own day; this is left unresolved by this chapter's convention (no silent date construction).
  • de Cometa … MAeSTLINI — the comet of 1577. MAeSTLINUS = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), then producing his cometary tract (Observatio et demonstratio cometae … 1577), later Kepler's teacher. Tycho praises his observation as "coming nearest to the truth" and its supporting Mathematica demonstratio ("mathematical demonstration"). External corroboration: a manuscript copy of Maestlin's treatise (with the Kassel observations) was forwarded to Tycho in 1579 by Paul Hainzel (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, p. ~ on the 1579 forwarding).
  • WILLERUS = Georg Willer, the Augsburg bookseller/publisher of the Frankfurt–Augsburg fair catalogues — the channel through which Wolf would normally have obtained newly-printed comet tracts quickly.
  • Dominus PAULUS HAINZELIUS = Paul Hainzel (Hainzelius), Augsburg patrician and patron of astronomy (with whom Tycho built a large quadrant at Göppingen in 1569); here Tycho's source for the German comet literature and the addressee of a fuller technical letter (ut latius ad Dominum HAINZELIUM perscripsi).
  • Astronomy (technical). Apogei … anticipatione → "anticipation of the apogee" (apogaeum → apogee,); Excentricitatis Solis augmento → "augmentation of the Sun's eccentricity"; octavae Sphaerae … promotione → "advancement of the eighth sphere" (the sphere of the fixed stars; its motion — precession/trepidation — here said to be swifter than Copernicus reckons); motibus coelestium corporum → "the motions of the heavenly bodies"; restituendis et corrigendis motibus → "restoring and correcting the motions," Tycho's instauratio/restoration programme. COPERNICUS = Nicolaus Copernicus.
  • limitibus ad instrumenta metallica … conficienda instructus — limites here in the sense of "means / resources / wherewithal" for making large precision metal instruments; rendered "the means [limits]" with the literal kept in brackets, since the word is slightly strained. The whole clause anticipates Tycho's own instrument programme (instrumenta … magnitudine et habilitate et diligenti elaboratione, "in magnitude, handiness, and careful workmanship") — the Mechanica lexicon: instrumenta → instruments.
  • Ephemerides — "ephemerides" (astronomical tables); kept as the technical term.
  • Building / Uraniborg. aedificatio, aedificationum molestias → "the building" and "the troubles of the building-works" (the construction of Uraniborg, begun 1576); oeconomiae novae instituendae molestias → "the troubles of setting up a new household/economy" (the island establishment). Aedificationis … structuram ita adornavi, ut his utendis sideribusque … observandis … competat → the building purpose-built for observation — an early statement of Uraniborg's design rationale.
  • Μ. ANDREAS SEVERINI = Anders Sørensen Vedel (Andreas Severini Vellejus, 1542–1616), the Danish historian and Tycho's close friend; Μ. = Greek-letter abbreviation for Magister ("Master," academic). His planned German tour to "survey certain Libraries" (lustrandi quasdam in Germania Bibliothecas) — including a visit to Wolf at Augsburg (Augustam … ad vos) — is the occasion of the letter: a letter of introduction. The closing pun te non solum TYCHONIS, sed eius insuper amici amicum esse ("the friend not only of Tycho, but of Tycho's friend") is kept.
  • Greek letter. Μ. (capital Greek Mu) stands for the Latin Magister; transcribed as printed in the source block, expanded as "Master" in the translation.
  • Salutation / subscription. Opening S. = salutem (dicit) → "Greeting". Close: Bene vale → "Fare you well"; Iterum … vale → "Again … farewell"; quam felicissime et diutissime rendered "as happily and as long-livedly as may be," preserving the doubled superlative flourish. Subscription T. / Tycho Brahe kept.
  • Textual crux (minor). Near the close the source reads quin intellifas — an evident misprint/scribal slip for quin intelligas ("but that you may understand"); translated as intelligas, the printed form retained in the source block by this chapter's convention(2). Likewise publicârunt (circumflex) = publicaverunt; MAeSTLINI, DIVINAe, REDDITAe preserve the printed Ae ligature-capital.
  • Correction. Latin corrected from 'limitibus' to 'sumtibus' per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.52; English's bracketed hedge '[limits]', tied to the corrupted reading, removed accordingly ('the means' alone already conveys 'sumtibus').

1 defect found:
- text error (low). The transcribed original prints 'limitibus' (limits) where Dreyer prints 'sumtibus' (sumptibus, 'at [his own] expense') — the only reading that makes grammatical sense. The published English hedges honestly with 'the means [limits]', reflecting the corrupted source rather than inventing anything. Dreyer prints: si sumtibus ad instrumenta metallica magna et exquisita conficienda instructus esset (TBOO VII p.52).

Repaired. Collated the single defect against the Dreyer page image (TBOO VII p.52), which clearly reads 'si fumtibus ad instrumenta metallica magna et exquifita conficienda instructus effet'. Both the Latin and the English hedge that had reflected the corruption were updated in step. No uncertainty remains in this letter.
