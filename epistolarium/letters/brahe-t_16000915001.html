The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MARTINUM BRASCHIUM

QUAS 8 die Augusti stylo vestro ad me dedisti literas, Eruditissime BRASCHI, eas heri, qui erat 14 Septembris iuxta reformatum Calendarium, per Dn. GODELMANNUM, Legatum Saxonicum hîc agentem, recepi, quibus nunc, ipso etiam sic ob nuncij festinationem admonente, hæc, quantum ferunt aliæ meæ occupationes, properanter rescribo. De Elegiaco tuo Carmine bene est, quod meas etiam qualescunque non respuis notas: incœperam quodam die pomeridianis horis (nam ante meridiem seriora tractanda sunt) nonnulla in eodem mutare atque aliter disponere, quædam etiam omittere, atque nonnullibi alia supplere, idque antequam ex Arce residentiæ meæ Bennaticâ huc Pragam a Sacra Cæsarea Maiestate, Domino nostro Clementiss. quamprimum Pilsenâ redierat, clementissime evocatus, ubi etiamnum usque in tertium Mensem ab eadem retineor, et quantum intelligo, cupit ipsius Maiestas, ut in viciniore magisque oportuno habitem loco. Toto vero illo tempore, dum hîc sum, tot mihi pro ipsius Maiestate et alias quoque peragenda fuêre, ut otium mihi non superfuerit ea vel semel in manus resumendi, nedum plenius recognoscendi, sed fiet id, nacta, uti spero, aptiore et tranquilliore commoditate. Interim vellem, ut carmen illud tacitus tibi reservares, nec cuiquam communicares; sunt enim quædam in eo, quæ minus apposite relata sunt, nec pro Cæsare (ut de me ipso nihil dicam) satis decore aut convenienter, de quibus, ubi ijs denuo vacare licuerit, te ingenue admonebo, vel nostra transmutatio id facile edocebit, neque enim dubito, quin pro tuo candore, prout etiam nunc polliceris, sis æqui bonique laturus. Alterum illud carmen Epicum de VARADINO, cuius meministi, nondum vidi; nec mihi cum Domino MELCHIORE RÖDERO, cuius mentionem facis, ulla hactenus intercessit noticia, neque etiam scio, an hîc sit, quantum relatione aliorum audio, in Ungaria est; quare de eo et ijs, quæ ipsum concernunt, non habeo amplius quod scribam.

MELISSUS tandem expectationem et spem, quam de ipso concepimus, redemit, missis, paulo antequam Benaticâ abirem, ternis ad me diversi generis carminibus, quorum Exemplum addam, si tempus descriptionem tulerit. Verum ut dicam quod res postulat, videtur in hisce segniore vena præditus quam alias, sive quod hæc ingravescens ætas (est enim, uti opinor, plus quam sexagenarius) spiritus Poëticos minus alacres et humiliores producat, sive, quod tu etiam fateris, ardor ille temporum intervallis plus vel minus incendatur, interdumque lentior evadat. Præ cæteris autem, Epigramma illud primum, quod de me lusit, minus vividum nec fatis concinnum apparet, unde hanc mihi sumsi audaciam, ut aliqua in eo alterarem, quamvis ne sic quidem per omnia arrideat, nec scio, an is meas animadversiones tam libenter ferat atque tu; quare Epigramma illud, cum alia in mei gratiam hinc inde concinnata carmina aliquando forte cudentur, omittendum potius duco, quam ut publicatum autori suo minus sit honorificum, aut mea ieiuna immutatio illi ingrata stomachum moveat.

De Poëmatis tuis vel orationibus Philosophicis Imperatori dedicandis, non scio quid consulam, nam licet Cæsarea Matas erga eruditos satis sit beneficax et liberalis, nec respiciat, cui religionis formæ addicti sint, tamen Consiliarij difficiliores sunt et lenti in exequendo, minusque favent ijs, qui catholicam (uti vocant) religionem non profitentur: si quid autem in ipsius Maiestatis honorem conscribere lubuerit, sit hoc potius carmen quoddam grave et nervosum, de rebus ab ipsius Antecessoribus inde a CAROLO V. et denique a se ipso hactenus præclare gestis, ubi etiam per occasionem inter cætera eius Elogia inferre poteris, quod Astronomiæ instaurationem ad instar ALPHONSI illius veteris Castiliæ et Siciliæ Regis nostro ævo benignissime promoveat. Oportet etiam succincta aliquot et arguta Epigrammata subiungere, nam talibus maxime afficitur Cæsar, et brevitate gaudet. Verum hæc tuo potius relinquo arbitrio, qui rectius nosti, quid hîc faciendum vel secus.

DAVIDEM CHYTRÆUM superiore Iunio Fatis concessisse iam antea inaudieram, uti et Maio antecedente nostrum in Dania HEMMINGIUM, ambos profectæ ætatis senes et de Republica literaria bene meritos, quorum animæ sint in benedictione et pace: memoria ad posteros unâ cum monumentis relictis perduret ac fructum faciat. Perlustravi ea, quæ in CHYTRÆI obitum concinnasti, apprimeque placuerunt. Legi etiam scriptulum istud unâ transmissum de ijs, quæ hoc Anno in Arctois oris usque ad Mensem Maium memoratu digna evenerunt, in quo sunt plæraque apud Suecos gesta auditu tristia minusque iucunda. Deus omnia moderetur in bonum! Scripserat ad me superiore Anno præfatus optimæ memoriæ CHYTRÆUS et consignationem quandam miserat de ijs, quæ historiolæ huius Anni, meam e Dania huc in Bohemiam emigrationem concernentia, inserere constituerat, eaque meo arbitrio (aliter si luberet) formanda reliquerat. Ego vero illi rescribens chartam literis imposui, qua id quod expetebat iuxta meam sententiam insinuavi. At cum in eo quod misisti scripto nihil eiuscemodi inveniatur, suspicor literas meas non satis mature ad ipsum pervenisse, aut quod morbo impeditus et postea morte præventus ea exequi nequiverit. Si itaque literæ illæ meæ unâ cum scheda inserta adhuc inter ipsius chartas posthumæ inveniuntur, cuperem ea, quæ illi annotâram, convenienti loco historiolæ huius Anni adhuc immisceri, neque enim dubito, quin quispiam Vestratium eam usque ad Anni finem continuaturus sit, ut sic Historia Arctoa ab optimo illo CHYTRÆO inde ab Anno millesimo quingentesimo inchoata hoc millesimo sexcentesimo compleatur, atque hac ratione integrum contineat centenarium. Poterit vero id quod ego fieri averem, mea opinione rectius subiungi ijs, quæ a CHYTRÆO consignata sunt, tanquam ab ipso, dum adhuc viveret, exarata, ne si aliqua ibi reperirentur vicinis Danis minus grata (uti est veritas plærumque odiosa) nemini inter Vestros Professores adhuc superstites id imputari posset. Placebit (uti opinor) Cæsari, ubi viderit longe etiam remotas Academias sua ob Astronomiæ promotionem deprædicare encomia, nam et ab Illustri ac Magnifico Domino CORRADUCIO, Imperij Procancellario, quam primum antecedente Anno e Legatione Italica et Romana huc reversus esset, quæsivit, an Romæ vel alibi in Italia audivisset, quod me ob Astronomiam redintegrandam huc vocârit, cumque is affirmaret, se tam Romæ quam in alijs locis hoc relatione præstantium Virorum percepisse, id Cæsari admodum placuit, et sibi ipsi vel hoc nomine quasi congratulari visus est, prout ab ipso Domino CORRADUCIO hæc ad me perlata sunt; atque hinc cætera, quæ desidero, æstimare licebit. Nihil tamen Vobis præscribo, sed omnia vestro arbitrio et consulto iudicio facienda [vel o]mittenda relinquo. Referet quoque plura ex me supranominatus Dn. Doctor GÖDELMANNUS, ubi vos, uti constituit, accesserit.

Vale et Clarissimum Virum Dn. D. LUCAM BACKMEISTERUM (cui etiam in ultimis meis literis de memorata re, si recte recordor, nonnulla scripsi) ex me officiose saluta. Si tabellarius tam diu expectârit, denuo illi scribam. Saluta etiam cæteros Amicos meos Rostochienses. Magister CHRISTIERNUS ante 6 septimanas hinc a me discessit in Patriam rediturus atque Vos in transitu allocuturus, quare eum iam istic aut esse aut fuisse existimo. Tu vero, mi BRASCHI, nulla intermissa oportunitate, mihi rescribe, habebis me semper tui studiosissimum.

Iterumque vale.
Dabantur festinanti calamo Pragæ die 15 Septembris Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO MARTIN BRASCH.

The letter you sent me on the 8th day of August, by your style [Old Style], most learned Brasch, I received yesterday — which was the 14th of September by the reformed Calendar — through Lord Gödelmann, the Saxon Legate acting here; to which I now, he too urging it on account of the messenger's haste, hastily write back this, so far as my other occupations allow. About your Elegiac poem, it is well that you do not reject even such notes of mine as they are. I had begun, one day in the afternoon hours (for before noon the more serious matters are to be handled), to change and arrange otherwise some things in it, to omit some, and here and there to supply others — and that before I was most clemently summoned from the castle of my residence at Benátky hither to Prague by the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, as soon as He had returned from Pilsen; where I am still kept by Him, now into the third month, and, so far as I understand, His Majesty desires that I dwell in a nearer and more convenient place. But in all this time, while I am here, I have had so much to do, both for His Majesty and otherwise, that no leisure has remained to me to take it [the poem] up even once more, much less to revise it more fully — but it shall be done, when (as I hope) a fitter and quieter convenience is got. Meanwhile I should wish you to keep that poem quietly to yourself, and share it with no one; for there are certain things in it less aptly stated, and not decorous or fitting enough for the Emperor (to say nothing of myself), of which, when I may again have leisure for them, I will frankly admonish you — or our revision will easily show it; nor do I doubt that, of your candour (as you even now promise), you will take it in good and equal part. That other Epic poem on Várad, which you mention, I have not yet seen; nor have I hitherto had any acquaintance with Lord Melchior Röder, of whom you make mention; nor do I even know whether he is here — so far as I hear by others' report, he is in Hungary; wherefore of him, and the things concerning him, I have nothing more to write.

Melissus has at last redeemed the expectation and hope we conceived of him, having sent me, a little before I left Benátky, three poems of different kinds — of which I will add a copy, if time allow the transcription. But, to say what the matter requires, he seems in these endowed with a more sluggish vein than otherwise — whether because his advancing age (for he is, I think, more than sixty) produces poetic spirits less lively and lower, or, as you too confess, because that ardour is kindled more or less by intervals of time, and sometimes turns slower. Above the rest, that first epigram, which he composed about me, appears less vivid and not quite elegant; whence I took this boldness, to alter some things in it — although even so it does not in all points please, nor do I know whether he would bear my emendations as gladly as you. Wherefore that epigram — when other poems composed here and there in my honour are perhaps one day struck off — I judge had rather be omitted than, published, be less honourable to its author, or my meagre alteration, unwelcome to him, move his stomach.

About your Poems or Philosophical orations to be dedicated to the Emperor, I know not what to advise; for although the Imperial Majesty is beneficent and liberal enough toward the learned, and does not regard to which form of religion they are addicted, yet the Councillors are more difficult, and slow in executing, and favour less those who do not profess the Catholic (as they call it) religion. But if you should wish to write something in His Majesty's honour, let it rather be a certain grave and sinewy poem, about the things gloriously done by his Ancestors from Charles V on, and at last by himself — where also, among his other Elogia, you may by occasion insert that he most kindly promotes, in our age, the restoration of Astronomy, after the likeness of that old Alfonso, King of Castile and Sicily [Alfonso X]. You ought also to subjoin some succinct and witty epigrams, for by such the Emperor is most affected, and he delights in brevity. But these I leave rather to your judgement, who know better what is here to be done or not.

That David Chytraeus passed away to the Fates last June, I had already heard before; as also, the May preceding, our Hemmingsen in Denmark — both old men of advanced age and well-deserving of the literary Commonwealth; may their souls be in blessing and peace, [their] memory endure to posterity, together with the monuments they left, and bear fruit. I have looked through what you composed on Chytraeus's death, and it pleased me much. I read also that little writing sent along, about the things worthy of mention that befell this year in the Arctic [northern] regions up to the month of May — in which are very many things done among the Swedes, sad and unpleasant to hear. May God moderate all to the good! The aforesaid Chytraeus, of best memory, had written me last year, and sent a certain memorandum about what he had resolved to insert in this year's "little history" [historiola], concerning my emigration from Denmark hither into Bohemia, leaving it to be shaped by my judgement (otherwise, if I pleased). And I, writing back to him, enclosed a paper by which I conveyed what he sought, according to my view. But since in the writing you sent nothing of the kind is found, I suspect either that my letter did not reach him in time, or that, hindered by illness and afterward forestalled by death, he could not carry it out. If, then, that letter of mine, together with the inserted sheet, is still found among his posthumous papers, I should wish what I had annotated for him to be yet inserted in a fitting place of this year's history; for I do not doubt that someone of yours will continue it to the year's end, so that the Arctic History — begun by that excellent Chytraeus from the year 1500 — may be completed in this year 1600, and thus contain a whole century. But what I should wish done, in my opinion, would be more rightly subjoined to what was recorded by Chytraeus, as though written by him while he yet lived — lest, if anything there be found less pleasing to the neighbouring Danes (truth being for the most part odious), it could be imputed to none among your Professors still surviving. It will please the Emperor (as I think) when he sees even far-remote Academies proclaim encomia for his promotion of Astronomy; for the Emperor asked the illustrious Lord Coraduz, Imperial Vice-Chancellor — as soon as, last year, he had returned hither from the Italian and Roman Legation — whether he had heard at Rome or elsewhere in Italy that he [the Emperor] had called me hither to restore Astronomy; and when [Coraduz] affirmed that he had perceived this, both at Rome and in other places, by the report of distinguished Men, it pleased the Emperor much, and he seemed, even on this account, as it were to congratulate himself — as these things were reported to me by Lord Coraduz himself; and from this you may estimate the rest. Yet I prescribe nothing to you, but leave all to be done or omitted by your judgement and considered opinion. The above-named Lord Doctor Gödelmann will also report more from me, when he comes to you, as he has resolved.

Farewell; and greet for me dutifully the renowned man Lord Doctor Lucas Backmeister (to whom too, in my last letter, I wrote some things about the said matter, if I remember rightly). If the courier waits so long, I will write him again. Greet also my other Rostock friends. Master Christiernus [Longomontanus] departed from me hence 6 weeks ago, to return to his fatherland and address you in passing; wherefore I think he is now either there, or has been. But you, my Brasch, with no opportunity let slip, write back to me; you shall have me always most zealous of you.

And again farewell.
Given with hurrying pen at Prague, the 15th day of September, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient (name flagged). Martin Brasch / Martinus Braschius (1550–1601), the Rostock professor and Neo-Latin poet — addressed here as Martinum Braschium. brahe-t_16000808001 gives the sender as "Nicolaus Braschius," which is most likely a catalogue error: the two letters are one exchange (the same elegiac poem, Carmen de Varadino, Melissus, Chytraeus, Bacmeister, Rostock circle). Identification corrected toward Martin; flagged for correction at brahe-t_16000808001.
  • Significance — Tycho's literary diplomacy and the Chytraeus "Arctic History." Reply to brahe-t_16000808001:
  • The verse business. Tycho is emending Brasch's elegiac poem but, summoned from Benátky to Prague and "kept into the third month" (the Emperor wanting him "in a nearer, more convenient place" — the coming move to the Curtius house), has no leisure; he warns Brasch to keep it private, as some lines are "not decorous enough for the Emperor." He hasn't seen the Carmen de Varadino or met Melchior Röder (said to be in Hungary). On Melissus (Paul Schede Melissus, now ">sixty," with "a more sluggish vein"), Tycho judges the epigram Melissus wrote about him weak, and would rather it be omitted than published or awkwardly emended.
  • Advice on dedicating to Rudolf II. A revealing political note: the Emperor is liberal "regardless of religion," but the Councillors favour Catholics and are slow. Tycho counsels a grave poem on Habsburg deeds "from Charles V", casting Rudolf as "a new Alfonso [X] of Castile" for promoting astronomy, capped by brief, witty epigrams ("the Emperor delights in brevity").
  • Two deaths. David Chytraeus (June 1600) and Niels Hemmingsen (May 1600) — the great Lutheran scholars — mourned ("may their souls be in blessing and peace"). (Cf. brahe-t_16000808001, brahe-t_16000919002.)
  • The "Arctic History" plan. Chytraeus's annual northern chronicle (historiola): Tycho had sent Chytraeus a sheet on his own emigration from Denmark to Bohemia for insertion, but it didn't make it in before Chytraeus's death; Tycho asks that, if the sheet survives among the papers, it be inserted — attributed to the dead Chytraeus, to shield surviving professors from Danish displeasure ("truth being for the most part odious") — so the chronicle, "begun from 1500," reaches "a whole century" in 1600. (Cf. the Sturtz-continuation plan, brahe-t_16000919002.)
  • The Coraduz/Rome anecdote. A vivid court vignette: Rudolf II asked Coraduz, back from the Roman legation, whether Tycho's call "to restore astronomy" was known in Italy — and was pleased to hear it praised at Rome.
  • Longomontanus left for Denmark "6 weeks ago"; greetings to Lucas Backmeister (the Elder) and the Rostock circle.
  • Cross-references. Pair with brahe-t_16000808001 (Brasch's letter, 8 August 1600) and the Chytraeus-chronicle thread (brahe-t_16000919002).
  • Persons & places named. Martin Brasch; Gödelmann (Saxon Legate); Melchior Röder; Paul Schede Melissus; David Chytraeus; Niels Hemmingsen; Rudolf II, Charles V, Alfonso X of Castile; Coraduz; Lucas Backmeister; Longomontanus. Places: Prague, Benátky, Pilsen, Rostock, Rome/Italy, Hungary, Denmark, Sweden.
