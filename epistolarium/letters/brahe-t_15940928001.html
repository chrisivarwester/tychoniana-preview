The original

NOBILISSIMO ET SINGVLARI ERVDITIONE, VIRTVTE, ET INTEGRITATE PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, EQUITI DANO, ETC. DOMINO ET AMICO SUO CUM OBSERVANTIA COLENDO. S. P.

QVANTOS sustinuerim hactenus cruciatus, vir Nobilissime et Eruditissime, non opus esse arbitror ut scribam. Tanti certê fuerunt, ut anima vix corpori inhærere potuerit, recurrente subinde arthritide et immanissimis nephriticis doloribus. Qui etiam in causa fuêre, quo minus ad Illustrissimum Principem ac Dominum meum ante ipsius obitum reuerterer, tibique ea, quæ promiseram, transmitterem. Sed iam ante paucas aliquot Septimanas Diuino fauore aliquo modo cæpi respirare. DEVS æternus Opt. Max. in cuius conspectu ego ambulo, pro sua inexhausta misericordia etiam in posterum auxilio suo mihi adsit, et quod cæpit, clementer ad finem perducat. De nostris autem studijs ut aliquid attingam, diligenter hactenus scrutatus sum Catalogos Francofurtenses, num a TYCHONE BRAHE quid editum in ijs invenire possem: Verum nihil reperi. Miror igitur, quid causæ sit, quod Opus illud tuum de Cometis supprimas, nec in publicum prodire sinas, præsertim cum non tantum illud iamdudum fuerit absolutum, verumetiam toties et tanto tempore tam certo promiseris, te propediem illud emissurum. Equidem libenter, quantum in me esset, VRANIAM nostram ornarem et excolerem. Sed quid mihi sit obstaculo, vides. Quod igitur mihi denegatur, tu eo ardentius suppleas. Nec enim Mathematica Studia parum illustrabis, Posteritatique commendabis, si Opus illud tuum divulgabis. Incusantur enim vulgo, quasi sint sine fructu. Sed cum ex Opere illo tuo animaduertent, nos ea, quæ hactenus ignoratione Matheseos pro veris credita sunt, falsissima esse, certissime demonstrare posse, obturabis ora tam temere iudicantium, illosque suæ temeritatis conuinces, modo apud ipsos veritatis inuentio et tam abditissimarum tamque a nobis procul dissitarum rerum cognitio, fructus censeatur. Incitabis simul etiam heroica et præclara ingenia ad æmulationem, turpeque sibi esse ducent, si hæc ignorent: quemadmodum solet præclaris ingenijs nihil detestabilius videri, quâm præclara quæque ignorare. Præterea quot viros doctos putas editionem tuam singulari cum desiderio expectare? Qui certe plures sunt, quam tu tibi persuadere potes, quique de te non aliter nisi præclare sentiunt. Noli igitur ulterius istorum omnium expectationem suspendere, aut etiam tua ipsis invidere. Sed spero non committes, ut studia Mathematica diutius isto tuo ornatu careant, proptereaque minus colantur. Hæc fuêre, quæ ad te hoc tempore perscribere volui: quæ licet fortasse non sint magni momenti; tamen nactus certum hominem, qui ad vos iret, vacuas ad Amicum Literas quam nullas dare malui. Accipies igitur hæc in meliorem partem, silentiumque meum diuturnum supradictâ de causâ sufficienter excusatum habebis. Hortum sororis tuæ Philosophicæ, Dominæ SOPHIÆ BRAHE Matronæ perquam solertissimæ et Nobilissimæ, Illustrissimo Principi ac Domino meo, felicissimæ recordationis, pro dignitate commendassem, si ipsius Celsit. accessissem. Attamen per Literas omnia Illustriss. ipsius Celsit. significaui.

Vale feliciter et optime mî Nobilissime et Eruditissime Domine BRAHE, Vraniamque, quod facis, splendide ornare pergito.
Bernburgi 28. Septemb. Anno 1594.
T. T.
CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the most noble man, most outstanding in singular erudition, virtue, and integrity, Master Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup, Danish knight, etc., his lord and friend to be honoured with respect. Heartiest greeting.

How great the torments are that I have endured up to now, most noble and most learned Sir, I do not think there is any need to write. They were certainly so great that my soul could scarcely cling to my body, with arthritis and most savage nephritic [kidney] pains recurring time and again. These were also the cause why I did not return to the Most Illustrious Prince, my lord, before his death, and why I did not send on to you those things I had promised. But now, some few weeks ago, by divine favour I have in some measure begun to breathe again. May the eternal God, Best and Greatest, in whose sight I walk, of his inexhaustible mercy be present to me with his aid hereafter too, and graciously bring to its end what he has begun. But to touch on something of our studies: I have hitherto diligently searched the Frankfurt catalogues, [to see] whether I might find anything published in them by Tycho Brahe; but I found nothing. I wonder, therefore, what the reason is that you suppress that work of yours on the comets, and do not let it come out into public, especially since it has not only long since been finished, but you have so often and over so long a time so surely promised that you would shortly send it out. For my part I would gladly, as far as lay in me, adorn and cultivate our Urania. But you see what is an obstacle to me. What therefore is denied to me, may you supply the more ardently. For you will not a little illuminate the mathematical studies, and commend them to posterity, if you publish that work of yours. For they are commonly charged [with being], as it were, without fruit. But when from that work of yours they shall perceive that we can most certainly demonstrate those things which hitherto, through ignorance of mathematics, have been believed true to be utterly false, you will stop the mouths of those who judge so rashly, and convict them of their rashness — provided that with them the finding-out of truth, and the knowledge of things so deeply hidden and so far removed from us, be reckoned a fruit. At the same time you will also spur on heroic and illustrious minds to emulation, and they will count it a disgrace to themselves if they should be ignorant of these things — just as to illustrious minds nothing is wont to seem more detestable than to be ignorant of whatever is illustrious. Besides, how many learned men do you think await your edition with singular longing? They are certainly more than you can persuade yourself, and they think of you in no other way than splendidly. Do not therefore any longer keep all these men's expectation in suspense, or even begrudge them what is yours. But I hope you will not let it come about that the mathematical studies should longer lack this adornment of yours, and on that account be the less cultivated. These were the things I wished to write out to you at this time; which, although they may perhaps not be of great moment, yet, having got hold of a trustworthy man who was going to you, I preferred to give empty Letters to a Friend than none at all. You will therefore take these in the better part, and will hold my long silence, for the cause stated above, to be sufficiently excused. I would, as was fitting, have commended the garden of your philosophical sister, the Lady Sophie Brahe, a matron exceedingly skilful and most noble, to the Most Illustrious Prince my lord, of most happy memory, if I had approached His Highness. Nevertheless by Letters I signified everything to His Most Illustrious Highness.

Fare happily and very well, my most noble and most learned Master Brahe, and continue, as you do, splendidly to adorn Urania.
At Bernburg, 28 September, the year 1594.
Yours truly [T. T.],
Christoph Rothmann.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann (c. 1550/60 – c. 1600), mathematician–astronomer at the court of Landgrave Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel, Tycho's principal scientific correspondent there and a defender of the Copernican system against Tycho. He left Kassel on 1 September 1590, ostensibly to return there, but went instead to his native Bernburg; after a long silence this is his last known letter to Tycho (Dreyer, Tycho Brahe, on the 1590 departure and the September 1594 letter). The closing T. T. = Tuus Totus ("Wholly yours" / "Yours truly").
  • Recipient titulature. Domino de Knudstrup, Equiti Dano = "Lord of Knudstrup, Danish knight" — Tycho's ancestral seat in Scania and his noble rank. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting," this chapter's convention).
  • Place. Bernburgi = Bernburg (Bernburg an der Saale, in Anhalt; Sachsen-Anhalt), Rothmann's birthplace and where he had withdrawn.
  • Illustrissimum Principem ac Dominum meum … felicissimae recordationis — "the Most Illustrious Prince, my lord, … of most happy memory": Rothmann's patron, identified from the literature consulted as Joachim Ernst, Prince of Anhalt (Anhalt-Bernburg) (1536–1586), who had supported his studies at Wittenberg. The phrase ante ipsius obitum ("before his death") and felicissimae recordationis mark the prince as already deceased; he had died in 1586, so the unfulfilled return and the significaui ("I signified … by letters") describe relations during the prince's lifetime — flagged below as a chronological crux.
  • Medicine. arthritide = "arthritis" (gout/joint disease); immanissimis nephriticis doloribus = "most savage nephritic pains," i.e. kidney pains (the stone) — period terms, kept and glossed. Both recur as Rothmann's standing complaint in this correspondence.
  • Catalogos Francofurtenses — the Frankfurt [book-fair] catalogues (the Messkataloge), the standard early-modern record of newly published books; Rothmann had searched them in vain for anything printed by Tycho.
  • Opus illud tuum de Cometis — "that work of yours on the comets," Tycho's projected/long-promised treatise on comets (the volume that would become De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, 1588, on the comet of 1577, and the planned further volume); Rothmann presses Tycho on its non-appearance in print. iamdudum … absolutum ("long since finished") is Rothmann's claim, kept as his assertion.
  • URANIAM nostram / Uraniam … splendide ornare — "our Urania" / "splendidly to adorn Urania": Urania, Muse of astronomy, here a metonym for the science of astronomy itself; ornare/excolere Uraniam ("to adorn / cultivate Urania") echoes the Tychonic sign-off register (cf. this chapter's convention, sacris Uraniae lita). Kept as figure, named in the translation.
  • studia Mathematica … isto tuo ornatu — "the mathematical studies … this adornment of yours": Mathematica/Mathesis in Rothmann's usage = the mathematical sciences (astronomy chief among them); the defence against the charge that they are sine fructu ("without fruit") is a topos of the period's apologetics for mathematics.
  • sororis tuae Philosophicae, Dominae Sophiae Brahe — "your philosophical sister, the Lady Sophie Brahe" (1559–1643), Tycho's learned younger sister, known for her work in horticulture, chemistry/alchemy, and genealogy; Philosophicae marks her as a fellow student of natural philosophy. Hortum … pro dignitate commendassem ("I would … have commended [her] garden as was fitting") points to her noted garden/horticultural interest. Matronae perquam solertissimae = "a matron exceedingly skilful."
  • Celsit. / Illustriss. ipsius Celsit. — Celsitudo = "Highness"; the abbreviations are expanded in translation as "His Highness" / "His Most Illustrious Highness."
  • Greek / symbols. None in the text.
  • Orthography (humanist Latin print, 1596 edition). câepi for coepi, fuêre for fuerunt, quâm for quam, câeperit-type ae→ae-ligature spellings, consonantal u/v (reuerterer, conuinces, diuulgabis, significaui) and i/j (studijs, ijs, Mathematica Studia) are the edition's forms, reproduced as printed; not normalised.
  • Calendar / place. Bernburgi 28. Septemb. Anno 1594 = "At Bernburg, 28 September 1594." A plain civil dateline that agrees with the catalogue label ("1594. 28. september. Bernburg"); no conversion is made. Bernburg in Anhalt kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar in 1594 (Protestant Germany did not adopt the Gregorian reform until 1700), so the date is read as O.S. — flagged for completeness rather than asserted from any in-text style marker.
