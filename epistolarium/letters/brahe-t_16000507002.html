The original

NOBILI ET CLARISSIMO ERUDITISSIMOQUE DOMINO IOHANNI IESSENIO A IESSEN, PHILOSOPHIAe ET MEDICINAe DOCTORI ET PROFESSORI VITEBERGENSI PUBLICO, AMICO SUO SINCERE DILECTO.

S

GLOBUM, quem Illustrissimo Principi tuo destinâram, nunc ad te mitto, amantissime Domine IESSENI, ut tuam rectius redimam isthic fidem (si quid de eo iam ante accessum scripsisti) quam tu propriam apud me, in eo, quod te huc rediturum pollicitus es, licet iam id ignorare ais atque detrectas. Addidi librum de Fixarum restitutione accuratissima viginti Annorum indefesso labore exantlata, eumque in membranam exaratum, tum pariter Electori Saxoniae (cui Celsitudini inclyto unâ scripsi) meo nomine humilime a te adferri cupio, ut habeat eius Celsitudo Canonicam Stellarum expositionem in promptu, si forte aliquando maiorem globum confieri curare velit. Exoptarem autem hunc Abacum stelliferum, cuius similem priora secula (absit invidia dicto) vix viderunt, ipsius Celsitudo sibi soli reservare velit, nec alijs communicare, ne sint, qui nostra inventa sibi arrogent atque ijs abutantur, prout aliquoties mihi in alijs quibusdam contigit. Habes etiam hîc Mechanicae Astronomiae exemplar Magnifico Domino Cancellario Vestro ex me, praemissa officiosa salutatione, porrigendum. Subscripsi saltem nomen meum, tu titulum eius, quem non satis perspectum habeo, superius appone, et librum, si sic lubet, compingi effice, antequam illi exhibeas. Adiunxi insuper literas ad nobilissimum Virum, Dominum GEORGIUM a WOLFRAMSDORF, Cubicularium eiusdem Principis, quas illi tradas velim. Habet praeterea [DANIEL?] secum exemplar pro Illustri Domino Cancellario Bohemico, quod illi exhibere vellet, ubi convenienter ligatum fuerit. Sic satis superque obtines ea, quae tibi redeunti committere in me recepi. Tuum est, si promissi memor esse velis, ea, quae a Domino MECKERO expiscanda tibi concredidi, ad me per literas ante abitum referre, et Procuratorem illum, de quo scis, huc ad me dirigere, ita ut cum DANIELE revertente accedat. Tu vero, mi IESSENI, feliciter domum redi, atque quam optime vale.

Dabantur ex arce Caesarea Benatica die 7 Maij Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the noble, most renowned and most learned Lord Johannes Jessenius a Jessen, doctor of philosophy and medicine and public professor at Wittenberg, his sincerely beloved friend.

Greeting.

The globe which I had destined for your Most Illustrious Prince I now send to you, most loving Master Jessenius, that I may there more rightly redeem your pledged word (if you have written anything about it before your arrival) than you keep your own with me in the matter of your having promised to return hither — though now you say you know nothing of it and beg off. I have added a book on the most accurate restoration of the Fixed [Stars], drained out by twenty years' indefatigable labour and inscribed on parchment; and this likewise I desire to be brought in my name, most humbly through you, to the Elector of Saxony (to which renowned Highness I have written at the same time), so that His Highness may have a canonical exposition of the stars ready at hand, should he perhaps some day wish to have a larger globe made. I would wish, however, that His Highness keep this star-table — the like of which past ages (be it said without envy) have scarcely seen — reserved for himself alone, and not share it with others, lest there be those who arrogate my discoveries to themselves and abuse them, as has several times befallen me in certain other matters. You have here also a copy of the Mechanics of Astronomy to be presented from me, with a dutiful greeting premised, to your Magnificent Lord Chancellor. I have subscribed only my name; do you add his title above (which I have not sufficiently ascertained), and, if you so please, have the book bound before you present it to him. I have moreover enclosed a letter to the most noble man, Lord Georg von Wolframsdorf, chamberlain of the same Prince, which I would have you deliver to him. Besides this, [Daniel?] has with him a copy for the Illustrious Lord Chancellor of Bohemia, which he is to present to him once it has been suitably bound. Thus you obtain, fully and more than fully, the things I undertook to commit to you on your return. It is your part — if you would be mindful of your promise — to report to me by letter, before your departure, the things I entrusted to you to be drawn out by inquiry from Master Mecker, and to direct hither to me that Procurator of whom you know, so that he may come with Daniel on his return. But do you, my dear Jessenius, return home happily, and fare you as well as may be.

Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 7th day of May, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe at Benátky nad Jizerou, the imperial castle assigned to him before the move to Prague.
  • Recipient. Johannes Jessenius a Jessen (Jan Jesenský, 1566–1621), physician and anatomist, professor publicus at Wittenberg, returning home to Saxony after a visit; he conducted the famous public anatomy at Prague in June 1600 and remained a friend of both Tycho and Kepler. He is the courier for the whole consignment of gifts and books described here.
  • Persons named.
  • Illustrissimus Princeps tuus / Elector Saxoniae / eius Celsitudo = the Elector of Saxony, i.e. Christian II (Elector 1591–1611; still a minor under the regency of Duke Friedrich Wilhelm until 1601) — Jessenius's territorial prince, the intended recipient of the celestial globe and star-catalogue.
  • Georgius a Wolframsdorf, Cubicularius eiusdem Principis = Georg von Wolframsdorf, chamber-junker (here Cubicularius, "chamberlain") to the Saxon Elector; the enclosed letter to him is brahe-t_16000508001 (Benátky, 8 May 1600 — dated the day after this one).
  • Magnificus Dominus Cancellarius Vester = the Chancellor of [Electoral] Saxony, recipient of a Mechanica (Tycho leaves the title blank, not knowing it exactly).
  • Illustris Dominus Cancellarius Bohemicus = the Chancellor of Bohemia, i.e. Zdeněk Vojtěch Popel of Lobkowicz (Chancellor from 1599), recipient of a further Mechanica.
  • [Daniel?] and Daniele revertente = a servant or assistant named Daniel carrying one of the presentation copies; the square brackets and query are the editor's, the name being uncertain in the source.
  • Dominus Meckerus = (Master) Mecker, otherwise unidentified, charged with explaining some matter to Jessenius; Procurator ille, de quo scis = an unnamed agent/proctor whom Tycho wants sent to him.
  • Works mentioned.
  • globus / Abacus stellifer / maior globus = a celestial globe and the prospect of a larger globe — echoing Tycho's own great Augsburg globe at Uraniborg.
  • liber de Fixarum restitutione … in membranam exaratus = the catalogue of the fixed stars (Tycho's restitutio of the stellar positions, the fruit of "twenty years' labour"), here a presentation copy written on parchment; Canonica Stellarum expositio = its "canonical exposition of the stars." restitutio kept as the programme-term.
  • Mechanica Astronomiae = Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica (Wandsbek 1598), Tycho's illustrated account of his instruments, distributed as presentation copies to patrons.
  • Place. arx Caesarea Benatica = the Imperial castle of Benátky.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. = salutem ("Greeting"). Vale / quam optime vale → "fare you as well as may be." Dabantur (epistolary) → "Given." The opening play on fides (Jessenius's broken promise to return vs. Tycho redeeming the pledge over the globe) is preserved, not smoothed.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 7 Maij Anno 1600; edition marks n.s. (New Style), consistent with Tycho's Imperial-service dating and with the companion Wolframsdorf letter of 8 May. Reproduced, not converted.
  • Correction. Latin: 'restitutione' corrected to 'restitutione accuratissima', and 'explicanda' corrected to 'expiscanda', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, pp. 316-317. English corrected to match at both points.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). The transcribed original silently drops the superlative adjective 'accuratissima' (most accurate/painstaking) that Dreyer prints modifying 'restitutione', losing Tycho's own emphasis on the exactness of his twenty-year star-catalogue restoration; the English inherits the omission ('the restoration' rather than 'the most accurate restoration'). Dreyer prints: Addidi librum de Fixarum reftitutione accuratißima viginti Annorum indefeßo labore exantlata, eumque in membranam exaratum (TBOO VIII p.316).
- text error (low). Dreyer prints 'expiscanda' (things to be found out / drawn out by inquiry from Mecker), but our transcribed original substitutes 'explicanda' (things to be explained by Mecker) — a different gerundive that shifts who does the active work (Jessenius eliciting information vs. Mecker volunteering an explanation). The English follows the erroneous the transcribed original reading. Dreyer prints: ea, quae a Domino MECKERO expifcanda tibi concredidi (TBOO VIII p.317).

Repaired. Verified both defects by direct visual inspection of the Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.315-318 page images. 'accuratißima' (p.316) and 'expifcanda' (p.317) are both unambiguous. Both corrections carried into the English per the text error repair rule, since each corrupted Latin word had misled the translation. No other divergence from Dreyer was found in this letter.
