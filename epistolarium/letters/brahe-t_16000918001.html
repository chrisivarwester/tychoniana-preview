The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MELCHIOREM IŒSTELIUM.

S.

DEDI iterum ad te literas, Eruditissime et amantissime Domine M. MELCHIOR IÖSTELI, ante paucos dies, de ijsdem ferme rebus, quas superioribus attigeram; ultimas autem hasce Domino Doctori FRANCIO, Iure consulto, nunc Comitis Embdensis Cancellario, hinc proficiscenti commisi, qui eas se recte curaturum addixit: interim commodum accidit, quod tuae antepenultimo Augusti Wittebergâ ad me datae intervenirent, quae eorum, de quibus antea memineram, resolutionem, quoad principaliora, continent; ad quam sic respondeo.

Polliceris ab initio te collationem Hypothesium brevi missurum, siquidem iam perfectiorem quam antea elaboratam habeas. Fac igitur, ut mature obtineam, et figuras decenter assigna; addenda est autem explicatio sufficiens, non quidem admodum prolixa, sed etiam non nimium concisa; descendendum etenim nonnunquam ad captum Tyronum. Spero itaque me intra duas vel tres hebdomadas id, quicquid erit, a te habiturum.

Quae de M. OeSTWALDO scribis, non improbo. Maneat isthic, siquidem id malit, et supputet nihilominus pro me, quae per ocij intervalla absolvere poterit, numerabo illi in singulos Anni quadrantes sex Ioachimicos, quod in singulas hebdomadas dimidium ferme Ioachimicum facit. Sed volo, ut etiam singulis Anni quadrantibus mittat, quae absoluta habuerit. Haec ad ipsum ferre poteris. Quamprimum Tabulae Lunares unâ cum ijs, quae huc requiruntur, ad mundum descriptae fuerint, mittam exemplar, ut inde Ephemerides supputet incipientes ab hoc labente 1600, et per sexennium instans continuandae, unâ cum Solari Diario atque aspectibus omniumque differentijs, prout antea fecit, solvam autem illi pro cuiuslibet Anni Ephemeride tres Ioachimicos, prout in ultimis ad te literis scripsi, sed vellem, ut calculum maturaret, quo omnia unâ cum adiungendis satis cito inprimi adque instantes Francofurti Nundinas Vernales mitti possint, in 4to maiori excusa.

Cum Typographo vestro M. ZACHARIA LEHMANO age, ut sex illos quaterniones, vel tres duplicatos, qui desiderantur in Progymnasmatis meis, inprimat, atque papyrum similem illi, quae in libro est, aeque nimirum albam et tenuem eiusdemque magnitudinis, sibi procuret, ita ut 1400 exemplaria de quolibet quaternione inprimat. Existimo, quod Ballam eiuscemodi papyri pro 10 florenis nanciscetur; nam et ego antea eam, quae in libro meo de Cometa Anni 77 est, Francofurti emi pro 8 florenis, Basileae paratam et longo itinere isthuc vectam, sed iam forte alia sunt tempora, omniaque nunc cariora. Quicquid id erit, solvam illi iusto et debito modo, ut indemnis sit: prospiciat saltem de papyro, uti dixi, quae conveniens sit, interea dum exemplum paratur. Mittam postea, quae praefationis loco praefigenda, tum quoque quae in conclusione subiungenda, ut et illa absolvat. Si pecunia mea tam cito e Mechelburgensi Ducatu advenerit, potes inde 25 Ioachimicos tecum retinere pro solutione tam papyri quam laboris Typographici, successive exponenda: verum si id non evenerit, mittam ipsemet cum Domino Doctore GÖDELMANO, qui hîc Legatum agit Saxonicum, pecuniam sufficientem; ille enim post 14 dies constituit hinc Rostochium proficisci, atque in itinere Wittebergam transire; forte etiam exemplum secum adferet, si alia negotia descriptionem non remorata fuerint.

Cognovi insuper ex hisce tuis, quâ de caussâ huc venire nequeas, et libenter tuo acquiesco consilio, neque amplius hac de re quidpiam movebo. Confido tamen, quod etiam isthic habitans operam tuam nonnunquam per otij oportunitatem, in ijs, quae a te fieri desiderâro, non denegaturus sis, inveniar vicissim gratus. Et siquidem medicinae applicâsti animum, et in ea facultate promovere constituisti, det Deus, ut omnia tibi feliciter et ex voto succedant. Memineris autem, medicinam rite praeparatam medicum efficere et pro ipso operari; non verba, sive HIPPOCRATIS, sive GALENI, sive cuiusvis alterius diserti authoris. An vero hoc, quod dixi, isthic assequeris, admodum dubito, optare id potius licet quam sperare. Sed efficies facile quod coeteri, ut mundus habeat, quod vult. Haec si IESSENIO nostro dixeris, partim risum, partim bilem illi movebit scio. Miror, quod ille mihi nihil scribat, nec indicet, quid de libro et Globo illi tradito factum sit.

De Genesi illa, quam nôsti, poteris suo tempore, cum rectius vacârit, dispicere et ad me quidpiam perscribere. Vale et primâ quaque occasione responde; saluta amicos meos Wittebergenses, quos nôsti.

Dabantur festinanter Praga Die 18 Septembris Anno 1600 stylo novo.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 364; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Rogo insuper velis providere de studioso uno vel altero ad haec studia apto, prout in ultimis scripsi.

TYCHO BRAHE TO MELCHIOR JÖSTEL.

Greeting.

I gave a letter to you again, most learned and most loving Lord Master Melchior Jöstel, a few days ago, about almost the same matters I had touched in earlier ones; this last one I entrusted to Lord Doctor Franz, the jurisconsult, now Chancellor of the Count of Emden, setting out hence, who pledged to take good care of it. Meanwhile it conveniently happened that yours, sent me from Wittenberg on the antepenultimate [day] of August, came in — which contain the resolution, as to the principal points, of the things I mentioned before; to which I respond thus.

You promise, at the outset, that you will soon send the collation of the Hypotheses, since you now have it elaborated more perfectly than before. Do then [so] that I obtain it in good time, and assign the figures [diagrams] becomingly; a sufficient explanation must be added — not very prolix, but also not too concise, for one must sometimes descend to the grasp of beginners. I hope, therefore, to have it from you, whatever it is, within two or three weeks.

What you write about Master Oswald I do not disapprove. Let him stay there, since he prefers it, and nonetheless compute for me what he can finish through intervals of leisure; I will pay him six joachimsthalers each quarter-year, which makes nearly half a joachimsthaler a week. But I wish him also to send, each quarter, what he has finished — you can convey this to him. As soon as the Lunar Tables, with what is required here, have been fair-copied, I will send a copy, that he may from it compute Ephemerides beginning from this passing [year] 1600, to be continued for the coming six years, together with the Solar Diary, the aspects, and all the differences, as he did before; and I will pay him three joachimsthalers for each year's Ephemeris, as I wrote in my last — but I should wish him to hasten the calculation, that everything, with the additions, may be printed quickly enough and sent to the coming Frankfurt spring fair, printed in larger quarto.

Deal with your printer Master Zacharias Lehmann, that he print those six quaternions (or three doubled) wanting in my Progymnasmata, and procure paper like that in the book — equally white and thin, and of the same size — so that he print 1,400 copies of each quaternion. I estimate he will get a bale of such paper for 10 florins (for I earlier bought that in my book on the Comet of [15]77 at Frankfurt for 8 florins — prepared at Basel and carried thither by a long journey — but now perhaps the times are different, and everything dearer). Whatever it be, I will pay him in a just and due manner, that he be without loss: let him only provide suitable paper, as I said, while the copy is being prepared. I will afterward send what is to be prefixed in place of a preface, and what to append in the conclusion, that he may finish those too. If my money come so quickly from the Duchy of Mecklenburg, you can retain 25 joachimsthalers from it for payment of both paper and printing, to be laid out successively; but if not, I will myself send sufficient money with Lord Doctor Gödelmann, who here acts as the Saxon Legate — for he has resolved to set out hence for Rostock after 14 days, passing through Wittenberg on the way, and perhaps he will bring the copy with him, if other business has not delayed the fair-copying.

I have moreover learned from this letter of yours for what cause you cannot come hither, and I gladly acquiesce in your decision, nor will I press the matter further. Yet I trust that, even dwelling there, you will not deny your effort sometimes, through the opportunity of leisure, in what I shall wish done by you; I will in turn be found grateful. And since you have applied your mind to medicine, and resolved to advance in that faculty, may God grant all succeed for you happily and according to your wish. But remember that rightly-prepared medicine makes the physician, and works for him — not words, whether of Hippocrates, or Galen, or any other eloquent author. Whether you will attain this, there [at Wittenberg], I much doubt — one may rather wish it than hope for it; but you will easily do what the rest [do], so that the world may have what it wants. If you say these things to our Jessenius, I know it will move him partly to laughter, partly to bile. I wonder that he writes me nothing, nor indicates what has been done about the book and Globe handed to him. About that Geniture you know of, you can, in your time, when you have better leisure, look into it and write me something. Farewell, and reply at the first opportunity; greet my Wittenberg friends whom you know.

Given hastily at Prague, the 18th day of September, the year 1600, New Style.

[Postscript:] I ask, moreover, that you be willing to provide one or another student apt for these studies, as I wrote in my last.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Melchior Jöstel, Tycho's Wittenberg computational collaborator (cf. brahe-t_16000327001, brahe-t_16001008001, brahe-t_16001118001).
  • Resolves the "Wolfgang Franz" identity (cross-ref to brahe-t_16000327002, brahe-t_16001008001). Tycho entrusts a letter to "Doctor Franz, the jurisconsult, now Chancellor of the Count of Emden" (East Frisia). This confirms that "Doctor Franz" is a jurist (not the Wittenberg theologian Wolfgang Franz, 1564–1628): the recurring Franz courier of Tycho's Wittenberg-orbit correspondence is a lawyer who became chancellor to Count Enno III of East Frisia at Emden. The earlier identification of the recipient of brahe-t_16000327002 as "the theologian" should accordingly be corrected to the jurist.
  • The planetary-hypotheses collation (technical;). Jöstel will soon send the collation of the Hypotheses (the Tychonic/Copernican/Ptolemaic comparison for the second Epistolae book; cf. brahe-t_16001118001), with diagrams and a clear explanation pitched "to the grasp of beginners."
  • Master Oswald — the ephemeris-computer (terms settled). Oswald (cf. brahe-t_16000327001, where Tycho had offered to bring him to Prague) will stay at Wittenberg and compute on retainer: 6 joachimsthalers per quarter (≈ ½ thaler/week) for general work, plus 3 joachimsthalers per year's Ephemeris. Once Tycho's Lunar Tables are fair-copied, Oswald is to compute Ephemerides for 1600 + six years (with Solar Diary, aspects, differences) for the Frankfurt spring fair, in larger quarto.
  • The printing of the Progymnasmata (cross-ref to brahe-t_16001118001). Tycho instructs that the printer Zacharias Lehmann print the six wanting quaternions of the Progymnasmata in 1,400 copies, on paper "white and thin" like the book's (≈ 10 fl./bale — he recalls buying paper for the comet-of-1577 book at 8 fl., "prepared at Basel"); prefatory and concluding matter to follow. Funding: 25 joachimsthalers from the Mecklenburg money if it arrives, else via Gödelmann (here "the Saxon Legate," about to travel Prague→Wittenberg→Rostock). A precise record of Tycho's late book-production economics.
  • The witty medicine aside (notable for Tycho's chymical bent). Since Jöstel turns to medicine, Tycho — the Paracelsian alchemist — needles him: "rightly-prepared medicine makes the physician … not the words of Hippocrates or Galen" — doubting Jöstel will learn that at Wittenberg ("you will easily do what the rest do, so the world may have what it wants"), and predicting it would move Jessenius "partly to laughter, partly to bile." A characteristic dig at bookish, non-chymical academic medicine.
  • The book and globe. Tycho wonders that Jessenius has not reported "what has been done about the book and Globe handed to him" — i.e. the fixed-star book + celestial globe sent to Elector Christian II of Saxony via Jessenius (brahe-t_16000506001). Also asks Jöstel to look into "that Geniture" (a horoscope;).
  • Persons named. Melchior Jöstel; Doctor Franz (jurist, Chancellor of the Count of Emden); Master Oswald; Zacharias Lehmann (the printer); Johann Georg Gödelmann ("the Saxon Legate"); Johannes Jessenius; Hippocrates and Galen (the medical authorities).
