The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM

GENEROSE AC MAGNIFICE VIR, DOMINE ET AMICE INPRIMIS HONORANDE.

CUM hic Nobilis et Eruditus Adolescens FRANCISCUS TENGNAGLIUS, quem tua Ma iam antea novit, nuper ex Italiâ huc in Bohemiam ad me reversus, in Patriam suam Parentum invisendorum gratiâ nunc cogitet, et in itinere aut reditu Sermum Electorem Coloniensem, Dominum nostrum Clementissimum, denuo humillime accedere, nisi nimia viarum pericula id vetent, omninô constituerit: idcirco vacuum meis ad Mam tuam literis venire non sum passus, ex quo Mae tuae erga me humanissima officia gratâ mente etiamnum recolam, et perpetuo eorundem meminerim, paria relaturus, si vires sufficiant, is aut occasio ulla sese exhibeat. Licet vero quae ad Mam tuam perscribam, praeter ea, quae is commodius et tutius oretenus exponet, non habeam, tamen Mam tuam hisce officiosissime salutare volui, utque dictum TENGNAGLIUM sibi quam commendatissimum habeat, et utriusque nostrûm negotia apud Sermum Principem, prout antea quoque perlubenter et benevole praestitit, promovere atque expeditionem maturam impetrare non gravetur. Cumque idem TENGNAGLIUS instar vivae Epistolae futurus sit, et tam me quam se ipsum concernentia Mae tuae plene et secreto indicaturus, ulteriore scriptione supersedeo, ac per eundem, quam primum fieri possit, favente Numine, ad me revertentem responsum expecto. Deus opt. max. Mam tuam Reip. et bonis literis quam diutissime prosperam et incolumem servet.

Dabantur ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ, quam nunc unâ cum meis inhabito, die 9 Martij 1600.
Mae Tuae studiosiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to an unnamed [correspondent].

Generous and magnificent man, lord and especially honoured friend.

Since the noble and learned youth Frans Tengnagel — whom Your Magnificence already knew before — lately returned hither to me, into Bohemia, from Italy, and now thinks of [going] into his homeland, for the sake of visiting his parents, and has altogether resolved, on the journey or the return, again most humbly to approach the Most Serene Elector of Cologne, our most clement Lord, unless the excessive dangers of the roads forbid it: I therefore have not suffered [him] to come empty of letters from me to Your Magnificence — seeing that I still recall Your Magnificence's most kind offices toward me with grateful mind, and shall perpetually remember them, intending to render the like, if my strength suffice and he, or any occasion, present itself. But although I have nothing to write to Your Magnificence beyond what he will more conveniently and safely set forth by mouth, yet I wished most dutifully to greet Your Magnificence with these [lines]; and [I ask] that you hold the said Tengnagel most commended to you, and that you not find it burdensome to promote the affairs of us both with the Most Serene Prince — as before, too, you very willingly and benevolently did — and to obtain a speedy dispatch. And since the same Tengnagel will be as a living letter, and will fully and secretly indicate to Your Magnificence the things concerning both me and himself, I refrain from further writing; and, through the same, returning to me as soon as may be (the Deity favouring), I await an answer. May God Best and Greatest preserve Your Magnificence, prosperous and unharmed, for the Commonwealth and for good letters, as long as possible.

Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, which I now inhabit together with my [household], the 9th day of March, 1600.
Most zealous of Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient — unnamed. The edition titles it ad anonymum. From the internal evidence, a councillor or official at the electoral court of Cologne — someone who had earlier "very willingly promoted" Tycho's affairs "with the Most Serene Prince," i.e. with Elector Ernst of Bavaria, Archbishop of Cologne (cf. brahe-t_15980625002, …0626002). Identification flagged: not named. (The companion letter brahe-t_16000308002, written the day before, addresses the same Cologne channel on the same errand.)
  • Tengnagel as "living letter." Franciscus Tengnaglius … instar vivae Epistolae — Frans Tengnagel, Tycho's noble assistant (later son-in-law), just back from Italy, now travelling to his homeland (the Lower-Rhine/Netherlands) to visit his parents, with the intention of approaching Elector Ernst en route; he will carry Tycho's business "as a living letter" and report orally and secretly. Tycho asks the recipient to commend Tengnagel and to expedite "the affairs of us both" (Tycho's and Tengnagel's) with the Elector.
  • Ma tua = Magnificentia tua ("Your Magnificence"); Sermum Electorem Coloniensem = the Most Serene Elector of Cologne.
  • Calendar. ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ … die 9 Martij 1600 = from the Imperial castle of Benátky, 9 March 1600; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style (the companion letter is edition-tagged n.s.). Header so marked.
  • Correction. Corrected the corrupted 'Domimnum' to 'Dominum'; Dreyer's print (TBOO VIII p.261) is unambiguous.
