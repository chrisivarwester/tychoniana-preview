The original

TYCHONI BRAHE NOBILI DANO, MATHEMATICO CLARISSIMO, AMICO PERCHARO HENRICUS BRUCÆUS.
S. P.

ChARISSIME TYCHO, pecuniam qua VITO Typorum fusori et mihi satisfieret, accepi. De Calendario per tuorum aliquem edendo utrum sententiam mutâris, nescio; in tanta tamen iudiciorum incertitudine malim nihil tale edi, ne artis vanitas confirmetur. Quod si quid certi subesse iudicas, age, prodeat ille tuus in lucem, et declaret, se vel a te vel aliunde plus didicisse quam alij. Quanquam ego ab omnibus et laboratum esse hactenus frustra et in posterum laboratum iri existimem, rationibus meo iudicio, quæ refelli nequeant, id ipsum demonstrantibus. Cedam tamen, si videro, me a quoquam ipsis hisce prædictionibus vinci, nec quæram contra experientiam rationes. De Astrologi istius operibus facile arbitrabar, te ita sensurum, cum aliquot paginas inspexissem. Sic magnis voluminibus sæpe et claris titulis imponitur mundo. Nihil hisce nundinis prodijt Mathematicum, nec nostri Bibliopolæ quicquam attulerunt venale. Literas si quas Pragam mitti ad HAGECIUM velis, dabo operam ut deferantur. Legi tuos versus,

qui in PTOLEMÆUM et COPERNICUM scripti sunt, et inprimis mihi placuere. Præter enim carminis venustatem multa sunt eleganter inventa et erudite tradita. Vide, ut tuos Cometas brevi habeamus, cum iam sis instructus Typis. Quod si plura a VITO desideres, offert suam operam. In me quoque nec voluntatem nec studium defuisse agnosces. Tractatus noster de Crepusculis est in fine libri secundi de motu primo; eum si legas, non dubito placiturum.

Bene vale Nobilissime amantissime TYCHO.
Rostochij pridie Iohannis An. Domini 1585.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Tycho Brahe, noble Dane, most renowned mathematician, his very dear friend — Heinrich Brucaeus.
Heartiest greeting.

Dearest Tycho, I have received the money with which Vitus the type-founder and I were to be satisfied [paid]. Whether you have changed your mind about a calendar being published by one of your people, I do not know; yet amid such great uncertainty of judgements I should prefer that nothing of the kind be published, lest the vanity of the art be confirmed. But if you judge that something certain underlies it, come, let that man of yours come forth into the light, and let him show that he has learnt more, whether from you or from elsewhere, than others have. Although for my part I reckon that thus far all men have laboured in vain, and that in time to come too the labour will be in vain — reasons that to my judgement cannot be refuted demonstrating this very thing. Yet I shall yield, if I see myself bested by anyone in these very predictions, and I shall not seek reasons against experience. As to that astrologer's works, I readily supposed you would think so, once I had inspected a few pages. Thus the world is often imposed upon by great volumes and resounding titles. Nothing mathematical has appeared at this fair, nor have our booksellers brought anything for sale. If you wish any letters to be sent to Prague, to Hagecius, I shall see to it that they are delivered. I have read your verses,

which were written against [on] Ptolemy and Copernicus, and they pleased me exceedingly. For besides the charm of the poem, many things are elegantly conceived and learnedly set forth. See to it that we soon have your Comets, now that you are furnished with a press. And if you should want anything more from Vitus, he offers his service. In me too you will recognize that neither goodwill nor zeal has been wanting. Our treatise on twilight is at the end of the second book on the primum mobile [the first motion]; if you read it, I do not doubt it will please.

Fare you well, most noble, most beloved Tycho.
At Rostock, the day before [the feast of] St. John, in the year of the Lord 1585.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Bruyaerts/Brucaeus, 1531–1598), Flemish-born professor of medicine and mathematics/astronomy at the University of Rostock and one of Tycho's senior correspondents and former teachers; he advised on observational and computational matters and (as here) on the propriety of publishing astrological calendars. Identification confirmed against Mosley, Bearing the Heavens (Cambridge, 2007).
  • Recipient address. TYCHONI BRAHE NOBILI DANO, MATHEMATICO CLARISSIMO — the conventional learned superscription ("noble Dane, most renowned mathematician").
  • S. P. = salutem plurimam (dicit), "(sends) heartiest greeting". Bene vale = "Fare you well".
  • Vitus the type-founder. VITO Typorum fusori — Vitus, the type-founder (caster of printing type) whom Tycho had engaged for his newly established Uraniborg press; Typorum fusor is the trade term for a type-caster (rendered "type-founder"). The opening sentence concerns money Tycho had remitted to settle Brucaeus's outlay on Tycho's behalf to this craftsman. satisfieret here in its commercial sense, "be paid / satisfied."
  • The calendar / astrology dispute. De Calendario per tuorum aliquem edendo — Tycho had evidently proposed that one of his associates ("one of your people") publish an annual astrological calendar / prognostication. Brucaeus, a confirmed sceptic of judicial astrology, counsels against it "lest the vanity of the art be confirmed" (ne artis vanitas confirmetur). His epistemic stance is preserved verbatim: he holds that all such labour has been and will be frustra ("in vain"), by reasons he believes irrefutable, yet explicitly concedes he will yield (cedam) if shown to be bested by actual successful praedictiones and will "not seek reasons against experience" — a hedged, falsifiability-minded position kept intact, not sharpened into flat denial.
  • De Astrologi istius operibus — "that astrologer's works": an unnamed astrologer whose books Tycho and Brucaeus both judged inflated ("the world is often imposed upon by great volumes and resounding titles"). The identity is not given in the text; left unspecified rather than guessed.
  • nundinis — "this fair," i.e. the book fair (the great Frankfurt/Leipzig nundinae); rendered "fair." The complaint is that no mathematical book appeared there this season.
  • Hagecius. HAGECIUM … Pragam = Thaddaeus Hagecius (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku) of Prague, the imperial physician-astronomer and a key node in Tycho's correspondence network; Brucaeus offers to forward Tycho's letters to him. Pragam = Prague.
  • The verses on Ptolemy and Copernicus. tuos versus, qui in PTOLEMAeUM et COPERNICUM scripti sunt — Tycho's Latin verses (epigrams) on / against Ptolemy and Copernicus; in + acc. here is the dedicatory/topical "on, addressed to" rather than purely hostile, hence "against [on]" with the ambiguity flagged. (These are the elogia Tycho later printed; the line in the source between the two paragraphs falls mid-sentence — a layout artefact of the transcription, preserved.)
  • The Comets. tuos Cometas … cum iam sis instructus Typis — Brucaeus urges Tycho to bring out his work on comets (the De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, on the comet of 1577, then in preparation), "now that you are furnished with a press [Typis]" — i.e. the Uraniborg printing office. Typis "(printing-)type / press."
  • Brucaeus's own treatise. Tractatus noster de Crepusculis est in fine libri secundi de motu primo — "Our treatise on twilight is at the end of the second book on the primum mobile." crepusculum = twilight. de motu primo = on the primum mobile / the "first motion" (the diurnal rotation of the heavens), the standard subject heading of a sphaera / spherical-astronomy textbook; Brucaeus refers to his own such work, which contains a section on the duration/geometry of twilight (rendered "the first motion" with the technical term glossed).
  • Calendar / place. Rostochij pridie Iohannis An. Domini 1585 = "At Rostock [Rostochium,], the day before [the feast of] St. John [the Baptist], in the year of the Lord 1585." The Nativity of St. John the Baptist is 24 June; pridie ("the day before") therefore yields 23 June (O.S.; Denmark and Lutheran Mecklenburg both kept the Julian calendar in 1585). This agrees with the catalogue label ("1585. 23. juni"); no discrepancy. The dateline is given by liturgical idiom, glossed to the civil date by the edition's convention
