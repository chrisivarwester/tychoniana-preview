The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD SIGFRIDUM RINDSCHAT].

MIN gandske wenlig hilsen nu och altid forsend met Vor Herre. Welbørdig her Seyffert Rindtschadt, gunstige gode wen, nest min wenlig tacksigellße for allt beuist gode, huilket igien att forskylde ieg stedze will findis aff formuen willig, y hues maade ieg kand giøre nogit, som eder till aere och gode kan werre. Giffuendis eder derhos gandske wenligen tillkiende, att eders gode och vellmeente skriffuelße er mig for en 10 eller 12 vger siden tillhende kommen met m. Johanne Keplero, som fuldis hid y dette kongerige Behemen met den welbørdige fryherre, her Johan Friderick Hoffman, eders slecht och byr och min gunstige gode wenn, och war samme eders skriffuelße mig megit kierkommen, saa well som forbeneunte m. Kepler, thi ieg haffde y langthiidt adtraad att wille wijde, hor y war och horledis det gick eder, haffde och, der ieg bode paa Wandeßbechk wed Hamburgk paa itt aars thiid att giørre, førend røm. key. may., voris allernaadigst herre, lodt naadiste kallde mig hid till sig, skreffuit itt breff till eder och sendt det ind vdi Jüdland, y den ein, som ieg haffde meent, atty bode, mens ieg fick beskeen ygien, att y war dragen derfran hen y eder faederne landtt.

Angaendis forbemelte m. Keplero, war hans ankombst mig ochsaa saare angenehm, thi ieg haffuer vdj lang thiidt holdit megit aff hannom for hans herlige ingenium och skickelligheet skyldt y die mathematische och astronomische konster, som y wijde mig ocsaa att haffue y sønderlighedt løst till, och der hand war kommen till Prag, sende strax derepther forbeneunte her Jehann Friderich Hoffmann hannom hid till mig, och bleff hand saa her hos mig met samme her Hoffmans gode wille och samtøcke ved 2 maaneder. Siden drog handt till her Hoffman vdi Prag ygien och var der hos hannom vdj 2 eller 3 wger, epther hans beger, och der hans welbørdighed war saa dragen hen y itt baadt, for hans sundhedt skyld, lod hand mester Keplerum komme hidt till mig igien, paa det ieg, som tillforn, ydremere kunde conuersere met hannom y die astron[om]ische konster, intill hand kunde bekomme god gelegenhed att drage tillbage vdjgien y Stiermarck, och haffde hand achtedt sig adsteedt did hen y en aff disse dage met første leylighed, som hand kunde bekomme fra Prag.

Mens midlertiidt er hidkommen till mig aff Danmarck en herremand aff fornemme adell och Rossenkrantz slecht, der y landit børdig, som er och min nehr slecht och bør. Handt er tillsinds att drage ind vdi Østerich, der underdanigst att praesentere sin tienist høyborne erßhertzog Mathias, och siden att lade bruge och forsøye sig y den wngerische krig, och achter hand sig nest Gudz hielp vdj morgen herfran och hen till Pilsen, hor røm. key. may. endnu er, der att forsøye, om handt kand forhuerue en forschrifft till høybemelte ershertzog Matias, att hans først: naade wille thage hannom vdi tieniste, och att hand wille lade hannom werre sig met all gunst och naade befallid. Saa haffuer ieg tallt met samme Rossenkrantz, att hand wille tage mester Keplerum paa wognen met sig till Wien y Østerich och staa hans tering, huilket hand och godwilligen haffuer loffuid att wille giørre for min skyld; haffuer ieg och raad och offuertallt mester Kepler, att hand sin reysse wille forholle till samme herremand kommer hid ygien fra Pilsen, huilket ieg haabis att kunde skee inden 14 dage y det seeniste, saa att hand da kandt følgis med hanom lettelligen och foruden all bekaastning intill Wien. Och endog dette søntis mester Kepler att wille falle for langt, thi hand yller hiem ygien for hues hand haffuer att giøre for herrene y Stirmarkid och for sin høstruff skyld, saa haffuer hand dog omsier herudj beuilliget och eptherfølt mit raad, eptherdi hand formerkte, att ieg mente det gott och troligen met hannom, som han och stedtze herepther skall befinde hos mig, epthersom ieg och mercker hannom att werre wertt affholdid och promouerit till det beste. Kierre herr Syberet, dette kunde ieg icke lade att giffue eder paa denne tiid tillkiende, om m. Keplero, som y kommenderit till mig, paa det y kunde wijde aarsagen, horfor hand bliffuer saa lenge wde, och haffue hannom wndskyldit, baade hos die andre herrer vdj Styrmarket och hos eder selff, och wdj ligge maade lade giffue hans høftruff, naar eder stedis boedt, dette tillkiende. Jeg skriffuer nu icke ydermere suar paa eders skriffuelße till mig for tidsendz korthedt skyld, menß achter det nest Gudz hielp att giørre met m. Kepler, naar hand drager did y landit ygien, thi hand haffuer set sig forre paa weyen att besøge eder, saa achter ieg att suare eder nochsom till alle puncter, som eders breff indholder och enda ydermere. Medlertid beder ieg eder gandske gierne, att y will haffue mig vndskyldid och ochsesaa handle det, saa att det bliffuer icke tagid m. Kepler till mødstøcke, att [hand] bliffuer saa lenge vde. Ydermere aarsag dertill skall y, nest Gudz hielp, faa att wiide y min anden skriffuelße, naar hand ankommer. Och will hermed nu och alltid haffue eder Gud allmechtugste befalid, giører vell och siger eders kierre høstruff, fru Magdalene Munck, mange gode netter paa min och min kierre høstruff och børns weyne.

Daterit paa det keyserlige slot Bennatickden.

[TYCHO BRAHE TO SIGFRID RINDSCHAT].

My quite friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed by Our Lord. Well-born Sir Seyfried Rindschadt, favourable good friend: after my friendly thanks for all the kindness shown me — which to requite I shall always be found willing according to my means, in whatever way I can do anything that may be to your honour and good. Giving you herewith, quite kindly, to know that your good and well-meant letter came to my hands some 10 or 12 weeks ago, with Master Johannes Kepler, who came hither into this Kingdom of Bohemia with the well-born baron, Sir Johann Friderich Hoffmann — your kinsman and townsman, and my favourable good friend — and that same letter of yours was very welcome to me, as was the aforesaid Master Kepler; for I had long desired to know where you were and how it fared with you. I had also — when I dwelt at Wandsbek near Hamburg for about a year, before the Roman Imperial Majesty, our most gracious lord, graciously had me called hither to him — written a letter to you and sent it into Jutland, to the place where I had thought you dwelt; but I received word back that you had moved away thence into your fatherland.

Concerning the aforesaid Master Kepler, his arrival was likewise very agreeable to me; for I have long thought much of him, for the sake of his splendid genius and aptitude in the mathematical and astronomical arts — in which, as you know, I too have particular delight. And when he had come to Prague, the aforesaid Sir Johann Friderich Hoffmann straightway sent him hither to me; and so he stayed here with me, with the said Sir Hoffmann's good will and consent, about 2 months. Then he went back to Sir Hoffmann in Prague, and was there with him 2 or 3 weeks at his request; and when his lordship had gone off to a [healing] bath for his health's sake, he let Master Kepler come hither to me again, so that I, as before, might further converse with him in the astronomical arts, until he could get good opportunity to travel back again into Styria; and he had intended to set off thither one of these days, with the first occasion he could get from Prague.

But meanwhile there has come to me from Denmark a gentleman of distinguished nobility and of the Rosenkrantz family, born there in the country, who is also my near kinsman and relative. He is minded to travel into Austria, there most humbly to present his service to the high-born Archduke Matthias, and then to let himself be employed and try himself in the Hungarian war; and he intends, with God's help, tomorrow [to go] from here on to Pilsen, where the Roman Imperial Majesty still is, there to try whether he can obtain a letter of recommendation to the said Archduke Matthias, that his princely Grace would take him into service and let him be commended to him with all favour and grace. So I have spoken with this same Rosenkrantz, that he would take Master Kepler on his wagon with him to Vienna in Austria and bear his expenses — which he has willingly promised to do for my sake; and I have also advised and prevailed upon Master Kepler to hold off his journey until this same gentleman comes here again from Pilsen — which I hope can happen within 14 days at the latest — so that he may then travel with him easily, and without any cost, as far as Vienna. And although this seemed to Master Kepler that it would fall out too long [a delay] — for he hastens home again, on account of what he has to do for the lords in Styria, and for his wife's sake — yet he has at last consented herein and followed my advice, since he perceived that I meant it well and faithfully toward him (as he shall also always find with me hereafter), seeing that I judge him worthy to be esteemed and advanced to the best.

Dear Sir Syfried, this I could not omit to make known to you at this time, concerning Master Kepler whom you commended to me, so that you might know the reason why he stays away so long, and may have him excused — both with the other lords in Styria and with you yourself — and likewise let his wife know this, when you get a messenger. I do not now write a fuller reply to your letter, for shortness of time, but intend, God willing, to do it through Master Kepler when he travels thither into the country again — for he has resolved to visit you on the way — so that I intend to answer you sufficiently on all the points your letter contains, and even more besides. Meanwhile I beg you quite gladly that you will hold me excused, and also handle it so that it not be held against Master Kepler that he stays away so long. Further reason for it you shall, God willing, get to know in my next letter, when he arrives. And herewith I will now and always have you commended to God Almighty. Fare well, and bid your dear wife, Lady Magdalene Munck, many good-nights on behalf of me and my dear wife and children.

Dated at the imperial castle of Benátky.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Sigfrid / Seyfried Rindscha[d]t (the address varies: Seyffert Rindtschadt, herr Syberet, Sigfrid) — Kepler's patron-connection in Styria/Graz, a kinsman and "townsman" of the baron Hoffmann; his wife is Lady Magdalene Munck (a Danish-sounding name, suggesting a Dano-Styrian link that explains Tycho's writing him in Danish). Not further identified here.
- Significance — the warm face of the Tycho–Kepler crisis (companion to the bitter Jessenius letter). This Danish draft was written from Benátky in the very same weeks as the angry letter to Jessenius (brahe-t_16000408001, 8 April 1600) — and shows the opposite register entirely. To Kepler's patron, Tycho:
- praises Kepler's "splendid genius (herlige ingenium) and aptitude in the mathematical and astronomical arts," saying his arrival was "very agreeable";
- narrates Kepler's stay matter-of-factly (≈2 months with Tycho → 2–3 weeks back with Hoffmann in Prague → returned to Tycho while Hoffmann took a cure);
- arranges and funds Kepler's return to Styria — persuading a visiting Rosenkrantz kinsman (bound for Archduke Matthias's service in the Hungarian war, via Pilsen and Vienna) to carry Kepler on his wagon to Vienna at no cost, and persuading Kepler to wait c. 14 days for him;
- asks the patron to excuse Kepler's long absence to "the other lords in Styria" and to Kepler's wife, insisting "I meant it well and faithfully toward him … I judge him worthy to be esteemed and advanced to the best."
The juxtaposition is the heart of the document: even while venting fury to the mediator, Tycho protects Kepler's standing with his employers and pays his way home. (The reconciliation that followed, and Kepler's eventual succession, are prefigured here far more than in the Jessenius letter.)
- Biographical details. Confirms Kepler travelled to Bohemia with Hoffmann; the two-month first residence at Benátky; the shuttling to Prague and back; and Tycho's own earlier year at Wandsbek near Hamburg before the imperial call, during which he had tried (in vain) to reach Rindschat by a letter sent into Jutland.
- The unnamed Rosenkrantz. "A gentleman … of the Rosenkrantz family … my near kinsman," bound for Archduke Matthias and the Hungarian (Turkish) war — not individually identified; left as given.
- Persons named. Sigfrid/Seyfried Rindschat; Johannes Kepler; Johann Friderich Hoffmann (baron, Kepler's host); Archduke Matthias; Rudolf II (at Pilsen); the unnamed Rosenkrantz kinsman; Lady Magdalene Munck (Rindschat's wife); Kepler's wife (Barbara, unnamed). Places: Benátky, Prague, Wandsbek/Hamburg, Jutland, Styria, Austria/Vienna, Pilsen.
- Date. Undated in the draft ("Dated at the imperial castle of Benátky," no day); identifier 16000000001 and label "Benatky" carry only the year. Internal evidence — "10–12 weeks since your letter came with Kepler," Kepler now about to leave for Styria, the Rosenkrantz departing "tomorrow" for Pilsen — ties it to the same spring-1600 weeks as the 8 April 1600 Jessenius letter; dated here [Benátky, c. April 1600] without inventing a precise day.
- Language note. Tycho's Early Modern Danish chancery hand, read securely; spelling is heavily phonetic (ingenium, conuersere, promouerit are his own Latinisms in the Danish). The witness is a draft (Kladde) in the Basel autograph collection.
- Correction. Source text corrected against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Opera Omnia XIV, page 205: "Vor Horre" corrected to "Vor Herre" (the standard Danish greeting-formula "Our Lord"). The English translation already read correctly and required no change.
