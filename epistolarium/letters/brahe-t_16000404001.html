The original

REVERENDISSIMIS, NOBILISSIMIS, CLARISSIMISQUE VIRIS D:NIS DECANO, CŒTERISQUE ECCLESIÆ METROPOLITANÆ IN ARCHIEPISCOPATU MAGDEBURGENSI CAPITULARIBUS ET DOMINIS ET AMICIS SUIS HONORANDISSIMIS.

Reverendissimi et nobilissimi viri d:i et amici honorandissimi.

CUM sa:a cæs:a m:tas, dominus noster clementissimus, jam antea literas dederit ad amplissimum senatum Magdeburgensem, ut jnstrumenta mea astronomica atque supellectilem isthic repositam huc in Bohemiam, intermediante Albi fluvio, suis sumptibus in ipsius ma:tis gratiam transferri procurare velint, illj vero id ipsum suo quodam consilio renuere visi sint, utut bis ab ipsius cæs:a ma:te hac de re clementissimis literis compellarentur, non igitur placuit ipsius ma:ti de his sæpius ad illos scribere, sed potius ad vestrum reverendissimum capitulum literas hoc nomine dirigere, præsertim cum subintellexerit vestras reverend:mas dominationes ab hoc officio suæ cæs:æ ma:ti obsequiose et reverenter præstando minime alienas esse. Quare nunc ipsius ma:tis literas hæc ipsa continentes ad reverend:mas vestras dominationes per ministrum meum mitto, officiosissime et reverenter simul rogans, ut, quemadmodum ipsius ma:tis clementissimæ petitioni obsequentissimo animo obtemporaturas nihil ambigo, sic etiam effecturas ut omnes res meæ isthic reservatæ qua fieri potest maturatione, sartætectæ, advehantur, atque de iis, quæ huc conducunt, provideatur. Accipiet illud ipsius ma:tas clementissima favorabili mente eritque simul, vti spero, reip. literariæ utile, cujus promotionem vos non solum genere jllustres, sed et eximia eruditione conspicuos, exoptare et auxiliatrices manus præbere velle persuasum habeo; neque enim ea quæ in sublimi illa et divina scientia astronomica integre restituenda plurimis abhinc annis indefesso labore sumptibusque non modicis molior et favente cœlestium opifice, in augustissimi hujus imperatoris totiusque jmperii honorem nunquam intermoriturum, sedulo elaborare satago, privata sunt, aut ad me meosque solummodo pertinentia, sed ad universam literaturam eruditam ubivis locorum quoad sol et sidera durabunt (prout confido) conducentia. Jdeoque v:s d:es commune hic bonum lubenti animo adjuturas non est dubium, [omnino mihi polliceor]; quod equidem officium, quantum ad me attinet, etsi compensare vires non sufficiant, pro meo tamen modulo inveniar perpetuo erga reverendissimum vestrum capitulum hoc nomine gratus; sique nihil aliud referre vicissim potero, vestram tamen hanc præclaram voluntatem apud alios extollere et deprædicare non desistam. Si quempiam ex vestris fidelibus ministris adjunxeritis, qui res meas huc comitetur et earum curam gerat, poteritis (modo vestris dominationibus ita placuerit) vina Bohemica, quæ superiori æstate adeo generosa hic creverunt, ut plurimi antecedentes anni vix similia dederint, secum in aliqua copia iustoque precio recipere: quod ut commode fiat, vel si quid aliud ex his oris desideratis, quod isthic pluris æstimatur, dabo operam, ut quantum in me est vestræ voluntati hac in parte satisfiat, sicque sumptus erogati quodammodo mea opinione resarciri poterunt. Plura et particulariora clar:us et præst:mus vir d:nus Georgius Rollenhagius isthic scholæ rector, singularis meus amicus, ad vestras do:nes, sicubi opus fuerit, referet.

Deus opt. max. vos omnes diu conservet florentes et incolumes.
Dabantur ex arce Cæsarea Benatica die 4 aprilis stylo novo anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most reverend, most noble, and most renowned men, the Lords Dean and the other Capitulars of the Metropolitan Church in the Archbishopric of Magdeburg, his most honoured Lords and friends.

Most reverend and most noble men, Lords and most honoured friends.

Since the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement lord, has already before given a letter to the most ample Senate of Magdeburg, that they would procure my astronomical instruments and the furniture stored there to be transferred hither into Bohemia, by way of the river Elbe, at their own expense, for His Majesty's sake — but they seemed, by some counsel of their own, to refuse this very thing, though they were twice addressed by His Imperial Majesty on the matter with most clement letters — it therefore did not please His Majesty to write to them more often about it, but rather to direct letters on this account to your most reverend Chapter, especially since he understood that your most reverend Lordships are by no means averse from rendering this service obediently and reverently to His Imperial Majesty. Wherefore I now send His Majesty's letters containing these very things to your most reverend Lordships through my servant, at the same time most dutifully and reverently asking that — just as I doubt not you will obey His Majesty's most clement petition with most compliant mind — so also you will bring it about that all my things kept there be conveyed, with what haste is possible, safe and sound, and that provision be made for the things conducing thereto. His most clement Majesty will receive this with favourable mind; and it will at the same time, as I hope, be useful to the Republic of Letters, whose advancement I am persuaded you — illustrious not only by birth but also conspicuous for outstanding erudition — desire, and are willing to lend helping hands to. For those things which I labour at, in that sublime and divine astronomical science to be wholly restored — with untiring toil these many years past, and at no small expense, and (by the favour of the Maker of the heavens) earnestly strive to elaborate to the never-dying honour of this most august emperor and of the whole empire — are not private, or pertaining only to me and mine, but conduce to the whole learned world everywhere, as long as the sun and stars shall endure (as I trust). And therefore there is no doubt that your Lordships will gladly assist the common good here [I wholly promise myself]; which service, as far as concerns me, though my powers do not suffice to repay it, yet according to my measure I will be found perpetually grateful for, toward your most reverend Chapter; and if I can return nothing else in turn, yet I will not cease to extol and proclaim this your splendid goodwill among others. If you adjoin any of your faithful servants to accompany my things hither and take care of them, you will be able (if it so please your Lordships) to receive with him, in some quantity and at a just price, Bohemian wines — which grew so generous here last summer that very many preceding years scarcely gave the like; that this may be conveniently done, or if you desire anything else from these regions that is valued more highly there, I will take pains that, so far as in me lies, your wish be satisfied in this part, and so the expenses laid out can, in my opinion, in some way be recouped. More and more particular things the most renowned and excellent man Lord Georg Rollenhagen, the school rector there, my especial friend, will report to your Lordships, wherever there be need.

May God Best and Greatest long preserve you all, flourishing and unharmed.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 4th day of April, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipients. The Dean and Chapter (Capitulars) of Magdeburg Cathedral (the Metropolitan Church of the Archbishopric of Magdeburg). The MS is an autograph draft (Kladde).
  • Significance — the Magdeburg leg of the instrument-transport saga. A vivid document of the difficulty of moving Tycho's instruments to Bohemia. The imperial Senate of Magdeburg had twice refused Rudolf II's request (despite "most clement letters") to ship Tycho's astronomical instruments and furniture, stored at Magdeburg, up the Elbe at the city's expense. The Emperor therefore now appeals instead to the Cathedral Chapter; Tycho forwards the imperial letter (by his servant) and asks the Chapter to expedite the safe conveyance of all his goods. This complements the Hamburg-transit letter (brahe-t_15990929001) and the Wittenberg-college / Jöstel letters (brahe-t_16001009001, brahe-t_16001008001), which record the instruments coming from Benátky and Magdeburg via Leitmeritz up the Elbe to Prague.
  • The "Republic of Letters" argument. Tycho frames the favour as serving not his private interest but the respublica literaria — his work of "wholly restoring" astronomy being "not private … but conducing to the whole learned world … as long as the sun and stars shall endure," to "the never-dying honour of this most august emperor and the whole empire." A characteristic statement of Tycho's public, imperial self-presentation.
  • The Bohemian-wine inducement (a concrete sweetener). Tycho offers that, if the Chapter sends a servant to accompany the goods, that servant may take back Bohemian wines "in some quantity and at a just price" — the 1599 vintage having been exceptionally "generous" — or anything else from Bohemia valued at Magdeburg, "so the expenses laid out can … be recouped."
  • Persons named. the Dean and Chapter of Magdeburg; Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), rector of the Magdeburg school (the Altstädtisches Gymnasium), poet and Tycho's "especial friend" and local intermediary (a frequent figure in the edition, e.g. brahe-t_15880913001, brahe-t_16001009001); Rudolf II; the (refusing) Senate of Magdeburg.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 4 Aprilis stylo novo anno 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
