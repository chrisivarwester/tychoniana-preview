The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS ET DOCTRINÆ EXCELLENTIA PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONJ BRAHÆ, D:O DE KNUDSTRUP, PATRUO SUO CHARISS:

SI prospera fueris valetudine, patrue charissime, est quod gaudeam. Ego adhuc, Dej beneficio, bene valeo. Immortales tibj habeo gratias pro omnibus in me collatis beneficiis, inter quæ vel maximum reputandum, quod et parens charissimus pro sua prudentia et tua autoritate non minimum confirmatus studiis literarum nos instituere nec ullis impensis ad tam honestum propositum perficiendum hactenus parcendum censuerit. Quare si pro corporis et fortunæ bonis ad grates rependendas ipsa natura ducimur, quid ijs debebimus, qui vel opera sua mentes nostras (quæ, quantum cælum terræ, tantum corporibus præstent) humanitatis et doctrinæ donis imbuunt, vel imbuendas pro prudentia ac summo judicio suo effecere? Itaque quod officii mej erit, gratum me semper tibj præbebo, et mihi gratulabor, cuj talis contigit patruus, qui virtute, doctrina summorumque operum excellentia non Daniæ aut Germaniæ modo sed Europæ fere universæ venerabilis et charus est. Hanc animi mej significationem jam dudum est quod tibj, charissime patrue, debebam, sed cum de mansione tua nihil nobis certj constaret, hactenus differre coactus fuj. Nos vero jam ultra semestre Herbornæ vivimus, et ad festum Michaelis, ni fallor, sumus mansurj, nec quicquam adhuc ex Dania certj intelleximus. Nam quotidie tabellarium Æschildum expectamus. Fratrem autem meum Otthonem cum Aslachio jam domi esse censeam; cum enim hac commigraremus, in Anglia sive Scotia commorarj acceperamus. Relatum quoque nobis, Tagonem Tot Heidelberge litteris operam navare et alios quosdam nobiles Danos, quos jam credo Genevam excurrisse. Ex Dania rumor hic fuit, Æschilldum Sefeldium post celebratas filiæ nuptias subjto vita defunctum, et Fridericum Rosenkrantium caussa cecidisse et infamem pronuntiatum. Sed te diutius fortasse qvam par est tali garritu detineo. Jtaque te his, patrue charissime, feliciter Deo optimo maximo commendatum habeo, et amanter peto, ut me cum fratre meo Georgio et patruelj Falchone, qui hic quoque una mecum literis incumbunt, diligere porro atque consilio tuo dirigere non dedigneris.

Datum Herbornæ Nassoviorum 12 maj anno 1600. T. T.
Ericus Brahæ Stennonides
m. pp.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man most outstanding in nobility of birth and excellence of learning, Lord Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup, his dearest uncle.

If you are in prosperous health, dearest uncle, I have cause for joy. I myself, by God's goodness, am still well. I owe you undying thanks for all the benefits conferred upon me — among which even the greatest is to be reckoned this: that my dearest father, confirmed in no small measure by his own prudence and by your authority, has resolved to school us in the study of letters, and has judged that no expense should be spared, thus far, to accomplish so honourable a purpose. Wherefore, if we are led by nature herself to repay thanks for the goods of body and of fortune, what shall we owe to those who either by their own labour imbue our minds — which excel bodies as far as heaven excels earth — with the gifts of humanity and learning, or, by their prudence and highest judgement, brought it about that they should be so imbued? Therefore, in whatever shall be my duty, I will always show myself grateful to you, and will count myself fortunate, to whom such an uncle has fallen — one who, by virtue, learning, and the excellence of his supreme works, is venerable and dear not to Denmark or Germany only, but to nearly all Europe. This declaration of my heart is something I have long owed you, dearest uncle; but since nothing certain was established to us about your dwelling-place, I was hitherto compelled to defer it. We have now been living more than half a year at Herborn, and shall stay, unless I am mistaken, until the feast of Michael; nor have we yet learned anything certain from Denmark. For we await the courier Eskild daily. My brother Otto, however, I should reckon to be now at home with Aslaksen — for when we migrated hither, we had heard they were staying in England or Scotland. It has also been reported to us that Tage Thott is plying his studies at Heidelberg, and certain other noble Danes, who, I now believe, have gone off to Geneva. From Denmark there was this rumour: that Eskild Seefeld, after celebrating his daughter's wedding, suddenly departed this life; and that Frederik Rosenkrantz had lost his case and been pronounced infamous. But perhaps I detain you longer than is fitting with such chatter. And so with these [words], dearest uncle, I hold you happily commended to God, best and greatest; and I lovingly ask that you not disdain to go on cherishing me, together with my brother Georg and my cousin Falk — who here too apply themselves to letters along with me — and to direct me by your counsel.

Given at Herborn in Nassau, the 12th of May, in the year 1600. Wholly yours,
Erik Brahe, son of Steen,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound family letter to Tycho. Erik Brahe Stensen (Stennonides, "son of Steen") — a nephew of Tycho, son of Tycho's brother Steen Brahe — writing as a young student abroad. Warmly dutiful in tone, it credits Tycho's "authority" with having helped persuade his father to fund the boys' education.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, Dominus de Knudstrup, addressed as patruus (paternal uncle) — the precise kin-term confirming Erik as a brother's son.
  • The student colony at Herborn. Erik and his companions have been more than half a year at Herborn (the Calvinist Hohe Schule in Nassau, a noted centre of Reformed learning), intending to stay until Michaelmas (29 September). With him are frater meus Georgius = his brother Jørgen (Georg) Brahe and patruelis Falcho = his cousin Falk Brahe, all studying together. They await news from Denmark via tabellarius Aeschildus = the courier Eskild.
  • Other Danes abroad (the "chatter").
  • frater meus Otto … cum Aslachio = Erik's brother Otte Brahe, travelling with Cort Aslaksen (Aslachius) (1564–1624), the Danish theologian and natural philosopher — last heard of in England or Scotland, now presumed home.
  • Tago Tot = Tage Thott, a Danish nobleman studying at Heidelberg; other noble Danes are said to have gone on to Geneva.
  • News from Denmark (datable rumours).
  • Aeschildus Sefeldius … subito vita defunctum = Eskild Seefeld (Skeel?), reported to have died suddenly just after his daughter's wedding.
  • Fridericus Rosenkrantius caussa cecidisse et infamem pronuntiatum = Frederik Rosenkrantz (1571–1602), reported to have lost his case and been declared infamous — the legal aftermath of the 1599 court scandal (the affair with Rigborg Brockenhuus) that cost him honour and estates. (The same Frederik Rosenkrantz whose later, "happier breeze" Winshemius reports in brahe-t_16000813001 — the two letters bracket his disgrace and partial rehabilitation.)
  • Place. Herborna Nassoviorum = Herborn in Nassau.
  • Epistolary formulae. The health-formula opening (Si … vales, … est quod gaudeam; ego … valeo, the classical si vales bene est topos); T. T. = Totus Tuus ("Wholly yours"); m. pp. = manu propria ("with his own hand"). Stennonides is a patronymic ("son of Steen").
  • Calendar. Dateline 12 maj anno 1600 at Herborn; no style marker. Reproduced as written, not converted.
