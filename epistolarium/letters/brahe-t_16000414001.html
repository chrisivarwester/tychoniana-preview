The original

ILLUSTRISSIMO ET GENEROSISSIMO VIRO DOMINO COSMO CONCINIO E COMITIBUS PENNAe SERENISSIMI MAGNI DUCIS HETRURIAe APUD SACRAM CÆSAREAM MAIESTATEM ORATORI MAGNIFICENTISSIMO DOMINO OBSERVANDISSIMO.

ILLUSTRISSIME ET HONORANDISSIME DOMINE.

SERENISSIMI Principis, Magni Ducis Hetruriae, Clementissimas literas ab Illma Domne Vestra mihi favorabiliter missas, unâ cum proprijs suis, accepi, et magna inde affectus sum voluptate, cum quod Serma eius Celsitudo meis Clementissime respondere, ijsdemque benignissimo animo annuere non dedignata sit, tum etiam quod Illma Domo Vestra mihi tam benevole scripserit, et Sermi Principis sui voluntatem, ubi Pragae adfuero, plenius mihi exponere haud degravetur. Adveniam itaque, auxiliante Deo, quamprimum nonnulla ad aedificia, quae pro Instrumentis meis Astronomicis hîc debito modo disponendis, nunc parantur, pertinentia, adoravero; et observationes quasdam, quibus per instantes aliquot dies (neque enim alias intra annum eiuscemodi revertuntur) invigilandum, Coelitus deduxero. Spero autem haec, et quaecunque praeterea me remorari poterint, intra 14 ad summum dies expediri posse, ut Illmae Doi Vrae, ab hisce curis liberatus, promptius adesse queam, et quae nomine Sermi Principis sui mihi referenda habet, obsequentissimo animo percipere. Interim ut hanc morulam, pro consueta sua humanitate, candide accipiat, meque sibi commendatum habeat, officiosissime peto.

Deus Opt. Max. Illustrm D. V. diu florentem et incolumem conservet.
Dabantur ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ Die 14 Aprilis Anni 1600.
Illustrissae Domni Vae
Reverenter addictissimus
T. B.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most illustrious and most generous man, Master Cosimo Concini, of the Counts of Penna, most magnificent Ambassador of the Most Serene Grand Duke of Tuscany with His Sacred Imperial Majesty, most observed Lord.

Most illustrious and most honoured Lord.

I have received the most clement letter of the Most Serene Prince, the Grand Duke of Tuscany, favourably sent me by Your Illustrious Lordship, together with your own; and I have been affected thereby with great pleasure — both because his Most Serene Highness did not disdain most clemently to answer mine, and to assent to them with most kindly mind, and also because Your Illustrious Lordship wrote to me so benevolently, and does not find it burdensome to set forth to me more fully the will of his Most Serene Prince when I shall be at Prague. I will therefore come, God helping, as soon as I have attended to certain things pertaining to the buildings which are now being prepared for arranging my astronomical instruments here in due manner; and as soon as I have brought down from heaven certain observations which must be watched for over the next several days (for they do not otherwise recur within a year). But I hope these — and whatever else can delay me — can be despatched within at most fourteen days, so that, freed from these cares, I may the more promptly be present to Your Illustrious Lordship, and with most obedient mind receive what he has to report to me in the name of his Most Serene Prince. Meanwhile I most dutifully ask that you take this little delay candidly, according to your accustomed kindness, and hold me commended to you.

May God Best and Greatest preserve Your Illustrious Lordship, flourishing and unharmed, for long.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 14th day of April, in the year 1600.
Reverently most devoted to Your Illustrious Lordship,
T. B.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Cosimo Concini, e comitibus Pennae (of the Counts of Penna), the resident ambassador (orator) of Grand Duke Ferdinando I of Tuscany at the Imperial court in Prague. Tycho answers the Grand Duke's gracious reply (relayed via Concini) and promises to come to Prague to hear the Grand Duke's wishes in person.
  • Same-day Tuscan cluster. Written the same day as brahe-t_16000414002 (to Raphael Ciaparenius, the Florentine ambassador's house-prefect) — both part of Tycho's cultivation of the Medici/Tuscan channel at Prague, the continuation of the 1598 book-gift to the Grand Duke (brahe-t_15981108001).
  • The instruments and the time-critical observations. Tycho excuses a short delay: he must finish arranging the buildings being prepared for his astronomical instruments, and must make "certain observations … to be watched for over the next several days — for they do not otherwise recur within a year" (an annually-recurring configuration, e.g. of the Sun or a planet at a particular elongation). He expects to be free "within at most fourteen days." A nice glimpse of observational priorities overriding court courtesy.
  • orator = (resident) ambassador; Serma eius Celsitudo = his Most Serene Highness (the Grand Duke). T. B. = Tycho Brahe.
  • Calendar. ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ Die 14 Aprilis 1600 = from the Imperial castle of Benátky, 14 April 1600; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Restored the closing honorific to 'Illustrissae Domni Vae' (Illustrissimae Dominationi Vestrae); Dreyer's print (TBOO VIII p.301) confirms this addresses Concini directly, not 'the Most Serene Prince', so the translation's bracketed guess is replaced with 'Your Illustrious Lordship'.
