The original

ILLVSTRISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO, DOMINO WILHELMO, LANDGRAVIO HASSIÆ, COMITI IN CATZENELLEBOGEN, DIETZ, ZIEGENHAIN ET NIDDA: PRINCIPI AC DOMINO CLEMENTISSIMO. S. D.

AVENIT iam nuper huc in Daniam ad me T. Celsit. Mathematicus CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS, Princeps Illustrissime, tuæque Celsit. literas attulit, quibus adeo diutinam in scribendo ad me intermissionem tum ob curarum molem, tum etiam Mathematici aduersam valetudinem, benigne exponit. Quod vero studium Mathematicum T. Celsit. ob tot tantaque administrationis publicæ, senij etiam successiue obrepentis impedimenta, non prorsus repudiet, sed quantum per ocium licuerit, etiamnum continuet, mihi quam iucundum auditu est. Attamen, quod pari alacritate, quâ antea, priora inuena nunc recognoscere, tractareque vix promtum sit, mirum esse non debet; cum anni, curæ et morbi, vna cum corporeæ sanitatis molestatione, ipsum quoque animum atque ingenij vires, potiorem nostrî partem, non parum aggrauent atque obnubilent. Verum enimuero quicquid in hoc genere T. Celsit. propriâ operâ adeo sufficienter atque copiose, vt prius, ob causas antedictas, in posterum præstare vix suffecerit, id per alios idoneos artifices sua autoritate atque hortatu, tum etiam sumtibus et liberalitate, compensare facile sustinebit.

Intelligo ROTHMANNVM valetudinis causâ aërem mutasse, quod licet T. Celsit. atque alijs minus tutum atque consultum ab initio videbatur, tamen quantum ex ipsiusmet relatione cognosco, illi non infeliciter cessit. Nam statim ab itineris ingressu successiue vires recuperauit, et interea dum hîc moraretur, non admodum infirmo vsus est habitu. Quamuis resolutiones et coagulationes istæ Tartareæ, quibus intrinsecus infestatur, adeo alte etiam in hac iuuenili ætate radices egerint, vt non tam facile per solam loci mutationem, atque victus rationem, prorsus exstirpari queant, cum et Galenistarum, vtut satis verbose et affectate de his et similibus disserentium medicationes quasi ex alto despiciant. Neque enim citra rem aiebat incomparabilis ille Germanorum Philosophus et Medicus, a quamplurimis citius improbatus quam intellectus, Aureolus Theophrastus, Tartaream Roades nequeunt curare Podagram, per Roades (suo more ludens in vocabulis) rudes et inexpertos Medicos intelligens. Excellentiora sane et præsentiora hîc requiruntur Medicamenta, quam Syrupi, atque Decocta, Pillulæque aut similes Seplasiariorum impuræ et confusæ mixtiones suppeditare queant. Sed de his, cum alterius sint negotij, plura non addam.

Fuit autem ipsius ROTHMANNI accessus mihi inprimis gratus, tam quod a T. Celsit. mihi commendabatur, quam quod ipsum ob egregiam et penitiorem Artis Astronomicæ et rerum Mathematicarum cognitionem plurimi semper fecerim. Inspexit ille omnia mea Instrumenta Astronomica, de quibus ipsemet ad T. Celsit. referet. De Globo quoque Orichalcico quadrupedali, vel potius ampliori in diametro, quem Automatopæus T. Cel. iam præ manibus habet, me certiorem fecit. At vereor, vt sola tornatione eum ad exactam rotunditatem vndiquaque perducat. Id quod meus, qui maior adhuc est, vtpote sexpedalis, me satis superque docuit. Sed ipsa experientia in his et similibus optima erit Magistra.

Quantum ad Statum Regni nostri attinet, omnia adhuc, diuino fauore, tuta et pacata sunt. Serenissimus Rex noster electus prospere degit, et cum Regni Primatibus nunc in Cymbria comitia peragit, quæ tamen hisce diebus finiuntur. Ego meo more studijs indulgeo Philosophicis, quantum per alias occupationes atque auocamenta licet. Liber noster primus recentiorum cœli Phænomenon fere extremam Typographi manum subijt, sed Papyri impressoriæ defectus remoram aliquam, quo minus prorsus absolutus sit, iniecit. Licet enim ipsemet in hac Insula Moletrinam pro Papyro conficienda non paruis sumtibus extrui curârim, tamen cum Chartopæo idoneo destituam nihil adhuc subsidij, quantum ad Papyri præparationem, hinc obtinere licuit. Spero tamen me breui e vicina Germania eiuscemodi artificem obtenturum, qui per instantem Hyemem, ea quæ desiderantur, confecturus sit; sicque in libro illo deficientia supplere commodum erit.

Scripsi circa initium præteriti Aprilis tuæ Celsitudini, de Cometâ huius Anni, qui paulo ante illuxerat, vnaque eius succinctam descriptionem e nostris Obseruationibus deriuatam transmisi, prout per literas ad ROTHMANNVM antea datas me facturum pollicitus eram. Verum, cum ab eodem nunc intelligam, id quicquid erat, T. Celsit. interea non esse redditum, in itinere intercidisse suspicor. Curaui itaque denuo eiusdem Cometæ expositionem transscribi, et his literis adiungi, quam vt T. Celsit. expendat, dijudicet, bonique consulat, rogo.

Plura non addam, ne Celsit. T. alijs forte occupationibus præpeditam nimium detineam. Vale.

Datæ Vraniburgi, Calendis Octob. Anno 1590.
T. Celsit. Addictiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

VENITAVENIT

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Illustrious Prince and Lord, Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, Count in Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain and Nidda: the most clement Prince and Lord. Greeting.

Your Highness's mathematician Christoph Rothmann has just now come hither to me into Denmark, Most Illustrious Prince, and has brought Your Highness's letter, in which you kindly set forth the cause of so long an intermission in writing to me — both the burden of cares, and likewise the mathematician's ill health. But that Your Highness's mathematical pursuit — amid so many and so great impediments of public administration, and of old age too creeping on by degrees — is not utterly given up, but still continued so far as leisure has allowed, is most pleasant for me to hear. And yet that it should now be scarcely ready to review and handle your earlier findings [priora inuena] with the same eagerness as before ought to be no wonder, since the years, the cares and the maladies, together with the affliction of bodily health, weigh down and overcloud not a little the mind itself too and the powers of the intellect, the better part of us. But truly, whatever in this kind Your Highness, by reason of the aforesaid causes, shall hereafter scarcely have been able to perform by your own labour as fully and copiously as before, that you will easily manage to make good through other suitable craftsmen, by your authority and encouragement, and likewise by your outlays and liberality.

I understand that Rothmann changed air for his health's sake; and although this seemed at the outset less safe and less advisable to Your Highness and to others, yet, so far as I learn from his own report, it has turned out for him not unhappily. For straightway from the start of the journey he recovered his strength by degrees, and meanwhile, while he tarried here, he was of no very infirm condition — although those tartareous dissolutions and coagulations by which he is inwardly beset have driven their roots so deep, even at this youthful age, that they cannot so easily be wholly rooted out by mere change of place and regimen of diet, since they as it were look down from on high upon the medications of the Galenists too, however verbosely and affectedly these discourse of such matters and the like. For not without reason did that incomparable Philosopher and Physician of the Germans — sooner condemned than understood by very many — Aureolus Theophrastus, say: ["]The Roades cannot cure tartareous Gout["], meaning by Roades (playing on words after his manner) crude and inexperienced physicians. More excellent and more efficacious medicines are surely required here than the syrups, and decoctions, and pills, or like impure and muddled mixtures of the drug-sellers, can supply. But of these, since they belong to another business, I shall add no more.

Now Rothmann's coming was especially welcome to me, both because he was commended to me by Your Highness, and because I have always set great store by him for his outstanding and more inward knowledge of the astronomical art and of mathematical matters. He inspected all my astronomical instruments, of which he will himself report to Your Highness. He also informed me concerning that four-foot brass globe — or rather, somewhat larger in diameter — which Your Highness's automaton-maker [Automatopaeus] now has in hand. But I fear whether he will bring it to exact roundness on every side by turning alone. The which my own, which is still larger, being six feet, has taught me well enough and more. But experience itself, in these matters and the like, will be the best mistress.

As to the state of our realm, all is still, by divine favour, safe and at peace. Our Most Serene elected King is faring prosperously, and is now holding an assembly with the chief men of the realm in Cymbria [Jutland], which however is ending in these very days. I, after my manner, give myself to philosophical studies, so far as other occupations and distractions permit. Our first book of the more recent phenomena of the heavens has all but received the printer's final hand, but a shortage of printing-paper has thrown in some delay, whereby it is the less quite finished. For although I myself have, at no small cost, had a mill for making paper built on this island, yet, being without a suitable papermaker, I have as yet been able to obtain no help from it as regards the preparation of the paper. I hope nevertheless that I shall shortly obtain such a craftsman from nearby Germany, who through the coming winter will make what is wanting; and so it will be convenient to supply what is deficient in that book.

I wrote to Your Highness about the beginning of last April concerning the comet of this year, which had shone forth a little before, and along with it I sent a brief description of it derived from our observations, as I had promised in an earlier letter to Rothmann that I would do. But since I now learn from him that whatever it was has not in the meantime been delivered to Your Highness, I suspect it was lost on the way. I have therefore had the account of the same comet transcribed afresh, and joined to this letter, which I beg Your Highness to weigh, judge, and take in good part.

I shall add no more, lest I detain Your Highness too long, hindered as you perhaps are by other occupations. Farewell.

Given at Uraniborg, on the Kalends of October [1 October], the year 1590.
Most devoted to Your Highness,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe → Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince of Kassel and Tycho's chief observational peer; their decade-long correspondence is the backbone of the 1596 Epistolarum astronomicarum liber I, in which this letter was printed.
  • DATING CRUX (resolved). The source dateline reads "Calendis Octob. Anno 1590" = 1 October 1590 (O.S.). The edition label gives "1587. 1. oktober." — a catalogue error, contradicted by the letter's own internal evidence: it is written just after Christoph Rothmann's stay on Hven (interea dum hîc moraretur — "while he tarried here"), which the biographies date to August 1590 (Rothmann sailed to Hven on the evening of 1 Aug. 1590 and stayed the month; Christianson, On Tycho's Island; Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg). It also mentions the elected King Christian IV's assembly in Cymbria and the comet huius anni (the comet of 1590). The header therefore leads with the as-written 1590 date and appends the catalogued 1587 in parentheses; by this chapter's convention the dateline is not silently altered, and the discrepancy is recorded here, not reconciled in the body. The year-as-written ("Anno 1590") is unambiguous in the source.
  • Salutation / subscription formulae. S. D. = salutem dicit, "sends greeting". T. Celsit. Addictiss. = Tuae Celsitudini Addictissimus, "Most devoted to Your Highness." Celsit. / Celsitudo throughout rendered "Your Highness". The recipient's full titulature (Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, Nidda) is preserved.
  • Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, mathematician at the Kassel court; the carrier of this letter's enclosed comet-account and Tycho's interlocutor of the preceding years.
  • Paracelsus passage (medical / alchemical). resolutiones et coagulationes istae Tartareae — "those tartareous dissolutions and coagulations," the Paracelsian tartarus doctrine of mineral concretions (here Rothmann's chronic ailment); kept as the period term, not modernised. incomparabilis ille Germanorum Philosophus et Medicus … Aureolus Theophrastus = Paracelsus (Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim, "Aureolus"), named with conspicuous praise — Tycho's own Paracelsian sympathy. Galenistarum … medicationes — the rival Galenist physicians, whom Tycho has the tartarus "look down on from on high." Seplasiariorum … mixtiones — the seplasiarii, retail drug-/ointment-sellers (after the Seplasia, the perfumers' street in Capua); rendered "drug-sellers." See this chapter's convention (period medical terms glossed, not modernised).
  • The Paracelsus tag — CRUX. "Tartaream Roades nequeunt curare Podagram" is given as a saying of Paracelsus and is rendered literally ["The Roades cannot cure tartareous Gout"], with Roades kept untranslated because Tycho immediately glosses it himself: per Roades (suo more ludens in vocabulis) rudes et inexpertos Medicos intelligens — "by Roades (playing on words after his manner) meaning crude and inexperienced physicians." Roades is thus a deliberate Paracelsian word-coinage / pun (on rudes, "crude"); it is not smoothed into ordinary Latin. Podagra (lit. gout of the foot) is here the genus "gout"; rendered "Gout." The maxim's exact wording may be corrupt or a paraphrase of a Paracelsian dictum — flagged, not silently corrected.
  • priora inuena — rendered "your earlier findings." inuena is unusual (apparently for inventa, "things found/discovered"); the reading is kept as printed in the Source block and the sense flagged here rather than emended.
  • Instruments. Instrumenta Astronomica → "astronomical instruments." Globo … Orichalcico quadrupedali → a four-foot brass globe (orichalcum = brass/latten); Tycho contrasts it with his own sexpedalis (six-foot) globe — the celebrated great brass globe of Uraniborg. Automatopaeus (Greek-formed, "maker of self-moving devices / automaton-maker") → the Landgrave's instrument-/automaton-maker at Kassel, i.e. Jost Bürgi; rendered "automaton-maker" with the Greek-derived term flagged. sola tornatione … ad exactam rotunditatem — "to exact roundness by turning [on the lathe] alone," a technical worry about globe-truing.
  • Liber noster primus recentiorum coeli Phaenomenon — "our first book of the more recent phenomena of the heavens": the volume in press, i.e. the Epistolarum astronomicarum liber I (eventually 1596), here described as fere extremam Typographi manum subijt ("has all but received the printer's final hand") but delayed by a paper shortage.
  • Paper-mill / papermaker. Moletrinam pro Papyro → the paper-mill Tycho built on Hven; Chartopaeo idoneo destituam — "being without a suitable papermaker" (chartopoeus, Greek-formed, "paper-maker"); he hopes to fetch one e vicina Germania ("from nearby Germany"). Tycho's epistemic hedge Spero … obtenturum ("I hope … I shall obtain") is preserved as a hope, not an accomplished fact.
  • Serenissimus Rex noster electus … in Cymbria comitia — the "elected King" is Christian IV (elected 1580 as a child, crowned 1596); in Cymbria = in Cimbria / Jutland (the Cimbrian peninsula), where a comitia (assembly / herredag) of the realm's Primates (great men) was being held. Calendar context O.S.
  • The comet of 1590. Cometâ huius Anni, qui paulo ante illuxerat — the comet of 1590, on which Tycho had sent Rothmann a succincta descriptio … e nostris Obseruationibus deriuata ("a brief description derived from our observations") circa initium praeteriti Aprilis (about the beginning of last April); the original being lost in transit (in itinere intercidisse suspicor — "I suspect it was lost on the way"), Tycho re-encloses a fresh transcript. Tycho's suspicion is kept as a suspicion (suspicor), not asserted.
  • Source-text reconstruction note. The working transcription had collapsed the original print's paragraph breaks (line/paragraph boundaries survived only as stray inserted capitals — .AVENIT, senijA, adeoA, suffeceritA, morareturA, requirunturA, mihiA, amplioriA, meA, tutaA, rogo.A, .A Plura, Calendis Octob.A). The Source block above restores the paragraphing on these markers and on the letter's clear logical movements (salutation; Rothmann's arrival + the Landgrave's studies; the Paracelsian medical digression; the instruments / globes; the realm + book-in-press; the comet; close + dateline); the stray capitals have been removed and the word-joins they marked separated. No word of the source has been added or dropped. Greek-derived coinages (Automatopaeus, Chartopaeus, Seplasiarii) are Latin-script in the source and so kept; there is no Greek-alphabet text in this letter.
  • Calendar. Calendis Octob. Anno 1590 = "on the Kalends of October (1 October), the year 1590," Old Style (Denmark and the Hessen court both kept O.S.). Roman-calendar idiom rendered with the idiom and glossed to the civil date by this chapter's convention.
