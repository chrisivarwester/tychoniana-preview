The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM

S. P.

ATTULIT literas tuas, Nobilis et Eruditissime Vir, filius meus natu maior, in principio Ianuarij superioris huc rediens, ex quibus caussas diutini tui silentij et intermissionis literarum, ob absentiam in Livoniâ, cognovi, quare te eo libentius excusatum habeo, nihil interim de tuâ constantiâ et semel incepto erga me amore addubitans; invenies et me eundem TYCHONEM meique semper similem in te redamando. Utinam tu sedes Paternas, quas iure merito affectas, æque feliciter recuperare possis, et tranquillum invenire portum, atque mihi et meis studijs, Divinâ Clementiâ, hîc apud Augustissimum Imperatorem pro his primordijs benignissime provisum est, spero in posterum, eodem Numine favente, adhuc uberiora. Si qua in re apud Magnates hîc in Aula tibi aliquando usui esse potero, non deerit tui honorifica mentio secundum nostram in te propensionem. Filius tuus, ubi accesserit, erit mihi acceptus; optarem et te Patrem, si fieri posset, hîc semel videre.

Occupatio Regni Suetici, quo CAROLUM nunc potiri audio, meâ quoque opinione maiores turbas dabit, modo SIGISMUNDO non defuerint vires. Non minor est virtus, quam quærere, parta tueri. Deus aliquando pacem Regionibus illis, diu tempestate bellorum et aliarum calamitatum conquassatis, largiatur. Ubi occasio huc mittendi tibi data fuerit, non intermittas, quæso, tam de publicis, quæ in istis oris geruntur, quam privatis tuis rebus, scribendi officium.

Vale.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsarea Benatica Die 29 Martij styl. nov. Anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to an unnamed [correspondent].

Hearty greeting.

My elder-born son brought your letter, noble and most learned man, when he returned hither at the beginning of last January; from which I learned the causes of your long silence and interruption of letters — namely, your absence in Livonia. Wherefore I hold you the more willingly excused, doubting nothing meanwhile of your constancy and of the love once begun toward me; you will find me, too, the same Tycho, and ever like myself, in loving you in return. Would that you might as happily recover the paternal seats which you rightly and deservedly strive for, and find a tranquil harbour, as it has been most kindly provided — by Divine Clemency — for me and my studies, here with the most august Emperor, for these beginnings; I hope, hereafter, the same Deity favouring, [for things] yet more abundant. If in any matter I can sometime be of use to you among the Magnates here at court, an honourable mention of you will not be wanting, in accordance with our inclination toward you. Your son, when he comes, will be welcome to me; I would wish to see you, the father, here too, once, if it could be done.

The occupation of the Kingdom of Sweden, which I hear Charles now holds, will, in my opinion too, give greater disturbances — provided strength has not failed Sigismund. It is no less a virtue to guard what has been gained than to seek it. May God at length grant peace to those regions, long shaken by the tempest of wars and other calamities. When occasion of sending hither is given you, do not, I pray, omit the duty of writing — both about public matters that are done in those parts, and about your private affairs.

Farewell.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 29th day of March, new style, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient — unnamed. The edition titles it ad anonymum. The internal evidence points to a Baltic / Livonian nobleman: he had been "absent in Livonia," is striving to "recover his paternal seats" (sedes paternas … recuperare), and is asked to report "public matters done in those parts" — i.e. the theatre of the Polish–Swedish war over Livonia and the Swedish crown. Identification flagged: no secure name. (He is plainly a person of standing, with a son who would call on Tycho.)
  • filius meus natu maior = Tycho Brahe the Younger, who returned to his father at Benátky at the start of January 1600 (cf. brahe-t_16000329003, …0414002), carrying the correspondent's letter.
  • The Swedish succession war. Occupatio Regni Suetici, quo CAROLUM nunc potiri audio … modo SIGISMUNDO non defuerint vires — Tycho's political comment on the deposition of Sigismund III Vasa (King of Poland and, until 1599, of Sweden) and the seizure of Sweden by his uncle Duke Charles of Södermanland (the future Charles IX). Tycho foresees "greater disturbances … provided Sigismund's strength has not failed," and moralises with the tag parta tueri — "to guard what has been won [is no lesser virtue than to win it]" (after Ovid, Ars amatoria II.13, nec minor est virtus quam quaerere parta tueri).
  • Calendar. ex Arce Caesarea Benatica Die 29 Martij styl. nov. 1600 = 29 March 1600, New Style (styl. nov. explicit); Bohemia, imperial. (The edition label gives only "Benatky"; the body supplies the date — the same day as brahe-t_16000329003.)
