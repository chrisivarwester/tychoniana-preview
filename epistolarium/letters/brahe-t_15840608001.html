The original

PRISCÆ AC VERÆ NOBILITATIS INSIGNISQUE ERUDITIONIS AC VIRTUTIS VIRO DN. TYCHONI BRAHE OTTONIDI, DOMINO INSULÆ HUENÆ, DN. ET AMICO SUO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO.
S.

LITERÆ tuæ, generose Dn. TYCHO, mihi duabus de causis fuerunt gratissimæ, cum quod familiaritatem inter nos hîc Augustæ conflatam, literarum beneficio fulcire non dedignatus es, tum quod scirem, eum qui dicebatur obijsse anno 82, adhuc in vivis esse et hac aura inter superstites felicissime frui. Qua felicitate ut quam diutissime vel propter Rempubl. literariam ad artes Mathematicas restaurandas temporum iniuria collapsas, fruaris, Deum Opt. Max. toto pectore oro. Porro ad petitionem tuam quod attinet de emendis libris, feci quod potui. Inprimis autem mihi curæ fuit SCALIGERI de emendatione temporum, qui in fine appendicem habet complectentem iudicium, quo Gregorianum seu Lilianum, ut ipse vocat, Calendarium in universum improbat, uti videbis. Huic adiunxi aliorum in Germania editorum scripta quotquot habere potui, quæ in unum volumen sunt coniecta et Heidelbergæ edita post. Edidit et IACOBUS HEERBRANDUS Theologus Tubingensis Theses contra Calendarium, quibus nostrorum Theologorum sententiam de non recipiendo Calendario comprobavit, sed a Senatu sunt prohibitæ et explosæ, ut sic loquar. Superioribus scriptis adiunxi HAGECIJ Responsionem ad HANNIBALEM et GRAMINÆUM de Stella Nova, quæ ante triennium per me ad te non missa esse recordor. Proinde tibi non ingrata futura auguror. Librum de Triangulis, quem inprimis desiderare videris, venalem non reperi. Precium singulorum, quorum undecim in peculiari scheda his literis inclusa notatum invenies, pro quibus affinis meus mihi, ut voluisti, satisfecit. WERNERI libros cum de motu octavæ Sphæræ, tum de Observationibus stellarum fixarum, si aliquando in vetere quadam Bibliotheca invenero, faciam, ut me tui non immemorem fuisse intelligas. Reperi et alios libros Astronomicos in Catalogis WILLERIANIS, partim de Horologijs, partim de Astrologia, h. e. illa divinatrice arte tractantes, qui etsi plerique in promptu non erant, et a tuis studijs mihi quodammodo alieni videantur esse, singulorum tamen titulos consignare volui, precio plerisque adiecto. Si igitur ex his quidam tibi arridens tuum erit semestre spacium vel circiter ante me certiorem facere, ut si WILLERUS eius generis libros in promtu non habeat, eos tamen interea temporis huc transferendos curare possit. Qua in parte tibi meum studium et operam polliceor. Doctoris AMBROSIJ libri ante triennium WELSERO mortuo ab hæredibus sunt distracti et venditi.

Novarum rerum fere nihil habeo, nisi quod inter Senatores civitatis nostræ propter Calendarium Gregorianum magna animorum distractio fuerit. Maior enim pars non tam Pontifici] quam nostri contenderunt, Calendarium propter vicinos Bavaros et Episcopum Augustanum recipiendum esse tanquam politicam causam, ut correspondentia cum vicinis in commercijs, iudicijs et contractibus servari possit. Pauci ex nostris, tres præsertim Ædiles Curules asserentes Theologorum nomine, Calendarium non esse mere politicum negotium, sed etiam Theologicum, eo quod a Pontifice sit ortum et sub pœna excommunicationis recipiendum mandetur, minime igitur nostris Civibus et Ecclesijs obtrudendum esse, provocantes ad Cameram Imperialem mense Martio 1583. Tandem mense Maio An. 84 est lata sententia contra Ædiles et adhærentes, quapropter die 18. publicatur sententia, et ijdem 4 Domini iubentur domi suæ se continere, donec aliud quid de illis statuatur. Die 25. Maij, circa meridiem Præfectus Urbis, der Stadtvogt, iussu Senatus Doctorem MYLIUM Augustanum, nostrum primarium Theologum ex urbe in pilento educere conatur. Sed quid fit? Populus hoc audito accurrit et aurigam, quo minus progrediatur, strictis gladijs adoritur, MYLIUMQUE e pilento vi extractum in ædibus Pistoris prope portam Geggingensem habitantis abscondit, neglectis militibus custodiam in porta agentibus. Mox confluunt Cives armati vociferantes: nostrum Theologum cupimus. Dum hæc aguntur, Senatus ceteros Theologos in Curiam convocat, tractaturus cum illis de Calendario recipiendo; quod tum constanter denegârunt. Altero die post eliguntur ex singulis ordinibus, Patricio, Mercatorio et Plebeio 6 viri, qui de pace et tumultu sedando cogitent. Mox ultimo Maij advolant legati cum Wirtenbergici, tum Ulmenses, qui litem ita composuerunt, ut ex scripto vel publicatione in fasciculum tuum coniecta videre est. Non dubito, quin diversorum diversa sint futura iudicia de Theologis nostris. Sed non solum Legati, Iurisconsulti etc. verum etiam Tubingenses ipsi recipiendi Calendarij authores fuerunt, respicientes præsens periculum, et desertionem Ecclesiæ, salvo verbo et libertate Ecclesiæ retenta. Sed de statu huius urbis nostræ fortasse prolixius, quam par est. Id saltem a te, si non nimis audax viderer, expeterem, ut tuo nos iudicio de hoc Calendario neoterico informares ac firmares, an videlicet id priori præstet ac cœlo rectius congruat, nec ne. Id enim te unicum efficere posse, utpote qui luminarium motibus sedulo invigiles, persuasum habemus. Et sunt vota non nostra saltem, sed plurium. Quod superest, mi TYCHO, Deum Opt. Max. precor, ut tibi largiatur et vires animi et corporis, qui organa et opera in gratiam Matheseos studiosis inchoata absolvere, feliciterque ad finem perducere queas, quo sane nihil neque cognitu iucundius neque gratius accidere poterit.

Vale feliciter.
Datæ Augustæ Rheticæ vulgo Vindelicorum postridie Pentecostes CIↃ IↃ LXXXIV.
IOHAN. MAIOR.

To a man of ancient and true nobility and of distinguished learning and virtue, Master Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, lord of the island of Hven, his lord and friend much to be respected.
Greeting.

Your letter, noble Master Tycho, was most welcome to me for two reasons: both because you did not disdain to shore up, by the kindness of a letter, the friendship struck up between us here at Augsburg, and because I [thereby] learned that the man who was said to have died in the year [15]82 is yet among the living and most happily enjoys this air among those who survive. That you may enjoy that happiness as long as possible — if only for the sake of the republic of letters, toward restoring the mathematical arts that have collapsed through the injury of the times — I beg God, Best and Greatest, with my whole heart. Further, as concerns your request about the books to be bought, I have done what I could. First of all my care was for Scaliger's On the Emendation of Times, which at the end has an appendix containing a judgement whereby he wholly condemns the Gregorian, or as he himself calls it the Lilian, Calendar, as you will see. To this I have added the writings of others published in Germany, as many as I could obtain, which have been thrown together into a single volume and afterwards published at Heidelberg. Jacob Heerbrand, the Tübingen theologian, also published Theses against the Calendar, in which he confirmed our theologians' opinion about not accepting the Calendar; but they were banned by the Senate and hooted down, so to speak. To the foregoing writings I have added Hájek's Reply to Hannibal and Graminaeus concerning the New Star, which, I recall, was not sent to you by me three years ago. Accordingly I forecast it will not be unwelcome to you. The book On Triangles, which you seem especially to want, I have not found for sale. The price of each item — of which you will find eleven noted on a separate slip enclosed with this letter — [is set out], for which my kinsman has, as you wished, made satisfaction to me. As for Werner's books, both On the Motion of the Eighth Sphere and On the Observations of the Fixed Stars, if at some time I find them in some old library, I will see to it, so that you may understand I have not been unmindful of you. I have found also other astronomical books in the Willer catalogues, treating partly of timepieces, partly of astrology — that is, of that divinatory art — which, although most were not readily at hand, and may seem to me somewhat foreign to your studies, I nevertheless wished to record the titles of each, with the price added in most cases. If, therefore, some one of these takes your fancy, it will be for you to inform me about a half-year, or thereabouts, in advance, so that, should Willer not have books of that kind ready to hand, he may yet be able in the meantime to arrange for them to be brought here. In that matter I promise you my zeal and effort. Doctor Ambrosius's books were, three years ago, on Welser's death, dispersed and sold off by the heirs.

Of new matters I have almost nothing, except that among the senators of our city there has been a great clash of minds over the Gregorian Calendar. For the greater part — not so much [out of regard for] the Pope as our own [men] — contended that the Calendar was to be accepted, on account of the neighbouring Bavarians and the Bishop of Augsburg, as a political matter, so that correspondence with the neighbours in commerce, lawsuits, and contracts could be maintained. A few of our own — three Aediles Curule especially, asserting in the theologians' name that the Calendar was not a merely political business but also a theological one, in that it had arisen from the Pope and was being ordered to be accepted under pain of excommunication, and therefore by no means to be forced upon our citizens and churches — appealed to the Imperial [Chamber of Justice] in the month of March 1583. At length in the month of May, in the year [15]84, sentence was passed against the Aediles and their adherents; wherefore on the 18th day the sentence is published, and those same 4 gentlemen are ordered to confine themselves to their houses until something else be decided about them. On the 25th day of May, about noon, the Prefect of the City — der Stadtvogt — by order of the Senate attempts to lead Doctor Mylius of Augsburg, our chief theologian, out of the city in a carriage. But what happens? The people, on hearing this, run up and, that he may go no further, set upon the driver with drawn swords, and, Mylius having been dragged out of the carriage by force, hide him in the house of a baker living near the Göggingen gate, the soldiers keeping guard at the gate being disregarded. Soon the citizens flock together in arms, crying out: "We want our theologian." While these things are going on, the Senate summons the rest of the theologians to the Town Hall, intending to treat with them about accepting the Calendar; which they then steadfastly refused. The day after, there are chosen from each of the orders — Patrician, Mercantile, and Plebeian — 6 men, to consider the settling of the peace and the tumult. Soon, on the last [day] of May, envoys hasten in, both from Württemberg and from Ulm, who composed the dispute in such a way as may be seen from the written notice, or publication, thrown into your packet. I do not doubt that different men's judgements of our theologians will be different. But not only the envoys, jurists, etc., but even the Tübingen men themselves were authors of accepting the Calendar, looking to the present danger and the desertion of the Church — the Word and the liberty of the Church being kept safe. But [I write] of the state of this our city at greater length, perhaps, than is fitting. This at least I would ask of you, if I should not seem too bold: that you inform and confirm us, by your judgement, about this newfangled Calendar — namely whether it is better than the former one and agrees more rightly with the heaven, or not. For we are persuaded that you alone can settle this, inasmuch as you watch diligently over the motions of the luminaries. And these are the wishes not of us alone, but of many. For what remains, my Tycho, I pray God, Best and Greatest, to bestow on you strength both of mind and of body, that you may be able to complete the instruments and works begun for the benefit of those devoted to mathematics, and bring them happily to their end — than which, in truth, nothing more delightful or more welcome to know could befall.

Fare you happily well.
Given at Augsburg of Raetia, commonly [Augsburg] of the Vindelici, the day after Pentecost, 1584.
Johannes Major.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iohan. Maior = Johannes Major (Hans Mair), an Augsburg correspondent and book-agent for Tycho in the early 1580s; he procures and prices books from the Augsburg trade (notably the Willer catalogues) on Tycho's commission. (Not to be confused with the Wittenberg poet Georg Major or the earlier theologian.) The MS is an Afskrift (copy) in the same Vienna codex (cod. Vindob. lat. 10686) that preserves Tycho's incoming Augsburg correspondence.
  • Address & opening. Ottonides — "son of Otte," the Greek-style patronymic for Otte Brahe, Tycho's father (cf. the Wolf letter, 1571). Dominus insulae Huenae = "lord of the island of Hven," Tycho's seat (Uraniborg). S. = Salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting".
  • "the man said to have died in [15]82." A rumour of Tycho's death had reached Augsburg; Major expresses relief at finding him alive. Reported, not glossed away.
  • Books named.
  • Scaliger, De emendatione temporum (Paris, 1583) — Joseph Justus Scaliger's chronological work; Major flags its closing appendix condemning the Gregorian ("Lilian") calendar. Lilianum after Luigi Lilio (Aloysius Lilius), the calendar's designer.
  • Jacob Heerbrand, Theses contra Calendarium — the Tübingen Lutheran theologian's anti-Gregorian theses.
  • Hájek, Responsio ad Hannibalem et Graminaeum de Stella Nova — Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius), the Bohemian astronomer, replying to Annibale Raimondo ("Hannibal") and Theodor Graminaeus on the new star of 1572. ante triennium = "three years ago."
  • Liber de Triangulis — a "book On Triangles," unfound for sale; most likely Regiomontanus, De triangulis omnimodis, though the bare title is not decisive (so kept untranslated-in-title and not pinned to an author).
  • Werner, De motu octavae sphaerae and De observationibus stellarum fixarum — Johann Werner of Nuremberg.
  • Catalogi Willeriani / Willerus — the famous Augsburg book-trade catalogues of Georg Willer, issued for the Frankfurt fairs; Willerus is the bookseller himself. Horologia rendered "timepieces."
  • Doctoris Ambrosii libri … Welsero mortuo — the library of a "Doctor Ambrosius," dispersed by the heirs on the death of a Welser (the great Augsburg patrician house); the identity of "Doctor Ambrosius" is not fixed by the text and is left as the name stands.
  • The 1584 Augsburg Calendar dispute (the Kalenderstreit). The letter's chief news is the Augsburg confessional riot over the Gregorian reform of 1582, a contemporaneous event of which Major is an eyewitness reporter. Aediles Curules = the city's senior magistrates (rendered "Aediles Curule," the antique title Major uses for them); Camera Imperialis = the Reichskammergericht (Imperial Chamber of Justice), rendered "Imperial [Chamber of Justice]" with the bracketed noun supplied. Praefectus Urbis, der Stadtvogt — Major glosses his own Latin with the German civic title; the code-switch der Stadtvogt is kept verbatim and italicised. Doctor Mylius Augustanus = Georg Mylius (Müller), the leading Lutheran preacher of Augsburg, whose attempted expulsion (25 May) triggered the popular tumult. porta Geggingensis = the Göggingen gate (Gögginger Tor). The settling envoys come from Württemberg and Ulm; the Tübingen theologians ultimately favoured accepting the calendar salvo verbo et libertate Ecclesiae ("the Word and the liberty of the Church kept safe").
  • non tam Pontifici] quam nostri — the printed/transcribed text carries an editorial bracket after Pontifici, marking a defective construction (an expected verb/participle governing the dative, e.g. obsequentes, is wanting). Rendered "not so much [out of regard for] the Pope as our own [men]," with the supplement bracketed; the crux is flagged, not smoothed (see Confidence).
  • The question put to Tycho. Major asks Tycho's astronomical verdict on whether the Calendarium neotericum ("newfangled / new-fashioned Calendar," the Gregorian) coelo rectius congruat — "agrees more rightly with the heaven" — appealing to him as one who luminarium motibus sedulo invigiles ("watches diligently over the motions of the luminaries"). luminaria = the two luminaries (Sun and Moon).
  • Technical / glossary terms. artes Mathematicas restaurandas … collapsas — the restoration of the mathematical arts that have "collapsed" (Tycho's instauratio programme,), rendered "restoring the mathematical arts." organa et opera — organa = instruments, here paired with opera ("works"); rendered "the instruments and works." Matheseos (gen. of Mathesis) — "of mathematics" (the divine science of mathematics, as in the Wolf letter). Astrologia, h. e. illa divinatrix ars — astrology glossed by Major himself as "that divinatory art."
  • Numerals & money. undecim … in peculiari scheda — eleven priced items on an enclosed slip (the scheda); affinis meus … satisfecit — Major's kinsman has paid him on Tycho's behalf. die 18., die 25. Maii, ultimo Maii, mense Martio 1583, mense Maio An. 84 — dates kept as written.
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Augustae Rheticae vulgo Vindelicorum postridie Pentecostes CIↃ IↃ LXXXIV = "Given at Augsburg of Raetia, commonly [Augsburg] of the Vindelici, the day after Pentecost, 1584." The Roman-numeral year CIↃ IↃ LXXXIV = MDLXXXIV = 1584 (the apostrophus form CIↃ = 1000, IↃ = 500). Augsburg in 1584 was a Protestant correspondent writing Old Style (Julian) — indeed the whole letter is about Augsburg's refusal of the Gregorian reform — so the dateline is O.S.: Pentecost (Whitsunday) fell on 7 June 1584 O.S. (Julian Easter 19 April + 49 days), and the day after = 8 June 1584 O.S., which agrees with the edition's catalogue date "1584. 8. juni." Header date follows the as-written dateline; no silent conversion. Augusta Rhaetica vulgo Vindelicorum — Major's learned double name for Augsburg (Roman Augusta Vindelicorum; here also styled "of Raetia"),.
  • Subscription. Vale feliciter = "Fare you happily well".
