The original

THADDÆUS HAGECIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE. S.

CUM disiungerer a te Ratisbonae hyeme praeterita, nobilissime D. TYCHO, non minus moeroris reliquit ea disiunctio nostri quam tuus laetitiae adventus attulerat. Nam postquam te ex primo congressu mutuoque nostro colloquio cognovi, ita eram studio tui propter tuas eximias ingenij dotes et maximas virtutes incensus, ut tum demum conquiescerem animo, cum tecum essem. Itaque tam citam a nobis disiunctionem, quae me dulcissimo fructu iucundissimae conversationis nostrae privavit, non potui non dolere et conturbatiore animo ferre, neque illi ullum aliud levamen afferre potui, quam amorem et cogitationem tui, ut cum corpore tecum esse non liceret, saltem animo amantissimo tecum essem. Ac talem meum animum erga te iamdudum declarassem mea epistola, nisi toto hoc praeterito tempore multis hisque maximis fuissem districtus occupationibus, praesertim vero forensi quadam lite, cuius causa etiamnunc hîc me commorari diutius quam vellem oportet. Illa negotia, mi TYCHO, adeo me perturbarunt, ut hactenus neque in studijs nobis communibus, neque in his quae mecum liberalissimo animo communicasti, quicquam facere potuerim. Sed dabit Deus his molestijs quoque finem. Noster PIRCKMANNUS Ratisponâ domum reversus, re communicata cum fratre suo de opere TRAPEZUNTIJ imprimendo, quod Basiliensibus Typographis ipsomet consultore, ut scis, daturus eram, studiose a me fratris nomine binis literis petijt, ut sibi imprimendum concederem. Ego qui prae ceteris ipsi gratificari cupiebam, proxima Quadragesima misi opus illud. Nunc tantopere expetitum plane negligere videtur. Nuper enim scripsit ad me fratrem suum cum PLANTINO de impressione agere velle, putare etiam quosdam, antea in Italia fuisse evulgatum hunc librum. Hinc videtur extinctum esse desiderium excudendi operis. Ego vero impressum antea fuisse non existimo. Nusquam enim exstant exempla etiam in Bibliothecis clarorum virorum. GESNERUS quoque in sua Bibliotheca mentionem faciens operum TRAPEZUNTIJ et pro more suo loca, ubi singula impressa sunt indicans, huius nostri operis meminit quidem, sed non ait aliquando fuisse impressum. Utut sit, ego doleo quod librum non habeam in meis manibus et quod vagetur incertis sedibus. Utinam saltem integer redeat ad me, non facile deinceps exibit et custodietur arctius. De nostra stella an aliquid annotaveris, scire aveo. Ego meam responsionem contra RAYMUNDUM hyeme praeterita miseram imprimendam Francofurtum WECHELO, ut prodiret ad ferias Paschatis. Is vero cum primo spem fecisset, tandem nimis sero se excusavit imprimere non posse. Quoniam autem ea responsio iam antea fuit in multorum manibus, quibus legendam et iudicandam dederam, omnesque evulgationem eius expectabant exoptabantque, maturare editionem oportuit. Itaque curavi illam Pragae excudi. Eius exemplum tibi mitto, rogoque ut eam legas, et quae tibi in ea displicuerint, in ijs me libere moneas et coarguas. Si quid praeterea habueris ex tuis observatis distantijs stellarum, aut quidpiam aliud, mecum communicare ne graveris rogo. De HEMMINGIO vestro et statu Ecclesiarum vestrarum aliquid etiam adijcies. Tuo Achati quem tecum adduxeras Augustâ, si adhuc tecum est, adscribo salutem.

Bene vale.
Pragae 23. Augusti 1576.
T. ex animo THADDÆUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Thaddaeus Hagecius to Tycho Brahe — greeting.

When I was parted from you at Regensburg last winter, most noble Master Tycho, that parting left no less of grief in me than your coming had brought of joy. For after I came to know you from our first meeting and mutual conversation, I was so kindled with devotion to you, on account of your outstanding gifts of mind and your very great virtues, that I found rest in my spirit only then, when I was with you. And so I could not but grieve at so swift a parting from us — one that robbed me of the sweetest fruit of our most delightful conversation — and bear it with a more troubled mind; nor could I bring to it any other relief than my love and the thought of you, so that, since I was not permitted to be with you in body, I might at least be with you in a most loving mind. And I would long since have declared such a disposition of mine toward you by a letter of mine, had I not, throughout all this past time, been distracted by many and very great occupations, and especially by a certain lawsuit, on whose account I am even now obliged to linger here longer than I would wish. Those affairs, my Tycho, have so disturbed me that until now I have been able to accomplish nothing, neither in the studies common to us, nor in those matters which you shared with me in your most generous spirit. But God will grant an end to these troubles too. Our friend Pirckmann, having returned home from Regensburg, after the matter had been discussed with his brother concerning the printing of the work of Trapezuntius — which, as you know, I was myself, as adviser, going to give over to the printers of Basel — earnestly asked of me by two letters, in his brother's name, that I would grant it to him to be printed. I, who desired to oblige him before others, sent that work this past Lent. Now, though it was so greatly sought after, he seems plainly to neglect it. For he recently wrote to me that his brother wishes to treat with Plantin about the printing; and that some men even think this book was previously published in Italy. Hence the desire to print the work seems to have been extinguished. I, for my part, do not judge it to have been printed before. For nowhere do copies survive, even in the libraries of famous men. Gesner too, in his Bibliotheca, when making mention of the works of Trapezuntius and indicating, after his custom, the places where each was printed, does indeed recall this work of ours, but does not say that it was ever printed. However that may be, I grieve that I do not have the book in my own hands and that it wanders about in uncertain abodes. Would that it might at least return to me whole; thereafter it will not easily go out, and will be guarded more closely. I am eager to know whether you have noted down anything about our star. As for me, I had sent my reply against Raymundus last winter to be printed at Frankfurt by Wechel, so that it might come out at the Easter fair. But he, although at first he gave me hope, at last — far too late — excused himself, [saying] that he could not print it. But since that reply had already before been in the hands of many, to whom I had given it to be read and judged, and all were awaiting and longing for its publication, it was necessary to hasten the edition. And so I had it printed at Prague. I send you a copy of it, and I ask that you read it and, in whatever in it has displeased you, freely admonish and correct me. If besides this you have anything from your observed distances of the stars, or anything else, I ask you not to find it burdensome to share it with me. About your friend Hemmingius and the state of your churches you will add something too. To your Achates, whom you had brought with you from Augsburg, if he is still with you, I add my greeting.

Fare you well.
Prague, 23 August 1576.
Yours from the heart, Thaddaeus.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), the imperial physician and astronomer at Prague, author of the Dialexis de novae … stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt, 1574) on the 1572 nova. He and Tycho had met at the Imperial Diet of Regensburg in the winter of 1575 (here hyeme praeterita, "last winter"), where the imperial coronation of Rudolf II took place; the meeting began their long correspondence and friendship.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe — addressed as nobilissime D. TYCHO, "most noble Master Tycho." The edition header repeats "Tycho Brahe Tycho Brahe"; only one recipient is meant.
  • Ratisbona = Regensburg (where the two had met); Ratisponâ (ablative) "from Regensburg." Augusta = Augsburg. Francofurtum = Frankfurt (am Main), site of the great book fair (ferias Paschatis, "the Easter fair").
  • opus TRAPEZUNTIJ — a work of George of Trebizond (Georgius Trapezuntius, 1395–1486), the Byzantine-Italian humanist. Hagecius held a manuscript of an unpublished Trapezuntius work which he was trying to see into print; the whole middle passage concerns the frustrated negotiations over it (Basel printers → the Pirckmann brothers → Plantin).
  • PIRCKMANNUS — Pirckmann (Pirckheimer/Pirckmann), the agent at Regensburg acting with his brother over the printing; not securely identified beyond the letter. Left in the period form. fratre suo = "his brother."
  • PLANTINUS = Christophe Plantin (c. 1520–1589), the great Antwerp printer.
  • GESNERUS … in sua Bibliotheca = Conrad Gessner (1516–1565) and his Bibliotheca universalis (Zurich, 1545), the standard bibliographical reference; Hagecius cites it as evidence that the Trapezuntius work was never printed. Bibliotheca here = the bibliography/catalogue of authors, not a physical library; rendered as the title Bibliotheca.
  • nostra stella — "our star," i.e. the new star (nova) of 1572 in Cassiopeia, the shared subject of Hagecius's and Tycho's work; De nostra stella an aliquid annotaveris asks whether Tycho has recorded fresh observations of it.
  • responsionem contra RAYMUNDUM — Hagecius's reply against "Raymundus" = Annibale Raimondo of Verona, who had argued the 1572 nova was a sublunary (atmospheric) phenomenon; Hagecius's rebuttal is the Responsio ad virulentum … scriptum … Hannibalis Raymundi (Prague, 1576), which he here says he has just had printed at Prague (after Wechel at Frankfurt declined) and encloses for Tycho. Tycho too criticized Raimondo on the nova. Period form RAYMUNDUM kept in translation as "Raymundus."
  • WECHELO = the Frankfurt printing house of Wechel (André/Andreas Wechel, d. 1581); dative Wechelo "to Wechel."
  • HEMMINGIO vestro … Ecclesiarum vestrarum — Hemmingius = Niels Hemmingsen (1513–1600), the leading Danish Lutheran theologian at Copenhagen, then under suspicion of crypto-Calvinism; vestro / vestrarum ("your," plural) marks him and the Danish churches as belonging to Tycho's national sphere. Period form kept as "Hemmingius."
  • Tuo Achati — "your Achates": a classical allusion (fidus Achates, the faithful companion of Aeneas, Aeneid) for the trusted assistant/companion whom Tycho had brought from Augsburg; Hagecius does not name him and neither does the translation. Kept as the allusive "Achates."
  • forensi … lite — "a certain lawsuit / forensic suit" detaining Hagecius at Prague. commorari … diutius quam vellem: "to linger longer than I would wish" — the hedge ("than I would wish") preserved.
  • incertis sedibus — "in uncertain abodes," of the wandering Trapezuntius manuscript; the personifying metaphor (vagetur, "wanders") kept.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. = salutem (dicit), "greeting". Bene vale = "Fare you well." T. ex animo (subscription) = "Yours from the heart" (Tuus ex animo).
  • Calendar / place. Pragae 23. Augusti 1576 = "Prague, 23 August 1576." A Catholic correspondent in Imperial service, but the dateline is given plainly without a style mark; in 1576 (pre-1582) Old and New Style coincide, so no O.S./N.S. divergence arises. Marked [O.S.] in the header by project convention for a pre-Gregorian Julian date; not silently converted.
  • Quadragesima = Lent ("this past Lent," proxima Quadragesima), here a season-dating of when he sent the work.
  • Catalogue note. The edition label ("1576. 23. august") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Correction. Latin corrected from 'ipsemet' to 'ipsomet' (ablative, agreeing with 'consultore'), per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII p.37.
