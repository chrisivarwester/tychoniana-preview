The original

CLARISSIMO ET VARIA ERUDITIONE EXCULTISSIMO DN. THADDÆO HAGECIO AB HAICK, MEDICO ET MATHEMATICO PERITISSIMO, AMICO SUO PERPETUO COLENDO.
AHM HERN DOCTOR THADDEO HAGETIO ZU PRAG WONHAFFTIG.
S.

CUM hodie primum a nobili iuvene TAGONE KRABIO nostrate hinc in Cæsariam aulam iamiam abituro cognovissem illum Pragam transiturum, non intermittendum duxi, quin aliquid literarum ad te darem, optime et eruditissime mi THADDÆE, præsertim quia nihil adhuc responsi ad ultimas meas receperim, quod tamen indies avide expecto; dilationem vero hanc internunciorum inopiæ et hyemis importunitati imputandam censeo, absit enim ut de tua ergo me promta voluntate quicquam addubitem. Nunc itaque cum per hunc nobilem Danum rescribendi occasio percommodum offeratur, ne responsione ulterius supersedeas, te etiam atque etiam rogatum habeo, imprimis vero ut de ijs, quæ liber noster secundus Cometæ anni 77 tibi dudum transmissus continet, mecum ingenue et aperte conferre non gravatim duxeris. Eum enim te iam evolvisse satis mihi persuadeo. Primus liber, qui de Stella Nova affatim tractat, nunc typis meis excuditur; procrastinationi, quæ interiecta est, non saltem remoratio Privilegij Gallici Cæsariano adiungendi, sed etiam papyri impressoriæ defectus præter expectationem meam ansam dederunt. Nunc vero cum utrisque his remoris satis provisum sit, spero hunc quoque tomum brevi publici iuris futurum. Et quantum per impedimenta ac distractiones (quibus utplurimum æstivo tempore implicor) licebit, operis istius maturationem urgebo. In hoc non solum ea, quæ ad Novam Stellam proprie faciunt, accurate et luculenter declarata demonstrataque invenies, sed multa insuper alia ad totius Artis Astronomicæ redintegrationem plurimum facientia; quæque hactenus diu frustraque desiderata fuêre, qualia sunt: Solaris curriculi e multorum annorum exquisitissimis observationibus Geometrice ostensa et in numeros hisce proximis sæculis satisfacientes reducta restitutio, item affixarum Stellarum, præsertim vero earum, quæ circa Zodiacum versantur, præcisa et ipsi cœlo adamussim consona verificatio, et pleraque alia nunc non attinet singillatim referre. Id saltem te oratum cuperem, ut MUNOSI Hispani et Italorum istorum tum etiam aliorum, qui de hac stella aliquid publicârunt, scripta mecum communicare non intermittas, si quæ forte tibi innotuerunt ad nos non perlata. Idem etiam circa rem Cometicam expeterem. peterem. Constitui enim in singulis iuxta librorum finem, aliorum placita et animadversiones sub incudem veritatis revocare, ubi in primo atque ultimo tomo, quibus de Nova hac Stella et Cometa anni 80 agitur, tuarum lucubrationum honorificam, ut par est, introducere mentionem commodius erat, quam in illo intermedio libro de Cometa anni 77. Circa hunc enim nimium ab ipso cœlo (vel teipso iudice) deflexisse deprehenderis. Plura hoc tempore scribere festinus Nobilis huius abitus non patitur, verum ubi per hunc ad nos redeuntem, vel quacunque alia oblata oportunitate responsum a te recepero, uberius et pluribus de toto hoc negotio tecum agam. Intereaque et semper quam diutissime et felicissime vale, et Amplissimos Generosissimosque Barones D. IOHANNEM SEPTIMIUM a Lichtenstein et D. FELICIANUM ab Herberstein , quorum uterque Daniam superioribus annis pertransiens, me Uraniburgi invisere non gravatus est, meo nomine officiose saluta. Receperunt vero ambo in se WITICHIANORUM scriptorum et librorum, quos post fata reliquit, meo nomine procurationem, de qua ut eos per commoditatem oblatam admonere velis, peramanter peto.

Iterum et sæpius vale.
Raptim Hafniæ die 22. Martij Anno Domini 89.
Tui amantiss.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and, in varied erudition, most accomplished Master Thaddaeus Hagecius of Hájek, most skilled physician and mathematician, his perpetual friend to be honoured.
To Herr Doctor Thaddeus Hagetius, resident at Prague.
Greeting.

Since only today I learned from the noble young man Tage Krabbe, our countryman — now on the very point of departing hence for the Imperial court — that he will be passing through Prague, I judged that I must not let it slip without giving you some letter, my most excellent and most learned Thaddaeus, especially because I have as yet received no reply to my last [letter], which nevertheless I daily await eagerly; this delay, however, I reckon must be charged to the scarcity of go-betweens and the inclemency of winter, for far be it from me to doubt anything of your ready good will towards me. Now therefore, since through this noble Dane a most convenient occasion of writing back is offered, I have you asked again and again that you put off your reply no further — and above all that you do not find it irksome to confer with me frankly and openly about the things contained in our second book, on the comet of [the year] '77, sent to you long ago. For I am sufficiently persuaded that you have already perused it. The first book, which treats fully of the New Star, is now being printed on my own press; the delay that has intervened was occasioned, not only by the holding-back of the French privilege that is to be joined to the Imperial one, but also, beyond my expectation, by a shortage of printing-paper. But now that both these hindrances have been sufficiently provided against, I hope that this volume too will shortly come into the public domain. And so far as my obstructions and distractions allow (in which I am for the most part entangled in the summer season), I shall press on the ripening of that work. In it you will find not only those matters that pertain properly to the New Star accurately and lucidly set forth and demonstrated, but, besides, many other things conducing greatly to the renewal of the whole Art of Astronomy; and such things as have hitherto long and vainly been longed for, namely: a restoration of the Sun's course, shown geometrically from the most exquisite observations of many years and reduced into numbers satisfactory for these latest centuries; likewise a precise verification of the fixed stars — and especially of those that move about the Zodiac — exactly consonant with the heaven itself; and many other things which it is now not fitting to recount one by one. This at least I would wish you entreated: that you not omit to share with me the writings of Muñoz the Spaniard and of those Italians, and also of others, who have published something about this star, if any perchance have come to your knowledge that have not been conveyed to us. The same I would also request concerning the matter of the comet[s]. For I have determined, in each [volume], near the end of the books, to recall the opinions and observations of others to the anvil of truth, where, in the first and last volume — in which this New Star and the comet of [the year] '80 are treated — it was more convenient to introduce honourable mention of your lucubrations, as is fitting, than in that intermediate book on the comet of '77. For concerning this [comet] you will be found to have strayed too far from the heaven itself (even by your own judgement). The haste of this nobleman's departure does not allow me to write more at this time; but when I have received a reply from you — through this same man returning to us, or by whatever other opportunity offered — I shall deal with you more fully and at greater length about this whole affair. And in the meanwhile, and always, fare you as long and as happily as can be; and greet in my name, with all courtesy, the most ample and most noble Barons, Lord Johann Septimius von Lichtenstein and Lord Felician von Herberstein, each of whom, passing through Denmark in earlier years, did not find it irksome to visit me at Uraniborg. Both of them have, moreover, taken upon themselves the charge, in my name, of Wittich's writings and books, which he left behind after his death; concerning which I most lovingly ask that you be willing to remind them when the convenience is offered.

Again and oftentimes, farewell.
In haste, at Copenhagen, the 22nd day of March, in the Year of the Lord '89.
Most loving of you,
TYCHO BRAHE,
by my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Haick = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius, 1525–1600), Prague physician, astronomer and imperial counsellor at the court of Rudolf II; a leading observer of the 1572 nova and the comets of the 1570s–80s, and Tycho's principal scientific correspondent in the Empire. The two had exchanged cometary observations since the mid-1570s. The honorific styles him "most skilled physician and mathematician."
  • The two address lines. The first (Latin) is the formal superscription; the second is a German address/docketing line — "Ahm Herrn Doctor Thaddeo Hagetio zu Prag wohnhaftig" = "To Herr Doctor Thaddeus Hagetius, resident at Prague" — a routing direction (the code-switch is preserved by the edition's convention). AHM = am/an dem; WONHAFFTIG = wohnhaftig, "resident, dwelling."
  • S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting".
  • Bearer. nobilis iuvenis Tago Krabius nostras = the noble young Tage Krabbe ("our countryman," i.e. a Dane), here serving as letter-carrier on his way to the Imperial court (Caesarea aula) by way of Prague.
  • The works in progress. Tycho describes his Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata in its planned three-volume form: liber primus "de Stella Nova" (Book I, on the new star of 1572), liber secundus … Cometae anni 77 (Book II, on the comet of 1577 — i.e. the 1588 De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, already sent to Hagecius), and the ultimus tomus on the Cometa anni 80 (the comet of 1580). redintegratio totius Artis Astronomicae = "the renewal of the whole Art of Astronomy" — Tycho's instauratio/restitutio programme; restitutio Solaris curriculi = "restoration of the Sun's course"; verificatio affixarum Stellarum = "verification of the fixed stars."
  • typis meis excuditur — "is being printed on my own press": Tycho's Uraniborg printing-house. papyri impressoriae defectus = "a shortage of printing-paper"; Privilegium Gallicum Caesariano adiungendum = the French privilege (printing licence) to be joined to the Imperial one.
  • publici iuris futurum — "will come into the public domain / be made public," the standard idiom for publication.
  • sub incudem veritatis revocare — "to recall … to the anvil of truth," a metaphor (the anvil on which others' opinions are hammered/tested) kept as metaphor; lucubrationes = "lucubrations" (works of night-study/learned labours), kept.
  • Muñoz. Munosi Hispani … scripta = the writings of Jerónimo Muñoz (Hieronymus Munyos/Munosius, c. 1520–1591), the Valencia mathematician whose Libro del nuevo cometa (1573) reported the 1572 nova; Tycho asks Hagecius to forward Muñoz's and the Italians' tracts on the new star.
  • rem Cometicam — "the matter of the comet[s]," i.e. the cometary controversy/literature generally.
  • The named barons. Iohannes Septimius a Lichtenstein = Johann Septimius von Liechtenstein; Felicianus ab Herberstein = Felician von Herberstein — Austrian/Imperial noblemen who had visited Tycho at Uraniborg on earlier passages through Denmark. Daniam … pertransiens "passing through Denmark"; me Uraniburgi invisere "to visit me at Uraniborg."
  • Wittich's books. Witichianorum scriptorum et librorum … quos post fata reliquit … procuratio = the charge (custody/administration) of the writings and books Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586) left at his death; the two barons had undertaken to manage them, and Tycho asks Hagecius to remind them — a matter bearing on the books and papers Tycho hoped to recover from Wittich's estate. post fata reliquit = "left behind after his death" (fata, lit. "the fates" = death), rendered straight.
  • Dateline / place. Raptim Hafniae die 22. Martij Anno Domini 89 = "In haste, at Copenhagen (Hafnia), the 22nd day of March, in the Year of the Lord [15]89" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar; the edition's convention). The catalogue label ("1589. 22. marts. Copenhagen") agrees with the dateline. manu propria = "by my own hand," marking an autograph subscription.
  • Textual crux (flagged, not smoothed). After Idem etiam circa rem Cometicam the source reads "expeterem. peterem." — a duplicated/dittographic verb (the compound expeterem "I would request" followed by an isolated peterem "I would ask," with an intervening full stop). This is almost certainly a printing/transmission slip; the sense ("the same I would also request concerning the matter of the comet[s]") is not in doubt, but the doubled reading is preserved verbatim in the Source block by the edition's convention rather than silently emended. Likewise Tagone Krabio (ablative): the bearer's forename appears as Tago; rendered "Tage Krabbe."
  • No date discrepancy and no negation reversal. The dateline matches the label; the letter's several negations are reproduced exactly (non intermittendum, absit … ut … addubitem, ne … supersedeas, non gravatim, non intermittas, non gravatus est, non patitur), with no count altered.
