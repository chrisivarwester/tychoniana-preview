The original

NOBILISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE OTTONIDI DANO, DOMINO IN KNUDSTRUP, ASTRONOMORUM HAC TEMPESTATE PRINCIPI ILLUSTRI, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO ΕΣ ΧΕΙΡΕ.
S.

MISI tibi, Nobilissime Domine Tycho, Domine et amice honorande, quod petivisti a me proxime, testimonium de plagio Liddeliano; id ut omnino spero, legisti, intellexisti. Nunc exspectas a me Propempticon ad tuam Uranien scriptum. Id ego promisi; en solvo. Sed nescio an moneta sat ponderosa. Iterum autem ex te meoque intime dilecto et honorando ROSENCRANTZIO exspecto responsum et in hanc et alias meas literas. Spes ne me confundat, uterque facietis. Haec paucula habui. Te Domino commendo, et iam iam post dies octo ad promotionem meam Doctoralem Witebergae σὺν θεῷ habendam eo. Tu felicissime vale cum OLIGERO. Stetini. Pridie Ascens. Domini Anno 98· T. M. Add.

Dan. Cramerus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe Ottonides the Dane, Lord in Knudstrup, illustrious prince of astronomers in this age, his lord and friend — into [his] hands [ΕΙΣ ΧΕΙΡΑ].
Greeting.

I sent you, most noble Master Tycho, lord and friend to be honoured, what you lately asked of me — the testimony concerning the Liddelian plagiarism; that, as I altogether hope, you have read and understood. Now you await from me a Propempticon [a send-off poem] written to your Urania. That I promised; behold, I pay it. But I know not whether the coin be of weight enough. And again I await from you, and from my intimately beloved and honoured Rosenkrantz, a reply both to this and to my other letters. That hope may not confound me — you will both [see to it]. These few things I had [to say]. I commend you to the Lord; and now, in eight days' time, I go — God willing [σὺν θεῷ] — to my Doctoral promotion, to be held at Wittenberg. May you fare most happily, together with Holger. Stettin. The day before the Lord's Ascension, in the year [15]98. Most devoted to Your [self],

Daniel Cramer.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Daniel Cramer (1568–1637), Pomeranian Lutheran theologian, pastor and professor at Stettin (Szczecin) — the same correspondent as in the long Liddel-plagiarism deposition.
  • Tie to brahe-t_15980331001. This cover-note accompanies, and refers back to, Cramer's deposition on the "Liddelian plagiarism" (the 31 March 1598 letter to Holger Rosenkrantz, ordered to be read also by Tycho); here Cramer confirms he has sent that testimonium and now discharges a further promise.
  • Propempticon ad tuam Uranien — a propemptikon, the classical Greek genre of a send-off / valedictory poem; Cramer had promised Tycho a commendatory poem addressed to "your Urania" (Tycho's astronomy / his Muse / the Uraniborg programme). en solvo "behold, I pay it"; nescio an moneta sat ponderosa "I know not whether the coin be of weight enough" — a modest disclaimer of the poem's worth (the coin-metaphor for the verse he encloses).
  • Oligerus / Rosencrantzius = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), Cramer's former pupil and Tycho's kinsman, the recipient of the deposition.
  • Cramer's doctorate. post dies octo ad promotionem meam Doctoralem Witebergae … habendam eo — Cramer reports he is about to proceed to his doctoral promotion at Wittenberg in eight days. σὺν θεῷ ("with God / God willing").
  • Greek address. Printed ΕΣ ΧΕΙΡΕ — a garbled εἰς χεῖρα(ς) "into [his] hand(s)," the same address-direction (and the same corruption) found in brahe-t_15980331001; kept verbatim in the Source block, standard reading followed in translation.
  • S. = Salutem [dicit], "[sends] greeting." T. M. Add. = Tuae Magnificentiae Addictissimus (or Tibi … Addictissimus), "Most devoted to Your [Magnificence]."
  • Calendar. Pridie Ascensionis Domini Anno 98 = "the day before the Lord's Ascension, 1598." Ascension Thursday fell on 25 May 1598 (Old Style), so the eve = 24 May 1598, agreeing with the edition label. Stettin (Lutheran Pomerania) kept the Julian / Old Style calendar; the Ascension idiom is rendered and glossed to the civil date.
  • Correction. Restored the dropped epithet 'intime dilecto' (intimately beloved) in 'meoque intime dilecto et honorando ROSENCRANTZIO', per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.69; the English was correspondingly updated to translate the restored words, which the truncated the transcribed original had never carried into the translation.

- text error (low). Repaired. Verified against Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.69; the phrase is legible and unambiguous. Both the Latin and its corresponding English clause were updated.
