The original

DEM DURCHLEUCHTIGEN / HOCHGEBORNEN FÜRSTEN VND HERRN/ HERRN MAVRITZ, GEBORN LANDTGRAUEN ZU HESSEN/ GRAUEN ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ DIETZ/ ZIEGENHAIN VND NIDDA: MEINEM GNEDIGEN HERRN.

DURCHLEUCHTIGER/ Hochgeborner Fürst. Ewer F. G. seyndt meine Dienste nach allem vermügen jederzeit zuvor an bereit. Gnediger Herr. Wiewol ich mit Ew. F. G. weiter nicht bekant bin/ dan da ich Ew. F. G. Herrn Vater/ vor etlichen jaren zu Cassel besuchte/ zu welcher zeit Ew. F. G. noch gar jung war/ So hab ich doch gleichwol sieder der zeit von andern vielmals verstanden/ daß Ew. F. G. mit fürtrefflichen Fürstlichen Tugenden/ auch nicht mit geringer gelehrtheit soll begabt seyn. Vnd hat mir Ew. F. G. Herrn Vaters Mathematicus ROTHMANNVS alhie gesagt/ daß Ew. F. G. mein Name so sehr vnbekandt nicht sey: Viellicht auß den Schreiben/ die ich zu etlichen maln an Ew. F. G. Herrn Vater/ von Astronomischen Sachen dienstlich gegeben hab. Auß welchen vnd andern Vrsachen ich nicht habe vnderlassen wollen/ Ew. F. G. mit diesem meinem geringen/ jedoch diensterbietigem Schreiben zu besuchen/ In hoffnung/ Ew. F. G. werde es im besten auffnemen. Wan mir dan beyde von gedachtem Mathematico vnd andern/ die da gute Wissenschafft darumb haben/ glaubwürdiger Bericht gethan ist/ daß Ew. F. G. ohn dem/ daß sie in Hebraica, Græca, et Latina lingua wol erfahren vnd geübet sey/ vnd andere Artes liberales in pretio halte/ ein excellente gute venam Poëticam, vnd grosse Lust zu solchem Poëtico studio haben solte: So hab ich mich vnderstehen dürffen/ Ew. F. G. dienstlich anzulangen/ daß sie wolte vnbescheret seyn/ per otium ein Carmen commendatorium in Opera mea Astronomica,quæ præ manibus edenda habe zu componiren, vnd mir bey dieser Botschafft/ da die von der Franckfurter Messe widerumb zu rücke zeucht/ gnedich mitzutheilen. Ich versehe mich/ es werde Ew. F. G. solches keines weges inhonorificum seyn/ dan ich bin willens/ dasselbige gestracks auff das andre Blat in meinem primo Tomo (worvon ich drey Exemplaria des ersten Bogens hiebeygelegt habe) drucken zu lassen. Da derowegen Ew. F. G. sich wolte verdemüthigen/ dasselbige Carmen auff die eine Seite/ die da leer ist/ nechst für dem Diplomate Cæsareo, mit eigener Hand selber zu schreiben/ vnd mir also eines darvon widerumb zuzuschicken/ daß ich es darnacher baldt nechstfolgendt einem andern (welches ich a Serenissimo Rege Scotiæ, der auch ein trefflich guter Poët ist/ in gleicher massen täglich gewertig bin/ vnd auff der ersten Seit/ nechst dem Effigie, dieweyl er ein König/ vnd mit vnserm Königlichen Stamme beschwägert ist/ stehen wirdt) möchte imprimiren lassen: Erzeigte Ew. F. G. in dem nicht allein jren Sonderlichen gnedigen Willen gegen mich/ sondern auch ein wolgeneigt liebtragendes Gemüt gegen die herrliche Astronomische Kunst vnd andere hohe Studia, Vnd were es Ew. F. G. ohn das/ von wegen jres Hochgeliebten Herrn Vaters/ der zu dieser Kunst viel Jar her einen grossen Lust vnd gefallen getragen/ vnd hoch darein erfahren ist/ nicht vnrühmlich. Dan je vnd allezeit von Anfang der Welt/ sölche fürtreffliche Kunst/ von hohen mit reichem Verstande erleuchteten Potentaten, Keysern/ Königen vnd Fürsten/ wie auch insonderheit von Ew. F. G. Herrn Vater/ wie gesagt ist/ in grosser Liebe vnd Würde gehalten/ auch nicht ohn gebürliche Vnkosten befördert vnd gehandthabt seyndt. Ich zweiffel nicht/ Ew. F. G. werde gleichsfalls darüber zu halten geneigt seyn/ vnd jr Fürstlich Gemüt in solchen wissendtwürdigen Himlischen vnd hohen Sachen/ bißweylen von den Irdischen (die man doch tractiren muß) erhaben/ je lenger je mehr delectiren vnd acuiren, vnd daher gnediglich gewogen seyn/ meiner obangeregten Bitt bey sich stat sinden zu lassen. So baldt etliche von meinen Tomis durchauß verfertigt seyndt/ wil ich nicht alleine Ew. F. G. Herrn Vater/ dieweyl seine F. G. einen besondern wolgefallen darzu tregt/ vnd dasselb gnedigst von mir begert hat/ sondern auch Ew. F. G. etliche Exemplaria davon communiciren. Vnd wo ich Ew. F. G. nach meinem geringen vermügen sonst dienen kan/ erbiete ich mich williger dan willig/ vnd soll Ew. F. G. in dem jederzeit vber mich zu gebieten haben.

Wil Ew. F. G. in deß Allerhöchsten gnadenreichen Schirm/ zu langwirigem Wolstande/ hiemit dienstlich vnd getrewlich befohlen haben.
Datum Vraniburg, den 20. Februarij, Anno 1592.
Ewer F. G.
Dienstwilliger
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Illustrious, High-born Prince and Lord, Lord Moritz, born Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain and Nidda: my gracious Lord.

Most Illustrious, High-born Prince. My services, to the full extent of my ability, are at all times beforehand ready for Your Princely Grace. Gracious Lord. Although I am not further acquainted with Your Princely Grace than from the time when, some years ago, I visited Your Princely Grace's Lord Father at Kassel — at which time Your Princely Grace was still quite young — I have nonetheless since that time understood many times from others that Your Princely Grace is said to be endowed with excellent princely virtues, and also with no slight learning. And Your Princely Grace's Lord Father's mathematician Rothmann has told me here that my name is not so very unknown to Your Princely Grace: perhaps from the letters which I have at various times rendered, in matters astronomical, to Your Princely Grace's Lord Father. For these and other reasons I have not wished to omit to visit Your Princely Grace with this my humble yet dutifully-tendered letter, in the hope that Your Princely Grace will take it in the best part. Since, then, credible report has been made to me, both by the said mathematician and by others who have good knowledge thereof, that Your Princely Grace — besides being well-versed and practised in the Hebrew, Greek, and Latin tongue [in Hebraica, Graeca, et Latina lingua], and holding the other liberal arts in esteem — is said to have an excellent good poetic vein [venam Poëticam] and a great delight in such poetic study [Poëtico studio]: I have therefore made bold to approach Your Princely Grace dutifully [with the request] that you would be willing, at your leisure [per otium], to compose a commendatory poem upon my astronomical works which I have in hand to publish [ein Carmen commendatorium in Opera mea Astronomica, quae prae manibus edenda habe], and graciously to convey it to me by this messenger, when he travels back again from the Frankfurt fair. I am persuaded that this will in no way be dishonourable [inhonorificum] to Your Princely Grace, for I am minded to have the same printed straightway on the second leaf in my first volume [primo Tomo] (of which I have enclosed herewith three copies [Exemplaria] of the first sheet). If, therefore, Your Princely Grace would condescend to write that same poem, with your own hand yourself, upon the one side which is blank, next before the Imperial Diploma [Diploma Caesareo], and so to send me one of them back again, so that I might thereafter have it printed shortly next following another [poem] (which I am likewise daily expecting from the Most Serene King of Scotland [a Serenissimo Rege Scotiae], who is also a remarkably good poet, and which will stand on the first side, next to the portrait [Effigie], inasmuch as he is a King and is allied by marriage with our Royal line): Your Princely Grace would thereby show not only your particular gracious good-will toward me, but also a well-disposed, affectionate spirit toward the glorious astronomical art and other high studies [Studia]; and it would, besides, not be inglorious for Your Princely Grace, on account of your dearly-beloved Lord Father, who for many years has borne a great delight and pleasure in this art, and is highly skilled in it. For ever and always, from the beginning of the world, such an excellent art has been held in great love and worth by exalted potentates, emperors, kings and princes illumined with rich understanding — as also in particular by Your Princely Grace's Lord Father, as has been said — and has been furthered and maintained not without due expense. I do not doubt that Your Princely Grace will likewise be inclined to uphold it, and — your princely spirit at times raised up, in such heavenly and high matters worthy of knowing, above earthly ones (which yet one must handle) — will the longer the more take delight and be quickened therein [delectiren vnd acuiren], and hence be graciously disposed to let my above-mentioned request find a place with you. As soon as some of my volumes [Tomis] are quite finished, I will impart several copies [Exemplaria] of them not only to Your Princely Grace's Lord Father — since His Princely Grace bears a particular pleasure in it, and has most graciously desired the same of me — but also to Your Princely Grace. And wherever else I can serve Your Princely Grace according to my humble ability, I offer myself more willing than willing, and Your Princely Grace shall in that matter at all times have me at your command.

Herewith I would dutifully and faithfully commend Your Princely Grace to the gracious protection of the Most High, for long-enduring well-being.
Given at Uraniborg, the 20th of February, in the year 1592.
Your Princely Grace's
Devoted servant,
TYCHO BRAHE.

Below this letter Tycho had himself appended, in his own hand, the following epigram.

EPIGRAM.

Illustrious Maurice, grant your favour to lofty undertakings,
if you have leisure, and your care is not called elsewhere:

Nor let it weary you to give lustre to our labours with that
honey-flowing vein which your patron Apollo gave you.

You will do nothing unworthy of your Father, nor foreign to him,
who excellently knows and loves the laws of the Pole [the heavens]:

Nor is it any disgrace to you that a Prince's god-filled mind, not
bound to the earth alone, should act, and would fly aloft.

Add to this, that among the nine Muses whom you cherish,
Urania alone is to be regarded first;

If, then, you adorn her with even a modest song, she will become,
together with her brother [Apollo] and the rest of the choir, the more your friend.

I too — if small things may be set beside great — shall be
grateful, should my own little Muse be worth anything.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven. At this date (1592) Tycho was assembling his Epistolarum astronomicarum libri, the first volume of which he printed at his own Uraniborg press.
  • Recipient. Mauritz, geborn Landtgrave zu Hessen = Moritz "the Learned," Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632), then nineteen; he succeeded his father Wilhelm IV in August 1592, six months after this letter. The opening confirms the relationship: Tycho is "not further acquainted" with Moritz than from a visit to Moritz's Lord Father, Wilhelm IV, years earlier (Tycho's 1575 Kassel visit), when Moritz "was still quite young." Throughout, Ew. F. G. Herrn Vater ("Your Princely Grace's Lord Father") is Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), Tycho's princely correspondent and fellow-observer, here spoken of as living.
  • Rothmannus — Christoph Rothmann, Wilhelm IV's court mathematician at Kassel, who had visited Tycho on Hven in 1590 and could vouch for Tycho's name to the young Moritz.
  • Titulature. The address gives Moritz's full style: Landtgrave zu Hessen, Grave zu Catzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain vnd Nidda — "Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain and Nidda," the standard Hessian comital titles, preserved by this chapter's convention/. Ew. F. G. = Euer Fürstliche Gnade(n), "Your Princely Grace," kept throughout.
  • The request — the central matter. Tycho asks Moritz to compose, in his own hand, a Carmen commendatorium (commendatory poem) to be printed in the front-matter of the primo Tomo of his astronomical works — specifically on the blank verso (die eine Seite, die da leer ist) next before the Diploma Caesareum (the Imperial privilege/diploma Tycho had obtained). A companion poem from a Serenissimo Rege Scotiae — James VI of Scotland (later James I of England), whom Tycho had hosted on Hven in 1590 — was to stand on the recto next to the Effigies (Tycho's engraved portrait), James being given precedence as a crowned king "allied by marriage with our Royal line" (James had married Anne of Denmark in 1589). Adam Mosley (Bearing the Heavens, 2007) treats exactly this letter, reading the offer as a status-exchange in which Tycho dangles proximity to an Imperial privilege and a royal poem to secure the patron's contribution.
  • Latin code-switching. This is genuine chancery German, but Tycho switches into Latin for nearly every technical or bibliographic noun: in Hebraica, Graeca, et Latina lingua; Artes liberales; venam Poëticam; Poëtico studio; per otium ("at leisure"); Carmen commendatorium in Opera mea Astronomica, quae prae manibus edenda habe ("a commendatory poem upon my astronomical works, which I have in hand to publish"); inhonorificum; primo Tomo; Exemplaria; Diplomate Caesareo; a Serenissimo Rege Scotiae; Effigie; Studia; delectiren vnd acuiren (Latin verbs delectare/acuere given German chancery infinitives — "take delight and be quickened/sharpened"); Tomis; communiciren; imprimiren. All are kept in the source and shown bracketed in the translation by this chapter's convention
  • Litotes / negation (tracked). The letter's courtesy runs on understatement; the negations are reproduced one-for-one and not flattened: weiter nicht bekant bin ("not further acquainted"); nicht mit geringer gelehrtheit ("with no slight learning"); mein Name so sehr vnbekandt nicht sey ("my name is not so very unknown"); nicht habe vnderlassen wollen ("have not wished to omit"); keines weges inhonorificum ("in no way dishonourable"); nicht allein … sondern auch ("not only … but also"); nicht vnrühmlich ("not inglorious"); nicht ohn gebürliche Vnkosten ("not without due expense"); Ich zweiffel nicht ("I do not doubt"). Nine negations in the body, nine in the rendering.
  • williger dan willig — "more willing than willing," a chancery flourish of self-abasement; kept literal.
  • Calendar / place. Datum Vraniburg, den 20. Februarij, Anno 1592 = "Given at Uraniborg, 20 February 1592." Denmark used the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1592; the date is O.S. and matches the edition label (1592. 20. februar. Uraniborg) — no in-letter discrepancy.
  • Edition note. The text-of-record is Tycho's own Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596) — the very volume the letter is soliciting verses for — corresponding to Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI. No manuscript witness is recorded in the edition.
  • What it is. An autograph Latin epigram — not part of the dictated/scribal letter body — that Tycho appended in his own hand beneath a German-language letter to the young Landgrave Moritz of Hesse-Kassel, six months before Moritz succeeded his father Wilhelm IV as Landgrave (Wilhelm IV died August 1592). The scribal heading identifying it as autograph is itself part of the recovered text.
  • Occasion. Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel was Tycho's most important astronomical correspondent and patron among the German princes; Tycho later dedicated the Epistolae astronomicae (1596) to Moritz in his late father's memory [Mosley, Bearing the Heavens (2007)]. This epigram shows Tycho already cultivating the son directly as the succession approached, urging him to try his own hand at astronomical verse.
  • Content. The epigram flatters Moritz's poetic gift ("that honey-flowing vein which your patron Apollo gave you") and urges him to write in honour of Urania, Muse of astronomy — framing this as consonant with his father's own devotion to "the laws of the heavens" and as no derogation from princely dignity for a mind naturally inclined to fly high.
  • Crux. Lines 7–8 ("Nec tibi dedecorum, quia Principis enthea, soli / Haud obstricta Solo mens agat, alta volet") turn on a compressed, possibly punning contrast between soli ("alone") and Solo ("the ground/earth" — perhaps also evoking Sol, the sun) that resists fully certain resolution; the translation given follows the most natural sense but the exact syntactic binding is not certain.
  • Closing self-deprecation. Tycho signs off comparing his own poetic effort to Moritz's with an inverted Virgilian modesty tag ("si parva componere magnis licet", here "magnis...parva"), a conventional humanist topos.
