The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD HUGONEM BLOTIUM.

S.

CHARISSIME et præstantissime vir, amice plurimi semper mihi faciende. Cum forte per literas communis amici nostri Domini Doctoris HELLERI aut etiam aliunde intellexeris, me nunc in Bohemia degere, et occasiones, cur huc venerim, simul ad te, ut existimo, relatæ sint, nolo nunc pluribus ijs recensendis immorari. Id saltem te rogo, ut veteris inter nos amicitiæ Basileæ, uti scis, contractæ, et postea cum in Dania adhuc subsisterem, continuatæ memor, eorum etiam, quæ cum apud Nostrates hærerem, pollicitus es, recorderis, nimirum ut catalogum librorum in inclyta istic Cæsareana Bibliotheca, præsertim quod ad Philosophicos et Mathematicos attinet, mecum communices. Id si etiamnum in hac ob loci maiorem commoditatem præstiteris, rem equidem acceptissimam feceris. Literas hisce adiunctas cum libris quibusdam excellenti in Styria Mathematico IOHANNI CÖPLERO destinatis ad eum, ubicunque isthic vel alibi in vicinia moretur, rogo, ut per certum internuncium dirigere non degraveris. Habitavit antea in urbe Græcia, nescio an etiamnum ibi sit, intellexi enim eorum res turbatas esse. Si quod ab ipso responsum acceperis, Pragam ad dictum Doctorem HELLERUM mitte, mox me inveniet. Vale. Dabantur properanter ex arce Cæsareana Girsitza, ubi nunc aliquantisper ago, donec lues Epidemica Bennachiæ, ubi alias ex clementissima Cæsaris voluntate habito, prorsus remiserit.

Iamque iterum et sæpius vale.
14. die Decembris Anno 1599.
Tui amantiss.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Hugo Blotius.

Greeting.

Dearest and most excellent man, friend ever to be most highly esteemed by me. Since you have perhaps learned — through the letters of our common friend Master Doctor Heller, or also from elsewhere — that I now dwell in Bohemia, and since the occasions for which I came hither have, as I suppose, at the same time been reported to you, I do not now wish to linger in recounting them at greater length. This only I ask of you: that, mindful of the old friendship contracted between us at Basel (as you know), and afterward continued while I still resided in Denmark, you remember also those things which you promised while I was staying among our [people] — namely, that you would share with me the catalogue of the books in the renowned Imperial Library there [at Vienna], especially as regards the Philosophical and the Mathematical [ones]. If you would perform this even now — on account of the greater convenience of [my present] location — you would do me a most acceptable thing indeed. The letters adjoined to these, with certain books destined for the excellent Mathematician in Styria, Johann Kepler, I ask that you not find it burdensome to direct to him — wherever he may be staying there, or elsewhere in the vicinity — through a sure intermediary. He dwelt formerly in the city of Graz; I know not whether he is even now there, for I have understood that their affairs have been disturbed. If you receive any answer from him, send it to Prague, to the said Doctor Heller; it will soon find me. Farewell. Given hastily from the Imperial castle of Girsitz, where I now stay for a little while, until the epidemic plague at Benátky — where otherwise, by the most clement will of the Emperor, I dwell — has entirely abated.

And now again, and often, farewell.
The 14th day of December, in the year 1599.
Most loving of you,
Tycho Brahe,
I wrote with my own hand.

I have learned that Hungarian vitriol, and Hungarian turpentine too, and likewise the tartar that clings to the casks of Hungarian wine, are to be had there at a moderate price, though each of these sells dear here. If, then, you would let me know in good time what a pound of each of these costs there — especially if they are available in any quantity — and at the same time at what price a pound of your [region's] saffron may be bought, you will thereby do me a welcome service. And if, at the price stated, they suit me, I will send money with which they may be bought. I should like to have a good quantity of the three materials named first, that is, at least a hundredweight of each.

I have enclosed certain poems by various poets, composed in my honour since I removed from Denmark into Germany, which you may read at your leisure and afterward share with Master Johannes Kepler, sending them to him along with that bundle intended for him. If there are among you poets gifted with a witty vein — as I do not doubt there are — see that they add to the number. For I recall that you once sent me, from your Vienna, epigrams sharp and sonorous enough, into Denmark.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Hugo Blotius (Hugo Blotz, 1533/4–1608), the Imperial Court Librarian at Vienna (the Hofbibliothek), a Netherlandish humanist; Tycho's friend from their Basel days (1568–70) and during Tycho's Danish years. The witness is, fittingly, in Blotius's papers at the Vienna Nationalbibliothek (cod. Vindob. lat. 9737).
  • The two requests. (1) Tycho asks Blotius to make good an old promise: to send him the catalogue of the Imperial Library's holdings, especially in Philosophy and Mathematics — now the more useful since Tycho is close by in Bohemia. (2) Of high significance for the Tycho–Kepler relationship: Tycho asks Blotius to forward an enclosed letter and certain books to "Johann Kepler [Iohanni Cöplero], the excellent Mathematician in Styria," wherever he may be — Tycho noting that Kepler "dwelt formerly in the city of Graz [urbs Graecia = Graz/Graecium]," but that he hears "their affairs have been disturbed." This refers to the Counter-Reformation expulsion of Protestants from Inner Austria/Graz (1598–1600), which was about to drive Kepler out — and which helped bring him to Tycho at Benátky in February 1600. The letter thus documents Tycho's outreach to Kepler two months before their first meeting.
  • Doctor Helleri / Hellerum = "Doctor Heller," a common friend at Prague serving as Tycho's forwarding address. Identification flagged: not securely fixed (a Prague-based Heller).
  • Place. arce Caesareana Girsitza = the Imperial manor of Girsitz / Jiřice (near Benátky), where Tycho had temporarily withdrawn while the lues epidemica (plague) at Benátky abated. (The edition label gives only "Girsitz"; the body supplies the date.)
  • Calendar. 14. die Decembris … 1599 = 14 December 1599; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style (no n.s. tag in the edition, but the imperial location fixes it). Header so marked.
  • Subject matter. This is Tycho's own request, appended to a letter otherwise devoted to his temporary quarters at Girsitz during the Benátky plague, for raw materials used in his chemical/spagyric work: Hungarian vitriol (a sulphate salt, a staple of Paracelsian pharmacy and of Tycho's own medicinal preparations), Hungarian turpentine, cask-tartar from Hungarian wine (cream of tartar, likewise a spagyric staple), and saffron (crocus) — he asks Blotius to price each by the pound and offers to send money once quantities and prices are confirmed, wanting "at least a hundredweight" of the first three.
  • Addressee. Hugo Blotius (1533–1608), the Dutch-born prefect of the Imperial Library in Vienna and a long-standing correspondent of Tycho's since their student days together at Basel (Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg) — Vienna is the natural market for goods arriving from Hungary, which explains why Tycho asks Blotius rather than a Prague contact.
  • Kepler cross-reference. Tycho refers to "Master Johannes Kepler" by name and asks Blotius to pass on a bundle of commemorative poems to him — at this date (14 December 1599 N.S.) Kepler had not yet arrived in Prague (he would not meet Tycho in person until early 1600), so this is a trace of the pre-meeting correspondence network through which the two astronomers were already circulating material before their historic collaboration began.
  • Literary networking. Tycho mentions poems written in his honour by "various poets" since his move from Denmark into the Empire, and explicitly solicits more from poets in Blotius's Viennese circle, recalling that Blotius had once sent him "sharp and sonorous" epigrams from Vienna — evidence of Tycho actively cultivating a corpus of Latin encomiastic verse around his own person and exile, material that (via Kepler) may have been intended for a future printed collection.
  • Crux: Croci vestratis. "Your [region's] saffron" is translated as ordinary saffron (Crocus sativus, a valuable spice/dye/medicinal ingredient), consistent with its being priced alongside vitriol, turpentine and tartar by the pound; the same Latin word could in principle denote an alchemical "crocus" preparation (e.g. crocus martis, an iron-oxide medicinal powder), but the absence of any qualifying noun (Martis, Veneris, etc.) and the plain commodity-pricing context both favour the literal spice/dye reading.
