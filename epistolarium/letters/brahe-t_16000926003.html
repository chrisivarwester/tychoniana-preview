TYCHO BRAHE AD GEORGIUM ROLLENHAGIUM.

S.

QUOD supellex mea, quae Magdeburgi reservabatur, nunc tandem in has Bohemiae oras appulerit, est cur Reverendissimo Capitulo Vestro, quod sumtus in eos usus liberaliter suppeditavit, tum quoque tibi, Excellentissime et amantissime Domine ROLLENHAGI, gratias agam quam maximas, prout etiam peculiaribus literis ad honorandissimum et nobilissimum Dominum Decanum reliquosque Capitulares, quas illi per te officiosissime meo nomine tradi velim, testatus sum. Quod autem cum ANDREA ministro meo isthuc pro rebus illis huc adferendis emisso Reverendissimae illius Dominationi caeterisque nobilissimis Collegis nihil scripserim, non ulla praepostera voluntate, vel quod hoc ipsis me debere gratitudinis loco officium non agnoscerem, factum est, sed cum ijs ipsis diebus in quibusdam a Sacra Caesarea Maiestate, Domino nostro Clementissimo, mihi iniunctis, quae ocius absolvi debebant, essem occupatissimus, et aliâs plurimis praepedirer interpellationibus, ita ut varijs obruerer negocijs, vix tantum mihi supererat temporis, ut ad te nonnulla, quem ob familiaritatem nostram transcursorie alloqui licuit, exarare potuerim. Quare praevidens, me id, quod iuremerito facere oportuisset, ut indecenter omitterem, compelli, te diligenter rogavi, ut hanc literarum intermissionem apud Reverendissimum Capitulum excusares, meque cum nautis, ubi res meas huc advexissent, ad vos redituris illis scripturum indicavi, quod et nunc effeci, non quidem per nautas, siquidem illi nimis cito Leutmeritzio discesserunt, sed cum hoc tabellario Hamburgo adveniente et commodum sese offerente, qui in illas oras confestim rediturus est.

Sororem meam SOPHIAM adhuc Segebergae prope Lubecam haerere, idem tabellarius refert, at ubi ERICUS LANGIUS sit, ignorat. Doleo utriusque fortunam pertinaciter reluctantem. Scripsi SIMONI â PETKUM Procuratori meo Hamburgensi, ut ferreas fornaces, et si quae alia ibi ex meis rebus residua sunt, Magdeburgum per primam quamque oportunitatem ad te mittat, quae, uti polliceris, in tuis aedibus, neque enim multa sunt, reservabis, donec occasio suppetierit Leutmeritzium absque magno sumtu ea dirigendi. Optarem autem fornaces, de quibus dixi, sive integras sive fractas, huc ante Albis congelationem, modo fieri possit, afferri.

Instrumenta Astronomica, quae Benaticae habui, quotquot mecum in Bohemiam attuleram, quae numero erant sex maiora et aliquot minora, Caesar iussit, ut huc Pragam transferrem, ubi ex ipsius etiam Clementissima voluntate in domo quadam magnifice et splendide iuxta hortum prope arcem a FERDINANDO avo aedificata disposita sunt. Cumque ipsius Maiestas quodam die praeteritae septimanae ea perlustrare dignata esset, dedit mihi in mandatis, ut caetera omnia, quae Leutmeritzium impensis Reverendissimi Capituli Vestri iam advenisse retuli, quod et antea cognoverat, ibidem collocarem, ut omnia simul essent, tunc se, postquam singula perspexisset, de oportuniore loco, quam est in arce Benatica, ubi collocari et in debito usu conservari possent, provisurum clementissime addixit. Inclinare autem visus est ad aedes Curtianas, hac de caussa a vidua relicta comparandas, siquidem non longe ab arce sublimi prospectu constituantur. Domus etiam amplae et in edito loco sitae et dimidio saltem milliari hinc remotae atque in formam stellae cuiusdam, unde etiam nomen habet, a FERDINANDO Archiduce sumtuose extructae, quam hortus ferarum circuit, mentionem fecit, si forte ea observationibus et instrumentorum tractationi accommoda foret. At ego perinde esse, ubinam eius Maiestas id fieri vellet, modo perpetuari res possit, ne idem aliquando eveniret quod in Dania, subiectissime respondi: tunc ipsius Maiestas de aedificio quodam Viennae a MAXIMILIANO parente in alto quodam loco eleganter nec parvis impensis extructo nonnulla insinuavit, quod Viennam iter spacio paucorum dierum faciens spectare, et an pro his exercitijs commodum foret, dispicere possem: ibi se certum esse, quod perpetuo conservari possint omnia. Sed nihil conclusum est, antequam caetera instrumenta huc allata fuerint, et illa etiam in dicta domo iuxta hortum collocata viderit Caesar.

Ad ea, de quibus me commonefacis, in Postscriptis respondebo. Quae ANDREAS minister meus sui purgandi caussa tibi dixit, more consueto, quo leviter quidvis loquitur, fecit; neque enim per omnia ita se habent: discesserat is, cum adhuc Benaticae essem, me inscio et non interrogato, nec per octiduum quaesitus uspiam comparuit. Postea sponte redijt, cum alium forte Dominum invenire non posset, nec pecunia, quam abliguriret, illi superesset; at ego illum tunc recipere nolui. Sed ut Capitaneo Brandeisensi inserviret, permisi, quod non diu durare potuit. Falsa etiam sunt, quae de pecunia illi a me debita narravit: nam persoluta sunt illi omnia, quae stipendij loco addixeram, nec habet, quod iure ulterius poscat, ut vel hoc nomine, quod iniuste de me apud alios conqueratur, cohercendus foret, verum ego illi ob tuam intercessionem ignoscam, quia, uti ais, vere simplicissimus est minusque prudens, et quidvis inconsiderate effutit nec sibi ipsi constat. Si in posterum frugi esse voluerit, videbo, quid in tui gratiam facere potero, aut si sui similis permanserit, uti proculdubio faciet, dabo operam, ut alium acquirat Dominum, quod et antea hîc molitus sum apud satellitum praefectum, sed necdum succedere potuit.

Quod Dominus CASPAR ALEMANN Consul Vester aegre tulerit, in sua absentia reliquum Senatum officium in advehendis huc meis rebus Caesari denegasse, libenter audio, illique pro hoc suo erga me favore gratias habeo. Praestitisset forte reliquos civiliores fuisse et Caesareae Maiestati, summo Magistratui, obsequentiores se exhibuisse. Verum ego id potius, quomodocunque datur, excusare quam aggravare malim.

De URSO isto quae subiunxisti, ita ferme evenerunt, prout ominari visus es; nam sive in Castris Gallicis, quibus assuefactus erat, sive Asthmate et Phthisi, vel potius utraque de caussa consumtus ante sex septimanas expiravit, idque ea ipsa hora, qua Citatio Commissariorum Caesaris, quos, ut de ipso eiusque famoso scripto iudicarent, impetraram, insinuanda fuit: quam primum enim huc Pragam (ut conscius sis) a Caesare, statim ac Pilsena redierat, Clementissime vocatus adveneram, atque URSUM istum periculose decumbere mihi relatum esset, misi ad illum duos Iuris Doctores unâ cum publico Notario, quaesitum, an scriptum istud maledicum et calumnijs scatens revocare vellet: simulque notulas praecipuas calumniarum illi praelegi feci; is licet plaeraque in ijs infitiaretur, quamvis in scripto isto indicata pagina expresse invenirentur, revocare tamen noluit, sed iudicum arbitrio omnia submisit, quod et lubentius vidi, ideoque a Caesare humilime obtinui, ut quatuor deputarentur commissarij, qui caussam iure deciderent, inter quos duo sunt Barones, Dominus CHRISTOPHORUS de SCHLEUNITZ et Dominus ERNFRIDUS de MINCKWITZ, reliqui duo Doctores Iuris ex ipsius Maiestatis Consiliarijs. Defuncto autem reo, et citatione ante obitum ipsi non praelecta, actio suspensa est, donec novum decretum, quod et brevi futurum confido, superveniret. Expecto saltem reditum Procuratoris mei in hac caussa, Doctoris cuiusdam Iuris IOHANNIS FRITZSCHIJ, qui Budissinum in Lusatiam profectus est. Nam actio non est mere personalis, sed etiam realis, ob scriptum publice editum, perpetuo loco sui authoris loquens. Ideoque contra hoc procedendum, omnesque calumniae, quibus abundat, detegendae, refutandae et condemnandae sunt, posteaque alio scripto in publicum emisso propalandae, in quo totus processus omnesque contrariae probationes continebuntur, adiuncto Caes. Matis decreto, quo per totum Imperium famosus iste liber irritus reddetur, seu, uti aiunt, annullabitur. Et iam Reverendissimo Archiepiscopo Pragensi a Caesare iniunctum est, ut in Typographum animadvertat, atque exemplaria omnia conquiri faciat, quotquot Pragae reperiuntur, eaque Vulcano tradat: si aliquantisper supervixisset author, sententiam talem, uti ex commissarijs intellexi, accepisset, ut infamis redditus, capite plecteretur, aut secundum leges Bohemicas in quatuor partes dissecaretur; sed mihi non tam erat quaestio de ipsius persona, quam scurrilem et vanam fuisse omnes norunt, sed potius de libro isto tot iniurijs et mendacijs scatente abolendo, et mea meorumque fama ac existimatione vindicanda, quod etiamnum volente Deo fiet. Quin et ROTHMANNUM, sive adhuc in vivis sit, sive non, ab ijs, quibus illum aspersit maculis et vaniloquijs, liberabo, atque iniuriose ipsi illatas esse pronunciari efficiam, quod ad ipsum, si adhuc superest, perscribas velim. Et si quas habes eius manu scriptas literas, eas tuis, quibus ipsius esse Chirographum testeris, inclusas ad me mitte, ut inde probare possim, alteras tibi sub finem antecedentis anni inscriptas mecumque communicatas ipsius quoque manu exaratas esse. Ubi processus totus absolutus fuerit, omniaque in codicillos relata et typis edita, faciam te eorum participem.

Vale.
Dabantur Pragae die 26 Septembris stylo novo Anno 1600.

POSTSCRIPTA. Rogas, ut Reverendissimo Archiepiscopatus Vestri Domino Decano, qui tam benigne et libenter res meas huc in Bohemiam promovit, aliquid medicamenti contra calculi dolores, quibus quotidie infestatur, quod sane quam libentissime facerem, si eiuscemodi praesens remedium mihi nunc in promptu esset. Laboratorium, quod Caesar mea de caussa Benaticae pro praeparationibus Spagyricarum medicinarum extrui clementissime mandavit, propter eius Maiestatis diutinam Pilsenae absentiam, non est hactenus absolutum: ideoque eiuscemodi pyronomicis confectionibus, prout alias voluissem, operam dare non licuit. Ne vero optimum et honorandissimum illum virum de me tam bene meritum suo hac in parte desiderio plane frustrare viderer, subiungam quandam medicamenti formam, quae isthic non difficulter confieri poterit. Cuius usum in tali affectione praesens afferre subsidium compertum est. Rcp. Semin. Feniculi, Petroselini, Anisi, Saxifragiae, Cardamomi, Oculorum cancri, Lupi piscis, Nucl. persic. ana oz. I, Cynosbaton Germ. hainbutten beeren, Mespillorum, Charabes albi ana oz. II. Contundantur omnia in mediocriter tenuem pulverem, et cucurbitae vitreae imponantur, moxque superfundatur de spiritu iuniperorum, qui instar aquae vitae est, tantum, ut duobus vel tribus digitis supereminent, clausoque postea et bene obturato vitro in tepidum balneum omnia simul per unam hebdomadam digerenda collocentur, et singulis diebus vitrum circumspecte exemptum bis vel ter cum imposita materia circumagatur, et nonnihil hinc inde moveatur, ut specierum impositarum essentiae eo melius extrahi et spiritui iuniperino uniri possint. Quo peracto omnis materia frigefacta una cum liquore superfuso primum per lineum pannum fortiter exprimatur, deinde per bibulam papyrum coletur et sistatur, ut purior evadat: postmodum hic totus liquor essentiam specierum in se continens in balneo mediocriter calido per Alembicum distilletur, donec materia quaedam instar liquidi mellis in fundo remaneat, cui materiae tantundem quasi de spiritu Therebinthinae optimo, qui levi calore in balneo ascendit, addendum est, cum quo extractum illud denuo in tepido balneo, vitro bene clauso, per spatium unius septimanae digerendum; posteaque simul per bibulam papyrum iterum filtrandum, addito aliquantulo de liquore iuniperino prius destillato, si opus fuerit, ut promptius filtratio absolvatur. Qui tamen liquor lentissimo calore balnei inde postea auferendus est, ne sua ignea et acuta vi medicamento officiat. Ex hoc dicto modo diligenter praeparato medicamento dentur patienti tres vel quatuor guttae in vino mane singulis diebus, tunc quoque post meridiem ante caenam, idque quando et quamdiu opus fuerit. Ex liquore autem iuniperino, qui per Alembicum ascendit, poterit etiam parum quasi quartae partis unius cochlearis vesperi ante decubitum assumere instar alterius aquae vitae, si modo eam ferre poterit, idque potius hyeme quam aestate fiat, et non nisi bis vel ter in septimana eo vesperi, uti dictum, utatur. Haec si rite et praescripta ratione executus fuerit, sentiet inde favente Deo morbi non mediocre levamentum. / POSTSCRIPTA ALTERA. Quod amice me admones de ijs, quae quidam ex aula hac Caesareana cuidam praecipuo apud vos nobili scripsit, gratum est, licet omnia illa quae retulit, prout recte in literis tuis conijcis, falsissima (quod et praestat) sint. Ipsius Maiestas nunquam adhuc mecum de talibus locuta est, nec per literas aut oretenus Turcarum aut belli Turcici mentionem ullam fecit, multo minus ut ego, qui alienis rebus me non immisceam, nec vaticinia profitear, tale quid protulerim: imo, quod plus est, ea potius semper insinuavi, quae a melancholicis curis, animi deiectione, suspicionibus et superstitionibus otiosis, alijsque eiuscemodi mentem sanam turbantibus avertere possunt, idque ita sedulo hactenus egi, ut forte vix alius quispiam, qui diu illi ab intimis fuit consilijs, tam cordate et praesenter facere ausus fuisset, quod optime novit magnificus Dominus Barvitius Secretarius praecipuus, qui Caesari fere semper praesto est. Ille eo nomine aliquoties ex animo mihi gratias egit, seque hanc ingenuitatem, quodque ea, quae decent et conducibilia sunt, non dissimulem, sed sincere ipsius Maiestati, cum subiectissima tamen discretione, aperiam, deque ijs oportune commonefaciam, ad caeteros consiliarios praecipuos depraedicasse, non semel indicavit, quibus hoc gratissimum fuit, adeo ut res longe aliter se habeat, quam quidam ignari et nescio quid sinistro iudicio somniantes suspicari fortasse possunt. Sic non defuerunt, qui Caesaris tam diutinam Pilsenae absentiam mihi vanissime imputarent, atque id non saltem Pragae, sed et in varijs Bohemiae locis spargere non sunt veriti, cum tamen id mihi nunquam in mentem venerit, priusquam ab alijs referri audivi: et vix alius magis quam ego ipsius Maiestatis praesentiam desideravi, siquidem, interea dum abfuit, res meae minus promoverentur, utut essent, qui earum executionem in mandatis haberent. Nec scio quenquam hic, qui mei odio laboret: neque enim ulli occasionem dedi, imo quam plurimi adeoque praecipui quique non saltem in aula, sed et alibi in regno hoc amicitiam et conversationem meam sponte exoptant, et magis quam cuperem ambiunt, ut sic et temporis et valetudinis iacturam saepius quam vellem facere cogar. Quin et illustriores regni Primates mihi proprio motu nec rogati obtulerunt, ut in regni indigenam adsciscerer, nec alio me converterem; quod se primis comitiis effecturos prompto animo polliciti sunt. Si qui inter vulgares latitant morosuli, invidi et malevoli, quorum tamen neminem novi, illi in sua sese inescent et radant malevolentia ac iudicij perversitate, haecque habeant secum serventque sepulchro: infelix et miser est, cui nemo invidet, nec splendet aut conspicua virtus, quae livore suapte umbra caret. Vides itaque, mi Rollenhagi, quanta iniuria non saltem meam personam, sed, quod longe plus est, ipsam Sacram Caesaream Maiestatem, Imperij Caput et Dominum nostrum clementissimum, qui praesenti est animo, nec Turcas formidat, sed et Pacem, nisi honorifica et utilis sit, etiam ultro oblatam respuit, afficere non erubescunt, dum tam sutilia et a veritate alienissima scribunt. Si simul essemus, possem multis documentis et Tecmirijs irrefragabiliter ostendere istorum hominum vaniloquentiam: quodque contrarium potius verum esse. Interim tamen secreto ex te scire averem, quisnam fuerit, qui talia suis literis inspersit, et ad quem eas exaravit, non quod tales nugas morer, aut quod illi id obijcere velim (sunt enim multa dissimulanda et ex alto despicienda), sed saltem ut mihi ab homine tam sinistra et frivola suspicante et malevolo cavere possim, si forte ipsum novi aut alicubi in illum inciderim. Ut igitur eundem pauculis vel etiam tectis verbis mihi indices, quamprimum denuo ad me scripseris, te per amicitiam nostram rogatum habeo. Fiet id absque omni tuo periculo. Scio enim prudenter et taciturne in eiuscemodi malitiosis et perversis cavillationibus agendum esse. Vale. Datum ut supra in literis. / Addo et hoc, ea, quae superioribus literis de persecutione eorum, qui Euangelicam doctrinam profitentur in Austria, consensu Caesaris per Iesuitas fieri innuebas, falso ad vos relata esse. Omnia enim in Austria quoad religionem in priori sunt statu, nec Caesar permittit Iesuitas aut alios, qui se Catholicos nuncupant, turbas excitare, neque quemquam ob religionis diversitatem odit aut male tractat, cum etiam multos in sua Aula patiatur a Pontificiis dissentientes. Sed in Styria magna suborta est Euangelicorum afflictio, Iesuitis ex authoritate Principis neminem, qui non cum illis sentiat, istic ferentibus, quod tamen invito et aliter suadente Caesare evenit. Sunt qui ominantur, Turcam supervenientem controversias illas et persecutiones dirempturum, nam occupato (quod tamen Deus avertat) Canisio, nullum restat munitum propugnaculum, antequam Styriam invadere possit. Sed de his providebit is, in cuius manu sunt omnia.

TYCHO BRAHE TO GEORG ROLLENHAGEN.

Greeting.

That my goods, which were kept at Magdeburg, have now at last arrived on these coasts of Bohemia, is cause that I give the greatest thanks to your Reverend Chapter — which liberally supplied the costs for those uses — and also to you, most excellent and most loving Lord Rollenhagen, as I have also testified in a separate letter to the most honoured and noble Lord Dean and the rest of the Chapter, which I should wish delivered to him most dutifully in my name by you. That I wrote nothing to that Reverend Lordship and the rest of his most noble Colleagues when my servant Andreas was sent thither to bring those things hither, was done by no perverse intent, nor that I did not acknowledge I owe them this duty by way of gratitude — but because in those very days I was most busy with certain things enjoined on me by the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, which had to be finished quickly, and was otherwise hindered by very many interruptions, so overwhelmed by various business that scarcely so much time remained me as to scratch a few lines to you (whom, on account of our familiarity, I might address cursorily). Wherefore, foreseeing that I was compelled to omit unbecomingly what by right I ought to have done, I diligently asked you to excuse this omission of a letter to the Reverend Chapter, and indicated that I would write them with the sailors, when they had carried my things hither, returning to you — which I now do, not indeed by the sailors (since they left Leitmeritz too quickly), but by this courier, arriving from Hamburg and conveniently offering himself, who is about to return at once to those parts.

My sister Sophia is still lingering at Segeberg near Lübeck, the same courier reports; but where Erik Lange is, he does not know. I grieve the fortune of both, stubbornly resisting. I have written Simon von Petkum, my proctor at Hamburg, to send the iron furnaces — and any other things of mine remaining there — to Magdeburg by the first opportunity, which (as you promise) you will keep in your house (for they are not many), until occasion serve to direct them to Leitmeritz without great cost. I should wish the furnaces of which I spoke, whether whole or broken, brought hither before the Elbe freezes, if it can be done.

The Astronomical Instruments I had at Benátky — as many as I had brought with me into Bohemia, six larger and some smaller in number — the Emperor ordered me to transfer hither to Prague, where, by his most clement will too, they have been arranged in a certain house, magnificently and splendidly built by the garden, near the castle, by Ferdinand the grandfather [the Royal Summer Palace / Belvedere, built by Ferdinand I]. And when His Majesty, one day last week, deigned to inspect them, he gave me in charge to place all the rest (which I reported had now arrived at Leitmeritz at the expense of your Reverend Chapter — as he had known before too) in the same place, that all might be together; then, after he had examined each, he most clemently promised that he would provide a more convenient place than Benátky castle, where they could be set up and kept in due use. He seemed to incline toward the Curtius house — to be bought, on this account, from the widow left [behind] — since it is set not far from the castle, with a lofty prospect. He also made mention of a large house, set in a high place, half a mile distant hence, built sumptuously by Archduke Ferdinand in the form of a certain star (whence too it has its name), surrounded by a game-park [the Star Villa / Hvězda], if perchance it might be suited to observations and the handling of instruments. But I answered most submissively that it was all one where His Majesty wished it done, provided the thing could be made permanent, lest the same should sometime happen as in Denmark. Then His Majesty intimated something about a building at Vienna, built elegantly and at no small cost, in a certain high place, by his father Maximilian [the Neugebäude] — which, making the journey to Vienna in a few days' space, I could view, and consider whether it would be convenient for these exercises: there, he was sure, all could be permanently kept. But nothing has been concluded before the other instruments have been brought hither, and the Emperor has seen them too placed in the said house by the garden.

To the things of which you remind me, I will respond in the Postscripts. What my servant Andreas said to you, to clear himself, he did in his usual manner — by which he speaks anything lightly; for it is not in all respects so. He had left, while I was still at Benátky, without my knowledge and unasked, nor for eight days was he found anywhere; afterward he returned of his own accord, when he could find no other master, and had no money left to squander; but I would not take him back then. Yet I allowed him to serve the Captain of Brandeis, which could not last long. False too are the things he told about money owed him by me; for all that I had assigned by way of stipend has been paid him, nor has he anything he can justly demand further — so that on this very score, that he unjustly complains of me to others, he ought to be restrained. But I will forgive him, for your intercession's sake, since (as you say) he is truly most simple and not very prudent, and blurts anything thoughtlessly, and is not consistent with himself. If hereafter he will be frugal, I will see what I can do for him, for your sake; or if he remain like himself (as doubtless he will), I will take pains that he get another master — which I attempted here before, with the prefect of the guards, but it could not yet succeed.

That Lord Caspar Alemann, your Consul, took it ill that in his absence the rest of the Senate denied the Emperor the service of conveying my things hither, I gladly hear, and thank him for this his favour toward me. It would perhaps have been better that the rest had been more civil, and shown themselves more obedient to the Imperial Majesty, the supreme Magistrate. But I would rather excuse it, however it may be, than aggravate it.

What you subjoined about that Ursus came about almost as you seemed to presage; for — whether consumed in the French camps to which he was accustomed, or by Asthma and Phthisis, or rather by both causes — he expired six weeks ago, and that at the very hour when the Citation of the Emperor's Commissioners (whom I had obtained to judge of him and his infamous writing) was to be served. For as soon as I had come hither to Prague (that you may be aware), summoned most clemently by the Emperor as soon as he had returned from Pilsen, and it had been reported to me that Ursus lay dangerously ill, I sent to him two Doctors of Law, with a public Notary, to ask whether he would revoke that slanderous writing, teeming with calumnies; and at the same time had the chief notes of the calumnies read to him. Though he denied most of them (though they are expressly found on the indicated page in that writing), he yet would not revoke, but submitted all to the judges' arbitration — which I saw the more gladly; and therefore I humbly obtained from the Emperor that four Commissioners be deputed to decide the case at law — among them two Barons, Lord Christopher von Schleinitz and Lord Ernfried von Minckwitz, the other two Doctors of Law from His Majesty's Councillors. But, the defendant being dead, and the citation not read to him before death, the action is suspended until a new decree come (which I trust will be shortly). I only await the return of my proctor in this case, a certain Doctor of Law, Johannes Fritzsch, who has gone to Bautzen in Lusatia. For the action is not merely personal, but also real, on account of the publicly-published writing, which perpetually speaks in place of its author; and so it must be proceeded against, and all the calumnies it abounds in detected, refuted, and condemned, and afterward made public in another writing, in which the whole process and all the contrary proofs shall be contained — with the Imperial Majesty's decree adjoined, by which throughout the whole Empire that infamous book shall be made void, or, as they say, annulled. And already the Reverend Archbishop of Prague has been enjoined by the Emperor to animadvert on the Printer, and to have all copies sought out — as many as are found at Prague — and given to Vulcan [burned]. If the author had survived a while, he would have received such a sentence (as I learned from the Commissioners) that, rendered infamous, he should be beheaded, or, according to the Bohemian laws, cut into four parts. But for me the question was not so much about his person (whom all know to have been scurrilous and vain), as about abolishing that book, teeming with so many injuries and lies, and vindicating my own and my people's fame and reputation — which, God willing, shall still be done. Nay, I will also free Rothmann — whether he be yet alive or not — from the stains and vain slanders with which [Ursus] bespattered him, and will bring it about that they be pronounced injuriously inflicted on him — which you would write to him, if he still survives. And if you have any letters written in his hand, send them to me, enclosed in yours (testifying that it is his handwriting), that I may thence prove that the others addressed to you at the end of last year, and shared with me, are also written in his hand. When the whole process is finished, and all reported in codicils and published in print, I will make you partaker of them.

Farewell.
Given at Prague, the 26th day of September, New Style, the year 1600.

[Postscript:] POSTSCRIPTS. You ask that [I send] something of medicine to the most Reverend Lord Dean of your Archbishopric — who so kindly and willingly forwarded my affairs hither into Bohemia — against the pains of the stone, with which he is daily afflicted; which I should indeed most willingly do, were such a ready remedy now at hand for me. The laboratory which the Emperor, on my account, most clemently ordered to be built at Benátky for the preparation of Spagyric medicines is not yet finished, owing to His Majesty's long absence at Pilsen; and so I have not been able, as otherwise I should have wished, to give my labour to confections of that pyronomic kind. But lest I should seem wholly to disappoint that best and most honourable man, so well deserving of me, in his desire in this matter, I will subjoin a certain formula of a medicine, which can be made up there without difficulty, and whose use in such an affliction is known to bring present relief. Take: seed of fennel, of parsley, of anise, of saxifrage, of cardamom; crab's eyes; [the bone of] the lupus-fish; kernels of peach — of each one ounce; Cynosbaton (German hainbutten beeren [rosehips]), medlars, white amber — of each two ounces. Let all be pounded into a moderately fine powder and put into a glass cucurbit, and straightway let spirit of junipers (which is like aqua vitae) be poured over them, so much that it stand two or three fingers above; and then, the glass closed and well stopped, let all be set in a warm bath to digest for one week, and each day let the glass be carefully taken out and turned round two or three times with the matter within, and moved a little this way and that, so that the essences of the ingredients may be the better extracted and united to the juniper spirit. This done, let all the cooled matter, with the liquor poured over it, be first strongly pressed out through a linen cloth, then strained through blotting-paper and left to settle, that it come out purer; afterward let this whole liquor, holding the essence of the ingredients, be distilled in a moderately warm bath through an alembic, until a matter like liquid honey remain in the bottom; to which must be added about as much of the best spirit of turpentine (which rises by a gentle heat in the bath); with which that extract is again digested in a warm bath, the glass well closed, for one week; and afterward filtered once more through blotting-paper, with a little of the juniper liquor distilled before added if need be, that the filtration finish more readily. This liquor, however, must thereafter be drawn off by the slowest heat of the bath, lest by its fiery and sharp force it harm the medicine. Of this medicine, diligently prepared in the said manner, let three or four drops in wine be given to the patient each morning, and likewise after midday before supper, whenever and as long as need shall be. And of the juniper liquor that rises through the alembic he may also take a little — about a quarter of a spoonful — in the evening before bed, as a further aqua vitae, if he can bear it; and let this be done rather in winter than in summer, and only twice or thrice a week in the evening, as said. If he carries these out duly and in the prescribed manner, he will, God favouring, feel thence no slight relief of the disease. / FURTHER POSTSCRIPTS. That you in friendship admonish me of what a certain person of this Imperial court wrote to a certain principal nobleman among you, is welcome — although all he reported is, as you rightly conjecture, most false (which is also for the best). His Majesty has never yet spoken with me of such things, nor by letter or word made any mention of the Turks or of the Turkish war, much less that I — who do not meddle in others' affairs nor profess prophecies — should have brought forth any such thing; nay, what is more, I have rather always insinuated those things which can turn the mind away from melancholy cares, dejection, suspicions, idle superstitions, and the like that disturb a sound mind; and this I have done so sedulously that scarcely any other long of his intimate councils would have dared to do it so heartily and so readily — which the magnificent Lord Barvitius, the chief Secretary, almost always at the Emperor's side, knows very well. He has on that account several times thanked me heartily, and more than once shown that he has extolled this candour of mine before the other chief councillors — that I do not dissemble what is fitting and profitable, but sincerely (yet with most submissive discretion) lay it open to His Majesty and seasonably admonish him — which was most welcome to them; so that the matter stands far otherwise than certain ignorant men, dreaming I know not what by a sinister judgement, may suspect. Thus there were not wanting those who most vainly imputed to me the Emperor's so-long absence at Pilsen, and did not fear to spread it not only at Prague but in various places of Bohemia — though it never came into my mind before I heard it reported; and scarcely any man longed for His Majesty's presence more than I, since, while he was away, my affairs were the less advanced, however many had charge of executing them. Nor do I know anyone here who labours under hatred of me; for I have given no one occasion — rather, very many, indeed all the chief men, not only at court but elsewhere in this kingdom, of their own accord desire my friendship and conversation, and court it more than I could wish, so that I am forced to spend time and health oftener than I would. Nay, the more illustrious Magnates of the kingdom have offered me, of their own motion and unasked, that I be enrolled a native of the kingdom and turn nowhere else — which they have promised, with ready mind, to bring about at the first Diet. If among the common sort there lurk some peevish, envious, and malevolent fellows — of whom I know none — let them ensnare and gnaw themselves in their own malevolence and perversity of judgement, and keep these things to themselves and guard them in the grave: unhappy and wretched is he whom no one envies; nor does that virtue shine or stand conspicuous which of its own [worth] lacks the shadow of envy. You see therefore, my Rollenhagen, with how great injury they do not blush to affect not only my person but — far more — the Sacred Imperial Majesty itself, Head of the Empire and our most clement Lord, who is of present courage, neither dreads the Turks, but spurns Peace, unless honourable and useful, even when freely offered — while they write things so flimsily-patched and most alien from the truth. Were we together, I could show irrefragably, by many documents and proofs [Tecmiria], the empty talk of those men, and that the contrary is rather true. Meanwhile I should secretly wish to learn from you who it was that scattered such things in his letters, and to whom — not that I dwell on such trifles, or would cast it in his teeth (for many things must be dissembled and looked down on from on high), but only that I may guard against a man suspecting things so sinister and frivolous and malevolent, if perchance I know him or fall in with him. That you indicate the same to me in a few or even veiled words, as soon as you write again, I hold you asked, by our friendship. It shall be done without any danger to you; for I know that in such malicious cavillings one must act prudently and silently. Farewell. Given as above in the letter. / I add this too: that what you intimated in your earlier letter — of a persecution of those who profess the Evangelical doctrine in Austria, [done] by the Jesuits with the Emperor's consent — has been falsely reported to you. For all things in Austria, as to religion, are in their former state, nor does the Emperor permit the Jesuits or others who call themselves Catholics to stir up disturbances, nor hate or ill-treat anyone for diversity of religion, since he even tolerates many in his own Court who dissent from the Papists. But in Styria a great affliction of the Evangelicals has arisen, the Jesuits, by the Prince's authority, bearing with no one there who does not agree with them — which, however, happens against the Emperor's will and counsel. There are those who presage that the oncoming Turk will break off those controversies and persecutions; for, Kanizsa being taken (which God avert!), no fortified bulwark remains before he could invade Styria. But of these things He will provide, in whose hand all things are.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Georg Rollenhagen, the Magdeburg rector and Tycho's transport-agent there (cf. brahe-t_16000405001).
  • Significance — the Magdeburg goods arrived, the Prague-observatory search, and the legal endgame of the Ursus affair.
  • The goods arrive. Tycho's instruments and effects, long stuck at Magdeburg, have reached Leitmeritz (Litoměřice) at the cathedral Chapter's expense (cf. the whole Magdeburg-transport thread, brahe-t_16000405001, brahe-t_16000000002); thanks to the Chapter and Rollenhagen.
  • The search for a permanent observatory (key topographical document). The Emperor, having seen the six larger + some smaller instruments installed in the garden house "built by Ferdinand the grandfather" (the Royal Summer Palace / Belvedere, Ferdinand I), promised a better place than Benátky — inclining toward the Curtius house (to be bought from the widow; the eventual choice — cf. brahe-t_16010414001), but also floating the Star Villa (Hvězda) "built by Archduke Ferdinand in the form of a star, with a game-park" (Ferdinand II of Tyrol's villa), and even the Vienna Neugebäude built by Maximilian II. Tycho's one condition: permanence, "lest the same happen as in Denmark."
  • Sophie Brahe & Erik Lange. His sister Sophie at Segeberg near Lübeck, Erik Lange (her alchemist husband) of unknown whereabouts — "I grieve the fortune of both." His iron furnaces (alchemical) to be shipped via Petkum/Magdeburg before the Elbe freezes.
  • The servant Andreas. A candid character-sketch: Andreas (the man sent to fetch the goods) had absconded at Benátky, returned penniless, was passed to the Captain of Brandeis, and falsely claims unpaid wages; "most simple, not prudent, blurts anything" — forgiven for Rollenhagen's sake.
  • The Ursus legal endgame (the centerpiece). Ursus (Nicolaus Reimers Bär) died six weeks earlier (≈ mid-August 1600), "consumed in the French camps … or by asthma and phthisis" — "at the very hour the citation was to be served." Tycho recounts the deathbed visit by two lawyers and a notary asking Ursus to recant (he refused, submitting to the judges); the four imperial Commissioners (Barons Christopher von Schleinitz and Ernfried von Minckwitz + two juristic councillors); the action suspended by Ursus's death, awaiting a new decree; his proctor Johannes Fritzsch at Bautzen. Crucially, the suit is "real, not merely personal" — against the book, which "perpetually speaks in place of its author": the plan is a published refutation + an imperial decree annulling the book throughout the Empire, and the Archbishop of Prague ordered to punish the printer and burn all Prague copies ("given to Vulcan"). Had Ursus lived, the Commissioners would have had him beheaded or quartered under Bohemian law. Tycho's stated aim: not the man but abolishing the book and vindicating his (and Rothmann's) reputation — he will clear Rothmann of Ursus's slanders, and asks Rollenhagen for samples of Rothmann's hand to authenticate the letters. (Cf. the Ursus thread: brahe-t_16000911001 [Kepler's analysis], brahe-t_15990922001 [Ursus's flight], brahe-t_16000401001, brahe-t_16000408001.)

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VIII pp. 368–375; defects the audit noted: text error (6).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Collated the full letter (salutation through 'Vale', plus both Postscripta) against Dreyer TBOO VIII, read as page images. Checked every proper name (Rollenhagen, Andreas, Sophia, Erik Lange, Simon von Petkum, the two Ferdinands, Maximilian, Caspar Alemann, Ursus, Christoph von Schleunitz, Ernfried von Minckwitz, Johannes Fritzsch, Rothmann, Barvitius), place-name (Magdeburg, Leitmeritz, Benatky, Prague, Segeberg, Lubeck, Hamburg, Vienna, Bautzen/Budissinum, Lusatia, Styria, Austria, Kanizsa), date (26 September 1600 twice; 'six weeks'; 'eight days'), and recipe quantity (oz. I / oz. II groupings) against the page images. The main letter body (through the first 'Vale') and the second Postscript (Postscripta Altera, on the Turkish-war rumour and Styria) translate accurately with no fabrication, omission, mistranslation, or hedge loss found. The first Postscript (the medical recipe) is where the transcribed original itself is marked as 'restored... dropped by the working transcription' — and that restoration turns out to be imperfect: six small but genuine departures from Dreyer's print surfaced there, one of them (the bracketed '[tanti?]' guess for Dreyer's clear 'simul') materially changing the sense of a sentence. Per the grading rule, any text error caps the grade at C regardless of the translation's own accuracy.

6 defects found:
- text error (high). The the transcribed original's own bracketed guess '[tanti?]' for an unread word is wrong: Dreyer clearly prints 'simul' (together), not a word meaning 'of such worth/account' — the translation faithfully carries the guess forward, but the guess itself materially changes the sense of the sentence. Dreyer prints: Si fimul effemus, poßem multis documentis et Tecmirijs irrefragabiliter oftendere iftorum hominum vaniloquentiam (p.374) — i.e. 'Si simul essemus' = 'If we were together'.
- text error (medium). Dreyer prints 'deiectione' (dejection of mind); the transcribed original corrupts this to 'delectione' (not a standard word), and the translation renders the corrupted form as 'delusion', a concept matching neither the correct reading (dejection) nor the corrupted one. Dreyer prints: quæ a melancholicis curis, animi de-iectione, fufpicionibus et fuperftitionibus otiofis (p.374) — 'deiectione' = dejection/despondency.
- text error (low). Dreyer's recipe reads 'Feniculi' (fennel seed); the transcribed original corrupts the initial letter to 'Peniculi' (not a plausible ingredient). The English translation is unaffected — it correctly says 'fennel' regardless. Dreyer prints: Rcp. Semin. Feniculi / Petrofelini / Anifi / Saxifragiae / Cardamomi (p.372).
- text error (low). Dreyer's German gloss is 'hainbutten beeren' (rosehip berries); the transcribed original silently drops 'hainbutten', leaving only the generic 'beeren' (berries), which the translation then has to gloss speculatively as '[hips]'. Dreyer prints: Cynofbator. Germ. hainbutten beeren (p.372).
- text error (low). Dreyer prints 'isthic' (there); the transcribed original has the near-homograph 'istinc' (thence/from there) — a minor place-adverb substitution that does not change the English rendering ('there'). Dreyer prints: quæ ifthic non difficulter confieri poterit (p.372) — 'isthic' = 'there, in that place'.
- text error (low). Dreyer's 'Euangelicorum' (of the Evangelical people) is corrupted in the transcribed original to 'Euangeliorum' (of the Gospels); the translation is unaffected — it correctly says 'the Evangelicals' regardless of the corrupted source. Dreyer prints: magna fuborta eft Euangelicorum afflictio (p.375) — 'of the Evangelicals', not 'of the Gospels'.

Repaired. All six defects were verified directly against the Dreyer page images (TBOO VIII, pp. 372-375, supplied as the letter's extract PDF) before any change was made; each corrected reading matches what Dreyer prints exactly. One page citation in the audit was off by one page (the 'delectione/deiectione' defect: audit cited p.374, the word actually falls at the bottom of p.373) — this is noted in that outcome's dreyer_page field but did not affect the substance of the fix. No cruxes remain; nothing outside the six listed defect sites was touched.
