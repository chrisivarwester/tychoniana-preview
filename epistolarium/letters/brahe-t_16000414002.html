The original

EXCELLENTISSIMO ET ERUDITISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO RAPHAELI CIAPARENIO, APUD ILLUSTRISSIMUM ORATOREM FLORENTINUM PRAGAe DOMUS PRÆFECTO, DOMINO ET AMICO HONORANDO.

QUAS iussu Illmi et Magnifmi Oratoris Florentini ad me Excella Vra per certum tabellarium direxit literas, modo accepi, atque pro hoc mihi praestito officio plurimas eidem gratias habeo, relaturus, si quid unquam meâ opellâ praestari poterit, quod illi vicissim acceptum esse queat. Praedicavit mihi iam antea TENGNAGLIUS noster, qui ante mensem circiter in Patriam, Parentes suos invisurus, abijt, singularem Exae Vrae humanitatem et erga se benevolentiam, quam filio quoque meo, cum vobis adesset, praestitam, magni beneficij loco duco. Daboque operam, quantum in me est, ne se certasse priorem poeniteat, speroque nos, ubi Pragam advenero, ulterioris notitiae atque amicitiae fundamina coram posituros.

Interim et semper Exa Va quam optime valeat.
Dabantur properanter ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ Die 14 Aprilis Anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most excellent and most learned man, Master Raphael Ciaparenius, house-prefect [major-domo] with the Most Illustrious Florentine Ambassador at Prague, his lord and friend to be honoured.

The letters which, by command of the Most Illustrious and Magnificent Florentine Ambassador, Your Excellency directed to me by a sure courier, I have just received; and for this office rendered me I give him very many thanks, intending to repay it — if ever anything can be performed by my poor effort that may in turn be acceptable to him. Our Tengnagel — who left about a month ago for his homeland, to visit his parents — had already proclaimed to me Your Excellency's singular kindness and benevolence toward himself; which, shown also to my son when he was with you, I count in the place of a great benefit. And I shall give my effort, as much as is in me, that he may not regret having been the first to enter [this contest of courtesy]; and I hope that, when I come to Prague, we shall lay in person the foundations of further acquaintance and friendship.

Meanwhile, and always, may Your Excellency fare most well.
Given hastily from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 14th day of April, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Raphael Ciaparenius (Ciaperenius), domus praefectus — the house-prefect (major-domo / steward) of the Florentine Ambassador (orator) at Prague, i.e. the resident of the Grand Duke of Tuscany at the Imperial court. A courtesy letter of thanks; not a technical letter. (The printed address runs Oratorem FlorentinumPragae without a space — a typesetting artefact; read …Florentinum, Pragae, domus praefecto.)
  • Occasion. Ciaparenius had forwarded letters to Tycho at the ambassador's command; Tycho thanks him and looks forward to meeting at Prague. He notes that Frans Tengnagel (his assistant, see brahe-t_15990501001) had praised Ciaparenius's kindness, including a courtesy shown to Tycho's son (the younger Tycho) during a visit — Tengnagel having left "about a month ago" to visit his parents in his homeland.
  • Idiom. ne se certasse priorem poeniteat — "that he may not regret having been the first to compete," the courtesy topos of a "contest in good offices": Tycho will strive not to be outdone in kindness. meâ opellâ — "by my poor little effort" (modest diminutive of opera).
  • Calendar. ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ Die 14 Aprilis 1600 = from the Imperial castle of Benátky [nad Jizerou], 14 April 1600; Bohemia (imperial / Catholic) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Restored the dropped space in the address header 'FLORENTINUM PRAGAe' (was glued as one word, 'FLORENTINUMPRAGAe'), per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.302.
