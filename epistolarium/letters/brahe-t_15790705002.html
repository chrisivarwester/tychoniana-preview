The original

MAGNIFICO ET GENEROSO VIRO DOMINO CAROLO DANZÆO, REGIS GALLIARUM LEGATO ET CONSILIARIO PRUDENTISSIMO, AMICO SUO PERPETUO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO.

MAGNIFICE Domine DANZÆE, Amice semper colende, pollicitus es cum ultimo Haffniae simul essemus, te Huenenses Lares invisurum, quam primum intelligeres me huc reversum esse, quia vero interea temporis nunc e Selandia in Scaniam, nunc ex hac rursus in illam, partim Regis partim Amicorum negotijs et ex parte etiam proprijs impellentibus iactatus sum tanquam pila missus ab illis, ut noster de aulica senectute dixit PALINGENIUS, adeo ut ante hoc tempus non licuerit nisi aliquantulam requiem hîc figere. Peto itaque ut nunc promissis stare et cum Amicis quoscunque elegeris meas Penates utquamvis pro conditione temporis et loci tenues, tamen ab urbium solenni fastu animum quietis anhelantem nonnihil revocantes invisere non dedigneris. Si Dominus Quaestor Regius comitem se adiungere prae negotijs publicis, quibus occupatur, non detrectaverit, fecerit et is mihi rem gratam. Scripsi M. ANDREAe, quem puto una venturum, secumque M. LAURENTIUM nostrum adducturum. Tuum vero erit quoscunque voles unâ adducere.

Vale.
Datae Huenae 5. Iulii Anno 1579.
T.
Tycho Brahe.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and noble gentleman Master Charles de Dançay, ambassador and most prudent counsellor of the King of France, his perpetual friend, most worthy to be honoured.

Magnificent Master Dançay, ever to be esteemed friend, you promised, when we were last together at Copenhagen, that you would visit the household of Hven as soon as you should learn that I had returned here; but because in the meantime I have been tossed about — now from Zealand into Scania, now back again from the latter into the former — driven partly by the King's affairs, partly by my friends', and in part by my own, "like a ball flung" by them, as our Palingenius said about old age at court, so much so that until now I have not been able to fix more than a very little rest here. I ask, therefore, that you now stand by your promise, and that you not disdain to visit, together with whatever friends you may choose, my household gods — slender though they are, as befits the condition of the time and place, yet recalling somewhat a mind that pants for quiet from the solemn pomp of the cities. If the Lord Royal Treasurer should not decline to join you as a companion, despite the public affairs with which he is occupied, he will do me too a welcome service. I have written to Master Andreas, who I think will come along, and will bring with him our Master Laurentius. But it will be yours to bring along whomever you wish.

Farewell.
Given at Hven, 5 July, the year 1579.
T[ycho].
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Carolus Danzaeus = Charles de Dançay (d. 1589), the long-serving resident ambassador of the King of France (then Henri III) at the Danish court — regis Galliarum legato et consiliario — and an early friend and supporter of Tycho. The honorific style magnifico et generoso viro … amico suo perpetuo plurimum observando is rendered in full by the edition's convention
  • Author/place. Written by Tycho from Hven (Huena; the island granted him by Frederik II in 1576, seat of Uraniborg), inviting Dançay to come over and visit. Huenenses Lares / meas Penates — literally "the Lares of Hven" / "my Penates" (household gods); rendered "the household of Hven" / "my household gods," the domestic-deity idiom kept for the warmth it carries.
  • Places. Haffnia = Copenhagen; Selandia = Zealand; Scania = Scania (Skåne, then Danish) — Tycho's family seat Knudstrup lay in Scania, hence the shuttling "now from Zealand into Scania, now back again."
  • Palingenius. ut noster … dixit PALINGENIUS = "as our Palingenius said": Marcellus Palingenius Stellatus (Pier Angelo Manzolli, c. 1500–c. 1543), author of the widely-read didactic poem Zodiacus Vitae. de aulica senectute = "about old age at court" (a theme of the poem's courtly books); the simile iactatus … tanquam pila missus ab illis = "tossed about … like a ball flung [to and fro] by them" echoes Palingenius's image of the courtier as a plaything. The quotation-marks in the translation flag pila missus as the borrowed phrase; "by them" (ab illis) refers back to the King's, friends', and his own affairs.
  • Dominus Quaestor Regius — "the Lord Royal Treasurer" (the Danish rentemester / royal quaestor); a specific officer Tycho hopes will accompany Dançay, here left by his office rather than named.
  • M. Andreae … M. Laurentium nostrum. Two academic Magistri ("Master,") Tycho has invited. M. Andreae (dative of Andreas) is most plausibly Anders Sørensen Vedel (Andreas Severini Vellejus), Tycho's close friend and correspondent, though the bare praenomen does not put it beyond doubt; M. Laurentium nostrum ("our Master Laurentius") is a Lauritz/Laurentius of Tycho's circle, not further specified in the text. Identification crux: the letter gives only the forenames, so neither Master can be fixed with certainty from this text alone; flagged rather than asserted.
  • Orthography (humanist Latin). negotijs, proprijs, Penates … anhelantem: the -ijs spellings are the printed/scribal -iis; hîc and unâ carry the source's circumflexes. DANZAeO / DANZAeE: the Ae ligature of Danzaeus reproduced as printed.
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Huenae 5. Iulii Anno 1579 = "Given at Hven, 5 July 1579" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline matches the catalogue label (1579-07-05, Hven); no discrepancy. The subscription T. / Tycho Brahe is rendered "T[ycho]. / Tycho Brahe," the abbreviated first line bracketed.
  • Correction. Restored the dropped pronoun 'is' in 'fecerit et is mihi rem gratam', per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.56; no change to the English, which already supplied the implicit 'he'.
