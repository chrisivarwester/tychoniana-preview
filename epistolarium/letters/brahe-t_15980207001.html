The original

AD REGEM DANIÆ.

SERENISSIME et potentissime rex, domine clementissime. Regiæ tuæ Majestati in hoc novo conjugio adeoque toto regimine optima quæque et faustissima ex animo precor et voveo ac gratias, quas possum, maximas pro beneficiis a laudatissimæ memoriæ parente Friderico mihi præstitis quamplurimis submisse ago, quodque inde ab eo anno, quo Regia Tua Majestas in lucem hanc prodiit, divina illa scientia astronomica in Dania, patria mea dulcissima, liberaliter sustentata sit. Et sane non minora de Regia Tua Majestate mihi indubie pollicebar et citra frustraneam spem expertus fuissem, ni quorundam insatiabili mei odio laborantium (uti non exiguis indiciis reor) malevolus in me animus obstitisset, quos tamen nullo pacto læsi unquam, imo cuidam, quem horum latentem et inveteratum machinatorem existimo non parum profui. Quamobrem statim a Regiæ Tuæ Ma:tis coronatione mutationum incidere occasiones, uti sunt rerum humanarum subinde quædam vicissitudines. Idque forte fataliter evenit, ipsis jam astris artis suæ excercitium alio dirigentibus et destinantibus, cum et tertius annorum climactericus, per se alterationibus obnoxius, a prima Uraniburgi isthic fundatione ageretur. Præterea Regiæ Tuæ Majestati humiliter et enixe gratias habeo, quod, siquidem ita necessitas postularet, ut me cum iis, quibus tantopere invigilo, exercitiis astronomicis in vicinam Germaniam conferrem, nec iter impedire, nec retinere, nec etiam, cum Rostochii per anni quadrantem in propinquo manerem, aut postea, dum in extremis Holsatiæ finibus moror, revocare voluerit, quo mea studia non retardarentur, et astronomiam universaliter, ut toti orbi noster undiquaque inserviret labor et acceptum sit condendi propositum. Quo sane nomine eo magis Regiæ Tuæ Majestati me obstrictum video. Nam licet hoc maximo meo factum fuisset incommodo, et majore fortassis aliorum, qui nostra undiquaque avide expetunt et nequaquam contemptui habent, ob amorem tamen in Patriam et honorem regi meo serenissimo præstandum nolens volens obtemperare atque particularia universalibus præponere coactus fuissem, cum minori meo et aliorum emolumento. Quapropter etiam, cum Rostochii ea de caussa tam diu non sine periculo ob luem epidemicam isthic grassantem neque sine magnis expensis ob familiæ copiam et annonæ caritatem subsisterem, ad Regiam Tuam Maiestatem submisse literas dedi et abitus mei caussas nonnihil, quantum licuit, attigi, meamque operam Regiæ Tuæ Majestati et patriæ submisso animo sinceraque intentione obtuli, in omnibus, quæ in ipsius et patriæ honorem per me unquam præstari possent, modo tolerabilibus conditionibus id fieret, et indemnitati meæ competenter consuleretur. Quæ literæ, quod non eo, quo a me scriptæ, candido nimirum et in patriam studioso affectu animoque in serenissimum regem meum submisse obsequioso, acceptæ sint aliorum, uti existimo, eas sinistre admodum interpretantium malevolentia admissum est, sive quod aliquo clanculario mei laborarent odio (cujus tamen me iis caussas dedisse non sum conscius) sive quod suo sic abundare sensu vellent. Quod equidem nec iis invideo, nec ægre fero. Regiam vero Ma:tem Tuam, horum neutiquam consciam, lubenter in his excusatam habeo. Verum sit hoc quicquid velit, quod veritas, temporis filia, aliquando prodet meamque integritatem tuebitur, tamen de patria mea (uti par est) honorifice sentire, et Regiæ Majestatis Tuæ honori, quantum in me est, submisse studere ejusque virtutes deprædicare non desinam. Utque id aliqua nunc ex parte declarem atque Majestati Tuæ pro beneficiis clementer præstitis, qua possum, submissione me gratum ostendam, duos eidem libros, in ipsius regno primum natos, hic vero in Germaniæ vicinæ finibus nuper completos offero et exhibeo. Regiam etiam Tuam Majestatem, cum et aliis regibus et principibus viris eadem mittam, præterire minime decuit aut licuit. Submisse itaque et humiliter rogo, Regia Tua Majestas hoc regio et clementi animo acceptare non dedignetur. Plura et his majora de rebus astronomicis, annuente astrorum opifice, subjuncturus sum, quorum maiorem partem itidem in Regiæ Tuæ Majestatis regno, patria mea charissima, pluribus jam annis a me elaboratam, reliquam vero hic per otium hybernum ulterius confectam atque absolutam, in Regiæ tuæ Majestatis et regni honorem, favente eodem numine, propediem publicare decrevi et Regiæ Tuæ Majestati, modo id ei non ingratum intellexero, demisse offerre. Interim et semper, ut Regiæ Tuæ Majestati ego meaque omnia quam commendatissima sint, qua possum et decet submissione precor.

Deus opt: max: Regiam Tuam Ma:tem in regnorum et ditionum gubernandorum utilitatem et commodum cum ser:ma et optima conjuge quam diutissime florentem et incolumem conservet.

Datæ ex arce Ranzoviana Wandesburgk, prope Hamburgum sita, die 7. februarii anni 1598.

TO THE KING OF DENMARK.

Most serene and most mighty king, most gracious lord. To Your Royal Majesty I pray and vow from my heart all that is best and most auspicious in this new marriage, and indeed in your whole reign; and I humbly render the greatest thanks I can for the very many benefits bestowed on me by your parent Frederick of most laudable memory, and because, ever since the year in which Your Royal Majesty came forth into this light, that divine science of astronomy has been liberally maintained in Denmark, my sweetest homeland. And indeed I was assuredly promising myself no lesser things from Your Royal Majesty, and should have experienced them without disappointment of that hope, had not the malevolent disposition of certain men toward me — men labouring under an insatiable hatred of me (as I judge from no slight indications) — stood in my way; men whom I nevertheless never in any way injured, nay, to a certain one of them, whom I reckon the hidden and inveterate contriver of these things, I did no small service. For which reason, immediately after Your Royal Majesty's coronation, occasions of changes began to arise, as there are from time to time certain vicissitudes in human affairs. And this perhaps befell by fate, the very stars now directing and ordaining the exercise of their art elsewhere, since the third climacteric year — of itself liable to alterations — was being reckoned from the first founding of Uraniborg yonder. Furthermore, I humbly and earnestly give thanks to Your Royal Majesty, that — since necessity so demanded that I betake myself, together with those astronomical pursuits over which I keep so watchful a vigil, into neighbouring Germany — you were unwilling either to impede my journey, or to detain me, or even, when I was staying nearby at Rostock for a quarter of a year, or afterwards while I tarry in the farthest bounds of Holstein, to recall me, so that my studies might not be retarded, and that our labour might everywhere serve astronomy universally [and] the whole world, and the purpose of establishing it might be welcome. On which account, to be sure, I see myself the more bound to Your Royal Majesty. For although this had been done to my very great inconvenience, and perhaps to the greater [inconvenience] of others, who eagerly seek out our works on every side and by no means hold them in contempt, yet out of love toward my Homeland and the honour to be rendered to my most serene king I should have been compelled, willing or unwilling, to comply, and to set particular [interests] before universal ones, to the lesser profit of myself and of others. Wherefore also, since for that cause I was staying at Rostock so long, not without danger from the epidemic plague raging there, nor without great expenses on account of the size of my household and the dearness of provisions, I humbly addressed a letter to Your Royal Majesty and touched somewhat, so far as was permitted, upon the reasons for my departure, and offered my service to Your Royal Majesty and to my homeland, with a humble disposition and sincere intention, in all things that could ever be performed by me toward the honour of [Your Majesty] and of the homeland — provided it were done on tolerable conditions, and provided my own being-kept-harmless were duly looked to. That this letter was not received in the spirit in which it was written by me — namely with that frank affection, zealous toward my homeland, and with a mind humbly deferential toward my most serene king — came about, as I think, through the malevolence of others who interpret it altogether perversely, whether because they laboured under some clandestine hatred of me (for which, however, I am not conscious of having given them cause), or because they wished thus to overflow with their own opinion. Which, for my part, I neither begrudge them nor take amiss. But Your Royal Majesty, in no wise privy to these things, I gladly hold excused in this matter. Yet be this whatever it will — for that the truth, the daughter of time, will one day bring it forth and will protect my integrity — nonetheless I shall not cease to think honourably of my homeland (as is fitting), and, so far as in me lies, humbly to study Your Royal Majesty's honour and to proclaim your virtues. And that I may now in some part declare this, and show myself grateful to Your Majesty, with what humble submission I can, for the benefits graciously bestowed, I offer and present to the same two books, first born in [Your Majesty's] own kingdom, but here lately completed within the bounds of neighbouring Germany. Nor was it at all seemly or permissible to pass over Your Royal Majesty, since I am sending the same [books] to other kings and princely men as well. Humbly therefore and submissively I ask that Your Royal Majesty may not disdain to accept this with a royal and gracious disposition. More things, and greater than these, concerning astronomical matters I shall append, the Maker of the stars granting it; the greater part of which, likewise elaborated by me over many years now within Your Royal Majesty's kingdom, my dearest homeland, and the remainder further wrought and completed here during the winter's leisure, I have resolved — the same divine power favouring — shortly to publish, to the honour of Your Royal Majesty and the kingdom, and humbly to offer to Your Royal Majesty, provided I understand it to be not unwelcome to him. In the meantime, and always, I pray with what submission I can and as is fitting, that I and all that is mine may be most heartily commended to Your Royal Majesty.

May God, Best and Greatest, preserve Your Royal Majesty, to the profit and advantage of the kingdoms and dominions to be governed, flourishing and unharmed together with your most serene and most excellent consort for the very longest time.

Given from the Rantzau castle Wandsburg, situated near Hamburg, on the 7th day of February in the year 1598.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from his self-imposed exile after leaving Hven. The letter is a draft (Kladde) preserved in the Basel autograph collection.
  • Recipient. Regem Daniae = Christian IV of Denmark (1577–1648, r. from 1588, crowned 1596). The opening congratulations on the king's "new marriage" refer to his marriage to Anne Catherine of Brandenburg (concluded late 1597). The phrase inde ab eo anno, quo Regia Tua Majestas in lucem hanc prodiit ("ever since the year in which Your Royal Majesty came forth into this light," i.e. 1577) links the king's birth-year to the founding period of Uraniborg (Hven granted 1576, building begun 1576/77).
  • Occasion & the "two books." Tycho writes from Wandsbek, where he was a guest of Heinrich Rantzau, after leaving Denmark (he had wintered at Rostock, then moved into Holstein). The duos libros … in ipsius regno primum natos, hic vero in Germaniae vicinae finibus nuper completos ("two books, first born in [your] kingdom but lately completed here in the bounds of neighbouring Germany") is the presentation of the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (Wandsbek, 1598) together with the companion volume Tycho was issuing at the same time. Identification flagged: the text names neither book; the standard pairing of the two Wandsbek presentation volumes is the Mechanica and the printed star-catalogue (the thousand-star catalogue, circulated as a manuscript/printed companion to the Progymnasmata). The translation keeps the neutral "two books"; the specific titles are supplied here as context, not asserted from the letter.
  • Place. arce Ranzoviana Wandesburgk, prope Hamburgum = the Rantzau castle at Wandsbek (modern Wandsbek, a district of Hamburg), the seat of Heinrich Rantzau, Statthalter of the Danish king in Holstein; rendered "Wandsburg" in the header to match the as-written Wandesburgk. Holsatia = Holstein; Rostochium = Rostock.
  • Astronomy / programme. astronomiam universaliter … condendi propositum — Tycho's lifelong programme of establishing/restoring astronomy (cf. astronomia instaurata,, restitutio / instauratio); toti orbi … noster … labor — "our labour [serving] the whole world." astrorum opifice / eodem numine — "the Maker of the stars" / "the same divine power," reverent periphrases kept.
  • Astrology. tertius annorum climactericus … a prima Uraniburgi … fundatione = "the third climacteric year reckoned from the first founding of Uraniborg" (a climacteric at a multiple of seven; Uraniborg founded 1576 → the 21st year, 1597, falls in this window). ipsis jam astris artis suae excercitium alio dirigentibus et destinantibus — "the very stars now directing and ordaining the exercise of their art elsewhere": Tycho frames his departure in astrological-fatalist terms while attributing the proximate cause to court enemies. The hedge forte fataliter ("perhaps by fate") is preserved.
  • Polemical hedges (preserved, not sharpened). Tycho repeatedly veils his accusations: quorundam insatiabili mei odio laborantium (uti non exiguis indiciis reor) — "certain men labouring under an insatiable hatred of me (as I judge from no slight indications)"; cuidam, quem … latentem et inveteratum machinatorem existimo — "a certain one whom I reckon the hidden and inveterate contriver." The unnamed "contriver" is left unnamed in the translation, as in the source. The qualifier quantum licuit ("so far as was permitted") and the conditional modo tolerabilibus conditionibus … et indemnitati meae competenter consuleretur ("provided on tolerable conditions … and provided my keeping-harmless were duly looked to") are kept with their hedged force.
  • Negation count. The clause listing the king's forbearance contains a coordinated triple negation governed by one voluerit: nec iter impedire, nec retinere, nec etiam … revocare voluerit — "you were unwilling neither to impede my journey, nor to detain me, nor even … to recall me." Rendered as three negated infinitives under "you were unwilling," preserving the king's non-interference (the king did not obstruct Tycho's departure). No negation added or dropped.
  • veritas, temporis filia — "the truth, the daughter of time" (Veritas filia Temporis), the classical-Renaissance commonplace (Gellius via Erasmus' Adagia); kept as the sententious aside it is.
  • Sign-off / titulature. Deus opt: max: = Deus Optimus Maximus, "God, Best and Greatest"; ser:ma et optima conjuge = "most serene and most excellent consort" (Anne Catherine). The dense honorific register (Regia Tua Majestas, submisse, humiliter, demisse, clementer) is kept throughout as Tycho's deferential address to his sovereign.
  • Calendar. die 7. februarii anni 1598 = 7 February 1598. Denmark was on the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1598; the date is so marked. The dateline agrees with the catalogue label ("1598. 7. februar. Wandsburg"); no discrepancy. Date not converted.
  • Orthography / abbreviations. Ma:tis / Ma:tem = Majestatis / Majestatem; ser:ma = serenissima; opt: max: = Optimus Maximus; caussas / caussa = causas / causa (period spelling); excercitium = exercitium; charissima = carissima; hybernum = hibernum. Forms kept as printed in the Source block.
