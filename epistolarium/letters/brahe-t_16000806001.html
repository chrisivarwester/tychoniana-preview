The original

NOBILISSIMO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, MATHEMATICO NOSTRO SÆCULO PRAESTANTISSIMO, DOMINO AC FAUTORI SUO UNICE COLENDO.
SALUTEM IN CHRISTO IESU

NOBILISSIME Domine TYCHO, remunerationem quinque scilicet Ioachimicos, propter diarium Solis et Lunæ, in Martio huius anni 1600 nobilitati tuæ transmissum, a M. CHRISTIANO LONGIMONTANO accepi, Quare T. N. ut par est, gratias ago et habeo. Quod in superioribus meis literis, num in oras Bohemicas me vellem conferre nec ne, nihil habetur, causa fuit non mea in scribendo negligentia, vel oblivio, sed quod 14 diebus, antequam N. T. sententia de re illa huc allata esset, istæ meæ literæ fuerunt scriptæ et obsignatæ. Ideoque M. MELCHIOREM IOSTELIUM rogavi, ut tuæ nobilitati, mecum prius deliberandum, scriberet. In tali autem deliberatione reperi, consultius fore, si Witebergæ permanens tuæ N. in rebus astronomicis pro animi mei tenuitate inservirem, singulisque diebus aliquot certas horas, utpote tres vel quatuor, in laborem istum impenderem. Tuæ nobilitati si hoc meum propositum placuerit, certum stipendium ex æquo et bono mihi determinare, et quid expectandum, data aliqua occasione, haud gravatim significare poterit.

Vale.
Dabantur Witebergæ 6 Augusti veteri stylo Anni 1600 Christi.
T. N.
humiliter colens
M . IOHANNES OSWALDUS,
Zeillensis Francus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble Master Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup, the most outstanding Mathematician of our age, his lord and patron uniquely to be honoured.
Greeting in Christ Jesus.

Most noble Master Tycho, I have received from Master Christian Longomontanus the remuneration — namely five Joachims [Joachimsthaler] — for the diary [ephemeris] of the Sun and Moon transmitted to Your Nobility in March of this year 1600. Wherefore I give and render thanks to Your Nobility, as is fitting. That in my earlier letter nothing is contained as to whether I would betake myself to the Bohemian regions or not — the cause was not negligence or forgetfulness on my part in writing, but that these my letters were written and sealed fourteen days before Your Nobility's opinion on that matter was brought hither. And therefore I asked Master Melchior Jöstel to write to Your Nobility that [the matter] must first be deliberated with me. But in such deliberation I have found that it would be more advisable if, remaining at Wittenberg, I were to serve Your Nobility in astronomical matters according to the slenderness of my mind, and to spend, each day, some certain hours — say three or four — upon that labour. If this my proposal shall please Your Nobility, you will be able to determine for me a certain stipend, in fairness and goodness, and to signify — when some occasion is given — what is to be expected, [and that] not grudgingly.

Farewell.
Given at Wittenberg, 6 August, old style, in the year of Christ 1600.
Humbly honouring Your Nobility,
Master Johannes Oswaldus,
a Franconian of Zeil.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johannes Oswaldus (M. Johannes Oswaldus, Zeillensis Francus — "a Franconian of Zeil" [am Main]), a Wittenberg-based mathematical computer offering himself to Tycho. He acknowledges payment of five Joachimsthaler (Ioachimici — the silver coins of Joachimsthal), conveyed through Longomontanus, for a diarium Solis et Lunae (a computed Sun-and-Moon ephemeris/"diary") sent to Tycho in March 1600.
  • The proposal. Rather than move "to the Bohemian regions," Oswaldus proposes to remain at Wittenberg and serve Tycho remotely in astronomical computation, "three or four hours each day," for a fixed stipend — and asks Tycho to set the terms. A glimpse of Tycho's distributed network of paid calculators.
  • M. Melchior Iostelius = Melchior Jöstel (1559–1611), professor of mathematics (and later medicine) at Wittenberg, Tycho's contact there, who had relayed the negotiation.
  • Calendar. Witebergae 6 Augusti veteri stylo … 1600 = 6 August 1600, Old Style (veteri stylo explicit; Lutheran Wittenberg kept the Julian calendar). Header agrees with the label. (Note: dated two days after Tycho's testimonial for Longomontanus, brahe-t_16000804001, which is N.S. at Prague — the same Longomontanus is the link between the two.)
