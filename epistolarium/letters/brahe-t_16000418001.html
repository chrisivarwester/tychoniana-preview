The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

MITTO, Vir nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile princeps, ut libri, quos petijsti, ad te transferantur, his iuncta

IOANNIS VIRDUNGI tabulas resolutas de supputandis motibus syderum,

Tabulas Prutenicas ERASMI RHEINHOLDI,

COPERNICUM de triangulis et Algorithmum demonstratum No. 76.

Habemus exemplar græce manuscriptum HIPPARCHI Bithyni in ARATI et EUDOXI Phænomena librorum 3, cum Asterismis (uti inscribitur in Codice Florentiæ Ao 1567 impresso. Nec enim Asterismi illi sunt HIPPARCHI, nec forte reliqua) et alterum exemplar Magnæ Syntaxeos CL. PTOLEMÆI, literâ pervetustâ et eleganti conscriptum. Quod si tibi dubiæ lectiones occurrant, lubens admodum et sedulo conferam et ad te perscribam. Nec inpræsentiarum occurrunt iam alia, sed hisce tibi officia mea promptissime offero.

Datæ (18 Apr. circiter) A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

I send, most noble man and easily prince of all mathematicians — so that the books you sought may be conveyed to you — joined herewith:

the Resolved Tables of Johann Virdung, on computing the motions of the stars;

the Prutenic Tables of Erasmus Reinhold;

Copernicus On Triangles, and the Algorithm Demonstrated, No. 76.

We have a Greek manuscript exemplar of Hipparchus of Bithynia, on the Phaenomena of Aratus and Eudoxus, in 3 books, with the Asterismi (as it is inscribed in the codex printed at Florence in the year 1567 — for those Asterismi are not Hipparchus's, nor perhaps the rest); and another exemplar of the Great Syntaxis of Claudius Ptolemy, written in a very ancient and elegant hand. And if doubtful readings should occur to you, I will most gladly and diligently collate them and write them out to you. Nor at present do other [matters] occur; but with these I most readily offer you my services.

Given (about 18 April), in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (see the Munich series), forwarding a further consignment from Tycho's desiderata and offering to collate two important Greek manuscripts in his keeping.
  • Printed books sent. Ioannis Virdungi tabulae resolutae = the Tabulae resolutae (for computing planetary motions) of Johann Virdung of Hassfurt. Tabulae Prutenicae Erasmi Rheinholdi = the Prutenic Tables of Erasmus Reinhold (1551), the Copernican-based tables. Copernicus de triangulis = Copernicus, De lateribus et angulis triangulorum (the trigonometric tract, Wittenberg 1542), here bound with an Algorithmus demonstratus (No. 76).
  • Greek manuscripts offered. Hipparchi Bithyni in Arati et Eudoxi Phaenomena libri 3 = Hipparchus of Nicaea (Bithynia), Commentary on the Phaenomena of Aratus and Eudoxus — the only surviving work of Hipparchus, in three books, with an appended Asterismi (star-list); Herwart notes, shrewdly, that the Asterismi "are not Hipparchus's, nor perhaps the rest [of the appended matter]," citing the Florence 1567 printed edition's own rubric. The second is "another exemplar of the Great Syntaxis [the Almagest] of Ptolemy," in a very old and elegant Greek hand. Herwart offers to collate dubiae lectiones (doubtful readings) for Tycho — a scholarly textual-criticism service.
  • Magna Syntaxis = Ptolemy's Almagest (Gk Μεγάλη Σύνταξις).
  • Calendar. Munich (Catholic Bavaria) = New Style. The dateline is itself approximate in the source — Datae (18 Apr. circiter) "given about 18 April"; the header therefore carries " c. 18 April 1600," matching the edition's "(18 Apr. circiter)."
