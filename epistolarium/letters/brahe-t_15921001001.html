The original

PETRO IUNIO TYCHO BRAHE. S.

QUAMVIS, nobilissime et amplissime vir, ultimis meis de neglecta ad priores aliquot responsione familiari quadam libertate tecum expostulantibus, nihil adhuc rescripseris: tamen et has illis aggregare non supersedebo, utut diutinam hanc tuam sive oblivionem sive spontaneam forte scribendi intermissionem vix (amicitia nostra sic exigente) excusatam habeam. Eroque ob id nunc brevior: praesertim cum superiora tibi adhuc satis in praesenti esse memoria (in quibus etiamnum tuam operam obnixe desidero) mihi persuadeam. Neque enim meas tibi non relatas diffido: ex quo a magnifico D. IO. METELLANO, Cancellario vestro, responsum acceperim, cui eodem tempore literas tuis simul coniunctas destinâram. Cum vero in superioribus praeter alia ut a Serenissimo Rege carmen quoddam in mearum lucubrationum Astronomicarum commendationem impetrares, peramanter te rogassem: et nunc ad ipsius Maiestatem ipsemet hac de causa elegiacam hanc qualemcunque epistolam exarârim (quam tibi ipsius Maiestati meo nomine submisse offerendam unâ mitto) idcirco etiamnum a te idipsum quibus possum precibus rogo, ut tua intercessione, eaque, qua apud ipsam Maiestatem polles, authoritate et consilij grata (quod et mereris) acceptatione, quo id clementissimi beneficij mihi impertiatur, quantum in te est, elaborare non degraveris. De privilegio ab ipsius Maiestate obtinendo, cuius apud te antea satis superque mentionem feci, nunc nihil superaddam. Haec igitur singula, et si quae praeterea sunt, alias significata, tuae erga me benevolentiae et amicitiae nostrae fidelitati fideliter etiam exequenda committo.

Vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 1 Oct. A. 1592.
Tuae Magnificentiae studiosissimus
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Peter Young. Tycho Brahe sends greeting.

Although, most noble and worthy sir, you have as yet sent no reply to my last letter, in which with a certain familiar freedom I remonstrated with you over your neglect to answer some earlier ones, I shall nonetheless not refrain from adding these too to those — even though I can scarcely hold this long silence of yours, be it forgetfulness or perhaps a deliberate intermission of writing, to be excused (our friendship requiring as much). And for that reason I shall now be the briefer, especially since I persuade myself that those earlier matters are still sufficiently present to your memory (in which matters I still earnestly desire your good offices). For indeed I do not distrust that my [letters] have [in fact] reached you: seeing that I have received an answer from the worshipful Master Jo. Maitland, your Chancellor, to whom at the same time I had addressed a letter joined together with yours. But since in those earlier letters I had, among other things, very affectionately asked you to obtain from the Most Serene King some poem in commendation of my astronomical labours, and since now, for this very reason, I have myself drawn up this elegiac epistle, such as it is, to His Majesty in person (which I send herewith, to be humbly presented to His Majesty in my name by you), I therefore ask the very same thing of you yet again, with what entreaties I can: that by your intercession — and by that authority in which you are strong with His Majesty, and by his gracious acceptance of the counsel (as indeed you deserve) — you will not think it a burden to labour, so far as in you lies, to the end that this most clement favour be bestowed on me. Of the privilege to be obtained from His Majesty, of which I have already made mention to you more than enough, I shall now add nothing further. These several matters, then, and any others besides that have been indicated elsewhere, I commit to be faithfully carried out by your goodwill toward me and by the fidelity of our friendship.

Farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 1 October, the year 1592.
Most devoted to Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender & recipient / address line. PETRO IUNIO · TYCHO BRAHE. S. — the source prints the two elements run together (PETRO IUNIOTYCHO); read as Petro Iunio. Tycho Brahe S[alutem]. = "To Peter Young, Tycho Brahe (sends) greeting". Peter Young (Petrus Iunius, 1544–1628), Scottish humanist, royal tutor (paedagogus) to the young James VI and one of the King's most trusted advisers and book-agents; he had visited Hven and was Tycho's standing channel to the Scottish court.
  • Recipient's honorific. nobilissime et amplissime vir → "most noble and worthy sir"; the subscription Tuae Magnificentiae studiosissimus → "Most devoted to Your Magnificence" (the Magnificentia register carried over from's studiosiss. formula).
  • IO. METELLANO, Cancellario vestro — John Maitland of Thirlestane (c. 1543–1595), Lord Chancellor of Scotland; Metellanus is the latinised form of Maitland. vestro ("your," plural) = the Chancellor of your [Scottish] realm. Tycho elsewhere corresponded with Maitland directly (letter of 16 May 1592, Lethington).
  • Serenissimo Rege / ipsius Maiestas — James VI of Scotland (1566–1625; later James I of England), Young's pupil and sovereign, who had visited Uraniborg in 1590. Not named in the letter; identified here on first occurrence. The carmen … in commendationem is the commendatory poem Tycho sought from the King for his astronomical publications.
  • elegiacam hanc qualemcunque epistolam exarârim — "I have drawn up this elegiac epistle, such as it is": Tycho's own verse-letter to James VI, composed in elegiac couplets, enclosed for Young to present. qualemcunque ("such as it is") is Tycho's self-deprecating hedge, preserved. (exarârim = syncopated exaraverim; destinâram = destinaveram.)
  • lucubrationes Astronomicae → "astronomical labours / lucubrations" (literally night-work by lamplight); here Tycho's published astronomical writings, for which the royal commendation is sought.
  • De privilegio ab ipsius Maiestate obtinendo → "the privilege to be obtained from His Majesty": a printing/publishing privilege (the standard early-modern grant of exclusive right against piracy), which Tycho wished the King to grant; he declines to repeat the particulars here.
  • Latin idiom / syntax. meas tibi non relatas diffido carries a double negative whose force is "I do not distrust that my [letters] have been delivered to you" — i.e. Tycho is confident they arrived; the two negations (non … diffido) are reproduced as a litotes of confidence, not collapsed. non supersedebo → "I shall not refrain"; non degraveris → "you will not think it a burden"; each negation is rendered one-for-one.
  • Dateline. Datae Uraniburgi 1 Oct. A. 1592 = "Given at Uraniborg (on Hven), 1 October, the year 1592" (O.S.; Denmark kept Old Style). The dateline date agrees with the catalogue label (1592. 1. oktober. Uraniborg); no discrepancy.
  • MS witness. A = Oxford, Bodleian Library, Smith 77 (an Afskrift / copy). Edition of record per the edition: Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (Dreyer VII).
  • Correction. Latin heading 'IUNIOTYCHO' corrected to 'IUNIO TYCHO' (missing space) per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.344.
