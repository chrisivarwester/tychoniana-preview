[To Tycho Brahe.]

My quite friendly greeting, now and always sent with Our Lord. Dear Tyge Brahe and dear brother, I thank you quite friendly and gladly for all the good you have done and shown me — which to requite, you shall always find me willing, in whatever I can be to your honour, will, and service.

Dear brother, your letter reached me here in Copenhagen by your son Tyge; and among other things you write me, you let me know the good situation you have come into with the Roman Imperial Majesty — which is very dear to me (as to your other good friends who have now heard it here at this Herredag [assembly of nobles]); God give you further luck. Concerning the 50 thaler you write I should let M. Christen Lenvig[?] [Longomontanus?] have — your son Tyge will tell you well about it; for I perceive he travels with them [the instruments], else he would gladly have got them, even had more been at stake. Concerning your instruments, I have told your son my opinion how I think they could best be got out — but first from here to Lübeck. I cannot tarry here in the town so long that Tyge finishes packing both the instruments and his other business, for I am under certain royal commands, [for] which I now set out this very day, [to go] thither where they are to be carried out.

I perceive that the 700 thaler I sent out to you some time ago had not reached you when your son left you; yet I perceive from Tyge that on this journey he has got some account of them. I have not yet satisfied Lady Sidsel Urne, for I still cannot agree with her: she wants the interest with the principal; I will yet offer her the principal; if she will not accept it, then it is to be feared she will have you sued for the principal and interest. And as you write that I should take a summons against her to the Herredag — you well know it is not worth it, that in such cases one [cannot] summon to the Herredag before the case is pursued at herredsting and landsting [district + provincial courts], and perhaps it would reach as far as the realm's Chancellor, if one were to sue her by Lady Johan Oxe's letter; and you can well think yourself, if the case went against you, what damage and trouble it would give you. And that you may the better learn that Lady Johan Oxe's letter cannot count for much, I send you a copy of a herredsting verdict in which the … contract is entered, so that you can well learn the whole situation thence. I have also let your son see a guarantee-letter you have signed with your own hand, in which you bind yourself to pay the principal sum with whatever due interest may run from it. From your letter I also learn that you would come in [to Denmark] for the Herredag, [if it] should come now first; but you may well believe that this summer no Herredag will be held, since this one has now stood so late in the year. Further I have spoken about this with your son Tyge, as he can report to you by word of mouth.

As concerns the 300 thaler interest-money to be sent out to Land-Holstein, it shall be sent thither, whether I get it from Axel Gyldenstierne or wherever I take it. And I perceive you would gladly have the principal sum — which is 5000 thaler — paid, so that you could get back your bond, which reads on 10,000 thaler; but one does not know whether there is a redemption-clause in the bond, on half-year or quarter-year [terms] — wherefore you [should] send me a copy of the bond, so that one could give notice on it for another year; for to this [Lübeck] settlement-term [omslag] I do not think it can be done, nor can one so quickly raise such a sum of money — unless one had known it by St. Hans' time [24 June], or at the latest by Laurence [10 August]. Concerning your stud [of horses] you had on Hven, I could get them sold no otherwise than [that] I had to send them over to Ellinge, where the bailiff has now sold them — some to peasants and some to priests — so that I do not know how dearly he sold them, until I now come home and can learn it.

The horses you write I should buy for you: I have got 5 from Oluf Bilde — the 3 that went before his wagon, the eldest 7-and-in-his-eighth-year; and I bought a young foal from him, 3-and-in-his-fourth-year, fit to go before the wagon with the other three; the fifth I bought for you yourself to ride, in his fifth year — and there is no doubt he is a noble good horse: if he is otherwise cared for and gets oats enough, he will become such a horse that a prince might well ride him; were it not for your sake, I truly would not have sold him again for 150 thaler. I shall give 300 thaler for all 5 horses, to be paid now at Christmas. There are 2 foals on Hven that fall to you, a red-blazed and a brown-starred, which cannot be your riding-horse [ganning?]; for I would gladly have sold them for 40 thaler, but I am offered no more than 30 thaler for both — yet I have advised your son to take one of them with him, which the boy could ride and lead the other, so the other could be spared. The 6th horse I have not yet been able to get, because they think there will be a muster here, so everyone has need of his horses. I have taken a coachman into service on your behalf, who has promised to follow your son out [to you]; and I have promised him that when he no longer wishes to stay out there with you, he shall have his passport and his wages, so he may come back into this country; he has also promised to conduct himself well in his service with you. I have likewise engaged a stable-boy on your behalf, who has given me his hand to serve you a year; and you shall give him, for shoes and shirts a year, 6 thaler, and an English-cloth garment and a pair of knee-boots; and I have likewise promised him that when his year is up and he does not wish to serve longer out there, he shall have his passport and his wages, so he may come back into the realm — and he had a good service, so that it stood hard [for me] to get him free where he served.

As concerns the fish you would have salted down, your son Tyge was himself there on the land and arranged with them himself.

Now I have given you my oath on this your letter, and further I have spoken with your son, as he can report to you by mouth. I should be content [if] I had David Pedersen's accounts here, for a certain reason.

Likewise I cannot come to any end with Dirik the dyer's wife, unless I had back her accounts, which I sent out to you; and I fear she dares supplicate to the King's Majesty or to the Court-Master [Hofmester], so that I can come no way with her until I get back the accounts I sent out to you — from which one can learn that the account is not quite clear. Yet I cannot understand otherwise — by your issued notes, and by the proof she has from merchants and shopkeepers — than that you do remain owing her money.

The priest on Hven desires to get the smithy you had set up there on the land, since a big [building] decays — for the parsonage became dilapidated in your time; yet I would not grant him the same smithy before I got your will on it. The manor-farm [Ladegård] stands and decays, so that it is to be feared the cowhouse will collapse this summer, or fall down of itself. Otherwise I know nothing else at this time to write you; but will now and always commend you to God Almighty, with many good-nights — as my father and mother, and Eske Brock and Jørgen Brockenhus and I wish you, and all yours, and those who wish you well.

From Copenhagen, in great haste, the 29th of October, the year 1599.
Eske Bille.

[Postscript:] Dear Tyge, since this letter was written, I have come back here to town, and I have spoken with your son, and I perceive that he has received word that he is yet to procure for you, sent out, as many as a full 14 horses altogether — which I do not think possible, that he can raise even 7 horses in so short a time; for in these 4 weeks that Tyge has been here in the country I have had great trouble before I could bring together these which I have got. Today I have bought 2 horses: one from Axel Oggesen for 45 thaler — which horse I have inquired about [and found] to be a good unspoiled cob, so that he can well serve for you yourself to ride, or [for] whichever of your friends you would give him to, [and] then you may very well make do with him. The other I bought from Baldzer Fox for 40 thaler; a good horse too will come of him, though he is unbroken. When he comes to fodder, you may likewise make do with him [for] whomever you would give him to, for he too shall get a good gait and a pretty trot; and there is none of these 2 horses older than in his 5th year. And it now amounts, in money, for the 7 horses I have procured for your son, to three hundred and eighty-five thaler. God grant they may come out to you without harm. I will not advise you to part with the one horse that I bought from Oluf Bilde; were I to give 200 thaler for a horse for you or another friend of mine, I should not know how to get such a one.

Your son and I have today dealt with your old wagon-driver, so that he has promised he will follow your son out [to you]; and I have promised him, for wages a year, 14 thaler and a long coat, and I have likewise promised him that, when he no longer wishes to stay out there, he shall have his good discharge from you, so that he may well come back into this country.

As concerns your house here in the town: Soffi Pax has offered me 1500 thaler for it, yet to be paid over 2 years; Lady Margrethe Rosenkrantz has likewise offered me 1400 thaler for it, to be paid in one year; yet I have been able to close no sale with either of them, because your sister Sophie is not in the country, and I understand that she is to have a say in the selling of the same house. Moreover, whoever wishes to buy the same house must bind himself to you to keep a dyer on the premises — which I hardly believe any of these ladies would do. Be so good as to keep for yourself the horse I got from Oluf Bilde; your son will speak further with you about it. I will have commended you to God Almighty, as I wish you all [well].

Done at Copenhagen, the 11th of November [15]99.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Eske Bille (Eskie Bylde) — Tycho's kinsman ("dear brother") and trusted Danish estate-agent (the "Eske Bille" who handles Tycho's Danish payments throughout the corpus, e.g. the Mecklenburg-debt thread) — to Tycho. An original autograph (Basel).
  • Significance — the winding-down of Tycho's Danish estate and Hven establishment (a rich documentary letter). A detailed account of Tycho's residual affairs, two-plus years after he left Denmark:
  • The instruments still at Hven, to be shipped out via Lübeck (Tycho's son Tyge [Tycho the Younger] packing them).
  • Debts and creditors: the 50 thaler for "M. Christen" (possibly Longomontanus); the 700 thaler already sent; the dispute with Lady Sidsel Urne (principal vs interest) and the legal counsel against summoning her to the Herredag (the proper course being herredsting → landsting → Chancellor); the "Lady Johan Oxe's letter" and a herredsting verdict sent as evidence; Tycho's own signed bond.
  • The 5000/10,000-thaler bond: the 300 thaler interest to Holstein (via Axel Gyldenstierne), and Tycho's wish to redeem the 5000-thaler principal to recover his bond "which reads on 10,000 thaler" — Bille asks for a copy to check the redemption-terms; not feasible by the coming Lübeck omslag (settlement-term).
  • The Hven livestock (the "stud") sold off at Ellinge; the 5 riding/draught horses bought from Oluf Bilde (one "a prince might ride," 300 thaler the lot at Christmas); the 2 Hven foals and the 6th horse held up by a coming muster.
  • Servants hired on Tycho's behalf — a coachman and a stable-boy (with detailed wages: 6 thaler + English cloth + knee-boots), both with return-passport guarantees.
  • The salted fish; David Pedersen's accounts; the unresolved dispute with Dirik the dyer's wife (whom Tycho "remains owing money").
  • The decay of Hven (poignant). The parsonage dilapidated "in your time," the priest wanting Tycho's smithy; and the manor-farm (Ladegård) decaying, the cowhouse near collapse — a vivid image of Uraniborg's working estate falling to ruin after Tycho's departure.
  • Persons & places named. Eske Bille; Tycho the Younger ("son Tyge"); "M. Christen Lenvig" (a garbled name, possibly Longomontanus — flagged); Lady Sidsel Urne; Lady Johan Oxe; Axel Gyldenstierne; Oluf Bilde; David Pedersen; Dirik the dyer & wife; the priest on Hven; Christian IV ("the King") & the Court-Master; Bille's father and mother, Eske Brock, Jørgen Brockenhus. Places: Copenhagen, Hven, Lübeck, Ellinge, Land-Holstein.

  • Reading notes. A few Danish words are uncertain in the copy: ganning (rendered "riding-horse" by context), "Christen Lenvig" (possibly Longomontanus), rodtzens ("the …'s contract" — left unfilled). Flagged; none affect the overall sense.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. The 11-November continuation/postscript (written after the 29 October signature): the 14-horses-impossible note; two horses bought (Axel Oggesen 45 dlr, Baldzer Fox 40 dlr → 7 horses = 435 dlr); keep the Oluf Bilde horse; the old wagon-driver hired (14 dlr/yr + a long coat); the Copenhagen-house offers (Soffi Pax 1500 / Margrethe Rosenkrantz 1400). Restored from Dreyer XIV pp.162–163 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • What the recovered text is. The final three sentences of the postscript Eske Bille added on 11 November 1599 (twelve days after the main letter of 29 October) to his long letter of family and estate business to Tycho Brahe, then in the course of relocating toward Emperor Rudolf II's court. The gap sat between "...because your sister Sophie is not in the country" and the closing "God Almighty..." formula: the prior translation truncated that very sentence and silently dropped the two that followed.
  • Sophie Brahe's role in the house sale. The recovered clause shows the sale of Tycho's Copenhagen house was stalled not only by Sophie Brahe's absence abroad but because she was understood to have a say in whether it was sold at all — a concrete 1599 data-point for her documented independence in managing family affairs (cf. Ellefsen, Søsteren og astronomen, 2023, the standard biography, on her travels and agency in these years).
  • The dyer covenant. A previously-lost, specific detail of Tycho's Copenhagen townhouse: any buyer had to bind himself to keep a farver (dyer) on the premises — evidently a tenant trade attached to the property — a condition Eske Bille judged neither prospective buyer would accept, and which he offers as his own explanation for why both offers stalled.
  • The two prospective buyers, named nowhere else in the letter: Soffi Pax (1500 thaler over 2 years) and Lady Margrethe Rosenkrantz (1400 thaler over 1 year).
  • The closing horse reminder ('the horse I got from Oluf Bilde') echoes, as a final aside before signing off, the fine riding-horse Eske Bille singles out earlier in the letter body ('such a horse that a prince might well ride him') and again in the postscript proper ('I will not advise you to part with the one horse that I bought from Oluf Bilde') — the same animal, restated rather than a new item.
  • Eske Bille, the letter's author, was a close friend of Tycho who visited him often on Hven and later (1602-10) held the fief of Helsingborg Castle [lensmand paa Helsingborg Slot] (per Vellev, in Danske Magazin); here he is acting as Tycho's man of business in Copenhagen during the 1599 departure, managing horses, debts, the Hven stud's dispersal, and now the town house.
  • Correction. The postscript's horse-purchase total, previously translated "four hundred and thirty-five thaler," is corrected to "three hundred and eighty-five thaler": the Danish "halfierdie hunder och 35 daller" (Dreyer, TBOO XIV p.163) uses the old Scandinavian counting form halvfjerde hundrede for 350, giving 350+35=385, confirmed independently by the letter's own itemised horse prices (300 + 45 + 40 = 385).
