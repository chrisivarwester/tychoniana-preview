CUM ante biduum per Studiosum Danum, qui se ea loca, ubi in Cimbria habitas, transiturum …

Text note. The copy leaves several politically sensitive words blank; Dreyer supplies them in brackets — [Regi]/[Rege] (the King = Christian IV), [Cancellarij] (the Chancellor), [Dania]. These are editorial reconstructions of deliberately-omitted names; rendered in the translation as the words they stand for, and flagged. A couple of short stretches are copy-corrupt (noted).

The original

TAM NATALIUM SPLENDORE QUAM PROPRIA VIRTUTE, PRÆEMINENTI DOCTRINA VIRO INPRIMIS CLARO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGUINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO DESIDERATISSIMO.

S. P.

CUM ante biduum per Studiosum Danum, qui se ea loca, ubi in Cimbria habitas, transiturum pollicitus est, quaedam ad te deproperassem, accidit ut heri sero eas, quas Hafniae antepenultimo Augusti ad me dederas, acceperim. Quare statim misso Hamburgum cursore meo, an Studiosus ille adhuc ibi haereret, inquiri iussi. Quem cum hodie primum ante meridiem isthinc discessurum rescivissem, has responsionis loco iterum festinanter exaro. Si alias ad me ante mensem circiter dedisti literas, quam eae erant, quibus ultimo rescriptum est, scias me illas non recepisse. Scribis nunc te scire, me currum promissum accepisse. At unde hoc? Hafnia enim tuae datae sunt, et meus, qui paulo ante apud te erat, amanuensis, indicabat eum ne Lubecam quidem nedum huc allatum esse. Quod et literae meae insinuabant. Rectius itaque scias, me eum non accepisse. Et nihil fere est, quod nunc adeo iter meum remoretur, atque hoc ipsum. Si Lubecam venisset, actutum me certiorem reddidisset de eo IOACHIMUS BUCH, aliquoties hoc nomine a me per literas admonitus. Si vis, ut eum obtineam, fac ut id quam citissime fiat. Caeteroquin Hamburgi alium coëmere necesse habeo. Scio tamen per te non stetisse, quod res haec tam lente procedat. Et gratias habeo pro hoc erga me officio maximas, relaturus per opportunitatem. De libris, cur non oblati sint, prolixe disseris. At eiusmodi argumenta apud me pondus non inveniunt, imo ne sexcenta his graviora, utpote qui adversariorum et calumniatorum perversas oblocutiones non ita ponderem, ut tu vel alij isthic sub alis eorum depressi, sed flocci faciam. Libertati enim me asserui, quam tuebor. Cum hîc esses, aliter sentiebas et sponte in te recepisti, quod libros [Regios] in manus tradere velles, imo et quaedam alia inculcare de rescriptis ad me [Cancellarij] literis, ipso forte [Rege] inscio. Tunc omnia placebant, adeo ut [Regi] quaedam scribenda relegeris et approbaveris; quae tamen nunc volens omisi, ne, postquam semel inclemens responsum ad Rostochio scriptas accepissem, ulterius supplex insimuler. At quorsum nunc vetus illa et avita constantia! An postquam in Daniam redijsti, Coelum ruit! o pavidae mentes! Miseret sane me Patriae quondam meae, quod nemo ferme in ea sit (uti audio) qui verum iustumque ingenue profiteri audeat, imo ne quidem de his mussitare. Quo haec vergant, tandem exitus monstrabit. Sed utinam non nimis sero tunc sapiatis atque animum resumatis! Non putarim, te Carminis illius, quod Astronomiae nostrae mechanicae praefixum est, paenitere atque ob id libros ipsummet exhibere tergiversari. Neque enim tibi incommodare poterit, quod communiter usu receptum est, nempe in Zoilos malevolos et inscios talia libris attexere; et ipsemet, cum praesens esses, sciebas, quomodo per alios (si qui oblatrarant) excusares. Quicquid id est, cum mei non sit moris, ea, quae semel deliberato constituta sunt, retractare, stat etiamnum, ut et ante, sententia, libros illos [Regi] ipsi in manus esse tradendos, quocunque tandem vultu eos accipiat, et quomodocunque a mei malevolis et osoribus id vertetur aut pervertetur. Altiora tracto, quam ut tam humiles censuras suscipiam. Optarem tamen id per te ipsum fieri, ea saltem de causa, ut tibi, non alijs, cederent libri illi, quemadmodum inter nos ventilatum est. Sin vero id fieri nequeat, offerat eos Affinis meus OLAUS ROSENSPAR, qui et literas meas tradidit, infrugiferas et perverse exceptas; ut sint libri hi quasi earum Corollarium et taciti testes, me non ignorare, quam parvam habeat proportionem [Dania] ad totam Terrae superficiem; utpote quae apice minimi digiti in Globo Terrestri bicubitali tota tegi possit. Et quid de immenso Coelo sentiendum, quod animo volvimus, quod mente comprehendimus et superamus? O humiles curae! Nihil peius me habet, quam quod non carmina quaedam ijsdem libris praenotarim, quae meos pungerent calumniatores, ad similitudinem eorum, quae alijs quibusdam Exemplaribus Viris Principibus transmissis praeposui. Nam, ut scias, Electori Coloniensi, Principi Uraniae MAURITIO, Duci Megapolitano ULDARICO Avo Regis, et Duci ADOLPHO Holsatiae, Affini eius (ut de SCALIGERO non dicam intelligentiore horum aestimatore) libri hi additis ijsdem, quos tibi praescriptos misi, versibus, communicati sunt. Num et illi hinc statuent, me sibi supplicem fieri? Num inde quis ratiocinabitur, me paenitudine duci, quod Caesari eos dedicarim, cui tamen necdum oblati sunt, cum ipsemet hoc efficere constituerim? quasi non moris sit, libros uni Regi vel Principi dedicatos alijs etiam dono dare. Neque sane unquam mihi in mentem venit, quidpiam operum meorum, etiamsi in Dania mansissem, [Regi] nuncupare, cum sciam eum ἄμουσον esse et literas aversari, imo forte nec cuiquam alij, cum librorum dedicatio adeo usitata illiberale quid et mendicitatem quandam prae se ferat, nisi id ad pares fiat, atque eorum, quae isthic tractantur, gnaros. Nam quod Landtgravio primum Epistolarum Tomum inscripsi, ut Patris memoriam et diutinam mecum amicitiam recolerem, id factum est, utque plus fidei illae Epistolae mererentur, quod nihil in ijs esset supposititium. Nec ego quidpiam inde reportavi, ne quidem gratiarum actionem, quamvis bis vel ter idem Princeps mihi postea scripserit. Quod tamen neutiquam curo, cum ad tam vilia non respiciam. Sed de his plus satis, et forte nimium. Tute facile, quid tibi faciendum sit, in his et alijs statues. Si nolit ROSENSPARRIUS, offerat RAMELIUS. Scurrilem istum librum, quem te remittere ais, non recepi. Fac ut eum mature obtineam. Constitui enim famosum ipsum scriptorem Pragae cum Caesaris venia secundum Ius tractari curare; nec aliud Exemplar in promptu est, unde accusationum capita depromam. Nec si esset, ulterius relegere vacat, aut annotationes marginales, pro ut ibi factum, assignari facere.

Apud BŒKELIUM iterum agam in tua causa quod potero. Vix tamen praeter verba quidpiam impetrabo. Quid tandem factum hîc fuerit, postmodum significabo. Si Sophiam praevalere Philosophiae non censes, senties. Utra autem Philosophia praeferenda, Platonicane an Aristotelica, tecum non disputo. Inexhausti sunt naturae abyssi. Inde veritatem petamus, non ex argutis disceptationibus, quibus ea saepius amittitur quam recuperatur. An versus et Epigrammata, quae misi, acceperis, non innuis, et iam etiam altum silentium de responsione Daniae ad Germaniam, et de Commentarijs in literas, quas scis, sponte pollicitis et pluribus eiusmodi. Existimarim illud Horatianum etiam apud te locum inventurum, quamvis in aliam sententiam detortum: Coelum non animum mutat, qui trans mare currit. Sed plura non addo. Neque enim plura vacat. Instat meridies, abitum parat Nuncius, animus est impatiens. Tu itaque cum utraque tua SOPHIA quam optime vale. Et, si lubet, actutum rescribe, missis Lubecam literis, quae me postea, sive hîc adsim sive non, facile invenient. Saluta etiam matrem tuam ex me officiose, et CRAGIUM nostrum, si forte apud te est, de quo tamen ambigo. Nam cum Iutus Iuto quidpiam spoponderit, idem reputo, ac si augur auguri.

Iterum quam felicissime valete.
Raptim Wandesburgo 9. Septembris Anno 1598.
Tuus ex asse TYCHO BRAHE, manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man pre-eminent as much by the splendour of his birth as by his own virtue, by his eminent learning especially renowned, Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, my most longed-for kinsman, relation, and friend.

Heartiest greeting.

When, two days ago, I had hurried off certain things to you by a Danish Student who promised he would pass through the places where you dwell in Jutland, it happened that late yesterday I received your letter sent me at Copenhagen on the antepenultimate of August. So at once, sending my courier to Hamburg, I ordered inquiry whether that Student still lingered there. And having learned that he would leave only today, before noon, I again hastily scratch these in answer. If you sent me another letter about a month ago, besides that to which I last replied, know I did not receive it. You now write that you know I received the promised wagon. But whence this? For yours were sent from Copenhagen, and my amanuensis (who a little before was with you) reported it had not even been brought to Lübeck, much less hither — as my letters also intimated. Know therefore, more rightly, that I have not received it. And there is almost nothing now that so delays my journey as this very thing. If it had come to Lübeck, Joachim Buch would at once have informed me, [he being] several times admonished by my letters by this name. If you wish me to obtain it, see it done as quickly as possible; otherwise I must buy another at Hamburg. I know, however, it has not stood by you that this matter proceeds so slowly; and I give the greatest thanks for this service toward me, to be repaid when occasion offers.

About the books — why they have not been presented — you discourse at length. But arguments of that kind find no weight with me — no, nor six hundred weightier than these — inasmuch as I do not so weigh the perverse cavils of adversaries and calumniators, as you, or others there, depressed under their wings, [do], but count them not worth a straw. For I have asserted myself to liberty, which I will defend. When you were here, you thought otherwise, and of your own accord undertook that you would deliver the [royal] books into [the King's] hands, nay, and inculcate certain other things about the [Chancellor's] letters written back to me, the [King] himself perhaps unaware. Then all pleased [you], so much that you re-read and approved some things to be written to the [King]; which, however, I now willingly omitted, lest — after I had once received an unkind reply to those written from Rostock — I be further charged as a suppliant. But to what end now that old, ancestral constancy! Did Heaven fall after you returned to Denmark? O timid minds! I truly pity my former fatherland, that there is almost no one in it (as I hear) who dares ingenuously to profess what is true and just — nay, not even to mutter of these things. Whither these things tend, the outcome will at last show. But would that you may not then grow wise too late, and resume your courage! I should not think that you repent of that Poem which is prefixed to our Astronomia Mechanica [the Mechanica], and on that account shirk presenting the books yourself. For it cannot incommode you — [it being] commonly received by usage to attach such things in books against malevolent and ignorant Zoiluses; and you yourself, when present, knew how to excuse it through others (if any barked). Whatever it is — since it is not my custom to retract what is once deliberately decided — the verdict stands, as before: that those books are to be delivered into the [King's] own hands, with whatever face he at last receive them, and however it be twisted or perverted by my malevolent haters. I handle higher things than to take up such humble censures. Yet I should wish it done through you yourself, at least for this cause: that those books should fall to you, not to others, as was discussed between us. But if it cannot be done, let my kinsman Oluf Rosensparre present them — he who also delivered my letters, fruitless and perversely received — so that these books may be, as it were, a Corollary to them, and silent witnesses that I am not ignorant how small a proportion [Denmark] has to the whole surface of the Earth: which could be wholly covered by the tip of the little finger on a two-cubit Terrestrial Globe. And what is to be thought of the immense Heaven — which we revolve in mind, which we comprehend and surpass? O humble cares! Nothing troubles me worse than that I did not prefix to these same books certain poems to sting my calumniators, after the likeness of those I set before certain other copies sent to Princely Men.

For, that you may know: to the Elector of Cologne, to the Prince of Urania, Moritz [of Hesse], to Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg, the King's grandfather, and to Duke Adolf of Holstein, his kinsman — not to speak of Scaliger, a more intelligent appraiser of these — these books were communicated, with the same verses added which I sent prescribed to you. Will they too conclude from this that I make myself a suppliant to them? Will anyone reason thence that I am led by repentance that I dedicated them to the Emperor — to whom, however, they are not yet offered, since I resolved to do this myself? As if it were not the custom to give books dedicated to one King or Prince to others also as a gift. Nor indeed did it ever come into my mind to dedicate any of my works — even had I remained in Denmark — to the [King], since I know him to be unmusical [ἄμουσος] and to shun letters — nay perhaps not to anyone, since the dedication of books, so common, carries something illiberal and a kind of beggary about it, unless it be made to equals, and to those knowing the matters there handled. For that I inscribed the first volume of the Letters to the Landgrave [Wilhelm IV] — to recall the Father's memory and his long friendship with me — was done that those Letters might merit more credence, [there being] nothing supposititious in them; nor did I get anything thence, not even thanks, though that Prince [Moritz] wrote me afterward twice or thrice. Which yet I in no way care for, since I look not to such cheap things. But of these, more than enough — and perhaps too much. You will easily decide, in these and other matters, what is to be done by you. If Rosensparre be unwilling, let Ramel present [them].

That scurrilous book [of Ursus] which you say you are returning, I have not received. See that I get it in good time; for I have resolved to have the infamous writer himself dealt with at Prague, by law, with the Emperor's leave, and no other copy is at hand from which to draw the heads of accusation. Nor, if there were, is there leisure to re-read it further, or to have marginal annotations assigned, as was done there. With Bökel I will again do in your cause what I can; yet I shall scarcely obtain anything beyond words. What is at last done here, I will signify afterward. If you do not judge that Wisdom [Sophia] prevails over Philosophy, you will feel it. But which Philosophy is to be preferred — Platonic or Aristotelian — I do not dispute with you. Inexhaustible are the abysses of nature. Thence let us seek truth, not from subtle disputations, by which it is more often lost than recovered. Whether you received the verses and Epigrams I sent, you do not indicate; and now too there is deep silence about "Denmark's response to Germany," and about the Commentaries on the letters you know of, voluntarily promised, and more of the kind. I should think that Horatian [line] will find a place even with you, though twisted to another sense: "He changes his sky, not his mind, who runs across the sea." But I add no more, for there is no leisure for more. Noon presses, the Messenger prepares to leave, my mind is impatient. So fare you most well, with both your Sophias. And, if you like, write back at once, sending the letters to Lübeck, which afterward — whether I am here or not — will easily find me. Greet too your mother dutifully from me, and our Krag [Niels Krag], if perchance he is with you (of which, however, I am doubtful). For when a Jutlander has promised a Jutlander anything, I reckon it the same as if [it were] augur to augur.

Again, fare most happily.
In haste from Wandsbek, 9 September 1598.
Yours to the last farthing,
Tycho Brahe, in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm — Tycho's kinsman (cf. brahe-t_15980925001, the later Wandsbek letter to him).
  • Significance — Tycho's defiant insistence on presenting the Mechanica to Christian IV, with a presentation-copy list.
  • The books / the King. Holger had argued at length against presenting Tycho's books (the Mechanica) to King Christian IV, given the court's hostility. Tycho refuses to retract: "the verdict stands … the books are to be delivered into the King's own hands, with whatever face he receive them." He'd prefer Holger to present them (so the gift reverts to Holger); failing that, his kinsman Oluf Rosensparre, or else Henrik Ramel. He frames the gift as "silent witnesses" of his cosmic perspective — Denmark being coverable "by the tip of the little finger on a two-cubit globe."
  • The bitter lament for Denmark. A striking passage: "I pity my former fatherland, that almost no one in it dares ingenuously profess what is true and just — nor even to mutter of it … O timid minds!" — and a rebuke of Holger's faltered "ancestral constancy" since returning to Denmark, capped by the Horatian "Caelum non animum mutant qui trans mare currunt" (Epist. I.11.27).
  • The Mechanica presentation-copy list (valuable for the distribution record). Tycho names recipients of gift copies of the Mechanica, with the same dedicatory verses he had sent Holger: the Elector of Cologne (Ernst of Bavaria); Moritz of Hesse-Kassel ("Prince of Urania"); Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg (Christian IV's grandfather); Duke Adolf of Holstein (his kinsman); and Joseph Scaliger — plus the intended Christian IV copy and the dedication to Rudolf II (not yet offered). A datable cluster of distribution events.
  • On dedication. Tycho disdains book-dedication as "illiberal … a kind of beggary" unless to learned equals — and bluntly calls Christian IV "unmusical [ἄμουσος] and a shunner of letters." He recalls dedicating the Epistolae vol. I to Landgrave Wilhelm IV (out of friendship), getting "not even thanks" from his son Moritz.
  • The Ursus prosecution. Tycho needs the returned copy of Ursus's "scurrilous book" because he means to prosecute Ursus at law at Prague, with the Emperor's leave — "no other copy is at hand from which to draw the heads of accusation." (Cf. the Ursus thread.)
  • Other. The late wagon (via Joachim Buch at Lübeck); the Bökel book-debt (cf. brahe-t_15980925001); the Platonic/Aristotelian aside ("the abysses of nature are inexhaustible — thence seek truth, not subtle disputation"); greetings to Holger's wife Sophie ("both your Sophias"), his mother, and Niels Krag.
  • Cross-references. A major addition to the Mechanica distribution layer (the five named gift recipients + verses); pair with brahe-t_15980925001, brahe-t_15980607001 (Otto Brahe copy), and the later Mechanica gifts (Fugger, Moritz, Matthias).
  • Persons & places named. Holger Rosenkrantz; Christian IV (bracketed "[King]"); the Chancellor (bracketed); Joachim Buch; Oluf Rosensparre; Henrik Ramel; Bökel; Niels Krag; Holger's wife Sophie and mother (Jytte Gyldenstierne); recipients Elector of Cologne, Moritz of Hesse, Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg, Duke Adolf of Holstein, Scaliger; Landgrave Wilhelm IV; Rudolf II; Ursus. Places: Wandsbek, Copenhagen, Hamburg, Lübeck, Rostock, Jutland, Prague.
  • Text/calendar notes. The bracketed [Regi]/[Cancellarij]/[Dania] are editorial reconstructions of words the cautious copy left blank (to avoid naming the King in a court-critical letter). Tuus es asse — "Yours to the last farthing" (wholly). Dateline 9 September 1598 at Wandsbek (Holstein O.S.; no stylo marker; label gives no N.S.) — read Old Style.
  • Correction. Closing formula corrected from 'Tuus es asse' to 'Tuus ex asse' per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p.113 (the classical idiom 'ex asse' = wholly/entirely); the English translation was already correct and required no change.
