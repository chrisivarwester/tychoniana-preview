The original

NOBILISSIMO TAM NATALIUM SPLENDORE QUAM PROPRIA VIRTUTE VIRO DOMINO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGUINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO SUO DESIDERATISSIMO.

S. P.

HAC ipsa hora domum rediens a quodam Holsato Nobili in vicinia habitante, reperi hîc tuas literas, Charissime Domine OLIGERE, et nunc primum ex ijs certior redditus sum, currum diu promissum Lubecam venisse, a Factore autem meo eundem Oldesloviam, pro ut mandaram, translatum percepi, unde crastina die, volente Deo, eundem huc adferri curabo. Mora autem nimia, quæ intercessit, undecunque tandem orta, et tot frustra ea de re a me perscriptæ literas tam in Daniam quam Lubecam, effecerunt, ut ante triduum alium mihi præter eum, quem de novo confieri curavi, Hamburgi compararem pretio 50 Ioachimicorum. Ago nihilominus tibi gratias pro tua in me benevola voluntate quam maximas, quod me hoc donare volueris, relaturus per opportunitatem. Et licet illum necdum viderim, spero nihilominus eiusmodi esse, ut usui meo inservire queat. Si quid desideratur, Hamburgi facile supplebitur. Moram illam plus quam Iuticam, si navi cum lignis combustioni idoneis Lubecam transmissa meo naulo, pro ut iam dudum pollicitus es, redimere volueris, et familiæ meæ muliebri, quam discedens hîc relinquo, pro arcendo hîc frigore ita mature prospicere, id quicquid est, promptius expiabis. Non possum ex hisce tuis, quæ 7. Augusti Hafnia datæ sunt, colligere, te omnes meas accepisse. Adeo parce rescribis, nec singula a me mota attingis. Quin et semel atque iterum ad te scripsi, ex quo in Cimbriam te redijsse coniectabar. Cuperem, ut ad singulas prompte responderes, nec verborum aut characterum ita tenax esses, ut nunc uxorius alijs forte quam antea distrahare negocijs. Si quid scripseris, mitte saltem hîc Wandesburgum, sive per Lubecam, sive alia via, licet fieri possit, ut tunc hîc præsto non sim. Constitui enim, favente Divina Clementia, post non adeo multos dies iter propositum ingredi; præsertim cum iam currus et cætera (tuæ tamen literæ me facile invenient) requisita sufficienter in promptu sint.

Redijt ante quatriduum D. D. VITUS VINSHEMIUS Witteberga, ubi diu abfuerat, et attulit secum fasciculum quendam libellorum de LUTHERI historia cum duabus ad te Epistolis, quas nunc ad te dirigo. Aderant 9 exemplaria eiusdem libelli, quorum octo tibi simul mitto, nonum mihi reservo, ut per otium videam, quæ contineat, et an dissona sint ab ijs, quæ me olim, cum Lipsiæ studerem (ab alio authore, cuius nomen nunc non succurrit, profecta) legisse memini. Variant enim in talibus scriptores, prout affectibus indulgent.

De cæteris, quæ hæ ultimæ tuæ continent, nihil addo. Nec enim moror, qualia apud vos fiant connubia; et de eo, quem tangis, iam diu antea scivi, quantum fidentium ei vel secus, ut non opus sit ex hac novitia affinitate me cautiorem reddi. Invaletudinem alterius non miror; imo potius miror, eum valere posse, qui alios multis modis invalentes reddidit. Viderit, ne ea citius illi eveniant, quam putat, quæ iam antea prædixi prædicoque. Sunt, qui ex me audiverunt. Quid BŒKELIUS responderit in tuo negocio de libris non præstitis, ex adiuncta scheda vides. Misi ad eum CLAUDIUM MULIUM cum M. CHRISTIERNO SEVERINI, qui diu mihi fuit domesticus. Nihil ab eo ferme reportamus præter morosa verba. Si rem vis iure decidi, fiat id per alios. Mihi ob iter instans id non est commodum. Sed suaderem potius, ut id omitteres, et ad Doctorem VITUM VINSHEMIUM, qui tui studiosus haberi vult, serio scriberes, ut is (siquidem pecuniam alteri, priusquam volebas, tradidit) efficeret et urgeret, ut quæ tibi revera debentur, obtineas. Miniteris etiam aliquantulum, te actionem contra eum moturum iuridicam, si per se id, quod æquum est, non fecerit. Sic VINSHEMIUS metuens, quod is huic actioni misceri debeat (uti est per se meticulosus) eo validius omnem movebit lapidem, ut tibi tua, quæ emisti, nisi quatenus quædam eorum pro lubitu remittere voles, cedant. Consultissimum iudicarem, ut, siquidem BŒKELIUS sponte obtulerit resolutionem pecuniarum, modo libros reciperet, eos illi prima oportunitate remitteres. Satis enim alias et habes et habere poteris librorum talium. Ego in hoc negocio quod potui feci. Quod autem expectationi utriusque nostrum non respondeat eventus, non est, quod mihi imputes, sed potius BŒKELIJ tenacitati, ne quid dicam amplius. Vix possum in proprijs rebus obtinere, ut Hamburgenses id, quod ius æquumque postulat, libera voluntate efficiant. Tanta est istorum hominum sordities et avaritia insatiabilis, a quibus tamen me post exiguum tempus liberatum iri spero.

Tu autem mi optime OLIGERE, diu cum nupera coniuge mea nepte a me diligenter salutata ac omnibus tuis quam felicissime vale; et si vacat (debet autem vacare) brevi rescribe.

Raptim Wandesburgo 25. Septembris 1598.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man — as much by the splendour of his birth as by his own virtue — Lord Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, his most longed-for kinsman, relation-by-marriage, and friend.

Heartiest greeting.

This very hour, returning home from a certain Holstein nobleman dwelling in the neighbourhood, I found your letter here, dearest Lord Holger; and now first from it I have been informed that the long-promised wagon has come to Lübeck — and I gather from my factor that it has been moved on to Oldesloe, as I had ordered, whence tomorrow, God willing, I will have it brought here. But the excessive delay that intervened — from wherever it at last arose — and the many letters I wrote in vain about it, both into Denmark and to Lübeck, made me, three days ago, buy another at Hamburg for 50 joachimsthalers, besides the one I had had newly built. Nonetheless I give you the greatest thanks for your benevolent goodwill toward me, in that you wished to make me this gift, to be repaid when occasion offers. And although I have not yet seen it, I hope it is of such a kind as can serve my use; if anything is wanting, it will easily be supplied at Hamburg. That more-than-Jutish delay you will the more readily atone for, whatever it amounts to, if you will redeem it by sending a ship with firewood fit for burning to Lübeck at my freight (as you long ago promised), and provide thus early for my womenfolk — whom I leave here on departing — against the cold here. I cannot gather from this letter of yours (dated at Copenhagen 7 August) that you received all of mine: so sparingly do you write back, nor do you touch on the several points I raised. Indeed I have written to you once and again, since I guessed you had returned to Jutland. I could wish you would answer each point promptly, and not be so chary of words or characters — now that, being a married man [uxorius], you are perhaps distracted by other business than before. If you write anything, send it at least here to Wandsbek, whether by Lübeck or another way — though it may happen that I am then not at hand here. For I have resolved, by Divine Clemency favouring, to set out within not very many days on the journey I have proposed — especially since the wagon and the other requisites are now sufficiently ready (your letters, however, will easily find me).

Four days ago Dr. Veit Winsheim returned from Wittenberg, where he had long been absent, and brought with him a certain bundle of pamphlets on the history of Luther, with two letters to you, which I now forward to you. There were 9 copies of the same booklet, of which I send you eight together; the ninth I reserve for myself, to see at leisure what it contains, and whether it differs from what I remember reading long ago, when I studied at Leipzig (from another author, whose name does not now come to me). For writers vary in such matters, according as they indulge their feelings.

Of the rest your last letter contains, I add nothing. For I do not concern myself with what marriages are made among you; and as for the man you touch on, I knew long before how far he is to be trusted or not, so that there is no need for me to be made more cautious by this new relationship-by-marriage. The other's ill-health I do not wonder at; nay rather I wonder that he can be well, who has made others unwell in many ways. Let him see to it that the things I foretold before, and foretell [still], do not befall him sooner than he thinks. There are those who have heard it from me. What Bökel replied in your affair, about the books not delivered, you see from the enclosed sheet. I sent to him Claus Mule together with Master Christen Sørensen [Longomontanus], who was long a member of my household. We bring back from him almost nothing but morose words. If you wish the matter decided at law, let it be done through others; for me, on account of the imminent journey, it is not convenient. But I should rather advise that you drop it, and write seriously to Dr. Veit Winsheim (who wishes to be held a friend of yours), that he — since he handed the money to the other party before you wished — should bring it about and press that you obtain what is truly owed you. Threaten even a little that you will move a legal action against him, if he does not of himself do what is fair. So Winsheim, fearing he must be mixed up in this action (being by nature timorous), will the more strongly move every stone, that your goods, which you bought, may fall to you — except so far as you may willingly remit some of them. I should judge it most prudent that, if Bökel of his own accord has offered a refund of the money provided he take back the books, you return them to him at the first opportunity — for you have, and can have, enough other such books elsewhere. In this business I have done what I could. That the outcome does not answer the expectation of us both is not for you to impute to me, but rather to Bökel's tenacity — to say no more. I can scarcely obtain, even in my own affairs, that the men of Hamburg do of their free will what right and equity demand: so great is the sordidness and insatiable avarice of those men — from whom, however, I hope to be freed after a short time.

But you, my excellent Holger — long, with your recent wife, my niece, diligently greeted by me — and with all yours, farewell most happily; and if you have leisure (and you ought to have leisure), write back soon.

In haste from Wandsbek, 25 September 1598.
Yours, Tycho Brahe, in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" of Rosenholm (1574–1642), Tycho's kinsman and (now) relation-by-marriage — he had recently married Sophie Brahe, daughter of Tycho's brother Steen Brahe, i.e. Tycho's niece (the closing "your recent wife, my niece" fixes this; cf. the sister-vs-niece distinction maintained across the corpus). Tycho twits him as uxorius — a distracted newlywed.
  • Setting — Wandsbek, on the eve of departure (autumn 1598). Written from Wandsbek (Wandesburgum), the Holstein estate of Heinrich Rantzau where Tycho lodged after leaving Hven — the very place and season the Mechanica was being printed and finished (cf. the documented production history of the Mechanica). Tycho is about to set out on "the proposed journey" (toward Dresden/Wittenberg, ultimately the Emperor), and is putting his household in order: he leaves his womenfolk wintering at Wandsbek and asks Holger to redeem a long delay by shipping firewood to Lübeck at Tycho's freight against the cold.
  • The wagon. A currus (travelling-wagon/carriage) Holger had promised arrived late at Lübeck → Oldesloe; the delay made Tycho buy another at Hamburg for 50 joachimsthalers (plus one newly built). He thanks Holger for the gift all the same.
  • Winsheim's Luther pamphlets. Dr. Veit Winsheim (Vitus Vinshemius, the Wittenberg academic; cf. the "Winshemius's son-in-law" note at brahe-t_15990507001) returned from Wittenberg with a bundle of booklets on the history of Luther and two letters for Holger; Tycho forwards 8 of 9 copies, keeping one to compare against a different account he had read as a student at Leipzig. His aside — writers vary "according as they indulge their feelings" — is a characteristic note on partisan historiography.
  • The veiled enemy. A cryptic passage on an unnamed man "who has made others unwell in many ways," whose illness Tycho does not pity and to whom he repeats a standing dark prediction ("there are those who have heard it from me"). Left unidentified per the no-fabrication rule.
  • The Bökel book-debt affair. Johann Bökel / Boeckel (the Hamburg physician) had not delivered books in a transaction of Holger's. Tycho sent his men Claus Mule (Claudius Mulius) and Master Christen Sørensen — Longomontanus (M. Christierno Severini, "long a member of my household") — to Bökel, getting "morose words." Tycho's advice: drop the suit, instead press Winsheim (who paid the money out prematurely) with a threat of legal action; and if Bökel offers a refund for the returned books, accept. A vivid complaint about Hamburg mercantile "sordidness and insatiable avarice."
  • Persons named. Holger Rosenkrantz; Sophie Brahe (his wife, Tycho's niece, unnamed but identified); Veit Winsheim (Vitus Vinshemius); Johann Bökel (Boeckelius, Hamburg); Claus Mule (Claudius Mulius); Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (M. Christiernus Severini); the unnamed veiled enemy. Places: Wandsbek, Lübeck, Oldesloe, Hamburg, Wittenberg, Leipzig, Copenhagen, Jutland (Cimbria), Denmark.
  • Notable phrase. Moram illam plus quam Iuticam — "that more-than-Jutish delay," a proverbial jab at proverbial slowness (Tycho had just guessed Holger "returned to Cimbria," Jutland); kept idiomatically and glossed.
  • Calendar. Holstein/Denmark were Old Style in 1598; the letter carries no stylo marker, so the dateline 25 September 1598 is read Old Style (per the catalogue label, which gives no N.S. flag); header left unqualified to match.
