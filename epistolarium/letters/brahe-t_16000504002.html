The original

ADMODUM MAGNIFICO ET EXCELLENTI VIRO DOMINO GALILÆO DE GALILÆIS, MATHEMATICO EXCELLENTISSIMO, AMICO HONORANDO.

CLARISSIME et excellentissime Vir; cum hisce diebus Pragæ fuissem, atque ibi Serenissimi Principis, Magni Ducis Hetruriæ, Oratorem apud Saram Cæsam Matem, Illustrissimum et Generosissimum Dominum COSMUM CONCINUM e Comitibus Pennæ convenissem, inter alia Illustrissmæ Dominationis eius humanissima mecum colloquia (uti sane est Vir eximia comitate, parique doctrina, præter Generis Illustrissmum splendorem, admirandus, nec unquam satis laudatus) incidit etiam Excellentiæ Tuæ honorifica mentio, ob singularem, qua, in Mathematicis præsertim, plurimos alios antecellis, eruditionem. Cumque a tanto Viro tuas dotes etiam deprædicari audirem, simulavit id prius de Excella Ta animo meo conceptam sententiam, ut non potuerim non has ad ipsam scribere, atque sic amicitiæ nostræ et ulterioris inter nos per literas correspondentiæ fundamina ponere. Quia vero a Nobili Adolescente FRANCISCO TENGNAGLIO meo domestico, ex Italia huc redeunte, percepi Excellam Tuam primum nostrum Tomum Epistolarum Astronomicarum perlustrasse, atque in eo nonnulla reperijsse, de quibus mecum conferre cuperet, ego certe idipsum nullatenus detrecto, sed si quid fuerit, quod Exa Ta in disquisitionem inibi vocare velit, erit id mihi gratissimum, invenietque me ad respondendum pro meo modulo quam paratissimum: Sive de Hypothesi nostra Cœlestium revolutionum, quæ Solem centrum facit circuitionis quinque Planetarum, Terram autem, et eam quiescentem, solummodo amborum Luminarium, atque Octavæ, quam vocant Sphæræ, cui assumptioni apparentias quam optime congruere depræhendi, ut nihilominus tollantur vasti illi Epicycli, quemadmodum apud COPERNICUM, et Terra in centro Universi, quod ille non admisit, immota maneat (sunt etiam particularia quædam in hac nostra inventione, quæ neque iuxta Ptolemaicam neque Coperniceam speculationem tam competenter excusari possunt) sive de Restitutione Fixarum Stellarum, sive de Cometis, quos omnes in ipso Cœlo curricula sua absolvere, contra quam volunt Peripatetici, probo, idque in septem a me diligenter observatis demonstratum relinquo, sive de quacunque tandem alia re, cuius in illo libro mentio fit, mecum disserere Excella Tua volet, faciat id ingenue pro suo arbitrio. Ego vicissim meam sententiam illi aperire, atque de rebus Astronomicis cum ea iucunde conferre non intermittam.

Valeat Excella Tua quam optime.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsarea Benatica Die 4 Maij Anni 1600.
Excellentiæ Tuæ
studiosissimus
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the very magnificent and excellent man, Lord Galileo de' Galilei, most excellent Mathematician, [his] friend to be honoured.

Most renowned and most excellent Man: when in these days I had been at Prague, and there had met the Ambassador of the Most Serene Prince, the Grand Duke of Tuscany, with the Sacred Imperial Majesty — the Most Illustrious and Most Generous Lord Cosimo Concini, of the Counts of Penna — there fell, among that Most Illustrious Lordship's other most courteous conversations with me (for indeed he is a Man admirable, and never sufficiently praised, for his rare affability and equal learning, besides the most illustrious splendour of his lineage), honourable mention also of Your Excellency, on account of the singular erudition by which, in Mathematics especially, you surpass very many others. And when I heard your gifts thus proclaimed by so great a Man, it confirmed the opinion I had previously conceived in my mind of Your Excellency, so that I could not but write this to you, and so lay the foundations of our friendship and of further correspondence between us by letters. But since I learned from the noble young man Franz Tengnagel, my servant, on his return hither from Italy, that Your Excellency had perused our first Volume of Astronomical Letters, and found in it certain things about which you would wish to confer with me, I for my part by no means decline this; but if there be anything which Your Excellency would wish to call into examination therein, it will be most welcome to me, and you will find me most ready to answer, according to my measure —

whether concerning our Hypothesis of the celestial revolutions, which makes the Sun the centre of the circuit of the five Planets, but the Earth — and that at rest — the centre only of the two Luminaries [Sun and Moon] and of the Eighth Sphere, as they call it (an assumption to which I have found the appearances to agree as well as possible, so that nonetheless those vast Epicycles are done away with, as in Copernicus, and yet the Earth remains unmoved in the centre of the Universe, which he did not admit — there are also certain particulars in this our discovery which can be accounted for so satisfactorily neither by the Ptolemaic nor by the Copernican speculation);

or concerning the Restitution of the Fixed Stars;

or concerning the Comets, all of which I prove to complete their courses in the heaven itself, contrary to what the Peripatetics will have it, and this I leave demonstrated in seven [comets] diligently observed by me;

or, in short, concerning whatever other matter mentioned in that book Your Excellency may wish to discuss with me — let [you] do it frankly, at your own discretion. I, in turn, will not fail to open my opinion to you, and to confer pleasantly with you about Astronomical matters.

May Your Excellency fare as well as possible.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 4th day of May, in the year 1600.
Your Excellency's most devoted,
TYCHO BRAHE,
I wrote [this] with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Significance. This is Tycho Brahe's letter to Galileo Galilei — the principal surviving document of contact between the two, surviving among the Galilean manuscripts at the Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale, Florence. Tycho, near the end of his life, opens correspondence with the young Galileo (then professor of mathematics at Padua), prompted by reports of Galileo's gifts and by Galileo's own reading of Tycho's published letters. Galileo never substantively replied during Tycho's lifetime; the letter thus stands as Tycho's outstretched, unreciprocated hand across the generational divide.
  • Sender & recipient. Tycho Brahe at Benátky; Galileo Galilei (1564–1642), mathematicus excellentissimus, then at Padua.
  • The occasion & intermediaries.
  • Cosmus Concinus e Comitibus Pennae = Cosimo Concini, of the Counts of Penna — the Tuscan ambassador (Orator) of Grand Duke Ferdinando I de' Medici at the imperial court — whose praise of Galileo at Prague prompted the letter.
  • Franciscus Tengnaglius = Franz Tengnagel, Tycho's assistant, returning from Italy, who reported that Galileo had read Tycho's letters and wished to raise questions (Tengnagel's Italian sojourn is detailed in brahe-t_16000306002).
  • primus noster Tomus Epistolarum Astronomicarum = the first volume of Tycho's Epistolae Astronomicae (Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus, Uraniborg 1596) — the book Galileo had perused.
  • The Tychonic world-system (the scientific heart; the edition's convention). Tycho states his geo-heliocentric hypothesis in nuce: the Sun is the centre of the orbits of the five planets, while the Earth, at rest, is the centre only of the Sun and Moon (the two luminaria) and of the eighth (fixed-star) sphere. He claims (i) the appearances are saved as well as possible (apparentias quam optime congruere — the salvare apparentias criterion,); (ii) the "vast epicycles" are eliminated as in Copernicus; yet (iii) the Earth stays immobile at the centre, which Copernicus denied; and (iv) certain particularia of his system fit "neither the Ptolemaic nor the Copernican speculation" — his standing claim to a genuine tertium quid.
  • The other offered topics.
  • Restitutio Fixarum Stellarum = the restoration of the fixed stars — Tycho's renewed star catalogue (restitutio,).
  • Cometae … in ipso Coelo curricula sua absolvere … contra Peripateticos … in septem a me diligenter observatis = his proof, against the Aristotelians (Peripatetics), that comets move in the heavens (not in the sublunary air), demonstrated from seven comets he had observed — the anti-Aristotelian thesis (post-1577) that abolished the solid celestial spheres.
  • Persons named. Galileo; Cosimo Concini (Tuscan ambassador); Franz Tengnagel; Copernicus; the Peripatetici (Aristotelians).
  • Place. Arx Caesarea Benatica = the imperial castle of Benátky.
  • Epistolary formulae. Valeat … quam optime → "fare as well as possible"; subscription Excellentiae Tuae studiosissimus → "Your Excellency's most devoted"; manu propria scripsi → "I wrote with my own hand" (autograph attestation). The honorific Excellentia Tua oscillates with third-person ipsa/illi; the shifts are preserved.
  • Calendar. Dateline Die 4 Maij Anni 1600 at Benátky; the edition marks n.s. (New Style) — correct for Tycho's imperial-service dating, and apt for a letter to Catholic Italy (already Gregorian). Reproduced, not converted.
