The original

AD DOMINUM TYCHONEM BRAHE, DANUM, MATHEMATICUM CLARISSIMUM, VIRUM NOBILISSIMUM. S. D.

MAGNA cum laetitia accepi, cum maiore voluptate legi literas tuas, vir vere nobilis, et adiuncta illis τὰ ἀπαμειβόμενα scripta, quibus erudito et artificibus summis digno examine confertis συμπαρατηρήσεις vestras illustrissimus Princeps Landgravius Hassiae WILHELMUS et tu, sed serius quam oportuit. Abs te enim missae Uraniburgo (quod Uranoscopiam vocare soleo, ut speculam coeli) 13. Septembris anno 88 pervenerunt ad me demum ultima Ianuarij Iuliani anno sequente 89; ubi latuerint aut haeserint, et quorum culpa, nescio. Tardius vero et ego respondeo, destitutus occasionibus et copia eorum, qui tuto ac mature perferunt. His nunc potitus adventu ad nos illustrissimi Principis, Ducis Holsatiae IOHANNIS, et volui satisfacere et tibi et magnifico Domino HENRICO RANZOVIO, quantum potui in hac ex senecta et carceribus debilitata virium ingenij et memoriae (qui tamdiu a libris et studijs Astronomicis absum) affectique corporis valetudine, et in his occupationibus, quibus nunc etiam obruor. Primum autem gratias tibi ago, quas debeo et possum, quod tam accurate exponis mihi occasionem, historiam et rationem tuarum observationum et hypothesium, in quibus vere affirmo me non reperire, quod desiderem. Haereo alicubi, ut in luminaribus nimirum, cum illa circumagas circulis Terrae homocentricis (quibus quam orbibus ut commodius sic rectius et verius uteris) utrum anomaliam apparentem in eis eadem qua in alijs Planetis quinque ratione (quibus Solem statuis centrum) per circellos duos parvos expedire velis COPERNICÆO exemplo, quod probas in Luna, in qua ille duobus epicyclis usus est, a quibus tu abhorres, an vero alia, quae mihi non occurrit. Reliqua ita plana, explicata et perspicua esse iudico, et ad doctrinae Astronomicae καὶ ψηφοφορίας facilitatem tam accommodata, ut me fateri oporteat, veterum hypotheses, si his conferantur, labyrinthos videri, illa orbium dissimilium varietate et multitudine nimia, qua tamen an assequantur, quod promittunt, experientia docet. Totum aethereae regionis tractum, stellarum corporibus exceptis, tenuissima constare aetherea substantia purissimaque et radijs omnibus pervia, eaque liquida et fluida, divinae sapientiae verbo coelesti patefactae testimonia non dubie contra Philosophos demonstrant. Quam mihi sententiam persuadent et confirmant non illa tantum ante prolata e Scriptura dicta, sed multo plura alia. MOYSI enim 1. Genesis coelum expansum quiddam est; id significat enim vox Hebraea Rakia, id est quiddam ἀραιωθὲν καὶ λεπτωθὲν κατὰ διάκρισιν veram, fusum atque dilatatum expansu immenso in corpus δυνάμει διαφανές, quod luce accensa ac diffusa creatione factum est διαφανὲς ἐνεργείᾳ. Diffusam hanc lucem omnipotentia Opificis summi postea die quarto collectam rursus coegit, coagmentavit et digessit κατὰ σύγκρισιν in luminarium ac stellarum distincta corpora. Septuaginta interpretes verterunt στερέωμα, unde firmamenti manavit appellatio. Utraque appellatione indicari existimo cum repagula illa admiranda nobisque inexplorata, quibus moles aquarum orbi creato circumfusa sustinetur ac coercetur, tum hoc nobis obvium et saeculis omnibus a Philosophis etiam agnitum ἀμετάβλητον regionis aethereae, quod propter ARISTOTELES inter secundas causas invexit τὴν ἀνάγκην ἀπὸ τῆς φύσεως distinctam ac diversam. Nec aliud per aeneos Coelos voluit HIOBUS; fortasse quia respexerunt ad pervulgatam tunc et vulgo creditam ex commentis Philosophorum in Assyria et Aegypto opinionem de orbibus coelestibus interpretes, ex consuetudine vulgi hinc usitatum, ut saepe fit, vocabulum adsumpserunt; cum Hebraeo enim non congruit. Talem autem Coeli expansum, diductumque ac dilatationem perpetuo Scriptura repetit et inculcat: ESAIAe 45: Manus meae tetenderunt Caelos. ESAIAe 44: Ego sum Dominus, faciens omnia, extendens Caelos solus, stabiliens Terram, et nullus mecum. IOB. 9: Qui extendit Caelos solus. IEREM. 1O et Psalmo 135: Qui praeparat orbem in sapientia sua, et prudentia sua extendit Caelos. Extensionem illam autem, quae solius omnipotentiae opus est, coepisse exordio facto ab aquis Terrae circumdatis, quae τῶν διαφανῶν sunt quasi principium, sed adhuc terrenae concretionis sordibus atque umbris densum et opacum, quibus in halitum fusis, et halitu illo magis magisque semper attenuato perpurgatoque ac ceu polito et diffuso usque ad illam tenuitatem et puritatem, quae recipiendae luci purissimae subiectum fuit idoneum stabile et perenne, emersisse illum immensum Coeli tractum Scriptura docet, ratio etiam et experientia convincit, quamquam non assequitur ullo modo attenuationis purgationisque rationem et stabilitatis immmotae causas. Neque uspiam legitur, quod in concretum, solidum, compactum et impervium systema fluidum illud atque aërium corpus rursus coiverit et coaluerit κατὰ σύγκρισιν et πῆξιν. Imo testimonia Scripturae expressa per omnia et in omnibus adversantur, quae praetereo, ut taceam cataractas, aquarum ascensus et descensus, accessus ad thronum aeternae maiestatis et recessus commemoratos in Biblijs, quos neutiquam pia Antiquitas ὁράματα aut φανερώσεις tantum φανταστικὰς et imaginarias, ut multi nunc somniant, sed actus veros et historicas de ijsdem narrationes esse credidit constanter. Quod ex CASTALIONIS versione citas (quam mihi non lectam, a plerisque tamen parum probari intelligo) id textus Hebraeus etiam habet, et ex Psalmo 104 videtur ab ESAIA desumptum. Verba Psalmi sunt: Extendens Coelos sicut pellem; quae cum membrana sensu idem est. Ego vero et ESAIAe illud 51 de Coeli Terraeque corruptione, qua fiet renovatio utriusque, ad naturam utriusque recte applicari posse existimo, cum dicit Propheta: Coeli sicut fumus liquescent, Terra sicut vestimentum atteretur. Coeli liquescent sicut fumus aut evanescent quia sunt velut fumus expertes soliditatis concretae. Terra crassa et solida atteretur. Maneo itaque in eadem tecum sententia, aetheream regionem totam impleri non contextu et compage διαφανῶν seu pellucidorum orbium, qui concretae materiae perspicuitate et soliditate crystallis similes sunt, etsi et his comparantur, non tamen soliditatis sed perspicuitatis ratione, sed sua quadam subtilissima et purissima substantia, quae aëre, a quo propagata est, tenuior multo sinceriorque et ab infusa luce perfectior est, quam complexu continent atque coercent suo circumfusae densae nubes et aquae circumfluae, a quibus Hebraei Coelum nominant Schamaim, id est: ibi aquae. De his aquis clara sunt et indubitata testimonia Scripturae sacrae, Genes. 1: Fecit Deus firmamentum et divisit aquas sub firmamento ab his, quae erant supra firmamentum, vocavitque firmamentum Coelum. Genes. 7, in historia diluvij: Abyssi Terrae rupti sunt, et cataractae Coeli apertae sunt, et facta est pluvia Super Terram. ESAIAe 24: Quia cataractae Coeli apertae sunt, et concutientur fundamenta Terrae. Psalm. 18: Posuit tenebras latibulum suum, in circuitu tabernaculi sui tenebrosam aquam et densas nubes. Psalm. 104: Qui contignat in aquis tabernacula sua. Quod aetherea regio circumfluis aquis summo extimoque ambitu inundet, quod nubibus densis operta sit atque obscurata, quod interfusae nubes, etiam ut aquae sedem supremam maiestatis divinae a creatis subtusque conclusis intraque coeli ambitum, circumflexu huius comprehensis rebus universis dirimant, cur dubitem, intuens auctoritatem et fidem immotam divinae veritatis patefactae? Sed quae sint illa repagula aut firmamenta, quae cataractae, quae clausae coelestes aquas pendulas in sublimi sua natura graves et nutu impetuque suo nitentes deorsum detinent, quae laxatae itidem aut adapertae easdem profundunt, ut in diluvio, nec Scriptura prodidit, nec Philosophorum quisquam arguti ingenij coniecturis pervestigabit, nec magis hoc perscrutabitur quisquam, quam explicatum est a quoquam, quomodo suis sese fultibus in medio mundi libret immotum terreni globi pondus. Nihil moveor Philosophorum quaestionibus percontantibus, utrum Terra, utrum Coelum sit in loco per se vel per accidens, quia extra Coelum non sit locus. Sed assentior Scripturae, quae induit et cooperit systema mundi aquarum mole fortasse, si non omne undiquaque, nubium tamen involucris omne, quae et concentum nobis docet esse inexploratum, sed certum tamen et admirandum, accessu recessuque inter superna et infima, inter aquas etiam coelestes et imas, quae denique aquarum et nubium obscura tenebrositate docet secludi aeternam sedem Maiestatis divinae aeterna luce plenam, in qua creaturis inaccessa habitat divinitas, a luce et sede creata inferiorum. Quid enim his est in Biblijs manifestius? Ratiocinor inde et multa de causis eorum, quae in Coelo creato nobis occurrunt, ut de Coeli colore sapphirino, qui interdiu noctuque Coelo sereno conspicitur, dissimiliter tamen, clarius et liquidius die, obscurius nocte, de dissimilibus Coeli partibus, de via lactea, quibus nemini leges et dogmata fero, sed coniecturas recito ex Dei verbo, relinquens expendendum cuique, his utrum habere fidem an Philosophorum rationibus velit. Colorem Coeli sapphirinum conijcio non esse aliunde quam ex aquis et nubibus supernis profusis et tinctis illa coelesti sempiterna luce, quae nobis tanto affulget purior et nitidior, quo Coelum nostrum splendet clarius. Inde etiam esse iudico, quicquid tenebrosum in octavo orbe, noctu praesertim, Coelo limpidiore cernitur. Nec video, quas alias Philosophi causas proferre possint, neutiquam enim sufficiunt, quae ab aetherea luce creata intra Coelum petuntur, ut maxime constituantur in Coelo orbes solidi, concreti, pellucidi, quos immissa et fracta lux luminarium et stellarum suo splendore imbutos caeruleo colore tingat, sicut sparsi per aequor radij Solis marinas aquas. Unde erit enim illa umbrosa tenebrositas, quae supra octavum orbem sistit ac finit visum, eo usque pervio et nusquam impedito excursu, Coelo puro, penetrantem? Viam lacteam autem conijcio esse rariores partes seu nubium, seu aquarum coelestium, seu repagulorum, vel horum omnium, per quas supernum, increatum et aeternum lumen penetrat aut pellucet radiatu evidentius, quam in densioribus et obscurioribus. Nec assentior Philosophorum somnijs, qui illam fingunt esse confusum confertumque lumen multarum et parvarum stellarum, quas propter exilitatem prospectus oculorum consequi ac discernere nequeat; malo enim cum Scriptura et ex hac ipsa philosophari, quam cum Philosophis rerum divinarum imperitis hanc contra nugari (in ijs praesertim, in quibus aut haerent plane, aut penitus caecutiunt) aut comminisci et assumere pro certis ac veris hypotheses veritati coelesti adversas, quod exemplo veterum COPERNICUS fecit in mobilitate Terrae. Propter has cogitationes ex Dei verbo in literis ad magnif. Dominum HENRICUM RANZOVIUM movi dubium illud in hypothesibus tuis de caeli vastitate, non quod hoc alicuius momenti esse censerem, aut quod hoc non considerarem, de quo me admones, sed ut ostenderem, me de octavo orbe et contigua sede cum hoc Planetarum aliquid a Philosophis diversum reperire; non imaginor enim octavum orbem unum quendam cavum orbem esse, cuius superficiei inerrantes stellae infixae sint, sed has mirando consilio et inenarrabili sapientia divina per supremum nubibusque et aquis coelestibus proximum, latissime patens tamen et profunde se demittens spatium eo ordine sedibus certis fixisque distributas esse, quo consistere et circumferri uno impetu, inter se immotae intervallis, cernuntur, non eodem positu tamen supremi coeli respectu, nec eadem a terris distantia; quod totum spatium pro octavo orbe est. Scio, quod obijcient Philosophi: Si orbes non sunt, si stellae partes orbium non sunt, unde pendebunt in sua regione, et qua vi, quo motu circumvehentur? Respondeo: Si ARISTOTELES, qui orbibus eas affigit et circumducit, νόους illis tamen seu intelligentias affingit, cur nos recte de Dei operibus edocti non multo magis tribueremus Deo opifici laudem in structura operis impervestigabilis sapientiae et potentiae impervestigabilis? Cur non statueremus motus stellarum admiranda et imperscrutabili sapientia divina regi, qua conditi sunt? Cur non fateremur ὰνεξερεύνητα esse opera Dei? Concludens itaque hanc epistolae partem acquiesco tecum in circulis.

In observationibus autem hoc requiro, cum te Martem ἀκρόνυχον καὶ περίγειον in Solis diametro intelligo multo humiliorem Sole reperisse, utrum etiam duos inferiores, Venerem et Mercurium, ἀπογειοτάτους, ut pro certo compertoque statuis, Sole deprehenderis altiores? quod et de Cometa viso anno 77 affirmas. Quae enim de 72 anni Cometa, quem tunc meo more coelum assidue sub noctem et auroram contemplans nostris primum ostendi, de illo ergo quae edidisti, visa mihi nondum sunt. Inquisivi de illis secundae partis titulo admonitus et ab amicis, sed consequi non potui. Scis fundamentum doctrinae Astronomicae esse certitudinem observationum, eoque magis miror, quod ex mei praeceptoris ERASMI REINHOLDI Tabulis ante mihi non animadversum de canonibus mediorum motuum ac προσθαφαιρέσεων profers. Quid sunt enim προσθαφαιρέσεις nisi discrimen inter vere apparentia inaequalia et media aequalia? Id vero discrimen quomodo exacte constituetur, si medij motus exacte definiti et cum observatis veris comparati non sint? Memini magnos etiam Astronomos observationes in COPERNICO desiderasse, qui suspicabantur, nec satis diligentem illum in hoc genere, quod praecipuum est, fuisse, nec tantum observationum habuisse, quantum operi tanto conficiundo fuit opus. In te vero nihil horum quisquam merito accusabit, qui tot modis totiesque variatis et emendatis, ubi necesse fuit, organis, idem toties summa cura expertus es. Imprimis vero laudo exquisitum accuratumque studium tuum in discrepantia notanda, quam parallaxes et διακλάσεις seu fractiones per vapores densiores pariunt, qua in re nescio, an ex veteribus etiam aliqui satis diligentes fuerint, confisi fortassis nimium serenitate et puritate coeli austrini Soli propioris. Inerrantium examen et emendatio per τηρήσεις, paucis exceptis, quae Eclipticae vicinae sunt, nemini admodum curae fuit, inde usque ab illis descriptionibus, quae HIPPARCHO tribuuntur. In his insigne inveniri a veterum notatione discrimen, consentientibus observatis vestris, illustrissimi Landgravij et tuis, hoc magis gaudeo, quo plus referre existimo, ut illarum sedes, saltem praecipuarum, sint exquisite certoque designatae.

Distributionem operis, quod instituis, valde probo, nec minus ingeniosa inventa, quae ad compendiosam facilemque ἐπιλογισμὸν motuum apparentium vel ex solo triangulorum canone promittis. Utinam vivam tamdiu, ut cognoscam, quomodo consensus experientijs comprobet ac stabiliat illam quam hypothesibus tuis condis novam fabricam in maximis et mirandis Dei operibus, quibus quantum de summa illa Dei sapientia consecuti simus, intelligemus tunc, quando αὐτόπται coram opera ipsa aspiciemus.

FRANCISCI VALESIJ Hispani nec nomen novi antea, nec legi librum, sed quae ex illo commemoras, mihi talia videntur, ut refutatione nec arguta nec operosa indigeant. Absurdius est, quod coelum ex elementis quatuor conflat, cui propter ἀμετακίνητον ἀφθαρσίαν Philosophi omnes essentiam quintam, aliam scilicet ab elementis, tribuerunt. Nec repererunt, quomodo τὸ διαφανές ἐνεργείᾳ seu natura sua et actu lucidum ex elementis detexerent aut demonstrarent.

Anno praeterito fui Cassellis apud illustrissimum Principem WILHELMUM Landgravium, Dominum et benefactorem meum clementissimum, ubi magna cum voluptate spectavi et consideravi instrumenta, quibus ad observationes ipse utitur, Sextantem imprimis ex tua, quantum intelligo, officina profectum et ibidem natum. Experiri vim et usum propter temporis brevitatem et copiam ac varietatem rerum, quae mihi ostendebantur, ut optâram, non potui. Nec scio, an possim in hoc genere quid praestare amplius, oculorum acumine hebescente. Literas vero ultro citroque scriptas inter vos, quas mecum humanissime communicasti, inspexi accurate, nondum perlegi, quod faciam, quamprimum me levâro aliqua parte illarum operarum, quibus nunc occupor et exerceor. Quae mihi ex Genethliaco meo themate ante tot annos praesagijsti nimis verus vates, sic evenerunt, ut praesagijsti; horum ego nonnulla, exilium praesertim, mei etiam fati sensu et monitu aliorum prospexi ac metui, sed tu omnia, causas etiam et occasiones, melius et integrius, eoque magis cum excellentiam ingenij tui in omnium artium ac praesertim disciplinarum Mathematicarum genere, tum vero imprimis in Prognostico δι’ ἀστρολογίας etiam ἀγχίνοιαν στοχαστικήν admiror. Non illa magis contemnenda iudico erudita et rationibus atque experientia nixa praedicta (cum sint in conditis a Deo operibus creata simul et ordinata ad naturae totius et singularum partium utilitatem et physicam gubernationem vestigia sapientiae, documenta providentiae et testimonia praesentiae divinae) quam coniecturas et praesagia medicorum. Plus scientiae de illis, plus studij et diligentiae in plerisque, qui tractant, et usum magis sobrium et sanum requiro in plurimis. Illa inficiari, quae λόγον habent καὶ πείραν, est derogando ordini et virtuti operum divinorum contumelia Deum afficere. Neque existimemus, casu aut temere alios alio syderum positu in lucem edi, etsi frequentius homines significatis syderum deteriores vita et moribus, infeliciores eventu ac successibus, raro meliores evadent ac deportenduntur aut feliciores. Tu vero praeclare fecisti, quod non repudiatis quidem, sed intermissis Prognosticis, ad divinas illas divinaque sapientia plenas artes totis te viribus convertisti, ingenijque acumine et alis Geometricis atque Arithmeticis ad ipsorum syderum praesentem quasi contuitum motuumque explorationem evolasti ac penetrasti, quae te nobilitabunt ad omnem posteritatem. Mirifice tamen et mihi et doctis omnibus, quibus exhibeo, Prognosticum tuum placet, deque eo communicato mecum, ut de alijs, gratiam me tibi eximiam debere agnosco. De editione epistolarum, et tuarum ad amicos et amicorum ad te, cur non vehementer probarem consilium Mag. HENRICI RANZOVII, causam non reperio. Quamquam enim ipsum sese et te auctorem opus satis cuique commendabit, tamen plus auctoritatis, fidei studijque utrique literae illae publicatae adiungent, si innotuerit palam, quantum industriae, operae, vigiliarum et sumptuum impenderis, et quantopere tibi curae fuerit aliorum etiam consulere et exquirere iudicia, quantumque tuum antecellat. Addo hoc etiam, me paucis diebus ante has literas scriptas, procurante Domino RANZOVIO, accepisse aeneam ἰχνογραφίαν et fabricam hypothesium tuarum, in cuius consideratione pleraque tua intellexi plenius, uno haerente de circellis, quod si in luminaribus, ut Planetis quinque, observas, nihil desidero. Bene et feliciter vale, meque te reverenter amantem et colentem propter singulares et eximias dotes divinas, quibus divinitus excitatum te video et praedico esse ad hanc Mathematicae doctrinae partem illustrandam et absolvendam, quam ego maximi feci semper, cumque admiratione et voluptate colui, sed avocatus alijs studijs nimis cito de manibus deposui, redama.

Datae Dessae 1O. die Maij Iuliani Anno 1589.

To Master Tycho Brahe, the Dane, most renowned mathematician, most noble gentleman. Sends greeting.

With great joy I received, and with greater pleasure I read, your letter, truly noble sir, and joined with it the writings exchanged in reply [τὰ ἀπαμειβόμενα], in which the Most Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, and you compared your joint observations [συμπαρατηρήσεις] in an examination worthy of the learned and of supreme craftsmen — but [they reached me] later than they ought. For, sent from you out of Uraniborg (which I am accustomed to call Uranoscopia, as a watch-tower of the sky) on 13 September in the year [15]88, they reached me at last only at the end of January, Julian [reckoning], in the following year [15]89; where they lay hidden or were held up, and through whose fault, I do not know. But I too answer rather tardily, lacking opportunities and a supply of those who carry [letters] safely and in good time. Having now obtained these [opportunities] through the coming to us of the Most Illustrious Prince John, Duke of Holstein, I wished to satisfy both you and the magnificent Master Henrik Rantzau, so far as I could amid this strength of mind and memory enfeebled by old age and imprisonment (I who have so long been away from books and astronomical studies), and amid the ailing health of an afflicted body, and amid these occupations by which I am even now overwhelmed. But first I give you the thanks I owe and can render, because you so accurately set out for me the occasion, the history, and the rationale of your observations and hypotheses — in which I truly affirm that I find nothing that I could wish [otherwise]. I am held up here and there, as namely in the case of the luminaries, since you carry them about on circles homocentric with the Earth (which you use, [being] better, more rightly and more truly than orbs): [I am uncertain] whether you wish to expedite their apparent anomaly by the same reasoning as in the other five planets (for which you set the Sun as centre), by two small circles, after the Copernican example — which you approve of in the case of the Moon, in which he used two epicycles, from which you shrink — or rather by some other [way] that does not occur to me. The rest I judge to be so plain, explicated, and clear, and so well suited to the ease of astronomical doctrine and of calculation [ψηφοφορίας], that I must confess that the hypotheses of the ancients, if they be compared with these, seem labyrinths, with that excessive variety and multitude of dissimilar orbs by which, nonetheless, whether they attain what they promise, experience teaches. That the whole tract of the ethereal region, save for the bodies of the stars, consists of a most rarefied ethereal substance, most pure and pervious to all rays, and that this is liquid and fluid — the testimonies of the divine wisdom revealed by the heavenly Word demonstrate beyond doubt, against the Philosophers. This opinion is persuaded and confirmed to me not only by those sayings adduced earlier from Scripture, but by very many others. For to Moses, in Genesis 1, the heaven is a certain expanse; for that is what the Hebrew word Rakia signifies, that is, a certain thing rarefied and made thin [ἀραιωθὲν καὶ λεπτωθὲν] by a true separation [κατὰ διάκρισιν], poured forth and dilated by an immense expansion into a body transparent in potency [δυνάμει διαφανές], which, by light kindled and diffused, was made by creation transparent in act [διαφανὲς ἐνεργείᾳ]. This diffused light the omnipotence of the supreme Maker afterwards, on the fourth day, gathered together again, compacted, and arranged by composition [κατὰ σύγκρισιν] into the distinct bodies of the luminaries and the stars. The Seventy translators rendered it στερέωμα [the firm thing], whence the name "firmament" flowed. By each appellation I judge there to be indicated both those barriers, wondrous and unexplored by us, by which the mass of waters poured around the created orb is sustained and confined, and also that unchangeableness [ἀμετάβλητον] of the ethereal region — obvious to us and acknowledged in all ages by the Philosophers too — on account of which Aristotle introduced among secondary causes that necessity from nature [τὴν ἀνάγκην ἀπὸ τῆς φύσεως], distinct and diverse. Nor did Job mean anything else by the "brazen heavens"; perhaps because the translators had regard to the opinion then widespread and commonly believed about the celestial orbs, [drawn] from the inventions of the Philosophers in Assyria and Egypt, and out of the custom of the common folk took up a word thence in use, as often happens; for it does not agree with the Hebrew. But such an expanse, drawing-out, and dilation of heaven Scripture perpetually repeats and inculcates: Isaiah 45: "My hands have stretched out the heavens." Isaiah 44: "I am the Lord, who makes all things, who stretches out the heavens alone, who establishes the Earth, and none [is] with me." Job 9: "Who stretches out the heavens alone." Jeremiah 10 and Psalm 135: "Who prepares the world in his wisdom, and by his prudence stretches out the heavens." But that that extension — which is the work of omnipotence alone — began with a beginning made from the waters surrounding the Earth, which are as it were the principle of transparent things [τῶν διαφανῶν], but still dense and opaque with the filth and shadows of earthly concretion — these being poured forth into a vapour, and that vapour being more and more attenuated, purged, and as it were polished and diffused down to that thinness and purity which was a fitting, stable, and perennial subject for receiving the purest light — [and that] that immense tract of heaven emerged [from this]: Scripture teaches, reason too and experience prove, although it [reason] in no way attains the manner of the attenuation and purgation, nor the causes of the unmoved stability. Nor is it anywhere read that that fluid and airy body again came together and coalesced into a concrete, solid, compacted, and impervious system, by composition [κατὰ σύγκρισιν] and by congealing [πῆξιν]. Nay, the express testimonies of Scripture are opposed throughout and in all things — which I pass over, to say nothing of the cataracts [floodgates], the ascents and descents of waters, the approaches to and withdrawals from the throne of eternal majesty, recorded in the Bible, which pious Antiquity by no means steadfastly believed to be visions [ὁράματα] or merely fantastical and imaginary manifestations [φανερώσεις … φανταστικάς], as many now dream, but [believed to be] true acts and historical narrations concerning the same. As for what you cite from Castellio's version (which I have not read, though I understand it is little approved by most), the Hebrew text too has it, and it seems to have been taken by Isaiah from Psalm 104. The words of the Psalm are: "Stretching out the heavens like a skin"; which is the same in sense as "membrane." But I, for my part, judge that that passage in Isaiah 51 too, about the corruption of heaven and Earth — by which the renewal of both shall come about — can rightly be applied to the nature of each, when the Prophet says: "The heavens shall melt away like smoke, the Earth shall be worn away like a garment." The heavens shall melt away like smoke or shall vanish, because they are, like smoke, devoid of concrete solidity. The Earth, gross and solid, shall be worn away. I therefore remain of the same opinion with you, that the whole ethereal region is filled not by a fabric and framework of transparent [διαφανῶν] or pellucid orbs — which are similar to crystals in the perspicuity and solidity of concrete matter, even though they too are compared with these, yet not by reason of solidity but of perspicuity — but by a certain most subtle and most pure substance of its own, which is much thinner and more sincere than air, from which it was propagated, and is more perfected by the light infused [into it]; which the dense clouds poured around it and the waters flowing around contain and confine in their embrace — from which [waters] the Hebrews name heaven Schamaim, that is: "there [are] waters." Concerning these waters there are clear and undoubted testimonies of sacred Scripture, Genesis 1: "God made the firmament, and divided the waters under the firmament from those that were above the firmament, and he called the firmament Heaven." Genesis 7, in the history of the Flood: "The depths of the Earth were broken up, and the cataracts of Heaven were opened, and rain came upon the Earth." Isaiah 24: "For the cataracts of Heaven are opened, and the foundations of the Earth shall be shaken." Psalm 18: "He made darkness his hiding-place, round about his tabernacle dark water and dense clouds." Psalm 104: "Who frames his dwellings in the waters." That the ethereal region floods over with surrounding waters at its highest and outermost circuit, that it is covered and darkened by dense clouds, that the interspersed clouds — as also the waters — separate off the supreme seat of the divine majesty from things created and shut up below and within the compass of heaven, all things being comprehended in the encircling of this [heaven]: why should I doubt, gazing on the authority and unshaken faith of the revealed divine truth? But what those barriers or firmaments are, what cataracts, what closed [structures] hold down the heavenly waters hanging on high — heavy in their own nature and striving downward by their own weight and impetus — what, likewise, when loosened or opened, pour them forth, as in the Flood: neither has Scripture disclosed, nor will any of the Philosophers, however acute in wit, track it out by conjectures; nor will anyone scrutinize this any more than it has been explained by anyone how the unmoved weight of the earthly globe balances itself by its own supports in the middle of the world. I am not at all moved by the Philosophers' questions inquiring whether the Earth, whether Heaven, is in a place per se or per accidens, since outside Heaven there is no place. But I assent to Scripture, which clothes and covers the system of the world perhaps with a mass of waters — if not entirely on every side, yet entirely with wrappings of clouds — [Scripture] which also teaches us that the harmony [concentum] is unexplored, yet certain and to be wondered at, by an approach and withdrawal between things above and below, between the heavenly waters too and the lowest, and which, finally, teaches that by the dark gloom of waters and clouds the eternal seat of the divine Majesty — full of eternal light, in which divinity dwells inaccessible to creatures — is shut off from the light and seat created for the lower things. For what in the Bible is more manifest than these things? From this I reason out many things too about the causes of those things that meet us in the created heaven: as about the sapphire colour of the sky, which is seen by day and by night when the sky is clear, yet differently — brighter and more limpid by day, darker by night — about the dissimilar parts of the sky, about the Milky Way; in which matters I impose laws and dogmas on no one, but recite conjectures from the Word of God, leaving it to each to weigh whether he will give credence to these or to the Philosophers' reasonings. I conjecture that the sapphire colour of the sky comes from nowhere else than from the upper waters and clouds, poured out and tinged with that heavenly everlasting light, which shines upon us the purer and more brilliant the more brightly our heaven gleams. From this too I judge to arise whatever dark [thing] is discerned in the eighth orb, especially by night, when the sky is clearer. Nor do I see what other causes the Philosophers can bring forward — for by no means do those suffice that are sought from the ethereal light created within Heaven — supposing that there be set up in Heaven solid, concrete, pellucid orbs, which the light of the luminaries and stars, let in and refracted, imbued with its own splendour, tinges with a blue colour, just as the Sun's rays scattered over the deep tinge the waters of the sea. For whence will that shadowy gloom come, which above the eighth orb halts and bounds the sight as it penetrates — the passage being pervious right up to that point and nowhere hindered, the heaven being pure? But the Milky Way I conjecture to be the rarer parts, whether of clouds, or of the heavenly waters, or of the barriers, or of all of these, through which the supernal, uncreated, and eternal light penetrates or shines through by radiation more evidently than in the denser and darker ones. Nor do I assent to the dreams of the Philosophers, who feign it [the Milky Way] to be the confused and crowded light of many small stars, which on account of their faintness the gaze of the eyes cannot make out and distinguish; for I prefer to philosophize with Scripture and from this very [Scripture], rather than with the Philosophers — unskilled in divine matters — to trifle against it (especially in those things in which they are either plainly stuck or utterly blind), or to fabricate and assume as certain and true hypotheses opposed to the heavenly truth, as Copernicus did, after the example of the ancients, in the mobility of the Earth. On account of these reflections from the Word of God, in my letter to the magnificent Master Henrik Rantzau I raised that doubt in your hypotheses concerning the vastness of the heaven — not because I reckoned this to be of any moment, or because I did not consider this of which you admonish me, but in order to show that I find, concerning the eighth orb and the seat contiguous to it together with that of the Planets, something different from the Philosophers; for I do not imagine the eighth orb to be some single hollow orb to whose surface the fixed stars are affixed, but [I imagine] that these, by a wondrous design and by ineffable divine wisdom, are distributed through the supreme space — nearest to the clouds and the heavenly waters, yet very widely extending and sinking deep down — in fixed and certain seats, in that order in which they are seen to stand and be carried around by a single impulse, unmoved among themselves at their intervals, yet not in the same position with respect to the supreme heaven, nor at the same distance from the Earth; which whole space stands for the eighth orb. I know what the Philosophers will object: If there are no orbs, if the stars are not parts of orbs, whence will they hang in their region, and by what force, by what motion will they be carried around? I answer: If Aristotle, who affixes them to orbs and leads them around, nevertheless attributes to them minds [νόους] or intelligences, why should not we — rightly instructed about the works of God — much more attribute praise to God the Maker in the structure of a work of unsearchable wisdom and unsearchable power? Why should we not lay it down that the motions of the stars are governed by the wondrous and inscrutable divine wisdom by which they were founded? Why should we not confess that the works of God are unsearchable [ὰνεξερεύνητα]? Concluding therefore this part of the letter, I rest in agreement with you concerning the circles.

But in the matter of observations I ask this: since I understand that you found Mars, when acronychal and at perigee [ἀκρόνυχον καὶ περίγειον], on the Sun's diameter, much lower than the Sun, [I ask] whether you have also found the two lower [planets], Venus and Mercury, at their highest [ἀπογειοτάτους], as you lay down for certain and ascertained, to be higher than the Sun? — which you affirm also of the comet seen in the year [15]77. For as to what you have published about the comet of the year [15]72, which I then — assiduously contemplating the sky toward nightfall and dawn, after my custom — first pointed out to our people: those things [you published] about it have not yet come into my view. I made inquiry about them, prompted by the heading of the second part and by friends, but I could not obtain them. You know that the foundation of astronomical doctrine is the certainty of observations, and I wonder the more that you bring forward, from the Tables of my teacher Erasmus Reinhold, something not previously noticed by me concerning the canons of the mean motions and of the prosthaphaereses [προσθαφαιρέσεων]. For what are prosthaphaereses but the difference between things truly apparent [and] unequal and the mean equal [motions]? But how will that difference be exactly constituted, if the mean motions are not exactly defined and compared with the true observed [motions]? I remember that great Astronomers too found observations wanting in Copernicus, and suspected that he was neither diligent enough in this kind — which is the chief thing — nor had as many observations as was needful for completing so great a work. But in you no one will justly accuse [you] of any of these things — you who, with instruments in so many ways and so many times varied and corrected where it was necessary, have tested the same thing so many times with the utmost care. And especially I praise your exquisite and accurate diligence in noting the discrepancy that parallaxes and refractions [διακλάσεις] — that is, the bendings through denser vapours — produce; in which matter I do not know whether even any of the ancients were sufficiently diligent, trusting perhaps too much to the serenity and purity of the southern sky, nearer to the Sun. The examination and emendation of the fixed [stars] by observations [τηρήσεις], with a few exceptions which are near the Ecliptic, has been a care to scarcely anyone, ever since those descriptions which are attributed to Hipparchus. That a notable discrepancy from the ancients' record is found in these, the observations of you both — of the Most Illustrious Landgrave and your own — agreeing, I rejoice at all the more, the more I reckon it to matter that their seats, at least of the principal [stars], be exquisitely and certainly determined.

The arrangement of the work that you are undertaking I greatly approve, and no less the ingenious devices which you promise for a compendious and easy reckoning [ἐπιλογισμὸν] of the apparent motions, even from the canon of triangles alone. Would that I might live long enough to learn how the agreement [of theory] with experience proves and stabilizes that new fabric which you build by your hypotheses among the greatest and most wondrous works of God — by which [works] how much of that supreme wisdom of God we have attained, we shall understand then, when, as eyewitnesses [αὐτόπται], we shall behold the works themselves face to face.

As for Francisco Valles the Spaniard, I neither knew his name before nor have I read his book, but the things you recount from it seem to me such as need no refutation, neither subtle nor laborious. More absurd is that he forges heaven out of the four elements — heaven, to which on account of its unmoved incorruptibility [ἀμετακίνητον ἀφθαρσίαν] all the Philosophers assigned a fifth essence, distinct, that is, from the elements. Nor did they discover how to disclose or demonstrate the transparent-in-act [τὸ διαφανὲς ἐνεργείᾳ], or [that which is] lucid by its own nature and in act, out of the elements.

Last year I was at Kassel, with the Most Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave, my Lord and most clement benefactor, where with great pleasure I beheld and considered the instruments which he himself uses for observations — above all the Sextant, which, as far as I understand, came out of your workshop and was born there. To try out its power and use I could not, on account of the shortness of time and the abundance and variety of things that were shown to me, as I had wished. Nor do I know whether I can accomplish anything further in this kind, the keenness of my eyes growing dull. But the letters written to and fro between you, which you most courteously shared with me, I inspected carefully, though I have not yet read them through, which I shall do as soon as I have relieved myself of some part of those labours by which I am now occupied and engaged. The things you presaged to me from my natal figure so many years ago — too true a prophet — turned out just as you presaged; some of these, exile especially, I too foresaw and feared, by a sense of my own fate and the warning of others, but you [foresaw] all of them, the causes too and the occasions, better and more completely; and on that account I admire all the more your excellence of intellect in the whole kind of all the arts, and especially of the Mathematical disciplines, and then, above all, your sagacity in conjecture [ἀγχίνοιαν στοχαστικήν] in Prognostication, through astrology [δι’ ἀστρολογίας] too. I judge those learned predictions, resting upon reasons and experience, to be no more to be despised — since they are, in the works founded by God, created together [with them] and ordered toward the usefulness and physical governance of the whole of nature and of its single parts: traces of wisdom, documents of providence, and testimonies of the divine presence — than the conjectures and presages of physicians. I require more knowledge about those [predictions], more study and diligence in most of those who treat [them], and a more sober and sound use in very many. To deny those things which have a rational account and a [trial by] experience [λόγον … καὶ πείραν] is, by derogating from the order and virtue of the divine works, to afflict God with contumely. Nor let us think that by chance or at random some are brought into the light under one position of the stars, others under another — even though more frequently men signified by the stars turn out worse in life and morals, more unfortunate in outcome and successes, and rarely turn out better or are portended [to be] happier. But you have acted nobly in this, that — your Prognostications not indeed repudiated, but laid aside — you turned yourself with all your strength to those divine arts, full of divine wisdom, and by the acuteness of your wit and on Geometrical and Arithmetical wings flew up and penetrated to a sort of present contemplation of the stars themselves and to the exploration of their motions — [things] which will ennoble you to all posterity. Yet your Prognostication pleases marvellously both me and all the learned to whom I show it; and I acknowledge that I owe you exceeding thanks for it being shared with me, as for the other things. As for the publication of letters — both yours to friends and friends' to you — I find no reason why I should not vehemently approve the counsel of Master Henrik Rantzau. For although the work will sufficiently commend itself to everyone, both by him and by you as author, yet those letters, once published, will add more authority, credit, and zeal to both [parties], if it becomes openly known how much industry, labour, vigils, and expense you have spent, and how greatly it has been your care to consult and seek out the judgements of others too, and how far your own surpasses [theirs]. I add this also, that a few days before this letter was written I received, through the agency of Master Rantzau, a copper-engraved ground-plan [ἰχνογραφίαν] and structure of your hypotheses, in the consideration of which I understood most of your matters more fully — with one [point] sticking, concerning the small circles: which [point], if in the case of the luminaries you observe [the same] as in the five planets, I want for nothing. Fare you well and happily, and love in return me who reverently love and honour you on account of those singular and exceptional divine gifts by which I see, and proclaim, that you have been divinely roused to illustrate and bring to completion this part of Mathematical doctrine — which I have always esteemed most highly, and have cultivated with admiration and pleasure, but, called away to other studies, all too soon laid down from my hands.

Given at Dessau, on the 10th day of May, Julian [reckoning], in the year 1589.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Caspar Peucer (1525–1602), Wittenberg physician, mathematician, historian, and astronomer; pupil and son-in-law of Philipp Melanchthon, and editor of Reinhold's astronomical work. A leading Philippist/Crypto-Calvinist, he was imprisoned 1574–1586 for his confessional position, then entered the service of the princes of Anhalt at Dessau as personal physician. The letter's repeated references — senecta et carceribus debilitata ("enfeebled by old age and imprisonment"), the long absence "from books and astronomical studies," and exilium ("exile") among the things Tycho's horoscope foretold — are autobiographical to this imprisonment and disgrace.
  • Recipient / salutation. S. D. = salutem dicit, "sends greeting".
  • Erasmus Reinhold. mei praeceptoris ERASMI REINHOLDI Tabulis — the Prutenic Tables (1551) of Erasmus Reinhold (1511–1553), Peucer's teacher at Wittenberg; the appeal to him grounds Peucer's standing to discuss the canons of mean motions and prosthaphaereses.
  • Other persons. Princeps Landgravius Hassiae WILHELMUS = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, Tycho's fellow-observer; their joint star observations (συμπαρατηρήσεις) are at issue. HENRICUS / Mag. HENRICI RANZOVII = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), Danish royal governor (Statthalter) of Holstein, humanist patron and intermediary, who proposed the publication of Tycho's correspondence (realized as the 1596 Epistolarum astronomicarum libri) and forwarded the engraving of Tycho's world-system. Dux Holsatiae IOHANNES = John (Hans) the Younger, Duke of Schleswig-Holstein-Sonderburg (1545–1622). FRANCISCI VALESIJ Hispani = Francisco Valles (Vallesius, 1524–1592), Spanish royal physician, author of De iis quae scripta sunt physice in libris sacris, sive de sacra philosophia (1587), whom Tycho had evidently cited. HIOBUS = Job; MOYSES = Moses; HIPPARCHUS = Hipparchus; COPERNICUS; ARISTOTELES; CASTALIO = Sébastien Castellio (1515–1563), whose much-criticized Latin Bible version Tycho had cited.
  • Places. Dessa(e) = Dessau (Anhalt), Peucer's place of residence/service. Uraniburgum = Uraniborg on Hven; Cassellae = Kassel. Peucer's gloss Uranoscopia … speculam coeli ("a watch-tower of the sky") is his own playful etymologizing of Uraniborg's name.

Dessau," but the letter's own dateline reads "Datae Dessae 10. die Maij Iuliani Anno 1589" ("Given at Dessau, the 10th day of May, Julian, 1589"), and the identifier encodes 15890510. The header therefore follows the dateline as written — 10 May 1589 [O.S.] — and appends the catalogued date. The body confirms 1589: the materials Tycho sent 13.   • The substance. The bulk of the letter is Peucer's Scripture-grounded defence of a fluid, non-solid heaven (no crystalline spheres) — agreeing with Tycho's rejection of solid orbs ("I rest in agreement with you concerning the circles") — built from Genesis 1 (Rakia / firmamentum), the "waters above the firmament," and the Schamaim etymology, against the Aristotelian quintessence and solid orbs and against Copernican terrestrial mobility. The technical replies follow: the open question about how Tycho handles the lunar/solar anomaly (two small circles, Copernican-style, vs. some other device); the query whether Venus and Mercury at apogee are found above the Sun (parallel to the 1577 comet); praise for Tycho's treatment of refraction and the fixed-star catalogue; and a closing endorsement of astrology rightly used and of Rantzau's plan to publish the correspondence.
  • Hedges preserved. Peucer's own qualifiers are kept un-sharpened: fortasse ("perhaps"), conijcio / coniecturas recito ("I conjecture / I recite conjectures"), nescio ("I do not know"), quantum intelligo ("as far as I understand"), uno haerente ("with one point sticking"), and the explicit disclaimer nemini leges et dogmata fero ("I impose laws and dogmas on no one").
