The original

DEM EDLEN VNND EHRENUESTEN VNNSERM LIEBEN BESONDERN TYCHONI BRAHE ZU [KNUDSTRUP], JETZO ZU ROSTOCK

VONN Gottes gnaden Vlrich, hertzogk zue Meckelburgk. Vnnsern gunstigen gruß vnd geneigten willen zuuor. Erenuester, lieber besonder, Wir haben ewer sc[h]reiben empfangen, vnnd darauß vngerne vermercket, das sich eine sulche Veranderung, alß jhr Vnnß darin zuerkennen geben, mit euch zugetragen, vnd Wir wol erachten können, das euch allerhande vngelegenheidt vnd beschwerung darauß entstehet, so tragen Wir zwar mit euch eine gnediges mitleiden, vnnd als Wir dan nitt allein selbst zuerinnern wißen, was Wir vor etlichen Jahren vor schöne instrumenta astronomica auff der jnsula Huen bey euch gesehen, sondern auch sonsten zum theil auß ewern in druck außgegeuen vnd Vns mitgetheilten operibus, zum theil aber auß anderer leutte bericht erfahren, wo hin jhr ferner ewer arbeitt gerichtet gehabt, so were dennoch wol zu wunschen, das jhr in der selben continuation an voriger ewer stelle hettet bleiben lasßen, vnd des wolangefangen werck zu glucklich endtschafft außfhuren mugen, wie Wir auch den euch derowegen bey verwantes kurtzes schreiben an die kön: w: zu Dennemarcken, Vnsern freundtlichen hertzvielgeliebten herrn son, mittheilen wollen, diesen einhalt ihr auß der abschriefst zuersehen, vnd Wir nun zu eweren bedencken stehen, ob eß euch dienstlich sein mochte, das jhr eß vbergeben lasßet oder nicht. Sonsten können Wir gar wolleiden, das ihr euch ewer gelegenheidt nach alda in Vnser stadt Rostogk auffhaltet biß zu andern ewer besßer beqvemlichheidt, dan Wir kein vrsachen wisßen, warumb Wir solches zu wiedern sein lasßen, welcher gestalt aber die von euch nun lange zeit hero getrieben astronomische sachen ferner dieser orter oder sonsten in Deutschlandt dergestaldt, wie es von euch nun edtlicher iher hero damit gehalten werden, möchten vergesetzt vnd nach notturft befordert werden konnen, darauf werdet jhr ohne zweiffel selbst wolgedacht haben. Sonsten musen woll bekennen, das eine grosßes dazu gehordt, vnnd in eines jeden vermugen nicht ist solche sachen außzufuhren, vnd das derowegen dieselben groser potentaten patrocinij vnnd befurderung wol bedurffen. Dieweil euch aber die gelegenheidt in Teudtschlandt beser bekendt, also das sie euch anzuteutten von notten, sintemal ihr selbst viele jahr darin zugebracht, so wertet ihr auch selbst zum besten wiesen, wes euch zu thun sein wil. Haben euch zue gnedigsten antwort nicht verhalten wollen, vnd Wir sein mit gnaden wolgewogen.

Datum Gustrow, den 4. augustij anno 1597.
Vlrich h: z: Meckelburgk.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the noble and honourable, our trusty and well-beloved Tycho Brahe of [Knudstrup], now at Rostock.

Ulrich, by the grace of God Duke of Mecklenburg. Our gracious greeting and favourable goodwill beforehand. Honourable, [our] well-beloved, We have received your letter, and from it noted with displeasure that such a change as you give Us to understand therein has befallen you; and We can well judge that all manner of inconvenience and hardship arises for you from it. We do indeed bear you a gracious compassion; and inasmuch as We not only know of Our own recollection what fine astronomical instruments [instrumenta astronomica] We saw with you some years ago on the island of Hven, but have also learned, partly from your works [opera] put out in print and communicated to Us, partly from other people's report, whither you had further directed your work, it were nonetheless much to be wished that you had let yourself remain in the continuation of the same in your former place, and might have carried the well-begun work through to a happy conclusion — as We, accordingly, are also minded to communicate to you herewith by an enclosed short letter to His Royal Dignity of Denmark, Our friendly and heartily-beloved lord [and] son, the content of which you are to see from the copy; and We now leave it to your consideration whether it might be of service to you that you have it delivered, or not. For the rest, We can very well suffer that you, as your circumstances require, lodge there in Our city of Rostock until [you find] another, better convenience for yourself, for We know no reason why We should let this be opposed; but in what manner the astronomical matters carried on by you now this long while past might further, in these parts or otherwise in Germany, be set forth and promoted according to need, in the way they have now for several years been conducted by you — on that you will doubtless yourself have well reflected. For the rest, We must indeed confess that a great deal is required thereto, and that it is not within everyone's power to carry such matters through, and that they therefore have good need of the patronage and furtherance of great potentates. But since the circumstances [or opportunities] in Germany are better known to you — so that to point them out to you [is] needful, ‹seeing› that you yourself have spent many years therein — you will also yourself best know what is for you to do. We have not wished to withhold from you [this] as a most gracious answer, and We are graciously well-disposed [towards you].

Given at Güstrow, the 4th of August, year 1597.
Ulrich, Duke of Mecklenburg.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Ulrich III, Duke of Mecklenburg[-Güstrow] (1527–1603), reigning from Güstrow. He was the father of Sophie of Mecklenburg, queen of Denmark (wife of Frederik II), and hence the maternal grandfather of Christian IV; this kinship governs the letter's diplomatic offer (see below). The edition's heuristic tags the letter de — correct here: this is genuine chancery German, not German-orthography Danish.
  • Recipient address. "…TYCHONI BRAHE ZU [KNUDSTRUP], JETZO ZU ROSTOCK" — "Tycho Brahe of [Knudstrup], now at Rostock." [KNUDSTRUP] is bracketed in the source as printed by Dreyer (the place is supplied editorially / partly illegible in the apograph). Knudstrup (Knutstorp, Skåne) was the Brahe family seat. The letter dates from the months after Tycho's self-imposed departure from Denmark (spring 1597) and his stay at Rostock before moving on toward Imperial patronage; "the change that has befallen you" is his loss of the Hven establishment.
  • Recipient as instrument-maker's patron and witness. "schöne instrumenta astronomica auff der jnsula Huen" — Ulrich recalls having himself seen Tycho's fine astronomical instruments on Hven; operibus … in druck außgegeuen vnd Vns mitgetheilten = "your works put out in print and communicated to Us," i.e. presentation copies (the Mechanica, the Epistolae, the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata in progress). Latin technical nouns code-switched into the German body (instrumenta astronomica, opera/operibus, continuation, patrocinij) are preserved by this chapter's convention; instrumenta rendered "instruments" by this chapter's convention
  • "die kön: w: zu Dennemarcken … Vnsern … herrn son." kön: w: = königliche Würde, "His/Her Royal Dignity"; the king is Christian IV (r. 1588–1648). Ulrich calls him "Vnsern freundtlichen hertzvielgeliebten herrn son" — literally "our friendly, heartily-beloved lord [and] son." In strict relationship Christian IV was Ulrich's grandson through his daughter (Sophie); Dreyer's heading to the immediately following letter (no. 128, the enclosed letter itself) glosses it "ad Christianum IV regem, nepotem ex filia" ("to King Christian IV, his grandson through [his] daughter"), and in that letter Ulrich writes of "den grosvaterlichen verwantnus" ("the grandfatherly kinship"). "Son" is therefore the loose princely-courtesy register, not a literal filiation; rendered "lord [and] son" with the bracketed [and] marking the editorial reading of the asyndeton, and the true relationship recorded here rather than substituted into the text.
  • The enclosed intercession. Ulrich offers Tycho an enclosed "kurtzes schreiben" of intercession to the Danish crown (the abschriefst / copy Tycho is given), leaving it to Tycho's discretion "ob eß euch dienstlich sein mochte, das jhr eß vbergeben lasßet oder nicht" = "whether it might be of service to you that you have it delivered, or not." The disjunction oder nicht is preserved.
  • Negations / conditional force (checked, by the audit's rule). Six negations in the body, all reproduced: nitt allein … sondern auch ("not only … but also," correlative); kein vrsachen … warumb Wir solches zu wiedern sein lasßen ("no reason why We should let this be opposed"); in eines jeden vermugen nicht ist ("it is not within everyone's power"); oder nicht (above); nicht verhalten wollen ("have not wished to withhold"). The closing "Haben euch … nicht verhalten wollen" is the standard chancery litotes ("we did not wish to keep [this answer] from you"), kept literal.
  • bey verwantes … schreiben — verwantes here = verwandt(es) in the chancery sense "enclosed / appended," not "related (by kin)"; rendered "an enclosed … letter."
  • Place / calendar. Güstrow — the Mecklenburg-Güstrow residence town. Datum Güstrow, den 4. augustij anno 1597 — 4 August 1597, Old Style (Mecklenburg, like Denmark, kept the Julian calendar; the dateline year 1597 matches the catalogue label). The working transcription gives anno 1597; the Dreyer page image abbreviates anno 97 — no substantive difference.
  • Textual crux (governs the grade). "Dieweil euch aber die gelegenheidt in Teudtschlandt beser bekendt, also das sie euch anzuteutten von notten, sintemal ihr selbst viele jahr darin zugebracht." As transmitted (and as printed by Dreyer from the Langebek apograph), the clause reads literally "so that to point them out to you [is] needful, seeing that you yourself have spent many years therein" — but the sintemal ("seeing that / since") clause supplies a reason for the opposite sense ("there is no need to point them out to you, since you have spent many years there"). The expected nicht (… euch anzuteutten nicht von nöten) is absent from the source; the witness is a later copy (Afskrift / apographum), so a copyist's omission of nicht is the most economical explanation. By this chapter's convention the missing negation is not silently supplied: the translation renders the words as they stand and brackets nothing into the negative; the intended meaning ("no need to point them out") is recorded here only. The ‹seeing› in angle brackets marks sintemal rendered as the subordinator.
