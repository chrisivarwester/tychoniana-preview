The original

NOBILISS. ET DOCTISS. D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO URANIBURGI, DOMINO ET AMICO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO.

NOBILISS. Domine TYCHO et amice plurimum observande. Accepi binas abs te literas, eodem tenore verborum, alteris adiuncta Apologia. Deinde die 24. Aprilis novo stylo accepi alias a studioso quodam Rostochiensi, scriptas abs te 25. Ianuarij. Ad eas omnes a me et a Domino CURTIO propediem accipies responsum plenissime, unâ cum Privilegio luculentiori, quae omnia noster tabellarius, quem ipsemet D. CURTIUS ad te ablegabit. Sic enim inter nos iam est constitutum. Haec brevibus per eundem studiosum, qui Rostochium revertitur, tibi significanda esse duxi.

B. V.
Datae
Pragae die 1. Maij novo stylo Anno 1590.
T. studiosiss.
THAD.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 244; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Si fieri posset, ut mihi modellam, ut vocant, seu exemplum tuorum instrumentorum observatoriorum (puta Sextantis et Armillarum; in Quadrante enim non erit ulla difficultas) ex charta conglutinata aut asserculis mitteres, ad quorum exemplum similia iustae magnitudinis mihi construi curarem, faceres mihi rem gratissimam, et posset ea noster tabellarius, qui ad te cum nostris literis veniet, mihi afferre. Non puto enim tibi deesse manuarios artifices, qui modellas illas construere possunt. Indicabis etiam mihi, quantae magnitudinis a me sint omnia illa construenda.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most learned Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Uraniborg, his lord and friend much to be honoured.

Most noble Master Tycho and friend much to be honoured. I have received two letters from you, in the same wording, with the Apologia enclosed with one of them. Then, on 24 April New Style, I received others from a certain student of Rostock, written by you on 25 January. To all of these you will shortly receive a most full reply from me and from Master Curtius, together with a more splendid Privilege — all of which our courier — whom Master Curtius himself will send to you — will bring. For so it has now been settled between us. These few things I thought ought to be made known to you by the same student, who is returning to Rostock.

Fare you well.
Given
at Prague, the 1st day of May New Style, in the year 1590.
Most devoted to you,
Thad[deus].

[Postscript:] If it could be done that you would send me a model, as they call it, or a specimen of your observatory instruments (the Sextant, say, and the Armillae; for in the case of the Quadrant there will be no difficulty), made of glued paper or of little boards, so that on their pattern I might have similar ones built for me of the proper size, you would do me a most welcome thing; and our courier, who will come to you with our letters, could bring them to me. For I do not suppose that you lack handicraftsmen who can construct those models. You will also indicate to me of what size all those things are to be built by me.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer and personal physician to Emperor Rudolf II at Prague; Tycho's foremost correspondent in the Empire and the conduit through whom Tycho cultivated his standing at the imperial court. He signs himself only THAD. (an abbreviated forename).
  • Recipient / place. D. Tychoni Brahe Dano, Domino Uraniburgi = "Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Uraniborg" (Tycho's seat on the island of Hven; the edition's convention). The dateline place Pragae = Prague.
  • Curtius / D. Curtius / Domino Curtio — Jacob Kurz von Senftenau (Iacobus Curtius, d. 1594), Imperial Vice-Chancellor (Imperii Vicecancellarius) under Rudolf II, learned in letters and mathematics, who of his own accord procured, drafted and dispatched at his own expense the imperial printing privilege for Tycho. (His role is stated explicitly in a companion letter of the same correspondence: "Iacobus enim Curtius, Imperii Vicecancellarius … hoc Caesaris privilegium mihi sponte propria procuravit, conscripsit, suisque sumptibus conducto tabellario transmisit.") Here he is named both as co-author of the forthcoming reply and as the tabellarius (courier) who will convey it.
  • Privilegio luculentiori — "a more splendid Privilege": the imperial printing privilege. Tycho had been dissatisfied with the first imperial privilege as too weak; Hagecius arranged a stronger one, dated 13 June 1590 and drafted by Vice-Chancellor Curtius, printed in the Mechanica (Norlind, Tycho Brahe, 1970). The comparative luculentiori ("more splendid / more substantial") is Hagecius's own hedge, signalling the improvement over the earlier grant; preserved, not flattened to a bare "privilege".
  • Apologia — a defensive/explanatory tract by Tycho enclosed with one of the two letters; left in Latin as a title-word. Tycho repeatedly used Apologia for self-vindicating pieces (cf. the lost Danish Apologia and the apologetic prefatory matter of this period); the referent here is not further specified in the letter and is not pressed.
  • eodem tenore verborum — "in the same wording": the two letters Hagecius received from Tycho were duplicates of one another (a common safeguard against loss in transit), not two distinct messages.
  • studioso quodam Rostochiensi … qui Rostochium revertitur — "a certain student of Rostock … who is returning to Rostock": an unnamed student serving as carrier in both directions; the present note is sent back by his hand. Rostock was Tycho's old university town and a node in his northern correspondence relay.
  • B. V. — Bene Vale, "Fare you well"; expanded silently in the source as the standard valediction.
  • T. studiosiss. — Tui studiosissimus, "Most devoted to you" (the edition's convention, on the model of E. Tuae studiosiss.).
  • Calendar. The dateline reads "die 1. Maij novo stylo Anno 1590" — explicitly New Style (Gregorian), consistent with the edition label (1 May 1590, Prague). As a correspondent in Catholic Imperial service, Hagecius writes N.S.. The two in-letter dates are reported as found and not converted: the receipt "die 24. Aprilis novo stylo" (24 April N.S.) is marked N.S. in the text; Tycho's own letter "scriptas abs te 25. Ianuarij" (written 25 January) carries no style marker — Tycho on Hven kept Old Style (Denmark retained the Julian calendar until 1700), so this date is presumably O.S. as written by Tycho, but the letter does not say so and the point is flagged rather than silently resolved.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (after the subscription "T. studiosiss. THAD."), restored from Dreyer VII, p. 244 (dropped by the working transcription) — Hájek's request for paper/board models of Tycho's sextant + armillae, which closes the cross-reference to the 1 Aug Tycho→Kurz reply. (Dreyer prints "medellam"; the incipit elsewhere "modellam".)
  • Correction. Three Latin corruptions corrected per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII pp.243-244: 'D. CURTIUS' restored to 'quem ipsemet D. CURTIUS' (the dropped relative clause that distinguishes the courier from Curtius, who dispatches him); 'modestas illas' restored to 'modellas illas' (those instrument-models, not 'those modest ones'); and 'medellam' restored to 'modellam' (model, not remedy). English corrected to match at the first two places, where sense was genuinely affected; the third needed no English change since the translator had already rendered the intended sense despite the corrupted Latin.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VII pp. 243–244; defects the audit noted: text error (3) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Collated the full letter (address, body, date, and the restored postscript) against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.243-244, reading the page images directly. Found three places where the published the transcribed original departs from Dreyer's printed text, one of which (the dropped 'quem ipsemet') changes who is said to act as courier, and one of which (modestas/modellas) changes the sense actually delivered in the English.

3 defects found:
- text error (high). the transcribed original silently drops 'quem ipsemet' (whom... himself); Dreyer distinguishes an unnamed courier from Curtius, who sends him, whereas the published text and its translation collapse the two into one person, misidentifying Curtius as the courier who will personally deliver the documents. Dreyer prints: quae omnia nofter tabellarius, quem ipfemet D. CURTIUS ad te ablegabit..
- text error (low). the transcribed original reads 'medellam' (remedy) where Dreyer prints 'modellam' (model); the translator evidently recognised the intended sense and rendered 'model' anyway, masking the corruption already present in the published the transcribed original. Dreyer prints: modellam.
- text error (medium). the transcribed original reads 'modestas illas' (those modest [ones]) where Dreyer prints 'modellas illas' (those models, referring back to the requested instrument-models); here the English follows the corrupted the transcribed original, so the sense delivered to the reader is genuinely altered. Dreyer prints: manuarios artifices, qui modellas illas construere poffunt.

Repaired. All three defects verified directly against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.243-244 page images (not the digitised text layer): 'quem ipfemet D. CURTIUS' (p.243), 'modellas illas' (p.244 l.11), and 'modellam' (p.244 l.6) all read exactly as the audit's Dreyer claim states. No other part of the letter was touched; every changed word is accounted for in outcomes above.
