The original

MAGNIFICO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, UNICO STUDIORUM MATHEMATICORUM HUIUS SECULI RESTAURATORI, CÆSA........ DOMINO ET AMICO SIC ........LENTER OBSERVANDO.

S.

TARDIUS mihi redditæ sunt literæ tuæ X Ianuarij scriptæ, Magnifice Domine TYCHO, Vir præstantissime: tardius etiam ad illas rescribo, partim propter adversam meam valetudinem (conflictor enim cum febri tertiana, quæ me graviter adhuc affligit) partim etiam quia Typographus non potuit citius de voluntate sua respondere, quam esset edoctus ab ijs, cum quibus egit de papyro sive charta. Dici autem non potest, quam gratæ fuerint literæ tuæ, e quibus libentissime cognovi de sede fortunarum, vel potius studiorum, hoc tempore tibi assignata ab Augustissimo Cæsare. Deum precor toto pectore, ut hæc omnia tibi velit esse secundissima, atque tuos laudatissimos conatus provehat ad optatissimum finem in tota Republica Christiana.

Doctor FISCHERUS agit tibi reverenter gratias, quod eum dignaris tua benevolentia, cui sese totum addit et commendat: forte non indignus, quem complectaris, præsertim ubi melius hominem nosse cœperis.

Ad libros Wittichianos quod attinet, scire te volo, eo ipso tempore, quo huc allatæ fuerunt literæ tuæ, sororem PAULI WITTICHIJ iam ætate provectam celebrasse nuptias secundas, quarum hic fuit exitus, ut nova nupta post LX dies moreretur. Filium unicum reliquit hæredem, cui nondum constituti sunt certi tutores: quod ubi factum fuerit, videbo, an et quid cum illis agi possit. Fuisset mihi res facilior, si ipsa Wittichiana vixisset diutius: videbo tamen, quid fieri et impetrari queat. Audio ab ijs, qui superstite adhuc sorore PAULI WITTICHIJ inspexerunt libros Wittichianos: illi aiunt, omnia esse varie confusa et distracta tanquam Sybillæ folia, nec miror. Erat mulier illa prorsus mirifica et suo sensu abundans.

Quod ad alterum illum Mathematicum attinet, qui ante aliquot annos hîc vixit, si non est molestum, hortor te, ut epistolam non magnam scribere velis ad Medicum huius urbis fortissimum et doctissimum Doctorem DANIELEM BUCRETIUM. Ei sunt noti hæredes, quos reliquit ille Mathematicus, et scio eum tui tam amantem et observantem atque studiosum esse, ut moturus sit omnem lapidem, et operam daturus, quantam poterit maximam, ut desiderio tuo satisfiat, quantum fieri omnino poterit.

Quod ad negocium typographicum attinet, egi cum nostro, quem habet hæc civitas, typographo, et ei significavi, quid de ipso scire velis. Is respondet scripto peculiari, quod mitto, et simul curavit fieri probam, ut nominant, typorum suorum. Tu hæc, mi Domine, leges et cogitabis, et quæ sit tua voluntas, nobis significabis. Ego, si quid hac in re facere potero, quod tibi gratum sit, aut usibus tuis serviat, faciam hoc summa voluntate.

Si quid ad me scribere voles, fac per certos homines mittas Pragam ad Dn. SIMONEM LUCK, Registrator vnd Taxator Ihrer Kay. Mt. bey der Hoffcammer. Is quicquid acceperit, bona fide ad me curabit. Pro versibus nunc missis, quos ad priora carmina aggregavi, magnas ago gratias. Si plane perducta est ad finem editio operis illius tui, quod aliquando vidi, sed adhuc mancum et mutilum, cui titulus TYCHONIS BR. Dani Astronomiæ instaurandæ progymnasmata volumen primum Uraniburgi 1592: peto a te magnopere, ut exemplum Bibliothecæ meæ donare velis, sicut etiam fecisti (quo nomine adhuc magnas tibi gratias ago) cum volumen primum Epistolarum Astron. ad me misisti. Mea officia et studia paratissima tibi semper constabunt pro viribus.

Non licet nunc plura propter febrim, quæ licet nonnihil per Dei gratiam remittat; tamen, nondum adhuc receptis viribus pristinis, admodum langueo. Ille me sanabit, qui me percussit, qui idem est vita nostra, et longitudo dierum nostrorum: in quo etiam sumus, vivimus, et movemur. Oro te, vir præstantissime, ut veniam d[are velis] epistolæ sic scriptæ, ut facile agnoscatur author eius f........

Bene et feliciter vale in Domino Deo nostro, a quo tibi ex animo precor omnes felicitates et quæcunque animus tuus potest, iuxta illius divinam voluntatem, optare.
Datæ subito XIIIJ Martij 1600.
T. M. omni studio et observantia unice colens et amans
IACOB MONAW.

To the magnificent and most noble man, Lord Tycho Brahe, sole restorer of the mathematical studies of this age, [Imperial …], his Lord and friend so … to be respected.

Greeting.

Your letter, written 10 January, was delivered to me rather late, Magnificent Lord Tycho, most excellent Man; and I reply to it rather late too — partly because of my own ill health (for I struggle with a tertian fever, which still grievously afflicts me), partly because the Printer could not answer about his willingness sooner than he was informed by those with whom he dealt about paper. But it cannot be told how welcome your letter was, from which I most gladly learned of the seat of [your] fortunes — or rather of [your] studies — assigned you at this time by the most august Emperor. I pray God with my whole heart that he would have all these things be most favourable to you, and advance your most praised endeavours to the most desired end, in the whole Christian Commonwealth.

Doctor Fischer gives you reverent thanks that you deign [to honour] him with your benevolence, to which he wholly devotes and commends himself — perhaps not unworthy for you to embrace, especially when you have begun to know the man better.

As concerns the Wittich books, I would have you know that, at the very time your letter was brought here, the sister of Paul Wittich — already advanced in age — celebrated a second marriage, of which this was the outcome: that the new bride died after 60 days. She left a single son as heir, for whom certain guardians have not yet been appointed; which, when it has been done, I will see whether — and what — can be transacted with them. The matter would have been easier for me had the Wittich woman herself lived longer; yet I will see what can be done and obtained. I hear from those who inspected the Wittich books while Paul Wittich's sister was still living: they say everything is variously confused and scattered, like the Sibyl's leaves — nor do I wonder; that woman was utterly eccentric and full of her own notions.

As concerns that other Mathematician, who lived here some years ago: if it be not troublesome, I urge you to write a not-large letter to the most stalwart and learned physician of this city, Doctor Daniel Bucretius. To him are known the heirs that Mathematician left; and I know him to be so loving, respectful, and devoted to you that he will move every stone, and give the greatest effort he can, that your desire be satisfied, as far as can at all be done.

As concerns the typographic business, I dealt with our printer, whom this city has, and indicated to him what you wish to know about him. He answers in a special writing, which I send, and has also had a sample (proof), as they call it, made of his types. You, my Lord, will read and consider these, and signify to us your will. I, if I can do anything in this matter welcome to you or serviceable to your uses, will do it with the greatest goodwill.

If you wish to write anything to me, see that you send it, by sure men, to Prague, to Lord Simon Luck, Registrar and Assessor of His Imperial Majesty at the Court-Chamber — whatever he receives, he will faithfully convey to me. For the verses now sent, which I have added to the earlier poems, I give great thanks. If the edition of that work of yours — which I once saw, but still incomplete and mutilated, whose title is Tychonis Brahe Dani Astronomiae Instaurandae Progymnasmata, Volume One, Uraniborg 1592 — has been quite brought to completion, I greatly ask that you would give a copy to my Library, as you also did (for which I still give you great thanks) when you sent me the first volume of the Astronomical Letters. My services and zeal will always stand most ready for you, according to my powers.

It is not permitted me now [to write] more, because of the fever — which, though by God's grace it somewhat remits, yet, my former strength not yet recovered, I quite languish. He will heal me who struck me, who is the same as our life, and the length of our days; in whom also we are, and live, and move [Acts 17:28]. I beg you, most excellent man, that you would [be willing to give] pardon to a letter so written that its author is easily recognized [as …].

Fare well and happily in the Lord our God, from whom I pray you, from my heart, all felicities, and whatever your mind can wish, according to His divine will.
Given hastily, 14 March 1600.
Cultivating and loving Your Magnificence uniquely, with all zeal and respect,
Jacob Monau.

Apparatus

  • Sender & occasion. Jacob Monau (Monavius) of Breslau — this is his reply to Tycho's letter of 10 January 1600 (brahe-t_16000110001), and the antecedent of the later Monau letters (brahe-t_16000516001 / brahe-t_16000614001). Written while Monau is ill with a tertian fever.
  • Significance — the Wittich-library provenance (key detail). Monau answers Tycho's standing request for Paul Wittich's books (incl. the annotated Copernicus copies; cf. brahe-t_16000110001): at the very time Tycho's letter arrived, Wittich's aged sister-heir had remarried — and died 60 days later, leaving a single son as heir for whom guardians were not yet appointed (the pupillus/tutores of brahe-t_16000516001). He reports the books, seen while the sister lived, are "variously confused and scattered, like the Sibyl's leaves" — "that woman was utterly eccentric." This is the pivotal document for the dispersal of the Wittich library (the "now that the difficult sister is dead" of brahe-t_16000516001 refers to exactly this).
  • The other Breslau astronomer & Bucretius. Monau routes Tycho's request for the dead Breslau astronomer's papers (cf. brahe-t_16000110001) through Daniel Bucretius, "the most stalwart and learned physician of this city," who knows the heirs and "will move every stone."
  • Printing & books. Monau forwards the Breslau printer's terms and a type-sample (proof); thanks Tycho for verses added to the commendatory collection; and requests for his Library a finished copy of the Progymnasmata (Astronomiae Instaurandae Progymnasmata, vol. 1, Uraniborg 1592), having only seen it "incomplete and mutilated" — as Tycho had earlier gifted him the first volume of the Epistolae Astronomicae. (A datum for the distribution of Tycho's books.)
  • The relay. Letters to Monau to go via Simon Luck, Registrar and Assessor (Registrator und Taxator) of the imperial Court-Chamber (Hofkammer) at Prague (German title kept by this chapter's convention).
  • Scripture. in quo … sumus, vivimus, et movemur — Acts 17:28 ("in him we live and move and have our being"); Ille me sanabit qui me percussit echoes Job 5:18 / Deut. 32:39.
  • Persons named. Jacob Monau (sender); Tobias Fischer; Paul Wittich (his sister-heir and her son); Daniel Bucretius; the unnamed dead Breslau astronomer; Simon Luck (imperial Hofkammer registrar); Rudolf II.
  • Textual flags. The witness has lacunae in the address (Caesa…… Domino et amico sic ……lenter observando) and in the closing (veniam d[are velis] … author eius f……), reproduced with brackets/ellipses; they do not affect the body. Calendar: dateline XIIIJ Martij 1600; Silesia (Breslau) on the Gregorian (New Style) by 1600; the edition gives no place (s.l.) — "[Breslau]" supplied as Monau's seat. Marked [N.S.].
