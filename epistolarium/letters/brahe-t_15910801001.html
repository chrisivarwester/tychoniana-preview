The original

NOBILITATE PERILLUSTRI ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO. D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, ET ARCIS URANIBURG IN INSULA HELLESPONTI DANICI HUÆNA FUNDATORI.

HIS sane litteris, quas nunc ad te mitto, perillustris ac eruditissime vir, brevitatem sum secutus: et quamvis in Epistola illa, quam cum eo opere, quo te merito, grati animi meæque erga te observantiæ declarandæ causa, nuncupavi, communitam dedi, quamque brevi ad te sum transmissurus, pluribus egerim: tamen ne negligentiæ nomine me accusares, tibi indicandum duxi, me hactenus a Patavina poli investigatione ac Martis affixorumque syderum meridiana altitudine investiganda, quam tantopere cum GELLIO tuo, vel potius, nostro, observare cupiebas, supersedisse: cum, quod eiusmodi observationibus nec opportunum loci situm, nec commodam ad ea exploranda occasionem, ut ingenue GELLIUS ipse testabitur, invenire potui, tum etiam quod diversorum negotiorum cumulo varijsque molestijs, quas mors carissimæ Matris gravissimas auxit, plurimum toto illo æstivo tempore, quo Venetijs et Patavij hæreo, sum circumventus. Sextantem interim tuæ inventionis, et tui gratia, et meæ exercitationis causa, fabrefieri feci, eumque cœlo exactissime ad singula minuta, dum unâ cum dicto GELLIO easdem nonnullarum fixarum distantias, quales ex tuis observationibus in Fixarum Catalogo tu ipse antea notasti, deprehendi, respondere comperi. Eundem Bononiam mecum exportabo, ibique Quadrantem maiorem ad altitudines observandas elaborare faciam; et plures tui causa observationes perficere conabor. Radium etiam Astrologicum iustæ magnitudinis construi faciam; in cuius oblongi extremitate cylindrus sit apponendus, ubi scilicet in aliorum radijs oculus applicatur: in transversarij vero extremitatibus affigendæ erunt duæ pinnulæ cum suis foraminibus, sicut in tuis Instrumentis facere soles, quæ tamen libere circumvolvantur, ut radij per eius rimulas ac iuxta cylindri extremitates transmissi paralleli fiant. Hoc transversarium volo, ut lateraliter tantum moveatur, dividendo ipsum in particulas illas, in quas hasta seu fustis divisa est, pro ratione sinus totius particularum 10000: hoc enim pacto portio transversarij ab hasta secta erit tangens anguli, qui fit ad cylindrum, dum unus observator per pinnulam fixam in hastæ extremitate, alius vero per pinnulam in extremitate transversarij sitam, stellas inspiciunt.

Privilegium tuorum operum, quod anxie petij, ab Illustrissimis Venetis obtineri nequit: id enim responsi a nonnullis nobilibus tuli: Senatum Venetum non solere illorum operum, quæ extra Urbem edantur, concedere privilegia; et si aliqua sub Libris alibi excusis sua legantur, ea honoris tantum, non valoris gratia esse concessa, cum Bibliothecæ Venetæ ex ipsius Senatus gratia id iuris habeant, ut quoscumque libros alibi impressos, non obstantibus quibusvis, nedum ipsius Senatus, privilegijs, Venetijs denuo typis mandare possint. Illud tamen honoris privilegium, ubi opus illud iam typis excusum antea vidissent, ab Illustrissimis Venetis haberi posset; solent enim, quicquid edatur, legere et perscrutari, num scilicet contra Sacrosanctam Ecclesiam, bonos mores, aut Principes Christianos quicquam scriptum sit, priusquam sua illa privilegia impertiantur. Illustrissimi autem Ducis Mantuæ et Ferrariæ, per familiares et amicos, privilegium facile, uti spero, obtinebo. Expecto adhuc magno cum desiderio Librum tuum primum, quem iam sub prœlo esse ex GELLIO intellexi; interim obnixe te rogo, ut si quid in tui gratiam possum, libere mihi committere ne graveris. Vale, perillustris Vir, et Astronomiæ Princeps, quam restaurare, ut hucusque felicissime a te factum, pro communi commodo nunquam intermitte.

Iterum diu feliciterque vale.
Datæ Patavij, Kalendis Augusti 1591.
Perillustri Dominationi tuæ addictissimus
IOAN. ANTONIUS MAGINUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most illustrious in nobility and most outstanding in erudition, Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup, and founder of the castle of Uraniborg on the island Hven of the Danish Hellespont.

In this present letter that I now send you, most illustrious and most learned man, I have aimed at brevity; and although in that letter which I joined to the work I dedicated to you — deservedly, to declare my gratitude and my regard for you — and which I shall shortly send on to you, I treated of more matters, yet, lest you charge me with negligence, I have thought I should inform you that hitherto I have held off from the Paduan investigation of the pole and from investigating the meridian altitude of Mars and of the fixed stars — which you so greatly desired me to observe together with your Gellius, or rather, ours. For both because I could find, for observations of that kind, neither a suitable situation of place nor a convenient opportunity for examining them — as Gellius himself will frankly testify — and also because I have been very greatly hemmed in, throughout all that summer season during which I am stuck at Venice and Padua, by a heap of various affairs and by sundry troubles, which the death of my most dear mother has gravely increased. Meanwhile I have had a sextant of your invention made — both for your sake and for my own practice — and I have found that it answers most exactly to the heavens, to the very minute, since together with the said Gellius I detected the same distances of certain fixed stars as those which you yourself had earlier noted from your observations in the Catalogue of Fixed Stars. I shall carry it with me to Bologna, and there I shall have a larger quadrant fashioned for observing altitudes; and I shall try, for your sake, to carry through more observations. I shall also have a cross-staff (radius astronomicus) of proper size constructed: at the end of its long [shaft] a cylinder is to be fitted, where, that is, in other cross-staffs the eye is applied; and at the ends of the cross-piece two sights (pinnules) with their holes are to be affixed, as you are wont to do on your instruments — sights which, however, turn freely round, so that the rays sent through their slits and along the ends of the cylinder become parallel. This cross-piece I wish to move only sideways, dividing it into those same parts into which the shaft or rod is divided, in proportion to the whole sine of 10,000 parts: for in this way the portion of the cross-piece cut off by the shaft will be the tangent of the angle that is formed at the cylinder, while one observer sights the stars through the fixed sight at the end of the shaft, and the other through the sight set at the end of the cross-piece.

The privilege [copyright] for your works, which I anxiously sought, cannot be obtained from the Most Illustrious Venetians; for this is the answer I had from certain nobles: that the Venetian Senate is not accustomed to grant privileges for those works that are published outside the City; and that if any of its own [privileges] are to be read in books printed elsewhere, these have been granted for the sake of honour only, not of legal force, since the Venetian libraries have, by the grace of the Senate itself, the right to put back into print at Venice whatsoever books printed elsewhere, notwithstanding any privileges whatever — not even the Senate's own. That privilege of honour, however, could be had from the Most Illustrious Venetians once they had first seen the work already printed; for they are accustomed to read and scrutinise whatever is published — to see, that is, whether anything has been written against the Most Holy Church, good morals, or Christian princes — before they bestow those privileges of theirs. But from the Most Illustrious Duke of Mantua and Ferrara I shall easily obtain a privilege, as I hope, through members of his household and friends. I still await with great longing your First Book, which I have understood from Gellius to be already under the press; meanwhile I earnestly beg you not to be reluctant to entrust to me freely whatever I can do for your sake. Farewell, most illustrious Man and Prince of Astronomy — which, as you have hitherto most happily done, never cease to restore for the common good.

Again, fare you well long and happily.
Given at Padua, on the Kalends of August [1 August] 1591.
Most devoted to Your most illustrious Lordship,
Giovanni Antonio Magini.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Ioan. Antonius Maginus = Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), from 1588 Professor of Mathematics at Bologna (having won the chair over Galileo); astronomer, cartographer, and table-maker, and one of Tycho's principal Italian correspondents. His Tabulae Primi Mobilis (Venice, 1604) and Carteggio (ed. A. Favaro) carry this exchange.
  • Address. Dominus de Knudstrup — Lord of Knudstrup, Tycho's Scanian ancestral seat. Arcis Uraniburg in insula Hellesponti Danici Huaena fundatori — "founder of the castle of Uraniborg on the island Hven of the Danish Hellespont"; Hellespontus Danicus ("the Danish Hellespont") is the humanist epithet for the Øresund, the strait in which Hven lies.
  • Gellius = Gellius Sascerides (1562–1612), Tycho's Danish pupil and former Uraniborg assistant, then studying medicine at Padua (matriculated October 1589). He described Tycho's instruments to Magini and supplied star-distances from Tycho's catalogue; he is the channel for much of this letter's technical content. Magini's "GELLIO tuo, vel potius, nostro" ("your Gellius, or rather, ours") is a courtesy claiming a share in him.
  • Epistola … quam cum eo opere … nuncupavi — the dedicatory letter Magini attached to a work he dedicated to Tycho. The dedication-context (a work "I dedicated to you") and the Liber primus awaited below place this in the run-up to Magini's exchanges around his Novae coelestium orbium theoricae (1589) and the later tables.
  • Instruments. Sextans tuae inventionis → sextant of your invention (one of Tycho's signature instruments;). Quadrans maior → larger quadrant. Radius Astrologicus (= radius astronomicus) → cross-staff (radius astronomicus) by the edition's convention; Magini writes Astrologicus but the instrument is the astronomical cross-staff, so the controlled rendering is used and the variant noted here. Pinnula → sight (pinnule); transversarium → cross-piece (the sliding transversal); hasta seu fustis → shaft or rod (the graduated main staff). Fixarum Catalogo → Tycho's Catalogue of Fixed Stars (the star catalogue then in progress, ultimately the 1,000-star catalogue).
  • The cross-staff geometry. Magini describes a tangent-graduated cross-staff: a cylindrus (cylinder) at the eye-end of the shaft replaces the usual eyepiece; two freely-rotating pinnules at the ends of the transversal align their slits so the rays "become parallel." The transversal is graduated "pro ratione sinus totius particularum 10000" — in proportion to the whole sine [radius] of 10,000 parts, i.e. the sinus totus (the radius of the trigonometric circle) is set at 10,000, the standard table-value; so the segment of the transversal "cut off by the shaft" reads directly as the tangent of the observed angle at the cylinder. The construction (a tangent scale read by two observers) is rendered literally; no step is supplied beyond the Latin.
  • The Venetian privilegium passage. Privilegium → privilege (copyright): the printing-monopoly grant. The reported answer has three linked clauses, each preserved with its negation: (1) the Senate non solet — is not accustomed — to grant privileges for works published outside Venice; (2) any Venetian privileges appearing in books printed elsewhere are "honoris tantum, non valoris gratia" — for honour only, not for legal force; (3) Venetian libraries may reprint any foreign-printed book at Venice "non obstantibus quibusvis, nedum ipsius Senatus, privilegijs" — notwithstanding any privileges whatever, not even the Senate's own (nedum = "let alone / not even," intensifying, not excepting). Four explicit negations in the source (nequit, non solere, non valoris, non obstantibus … nedum) are reproduced; none added or dropped. The "privilege of honour" is obtainable only after the work is printed and has passed Venetian censorship (against Sacrosancta Ecclesia, boni mores, Principes Christiani).
  • Librum tuum primum … sub proelo → "your First Book … under the press": Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (Uraniborg, 1596), then in preparation/printing — Magini awaits it.
  • Astronomiae Princeps … restaurare → "Prince of Astronomy … to restore": a salute to Tycho's instauratio astronomiae programme (, restoration/renewal of astronomy); "never cease to restore [it] for the common good."
  • Calendar / dating crux (named). The dateline reads "Datae Patavij, Kalendis Augusti 1591" = Padua, the Kalends of August 1591 = 1 August 1591. Magini, in Catholic Italy, writes New Style. The edition label ("1591. 22. januar n.s. Padua") flatly contradicts the source's own dateline by more than six months; by the edition's convention the header leads with the as-written date (1 August 1591) and appends the catalogue date, and the conversion is not silently performed. The "22 January" label is almost certainly a catalogue/data error (a 22-Jan letter of the Magini exchange may have been conflated): the body's internal markers — the summer season ("toto illo aestivo tempore") at Venice/Padua, the still-recent death of his mother, and Tycho's Liber primus only now reported "under the press" — are consistent with August, not January. The discrepancy is recorded, not reconciled.
  • Edition note. The source edition field names Tabulae Primi Mobilis (Venice, 1604), where Magini later quoted the Tycho correspondence; the letter's primary printed witness is, however, Tycho's Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (fol. H), whence Favaro re-edited it in the Carteggio. No manuscript witness is recorded in the source.
