The original

OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO, VIRO CORDATO ET MIHI AMICO, CONSANGVINEO ET AFFINI CHARISSIMO.

NOBILISSIME Domine OLIGERE, Consangvinee et Affinis desideratissime et præ multis dilecte. Incidit aliquid hodie post tuum a me discessum, quod in scheda memoriali tibi tradita annotatum non recordor: nempe quod sit nomen Baronis illius Austriaci, qui uxorem in Cimbria duxit. Illud enim mihi memoriâ excidit. Cuperem etiam scire, ubinam habitet, et quomodo intitulandus sit, iuxta morem vobis consuetum; quo illi scribam convenientius de putidissimo et mendacissimo isto Plagiario, cuius hîc facta est mentio; atque eorum, quæ ipsi de eo constant, cum mecum ab ERICO LANGIO adductus esset, indicationem efflagitem. Admoneas etiam, an Latinæ linguæ sit peritus, nec ne; quo sciam, an Germanice vel Latine illi scribendum sit, aut etiam Danice, si hoc callet idioma. Vale, et literas Regi destinatas, ad non Regias oblique respondentes per hunc nuncium remitte, quantum otium tibi permiserit, critizatas, atque glossis marginalibus auctas: quod etiam ut in prioribus facias, quibus hæ nonnulla respondent, rogo; nec te id effecturum diffido, quoniam sic in te recepisti.

Iamque ulterius et perpetuo quam felicissime vale, mi exoptatissime et desideratissime OLIGERE.
Raptim Wandesburgi die 14. Februarij Anni 98.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Holger Rosenkrantz, a man of sense and my friend, a kinsman and connexion by marriage most dear.

Most noble Master Holger, kinsman and connexion most longed-for and beloved before many. Something occurred to me today, after your departure from me, which I do not recall having noted down in the memorandum-slip handed over to you: namely, what is the name of that Austrian baron who took a wife in Holstein [Cimbria]. For it has slipped my memory. I should also like to know where he lives, and how he is to be styled, according to the custom usual among you [of the nobility]; so that I may write to him more fittingly about that most rotten and most lying Plagiarist, of whom mention was made here; and so that I may earnestly request a statement of those things which are established to him concerning the man, since he had been brought to me by Erik Lange. Advise me too whether he is skilled in the Latin tongue or not; so that I may know whether I am to write to him in German or in Latin, or even in Danish, if he is versed in this language. Farewell; and the letters destined for the King, which obliquely answer the not-royal ones, send back by this messenger — so far as your leisure shall allow you — once they have been subjected to critical judgement [critizatas] and enlarged with marginal glosses: which I ask that you do also in the case of the earlier ones, to which these in some part correspond; nor do I doubt that you will accomplish it, since you have so taken it upon yourself.

And now further and forever, fare you most happily, my most wished-for and most longed-for Holger.
In haste, at Wandsbek, on the 14th day of February of the year [15]98.
Yours, TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from exile after leaving Denmark in 1597. By February 1598 he was lodged at Wandsbek (see below), the residence of Heinrich Rantzau near Hamburg, where he completed the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica and the Epistolarum astronomicarum liber later that year.
  • Recipient. Oligerus Rosaekrantzius = Holger Rosenkrantz ("the Learned," 1574–1642), Danish nobleman, theologian and scholar, related to Tycho by blood and marriage (consanguineus et affinis). The address renders him vir cordatus — "a man of sense / good judgement"; cf. the same epithet applied to Rosenkrantz elsewhere in the correspondence.
  • Cimbria — the humanist Latin name for the Cimbrian peninsula, i.e. Jutland/Holstein (the Cimbric Chersonese); rendered "Holstein [Cimbria]" since the Austrian baron's marriage "in Cimbria" places it in that region. The exact district is not specified by the text and is not narrowed here.
  • Plagiarius ("Plagiarist") — Tycho's habitual term of abuse for Nicolaus Reimers Bär (Latin Ursus, 1551–1600), the Imperial Mathematician at Prague whom Tycho accused of having stolen and published his geo-heliocentric world-system (in Ursus's Fundamentum astronomicum, 1588). The intensifiers putidissimus et mendacissimus ("most rotten and most lying") are kept at full superlative force. Identification flagged: Ursus is not named in this letter; the equation rests on Tycho's consistent usage of Plagiarius for him in 1597–98 and on the context (a man Tycho means to denounce by letter). The text itself leaves the "Plagiarist" unnamed.
  • Ericus Langius = Erik Lange (1553–1613), Danish nobleman and alchemist, Tycho's friend and the betrothed of Tycho's sister Sophie Brahe; here named as the person who had earlier brought the Austrian baron into Tycho's company (cum mecum … adductus esset).
  • scheda memorialis — "a memorandum-slip" / aide-mémoire that Tycho had given Rosenkrantz on his departure; this letter supplies a point he had forgotten to include.
  • iuxta morem vobis consuetum — "according to the custom usual among you": vobis is read as the second-person plural of the noble/courtly class to which Rosenkrantz belongs (the proper styling and titulature of a baron). Rendered "among you [of the nobility]," the bracketed gloss marking the interpretive expansion.
  • literas Regi destinatas, ad non Regias oblique respondentes — "the letters destined for the King, which obliquely answer the not-royal ones." A compressed and deliberately oblique phrase: letters meant for the King (almost certainly Christian IV of Denmark) that reply indirectly to other, non-royal letters. Crux flagged: the referent of non Regias (the "not-royal" letters being answered) is not specified in the text and is left literal rather than resolved; oblique ("obliquely, indirectly") is Tycho's own hedge and is preserved, not sharpened into a definite construction.
  • critizatas — a Greek-derived coinage (from κριτής / κρίνειν, "to judge, sift") meaning "critically examined, subjected to critical judgement"; here, that Rosenkrantz go over the letters with a critic's eye before returning them. Kept as a flagged technical neologism and glossed rather than smoothed to a single English verb. Paired with glossis marginalibus auctas, "enlarged with marginal glosses" (annotations in the margin).
  • manu propria — "in his own hand": Tycho's autograph subscription. The sole MS witness, however, is a later copy (afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium, so the surviving text is a transcript, not the autograph itself.
  • Dateline & place. Raptim Wandesburgi die 14. Februarij Anni 98 = "In haste, at Wandsbek [Wandesburgum, the Rantzau castle near Hamburg, now in Hamburg], on the 14th day of February of the year [15]98" (O.S.; Denmark and the North-German Protestant correspondents kept the Julian calendar). Raptim ("in haste") preserved. The header date follows the dateline and agrees with the catalogue label (14 February 1598).
