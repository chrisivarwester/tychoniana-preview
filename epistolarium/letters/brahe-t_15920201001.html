The original

NOBILITATE PERILLUSTRI ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO TYCHONI BRAHE DANO DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, ET ARCIS URANIBURG IN INSULA HELLESPONTI DANICI HUÆNA FUNDATORI.
IO. ANTONIVS MAGINVS PATAVINUS MATHEMATICVS
S. P. D.

DIU multumque plurimis ab hinc annis cogitavi (Nobilissime ac doctissime vir) ut viam invenirem expeditam, ac facilem, qua cuiuscunque numeri quamvis magni quadrata radix colligi ex tempore posset. Opus sane arduum, et perquam difficile, in quo quantum insudarim, nemo plane credet, nisi qui ipse aliquando periculum fecit, et saepe quidem a proposito rei difficultas me deterruit. Verum enim vero res tandem ita mihi faeliciter successit, ut plus, quam optarem, invenerim: nam quid in hoc genere calculi praestiterim, tu primum doctissime Brahe, deinde reliqui omnes Matheseos cultores, ad quorum manus haec pervenerint, probe perspicient. Nolo autem hîc in laudandis meis inventis nimis audax studiosis videri, ne momus quispiam propriae laudis parum modestum praeconem me esse cavilletur. Caeterum qualecunque hoc sit opus, tibi, gloriosissimi nominis viro, ac nostrae aetatis Astronomo eminentissimo, grati animi et observantiae erga te singularis caussâ dicare volui, idque maximis de causis. Primum enim tu ipse es, cui maxime debeo, quod amicitiae tuae fores tam amanter ac humaniter mihi aperuisti, atque mihi plus honoris tribuisti, quam eruditio (si qua est) tenuis mea, ac perexigua postulabat, cum mihi tuum secundum librum de recentioribus mundi Phaenomenis per Danum quendam studiosum misisti, simulque per eundem, quid de illis sentirem, quaesivisti; nec non etiam quod meae Epistolae humanissime respondisti, multa mecum de rebus Astronomicis disserendo. Deinde tu unus es, cui hanc meam Tetragonicam tabulam acceptam fore maxime confido, propterea quod ipsa faelicissime uti poteris ad eliciendos inaequalitatum motuum planetarum angulos, et promptiori quidem compendio, quam per Tangentium, et Secantium tabulas. Tandem quia tu ille es praestantissimus Astronomiae instaurator, cui plurimum debet nostra aetas, et debebunt posteri omnes, quando quidem non ego solum, sed omnes fere sincerae mentis viri de tuis Astronomicis fundamentis spem eam conceperunt, ut a te solo Astronomiae restitutionem avide expectent. Etenim tu tales miro artificio, ac invento caelestium sphaerarum confinxisti hypotyposes, quales probe sufficere posse ad tuenda phaenomena, et apparentias coelitus animadversas, ac ad definienda coelestium luminum loca ad quaevis tempora, non ambigendum est. Hortor itaque te, obtestorque, exiguo hoc munusculo tibi oblato, ut, quod faeliciter exorsus es, et multo jam tempore in hoc doctrinae genere moliris, tandem absolvas: namque nullum alium rectius et faelicius id praestare posse quam te, qui ingenio es acerrimo natus ad Astronomiae collapsae illustrationem, recte ac sincere judico, idque sperant omnes. Etenim si tuum laboriosissimum ac praestantissimum Theatrum absolutum quamprimum nobis communicabis, iis profecto, qui olim motuum correctionem tentarunt, et qui nunc pertentant, omnem eripies gloriam, atque in posterum tentaturis omnem praeripies occasionem, et materiam.

Scio quidem post tuorum laborum editionem mea scripta correctionem desideratura, sed tantum abest, ut haec me perturbent, ut potius desiderem quamprimum tuas speculationes publici juris fieri: nam confestim Ephemerides meas corrigam, quarum secunda editio avide expectatur, vel, si opus erit, de novo alias pertexam. Illud etiam per hanc epistolam tibi significare liceat, me Patavii, et Bononiae poli investigationem, ac Martis, eorumque affixorum siderum meridianas altitudines, quas tantopere me observare optabas, hactenus praetermisisse, neque ob id tamen negligentiae nomine a te accusari velim, cum hoc praestandi mihi a variis negociorum fluctibus, qui me oppresserunt, erepta sit facultas, accedente etiam loci incommoditate, praesertim Patavii, de quo tibi idem iste testari poterit. Sextantem illum Astronomicum, quem ex tuo invento et in tui gratiam Patavii fabrefieri curavi, exactissime coelo respondere ad singula minuta recte percepi, quoniam easdem distantias adunguem nonnullarum fixarum, quas in tuo catalogo ex tuis observationibus notasti, deprehendi. Atque hunc jam Bononiam mecum exportavi, ubi observationes plurimas in tui commodum perficere tentabo. Quinetiam et hoc te latere nolo, Nobilissimum ac doctissimum virum Paulum Bonfilium patritium Bononiensem, tuique studiosissimum, fabrefieri curasse propriis expensis Quadrantem ex ligno, et metallo decempedalem, qui non tam minuta, quam minutorum partes in observationibus exhibebit. Idem Nobilissimus vir Sextantem quoque ejusdem magnitudinis habere studet, quibus Instrumentis in summa turri, quam in suâ domo habet, observationes coelestes in tui gratiam perficere commodius valeamus, de quibus omnibus suis loco et tempore certior fies. Hîc finem epistolae huic meae imponam, te enixe rogans, ut, si quid possum, tuo jure me utare. Vale vir praeclarissime, Astronomorumque decus, atque Astronomiam perficere tuis lucubrationibus et vigiliis perge, et Spartam, quam accepisti (ut ajunt) orna.

Iterumque diu, et faeliciter vale.
Bononiae Kalendis Februarii. M. D. XCII.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man of most illustrious nobility and most outstanding erudition, Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup and founder of the castle of Uraniborg on the Danish island of Hven.
Giovanni Antonio Magini of Padua, mathematician, sends heartiest greeting.

Long and hard, for very many years past, have I pondered (most noble and most learned sir) how I might find a ready and easy method by which the square root of any number, however large, could be obtained on the spot. A task indeed arduous, and exceedingly difficult, in which no one will at all believe how much I sweated, unless he be one who has himself at some time made the trial; and often, indeed, the difficulty of the matter deterred me from my purpose. But in truth the thing has at last so happily succeeded for me that I have found out more than I could have wished: for what I have accomplished in this kind of calculation, you first, most learned Brahe, and then all the rest of the cultivators of mathematics into whose hands these things shall come, will rightly perceive. Yet I do not wish here, in praising my own inventions, to seem too bold to scholars, lest some Momus carp that I am too immodest a herald of my own praise. But whatever this work may be, I wished to dedicate it to you — a man of most glorious name, and the most eminent astronomer of our age — out of a grateful spirit and of my singular regard toward you, and that for the greatest reasons. For first, you yourself are the one to whom I owe it most, that you opened the doors of your friendship to me so lovingly and courteously, and granted me more honour than my slender and very scant erudition (if there be any) deserved, when you sent me your second book on the more recent Phenomena of the world by a certain Danish scholar, and at the same time, through the same man, inquired what I thought of them; and likewise that you most courteously answered my letter, discoursing with me at length on astronomical matters. Next, you are the one man to whom I am most confident this Tetragonic table of mine will be welcome, because you will be able to use it most happily for eliciting the angles of the inequalities of the planets' motions, and indeed by a readier short-cut than by the tables of Tangents and Secants. Last, because you are that most outstanding restorer of astronomy, to whom our age owes most, and to whom all posterity will owe most — since indeed not I alone, but almost all men of sincere mind have conceived this hope from your astronomical foundations, that from you alone they eagerly await the restoration of astronomy. For you have, with wonderful skill and invention, fashioned such hypotyposes of the celestial spheres as — it is not to be doubted — can well suffice to save the phenomena and the appearances observed in the heavens, and to define the places of the celestial luminaries for any times whatever. I urge you therefore, and entreat you, by this little gift offered to you, that what you have happily begun, and have now for a long time been labouring at in this kind of learning, you may at last complete: for that no other can perform it more rightly and more happily than you — you who were born with a most keen genius for the illustration of fallen astronomy — I judge rightly and sincerely, and so all men hope. For if you will share with us, as soon as possible, your most laborious and most outstanding Theatre once completed, you will surely snatch away all glory from those who once attempted the correction of the motions, and who now keep attempting it, and you will forestall every occasion and matter from those who shall attempt it hereafter.

I know indeed that, after the publication of your works, my own writings will stand in need of correction; but so far is this from troubling me that I rather desire your speculations to be made public as soon as possible: for I shall at once correct my Ephemerides, of which a second edition is eagerly awaited, or, if need be, weave others anew. This too let me be allowed to make known to you by this letter: that I have hitherto neglected, at Padua and at Bologna, the investigation of the pole, and the meridian altitudes of Mars and of the fixed stars near them, which you so greatly wished me to observe; nor on that account, however, would I be accused by you under the name of negligence, since the power of performing this has been wrested from me by the various surges of business that have overwhelmed me, with the inconvenience of the place added besides, especially at Padua — to which this same man will be able to testify to you. That Astronomical sextant which, after your invention and for your sake, I had made at Padua, I have rightly perceived to answer the heavens most exactly to each single minute, since I found the same distances, to a hair's breadth, of several of the fixed stars which you have noted in your catalogue from your observations. And this I have now carried with me to Bologna, where I shall attempt to complete very many observations for your benefit. Moreover, this too I would not have hidden from you: that the most noble and most learned man Paolo Bonfilio, a patrician of Bologna and most devoted to you, has had made at his own expense a Quadrant of wood and metal, ten feet in size, which will exhibit in observations not so much the minutes as the parts of minutes. The same most noble man is eager to have also a Sextant of the same magnitude, so that with these Instruments, in the top of a tower which he has in his house, we may be the better able to complete celestial observations for your sake; about all of which you shall be informed in their place and time. Here I shall put an end to this letter of mine, earnestly asking you that, if I can do anything, you use me by your right. Farewell, most distinguished man and ornament of astronomers; and go on to perfect astronomy by your lucubrations and your vigils, and adorn the Sparta which you have received (as they say).

And again, long and happily, farewell.
At Bologna, on the Kalends of February [1 February]. 1592.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Io. Antonius Maginus Patavinus = Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), professor of mathematics at Bologna from 1588 (the chair Galileo also sought), astronomer, cartographer, and author of widely-used Ephemerides. He styles himself Patavinus ("of Padua") by origin, though he writes from Bologna. The address-superscription gives Tycho's full titulature: Dominus de Knudstrup (Lord of Knudstrup, his ancestral seat in Scania) and founder of Uraniborg on Hven.
  • insula Hellesponti Danici Huaena — "the island of Hven in the Danish Hellespont": a humanist conceit for the Øresund (the Sound), styled the "Danish Hellespont." Kept as the figure it is.
  • Greeting. S. P. D. = salutem plurimam dicit, "sends heartiest greeting".
  • The dedication. The letter is the dedicatory epistle prefixed to Magini's square-root work — hanc meam Tetragonicam tabulam, rendered "this Tetragonic table of mine" — i.e. his Tabula tetragonica, a table for extracting square roots, published Venice 1592. Magini commends it to Tycho as a computing aid "for eliciting the angles of the inequalities of the planets' motions … by a readier short-cut than by the tables of Tangents and Secants." This places the letter in the orbit of Tycho's own prosthaphaeretic computing interests, though that term does not appear in the source and is not imported here.
  • tuum secundum librum de recentioribus mundi Phaenomenis — "your second book on the more recent Phenomena of the world": Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis liber secundus (Uraniborg, 1588), sent to Magini, with Tycho's request for Magini's opinion, per Danum quendam studiosum ("by a certain Danish scholar" — unnamed here).
  • Momus — in Greek mythology the god of mockery and fault-finding; "lest some Momus carp" = lest some captious critic. Kept as the proper name.
  • Astronomy — technical terms: Astronomiae instaurator / restitutio / illustrationem … collapsae — Tycho as the restorer of fallen astronomy, the restitutio astronomiae programme, rendered "restoration / restorer of astronomy"; hypotyposes caelestium sphaerarum — "hypotyposes of the celestial spheres," i.e. his world-model (the term hypotyposis, an outline/representation, is kept); tuenda phaenomena … apparentias — "to save the phenomena and the appearances" (the standard salvare apparentias idiom); inaequalitatum motuum planetarum angulos — "the angles of the inequalities of the planets' motions"; meridianas altitudines — "meridian altitudes"; poli investigationem — "the investigation of the pole" (latitude determination); catalogus … fixarum — Tycho's star catalogue.
  • Theatrum — "Theatre": Magini's term for the great projected work Tycho is labouring at (his Theatrum astronomicum / the grand instauration), capitalised as in the source; rendered literally "Theatre" and flagged, since it is Magini's metaphor for Tycho's magnum opus rather than a fixed title.
  • Instruments: Sextans Astronomicus — "Astronomical sextant," built ex tuo invento ("after your invention") at Padua; Quadrans … decempedalis ex ligno et metallo — a quadrant "ten feet in size, of wood and metal," resolving "not so much the minutes as the parts of minutes." ad singula minuta / minutorum partes — arc-minutes and fractions of arc-minutes.
  • Paolo Bonfioli. Paulus Bonfilius patritius Bononiensis = Paolo Bonfioli, a Bolognese patrician and astronomy enthusiast ("most devoted to you"), who funded the large quadrant and sought a matching sextant, with a tower-top observing platform in his house. (Spelling of the surname normalised in the apparatus; the Latin form Bonfilius is kept in the Source and translation.)
  • Ephemerides … secunda editio — Magini's Ephemerides coelestium motuum; he undertakes to recompute them once Tycho's results appear. de novo alias pertexam — "weave others anew" (the weaving metaphor kept).
  • Spartam, quam accepisti (ut ajunt) orna — "adorn the Sparta which you have received (as they say)": the proverb Σπάρταν ἔλαχες, ταύταν κόσμει ("Sparta is your portion; do it credit"), via Erasmus, Adagia (ii.5.1) — "make the most of the lot assigned to you." Magini's ut ajunt ("as they say") flags it as the proverb it is; kept and glossed.
  • adunguem — ad unguem, "to a hair's breadth" (literally "to the fingernail," the sculptor's idiom for exactitude).
  • Calendar. Bononiae Kalendis Februarii M.D.XCII = "At Bologna, on the Kalends of February (1 February), 1592." Magini, a Catholic in Bologna, wrote New Style (Gregorian), as the edition label marks ("n.s."); the dateline and the catalogued date agree on 1 February 1592. The Roman-calendar idiom Kalendis Februarii is rendered with the idiom and glossed to the civil date by the edition's convention No in-letter date discrepancy.
  • Edition note. The edition's edition field names the Astronomiae instaurata mechanica; this dedicatory epistle to Magini's Tabula tetragonica is also printed among the Epistolae in Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII. Both are recorded; no MS witness is listed.
  • Correction. Corrected 'spectationes' (observations) to 'speculationes' (speculations/theoretical works) in the Latin per Dreyer, TBOO V p.128, l.14, and updated the corresponding English clause, which had shifted Magini's request from Tycho's theoretical system to mere observational data.
  • Correction. Corrected the run-together 'DANICIHUAeNA' to the two words 'DANICI HUAeNA' in the dedication heading's Latin per Dreyer, TBOO V p.127, l.4 (no change to the English).
  • Correction. Corrected the invented surname 'Bonfioli' to 'Bonfilio' in the English, matching the Latin 'Bonfilium' already present and confirmed at Dreyer, TBOO V p.128, l.30 (no change to the Latin).

Collated the full letter (address heading, all three main paragraphs, the sextant/quadrant/Bonfilio passage, closing) against Dreyer TBOO V pp.126-129 read as page images. Two text error found in the transcribed original against Dreyer's print (a glued heading word, and 'spectationes' silently substituted for Dreyer's 'speculationes' — a real word-level corruption with mild semantic consequence) plus one proper-name mistranslation (Bonfilium rendered 'Bonfioli' rather than 'Bonfilio'). Per the grading rule, any text error caps the grade at C regardless of individual severity; this letter was self-graded A, which the collation does not support.

3 defects found:
- text error (low). the transcribed original glues 'DANICI' and 'HUAeNA' into a single word; Dreyer prints them as two words. The English sense (island of Hven) survives, but the Latin transcription is corrupted. Dreyer prints: IN INSULA HELLESPONTI DANICI HUAeNA FUNDATORI (p.127 line 4 — two separate words).
- text error (medium). the transcribed original substitutes 'spectationes' (viewings/observations) for Dreyer's actual word 'speculationes' (theoretical works/speculations) — a genuine word-level corruption that shifts what Magini is asking Tycho to publish, from his theoretical astronomical system to mere observational data. Dreyer prints: ut potius defiderem quamprimum tuas fpeculationes publici juris fieri (p.128 line 14 — 'speculationes', not 'spectationes').
- mistranslation (low). Dreyer's and the transcribed original's Latin both read 'Bonfilium' (from 'Bonfilius'), which Italianizes as 'Bonfilio' (i.e. 'Bonfiglio'); the translation instead invents the surname 'Bonfioli', not supported by the Latin. At: “the most noble and most learned man Paolo Bonfioli”.

Repaired. Collated all three defects directly against the Dreyer page images (TBOO V pp.126-129): p.128 l.14 confirms 'fpeculationes', p.127 l.4 confirms the two separate words 'DANICI HUAeNA', and p.128 l.30 confirms 'Paulum Bonfilium'. All three are genuine, now corrected at the appropriate layer (Latin, or English, or both per defect type). No remaining crux.
