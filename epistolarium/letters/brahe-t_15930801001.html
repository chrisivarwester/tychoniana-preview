The original

MAGNIFICO ET GENEROSO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO IN KNUDSTRUP, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO SUMMA CUM OBSERVANTIA PERPETUO COLENDO. S. P.

ETSI diuturni istius mei silentij multas easque iustissimas excusationes, Generosissime ac Nobiliss. Domine, afferre possem, tamen culpam fateri consultius esse duco et ad veniam compendiosius, præsertim cum mihi res sit cum amico nec rigido nec inexorabili, ne si forte in me purgando putidiusculus essem, in aliam denuo offensam incautus relaberer; quare silentio quodam novo veteris silentij crimen refellam illudque saltem maiorem in modum deprecabor, ne ab immutata animi mei voluntate (quæ certe eadem tuo summo merito atque humanitate incredibili erga te est eritque perpetuo, quæ antea extitit) hoc profectum existimes. Quod si fideiussores res poscat, ipsi, uti confido, Mag. Domini Legati pro mea in te fide et constantia nihil ἀυτήν veriti, non gravate spondebunt. Diploma Regium, ut voluisti, expediendum curavi, quod unâ cum versibus Regijs, Regia etiam manu, ut efflagitasti, exaratis accipies. Ex utrisque Sereniss. Regis nostri ac Domini Clementiss. de te et tuis scriptis cuiusmodi sit iudicium, non obscure cognosces. Profecto te in oculis fert atque in cœlum laudibus effert, quoties de te mentio incidit. Incidit autem sæpissime, quoties nimirum aut iter illud suum Danicum recenset aut de Physiologia, præsertim Mathesi, verba facit, tum nunquam non prædicatur Uraniburgicum istud Sydus. CRAIGIUM medicum ἀνταγωνιστήν illum tuum eatenus ὑγιαίνειν iussi, ut argumentis tecum non convitijs certaret, quod et posthac hominem facturum spero. De BUCHANANO alias: ne si forte omnia simul, scribendi materia utrique desit. Cætera ex Magcis Dominis Legatis, qui quanta fide, prudentia atque industria, negotia sua hîc gesserint, omnes bonos conscios habent. Vale Generosiss. ac Nobiliss. Domine, omniaque in te a me studia atque officia, quæ quidem ego præstare potero, ita velim expectes, ut illa nulla alia re nisi vitæ interitu terminari posse certo tibi persuadeas. Datæ ex Regia Stæ Crucis Cal. Augusti 1593.

Tuæ Magtiæ studiosiss.
atque tibique obsequentiss.
Petrus Iunius a Setoun.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and noble Lord Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup, his lord and friend ever to be honoured with the utmost respect. Hearty greeting.

Although I could put forward many — and those most just — excuses for this long silence of mine, most noble and high-born Lord, I judge it more advisable, and a shorter road to pardon, to confess the fault; the more so since I have to do with a friend neither stiff nor implacable, and lest, if perchance I were a little too fastidious in clearing myself, I should incautiously slip back into yet another offence. I shall therefore refute the charge of my old silence by a certain new silence, and at least beg most earnestly for this: that you not think it [proceeded] from any change in my mind's good will toward you (which assuredly, by your supreme merit and incredible kindness, is and ever shall be the same toward you as it was before). And if the matter should call for sureties, the Honourable Lords the Ambassadors themselves will, as I trust, fearing nothing †ἀυτήν† [as to her/it], stand pledge ungrudgingly for my faith and constancy toward you. The Royal Diploma I have had drawn up for dispatch, as you wished, which you will receive together with the Royal verses, set down — as you urgently requested — in the King's own hand as well. From both of these you will recognise, not obscurely, what kind of judgement our Most Serene King and Most Clement Lord holds of you and of your writings. Truly he carries you in his eyes and exalts you to heaven with praises, whenever mention of you arises. And it arises very often — namely whenever he recounts that Danish journey of his or speaks of natural philosophy, and especially of mathematics; then never is that Star of Uraniborg not proclaimed. As for Craig the physician, that antagonist [ἀνταγωνιστήν] of yours, I bade him fare well [ὑγιαίνειν] only so far as that he should contend with you by arguments, not by abuse — which I hope the man will do hereafter as well. Of Buchanan, another time: lest, if perchance [I set down] everything at once, matter for writing should fail us both. The rest [you may learn] from the Honourable Lords the Ambassadors, who have all good men as witnesses with how great faith, prudence, and diligence they have conducted their affairs here. Farewell, most noble and high-born Lord; and all those zealous services and offices toward you that I shall be able to render — I would have you expect them on these terms: that you persuade yourself for certain they can be ended by no other thing than the close of [my] life. Given from the Royal Palace of Holyrood, on the Kalends of August [1 August] 1593.

Most devoted to Your Magnificence
and most obedient to you,
Peter Young of Seton.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Petrus Iunius a Setoun = Sir Peter Young of Seton (Easter Seaton), 1544–1628, Scottish humanist, co-tutor (with George Buchanan) to the young James VI, royal almoner and diplomat. He had served as James's envoy to Denmark in 1586–87 (the "iter illud suum Danicum," the "Danish journey," recalled below in fact refers to the King's own 1590 journey to Denmark and his visit to Hven on 20 March 1590). The signature form a Setoun renders his territorial designation "of Seton."
  • Addressee titulature. Domino in Knudstrup — "Lord of Knudstrup" (Knutstorp, the Brahe family seat in Scania), Tycho's patrimonial designation. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting," the edition's convention).
  • Sereniss. Regis nostri … Domini Clementiss. — James VI of Scotland (later James I of England), Peter Young's former pupil and patron. The King's high regard for Tycho is documented; he had visited Uraniborg in person in March 1590.
  • Diploma Regium — "the Royal Diploma": the royal privilege/letter James VI granted Tycho (the safe-conduct-cum-printing-privilege Tycho had solicited). Rendered "Royal Diploma," the period term kept.
  • versibus Regijs, Regia etiam manu … exaratis — "the Royal verses, set down in the King's own hand": James VI's celebrated Latin verses in Tycho's honour, autograph. exarare ("to plough out, write out") kept as "set down."
  • Magci Domini Legati / Magcis Dominis Legatis — "the Honourable Lords the Ambassadors" (Magnifici Domini Legati; the abbreviation Mag. / Magcis expanded). The Scottish embassy then concluding business in Denmark; their precise membership is not named in the letter and is left unspecified here.
  • Uraniburgicum … Sydus — "that Star of Uraniborg," a courtly epithet for Tycho himself (the De nova stella discoverer turned into a "star"); Sydus = sidus, the chancery -y- spelling kept in the source block.
  • The Craig affair. CRAIGIUM medicum … ἀνταγωνιστήν illum tuum = John Craig (d. 1620), Scottish physician (later physician to James VI), Tycho's learned antagonist in the dispute over the comet of 1577 and Tycho's cosmology; Craig later apologised. Young reports having urged Craig to dispute argumentis … non convitijs ("by arguments, not by abuse").
  • George Buchanan. De BUCHANANO alias = "Of Buchanan, another time": George Buchanan (1506–1582), the great Scottish humanist and James VI's principal tutor (with Young); the subject is deferred. (Buchanan being dead since 1582, the matter is presumably literary — likely his works or reputation — but the letter does not say, so it is left open.)
  • Greek.
  • nihil ἀυτήν veriti — "fearing nothing †ἀυτήν†." The accusative form as printed (ἀυτήν, with a smooth-breathing alpha) is anomalous: read most likely as αὐτήν "her / it" (feminine/the matter), making the sense "fearing nothing of it / in that regard." The construction (vereri + accusative of the thing feared) is sound, but the referent is unclear and the printed accentuation irregular; transliterated, kept verbatim in the source block, and flagged as a crux rather than smoothed. Transliteration: autḗn / autḗn.
  • ἀνταγωνιστήν (antagōnistḗn, acc.) — "antagonist, adversary"; in apposition to Craigium. Kept and rendered "antagonist."
  • ὑγιαίνειν (hygiainein) — literally "to be in good health"; here the epistolary leave-taking idiom χαίρειν / ὑγιαίνειν κελεύειν, "to bid (someone) farewell / good health." Rendered "I bade him fare well," with the play on "fare well … only so far as" preserved: Young's good wishes to Craig are conditional on Craig's debating fairly.
  • Tone / lexis. putidiusculus — a rare diminutive of putidus, "a little too affected / fastidious / over-nice (in self-justification)"; rendered "a little too fastidious." silentio quodam novo veteris silentij crimen refellam — the studied paradox ("refute the charge of old silence by a new silence," i.e. by not belabouring excuses) is kept as a paradox.
  • Dateline / place. Datae ex Regia Stae Crucis Cal. Augusti 1593 = "Given from the Royal Palace of Holyrood (Regia Sanctae Crucis, the Palace of Holyroodhouse, Edinburgh), on the Kalends of August = 1 August 1593." Scotland was on the Julian calendar (O.S.) in 1593; rendered O.S. with no conversion. The catalogue label ("Holyrood Palace (Edinburgh)," 1593-08-01) agrees with the dateline; Sancta Crux = Holyrood is identified here. The Roman-calendar idiom is kept and glossed by the edition's convention
  • Subscription. Tuae Magtiae studiosiss. atque tibique obsequentiss. = "Most devoted to Your Magnificence and most obedient to you" (Magtiae = Magnificentiae, abbreviation expanded).
