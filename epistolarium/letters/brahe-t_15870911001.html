English translation machine translation, Probable

To the steadfast, Our trusty and well-beloved Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup. — Wilhelm, by the grace of God Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda, etc. — Our gracious greeting beforehand, Our trusty and well-beloved. We have received your letter dated the 18th of January together with the observations. That you have now, at our request, sent the same so willingly to us — for this we graciously thank you. We may not, however, conceal from you that at that time we were burdened with other business and had travelled away to other places, whereby we were hindered from answering you. But as soon as we returned hither again, we compared [conferirt] your observations with our own, and found with particular astonishment that they agree with one another in the distances and declinations, even to fractions of a minute — which has never before been heard of. But that they differ [discrepirn] from one another by five minutes in the longitudes causes us no wonder, since we are well aware that neither Hipparchus, Ptolemy, nor any of all the others before [us] ever knew the true places of the stars to 5 minutes, nor on account of the smallness of the armillae could have known [them]; for with the armillae one could scarcely discern 10 minutes — as is plainly [evident] from the observations of the fixed stars — to say nothing of what else, on account of the pulling-apart of the circles [propter Circulorum hinc inde distractionem], may have been wanting in observing, since the circles, in such a connection, soon give way in the handling. Of our own observations we cannot at this time depart, for our mathematician, even with the perpendicular of the quadrant corrected, has repeatedly found [them] exact — not only by the clock [per Horologium], but also this past January several times observing Venus, which he, just as you [did], observed by day alongside the Sun. Yet we questioned our mathematician earnestly about this, and then he could not conceal from us how he had at that time found by Venus that your motion of the Sun [Motus Solis], which you sent us in your ephemeris, around the eighth degree of ♒ Aquarius, is too slow by several minutes; for at the end of January he found the place of the Sun [locum Solis] — also by our quadrant — no otherwise, and accepted it no otherwise, than as it stands in your ephemeris (which he has also duly extended [iusto modo … extendiret] to this year). None the less, Venus would yield no other place of the fixed stars: he supposes that you attribute somewhat too much to the refractions. He supposes also that, since you tracked the time by the equatorial armillae [tempus per Armillas Aequatorias indagirt], whereas the refraction of the Sun occurs in the vertical circle [in Circulo Verticali] and not according to the longitude of the ecliptic [secundum Longitudinem Eclipticae], it could easily be that you took the time somewhat too short — which could not happen if one tracked the time by the azimuths of the Sun [tempus per Azimutha Solis]. Yet, because you tested it by Venus around the autumnal equinox in the morning, he could not properly know wherein it is lacking. However that may be, we know not how to depart from our observations, which we have made with no less the highest diligence than you [made] yours. And since we have now for many years been occupied with the observations, and have gladly furthered astronomy through ourselves [as well], we have graciously commanded our mathematician to calculate the observations further and, together with the demonstrations and the methods of observing [Demonstrationibus et modis obseruandi], to publish [them], so that everyone may see our diligence and labour — as indeed he has already calculated nearly 200 stars, and has more than 200 still to calculate. He is, however, to make no mention of this trifling and slight discrepancy between ours and yours, nor to spread abroad [spargiren] yours. But whatever else you have communicated to us, he will know how to praise according to its worth [pro dignitate] and to render back to you with honourable mention of your name [cum honorifica nominis mentione restituiren]. Thus you will then be the better and more diligently able to inquire into our observations, and the matter will also the sooner find belief with others, since each one keeps to his own observations and does not change anything for another's favour [in gratiam alterius]. We also intend, after the completion of the work, to set up our instruments anew in another fashion; and if we then find that we have erred by the 5 minutes, we shall gladly correct ourselves — although this discrepancy is hardly worth making so much fuss about, and cannot be greatly regarded by those who have practised themselves in observations. This we did not wish to conceal from you upon your letter; and should there be anything further in it, our mathematician shall answer you thereon. And we are at all times with gracious good will quite well disposed toward you. Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on the 11th of September, in the year [15]87. WILHELM, LANDGRAVE OF HESSE.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), "Wilhelm the Wise," himself a serious observational astronomer who maintained an observatory and instrument workshop at Kassel; with Tycho he carried on a sustained technical correspondence. The full titulature (Graue zu Catzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain und Nidda) is the standard Hessian style, preserved by the edition's convention
  • Recipient address. Dem Vesten vnserm lieben besondern Tychoni Brahe, Herrn zu Knudstrup — "To the steadfast (Vesten = Festen, the address-epithet for the lower nobility), Our trusty and well-beloved Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup" (Tycho's family seat in Scania). Vnsern gnedigen gruß zuuor and Vester lieber besonder rendered per the fixed glossary.
  • The "mathematician" (Mathematicus). Wilhelm's court mathematician is Christoph Rothmann, Tycho's principal correspondent at Kassel; the Landgrave speaks throughout in the first person but reports Rothmann's technical findings. Rothmann is named only by office, not by name, in the letter.
  • The letter being answered. ewer schreiben de Dato den 18. Ianuaris = "your letter dated 18 January" — this is the incoming Tycho→Kassel letter (with enclosed observations and ephemeris) that the Landgrave is here answering, not the date of the present letter. No internal contradiction with the dateline: the present letter is dated 11 September [15]87 at its close; the 18 January is a back-reference. The catalogue label ("1587. 11. september. Kassel") agrees with the dateline, so the header carries 11 September 1587 as written.
  • Technical terms (kept on the controlled list,–5.2). distantiae → distances; declinationes → declinations; longitudines → longitudes; vera Stellarum loca / Locum Stellarum Fixarum → true places of the stars / place of the fixed stars; Armillae (Aequatoriae) → (equatorial) armillae; Quadrans / Quadrantis perpendiculo → quadrant / perpendicular of the quadrant; refractio → refraction; Circulus Verticalis → vertical circle; Azimutha → azimuths; Motus Solis / locus Solis → motion / place of the Sun; Ephemeris → ephemeris; Demonstrationes / modi obseruandi → demonstrations / methods of observing. partibus Minuti rendered "fractions of a minute" (lit. "in parts of a minute," i.e. to sub-arcminute precision).
  • The Latin code-switches (a feature, not noise). The letter constantly drops Latin technical phrases into the German matrix — in distantijs et Declinationibus, etiam in partibus Minuti; propter Armillarum paruitatem; etiam correcto Quadrantis perpendiculo; per Horologium; circa octauum gradum; in fine Ianuaris; iusto modo … extendiret; secundum Longitudinem Eclipticae; per Venerem circa Aequinoctium autumnale; pro dignitate; cum honorifica nominis mentione; in gratiam alterius — and likewise Latinate German verbs (conferirt, discrepirn, indagirt, calculiren, publiciren, spargiren, communiciret, restituiren, inquiriren, mutiret, corrigiren). These are rendered into English with the Latin italicised on first/notable occurrence by the edition's convention; the register (a learned prince writing technically) is preserved.
  • Symbol. circa octauum gradum ♒ — "around the eighth degree of ♒ Aquarius." The glyph ♒ is kept in the Source block and named "Aquarius" in the translation, by the edition's convention
  • Substance. The exchange is the well-known Kassel–Hven instrument-precision dispute: agreement to sub-arcminute in distances/declinations but a c. 5′ disagreement in longitudes. Rothmann's diagnosis — that the error may lie in Tycho's solar-longitude/refraction handling and in deriving time by the equatorial armillae rather than by the Sun's azimuths — and the Landgrave's intent to publish his own star catalogue (≈200 stars computed, >200 to go, with demonstrations and methods) are reported in the principals' own terms; no claim is sharpened.
  • Editorial markers ("A"). The transcription carries a stray uppercase "A" at numerous points — at the end of the address/titulature lines (KNUDSTRUP.A, ETC.A), mid-phrase (jegen euchA, gewesenA, So baldtA, dannA, vielfeltigA, bey tagkA, verhaltenA, esA, ObseruationibusA, Ais, gnedigklichA, lassenA, werdenA, DasA, wöllenA, VndA, gantzA, SeptembrisA), and at the close where words run together (…wol gewogen.Datum…, …Anno 87.WILHELM…). These are apparatus/segmentation anchors from the source transcription, not part of the text; they are reproduced verbatim in the Source block (by the edition's convention's "reproduce glyphs as printed") and ignored in the translation. They do not affect sense.
  • Orthography (Early New High German chancery). zuuor = zuvor; ewer/ewere/ewerigen = euer/eure/eurigen; jhr/jhn = ihr/ihn; vbereinstimmen = übereinstimmen; sintemahl = sintemal ("since, inasmuch as"); geschwiegen = "to say nothing of"; baldt = bald; iust = "exactly, just"; as 200 = als 200 ("than 200"); disse = diese; Vestung = Festung ("fortress").
  • Calendar / place. Datum … Cassel … am 11. Septembris, Anno 87 = "Given … [at] Kassel … on 11 September, the year [15]87" (O.S.; Protestant Hesse kept Old Style). Datum = "Given" (the chancery dating verb; cf. Actum,).
  • Correction. English corrected: 'we cannot at this time proceed [to write]... found [it] exact' now reads 'we cannot at this time depart... found [them] exact', matching the letter's own parallel idiom later in the text ('we know not how to depart from our observations'). No Dreyer extract exists for this letter (Wilhelm IV to Tycho, not one of Tycho's own letters in TBOO); the fix rests on internal textual evidence, not edition collation.

The translation and its checking

Grade Probable · not collated against Dreyer's pages; defects the audit noted: text error (1), mistranslation (3).

Not collated against Dreyer — no page reference for this letter, so the grade is capped at Probable however sound the translation looks. the edition gives no page reference (one of the 49 letters without a Dreyer extract); audited the published English against the transcribed original alone, clause-by-clause, including all titles, the five-minute/ten-minute longitude-discrepancy figures, the Venus-by-day verification method, the Aquarius degree, the 200-star calculation tally, and the closing date. One mistranslation found (detailed above), identified by internal contradiction with a later, correctly-translated occurrence of the same German idiom in the same letter — not a Dreyer-collation-dependent finding. By the audit's rule, any mistranslation that changes what a scholar would take away caps the grade at C regardless of collation status.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). "[von etwas] schreitten" here means to depart from / renounce, not "to proceed [in writing]" about it; the very same idiom recurs later in this letter ('wissen wir nicht von vnsern Obseruationibus... abzutretten') and is there correctly rendered 'we know not how to depart from our observations'. The causal 'dann' clause that follows (the mathematician found them exact) only makes sense as a reason to stand by the observations, not as a reason to delay writing about them. The mistranslation reverses Wilhelm's rhetorical stance in this sentence, from confidently defending his own data to sounding unprepared to discuss it. At: “Of our own observations we cannot at this time proceed [to write], for our mathematician, even with the perpendicular of”.

Repaired. No Dreyer extract was available for this letter (no page reference), so the text-error check does not apply; this is a mistranslation defect concerning the German source, resolved from the letter's own internal evidence. The idiom 'von [X] schreitten' (depart from X) is confirmed by its exact structural parallel later in the same letter, 'von vnsern Observationibus... abzutretten', which the existing translation already correctly renders as 'depart from'. The surrounding causal logic (the mathematician's finding the observations exact as grounds to stand by them, not as grounds to delay discussing them) corroborates the correction. Nothing else in the letter was altered.
