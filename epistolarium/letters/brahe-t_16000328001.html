TYCHO BRAHE AD IOHANNEM IESSENIUM A IESSEN.

S. P.

CLARISSIME Domine Doctor IESSENI, amice plurimum semper colende, scripsit quidam domesticus noster, quem nuper Pilsnae in Aula habui, in rebus meis solicitantem, se a praecipuo quodam Saae Caesae Matis, Domini nostri Clementissimi, Consiliario subintellexisse, URSUM quendam Dithmarsicum supplicationem iam pridem de me conquerentem Consiliarijs porrexisse, sed ignominiose, acri adhibitâ obiurgatione, reiectam: cumque is, qui haec mihi insinuavit, nihil praeterea de contentis supplicationis istius addiderit, et idem Pilsnâ in Patriam suam, quae ad Westphaliam est, abierit, nec eius reditum intra tres vel quatuor menses expectem, ac nihilominus mea intersit tempestive scire, quid impudens iste de me conqueri ausus fuerit, cum illum nullo pacto, imo ne verbulo quopiam, sive per me sive per alios, toto tempore quo in Bohemiâ sum, laeserim, utut iam antea intolerandas ab ipso perpessus iniurias atque calumnias, quas in isto famoso, quod ante triennium Pragae contra me in publicum edidit scripto (quo putidius et vanius, cordatorum iudicio, hoc seculum vix vidit) conglomerare non erubuit; sed peculiari consilio, tum quoque ob graviorum occupationum distractionem, omnia, ac si ea ignorarem, dissimulavi: et certe, si mihi obviam fieret URSUS iste, eum non nôssem, nec mihi constitit, an is interea Pragae, an vero alibi fuerit, nam de ipso percontari non dignatus sum, ut nisi eius supra modum perfricta frons et scurrilitas, mentiendique inverecundia mihi non alias constarent, mirari satis non possem, quid ille de me, ita taciturno, nullâque molestiâ illum afficiente, querelae loco, apud Amplissimos Dominos Consiliarios adferre possit: Neque enim quidpiam habet, quod veraciter dicat, nisi hoc ipsum obijcere velit, quod taceam, atque ex alto ipsius nugas hactenus despexerim, suo tamen forte tempore in ipsum retorquendas, quamvis per eas non tam meae famae atque existimationi (quae mille eiuscemodi Sycophanticos allatratus non moratur) nocuerit, quam quod suam petulantem dicacitatem et scurrilem naturam, convitijs et maledictis scatentem, toti Mundo omnibusque sincere iudicantibus, suo se iudicio, tanquam sorex, prodens, propalaverit. Licet vero Prudentissimi Domini Consiliarij illius levitatem morum et verborum forte non ignorantes, querelas istas non admiserint, sed ipsum obiurgatum dimiserint: ne tamen aliqua suspicio adhuc latitare possit, ipsum nonnulla, haud iniuria, conquestum fuisse, sed occasiones illi, a me vel meis, ut hoc faceret, datas esse: nosti enim illud nimis in mundo tritum: Calumniare audacter, semper aliquid adhaeret; te per amicitiam nostram, quibus possum precibus, rogo, velis omnem movere lapidem, ut exemplar supplicationis eius adipiscar, vel si id commode fieri nequeat, saltem ut summam eorum, quae continebat, resciscam. Quod si alio modo obtineri non possit, spero te apud Magcum et Nobilissimum Dominum MECKERUM (quem non dubijs inditijs, et etiam te, cum ultimo Pragae simul essemus, idipsum referente, mihi sincere faventem persuasum habeo) id, quicquid est, percepturum. Feceritis sic non saltem ille, sed et tu mihi rem admodum acceptam, atque ut mihi a talibus subdolis et immeritis delationibus inposterum praecavere valeam, haud inutilem, imo fortean etiam necessariam. Sic enim fieri conditiones meae nunc exigunt; sum enim hîc peregrinus, nec Illustrium aut authoritate praecellentium Virorum consanguinitate et necessitudine, ut antea in Patriâ, fultus, quin et praeterea hîc extra Aulam vitam ago umbratilem, ignarus, quid isthic de me in quamcunque partem dicatur, nec alias libenter rebus me immisceo alienis, sed meis solummodo vacans studijs, Spartam, quam nactus sum, pro virili ornare satago, ut in honorem optimi Imperatoris atque Imperij ea, favente supremo Numine, moliri atque exequi queam, quae ad omnem posteritatem utilia et memorabilia futura confido. Sed tu, mi amantissime IESSENI, pro ea quae nobis intercedit familiaritate, omnia satis perspicis, ut ulteriore commemoratione non sit opus, et te nostris hac in parte desiderijs non defuturum omnino mihi polliceor.

Vale et praedictum Magcum Dominum MECKERUM ex me officiosissime saluta.
Dabantur festinanti calamo ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ Die 28 Martij Anni 1600.

TYCHO BRAHE TO JOHANNES JESSENIUS A JESSEN.

Heartiest greeting.

Most renowned Lord Doctor Jessenius, a friend always much to be cultivated: a certain servant of ours, whom I lately had at the court at Pilsen, soliciting in my affairs, has written that he gathered from a certain chief Councillor of the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, that a certain Ursus, the Dithmarsian, had some time ago presented to the Councillors a supplication complaining about me — but [that it was] ignominiously rejected, with sharp rebuke applied. And since he who intimated this to me added nothing further about the contents of that supplication, and the same man has gone from Pilsen into his fatherland, which is in Westphalia (nor do I expect his return within three or four months), while it nonetheless concerns me to know in good time what that impudent fellow dared to complain of about me — when I have in no way, nay, not by any little word, whether through myself or through others, injured him the whole time I am in Bohemia (although I had already before suffered intolerable injuries and calumnies from him, which he did not blush to heap up in that infamous writing he published at Prague against me three years ago — than which, in the judgement of the wise, this age has scarcely seen anything more putrid and vain) — but, by particular design, and from the distraction of weightier occupations, I dissembled everything, as if I knew nothing of it. And certainly, if that Ursus came to meet me, I would not know him; nor is it established to me whether he was meanwhile at Prague or elsewhere, for I have not deigned to inquire about him — so that, did I not otherwise know his beyond-measure brazen front, his scurrility, and his shameless lying, I could not sufficiently wonder what he could bring as a complaint against me — I so silent, and afflicting him with no annoyance — before the Most Ample Lords Councillors. For he has nothing he may truly say, unless he wishes to object this very thing: that I am silent, and have hitherto from on high despised his trifles (to be retorted upon him, perhaps, in their own time) — though by them he has harmed not so much my fame and reputation (which heeds not a thousand such sycophantic barkings) as that he has made manifest, to the whole world and all sincere judges, his own petulant scurrility and scurrilous nature, teeming with insults and curses — betraying himself by his own judgement, like a shrew-mouse. But although the most prudent Lords Councillors — perhaps not ignorant of his lightness of manners and words — did not admit those complaints, but dismissed him rebuked: yet, lest some suspicion may still lurk that he complained of some things not without cause, but that occasions were given him, by me or mine, to do it (for you know that saying too trite in the world: "Slander boldly — something always sticks") — I ask you, by our friendship, with what prayers I can, that you would move every stone, that I may obtain a copy of his supplication — or, if that cannot conveniently be done, at least that I may learn the sum of what it contained. And if it cannot be had another way, I hope you will get it, whatever it is, with the Magnificent and most noble Lord Mecker (whom, by no doubtful signs — and you too reporting the same, when we were last together at Prague — I hold to be sincerely favourable to me). You will thus do me — not he alone, but you too — a thing very welcome, and not useless (nay, perhaps even necessary), that I may guard myself henceforth against such crafty and undeserved accusations. For so my present conditions require: I am here a stranger, not propped (as before in my fatherland) by kinship and the bond of illustrious or pre-eminently authoritative men; and besides, here I live a shadowy [retired] life outside the Court, ignorant of what is said there about me in any direction; nor do I otherwise gladly meddle in others' affairs, but, devoting myself only to my studies, strive to adorn, to the best of my power, the Sparta I have obtained — that, to the honour of the best Emperor and of the Empire, I may, by the favour of the supreme Power, contrive and accomplish things I trust will be useful and memorable to all posterity. But you, my most loving Jessenius, by the familiarity that passes between us, perceive all this well enough, so that there is no need of further mention; and I altogether promise myself that you will not fail my wishes in this part.

Farewell, and greet the aforesaid Magnificent Lord Mecker from me most dutifully.
Given with hurrying pen from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 28th day of March, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Johannes Jessenius (Jan Jessenský), the anatomist and Tycho's intimate (cf. brahe-t_16000324001, brahe-t_16000327002, brahe-t_16001008001), well placed at the imperial court.
  • Significance — a principal document of the Tycho–Ursus feud. This letter concerns Nicolaus Reimers Bär, "Ursus" (c. 1551–1600), of Dithmarschen — the imperial mathematician who had plagiarised Tycho's geo-heliocentric world-system and savaged him in print. Tycho has learned (via a servant, from a chief imperial councillor) that Ursus lodged a supplication/complaint against him with the Councillors, which was ignominiously rejected with sharp rebuke — and asks Jessenius to obtain a copy of that supplication (or at least its substance), if necessary through the favourable councillor Lord Mecker, "that I may guard myself against such crafty and undeserved accusations." This belongs to the legal/printed contest of 1597–1600 (Tycho was preparing the Apologia against Ursus that became, posthumously, the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata appendix / the suit pursued after Ursus's death in 1600). Cross-links to brahe-t_15990321001 (the "Dithmarsic brute" and the plagiarism brief).
  • The earlier defamatory book. "that infamous writing he published at Prague against me three years ago" = Ursus's De astronomicis hypothesibus (Prague, 1597) — "than which, in the judgement of the wise, this age has scarcely seen anything more putrid and vain." Tycho stresses he had met all this with deliberate silence ("from on high I have despised his trifles"), having "in no way injured him … the whole time I am in Bohemia."
  • Tycho's self-portrait (notable). A striking statement of his position as imperial exile: "I am here a stranger, not propped by kinship and the authority of great men as before in my fatherland," living "a shadowy life outside the Court," meddling in nothing, "devoting myself only to my studies" — and so the more exposed to undeserved slander, hence the need to know the charge. The closing "the Sparta I have obtained, I strive to adorn" (Spartam quam nactus es, hanc orna — the classical maxim of doing one's duty in one's appointed station) frames his astronomical work "to the honour of the best Emperor and of the Empire … useful and memorable to all posterity."
  • Proverbs / figures (kept and glossed). "Calumniare audacter, semper aliquid adhaeret" — "Slander boldly, something always sticks" (the maxim of calumny's residue); tanquam sorex … prodens — "betraying himself like a shrew-mouse" (the proverb sorex suo perit indicio, the shrew destroyed by its own squeak); Sycophanticos allatratus — "sycophantic barkings."
  • Persons named. Johannes Jessenius; Nicolaus Reimers Bär (Ursus), "the Dithmarsian"; Lord Mecker (a favourable imperial councillor); Rudolf II and the imperial Councillors.
  • Calendar. Dateline Die 28 Martij Anni 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Latin corrected from 'bonorem' to 'honorem' per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.282.
