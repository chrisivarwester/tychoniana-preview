The original

NOBILISSIMO STRENUO ET MAGNIFICO VIRO TYCHONI BRAHEN DANO, SACRAe CÆS. MATHEMATICO, IMO ET FAUTORI SUO MULTIS NOMINIBUS COLENDISSIMO.
PRAGA.
Εὖ ζῆν καὶ εὖ πράττειν.

QUANQUAM apud D. DAVIDEM FABRICIUM ad nobiliss. Magam T. scripserim de rebus omnibus, Nobiliss. atque Magce vir, Domine multis nominibus colende, quas quidem literas meas unâ cum earundem latore, FABRICIO nostro, te accepisse et vidisse non dubito, facere tamen non potui, quin nobiliss. Magam T. hac schedula inviserem, sciscitaturus saltem, num dictus FABRICIUS noster salvus atque incolumis ad vos pervenerit. Multa enim metuebat atque, ut mihi videbatur, sibi persuadebat, quare non sine periculo hoc iter perficere posset. Veruntamen omnia in DEI praepotentis manu posita esse firmiter credo, sic ut eundem FABRICIUM, nisi DEO aliter visum fuerit, incolumem omnino ac salvum huc reversurum plane persuasum habeam. Porro nobiliss. Magam T. rogatam velim, uti me sibi commendatum et persuasum plane habeat, me ex eorum esse numero, qui nobiliss. Magam T. modis omnibus salvam optet atque incolumem, eiusque honoribus faveat quam maxume.

Caeterum quid Sacrae Caes. Matis Legatus et Consiliarius praecipuus D. HANNIWALT hîc rerum agat, quamque honorifice, ne dicam magnifice, ab Illri et generoso principe meo ENNONE, Orient. Frisiae Comite, exceptus sit, et quae porro Sacrae Caes. Mati ab Illri Cels. eius exspectanda sint officia atque servitia, meum non est perscribere, cum tempus ipsum atque exitus reipsa comprobatura sint, quali affectu Illris Dominus noster erga Sacratiss. Caes. Matem sit praeditus, quam ut DEUS opt. max. unâ cum nobiliss. Maga T. diutissime servet, ex animo precor. Ac si forsan occasio fuerit, ut Sacrae Caes. Mati mea quamvis humilia servitia commendare possis, quaeso studiorum, si non eorundem, attamen communium memor esto.

Haec scripsi αὐτοσχεδίως et quasi per satyram, propterea quod propter praesentiam praefati Caes. Matis Legati, qui pridie huc appulit, plusculis eram occupatus negocijs. Quare et festinationi et lituris ignosces, tum FABRICIO nostro hasce inclusas literas cum salute uberrima tradere haud dedigneris. Utinam vero sit aliquid, in quo nobiliss. Magae T. vicissim gratificari possim, neque enim labori, vigilijs, studijs, sumptui parcerem.

Vale vir nobiliss. atque Magce.
Scripta ex arce Auricana Frisiae Orientalis pridie Eidus Iunij stilo veteri Anno postremi temporis Ch. 1601.
Nobiliss. Magae T.
addictiss.
EBERARTUS SCHELE,
Orient. Frisiae Secret.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 422; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Quae hoc ipso die de occupatione propugnaculi ante Bergam ex Haga Comitis allata sunt nova, cum et incerta adhuc sint nec vobis ignota diu eße poßint, lubens praetereo.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble, valiant and magnificent man Tycho Brahe the Dane, mathematician of the Sacred Imperial [Majesty], nay also his patron most-to-be-revered on many accounts. Prague.
To live well and to fare well. [Greek]

Although I wrote to your most noble Magnificence about all matters by way of Master David Fabricius — which letter of mine, together with its bearer, our Fabricius, I do not doubt you have received and read — yet I could not but call upon your most noble Magnificence with this note, [meaning] at least to inquire whether the said our Fabricius has reached you safe and unharmed. For he feared many things, and, as it seemed to me, persuaded himself that he could not complete this journey without danger. Nevertheless I firmly believe all things are placed in the hand of God almighty, so that I hold it plainly persuaded that the same Fabricius — unless it shall have seemed otherwise to God — will return here wholly unharmed and safe. Further, I would have your most noble Magnificence asked, to hold me commended, and be plainly persuaded that I am of the number of those who wish your most noble Magnificence safe and unharmed in every way, and who favour your honours as much as may be.

For the rest, what the Sacred Imperial Majesty's Legate and chief Councillor, Master Hannewald, transacts here, and how honourably — not to say magnificently — he has been received by my illustrious and generous prince Enno, Count of East Frisia, and what services and duties are further to be expected by the Sacred Imperial Majesty from His Illustrious Highness, it is not mine to write out; for time itself and the outcome will prove in fact with what affection our Illustrious Lord is disposed toward the most Sacred Imperial Majesty — whom, together with your most noble Magnificence, may God, best and greatest, preserve for the longest time, I pray from my heart. And if perhaps there be occasion that you can commend my services, however humble, to the Sacred Imperial Majesty, I pray you, be mindful of our studies — if not the same, yet shared.

These [lines] I have written offhand and as it were in a medley, because, on account of the presence of the aforesaid Imperial Legate (who landed here yesterday), I was occupied with rather many affairs. Wherefore you will pardon both the haste and the blottings; and you will not disdain to deliver to our Fabricius these enclosed letters, with most abundant greeting. Would, in truth, that there were anything in which I might in turn oblige your most noble Magnificence — for I would spare neither labour, nor vigils, nor studies, nor expense.

Farewell, most noble and magnificent man.
Written from the castle of Aurich in East Frisia, the day before the Ides of June, Old Style, in the year of the last age of Christ 1601.
Most devoted to your most noble Magnificence,
Eberhard Schele,
Secretary of East Frisia.

[Postscript:] The news that have been brought this very day from The Hague concerning the seizure of the fort before Bergen — since they are still uncertain, and cannot long be unknown to you — I gladly pass over.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound letter to Tycho at Prague. Eberhard Schele, Secretarius of East Frisia (Ostfriesland), writing from the comital seat at Aurich.
  • The David Fabricius thread (connective tissue). The note's chief purpose is anxious solicitude over David Fabricius (1564–1617), the Frisian pastor-astronomer, who was travelling to visit Tycho carrying Schele's fuller letter — and who, Schele reports, dreaded the journey as dangerous. This is the arrival half of the episode whose departure half Georg Rollenhagen describes (brahe-t_16010515001, 15 May 1601: Fabricius "had turned aside to me out of his journey, which he had set toward you"). Schele asks Tycho both to confirm Fabricius's safe arrival and to hand Fabricius the enclosed letters.
  • Persons named.
  • D. David Fabricius = David Fabricius, the courier and the object of Schele's concern.
  • D. Hanniwalt = Andreas Hannewald (Hanniwald), secretary/councillor of Rudolf II, here as imperial legate to East Frisia, "landed yesterday" — his visit is the diplomatic backdrop and the reason for Schele's haste.
  • Illustris princeps meus Enno, Orientalis Frisiae Comes = Enno III, Count of East Frisia (1563–1625), Schele's sovereign, host to the imperial legate.
  • Sacra Caesarea Maiestas = Emperor Rudolf II, to whom Schele hopes Tycho may commend his "humble services."
  • Greek (transcribed + glossed, this chapter's convention). The address bears the salutation-motto Εὖ ζῆν καὶ εὖ πράττειν — "to live well and to fare well" (the classical good-wish). In the body, αὐτοσχεδίως = "offhand, extempore"; the Latin idiom per satyram (= per saturam) = "in a medley / pell-mell."
  • Place. arx Auricana Frisiae Orientalis = the castle of Aurich, East Frisia.
  • Epistolary formulae. Vale → "Farewell"; subscription Nobiliss. Magae T. addictiss. = "Most devoted to your most noble Magnificence."
  • Calendar (this chapter's convention — explicit Old Style). Dateline pridie Eidus [Idus] Iunij stilo veteri = "the day before the Ides of June, Old Style" = 12 June (the Ides of June = the 13th). East Frisia, a Reformed/Lutheran territory, kept the Julian calendar; Schele marks it expressly stilo veteri — to be contrasted with the New-Style Prague dating of Tycho's imperial correspondence. The flourish Anno postremi temporis Ch[risti] 1601 ("in the year of the last age of Christ 1601") is an eschatological commonplace, reproduced. Not converted.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. News-postscript (Hague reports of the seizure of a fort before Bergen-op-Zoom), restored from Dreyer VIII p.422 (after "Eberartus SCHELE, Orient. Frisiae Secret."; dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. Restored the word-space in the closing dateline: Dreyer prints "Auricana Frisiae" as two words (TBOO VIII p.422), not the glued "AuricanaFrisiae" of the prior transcription.
