The original

TAM MAIORUM IMAGINIBUS QUAM PROPRIA VIRTUTE ET DOCTRINÆ PRÆEMINENTIA IUVENI INPRIMIS CLARO, OLIGERO ROSENCRANTZIO, GEORGIJ F. DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGVINEO ET AMICO SUO SINCERE DILECTO. S. P.

NOBILISSIME et Eruditissime OLIGERE, Consangvinee et Amice inter præcipuos et intimos dilecte. Quoniam præter voluntatem et expectationem necessitas mihi incumbit, quendam publice in ius vocare, et de quibusdam famæ meæ defensionem haud modice concernentibus testimonia contra ipsum ferri curare, unâque de nonnullis quæstionem proponere, ad quæ statuto tempore respondere ipsemet in se recepit, intelligam vero, eundem clam in Aulam, ad D. Principem et Regem nostrum electum clementissimum et Dominos Proreges confugere velle, ut istic caussam suam palliet, et diverticula quærat, quibus se ab hac actione subducat – inprimis autem a tuo honoratissimo parente effugij rationem impetraturum sibi pollicetur, quod etiam Lundis innuere, atque eo insolentius se gerere non verebatur – scripsi itaque hac de re ad integerrimum tuum parentem, omnino mihi persuadens, qua præditus est æqui rectique promovendi voluntate propensa, pari constantia et gravitate coniuncta, ipsum non saltem nullatenus quippiam admissurum, quod legitimam caussæ huius cognitionem et executionem impedire aut differre possit, quin potius, multo magis adiumento et subsidio futurum, ut omnia ad iustitiæ normam æqua bilance deducantur, quo ea, quæ mihi per inverecundas calumnias, ultra annuum tempus, quo in his conticui et me a respondendo continui, hinc inde aspersa sunt, nunc tandem redarguam, et famam meam, qua nihil antiquius esse debet, intemeratam conservem. Quare et te, mi OLIGERE, per consangvinitatem et intimam amicitiam nostram, quibus possum, modis, obtestatum volo, quantum in te est (est autem quamplurimum), apud utrumque tuum laudatissimum parentem efficere velis, ut nihil interveniat, quod caussæ huius tractationi et competenti determinationi obstaculo existat. Quod et illos sua sponte haud gravatim facturos, et te non invite enisurum, omnino mihi persuadeo. Quanti enim te semper fecerim (prout etiam meretur tua singularis eruditio, pari virtute et candore exornata) tu ipsemet ex parte conscius es, et alij, qui me præclara dona præ reliquis nostratibus genere claris tibi prædicantem audiverunt, testatum facere possunt. Sed malo hæc cum alijs de te, quam apud te.

Vale, et amicitiam nostram, quod ego constanter facturus sum, illibatam conserva.
Dabantur Uraniburgi die 22. Ianuarij Anno 1596.
Tui amantissimus
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Holger Rosenkrantz, son of Jørgen, of Rosenholm — a young man distinguished as much by the portraits of his ancestors as by the pre-eminence of his own virtue and learning — his kinsman and sincerely beloved friend. Heartiest greeting.

Most noble and most learned Holger, kinsman and friend beloved among my chief and closest. Since, against my will and expectation, a necessity presses upon me to summon a certain man publicly into court, and to see to it that testimony be brought against him concerning certain matters that bear in no small degree upon the defence of my good name, and at the same time to put a question to him on several points, to which he himself undertook to make answer at the appointed time — but since I learn that this same man wishes to flee secretly to the Court, to my Lord the Prince, our most clement King-elect, and to the Lord Viceroys, so that there he may cloak his cause and seek by-ways by which to withdraw himself from this suit — and above all promises himself that he will obtain the means of escape from your most honoured father, which at Lund too he did not scruple to hint at, and to bear himself the more insolently on that account — I have therefore written on this matter to your most upright father, persuading myself wholly that, endowed as he is with that ready will to advance equity and right, joined with an equal constancy and gravity, he will not in the least admit anything that might hinder or defer the lawful examination and execution of this cause; but rather will be much more of a help and support, so that all may be brought, by an even balance, to the standard of justice — whereby I may at last now refute the things that, through shameless slanders, over a period of more than a year, during which I held my peace in these matters and restrained myself from answering, have been bespattered upon me on this side and that, and may keep my good name, than which nothing ought to be held more precious, unblemished. Wherefore I would also have you, my Holger, by our kinship and our intimate friendship, beseeched in such ways as I can, that, so far as in you lies (and it lies very much in you), you would be willing to bring it about with each of your most praiseworthy parents that nothing intervene which might stand as an obstacle to the handling of this cause and its proper determination. And I am wholly persuaded that they will do this of their own accord without reluctance, and that you will strive for it not unwillingly. For how highly I have always esteemed you (as indeed your singular learning, adorned with equal virtue and candour, deserves) you yourself are in part aware, and others can bear witness — those who have heard me proclaiming to you [your] distinguished gifts above the rest of our countrymen of illustrious birth. But I had rather [say] these things about you with others than to your face.

Farewell, and keep our friendship — as I for my part shall constantly do — inviolate.
Given at Uraniborg, on the 22nd day of January, in the year 1596.
Most loving of you,
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender / "manu propria." Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven. The closing manu propria ("in his own hand") indicates Tycho subscribed the letter autograph; the surviving witness, however, is a later transcript (Afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium (Copenhagen, Rigsarkivet), not the autograph itself.
  • Recipient. Oligerus Rosencrantzius, Georgij f. de Rosenholm = Holger Rosenkrantz (1574–1642), later renowned as den lærde Holger ("the learned Holger"), theologian and Privy Councillor, owner of Rosenholm Castle. In 1596 he was a young man of 21; Tycho addresses him as a consanguineus (kinsman — the Brahe and Rosenkrantz lines were intermarried; Holger's mother was a Brahe connection) and flatters his birth and erudition.
  • "Georgij F." / "tuo honoratissimo parente." Georgius = Jørgen (Georg) Rosenkrantz (1523–1596) of Rosenholm, Holger's father, a member of the Rigsråd — hence one of the "Lord Viceroys" (Domini Proreges) whose influence at court Tycho fears the defendant will solicit. The phrase utrumque tuum laudatissimum parentem ("each of your most praiseworthy parents") embraces both father and mother; Holger's mother was Dorte Lange (cf. her 1589 portrait with Jørgen at Rosenholm).
  • The unnamed defendant ("quendam … in ius vocare"). Not named in the letter, but securely identified from the date and circumstance as Gellius Sascerides (Gellius Sascerides Hafniensis, b. 1562), Tycho's former pupil on Hven, whose broken betrothal to Tycho's daughter Magdalene Brahe had by 1595–96 turned into a bitter and very public lawsuit that Tycho framed as a matter of his own fama (good name). The "shameless slanders … over more than a year" refer to this affair; Tycho is the plaintiff seeking to compel testimony and prevent the defendant from finding political cover at court.
  • Legal vocabulary. in ius vocare = "to summon into court / to law"; actio = "(legal) suit / action"; caussa (= causa) = "cause / case"; cognitio et executio = "examination and execution [of the cause]," i.e. the judicial hearing and its enforcement; competens determinatio = "proper / competent determination [= verdict]"; diverticula quaerere = "to seek by-ways / evasions"; caussam palliare = "to cloak / gloss over one's cause." The mood and force of the negations are reproduced exactly: non saltem nullatenus quippiam admissurum is an emphatic double negation ("will not in the least admit anything") asserting that the father will permit no obstruction — rendered without flattening the doubled negative; te non invite enisurum = "that you will strive not unwillingly."
  • "D. Principem et Regem nostrum electum clementissimum et Dominos Proreges." The "Prince, our most clement King-elect" is Christian IV (b. 1577), elected king in 1596 but not yet crowned (the coronation followed in August 1596); during his minority the realm was governed by a regency of four Rigsråder (Domini Proreges, "Lord Viceroys / Regents"), among them Jørgen Rosenkrantz. electum ("elect[ed]") is retained literally and is chronologically exact for January 1596.
  • "Lundis." Lundis = "at Lund" (in Scania), where the defendant had reportedly hinted he would obtain the elder Rosenkrantz's protection; locative kept and glossed.
  • Honorifics / formulae. S. P. = Salutem Plurimam ("Heartiest greeting," this chapter's convention); the heading's parallel Tam maiorum imaginibus quam … praeeminentia ("as much by the portraits/images of his ancestors as by his pre-eminence") plays on imagines maiorum, the Roman ancestral portrait-masks denoting noble lineage. Vale … illibatam conserva = "Farewell … keep [it] inviolate"; Dabantur (epistolary plural for Datae) = "Given." Tui amantissimus = "Most loving of you."
  • Orthography. Humanist Latin with consonantal j for i (Georgij, ijs→hisce, Ianuarij, alij, amicitiam) and -ae written as Ae/ae in majuscule (DOCTRINAe, PRAeEMINENTIA, famae); caussa for causa and unâque, quâ with the circumflex are the editor's diacritics, reproduced as printed.
  • Calendar. die 22. Ianuarij Anno 1596 — 22 January 1596, Old Style (Denmark used the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline matches the edition label; no discrepancy. Note the historical caution: in Tycho's reckoning the year had begun on 1 January, so "1596" here is unambiguous.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Dreyer (Opera Omnia VII p. 374) reads quam propria virtute — “by his own virtue,” the antithesis to the inherited imagines maiorum; the transcription had dropped “-pria.” Corrected in the heading and its translation.
