The original

MAGNIFICO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO GEORGIO HERWARTO AB HOHENBURGK, SERENISSIMO UTRIUSQUE BAVARIÆ DUCI A CONSILIJS INTIMIS, PRÆSIDI SCHWABÆ ET STATUUM PROVINCIALIUM CANCELLARIO, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO. MÜNCHEN.

S. P.

POSTQUAM priores, circa medium Novembris ad te, Vir Nobilissime et Præstantissime, directas, et paulisper, donec in Aulam eas mittendi atque inde ad te procurandi suppeteret commoditas, mecum retentas, tandem cum adiunctis chartis emisissem, accidit, ut aliæ tuæ eodem ferme tempore, cum meæ illæ prolixiores scriptæ, mihi redderentur, idque primum paulo ante solstitium hybernum instantibus ferijs Natalitijs, quæ licet breviusculæ fuerint, plurimum tamen mihi acceptæ, cum quod te in amicitia nostra sic, donec aliter liceat, continuanda, perseverantem nec scribendi occasionem ullam intermittentem ostenderent; tum quia te patienter moram meam in rescribendo ferre (siquidem nulla in ijs usus esses, quod non immerito facere licuisset, expostulatione) inde colligerem, utut caussæ huius tarditatis tibi necdum perspectæ erant, quas te nunc acceptis meis cognovisse atque ratas habuisse mihi polliceor.

Accessit et hoc, quod literæ illæ tuæ steriles non fuerint, sed triplices in Sole Cassellis factas tribus diversis, attamen continuis annis sedulas observationes easdemque Geometrice et per numeros pro eruendo inde Apogæo et Eccentricitate Solari expensas, fœcundo partu secum adferrent. Fuit equidem id mihi admodum gratum, cum animadversiones illas prius non viderim. Neque enim a laudatissimæ memoriæ GULIELMO Principe Landtgravio aut etiam eius Mathematico CHRISTOPHORO ROTHMANNO mihi antea (quod omissum miror) communicatæ erant, alias Epistolarum Tomo primo, qui literas ijsdem annis inter nos commutatas habet, eas unâ cum competente disquisitione et cum nostris inventis collatione inseruissem, ut ob id probe a te factum, quod vel ex hac omissione coniecturam non fallacem conceperis, eas prius ad manus meas non pervenisse; eoque rectius et acceptius mihi nunc impertiris.

Multum autem me torsit, quod prima introspectione animadverterim, Apogæum quidem Solis cum nostro utcunque congruere. Nam dimidij gradus differentia discerni in tam subtili Pragmateia difficulter potest. Nec etiam sensibiliter quid officit, siquidem iuxta ipsum Apogæum non nisi unico minuto longitudinem Solis variet, in cæteris locis adhuc minus. Verum ubi Eccentricitatem trifariam investigatam et ubique quasi in ipsis secundis sibi constantem conspexissem, a nostro nihilominus deficere quasi 6 minutis de 60 partibus semidiametri Eccentrici, mirum in modum, quid sub his scrupulis lateret scrupuli, hæsitavi. Cum enim revisis meis Observationibus, quas circa eosdem dies in Uraniburgo nostro Danico habueram, et supputato simul loco Solis e nostro restituto calculo, quæ Eccentricitatem præsupponit part. 2 m. 9 ad minimum, viderem longitudinem ☉ ubique intra unicum minutum illi congruere, quam observata Cassellana tribuerunt, nec possibile esset, ex ijsdem datis tam prope consentientibus, adhibito non fallaci demonstrationis processu, qualem tuum quoque esse cognovi, tam diversimodam nihilominus prodire Eccentricitatem, quid taciti sese insinuasset obstaculi, non ita facile ab initio patuit. Nam et alia ab ea, qua tu usus, ratione, per prostaphæreses nimirum, repetito tuo pro maiori parte calculo; visus est is recte se habere. Nec poterat refractionis insinuatio quæ in primo exemplo, ubi ☉ 27 proxime gradibus 2 min. in ultimo, Sole tertium ♒ gradum transeunte, et 19 saltem gradibus elevato eandem 5 minutorum ingerebat (ut de intermedio, in quo hæc diversitas minus sensibilis erat, nihil addam) nostram intentionem turbare; siquidem loca ☉ in Ecliptica cum nostris numeris adeo prope consentientia, ROTHMANNUM non saltem Parallaxes Solis sed et refractiones, de quo aliquoties a me (uti ex Epistolis a te citatis liquet) admonitus erat, solerter præcavisse, satis probarunt. Neque etiam, quod Eclipticæ obliquatio maxima dimidio scrupulo minor in calculo transmisso assumpta sit, quam a nobis præsupposita, tam evidentem in Eccentricitate hac discrepantiam parere potuit, sicuti neglecta temporis æquatio insensibilem variationem suggerere. Circumspecte itaque omnia (uti tunc mihi videbar) in tuis demonstrationibus et superstructis numeris trutinando, et meas quoque rationes, quæ ab Æquinoctijs et medietate ♉ atque ♌ in Eccentricitate atque Apogæo ☉ eruendis paulo adhuc maiore tam compendio quam certitudine procedunt, denuo sub calculi incudem revocando, nihilque in ijs aberratum esse comprobando, abieci, tanquam mihi ipsi non satisfaciens, hanc aliquantisper disquisitionem; qua tamen peractis sacris Natalitijs denuo in manus resumpta et toto tuo calculo a principio usque in finem (quod antea circa extrema neglectum erat, ex quo cætera tam belle constarent) tandem inventum est, in quo latuerit huius discrepantiæ nodus. Cum enim circa ipsum calculi exitum, quando ex radice quadrata, quæ in omnibus tribus exemplis sibi ferme similis est, atque in numerum 3591 quam proxime conspirat, perventum est, ut inde Eccentricitatis quantitas eliceretur, depræhendimus, quod per incuriam aliquam, quod facile inter gravissimas tuas occupationes alias fieri potuit, aut si a quopiam alio hæc Pragmateia instituta est (neque enim cuius sit indicasti) numerus ille in Tabulam sinuum intromissus quantitatem Eccentricitatis perperam monstrarit, ideoque iusto minorem, cum proportionaliter saltem, prout 60 loco totius radij acciperentur, eruenda foret, sicque eadem quam proxime mecum prodibit Eccentricitatis determinatio; quæ etiam in pauculis scrupulis secundis, nullius pene momenti, nostram excedat potius, quam quod 6 minutis ab ea deficiat. Imo si observationes in Sole a nobis Anno 1583 diligenter factitatas in consilium adhibuerimus, eadem cum transmissa inquisitione prodibit Eccentricitas. Nam et nos eandem tunc adinvenimus part. 3592, qualium radius est sive sinus totus 100000, sive part. 2 m. 9⅓, qualium idem 60, Apogæo etiam tunc in part. 5¾ ♋ reposito; et eodem per septennium usque in Annum 1590, qui medius est trium ex tuis, per additionem 9 minutorum, prout requirit Apogæi mutatio a PTOLEMÆO eousque facta (nam quæ in libro modo misso Apogæi notatur progressio aliquanto tardior est, eo quod ex observatione REGIOMONTANI ante 100 Annos facta peculiari consilio saltem derivata sit, uti et Anni quantitas iusto paulo minor assumpta, nec tamen in utrisque his tanta est discrepantia, ut fert COPERNICI ratiocinatio) in ipso scrupulo (quod mirum est) cum Landtgraviano Anni 1590 Apogæo consentiens. Ut vero promptius et plenius percipias, qua methodo et quibus dedomenis nos et Apogæum et Eccentricitatem ☉ verificaverimus, atque unde hæc minutula differentia in nostra numeratione inter quæsita Anni 1583 et aliorum quorundam postea subsequentium irrepserit, en habes Progymnasmatum nostrorum de Astronomia instaurata Tomum primum, quatenus rerum nostrarum variæ conturbationes et vicissitudines eum hactenus typis complere permiserunt. Quali Titulo in publicum brevi, favente cœlesti Numine, emittendus, et quæ in eodem superaddenda restant, prima circumposita charta manifestat. Illic cap. statim primo hæc Solaris curriculi restitutionem concernentia affatim expediunt. Quæ de ☾ appendicis loco eidem subnectenda, suo tempore videbis. Secundum caput restitutionem affixarum Stellarum accuratissimam usque in octavum centenarium inde ab ipso fundamento extruit, tandemque canonice recenset. Quibus postea eas, quæ desiderantur, usque ad integrum millenarium supplevi. Reliqua voluminis pars de supra modum miraculosa Anni 1572 Stella luculenter tractat. Invenies alias in hoc opere nonnulla ex ijs, quæ prius inter nos ventilata sunt, plenius disquisita, et non pauca alia, quæ tuo (uti spero) arridebunt ingenio. Consule hunc boni, tibique soli reserva, donec eundem auctiorem et plane absolutum obtineas, unâ cum alijs quibusdam ex meis in hoc genere studiorum laboribus. Sphalmata Typographica, quæ nonnulla inter revidendum comperi, tute, qui rem ipsam probe capis, facile restitues. Vide quid pepererint ternæ ternorum etiam Annorum Solis observationes mihi a te benevole missæ. Si plures in alijs etiam Astris sive Cassellis sive alibi sedulo factas procuraveris, neque illæ sine fœnore redibunt. Intellexi WERNERUM quasdam a se habitas animadversiones cœlestes in publicum êdidisse, quæ si præter eas, quas de aëris mutatione vidi, aliquid circa Phænomena Astrorum cœlitus denotata habent, et tibi de eo libro quidpiam constat, cuperem exemplar mihi aliquantisper concedi.

Pluribus te nunc morari nolo; sed expecto brevi tam ad priores meas, quam hasce responsionem. Id saltem superaddam, inventionem Apogæi et Eccentricitatis ex Observatis Anni 1583 a me, prout in libro, quem nunc mitto, expositam, mihi magis placere alterâ, quam deinceps ex subsequentibus Annis adhibui; siquidem æquationis temporis ratio isthic applicata, postea vero omissa. Sicque Apogæum et Eccentricitas e Landtgravianis observatis petita rectius convenient cum nostrâ illâ priori (uti dixi) annotatione, licet differentia, quæ alias intercidit, nullius pene sit æstimationis. Quam tamen discrepantiunculam observationes ☉ hîc in Bohemiâ, favente Numine, continuandæ facile diriment; siquidem Æquator 6 ferme gradibus hîc altior quam in Daniâ ad quadragesimum Gradum extet, et ob id nulla Solis refractio, quæ sensibus obvia sit, iuxta ipsa Æquinoctia (prout in Daniâ sit) sese insinuet.

Vale itaque quam optime, et Anni huius instantis iubilæi, ac plurium aliorum curricula felici animi et corporis rerumque externarum statu transige.

Dabantur Girtzitzæ, unde cras, volente Deo, Benaticam reverti constitui, pridie Calend. Ianuar. Anni Domini 1600.
Tuæ Magæ addictissimus
TYCHO BRAHE manu propria scripsi.

To the magnificent and most noble man, Lord Georg Herwart von Hohenburg, Privy Councillor to the most serene Duke of both Bavarias, President of Swabia, and Chancellor of the Provincial Estates, his Lord and friend especially to be honoured. Munich.

Heartiest greeting.

After I had at last sent off — with the appended papers — my earlier [letter], directed to you about the middle of November (and kept by me a little while, until the convenience of sending it to the Court, and procuring it thence to you, should be at hand), it happened that another letter of yours was delivered me at almost the same time as that more prolix one of mine was written — and that first a little before the winter solstice, with the Christmas holidays at hand. Though it was rather brief, yet it was most welcome to me, both because it showed you persevering in our friendship (thus to be continued, until it may be otherwise), and missing no occasion of writing; and because I gathered thence that you patiently bear my delay in replying — since you used in it no expostulation, which you might not undeservedly have done — although the causes of this tardiness were not yet clear to you; which I trust you have now, on receiving mine, recognised and held excused.

This was added too, that your letter was not sterile, but brought with it, in a fertile birth, three diligent observations of the Sun made at Kassel in three different but continuous years, and the same worked out geometrically and by numbers, for deriving thence the solar Apogee and Eccentricity. This was indeed very welcome to me, since I had not seen those observations before. For they had not previously been communicated to me by the Landgrave Wilhelm, of most praised memory, nor by his Mathematician Christoph Rothmann (which I wonder was omitted); otherwise I should have inserted them in the first Volume of the Epistles (which has the letters exchanged between us in those very years), together with a competent disquisition and a comparison with my own findings. So that it was rightly done by you, that even from this omission you formed a not-false conjecture that they had not reached my hands before; and you the more rightly and acceptably impart them to me now.

But it much tortured me that, at first inspection, I noticed the Sun's Apogee indeed somehow to agree with mine — for a half-degree's difference can with difficulty be discerned in so subtle a practical computation [Pragmateia]; nor does it perceptibly hurt anything, since at the Apogee itself it varies the Sun's longitude by but one minute, in the other places still less. But when I beheld the Eccentricity — investigated three ways, and everywhere constant to itself almost in the very seconds — nonetheless falling short of mine by about 6 minutes of the 60 parts of the Eccentric's semidiameter, I hesitated wondrously, [as to] what scruple lurked beneath these scruples. For when, my own Observations revised (which I had made about the same days at our Danish Uraniborg), and the Sun's place at once computed from our restored calculation (which presupposes an Eccentricity of 2° 9′ at the least), I saw the Sun's longitude everywhere agreeing within a single minute with what the Kassel observations gave — and it was not possible, from the same data so closely agreeing, using a non-fallacious process of demonstration (such as I found yours too to be), for nonetheless so differing an Eccentricity to come out — what silent obstacle had insinuated itself was not so easily clear at first. For by a method other than yours (namely, by prosthaphaereses), repeating your calculation for the most part, it seemed to stand correctly. Nor could the insinuation of refraction disturb my intention — which in the first example, where the Sun [was near 27°], in the last (the Sun passing the third degree of Aquarius [♒], and elevated only 19°), introduced the same [of] 5 minutes (to say nothing of the intermediate, in which this diversity was less perceptible) — since the Sun's places in the Ecliptic, agreeing so closely with my numbers, sufficiently proved that Rothmann had skilfully forestalled not only the Sun's Parallaxes but also the refractions (about which he had several times been admonished by me, as is clear from the Epistles you cited). Nor could the fact that the maximum obliquity of the Ecliptic was assumed half a scruple less in the transmitted calculation than was presupposed by me produce so evident a discrepancy in this Eccentricity — any more than the neglected equation of time could suggest more than an insensible variation. So, weighing all things circumspectly (as I then thought) in your demonstrations and the numbers built upon them, and recalling my own reckonings too (which proceed — for deriving the Sun's Eccentricity and Apogee from the Equinoxes and the middle of Taurus [♉] and Leo [♌] — with even a little more compendium and certainty) to the anvil of calculation, and proving that nothing had been erred in them, I threw this disquisition aside for a while, as not satisfying myself. Yet, when the sacred Christmas-days were over, taking it again in hand and [going through] your whole calculation from the beginning to the end (which before had been neglected about the extremes, since the rest agreed so nicely), at last it was found wherein the knot of this discrepancy lurked. For around the very exit of the calculation — when, from the square root (which in all three examples is almost similar to itself, and conspires most closely to the number 3591), it was reached, so that thence the quantity of the Eccentricity might be elicited — we detected that, by some carelessness (which could easily otherwise happen amid your gravest occupations, or if this computation was instituted by some other — for you did not indicate whose it is), that number, introduced into the Table of sines, wrongly showed the quantity of the Eccentricity, and so [made it] less than just, since it ought to be derived proportionally (taking 60 for the whole radius); and so the same determination of the Eccentricity comes out most closely with mine — exceeding mine, indeed, by a few seconds of almost no moment, rather than falling short of it by 6 minutes. Nay, if the solar observations diligently made by me in the year 1583 be brought into counsel, the same Eccentricity comes out as the transmitted inquiry. For I too then found it 3592 parts (of which the radius, or total sine, is 100000) — or 2° 9⅓′ (of which the same is 60) — the Apogee then placed at 5¾° Cancer [♋]; and the same, through seven years to the year 1590 (which is the middle of your three), by adding 9 minutes (as the Apogee's change from Ptolemy down to then requires — for the Apogee-progression noted in the book just sent is somewhat slower, since it was derived by a special method from an observation of Regiomontanus made 100 years before, as also the year-quantity was assumed a little less than just; yet in both these the discrepancy is not so great as Copernicus's reckoning bears) — agreeing in the very minute (which is wonderful) with the Landgrave's 1590 Apogee.

But that you may the more readily and fully perceive by what method and from what data I have verified the Sun's Apogee and Eccentricity, and whence this minute difference in my numeration crept in between the result of 1583 and certain others subsequently following — behold, you have [here] the first Volume of our Progymnasmata of Restored Astronomy, so far as the various disturbances and vicissitudes of my affairs have hitherto permitted me to complete it in print. With what Title it is shortly to be published (Heaven favouring), and what remains to be added in it, the first appended sheet makes manifest. There, in the very first chapter, these things concerning the restitution of the Solar course are amply dispatched. What is to be subjoined about the Moon by way of appendix, you will see in time. The second chapter builds the most accurate restitution of the fixed Stars, up to the eighth hundred, from the very foundation, and at last reviews them canonically; to which I afterward supplied those that are wanting, up to the full thousand. The remaining part of the volume treats clearly of the supremely miraculous Star of the year 1572 [the supernova]. You will find, besides, in this work some of the things formerly ventilated between us more fully investigated, and not a few others which will (as I hope) please your genius. Take it in good part, and reserve it to yourself alone, until you obtain the same augmented and quite completed, together with some others of my labours in this kind of study. The typographical errors (of which I found some while revising), you, who grasp the matter itself well, will easily correct. See what those three solar observations of three years, benevolently sent me by you, have produced! If you procure more, diligently made — in other Stars too, whether at Kassel or elsewhere — these too will not return without interest. I understand that Werner has published some celestial observations of his own; if, besides those on the change of weather which I have seen, they have anything concerning the Phenomena of the Stars noted from the heaven, and you know anything of that book, I should wish a copy granted me for a while.

I do not wish to delay you longer now; but I expect shortly a reply both to my earlier [letter] and to this. This only will I add: that the invention of the Apogee and Eccentricity from the observations of the year 1583 (as set forth in the book I now send) pleases me more than the other which I afterward used from the subsequent years — since the reckoning of the equation of time was applied there, but afterward omitted; and so the Apogee and Eccentricity sought from the Landgrave's observations will agree more rightly with that prior annotation of mine (as I said), though the difference, which otherwise intervenes, is of almost no estimation. This little discrepancy, however, the observations of the Sun to be continued here in Bohemia (Heaven favouring) will easily resolve — since the Equator stands here almost 6 degrees higher than in Denmark, toward the fortieth degree, and on that account no Sun-refraction, perceptible to the senses, insinuates itself near the very Equinoxes (as it does in Denmark).

Fare, then, most well, and pass the course of this coming Jubilee Year, and of many others, in a happy state of mind and body and outward affairs.

Given at Girsitz — whence tomorrow, God willing, I have resolved to return to Benátky — on the day before the Kalends of January, in the year of the Lord 1600.
Most devoted to Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe, written in my own hand.

[Postscript:] I have appended the calculation of the Sun's Apogee and Eccentricity, repeated through the prosthaphaereses used by us in the three transmitted examples, which scarcely differs from yours — save for the correction of the Eccentricity, as I noted in the letter. I have also added a certain Ode, not without charm, sent to me from Pomerania these last days, that you may annex it to the other poems shared with you.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Hans Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (Munich) — addressed with his full Bavarian titles (Privy Councillor, President of Swabia, Estates' Chancellor). The third Tycho↔Herwart letter in the literature consulted (cf. brahe-t_15990831001 and brahe-t_15990917001; and the later brahe-t_16000911001).
  • Significance — a forensic analysis of the Kassel solar observations, and the sending of the Progymnasmata.
  • The Kassel solar data. Herwart had sent three diligent solar observations made at Kassel in three successive years (the Landgrave Wilhelm IV / Christoph Rothmann observatory), reduced for the solar Apogee and Eccentricity — never before seen by Tycho (who would else have printed them in his Epistolae vol. I).
  • The error hunt (a model of Tychonic forensic computation). Tycho found the Apogee agreeing (a half-degree being negligible) but the Eccentricity short of his own by c. 6′ of 60 — yet the underlying solar longitudes agreed "within one minute." After excluding refraction (Rothmann had guarded against parallax and refraction, on Tycho's earlier urging), obliquity, and the equation of time, he set the puzzle aside over Christmas, then traced the discrepancy to a computational slip: a square-root value (~3591) fed into the sine-table without the proper proportional scaling (radius = 60 vs 100000) — once corrected, the Kassel Eccentricity matches his own. His own values: Eccentricity 2° 9⅓′ (= 3592 of 100000), Apogee 5¾° Cancer in 1583, advancing c. 9′ over 7 years to 1590, "agreeing in the very minute with the Landgrave's 1590 Apogee." (He notes the Progymnasmata's slower apogee-progression derives from a Regiomontanus observation a century back.)
  • The Progymnasmata vol. I sent (a contents-inventory). Tycho sends Herwart the first volume of the Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata as far as printed: ch. 1 the solar restitution; an appendix on the Moon; ch. 2 the fixed-star catalogue (built to 800, supplied to a full 1000); the rest on the Star of 1572 (the supernova). To be kept private until the completed copy. (A datable Progymnasmata presentation to Herwart.)
  • The Bohemia advantage. Tycho notes solar observations are better in Bohemia: the Equator c. 6° higher than in Denmark, so no sensible refraction near the equinoxes — promising to resolve the residual discrepancy.
  • Werner. Tycho asks for a copy of Werner's published celestial observations, if they bear on star-phenomena (not just weather).
  • Cross-references. Pair with the other Herwart letters (brahe-t_15990831001, brahe-t_15990917001, brahe-t_16000911001); the Kassel / Wilhelm IV / Rothmann observational thread; and the Progymnasmata printing/distribution thread (brahe-t_16000107001, brahe-t_15990922001).
  • Persons & places named. Herwart von Hohenburg; Landgrave Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel (the late); Christoph Rothmann; Ptolemy, Regiomontanus, Copernicus; Werner (a published observer); the Star of 1572.
