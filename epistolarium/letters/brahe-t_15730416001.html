The original

VIRO VERE NOBILI ET GENEROSO TYCHONI BRAHE DE KNUDSTRUP, DOMINO OPTIMO ET AMICO SYNCERO. SALUTEM ET VER MELISSEUM.

RECEPI literas tuas, TYCHO optime, quibus negotia tua promovenda apud Artifices tuos mechanicos committis. Quod tardius ad te redeat hic tuus Tabellarius, in causa fuit, quod nundinæ Roschildenses me ultra propositum detinuerint: ubi mihi tantisper commorandum fuit, donec de vase meo et bonis Francofurto per Flensburgenses mercatores missis, fieri poteram certior.

Negotia tua sic se habent. Absolutæ sunt imagines omnes in Globo tuo. Divisus Meridianus et Horizon, paratum pedestale, sed hîc difficultas est non parva, de inauratione videlicet, de coloratione, seu pictura, ut scribis. Primum quidem Aurifaber non confidit se super argenteam totius Globi faciem, aureas stellas, cum debita imaginum repræsentatione, inducere posse. Etenim multis rationibus apud me nunc, tum apud te antea id explicavit. Quod ad Pictorem attinet, ille nunquam circulos illos et divisiones tam exacte, ut nunc insculptæ, exprimet, sed incrustabit quidem colorum crusta superficiem tersam et levem, quæ obtenebrabunt ea, quæ Aurifaber subtiliter delineavit. Ut interim sileam, quomodocunque hoc fuerit, non fore tandem diuturnum (etenim multis illa via incommodis obnoxia est) quam cito poterit vel læsione aliqua vel fortuito attactu pars crustæ coloratæ enucleari?

Sed audi meum consilium, quod ex Aurifabri quoque sententia tibi denuncio. Opus est nobile et regium imo aureum. Et quia Horizon et Meridianus et pedes deaurari debent, agedum deauremus prius totam superficiem Globi, hac tamen ratione, ut imagines ipsæ una cum stellis appareant minus tersæ: das ist matt, ut illi loquuntur. Partes autem intermediæ, seu superficies ipsa Globi polienda erit, et ad micationem et splendorem rutilantem deducenda, qui quoque splendor in ipsis stellis (si voluerimus) indui possit, ut iubar illud aureum, tanquam scintillans ex sublustri imaginis lumine, emicet.

Ita et forma illa stellarum insculpta exacte repræsentabit, quarum cuspidum subtilitatem coloribus æmulari, difficile est. Illi enim tenues illas cuspides et cavitates, quæ toti figuræ decus adijciant, oblinient, et replebunt venustate istorum angulorum deformata. Ita tandem aureus erit Globus, simplicitate cœli materiam et faciem æmulans, sic pedes capiti respondebunt, longe ablegatis versicoloribus et muliebribus illis oblectamentis oculorum: quæ quosdam delectant, ut monstra in picturis. Cedant variegationes instabiles, color unicus esto: color aureus esto. Sic non, si cervix equina humano capiti adiuncta fuerit, in piscem desinet atrum.

Sed mi TYCHO, hoc meum consilium est, tuo illæso et salvo iudicio. Ad pedem, Meridianum et Horizontem deaurandum significat se opus habere ad minimum 4 roseis nobel: si nostrum consilium amplexus fueris, addantur 3 vel 4 ad summum, et totum sese oculis nostris Silenus ille aperiet aureus. Tu imprimis sententiam tuam super hac re significa, et Aurifaber dicit se aurum hîc habere non posse: tuum erit ad pedes et Meridianum et Horizontem 4 nobiles rosatos, ut dixi, inprimis mittere. STEPHANUS hodie vel cras imponet rotulas, et experietur processum, urgebo quantum potero et promovebo. Instrumentum ferreum bene procedit, hac septimana, imo vesperi absolvetur, ut promisit Artifex.

Quod ad Typographum attinet, post prandium intelliges, tunc plura addam, iam enim animus est in patinis. Figuræ nunc excinduntur, instigo quantum possum. De papyro difficultas magna est: sed tamen corrademus. De epistola mea et carmine, quia nondum propter occupationes confecta sunt, ne sis solicitus, quoniam tota posterior pars operis primum absolvenda est, tuque interea nobis aderis. Sed ante 4 dies certum nuntium habebis, cum quo omnia mittam necessaria.

Titulum quoque una mitto, quem præfigemus operi; consulimus, ut ita relinquas. Etenim singula verba perlustravimus diligenter, sed si quid mutari voles, fiet id ubi veneris satis mature, quoniam titulus postremo cudetur. De reliquis iubeo te non esse sollicitum: curabimus ea diligenter. Verbum illud, quod titulo assuisti LVCVBRATIONES, nescio, an hîc locum habere possit. Nocturnæ quidem sunt, sed an lucubrationibus Atticis respondeant, etiamsi non dubitem: tamen ne magno aliquid hiatu promittamus et speciosis titulis et vastis fores exornemus, author sum. Non, quod etiam augustiore inscriptione dignum hoc opus tuum non iudicem: sed ut moderata illa submissiore in ipso limine adhibita, in ipso operis incalescentis progressu, potentius in animum lectorum, tantorum mysteriorum non præscium illabamur. Summa, titulum hunc immutare quoties libuerit, et Genialis tulerit impetus, dabitur occasio. Si pro contemplatione mathematica considerationem mathematicam poni voles, unum erit.

Lucubrationibus operam dant nigra lolligine ad lucernam chartas imbuentes, lucubrationibus pallescunt ad oleum legentes. Lucubrantur quoque qui ad mediam noctem vel Auroram ipsam madidi sunt. Et aurigis nautisque, si quibus alijs, suæ sunt lucubrationes. Nos exemti prostituta illa æquivocatione vocabuli, rerumque Domini facti, homonymias in eas terras relegabimus, in quibus vivos homines mortui incursant boves. Sed hæc post prandium. Nos sacra inebriet ambitio, non e vulgi fornicationibus petita, sed e castis Uraniæ et sororum Vestalium adytis etc.

Pro lignis mihi transmissis et dono datis gratias ago quam possum maximas, relaturus si potero. Ita hominum bonorum res comparata est, ut alij aliorum beneficis egeant. Vos tanto culmine mundi positi nobis inferioribus Dij esse potestis aut lupi, si exteriorem Anatomiam consideremus. Si abditam illam et interiorem, tu quoque et par et superior esse et potes, et, si cunabula vestra spectetis, debetis merito. Tu non exutus exteriori vinculo, etiam interiore nobiscum libertate triumphas, qua civitate donatus et eiusdem Reipublicæ civis et Augustus semper. Reliqua occupat fornicaria et infidum vulgus pellicibus satiatur suis. Illa pulchritudo te cæcum habet, oculis orbate sensilibus lucem ne respice.

Vale. Hafniæ raptissime 16. Aprilis Anni 1573.

Pratensis

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the truly noble and high-born Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, best of patrons and unfeigned friend. Greeting, and a spring of honey.

I have received your letter, excellent Tycho, in which you commit to me the furthering of your business with your mechanical craftsmen. That this courier of yours comes back to you later than expected was owing to the Roskilde fair, which held me beyond what I had intended: I had to wait there until I could get certain word of my cask and of the goods sent from Frankfurt by the Flensburg merchants.

Your affairs stand thus. All the figures on your globe are finished. The meridian and the horizon are divided, the pedestal is ready — but here the difficulty is not small: namely the gilding, and the colouring or painting, as you write. First, the goldsmith has no confidence that he can lay golden stars, with the figures duly represented, over the silvered face of the whole globe; he has explained this to me now at length, as he did to you before. As for the painter: he will never render those circles and divisions as exactly as they now stand engraved. He will merely crust over the clean, smooth surface with a crust of colours, and these will darken what the goldsmith has finely drawn. And let me say nothing, meanwhile, of the fact that however it turns out it will not last — for that method is exposed to many mishaps: how quickly might a piece of the coloured crust be picked away by some knock or a chance touch?

But hear my counsel, which I give you with the goldsmith's judgement behind it. The work is noble and royal — nay, golden. And since the horizon, the meridian and the feet must be gilded, come then: let us gild the whole surface of the globe first, but on this principle — that the figures themselves, together with the stars, should appear less burnished: das ist matt, as they say. The parts between, that is the surface of the globe itself, must be polished and brought to a glittering, ruddy brilliance; and that brilliance could be laid on the stars themselves too (if we wish), so that the golden radiance may flash out as if sparkling from the faint light of the figure.

In that way the engraved form of the stars will also represent them exactly — stars whose fineness of point it is hard to rival in colours. For the painters will smear over those slender points and hollows that give the whole figure its beauty, and will fill them in, the grace of those angles being spoilt. So at last the globe will be golden, rivalling in its simplicity the substance and the face of heaven; so the feet will answer to the head, once those particoloured, womanish delights of the eye are sent packing — the things that please certain people, as monsters do in paintings. Let unstable variegation give way; let there be one colour only: let the colour be gold. Then it will not happen that, a horse's neck having been joined to a human head, the thing tails off into a black fish.

But, my Tycho, this is my counsel — your own judgement untouched and intact. For gilding the foot, the meridian and the horizon he indicates that he needs at least 4 rose nobles; if you adopt our plan, let 3 or 4 more be added at the most, and that golden Silenus will open himself whole to our eyes. Signify your view on this above all — and the goldsmith says he cannot get gold here: it will be for you to send, first of all, the 4 rose nobles for the feet, the meridian and the horizon, as I have said. Stephanus will fit the little wheels today or tomorrow and will test the working of it; I shall press and further it as much as I can. The iron instrument is coming on well: it will be finished this week — indeed this evening, as the craftsman has promised.

As for the printer, you shall hear after lunch; then I shall add more, for at this moment my mind is on the dishes. The figures are being cut now; I urge it on as much as I can. About the paper there is great difficulty — but we shall scrape some together. As for my letter and my poem: do not be anxious that they are not yet finished, my occupations being what they are, since the whole latter part of the work has to be completed first, and you will be with us in the meantime. But within four days you will have a sure messenger, by whom I shall send everything needful.

I send the title as well, which we shall prefix to the work; we advise you to leave it as it stands, for we have gone over every single word with care. But if you should want anything altered, it can be done when you come, in good time, since the title is struck off last. About the rest I bid you not to worry: we shall see to it diligently. That word which you have stitched onto the title — LUCUBRATIONES — I do not know whether it can have a place here. Nocturnal these studies certainly are; but whether they answer to the Attic lucubrations — though I do not doubt it — still I am of the opinion that we should not promise something with a great gaping mouth, nor dress out the doorway with showy and cavernous titles. Not that I judge this work of yours unworthy even of a more august inscription; but that, a modest and more subdued one being used on the very threshold, we may steal the more powerfully into the minds of readers — who are not forewarned of such great mysteries — as the work warms to its progress. In sum, there will be occasion to change this title as often as you please, and as the genial impulse takes you. If you should want mathematical consideration put in place of mathematical contemplation, it will be all one.

They give their labour to "lucubrations" who steep their paper in black cuttlefish-ink by lamplight; readers grow pale over the oil at their "lucubrations". They "lucubrate" too who are soused till midnight or till dawn itself. And carters and sailors, if anyone, have lucubrations of their own. Let us, freed from that prostituted equivocation of the word, and made masters of the matter, banish such homonymies to those lands where dead oxen charge at living men. But of this after lunch. Let a sacred ambition intoxicate us — one sought not from the fornications of the crowd, but from the chaste sanctuaries of Urania and her Vestal sisters, etc.

For the timber sent me and given as a gift I give the greatest thanks I can, and shall repay it if I am able. Such is the condition of good men, that some have need of the kindnesses of others. You who are set at so great a height of the world can be to us below either gods or wolves, if we consider the outward anatomy; if that hidden and inward one, then you too can be both our equal and our superior — and, if you look to your cradle, you deservedly ought to be. You, not stripped of the outward bond, yet triumph with us in an inward liberty; endowed with that citizenship, a citizen of that same commonwealth, and always Augustus. The rest is taken up by the harlot, and the faithless crowd is sated with its concubines. That beauty holds you blind; you who are bereft of the eyes of sense, do not look back at the light.

Farewell. Copenhagen, in the greatest haste, 16 April 1573.

Pratensis

Apparatus

  • Correction of record — this letter is not lost. This edition previously published the letter as "(lost; editorial note only)", graded Possible, on the ground that the only surviving text was Dreyer's six-word note Responsio ad has interijt… ("the reply to these has perished"). That was an artefact of a defective transcription, not of the tradition. The harvested tekstnet text captured only the first block of the page — the editorial loss-note that stands before the letter — and dropped the letter itself. The letter survives complete: tekstnet prints it in full, and its own source-line cites TBOO VII pp. 10–13, four printed pages. The loss-note refers to a different, genuinely lost reply, and is retained above the letter as apparatus, where it belongs. The Latin and translation above are new.
  • Correspondent. Johannes Pratensis = Hans Philipsen Pratensis (1543–1576), professor of medicine at Copenhagen from 1571, Paracelsian, and Tycho's closest friend of these years; Tycho would write his epitaph in 1576. He is here acting as Tycho's agent in Copenhagen, managing both craftsmen and printer while Tycho is away (at Herrevad).
  • The globe. The object is a metal celestial globe with engraved constellation figures on a silvered surface, with a divided meridian and horizon ring and a pedestal — not the great wooden Augsburg globe. The crux of the letter is a finishing dispute: the goldsmith (Aurifaber) cannot lay gold stars over silver; the painter (Pictor) would smother the engraving under a crust of pigment. Pratensis's solution — gild the whole globe, leave the figures and stars matt and burnish the ground between them to a ruddy lustre, so the figures read as matt gold against bright gold — is argued on grounds at once technical and aesthetic. The German gloss das ist matt, quoted as the craftsmen's own word, is the vocabulary of the Copenhagen workshop breaking into the Latin.
  • Cost. The gilding of foot, meridian and horizon needs at least 4 rose nobles, with 3 or 4 more if the whole-globe scheme is adopted; and "the goldsmith says he cannot get gold here" — Tycho must send the coin itself. A rare, concrete costing of a Tychonic instrument at the point of manufacture.
  • The printing of De nova stella — the letter's principal historical value. The "work" whose figures are being cut, whose paper is scarce, and whose title is under negotiation is Tycho's first book, De nova stella (Copenhagen: Lorentz Benedicht, 1573). Three things follow:
  • Pratensis promises "my letter and my poem" (de epistola mea et carmine) for the volume. Both are in the printed book: the prefatory letter of Johannes Pratensis ("TYCHONI BRAHE OTTHONIDI… IOHANNES PRATENSIS, MEDICUS, S. D.") stands among its preliminaries.
  • Tycho had wanted LUCUBRATIONES ("Nocturnal Studies") on the title-page, and Pratensis talks him out of it — objecting that the word is debased by equivocation (scribblers, drinkers, carters and sailors all "lucubrate") and that a title should not "dress out the doorway with showy and cavernous titles." Lucubrationes does not appear on the printed title-page.
  • Pratensis offers Tycho a straight choice: "if you should want mathematical consideration put in place of mathematical contemplation, it will be all one." The printed title reads …Contemplatio Mathematica. The letter therefore catches the title of Tycho's first book at the moment it was settled, and shows which counsel prevailed.
  • Classical texture. The Latin is dense with allusion, and the translation preserves rather than flattens it: "a horse's neck joined to a human head… tails off into an ugly fish" and "monsters in paintings" are Horace, Ars Poetica 1–5, turned into an argument against polychromy; the golden Silenus that "will open himself whole to our eyes" is the Sileni Alcibiadis of Erasmus's Adagia (the coarse figurine that opens to disclose a god), here inverted — the outside is to be gold as well; "my mind is on the dishes" (animus est in patinis) is proverbial (Terence, Eunuchus 816). The closing turn — a sacred ambition drawn "from the chaste sanctuaries of Urania and her Vestal sisters" — names the muse Tycho would take for his own.
  • Calendar. Dated Hafniæ … 16. Aprilis Anni 1573. Denmark was on the Julian calendar; the date is Old Style, reproduced as written and not converted.
  • Flagged crux. The final clause, "oculis orbate sensilibus lucem ne respice", is syntactically strained (orbate taken as a vocative of orbatus) and its sense is compressed; it is rendered close to the Latin rather than smoothed, and should be checked against Dreyer's text before it is quoted.
  • Correction. "tails off into an ugly fish" corrected to "tails off into a black fish", restoring the colour-term atrum that Pratensis's Latin carries (Dreyer, TBOO VII, p.11) and that echoes Horace's Ars Poetica.
