The original

ILLUSTRI ET GENEROSO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, ASTRONOMORUM NOSTRI SECULI PRINCIPI, DOMINO ET PATRONO DILECTISSIMO.

S. P. D.

ILLUSTRIS et Generose Domine TYCHO, patrone observandissime, ex litteris, quas proxime per M. CHRISTIANUM misi, Generositati Vestræ innotescere potuit, quo in statu sint res Transylvani, qui ad Generositatem Vestram excurrerat. Petijt is interim aliquoties a me, ut eum apud Generositatem Vestram humiliter excusarem. Idoneum alium nullum possum exquirere, ut ut percuperem. Forsan autem in viciniâ vestrâ aliquis oblatus est.

Benedictus sit Dominus Deus noster, qui iam tandem Angelo percutienti iuga Bohemica ex misericordiâ suâ frenum iniecit. Is clementissime Halcyonia et aërem saluberrimum unâ cum longâ vitâ Generositati Vestræ concedat, ut in emolumentum publicorum studiorum et ad gloriam nominis divini omnia illa, quæ feliciter cœpit, ad exoptatissimum finem perducere possit. Speramus etiam rabiem Theologicam interim ibi nonnihil deferbuisse. Etsi vero nihil scriptione magnopere dignum nunc temporis habebam, tamen D. D. IESSENIUM, communem amicum nostrum, tanquam planetam ab Eclipticâ Witebergensi nunc iterum in latitudinem meridionalem Bohemicam excurrentem, sine aliquibus meis ad Generositatem Vestram pervenire nolui. Commendo Generositatem Vestram cum omnibus eidem addictis divinæ protectioni fideliter.

Witebergæ dominicâ Lætare, non secundum Lilianos, sed secundum Iulianos fastos, licet nunc concurrant in paucis, anno Christi 1600, quem tamen præter IOSEPHUM SCALIGERUM etiam BARTHOLOMEUS SCULTETUS 1601 esse comprobat.
G. V. studiosiss.
WOLFG. FRANZIUS

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious and generous man, Master Tycho Brahe, prince of the astronomers of our age, lord and most beloved patron.

Hearty greeting.

Illustrious and generous Master Tycho, most observed patron: from the letters which I lately sent through Master Christian [Longomontanus], it could become known to Your Generosity in what state are the affairs of the Transylvanian who had run off to Your Generosity. He has meanwhile asked me several times to excuse him humbly with Your Generosity. I can seek out no other suitable [man], however much I might wish to. But perhaps in your vicinity someone has presented himself.

Blessed be the Lord our God, who has now at last, out of his mercy, cast a bridle on the Angel smiting the Bohemian regions. May He most clemently grant Halcyon days and most healthful air, together with long life, to Your Generosity, so that — to the profit of public studies and to the glory of the divine name — you may be able to bring all those things which you have happily begun to a most-wished-for end. We hope, too, that the Theological frenzy has meanwhile cooled down somewhat there. And although I had nothing now greatly worth the writing, yet I did not wish Doctor Jessenius, our common friend — like a planet now again running out from the Wittenberg Ecliptic into Bohemian southern latitude — to reach Your Generosity without some lines of mine. I faithfully commend Your Generosity, with all those devoted to you, to the divine protection.

Wittenberg, on Laetare Sunday — not according to the Lilian [Gregorian], but according to the Julian calendar, though they now coincide in but few [points] — in the year of Christ 1600, which, however, besides Joseph Scaliger, Bartholomäus Scultetus too proves to be 1601.
Most zealous of Your Generosity,
Wolfgang Franzius.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Wolfgang Franz(ius) (1564–1628), theologian at Wittenberg; a learned, witty letter from Tycho's Saxon circle.
  • The Transylvanian. res Transylvani, qui ad Generositatem Vestram excurrerat — an unnamed Transylvanian who had "run off" to Tycho and whom Franzius cannot replace; he asks Tycho to excuse him. (Background to Tycho's shifting household of assistants.) M. Christianus = Longomontanus, the earlier courier.
  • The plague lifting. Angelo percutienti iuga Bohemica … frenum iniecit — "[God] has cast a bridle on the Angel smiting the Bohemian regions" (the plague-angel; iuga = "ridges/yokes," here the lands of Bohemia): the abatement of the Bohemian plague of 1599–1600 (cf. the plague at Benátky in brahe-t_15991214001). Halcyonia = "halcyon [calm] days." rabies Theologica = the "Theological frenzy" (the confessional/dogmatic controversies) — hoped to have cooled.
  • Jessenius as a "planet." D. D. Iessenium … tanquam planetam ab Ecliptica Witebergensi … in latitudinem meridionalem Bohemicam excurrentem — a charming astronomical conceit for Jan Jessenius (Jan Jesenský, 1566–1621), the anatomist and Tycho's friend (later author of Tycho's funeral oration), here "running south," like a planet leaving the ecliptic, from Wittenberg to Bohemia — and carrying this very letter.
  • ⚠ Calendar — and the year-number dispute. Franzius dates pointedly dominicâ Laetare … non secundum Lilianos, sed secundum Iulianos fastos — "on Laetare Sunday (the 4th of Lent), by the Julian, not the Lilian [Gregorian, after Aloysius Lilius] calendar." In 1600 (Julian) Easter fell on 23 March O.S.; Laetare Sunday = 2 March 1600 O.S., matching the identifier. His "they now coincide in but few [points]" is a learned aside on the two calendars. He adds the century-numbering controversy: that the year "is 1601" according to Joseph Scaliger and Bartholomäus Scultetus (the Görlitz mathematician) — i.e. the debate over whether 1600 or 1601 completed the century. All preserved, not "corrected." Wittenberg (Lutheran) = O.S.
