CORDATISSIME mi OLIGERE, cognate desideratissime et affinis exoptatissime. Postquam priores hisce .......................... viserint tandem an non potius sui ipsius, et patriæ, cuius honori studere merito debuissent, maiore incommodo quam meo ................ nostræ satis arguunt compositoris malevolum animum pari præiudicio et avaritiæ nefanda labe infectum:

English translation machine translation, Secure

To a man conspicuous in the first rank not only by the nobility of his lineage, but also by that of his spirit and his talent, Master Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, my kinsman, relation by marriage, and most upright friend.

Heartiest greeting.

Most sensible Holger, my most longed-for kinsman and most desired relation, after my earlier [letters?] with these …………………… [they?] shall at last have seen whether [it is] not rather to the greater detriment of themselves and of the homeland — to whose honour they ought by rights to have devoted themselves — than to mine …………… our [affairs?] sufficiently prove the ill-willed disposition of the contriver, infected alike with prejudice and with the abominable taint of avarice:

Alas, ye gods above, to what do you not drive mortal hearts —
the accursed thirst of the Rosy fount, and the sacred prebends to be bestowed!

It is to be lamented, indeed lamented beyond measure, that this young King — otherwise of his own nature capable of virtue and endowed with a generous spirit — is so blinded by such purblind men [glaucomatistae] and led into byways, that he cannot perceive and feel that which is true and right, and perhaps more honourable to the Kingdom and to himself, in lasting longer, than the many other things that are done and poured out to no purpose — being, as it were, one who both sees with others' eyes and hears with others' ears. There will come a time perhaps when he too will be made more clear-sighted and more hearing (experience and the practice of affairs thus counselling). But as far as concerns me and my studies, so long laboured through, [it will be] too late. I will rather forestall [it], and, with divine providence favouring me ………… while time still remains …………, and now the die is cast, the dice counted [are?] thrown ……… May it go well with the King, I pray — even though he is, beyond any fault or wrong on my part, not favourable to me. May it go well with you all …………… especially with the more upright. May it go well with all Denmark, I wish from my heart.

…………… to God …………… fortune, let us follow. And now farewell, my most beloved Holger, and what is wanting here, read in my earlier [letters] and in the postscripts; and never cease to return Tycho's love sincerely, as wholly your own — which indeed you will do of your own accord.

And again and yet again, farewell.
Given in haste at Wandsbek near Hamburg, on the 16th day of November in the year [15]97.
Your intimate, and your outermost,
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

POSTSCRIPT.

Most beloved Holger, if your affairs would in any way allow it, I should wish for nothing more than that, the Royal Wedding once concluded, you would be willing to come to Breitenburg, and there — the time having first been indicated to me, and that through a sure and faithful go-between — to meet me: partly for the sake of visiting that excellent old man Rantzau, our common kinsman and a most upright friend; partly so that you might survey his library, than which Germany scarcely has an ampler or more splendid one, whether the Statthalter be there at the time or not — for access is always free to me, nor do I doubt that it is to you as well, whom he likewise loves from the heart, as your worth deserves; and partly also, that we might address one another once more, if oftener is not allowed, before we are separated by a greater distance — which not only the tie of blood and the bond of friendship, but also the new kinship between us, demands. Nor indeed are you unaware that, after her father, I am the nearest — indeed the first — of the bride's kin, and therefore not to be neglected or passed over, as in other matters you have scarcely allowed me to be. And these will be sufficient reasons for you — nay, even any one of them alone — that you should go there, whether leave be obtained or not. And if you should judge it more prudent to say nothing of me, and to keep that a secret to yourself alone, I will gladly grant it. For it is all the same to me, whether anyone acts openly or secretly on my account, provided he does what is fair and what the matter requires. Only let me know at once what can be accomplished by you in these matters, so that I too may in the meantime so arrange my own affairs the more conveniently.

I do not now add a copy of the recent letters, of which I spoke in my earlier ones. You shall have it another time, or when we meet, if it is even now kept hidden in your Chancery, as things that hate the light are wont to be. For of your goodwill I do not doubt at all.

When, about a month ago, [the fair] weather inviting me, I resumed my astronomical observations here at Wandsbek, and wished to append these later ones in the book in which I had recorded the earlier ones, ending around Uraniborg at the vernal equinox — so that the occasion of their being interrupted for so long a time, beyond what was usual, might not in future slip from my memory or others' (though that is scarcely likely to happen) — I set out there first, in prose, the reasons, briefly comprised, for my departure from the Island of Hven, and indeed from the whole of Denmark; and presently, my genius dictating, there fell out an elegiac poem, which I appended there together with it. Of this I now make you a partaker, since I know you delight in poetic things, and have drawn tuneful waters from the Pegasean springs. If, then, you too should reply in kind — not, indeed, for the sake of consoling me, since you know that I think there is nothing harsh in these things, but rather that all works to my advantage — but so as to compose a Propempticon for Urania, migrating with me from your Denmark into other, more gracious and convenient places, and so to congratulate us both, you will render a thing not merely pleasant but also welcome, to her as much as to me. Nor will it be necessary to disclose the author's name: just as I wish my own [verses] to be kept secret with you, and nothing of them to be communicated to anyone. Rather, once you have read it, you should hand it over to Vulcan. It is enough to keep alive in practice that gift in which you excel, since you remember well enough, and more than enough, that this has always been so. And now, farewell many times over. Given as above in the letter.

T. B.

A SECOND POSTSCRIPT, OF LESSER MOMENT.

Since that Bailiff whom, on departing, I set over our Hven — David Petri by name — has been made suspect to me by that former Parish Priest of the Island, while he was lately dealing with me here (I know not whether from some private hatred, or from the truth of the matter), and related, among other things, that he, while in service to your father of blessed memory, had defrauded him of some hundreds, I ask that you be willing to indicate to me, in passing, on some note, what you have ascertained about this Bailiff's honesty or otherwise. For I do not know how far that Parish Priest is to be trusted, both because he was ill-disposed toward the other, and because, before his departure, he scorned me — who wished him extremely well — beyond what could be believed, so much so that he did not blush to utter insults and slanders to my face for no cause; which, however, bearing patiently, I laughed off loftily, and dismissed the man, as one not in possession of himself, with travel-money and a gift added. This I wished you to know, lest for my sake you should be further troubled about him. You will learn more from our Eskild Bille, who has the proceedings set down in writing and attested. Given as in the earlier letters.

T. B.

Apparatus

  • Sender / context. Tycho Brahe, writing from Wandsbek (Wandsburg), the castle near Hamburg held by his patron Heinrich Rantzau, where Tycho lodged in the autumn of 1597 after his departure from Denmark and Hven (he left in the spring/summer of 1597). The letter's bitterness toward "the young King" and his counsellors belongs to this moment of grievance over the loss of his royal support.
  • Recipient. Oligerus Rosaekrantzius de Rosenholm = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), the Danish nobleman of Rosenholm, theologian and humanist, Tycho's kinsman and relation by marriage (consanguineus, affinis). The address-superscription's word-play on generis / genij / ingenij (lineage / spirit / talent) is kept in the rendering.
  • Place. Wandesburgo prope Hamburgum = Wandsbek, then a Holstein estate of Heinrich Rantzau, now a district of Hamburg. (A companion letter to Rosenkrantz of 7 November 1597 is dated "Wandesburgo ex arce Ranzoviana" — "from the Rantzovian castle" — fixing the residence.)
  • "The young King." iuvenem hunc Regem = Christian IV of Denmark (b. 1577), who had assumed personal rule in August 1596 (aged 19); Tycho blames not the King himself but the counsellors who, he says, blind him.
  • Verse (the centre of the letter). Tycho adapts Virgil, Aeneid III.56–57: "Quid non mortalia pectora cogis, / auri sacra fames!" ("To what do you not drive mortal hearts, accursed thirst for gold!"). Tycho keeps the first line nearly verbatim (Proh superi quid non mortalia pectora cogis) and rewrites the second as a pun: "Sacra sitis Rosei fontis, praebendaque sacra!" — "the accursed thirst of the Rosy fount, and the sacred prebends." Rosei fontis ("of the rosy/Rosen fount") puns on the name Rosenkrantz / Rosenholm and/or on the courtier faction; praebenda are ecclesiastical/court prebends (stipendiary benefices). The line is kept as verse. Sacra is used antithetically — "accursed" (the Virgilian sacra = "execrable") in the first sense, "sacred/holy" (the benefices) in the second; the bitter irony is in the source and is preserved, not smoothed.
  • glaucomatistae — "purblind men," literally those afflicted with glaucoma (clouded sight): a coinage of contempt for the King's counsellors, picked up by the following "sees with others' eyes, hears with others' ears." Kept as metaphor.
  • compositor — compositoris malevolum animum = "the ill-willed disposition of the contriver/intriguer." Here compositor is the schemer who "composed" the situation against Tycho, not a typesetter; the referent (a specific hostile counsellor) is left unnamed in the text and so is not supplied here.
  • alea iacta est — "the die is cast" (the Caesarian/Suetonian tag); followed by numeratae tesserae re…… "the dice counted … [thrown?]." The verb after re is lost in the lacuna; [thrown] is offered conjecturally in square brackets (e.g. reiectae / reiacta) and flagged — the source breaks off.
  • Tuus intimus et extimus — "Your intimate, and your outermost": a paradoxical sign-off (innermost in affection, outermost — i.e. furthest off / on the periphery — in place or fortune), rendered literally and left as the pointed flourish it is.
  • ex asse tuum — "wholly your own" (ex asse = "to the whole, entirely," the Roman inheritance idiom).
  • Lacunae (textual crux — the governing limitation). The sole witness is a later afskrift (copy) in Langebek's Diplomatarium at the Rigsarkivet; it is damaged/illegible in many places, marked here by runs of dots exactly as transmitted. The lacunae fall in load-bearing positions — the opening conditional period (Postquam priores hisce … viserint … nostrae satis arguunt), the Praeveniam … alea iacta est sentence, and the closing Deo tra…… fortuna, sequamur clause — so the connective logic of several sentences is genuinely uncertain. No lacuna has been filled in the translation beyond the single bracketed conjecture [thrown] and bracketed clarifying subjects ([letters?], [affairs?], [they?]) flagged with question marks; nothing is silently restored.
  • Negations. The source carries three load-bearing negatives, all preserved: an non potius sui ipsius … incommodo ("whether not rather to [their own] detriment"); mihi praeter culpam et iniuriam non faventi ("not favourable to me, beyond any fault or wrong [on my part]"); and nunquam desiste ("never cease"). None added, none dropped.
  • Calendar. die 16. Novembris Anni 97 = 16 November 1597, Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline matches the catalogue label; no discrepancy. Dabantur ("Given/Issued") kept.
  • What the recovered text is. Both postscripts to Tycho's 16 November 1597 letter to Holger ("Oliger") Rosenkrantz — the main POSTSCRIPTA and the shorter ALTERA POSTSCRIPTA — were entirely absent from the prior transcription; only the letter's final valediction survived as the published tail.
  • Addressee and the new kinship. Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), later a leading Danish statesman and theologian, is here anticipating marriage into Tycho's own family: Thoren records that "in the late 1590s, Holger married a daughter of... Axel Brahe (Tycho's brother)" [Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg], and Christianson identifies her as Tycho's niece Sophie Axelsdatter Brahe, who with Holger "maintained a learned household" at Rosenholm comparable to Uraniborg [Christianson, On Tycho's Island]. This dates and explains the letter's talk of a "nova inter nos affinitas" (new kinship) and Tycho's claim to be, after the bride's father, her nearest kinsman.
  • The Royal Wedding. "Peractis Regijs Nuptijs" refers to the wedding of King Christian IV to Anna Catherine of Brandenburg, held at Haderslevhus in November 1597 — the very event this letter, dated 16 November 1597, anticipates. Christianson records that Heinrich Rantzau himself was travelling to attend the same wedding, and that Tycho corresponded with him about it at just this time [Christianson, On Tycho's Island].
  • Rantzau and Breitenburg. "Bredenberga" is Rantzau's seat of Breitenburg, whose library Tycho calls the ampler and more splendid than any other in Germany — consistent with Lohmeier's account of the Breitenburg library (c. 6,300 volumes by 1590). Tycho was resident at Rantzau's Wandsbek castle when writing.
  • A lost occasional poem. Tycho reports composing, about a month earlier (i.e. October 1597), a prose account of his reasons for leaving Hven and Denmark plus an accompanying elegiac Latin poem, entered into his observing log to mark the resumption of his astronomical observations at Wandsbek after the interruption since leaving Hven in March 1597. He asks Holger, in strict confidence — "hand it over to Vulcan" (burn it) once read — to compose a companion Propempticon (a classical send-off/farewell-poem genre) for "Urania" migrating with him from Denmark. This is a new, previously unpublished data point on Tycho's own poetic self-fashioning of the 1597 departure.
  • David Petri and Eskild Bille. The second postscript concerns David Petri, i.e. David Pedersen, independently confirmed as "Tycho Brahe's bailiff on Hven 1597-1601" left in charge of Uraniborg and Stjerneborg on Tycho's 1597 departure [Christianson, On Tycho's Island, Biographical Directory s.v. "Pedersen, David"]. Hven's former parish priest had accused David of once defrauding Holger's late father while in his service; Tycho, distrusting the priest (whom he says he dismissed with severance pay despite the priest's insults), asks Holger's independent judgment and names "Eschillus Bille" — Eskild (Eske) Bille — as holding the documented record; a letter from Eske Bille to Tycho is independently attested in Zeeberg's edition of the Sophie Brahe/Urania Titani correspondence register.
