The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM.

NOBILISSIME ET MAGNIFICE VIR, DOMINE ET AMICE HONORANDE.

ETSI tam propriâ notitiâ quam relatu familiaris mei DANIELIS FELS, qui aliquandiu Pilsnae apud vos substitit, satis exploratam habeo molem occupationum Mag. tuae, quae parum illi temporis legendis et scribendis epistolis relinquit: non esse tamen illi ingratas meas literas, tanto confidentius mihi persuadeo, quanto minus, urgentibus rebus, a scribendo abstinere possum.

Mitto ad Mag. tuam Nobilem et egregie doctum Iuvenem FRANCISCUM TENGNAGL, quo de iam antea, cum Pragae essem, cum Maga tua locutus sum, quod nonnulla mihi negotia, quae M. tuae recensebam, apud Reverendissimum et Serenissimum Archiepiscopum et Electorem Coloniensem expediverit. Fuit is primum in Dania meus aliquandiu domesticus, inde meae migrationis comes in Germaniam. Cum Latinam et Gallicam linguam calleret, Italiam etiam adivit, eamque linguam primum in aula Magni Ducis Hetruriae, postea Romae in aula R. Cardinalis a Dietrichstein probe addidicit. Nuper vero inde reversus in animum induxit primo patriam, quae est in extremis Westphaliae finibus, [et] parentes suos invisere, post, ubi huc, favente Numine, redierit, S. Caes. Mti, Domino nostro clementissimo, si Maiestas eius hac ipsum clementiâ dignata fuerit, in ministerijs aulicis, quibuscunque eidem usui esse possit, operam fidelissimam subiectissime offerre. Est in eo, praeter singularem doctrinam studiorumque cum politicorum tum philosophicorum cognitionem, virtutis amor, morum integritas et gratia: quod facile M. tua deprehendet, ubi notitiam eius qualemcumque ceperit. Rogo itaque M. tuam quam officiosissime, uti sibi hunc iuvenem admodum commendatum habeat, consilioque et intercessione suâ ad optima quaeque promoveat. Commisi praeterea illi paucula quaedam de meis negocijs nondum omnino expeditis, quae M. tuae, quando commodum erit, vel in memoriam revocet, vel a me significet. Quae M. tuae ad ea respondere placuerit, huic concredat, si non per ipsum, quem an hac viâ remeaturus sit, incertus sum, tum per antedictum DANIELEM, quem unâ mitto, ad me referenda.

Valeat Ma tua quam diutissime et felicissime.
Dabantur ex Arce Caesareanâ Benaticâ die 6 Martij 1600.
Magae Tuae studiosiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to an anonymous [correspondent].

Most noble and magnificent man, lord and friend to be honoured.

Although, both from my own knowledge and from the report of my familiar Daniel Fels — who has stayed for some while at Pilsen with you — I am well enough acquainted with the mass of Your Magnificence's occupations, which leaves you little time for reading and writing letters, yet that my letters are not unwelcome to you I persuade myself the more confidently, the less, with matters pressing, I can refrain from writing.

I send to Your Magnificence the noble and excellently learned young man Franz Tengnagel, of whom I already spoke before with Your Magnificence, when I was at Prague — [asking] that he might expedite for me certain matters which I laid before Your Magnificence, with the Most Reverend and Most Serene Archbishop and Elector of Cologne. He was first, in Denmark, for some time a member of my household; thereafter the companion of my migration into Germany. Being well versed in the Latin and French tongues, he went also to Italy, and learned that language thoroughly — first at the court of the Grand Duke of Tuscany, afterward at Rome, at the court of the Reverend Cardinal von Dietrichstein. Lately, however, returned thence, he has resolved first to visit his homeland, which lies in the farthest bounds of Westphalia, and his parents; afterward, when he shall have returned hither, God favouring, to offer most submissively his most faithful service to His Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord — should His Majesty deem him worthy of this clemency — in whatever courtly offices he can be of use. There is in him, besides singular learning and a knowledge of studies both political and philosophical, a love of virtue, integrity of character, and grace — which Your Magnificence will readily perceive, once you have taken any acquaintance of him at all. I therefore ask Your Magnificence most dutifully to hold this young man highly commended, and by your counsel and intercession to advance him to all that is best. I have besides entrusted to him a few small matters concerning my business not yet wholly dispatched, which he may, when convenient, either recall to Your Magnificence's memory or signify on my behalf. Whatever Your Magnificence shall be pleased to answer to these, let him entrust to this man, to be reported back to me — if not through himself (for I am uncertain whether he will return by this route), then through the aforesaid Daniel, whom I send along with him.

May Your Magnificence fare as long and as happily as possible.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 6th day of March 1600.
Most devoted to Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe at Benátky nad Jizerou.
  • Recipient. Anonymous in the source (ad anonymum): a high imperial official resident at Pilsen (Plzeň), where Rudolf II's court had withdrawn — addressed throughout as Magnificentia tua, "Your Magnificence." His identity is not recoverable from the text; he is Tycho's contact for advancing both Tengnagel's suit and Tycho's own pending business at court.
  • Purpose. A letter of recommendation carrying Franz Tengnagel to the official, doubling as a progress-note on Tycho's affairs.
  • Persons named.
  • Franciscus Tengnagl = Franz Gansneb Tengnagel von Camp (c. 1576–1622), Tycho's noble assistant and future son-in-law. The letter is a valuable biographical notice of him: first a member of Tycho's household in Denmark, then companion of Tycho's migration into Germany (1597); skilled in Latin and French; sent to Italy, where he perfected Italian, first at the court of the Grand Duke of Tuscany (Ferdinando I de' Medici, Florence), then at Rome in the court of Cardinal von Dietrichstein; now planning to visit his parents in Westphalia before entering imperial service. His earlier errand to the Archbishop-Elector of Cologne is also noted.
  • Daniel Fels = Tycho's familiaris stationed at Pilsen, his on-the-spot agent and the carrier of the reply.
  • Reverendissimus et Serenissimus Archiepiscopus et Elector Coloniensis = Ernst of Bavaria, Elector-Archbishop of Cologne, with whom Tengnagel had expedited business of Tycho's.
  • Magnus Dux Hetruriae = the Grand Duke of Tuscany (Ferdinando I de' Medici); R. Cardinalis a Dietrichstein = Cardinal Franz Seraph von Dietrichstein (created cardinal 1599), at Rome.
  • S. Caes. Maiestas, Dominus noster clementissimus = Emperor Rudolf II.
  • Places. Benatica arx = Benátky; Pilsna = Plzeň (the court's seat); Westphaliae extrema fines = Tengnagel's Westphalian homeland; Italia, Roma = Italy and Rome.
  • Epistolary formulae. Valeat … quam diutissime et felicissime → "fare as long and happily as possible"; Dabantur (epistolary) → "Given"; subscription Magae Tuae studiosiss[imus] = Magnificentiae Tuae studiosissimus → "Most devoted to Your Magnificence." Tycho's honorific oscillates between third person (illi … M. tuae) and direct address; the shift is preserved.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 6 Martij 1600; edition marks n.s. (New Style), consistent with the Benátky/Prague series of spring 1600. Reproduced, not converted.
  • Correction. Latin: 'subsistit' corrected to 'substitit' per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 256.
