The original

ERLLIIG OCH WELBYRDIG MAND TTYGE BRAHE TTILL KNUDSTRUP, MYNN ALLERKIERISTE BRODER, GANSKE HULLELIGEN OCH WENLIGENN THILLSKREUIT.

MYNN allerkieristhe broder, Gudtt allmectigsthe were nu och alltidtt hos eder och beuare eder nadelig och well fraa alltt dett, som eder kann were skadeligtt till liff eller siell, och Gud wnde migh altid att spørge, att dett maa gaa eder løkelig och well i alle maade, dett will iegh troligen ønske aff Gudtt. M. a. k. broder, giffuer iegh eder ganske wenligen advide, att iegh er nu paa reyssen medtt min Ttaghe, och fulde iegh hanem først till Hadersløff, och der iegh kom did, daa war Gertt och Ttalle affdragen hen ttill en aff deris garde, heder Lindvid, ny mille fra Harsløff, hor hand lader byghe; saa skreff di mig ttill, at jegh wille medtt Ttage kome did ttill dem och saa følgis med dem till Briedenburch, som er paa vij mille ner Hamburigh, saa ville Gertt forhøre om selskaff, som Ttage kune kome i følge medt ttill Brunsvigh och sa ud ttil Bassell, som ieg vil haa ham henn. Saa drogh jeg daa ttill denem ttill dett Lindvid, och war der i ttho eller ttre dage, och fulle denem saa hid ttill Briedenburgh och har werid her i 6 daghe, och giør di migh altt dett gode di kan, sa ieg er komen ttill rett gode wener her, som giør mig altt gott, Gudtt were louitt, och blir jeg her enu ttill paa mandagh, for da drager Gertt ttill Kill pa en ricksdagh, och systher drager ttill Harsløff igen, for di wentter kongenn did strax mod s. Mortens dagh, for der skall da were en richsdag ttill Flensburgh, hor kongen will were paa dentid. Saa actter jeg met Guds hielp att drage herfraa med Ttage ttill Wandersbeck wid Hamborig, och der were en 3 eler 4 dage och forhøre om gott selskaff, som Ttage kan komme i følge med ttill Brunsuigh, Gud giue ttill løcke.

Titans skriuer Mikell hade werid paa Hadersløff och mentt att fundet mig der for en 3 uger sinn, och der ieg ike da war der, drog han till Horssens, och fik mor fru Anne breuene till mig, och hun sene dem ouer till Køffuenhaffn, och der di kom did, war jeg aff dragen, saa sene jeg it bud fra Koling och ouer till Køffuenhaffn efftter same breff, och dett bud kom nu hid ttill mig ttill Briedenburg i andags gardtt, och fick iegh med dett bud eders søns Ttygis skriffuelsse, hor aff iegh formerker, att hand er i Køffuenhaffn nu och haffuer breff ttil mig fraa eder, huilkett hand ike kune nu sene mig, men ville sielff anttvorde mig dett, om jegh kom ttil Køuenhaffn ingen enn 3 ugher; ellers will hand faa dochter Nils Krag breuene, nar hand drager henn; och sener hand mig tthu breffue, itt till min broder Knud, och itt ttill min systher Margrette, huilke jeg strax will sende denem; och skriuer dochter Nils mig nogitt till om eders lieglighet, hor aff jeg formerker, att i lider nu rett well, Gud were loffuitt, och haffuer faid itt hus aff kiesserenn, och mer end 4000 gyllen ttill arlligh penge aff kiesseren; och brød jeg min systher Margrettis breff op och wille och der aff forfare, hor eder lide, di jeg har lenge lengtist efftther bud fra eder, sin i er komen ttill Pragh; saa er dett mig i sanhedtt saa rett hierttelig kiertt, att jegh forfarer, att dett gaer eder saa well, Gud were loffuitt, att i har funditt en plads, som i kan haa rolighedtt, och folk kann skøn paa eder, och holle aff eders handell, och giøre eder gott.

Jegh hade ttillforn hørtt nogitt aff dette, di dett bleff dochter Nils skreuit till aff Ttyskland, doch huem jegh sagde dett for, ville ike ttro dett, saa jeg wiste dett doch ike wist, førend jeg kom till Gertt Ranszuo; hand sagde mig, ad dett war vist, och ad hand hade selff nyligen skreuitt eder till, och kan i ike ttro, hor kiertt dett er bade ham och Ttalle, ad det gar eder well, y sandhed, jeg kan merkett, att det er dem bade rett kiertt; i hor det er gan til met det snak, der var sagd for eder, daa har det wertt en misforstand, dy har hatt derom, di Ttale er jer och rett god, des wid jegh wist; ieg har formerktt bade aff dem och aff Gerttis søster, at fru Kiersten, hans moder, er eder saa god och forttryder sa hartt, huad di har giortt jer i Danmark, och adtt hun ttaker eder for hinis kintt Lisebett, y har hiolpidtt, der ttal hun saa megitt om; saa har jegh nu derfor skreffuitt hinde ttill och ttakett hine saa for altt det gode, hun har beuist eder, och er ier wen sa god, och derhos lader hine wide, huad ttinder jegh har nu sportt om eder, och ad eders søn er i Køffuenhaffn efftter di instrumenter, som enu war ttilbaghe. Jegh hauer i disse daghe laditt Gertt, sett dett breff, som i hade skreuitt kongen til och huad suar i fik derpaa, jegh har selff lest dett for ham och ttyd ham meningen derpaa, sa Ttalle hørdett, di forstod dem well derpaa; saa lod jeg ham och se det lile breff, som kiesserens canzeller hade skreuitt eder till.

Jegh var y sist i ttall medtt Mercurius i Skane paa Lanskrune; daa spore hand migh om eder, daa sagde ieg, att jegh hobtist, adtt dett gik eder well, och lod ham saa lesse den same lile copii, som den romiske canzeler hade skreuitt; daa sagde hand, det war gott, och war ham kertt, ad det gik jer well; och litt der efftter sagde han mig nogen tiner om kiesseren, som mig ike behagitt; daa suarid jeg, det kune bli gott eligvell, i hade andre go vener der i haffuidtt; sa sad hand atter litt, saa kom hand med end it snak och sagde, tto aff di, som war eders beste vener der aff kiesserens rad, di war affsaatt; daa mente ieg dett kun endaa bli gott; dett ser jeg gierne, sagde handtt. Dett war saa smok snak, vi hade, och jegh forstod det saa well. Min bror Jørgen war och der, han spore mig sa flitig om eder och ønskett saa, att altting kune gaa well for eder der wde.

Nu ttenker jeg well, ad di nu i Køffuenhaffn ttill dene heredagh for besken att wide om eders hanell. M. a. k. broder, nu att skriffue nogitt om Titan, saa har hand hafft en febris i 3 uger, doch hobis jeg met Guds hielp, att dett er nu gott igen med ham. Dett synis som denn Hanns Ernst will ike gaa rett om medtt ham; Erich har lertt ham consten, och der war giortt hanell melem dem, adtt huem di skulle lere consten, der skull di bade were om, och bade were lige guode y huad penge di for derfore; nu hade di forskreuen nogenn folk, som di skall haa penge aff, nu wille Hans Ernst enne haa di penghe; och har hand och skreuit kiesseren till och ttilbøditt ham consten, och wille ike lade Erich wide der aff; doch Erich hobis hand will kome ham for; ieg ttenker, att Erich will sielff ttill kiesserenn; och wid jeg wist, att Erich har consten wist, och hade handtt ttilbøditt wor konge denn, men hand ville ingen suar skriffue der paa, men war kun spodsch der offuer, och hade Hans Ernst och skreuitt kongenn ttill siden och begertt, at han ville lade Erich bekome suar, hor aff jegh sener eder enn copii, di jeg lod selff anttvore kongen dett breff med sekreteren, men handtt wille inted suare der till, for di har nu giortt ham her altt huad di kune; nu er di ttillfris medtt ham, och lader som di wid intid aff huad di ttilforn har giortt, di Erich hade erindrid kongen nogit derom i sin suplicatz. Nu Gud almectigsthe hielpe well och giøre dett engang bedre for os. Jeg hade skreuitt Erich ttill, ad han ville møde mig istes vid Harsløff, om han war her nogenstes i egnen, sa kune hand da ike møde mig der; nu har ieg skreuitt ham ttill, om han vile kome hid ttil mig eler til Hamburig eller ttil Lüneburg eller hor han will, daa will jeg møde ham och ttalle med ham, før jegh drar her aff egnen, och har jeg latt itt bud løffuid ud ttill ham her fraa och wentter dett igen om en 3 eler 4 dage; dersom hand will møde migh her ittstes, da vill jeg bie ham, ellers vill jeg met Guds hielp følge Ttage till Brunsuigh, saa er det kun en dags reysse ttill dett Kelbna. Dersom hand holler hus, sa vill jeg drage did till ham, di jegh ma enelig ttalle med ham, men jeg er saa ner ham, med Guds hielp, och wide hor det er met hans hanell. Gud giffue wos løcke och radtt, ad wij motte och faa nogen godtt lieglighedtt engangh.

M. a. k. broder, nu om min Ttage, saa acter jeg att lade ham drage till Bassell och bli der itt aar; dochter Nils har skreuitt met ham till den docter Greneus der, Sten Bille har och skreuitt enn dochter till, er den andens broder, att di vell forhielpe Ttage till dett beste. Erich har skreuitt mig ttill, att ham syntist, att det wor best, att Ttage bleff till Hiedellbierg først itt aar och lerde sprogitt, att hand ike skulle først lere dett sleme suisisch sprog ttill Bassell, men ieg will enu se dett ann, før ieg slutter dett, hor han skall were, di han kan vell och lere god ttysk til Bassel; blir det ike forsnartt forkoltt att feris, da skall hand drage ttill Bassell, di den haffhanell, der er ttill Hiedelbierg, hauer ike migh; nar han nu komer did, som hand først skal vere, sa skall hand strax skriffue eder ttill; saa ttror jeg eder saa vell, att i skriuer for ham ttil di lerde paa det sted, att di vill fordre ham till dett besthe. Gud almectigste beuare ham well, i hor hand komer, och senne ham well hiem till mig ighenn.

M. a. k. broder, jeg ttenker min moder har vell nu faid eders skriuelsse, der blir hun gladtt vid, di hun war nu saa omhygelig for jer, sin i war dragen till kiesseren. Jeg sener jer itt breff, Henrich Romell skreff mig ttill om di bøgher. Jeg drager till Fusing til min søster fra Harsløff, nar jeg komer did igen, di hun har begert det aff migh; hinis søner er nu dragen y gardenn. Jeg skriuer nu dette breff her och sener dett ttill Køffuenhaffn, och hobis, att dett komer der førend eders Ttyche drager derfraa; hade jeg nu bekomid huis i har skreuitt mig till, dett hade jegh gierne seett, men jeg skal skriff efftter dett. M. a. k. broder, saa wid jegh nu intet syneligtt mer att skriffue eder ttill, men i skall altid finde mig for eders hulle systher, der altid gierne will giøre huis jeg wid eder kiertt er, och hobis wi finis well engang, Gud giue wy maa findis glade och helbredtt. Och will nu och altid haffue eder Gud allmectigste beffallidtt, bade till siell och liff, med all løcke och welfartt. Lader Giertt och Ttalle och min Ttaghe helsse eder medtt manghe m. guode netther; giører well och siger Kirsten och eders børn och alle eders guode wener (huilke ocssa ere mine) manghe m. guode netter paa minne wegnne.

Datum Briedenburgh denn 24. october 1599.
Sophia Brahe Ottis datter.

To the honest and well-born man Tyge Brahe of Knudstrup, my dearest brother, quite lovingly and friendlily written.

My dearest brother, may God Almighty be with you now and always, and keep you graciously and well from all that can be harmful to you, in body or soul; and may God grant me always to hear that it may go happily and well with you in every way — that I faithfully wish from God. My dear kind brother, I give you quite friendlily to know that I am now on the journey with my Tage; and I first accompanied him to Haderslev; and when I came there, Gert and Talle [Rantzau] had moved off to one of their estates, called Lindewedel [Lindved], nine miles from Haderslev, where he is building; so they wrote me that I should come with Tage there to them, and then accompany them to Breitenburg, which is within 7 miles of Hamburg — [where] Gert would inquire about a company [a travelling party] that Tage could join, to go to Brunswick and so out to Basel, where I want him. So I then went to them, to Lindewedel, and was there two or three days, and accompanied them hither to Breitenburg, and have been here 6 days; and they do me all the good they can, so that I have come to right good friends here, who do me all good, God be praised. And I shall stay here yet until Monday, for then Gert goes to Kiel for a Diet, and sister goes back to Haderslev — for they expect the King there soon, toward St. Martin's day [11 Nov], for there shall then be a Diet at Flensburg, where the King will be at that time. So I intend, with God's help, to go hence with Tage to Wandsbek by Hamburg, and be there 3 or 4 days, and inquire about good company that Tage can join to Brunswick. God grant good luck.

Titan's secretary Mikell had been at Haderslev, and thought to have found me there about 3 weeks ago; and since I was not there then, he went to Horsens, and mother Lady Anne got the letters to me, and she sent them over to Copenhagen; and when they came there, I had departed; so I sent a messenger from Kolding over to Copenhagen for the same letters, and that messenger now came hither to me at Breitenburg the day before yesterday; and I got, with that messenger, your son Tyge's letter, from which I perceive that he is in Copenhagen now, and has a letter for me from you — which he could not now send me, but would deliver it to me himself, if I came to Copenhagen within 3 weeks; otherwise he will give Doctor Niels Krag the letters when he travels [thither]. And he sends me two letters, one to my brother Knud, and one to my sister Margrette, which I will send them at once. And Doctor Niels writes me something about your situation, from which I perceive that you now fare right well, God be praised, and have got a house from the Emperor, and more than 4000 gulden in yearly money from the Emperor; and I broke open my sister Margrette's letter, and would also learn from it how you fare, for I have long longed for word from you since you came to Prague. So it is truly so heartily dear to me, that I learn it goes so well with you (God be praised), that you have found a place where you can have peace, and people can appreciate you and value your work [handel], and do you good.

I had heard something of this before, for it was written to Doctor Niels from Germany — though whomever I told it to would not believe it, so I did not know it for certain, until I came to Gert Rantzau; he told me that it was certain, and that he had himself lately written to you, and you cannot believe how dear it is, both to him and to Talle, that it goes well with you. Truly, I can tell it is right dear to them both. However it went with that talk that was said about you, then it has been a misunderstanding they have had about it, for Talle is right good to you too, that I know for certain; I have perceived, both from them and from Gert's sister, that Lady Kirsten, his mother, is so good to you, and regrets so hard what they did to you in Denmark, and that she thanks you for her child Lisbeth, whom you have helped — she talks so much about it. So I have now therefore written her, and thanked her for all the good she has shown you, and [she] is so good a friend to you; and besides, [I] let her know what tidings I have now learned about you, and that your son is in Copenhagen for the instruments that were still left behind. I have in these days let Gert see the letter you had written the King, and what answer you got on it — I read it myself for him, and explained the meaning of it to him, so that Talle heard it; for they understood it well. So I let him also see the little letter the Emperor's Chancellor had written you.

I was lately in conversation with "Mercurius" in Skåne, at Landskrona; then he asked me about you, then I said that I hoped it went well with you, and let him read the same little copy that the Roman Chancellor [Coraduz] had written; then he said it was good, and dear to him, that it went well with you. And a little after, he told me some things about the Emperor that did not please me; then I answered, it could be good nonetheless, [for] you had other good friends you had there. So he sat again a little; then he came with another remark, and said: two of those who were your best friends there, of the Emperor's council, they were dismissed [from office]; then I thought it could still be good. "That I see gladly," he said. It was such pretty talk we had, and I understood it so well. My brother Jørgen was also there; he asked me so diligently about you, and wished so much that everything could go well for you out there.

Now I do think that they are now in Copenhagen, for this Diet, for word [seeking news] about your situation. My dear kind brother, now to write something about Titan: he has had a fever for 3 weeks, though I hope, with God's help, that it is now well again with him. It seems as if that Hans Ernst will not deal rightly with him [Erik]: Erik has taught him the [alchemical] art [consten], and there was a deal made between them, that whomever they should teach the art, they should both be in on it, and both share equally in whatever money they got for it. Now they had engaged some people [investors], from whom they should get money; now Hans Ernst alone wanted to have those moneys; and he has also written the Emperor, and offered him the art, and would not let Erik know of it — though Erik hopes he will get ahead of him; I think Erik will himself [go] to the Emperor. And I know for certain that Erik has the art for certain, and he had offered our King it, but he [the King] would write no answer on it, but was only mocking about it; and Hans Ernst had also written the King since, and asked that he would let Erik get an answer — of which I send you a copy; for I myself had the King delivered the letter by the secretary, but he would answer nothing to it, for they have now done to him [Erik] here all that they could. Now they are content with him, and act as if they know nothing of what they did before — for Erik had reminded the King somewhat of it in his supplication. Now may God Almighty help well, and make it once better for us. I had written Erik that he should meet me lately near Haderslev, if he were anywhere in the region — so he could not meet me there [then]; now I have written him whether he will come hither to me, or to Hamburg, or to Lüneburg, or wherever he will, [and] then I will meet him and talk with him, before I leave the region; and I have sent a messenger out to him from here, and expect it back in 3 or 4 days. If he will meet me here lately, then I will wait for him; otherwise I will, with God's help, accompany Tage to Brunswick — so it is but a day's journey to that Kelbra [?]. If he keeps house [is settled somewhere], then I will go thither to him, for I must absolutely talk with him; but I am so near him, with God's help, [I shall] also [learn] how it is with his affairs. God give us luck and counsel, that we might also get some good opportunity once.

My dear kind brother, now about my Tage: I intend to let him go to Basel and stay there a year; Doctor Niels [Krag] has written, with him, to that Doctor Grynaeus there; Sten Bille has also written to a doctor [there], [who] is the other's brother, that they will help Tage to the best. Erik has written me that it seemed to him best that Tage stay at Heidelberg first a year and learn the language, that he should not first learn that bad Swiss tongue at Basel — but I will yet consider it, before I decide where he shall be, for he can well also learn good German at Basel; if it does not get too soon too cold to travel, then he shall go to Basel, for the … [haffhanell — unclear] that is at Heidelberg does not [please] me. When he now comes where he shall first be, then he shall write you at once; so I trust you so well that you will write for him to the learned at that place, that they will advance him to the best. God Almighty keep him well, wherever he comes, and send him well home to me again.

My dear kind brother, I think my mother [Beate Bille] has now got your letter; she will be glad of it, for she was so anxious for you since you went to the Emperor. I send you a letter; Henrik Ramel wrote me about the books. I go to Fusingø to my sister from Haderslev, when I come there again, for she has asked it of me; her sons have now gone into the estate. I write this letter here now, and send it to Copenhagen, and hope that it comes there before your Tyge leaves thence. Had I now got what you have written me, I would gladly have seen it — but I shall write for it. My dear kind brother, so I now know nothing notable more to write you; but you shall always find me your loyal sister, who always gladly will do what I know is dear to you; and [I] hope we meet well once — God grant we may meet glad and in health. And [I] will now and always have you commended to God Almighty, both to soul and body, with all luck and welfare. Gert and Talle and my Tage greet you with many good-nights; do well, and say to Kirsten and your children and all your good friends (who are also mine) many good-nights on my behalf.

Given [at] Breitenburg, the 24th of October 1599.
Sophia Brahe, Otte's daughter.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Sophie Brahe (Sophia Brahe Ottesdatter, 1559–1643) — Tycho's younger sister, the astronomer- and chemist-noblewoman, betrothed to the alchemist Erik Lange — to Tycho. An original autograph (Basel).
  • Significance — a centerpiece of the Sophie Brahe / Erik Lange story, and a warm family chronicle.
  • Escorting her son to Basel. Sophie is travelling with her son Tage Thott (by her first husband Otte Thott), bound for study at Basel via Brunswick, hosted by Gert Rantzau, his wife "Talle," and his mother Lady Kirsten Rantzau at Breitenburg. She asks Tycho to write recommendations; Niels Krag and Sten Bille have written to the Grynaeus brothers at Basel (cf. Tycho's own Grynaeus letter and the nephew Otto Brahe at Basel, brahe-t_15980607001). Erik Lange's advice: send Tage to Heidelberg first (to avoid "that bad Swiss tongue").
  • Joy at Tycho's imperial fortune. Sophie has heard (via Niels Krag, her sister Margrette's letter, and Tycho's son Tyge in Copenhagen) that Tycho "has got a house from the Emperor, and more than 4000 gulden a year" — "so heartily dear to me … that you have found a place where people appreciate you." She has shown the sympathetic Rantzaus Tycho's letter to King Christian IV, the unkind royal reply, and Coraduz's letter.
  • The Erik Lange alchemy saga (the emotional core). A vivid, intimate report on Erik Lange — Sophie's beloved, the ruined alchemist: his partner Hans Ernst is cheating him over "the art" [consten = alchemy] and the investors' money, secretly offering it to the Emperor; Erik "has the art for certain," offered it to the Danish King (who only mocked it), and may go to the Emperor himself. Sophie is desperate to meet Erik (Hamburg, Lüneburg, or wherever) before leaving — "I must absolutely talk with him." (Cf. Tycho's own query after Erik Lange, brahe-t_16000000002.)
  • Court intelligence. A coded conversation with "Mercurius" at Landskrona (a Danish court figure, codename) reveals two of Tycho's friends on the imperial council "were dismissed"; her brother Jørgen Brahe present. The family network — mother Beate Bille, siblings Knud, Margrette, the Fusingø sister, Henrik Ramel (about books) — threads throughout.
  • Cross-references. A keystone of the Sophie Brahe / Erik Lange thread (pair with brahe-t_16000000002, where Tycho asks Petkum after Erik Lange and reports Sophie at Wandsbek) and the Brahe-family network; the Basel/Grynaeus student connection (brahe-t_15980607001).
  • Persons & places named. Sophie Brahe; Tycho; Tage Thott (her son); Gert Rantzau & wife "Talle" & mother Lady Kirsten Rantzau; Mikell (courier); Lady Anne (a kinswoman — unidentified, flagged); Tyge (Tycho the Younger); Niels Krag; Knud Brahe, Margrette Brahe (siblings); "Mercurius" (Landskrona court figure — codename, flagged); Jørgen Brahe; Erik Lange; Hans Ernst; Rudolf II, Christian IV; the Grynaeus brothers (Basel); Sten Bille; Beate Bille (mother); Henrik Ramel; "Titan" (a person with a fever — unidentified, flagged). Places: Breitenburg, Haderslev, Lindewedel, Kiel, Flensburg, Wandsbek/Hamburg, Brunswick, Basel, Heidelberg, Copenhagen, Kolding, Horsens, Landskrona, Lüneburg, Fusingø.
  • Date. Source dateline denn 24. october 1599 (identifier 15991024001 agrees) → 24 October 1599 [O.S.]; the catalogue label's "24 September" is an error (contradicted by the dateline and identifier), flagged for review of the catalogue date.
  • Reading flags. "Titan" — a person Sophie reports ill with fever, distinct from Tage and Erik (possibly a nickname for a family member or an alchemy associate; unidentified). "Mercurius" — a coded designation for a Danish-court figure. "Lady Anne" — an unnamed kinswoman. "haffhanell" (the Heidelberg objection) — uncertain word. None affect the overall sense.
  • Correction. Two mistranslations corrected against Dreyer (TBOO XIV p.158): 'Titan writes that Mikell had been...' is corrected to 'Titan's secretary Mikell had been...' ('Titans skriuer Mikell' is a noun phrase naming Mikell as Titan's secretary, not a report-by-letter from Titan); and 'her kinswoman Lisbeth' is corrected to 'her child Lisbeth' ('kintt' = archaic Danish for 'child').

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.156-160 read as page images and collated the transcribed original word-for-word against pp.157-159, which carry the full letter (no.166, Sophie Brahe to Tycho, 24 Oct 1599): opening and closing lines, every proper name (Gert/Talle Rantzau, Lindved, Mikell, Tyge, Niels Krag, Knud, Margrette, Kirsten, Lisebett, Jørgen, Beate Bille, Henrik Ramel, Erik, Hans Ernst, Sten Bille, Grynaeus), every number (9 miles to Lindved, 7 miles Breitenburg-Hamburg, 2-3 days, 6 days, three separate '3 weeks', 4000 gulden, 'two of the Emperor's councillors'), every date (24 Oct 1599, St Martin's day, dispatch date), and every place-name (Haderslev, Lindved, Breitenburg, Kiel, Flensburg, Wandsbek, Horsens, Copenhagen, Kolding, Landskrona i Skåne, Heidelberg, Basel, Fusingø). Our Danish matches Dreyer's print throughout — no text error. Found one high-severity mistranslation that misidentifies Mikell's relationship to the code-named 'Titan' (almost certainly Erik Lange) and invents an act of writing absent from the source, plus one lower-confidence mistranslation of the archaic word for 'child' as 'kinswoman'. The first defect changes what a scholar reconstructing Sophie Brahe's/Erik Lange's correspondence network would take away, so the letter cannot stand at its the translator's own grade Secure.

2 defects found:
- mistranslation (high). 'Titans skriuer' is a noun phrase ('Titan's secretary/scribe'), with 'Mikell' in apposition naming him — Mikell is the subject of 'hade werid' (had been), i.e. Titan's (Erik Lange's) secretary who went to Haderslev looking for Sophie. The English instead makes 'Titan' the subject of an invented verb 'writes', turning a plain narrative clause into a false report-by-letter and dropping the master-secretary relationship between Titan/Erik Lange and Mikell. At: “Titan writes that Mikell had been at Haderslev, and thought to have found me there about 3 weeks ago”.
- mistranslation (low). 'Kind' is the archaic Danish word for 'child' (cognate with English 'kin' / German 'Kind'), not 'kinswoman'; Lady Kirsten more likely thanks Tycho for having helped her child Lisbeth than an unspecified female relative. Lower confidence than the Titan/Mikell finding since 'kinswoman' is not indefensible, but the more probable reading is 'child'. At: “she thanks you for her kinswoman Lisbeth, whom you have helped”.

Repaired. Both defects were verified directly against Dreyer's page images, TBOO XIV pp.156-159 (the full letter). Both source phrases match the transcribed original exactly as printed by Dreyer on p.158, confirming no text error and that both defects are pure translation issues. The Mikell/secretary reading is grammatically unambiguous. The 'kintt'/'child' reading is the more probable sense per the audit's own note (archaic Danish, cognate with 'kin'/'Kind') and is adopted, though 'kinswoman' was not indefensible — flagged low severity in the source audit and treated accordingly.
