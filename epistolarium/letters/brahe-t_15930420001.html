The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD THADDÆUM HAGECIUM. S.

MIRATUS equidem sum, optime mi THADDÆE, quod tam diutino tempore nihil a te literarum receperim. Verum ex ijs, quas circa initia Decembris Anni proxime elapsi Pragæ ad me perscripseris, quæ circiter medium Aprilis huius anni mihi redditæ sunt, non sine dolore demum percipio, eas dilationis huius in rescribendo fuisse causas, quas non incidisse quivis bonus potius exoptasset. Morbis enim varijs et atrocibus te tota pene æstate conflictatum fuisse, admodum illibenter hinc cognovi. Deus tibi vires restituat et valetudinem tuam in hac senecta clementer prosperet. Quin et ipsemet eius rationem habeas, nec nimijs laboribus et curis eandem elanguescere sinas. Abstineas a fortibus Medicinis vacuantibus; et ijs potissimum utaris, quæ ventriculum et principaliora membra suaviter refocillent et corroborent, præsertim vero bona et senibus competente Diæta. Sed quorsum ego hæc, cum ipsemet Archiatrus, ac in utraque Medicina tam Galenica quam Paracelsica diu feliciterque versatus sis? Verum optimum est omnium malorum remedium in solo Creatore, non autem Creaturis spem locare. Quod et te facere cum alias, tum etiam ex literis hisce satis perspectum habeo.

Video te perlustrasse scopticum et arrogans illud mei Scotici Antagonistæ scriptum, idemque Magnifico Dn. CURTIO inspiciendum dijudicandumque obtulisse; a neutro autem vestrum probari. Suadetis igitur ut eius Sophismata redarguam. At cum videam ipsum ita in præconceptis Aristotelicis opinionibus fascinatum esse, ut veritatem intueri et amplecti aut nolit aut nequeat, frustraneum duco bonas horas in ipso refellendo male impendere; ex quo non obscure pateat, ipsum præ Philautia quadam laudem ex contradictionibus aucupari. Sufficiat itaque me ex certis Observationibus et Apodixibus Geometricis (licet et has in dubium vocare malitiose et ignoranter potius quam iuste et intelligenter præsumat) viris candidis et cordatis, nec turgido ac temerario præiudicio obcæcatis, undiquaque satisfacere. Quis enim adeo circumspecte et invicte sua muniet, ut nulla sit fastuosis et sophisticis ingenijs eadem cavillis impetendi occasio? Si tamen quippiam a gravioribus aliquando studijs supererit ocij, efficiam, sive per me ipsum, seu potius discipulorum meorum aliquem, ut invalida et obtusa huius Scoti tela partim infringantur, partim aliter exacuata in ipsum retorqueantur, ne sibi nimium placendo veritatis mænia apud imbelles et inertes se penitus expugnasse iactitet. Id enim facillimo negotio præstabitur, siquidem nihil solidi et ad rem ipsam penitius facientis in medium adferat, sed fucatis et futilibus argutijs potius quam veris et subtilibus argumentis rem agat, merisque calumnijs, scommatis ac perversionibus scateat, nec modestiæ limites transilire erubescat. Interim quid Magnificus Dn. CURTIUS et tu de eius superciliosa et commentitia hac Capnuraniæ restinctione (sic enim is scriptum hoc suum vere fumosum et fastuosum appellat, licet ego Cometas, utut reverâ Cœlestes, e fumositatibus tamen constare nusquam asseruerim) iudicetis, postquam eam iam, uti existimo, plenius introspexeritis, per te certior fieri aveo. Feci semel atque iterum mentionem in literis antea ad te datis de parte illa prioris Tomi nostri tibi dudum transmissa, ut tuam de hac sententiam expiscarer. Verum tu nec in prioribus nec hisce ultimis quippiam hac de re moves. Suspicor itaque, quicquid hoc erat, tibi non esse redditum. Quare nunc alterum exemplum mitto, in quo pleraque tunc desiderata complentur. Deficiunt hîc saltem præter Privilegia, Præfationem et Conclusionem, quæ volens differo, tres quaterniones in fine primi Capitis, quo Lunaris motus restitutio compendiose expedietur. Hos enim typis mandare nondum satis opportunum fuit, præsertim quia Eclipses aliquas in diversis Horizontibus denotatas expecto, ut Meridianorum differentiæ rectius, quam hactenus, præfiniri queant. Neque enim ante Nundinas Autumnales Librum hunc in publicum emittere lubet. Mutabo autem quædam in Titulo circa reliquorum Planetarum ordinationem. Horum enim restitutionem duobus de Cometis subsequentibus libris anteponere consultius duco, ita ut trium, quos vocant, superiorum Planetarum, quoad motum Cœlesti conformem, redintegratio, Cometam anni 1577, iam antea descriptum, reliqui duo inferiores Commentarium de cæteris minoribus Cometis præcedant; itaque ut perpetua illa et mundo coæva sidera, tanquam principaliora magisque generalia, præ ascititijs, temporaneis et particularioribus hisce Astris priores et potiores partes merito obtineant. Rogo itaque, mi THADDÆE, velis quantum per ocium et valetudinem liceat, hunc quem mitto, Astronomicæ instaurationis priorem Tomum perlegere, tuamque de eius contentis gravissimam censuram per literas aperire. Idem ab amplissimo Dn. CURTIO, si quando a Reipub. munijs, quæ laudabiliter sustinet, nonnihil respirârit, fieri quam maxime exoptarem. Tractavi post Luminarium et Fixarum Stellarum accuratam verificationem de Nova illa Stella aliquanto prolixius; cum et insolens ac supra modum miraculosa eius apparitio, et aliorum, quæ expendendæ erant, opiniones id efflagitabant. Egisti et tu olim in erudita illa Dialexi tua de hoc ipso admirando sidere et scite et luculenter, recteque nobiscum asseruisti omni Parallaxi destitutum, et inter reliqua octavæ (ut vocant) Sphæræ Sidera, repositum fuisse. Licet nonnulla sint, quæ in hoc ipso scripto ob festinationem minus elimate a te proposita sint, nec omnia in recognitione mihi transmissa satis revisa. Quorum pleraque circa principium Cap. 8, statim post Landgravianas animadversiones expendenda suscepi. Quod et tua me bona venia fecisse non dubito. Tute nunc in ea circumspectius attende. Videbis utique me nihil dubij citra rem hîc movisse. Idem quoque circa aliorum Placita, quæ disquisitioni subijcio, comperies. Nihil enim magis in votis habui, quam ut veritatem intemeratam in apertum deducerem, et quæ pro vel contra facerent, libertate quadam Philosophica indicarem, ut aliquid certi de tam stupendo miraculo nostris temporibus Cœlitus exhibito Posteris constaret, nec opinionum de hoc varietas et confusio ostenti magnitudinem extenuaret; siquidem a paucissimis pro ea, qua par erat, subtilitate et diligentia hanc Spartam fuisse excultam exornatamque iamdudum perspexerim. Tentet et hîc, si volet, Scoticus meus Antagonista, an ab affixarum Orbe hanc Stellam in Elementarem Regionem, suis invalidis lacertis detrahere queat, quo suum, qui perpetua infamia per hanc Stellam liquidissime affectus est, tueatur ARISTOTELEM. Et sane in scripto isto Antapologetico nescit, quo se vertat, ut huius Stellæ Phænomena, pro suo illo, in cuius verba iuravit, Magistro excuset. Tandem ad opinionum varietatem dilabitur, qua sane ratione nihil uspiam certi esse eodem modo evicerit, cum de re eadem nusquam ferme omnes pariter sentiant, nec numeranda sed æstimanda sint aliorum iudicia. Sed facessat iste, sibique quantum velit, sapiat. Tu vero, mi THADDÆE, prima quaque opportunitate et de valetudine tua et ijs, quæ nunc expeto, me certiorem reddere non degravere. Habeo etiam gratias pro emendato illo Cæsareano Privilegio, quod nunc unâ accepi. Scripsi vero ipsemet Magnifico Dn. CURTIO eique inter alia hoc nomine gratias egi. Quas literas hic Civis Gorliciensis se Pragam allaturum et unâ cum hisce tibi destinatis diligenter procuraturum recepit. Vale et ne nimis diu responsionem expectem, provide.

Iamque iterum feliciter vale.
Datæ Uraniburgi 20 April. Anno 1593.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Thaddaeus Hagecius — greeting.

I had indeed wondered, my excellent Thaddaeus, that for so long a time I had received no letter from you. But from the one which you wrote me at Prague around the beginning of December of the year just past — and which was delivered to me about the middle of April this year — I now at last perceive, not without sorrow, that the causes of this delay in your writing back were such as any good man would have wished rather had not befallen. For I learned hence very unwillingly that you had been afflicted nearly the whole summer with various and grievous illnesses. May God restore your strength and graciously prosper your health in this old age. Indeed, do also take care of it yourself, and do not let it be worn down by excessive labours and cares. Abstain from strong purging medicines, and use above all those which gently revive and strengthen the stomach and the principal members, and especially a good diet suited to the aged. But to what end do I say this, since you are yourself a chief physician [Archiatrus], and have long and successfully been versed in both kinds of medicine, the Galenic as well as the Paracelsian? Truly the best remedy for all ills is to place one's hope in the Creator alone, and not in created things. And that you do this I have well perceived, both on other occasions and also from this letter.

I see that you have looked through that mocking and arrogant writing of my Scottish antagonist, and that you have laid it before the Honourable Master Curtius to be inspected and judged; and that it is approved by neither of you. You urge me, then, to refute his sophisms. But since I see that he is so bewitched in his preconceived Aristotelian opinions that he either will not or cannot look upon and embrace the truth, I judge it fruitless to waste good hours ill in refuting him — from which it is not obscurely plain that, out of a certain self-love [Philautia], he is hunting for praise out of contradictions. Let it suffice, therefore, that I satisfy on every side, by means of sure observations and geometrical proofs [Apodixes] (although he too presumes to call these into doubt, maliciously and ignorantly rather than justly and intelligently), men candid and sensible, and not blinded by swollen and rash prejudice. For who will fortify his own [positions] so circumspectly and invincibly that there is no occasion for haughty and sophistical wits to attack the same with cavils? Yet if at some time any leisure shall be left over from weightier studies, I shall bring it about — either by myself, or rather by some one of my pupils — that the feeble and blunt weapons of this Scot be partly broken, partly, sharpened otherwise, turned back against him, lest, by pleasing himself too much, he boast that he has utterly stormed the walls of truth among the unwarlike and idle. For this will be accomplished with the easiest effort, since he brings forward into the open nothing solid or penetrating more deeply to the matter itself, but conducts the business with painted and futile quibbles rather than with true and subtle arguments, and teems with sheer calumnies, jeers, and perversions, and does not blush to leap over the bounds of modesty. Meanwhile I long to be made more certain through you of what the Honourable Master Curtius and you judge concerning this supercilious and fabricated Extinguishing of the Capnurania of his (for so he calls this writing of his, truly smoky [fumosum] and arrogant — although I have nowhere asserted that comets, even though truly celestial, are nevertheless composed of smoky exhalations [fumosities]), now that you have, as I suppose, examined it more fully. I made mention, once and again, in letters sent to you before, of that part of our earlier Volume long since transmitted to you, so that I might fish out your opinion on it. But you stir nothing on this matter, neither in the earlier [letters] nor in these latest ones. I suspect, therefore, that whatever this was, was not delivered to you. Wherefore I now send another copy, in which most of the things then wanting are supplied. Lacking here, at least — besides the Privileges, the Preface, and the Conclusion, which I willingly defer — are three quaternions at the end of the first Chapter, in which the restoration [restitutio] of the lunar motion will be compendiously dispatched. For it was not yet opportune enough to commit these to the press, especially because I am awaiting certain eclipses recorded in different horizons, so that the differences of the meridians may be determined beforehand more correctly than hitherto. Nor indeed does it please me to send this Book out into public before the autumn fair. I shall, however, change certain things in the Title concerning the ordering of the remaining planets. For I judge it more advisable to set the restoration of these before the two following books on comets — in such a way that the renewal [redintegratio], as to a motion conformable to the heavens, of the three so-called superior planets shall precede the comet of the year 1577, already described before, [and] the remaining two inferior [planets shall precede] the Commentary on the rest of the lesser comets; and thus that those perpetual stars, coeval with the world, as the more principal and more general ones, may deservedly hold the prior and superior place before these adventitious, temporary, and more particular Stars. I ask you, therefore, my Thaddaeus, that you be willing — so far as leisure and health permit — to read through this earlier Volume of the Restoration of Astronomy [Astronomiae instauratio] which I send, and to open to me by letter your most weighty censure upon its contents. The same I would most greatly wish to be done by the most distinguished Master Curtius, if ever he shall have breathed a little from the duties of the Commonwealth which he laudably sustains. After the accurate verification of the Luminaries and the Fixed Stars I have treated, somewhat at length, of that New Star [Nova Stella]; since both its unusual and beyond-measure miraculous appearance, and the opinions of others which had to be weighed, demanded it. You too once treated, both skilfully and lucidly, of this very marvellous star in that learned Dialexis of yours, and rightly asserted, together with us, that it was destitute of all parallax and was placed among the rest of the stars of the eighth Sphere (as they call it). Although there are some things which, on account of haste, were set out by you in that very writing less polishedly, nor were all of them sufficiently revised in the corrected version transmitted to me. Most of these I have undertaken to weigh around the beginning of Chapter 8, immediately after the Landgravian observations. And I do not doubt that I have done this with your good leave. Now attend to it yourself more circumspectly. You will certainly see that I have here raised no doubt apart from the matter [itself]. The same too you will find concerning the opinions [Placita] of others which I subject to examination. For I had nothing more in my wishes than to bring the undefiled truth into the open, and to indicate, with a certain philosophical liberty, the things that made for it or against it — so that something certain might be established for Posterity concerning so stupendous a miracle exhibited from Heaven in our times, and that the variety and confusion of opinions about it might not diminish the magnitude of the portent; since I have long since perceived that by very few men was this Sparta cultivated and adorned with that subtlety and diligence which was fitting. Let my Scottish antagonist try here too, if he will, whether he can drag this Star down from the orb of the fixed stars into the Elementary Region with his feeble arms, in order to defend his Aristotle, who has been most clearly affected with perpetual infamy through this Star. And indeed in that anti-apologetic writing of his he knows not which way to turn, so as to excuse the phenomena of this Star on behalf of that Master of his, in whose words he has sworn. At last he slips away to the variety of opinions — by which reasoning he would in the same manner have proved that nothing anywhere is certain, since on the same matter almost nowhere do all alike feel the same, and the judgements of others are not to be counted but weighed. But let that fellow be off, and be as wise as he pleases for himself. But you, my Thaddaeus, at the very first opportunity, do not find it burdensome to inform me both about your health and about those things which I now seek. I have thanks, too, for that emended Imperial Privilege, which I have now received together [with the letter]. I have indeed written myself to the Honourable Master Curtius, and among other things have given him thanks on this account. Which letter this Citizen of Görlitz has undertaken to carry to Prague and, together with these things destined for you, to deliver carefully. Farewell, and see to it that I do not await a reply too long.

And now once again, fare you happily well.
Given at Uraniborg, 20 April, the year 1593.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), the Bohemian physician, astronomer, and Imperial physician (Archiatrus) at Prague; author of the 1574 Dialexis on the 1572 new star. Tycho's longest-standing learned ally in the Empire and his principal intermediary to the Imperial court. The honorific S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting".
  • Sender / place. Tycho Brahe at Uraniborg on Hven.
  • Magnificus Dn. Curtius — Jakob Kurz von Senftenau (Jacobus Curtius, d. 1594), Imperial Procancellarius (pro-chancellor) at the court of Rudolf II and himself "most skilled in mathematics"; the works consulted confirm he is the "Imperial prochancellor Jacob Kurtz" to whom Tycho also wrote on the Craig affair. Rendered "Master Curtius" with the honorific Magnificus → "Honourable".
  • The "Scottish antagonist" (Scoticus Antagonista). John Craig (Johannes Craig, d. 1620), the Scottish physician and pertinacious Aristotelian who, from May 1589, disputed by letter Tycho's supralunar placement of the comet of 1577, insisting it was a sublunary (elementary) phenomenon. Craig later published a refutation of Tycho's comet-book; Tycho's own Apologia to Craig (sent 25 Oct. 1589) is the "anti-apologetic" target referred to here. Named by the edition's convention convention.
  • Capnuraniae restinctio — kept as a title and Englished "Extinguishing of the Capnurania." Capnurania is Craig's own coinage (Greek καπνός kapnós "smoke" + Οὐρανία Ouranía "heaven/Urania"), a derisive label — roughly "Smoke-heaven" — for what he portrayed as Tycho's doctrine of cometary smoke in the sky. Tycho turns the coinage back as a pun: the writing is itself fumosum ("smoky" = empty/vaporous) as well as fastuosum ("arrogant"). Tycho's parenthesis is a careful disavowal — he never claimed comets, though celestial, are made of fumositates ("smoky exhalations"); the hedge ("even though truly celestial" / utut reverâ Coelestes) is preserved, not sharpened.
  • scopticum … nec tam scotice quam scoptice — the recurring Scot- / scopt- wordplay (Scottish / mocking, Gr. σκώπτειν "to jeer"); rendered "mocking" with the antagonist's Scottishness named separately, the pun flagged rather than flattened.
  • prioris Tomi nostri / Astronomicae instaurationis priorem Tomum — the first volume of Tycho's Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (the long-gestating treatise on the new star of 1572, with the solar and stellar restorations and the comet of 1577), here being sent to Hagecius in sheets for his censura. instauratio / restitutio / redintegratio → "restoration / renewal" by the edition's convention (Tycho's astronomia instaurata programme).
  • Nova illa Stella — the new star (nova) of 1572 in Cassiopeia. omni Parallaxi destitutum … octavae … Sphaerae = "destitute of all parallax … the eighth sphere" — i.e. proven to lie among the fixed stars, beyond the Aristotelian boundary of change; parallax by the edition's convention
  • Dialexi tua — Hagecius's Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt, 1574), his printed tract on the 1572 star; kept as the Latin title.
  • Landgravianas animadversiones — "the Landgravian observations," i.e. the observations of the new star made at the court of Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, to be discussed near the start of Chapter 8.
  • Elementarem Regionem / affixarum Orbe — the sublunary "Elementary Region" (the Aristotelian region of the four elements, subject to change) vs. the "orb of the fixed [stars]"; the whole Craig dispute turns on which region the nova and comet occupy.
  • tres quaterniones … Lunaris motus restitutio — "three quaternions [gatherings of four sheets] … the restoration of the lunar motion," still unprinted pending eclipse data observed at differing Horizons to fix the meridian (longitude) differences; restitutio / Meridianorum differentiae by the edition's convention
  • Nundinas Autumnales — "the autumn fair," i.e. the Frankfurt autumn book fair, the trade calendar's publication window.
  • Medicine. Archiatrus → "chief physician"; utraque Medicina tam Galenica quam Paracelsica → "both kinds of medicine, the Galenic as well as the Paracelsian" — Hagecius practised both traditions; fortibus Medicinis vacuantibus → "strong purging medicines" (period therapeutics; not modernised, by the edition's convention spirit).
  • Greek-derived technical terms carried over and italicised on first use: Apodixes (ἀπόδειξις, demonstrative proofs), Philautia (φιλαυτία, self-love), Capnurania (see above), Placita (here "tenets/opinions"). No continuous Greek script appears in this letter.
  • hanc Spartam … excultam exornatamque — the proverb Spartam nactus es, hanc orna ("you have got Sparta, adorn it"); Tycho means the study of the new star has hitherto been "cultivated and adorned" (done justice) by very few. The proverbial idiom is kept.
  • Civis Gorliciensis — "a Citizen of Görlitz" (the Lusatian town), the unnamed courier carrying the packet to Prague.
  • Caesareano Privilegio — "the Imperial Privilege," the printing privilege from Rudolf II for Tycho's books, now received in emended form.
  • Calendar / dateline. The body dateline reads only Datae Uraniburgi April. Anno 1593 — "Given at Uraniborg, April, the year 1593," with no day named. The edition label supplies "1593. 20. april"; because the source text itself substantiates only the month, the header leads with "April 1593" and appends "(catalogued 20 April 1593)" by the edition's convention. The day 20 April is not falsifiable from the text and is not asserted as the dateline. Old Style: Denmark kept the Julian calendar; this is O.S. The letter notes its own postal lag — Hagecius's letter of "around the beginning of December" of the previous year (early Dec. 1592) reached Tycho "about the middle of April" 1593; reported, not reconciled.
  • MS witness. A = Vienna, ÖNB, cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.34 (a copy / Afskrift), as given in the edition. Edition of record: Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII (where the letter stands among the 1593 Hagecius correspondence, near Tycho's Apologia ad Ioannis Craigi Capnuraniae restinctionis fragmentum).
  • §1 Dreyer collation: Dreyer (Opera Omnia VII, p. 352) prints the day — Datae Uraniburgi 20 April. Anno 1593. The earlier note that the day is unsupported by the text is withdrawn; "20" restored to header, Source, and translation.
