The original

HENRICUS BRUCÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

NOBILISSIME, charissimeque D. TYCHO, si vivis et vales, gaudeo. Superioribus enim mensibus spargebatur fama mihi acerba, quam avertant Superi, te nostri amantissimum e vivis excessisse. Cum tamen dubius essem, emi tibi Gnomonicen CLAVIJ Bambergensis, et MAGINI Tabulas novas et Ephemeridas ad Calendarij emendationem a GREGORIO XIII Pontifice Maximo institutam, compositas. Æstimat autem Bambergensis Gnomonicen Ioachimicis<sup></sup> 7, Ephemeridas vero, in quibus in motuum supputatione errores a STADIO commissos adiunxit, unâ cum Tabulis æstimat Ioachimicis XI vel XII, ut<sup></sup> tota summa Ioachimicorum sit 18. Emi autem hac conditione, ut si tibi forte non placerent, aut forte eosdem iam esses nactus, Bibliopola ad se recipiat. Quid igitur hac in parte fieri velis, quam primum significes cupio. Ego, quod mare esset clausum, citius procurare non potui. Nam ad Paschatis festum aut libri sunt restituendi aut pecunia numeranda. Iam enim libros ineunte hyeme apud me deposuit. Tu ut valeas et quid agas, fac intelligam, an aliquid sub prælo sit, et quid premas. Tuos Cometas expecto, post tot aliorum labores aliquid præstanti tuo ingenio dignum, quod miretur posteritas. Calendarij enim editioni<sup></sup> cum indicijs mutationum aëris non credo, quod aliquem ex tuis ridendum populo velis cum alijs præbere. Quod enim CATO de Aruspicibus, id ego de hisce prædictionibus cogitare soleo. Mirari se aiebat, si cum sibi mutuo occurrunt, non rideant. Nunc venio ad postremas tuas literas, in quibus (quantum memini) tu decoctiones, infusiones, et nescio quæ alia in Galenicis rides, nec quicquam videris probare, nisi quod summo labore extractum ex metallis aut quintas essentias aut tincturas aut spiritus sapiat. Patere nos obvijs uti paratuque facilibus; feras etiam in amico non moleste, plures ab eo restitui decoctis, infusis, et sola ptisana, quam maxima eorum turba, quise solos sapere arbitrantur, quintis essentijs, tincturis, spiritibus, alijsque occultis, ut ipsi iudicant, naturis, quæ tamen omnes a primis qualitatibus dependent. Decoctione certe facultates rerum coctibilium in liquorem transferri, nullus negaverit. At ex auro et lapidibus pretiosis, corallis nihil decoctione deciditur? Fateor id; nos hæc ijs relinquimus, quibus licet ex unico vitrioli spiritu multas tincturas parare, addito colore alterius rei mixtione. Scis quem notem. Neque hæc a me referuntur, ut si quid a posteris bene ad humani generis salutem sit inventum, id damnem; absit, immo laudem magis et sequar alijsque communicem. Insanientis enim hominis fuerit, ac animi plane maligni, calumniari, quæ bene sunt dicta, et aliorum inventa deprimere. Quid igitur, inquies, in hoc genere Chymico non laboras, et hoc quicquid est cognitioni humorali elementari primarum qualitatum, alijsque verbosis GALENI nugis non adiungis? Sic enim ex sulphure, sale et hydrargyro compositum genus hominum eructare solet. Dicam quod verum est; magistris destituor, mutos non intelligo. Tu itaque, qui iam aliquot annis in hoc genere laboras et sedulo versatus es, si quid habes, quod in gravissimis morbis Apoplexia, Epilepsia, Paralysi contractis Melancholia, divina manu succurrat, communica, et componendi rationem utendique modum et mensuram demonstra. Memini de te audivisse, Epilepticos non paucos a te curatos, verum an id per spiritum vitrioli facias ignoro. Si sic, aperi quæso quomodo pares, quæ admisceas, quomodo exhibeas, quoties et quo tempore. Age noli invidere amico, quæ habes, qui tui semper fuit et erit studiosissimus amantissimusque. Bene vale integerrimeque amantissimeque mi TYCHO, et me, quod facis, mutuo ama. Rostochij XV Martij An. Domini 1586.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Heinrich Brucaeus to Tycho Brahe.

Most noble and dearest Master Tycho, if you are alive and well, I rejoice. For in the months past a report bitter to me was being spread abroad — which may the Powers above avert — that you, most loving of us, had departed from among the living. Yet, since I was in doubt, I bought for you the Gnomonice of Clavius of Bamberg, and Magini's new Tables and Ephemerides, composed [to accord] with the reform of the calendar instituted by Pope Gregory XIII. Now the man of Bamberg values the Gnomonice at 7 Joachimsthalers<sup>[A]</sup>; the Ephemerides, however — in which he has appended the errors committed by Stadius in the computation of the motions — together with the Tables he values at 11 or 12 Joachimsthalers, so that<sup>[A]</sup> the whole sum comes to 18 Joachimsthalers. But I bought them on this condition: that if perhaps they should not please you, or perhaps you should already have got hold of the same, the bookseller take them back to himself. What, then, you would have done in this matter, I desire that you signify as soon as possible. I, because the sea was closed, could not procure them sooner; for at the feast of Easter either the books are to be returned or the money to be paid out — since he deposited the books with me at the beginning of winter. Make me understand how you are and what you are doing — whether anything is under the press, and what you are printing. I await your Comets: after the labours of so many others, something worthy of your outstanding genius, that posterity may marvel at. For as to an edition of a calendar<sup>[A]</sup> with forecasts of changes of the weather, I do not believe that you would wish to set forth any of your [men] to be laughed at by the people along with the others. For what Cato [said] of the soothsayers, that I am wont to think of these predictions: he used to say he wondered that, when they meet one another, they do not laugh. Now I come to your last letter, in which (as far as I recall) you laugh at decoctions, infusions, and I know not what else among the Galenicals, and seem to approve of nothing unless it taste of what is extracted with the utmost labour from metals — either quintessences or tinctures or spirits. Allow us to use the things that come to hand and are easily prepared; and do not take it amiss in a friend, [to hear] that more people are restored by him with decoctions, infusions, and barley-water alone than by the very great crowd of those who reckon that they alone are wise, with quintessences, tinctures, spirits, and other occult natures (as they themselves judge them) — which all nonetheless depend upon the first qualities. That by decoction the powers of decoctible things are transferred into a liquid, no one would deny. But from gold and precious stones, from corals, is nothing brought down by decoction? I grant it; we leave these things to those who are permitted, out of a single spirit of vitriol, to prepare many tinctures, the colour being added by the mixture of some other thing. You know whom I mark. Nor are these things recounted by me so that, if anything has been well discovered by later men for the welfare of the human race, I should condemn it — far from it; rather I would the more praise and follow it and share it with others. For it would be the part of a man out of his senses, and of a downright malicious mind, to slander things that are well said, and to disparage the inventions of others. What then, you will say, do you not labour in this Chymical kind, and not join this — whatever it is — to the humoral, elemental knowledge of the first qualities, and to Galen's other wordy trifles? For thus the breed of men compounded out of sulphur, salt, and quicksilver is wont to belch forth. I shall say what is true: I am bereft of masters; the dumb I do not understand. You, therefore, who now for some years labour in this kind and are diligently versed in it — if you have anything that, in the gravest diseases, in apoplexy, epilepsy, paralysis of contracted [limbs], melancholy, may by a divine hand bring relief, share it, and show the manner of compounding and the mode and measure of using it. I remember having heard of you that not a few epileptics were cured by you; but whether you do it by spirit of vitriol I do not know. If so, open up, I pray, how you prepare it, what you mix in, how you administer it, how often and at what time. Come, do not begrudge a friend the things you have — [a friend] who always has been and will be most devoted and most loving toward you. Fare you very well, most upright and most loving my Tycho, and love me, as you do, in return. At Rostock, 15 March, in the year of the Lord 1586.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Hendrik van den Broeck, 1530/31–1593), Flemish-born physician and professor of mathematics, then of medicine, at the University of Rostock; one of Tycho's older academic correspondents and an early teacher-figure. The letter answers a lost (or earlier) letter of Tycho's on remedies and engages him as a bibliographical agent.
  • Place. Rostochium → Rostock; the Hanseatic port and university town. quod mare esset clausum ("the sea was closed") refers to the winter closure of Baltic shipping, delaying delivery — not a political blockade.
  • Books purchased for Tycho.
  • Gnomonicen CLAVIJ Bambergensis = the Gnomonices libri octo (Rome, 1581) of Christophorus Clavius ("of Bamberg," Clavius's birthplace), the Jesuit mathematician and chief architect of the Gregorian reform. Gnomonice (the art of sundials/shadow-instruments) is kept as the work-title, not glossed to "dialling."
  • MAGINI Tabulas novas et Ephemeridas = the Ephemerides and Novae coelestium orbium theoricae/tables of Giovanni Antonio Magini of Bologna, here described as compositas … ad Calendarij emendationem … a GREGORIO XIII … institutam — computed to agree with the Gregorian calendar reform of 1582.
  • errores a STADIO commissos — the computational errors of Johannes Stadius (1527–1579), whose own widely-used Ephemerides Magini's apparatus corrected.
  • Currency. Ioachimici = Joachimsthalers (silver thalers struck at Joachimsthal/Jáchymov); the prices (7 + 11–12 = 18) are given in this coin. Rendered "Joachimsthalers," the Latin retained on first use.
  • Apparatus markers in the source. The superscript A appearing three times in the Dreyer text (Ioachimicis<sup>A</sup> 7, ut<sup>A</sup> tota summa, editioni<sup>A</sup> cum indicijs) is not part of the letter: it is Dreyer's witness/variant siglum (A = the Vienna MS, cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66) keyed to footnotes in Opera Omnia VII. It is reproduced in the Source block as printed and bracketed Secure in the translation, where it carries no semantic content.
  • Allusion: Cato on the soothsayers. Quod enim CATO de Aruspicibus … Mirari se aiebat, si cum sibi mutuo occurrunt, non rideant — the proverbial risus haruspicum: the elder Cato's remark (reported by Cicero, De divinatione 2.24.51) that he wondered how one haruspex could meet another without laughing. Brucaeus applies it to weather-prognostication; the soothsayer (haruspex) rendering is kept literal.
  • Astrology / publishing. Calendarij … editioni cum indicijs mutationum aëris = an edition of a (prognosticating) calendar "with forecasts of changes of the weather" — i.e. an astro-meteorological almanac; Brucaeus presumes Tycho would not lend one of his own people to such a popularly-derided genre. Cometas = Tycho's projected work on comets (the De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, on the 1577 comet, then in preparation/press); kept as the work-reference Comets.
  • Medicine / alchemy (the core of the letter). A Galenist's defence against Tycho's Paracelsian (chymical) leanings.
  • decoctiones, infusiones … sola ptisana → decoctions, infusions … barley-water alone (ptisana, the classic Galenic barley-tisane). decoctio by this chapter's convention → decoction.
  • quintas essentias / tincturas / spiritus → quintessences / tinctures / spirits — the Paracelsian preparations Brucaeus distrusts.
  • primae qualitates → the first qualities (hot/cold/wet/dry of Galenic-Aristotelian medicine); cognitio humoralis elementaris → humoral, elemental knowledge.
  • spiritus vitrioli → spirit of vitriol (the distillate of vitriol, ≈ sulphuric acid / its preparations); kept as the period term, not modernised.
  • ex sulphure, sale et hydrargyro compositum genus hominum → "the breed of men compounded out of sulphur, salt, and quicksilver" — a barbed reference to the Paracelsian tria prima (the three principles), turned against the Paracelsians themselves; hydrargyrus = quicksilver/mercury.
  • Diseases: Apoplexia, Epilepsia, Paralysi contractis [membris], Melancholia → apoplexy, epilepsy, paralysis of contracted [limbs], melancholy. contractis is read as agreeing with an understood membris ("contracted/cramped limbs"); the elision is flagged, the supplied word bracketed.
  • remedium-register: Brucaeus's claim that ordinary remedies restore (restitui) more patients is preserved as a comparative boast, not sharpened into a universal.
  • "You know whom I mark." Scis quem notem — Brucaeus pointedly declines to name the alchemist he is criticising (one ex unico vitrioli spiritu multas tincturas parare … addito colore — counterfeiting many tinctures from a single spirit of vitriol by adding colour); the studied reticence is kept, the target left unnamed as in the source.
  • Epistolary formulae. Opening si vivis et vales, gaudeo — the old "if you are alive and well, I rejoice" formula. Close Bene vale … mi TYCHO, et me … mutuo ama → "Fare you very well … my Tycho, and love me … in return." studiosissimus amantissimusque → in the affective superlative register.
  • Dateline / calendar. Rostochij XV Martij An. Domini 1586 = "At Rostock, 15 March, in the year of the Lord 1586" (O.S.; Denmark and Lutheran Mecklenburg both still on the Julian calendar in 1586). The edition label gives the place only ("Rostock"); the as-written date 15 March 1586 is carried into the header. No in-letter date discrepancy.
