The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD STENONEM BRAHE].

MIN gandske broderligen och venlig hilsen nu och althiid forsend med Vor Herre. Kierre Sten och kierre broder, Gud allermechtigste werre nu och allthiid hoß dig och beuare dig naadelligen och well fran allt ont. Jeg tacker dig venligen och gierne for alt beuist gode, huilket igien aff formuen att forskylde du stedtze skall finde mig godwillig och som din hulde broder. Kierre broder, haffuer ieg bekommit [dit breff] met min søn Thyge, som kom hid thill mig igien strax epther nøtaarsdag, och skallt du haffue megen tack for samme din gode och welmente skriffuelse, och for hueß du giorde bemelte min søn thillgode den stund, hand var der inde y landit. Hand berettid for mig, att din søn Otto skullde werre kommen hiem vdjgien met m. Kort, endog hand icke thallte met hannom føren hand affdrog, thi der gaffß ingen leylighed. Lad mig wiide, horledis du nu willde haffue det met Otte, om du willt haffue hannom thill hoffue eller lade hannom wijdre studere, och lad hannom sellff skriffue mig thill paa latine, som hand pleier att giørre. Jeg will inted nu formelde om mine wilkar och thilstand, thi du nochsom haffuer det fornummit aff min siste skriffuellße och hueß ieg vidløfftiger derom haffde skreffuit Elke Bilde till. Alltingest er endnu, Gud ske loff, y en god wesen, och forbedrer sig eptherhonen, Gud allmechtigste giffue wijdre løcke och benedidelße. Her bøggis nu flux, epther røm. key. may. nadigst befalling, gelegenhed och plaß till mine instrumenter ad sette, endog die icke endnu erre alle ankommen, thi winterren forhindrede, att ieg icke saa snart kunde faa denom hid paa Elben, saa att somme erre endnu thill Magdeburg, och die, min søn hentte siste gang aff Danmarck, formener ieg att werre thill Hamborgk. Hanß maytt. skreff die herrer aff Magdeburg till nogit for Michaelis nest forgangen, att die wilde forskaffue mig die, som der er, hid op y Behemen formedelst Elbstrømmen, saa fant die itt politisk vdloff y breffuit, att det icke vtrøcklig løde, att die skulde giørre det paa deris bekaastning. Huilket der hans may. fick att wijde, lod han itt andit breff vdgaa thill dennom, huilket tøde det første vdtrøckelligen, och der die dervdaff ingen praetext kunde finde, skreffue die mig till och varre begerendiß, att ieg vndertanigst paa deris wegne wille vndskølde dennom y die maader hoß keysern och forevende den hore bestolding, die haffde lit, hueß tuang die icke endnu haffde foruundet, huilket, der keyserens raad fick att wijde, warre di sposk och vnderlige deroffuer; thi det er icke langt fra itt halff hundrit aar siden bestoldingen war, och blef sagt, man kunde well ocsaa finde paa itt vdloff, nar die haffde keysern ygien y behoff, och hans may. tog det ocsaa hart till mødstøcke, och befoll strag att skriffue domherrene vdj Magdeburg till derom, huilke godwilligen indgick saadant att wille giørre hanß maytt. till naadigst gefallen. Jeg forseer mig ocsaa, att die herrer aff Hamborgk vdj lige maade selffuilligen retter dennom epther keyserens skriffuelse, som die och derudom haffuer bekommit, saa att ieg wenter bade mine jnstrumenter och andit vdj Tyskland eptherlat goß hid op met det første. Jeg haffde lenge siden thi[l]forn fornummit, att Gud haffde henkallid din høstru Cirsten, huilket mig giorde ont, menß man for att lade sig nøye met Gudz wille; haud vnde dig en anden god vdigien, som kan werre epther dit sin, dig och dinne till glaede. Det du skriffuer om, att du achtede ad Mons Bertelsen skulde haffue skriffuit mig till, er icke sked. Ved icke huad du mener, thi Monß haffuer inted skriffue[t] mig thill, endog ieg icke forgiette att besøie hannom met mit breff for voriß gammel kundskab skyld; aff huad aarsag hand saadant haffuer lat, maa hand selff wijde. Kierre broder, giør vell och vndskyld mig hos mine andre brødre, att ieg icke paa denne thid skriffuer dennom till, tj ieg haffuer icke gode stunder och ved icke heller andit att skriffue end som ieg siste gang giorde. Sig dennom met alle andre voris gode wenner mange gode netter paa mine wegne. Och vill hermet nu och allthiid haffue dig met alt, hueß du wellt wilst, Gud almechtigste befalit.

Daterit paa det keyserlige slot Benatick y lante Bemen den 21 dag martij stylo nouo anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TYCHO BRAHE TO STEEN BRAHE].

My wholly brotherly and friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with [the blessing of] Our Lord. Dear Steen and dear brother, may God Almighty be now and always with you, and preserve you graciously and well from all evil. I thank you amicably and gladly for all kindness shown, which to repay in return you shall always find me willing, and as your faithful brother. Dear brother, I have received [your letter] with my son Tyge, who came back to me right after New Year's Day; and you shall have much thanks for the same good and well-meant letter, and for what you did for my said son while he was there in the country. He reported to me that your son Otto was [said] to have come home again with Master Kort, although he did not speak with him before he departed, for no opportunity was given. Let me know how you would now have it with Otto — whether you want him at court [in service] or to let him study further — and let him write to me himself in Latin, as he is wont to do. I will not now report on my circumstances, for you have sufficiently perceived them from my last letter and from what I had written more fully to Eske Bille. Everything is still, God be praised, in a good state, and improves by degrees; may God Almighty give further fortune and blessing.

Here there is now busy building, by the Roman Imperial Majesty's gracious command, [for] accommodation and place to set up my instruments — though they have not yet all arrived, for the winter prevented me from getting them hither so soon on the Elbe, so that some are still at Magdeburg, and those my son fetched last time from Denmark are, I think, at Hamburg. His Majesty wrote to the lords of Magdeburg somewhat before last Michaelmas that they should convey to me those that are there, up into Bohemia by the Elbe stream; but they found a "political loophole" in the letter — that it did not expressly say they should do it at their own expense. Which when His Majesty learned, he had another letter go out to them, expressly construing the first; and when they could find no pretext from it, they wrote to me, desiring that I would humbly, on their behalf, excuse them to the Emperor and plead the grievous sacking they had suffered, the burden of which they had not yet overcome. Which, when the Emperor's council learned of it, they were scornful and astonished thereat; for it is not far from fifty years since the storming was, and it was said one could also well find a "loophole" the next time they had need of the Emperor; and His Majesty too took it ill, and ordered at once to write to the Cathedral canons at Magdeburg about it — who willingly agreed to do such as would be to His Majesty's gracious pleasure. I also expect that the lords of Hamburg will in like manner willingly conform to the Emperor's letter, which they too have received, so that I expect both my instruments and other goods left in Germany [to come] up here at the first [opportunity].

I had long ago perceived that God had called away your wife Kirsten, which grieved me; but one must be content with God's will. May He grant you another good [wife] in return, after your own mind, to the joy of you and yours. What you write — that you supposed Mons Bertelsen should have written to me — has not happened; I know not what you mean, for Mons has written nothing to me, though I did not forget to greet him with my letter, for our old acquaintance's sake; for what reason he has omitted it, he may himself know. Dear brother, be so good as to excuse me to my other brothers, that I do not write to them at this time, for I have not good leisure, nor know anything else to write than I did last time. Say to them, with all our other good friends, many good nights on my behalf. And herewith I will, now and always, commend you, with all that you wish well, to God Almighty.

Dated at the imperial castle Benátky, in the land of Bohemia, the 21st day of March, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Steen (Sten) Brahe (1547–1620), Tycho's brother, a Danish Rigsraad councillor. One of the cluster of same-day Danish family letters (21 March 1600 N.S.) — with the letters to their mother Beate Bille (brahe-t_16000321004) and to the kinsman Peder Brahe (brahe-t_16000323001). The MS is an autograph draft (Kladde).
  • Family matters. Tycho thanks Steen for kindness to his son Tyge (back from Denmark "after New Year's"); asks Steen's plans for his son Otto — court service or further study — bidding Otto "write to me himself in Latin, as he is wont"; offers condolence on the death of Steen's wife Kirsten and a wish for a new marriage; and notes the puzzle of Mons Bertelsen, who never wrote though Tycho had greeted him.
  • Significance — the Magdeburg instrument-transport saga (inside view). A vivid, candid account complementing the formal letter to the Cathedral Chapter (brahe-t_16000404001): Rudolf II had written the Magdeburg city council (before Michaelmas) to ship Tycho's stored instruments up the Elbe; the councillors found a "political loophole" (politisk udløb) — the letter did not say at their expense — and, when the Emperor's clarifying second letter closed it, asked Tycho to excuse them to the Emperor by pleading the old "sacking" they had suffered (the 1550–51 siege/sack of Magdeburg — "not far from fifty years" past). The Emperor's council were "scornful" (one could "find a loophole" the next time Magdeburg needed the Emperor), the Emperor "took it ill," and ordered the matter put to the Cathedral Chapter, which agreed — while Hamburg is expected to comply likewise. A revealing glimpse of imperial-municipal friction behind the instrument move.
  • Persons named. Steen Brahe (and his son Otto Brahe and late wife Kirsten); Master Kort (Otto's tutor/companion); Tyge (Tycho the Younger); Eske Bille; Mons Bertelsen; the Magdeburg city council and Cathedral Chapter; the Hamburg council; Rudolf II.
  • Calendar. Dateline Benatick den 21 dag Martij stylo novo anno 1600; New Style (Prague). Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Closing dateline restored to include 'y lante Bemen' ('in the land of Bohemia'), dropped from the source text, per Dreyer, TBOO XIV p.177; the English dateline updated to match.

- text error (low). The transcribed original silently drops the phrase 'y lante Bemen' ('in the land of Bohemia') that Dreyer prints between the castle name and the date; the English inherits the omission. Does not mislead a scholar since the letter's own heading and body already establish Benatky/Bohemia as the place of writing. Repaired. Verified against the Dreyer page image (TBOO XIV p.177), which clearly reads '...Benatick y lante Bemen den 21 dag martij ftylo nouo anno 1600'.
