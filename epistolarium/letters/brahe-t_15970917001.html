The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD HENRICUM RANZOVIUM.

S. P.

LITERÆ tuæ, Nobilissime et Amantissime Domine RANZOVI, amice et affinis honorande, per Præfectum tuum CHRISTIANUM mihi redditæ sunt, cum quo loca a te peramice oblata collustravi, et pro benevolo hoc erga me affectu gratias officiose ago quas possum maximas, pro virili oblatâ occasione relaturus. Placeret quidem utraque conditio, et præ cæteris ea, quæ Segebergæ monstrata est; verumenimvero, cum nonnihil remotiores sic simus ab urbe quâdam amplâ, nec ego otiosam libenter vitam traducerem, sed libros quosdam e meis prælo ferme absolutos omnino perficere vellem, ad quod Typographia requireretur, et Artifices, qui figuras sculpere nossent, tum quoque qui Instrumentum unum vel alterum pro Observationibus cœlitus obtinendis (modo nullum ex ijs quæ Haffniæ reposui, mature inde recepero) pararent. Si itaque prope Lubecam vel Hamburgum aliquod prædium mihi ad tempus, donec rebus meis commodius prospectum fuerit, concedere posses, feceris rem inprimis exoptatam. Constitui vero Deo volente, cras Bramstedium usque circa vesperam venire, quia tam cito negotia mea expedire non possum, præsertim in quibusdam scribendis, ut circa meridiem tibi adsim. Si interim Bredenbergam iveris, subsequar confestim, ita ut die Lunæ annuente numine ibi ulterius de omnibus consilium tecum capiam, et desiderium hoc visendæ Arcis eius amplissimæ, inprimisque splendidæ bibliothecæ tuæ, quod me iampridem incessit, nunc tandem expleam. Interea Mæ. Tæ. omnia fausta precor, eamque quam diutissime incolumem ex animo voveo.

Datæ Segebergæ 17. Septembris Anni 97.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Henrik Rantzau.

Heartiest greeting.

Your letter, most noble and most loving Master Rantzau, my friend and kinsman to be honoured, was delivered to me by your steward Christian, with whom I inspected the places you had so amicably offered; and for this kindly disposition towards me I dutifully give you the greatest thanks I can, intending to repay the offered occasion to the best of my power. Both arrangements would indeed please me, and above the rest the one that was shown [me] at Segeberg; but in truth, since we should thus be somewhat too far removed from some large town — and since I would not willingly pass an idle life, but should wish to bring quite to completion certain of my books, all but finished at the press, for which a printing-house would be required, and craftsmen who knew how to engrave figures, and likewise [someone] who might prepare one or another instrument for obtaining observations from the heavens (provided I recover in good time none of those which I have stored at Copenhagen) — if therefore you could grant me for a time some estate near Lübeck or Hamburg, until my affairs have been more conveniently provided for, you would do a thing especially longed-for. But I have resolved, God willing, to come tomorrow as far as Bramstedt about evening — since I cannot dispatch my business so quickly, especially in [the matter of] certain things to be written — so as to be with you about midday. If in the meantime you have gone to Breitenburg, I shall follow at once, so that on Monday, with the divine favour assenting, I may there take further counsel with you about everything, and may now at last fulfil this longing to see that most spacious castle of yours, and above all your splendid library, which has long since seized me. Meanwhile I pray every good fortune for Your Lordship, and from my heart I vow [you] safe and sound as long as possible.

Given at Segeberg, 17 September, the year [15]97.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing during his departure from Denmark. He left Hven in spring 1597 and, after a stay at Rostock, moved westward through Holstein; by 17 September 1597 he and his familia had reached Segeberg Fortress, the seat of the king's governor of Schleswig-Holstein, finding the governor away at Bramstedt (so "On Tycho's Island," confirming the situation behind this letter). The "books all but finished at the press" point to the works then in progress (the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata and Epistolae); the instruments "stored at Copenhagen" (Haffniae reposui) are those of his dismantled Hven establishment.
  • Recipient. Henricus Ranzovius = Henrik Rantzau / Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), the Danish crown's Statthalter (royal governor) of Schleswig-Holstein, a major humanist patron, author, and bibliophile; his castle of Breitenburg held one of the most celebrated private libraries of the age. He died the following year (1598). Addressed as affinis ("kinsman"): the Brahe and Rantzau families were connected by marriage.
  • Praefectum tuum Christianum — "your steward/bailiff Christian": Rantzau's praefectus (estate official) who carried the letter and guided Tycho over the offered properties; rendered "steward." First name only is given; not further identified here.
  • Places. Segeberga = Segeberg (Bad Segeberg, Holstein), the fortress where Tycho was lodged and from which the letter is dated. Bramstedium = Bramstedt (Bad Bramstedt), where Rantzau then was. Bredenberga = Breitenburg, Rantzau's principal seat with the great library (the Arx amplissima … splendida bibliotheca of the next clause); the Latinised place-name Bredenberga reflects the Low-German form Bredenberg. Lubeca = Lübeck; Hamburgum = Hamburg; Haffnia = Copenhagen.
  • Technical terms. praelo … absolutos / Typographia — "[books] finished at the press / a printing-house": Tycho's need to carry his own typographic and engraving capacity with him into exile. figuras sculpere = "to engrave figures" (the diagrams/illustrations of his astronomical books). Instrumentum … pro Observationibus coelitus obtinendis = "an instrument for obtaining observations from the heavens" — instrumentum per glossary ("instrument"); observationes kept as "observations." No planetary or zodiac symbols occur in this letter.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = salutem plurimam (dicit), "heartiest greeting". Mae. Tae. = Maiestati Tuae / Magnificentiae Tuae — see crux below. manu propria = "in his own hand" (an autograph subscription).
  • Reading crux — Mae. Tae. The abbreviation Mae. Tae. (expanded by Dreyer) is rendered "Your Lordship." It most plausibly stands for Magnificentiae Tuae ("Your Magnificence"), the courtesy due a high noble and governor; it is not Maiestati Tuae ("Your Majesty"), which would be reserved for a sovereign and would not suit a letter to Rantzau. The expansion is editorial; the precise courtesy-title behind the contraction is not certain from the abbreviation alone, and is flagged rather than silently fixed.
  • Calendar. Datae Segebergae 17. Septembris Anni 97 = "Given at Segeberg, 17 September, the year [15]97" (Old Style — Denmark and the Lutheran territories kept the Julian calendar). The dateline date (17 September) agrees with the catalogue label ("1597. 17. september. Segeberg"); no discrepancy. die Lunae = "on Monday" — left as the weekday named, not converted to a civil date.
