The original

TYGE BRAHE TILL KNUDSTRUP FICK BREFF FORBERØRTE HELLIGE THRE KONGER CAPELLES BYGNING ANRØRENDIS VT SEQUITUR.

Christian.

WOR gunst thilfornn. Viid, epter att Wij komme wdi forfaringe, huorledis Hellige [thre] konger capelle wed Roschilde domkircke schall møgit werre bygfeldig och forfalden, att thend klart will nederfalde och wdj grunden forderffuis, saa frampt ther till met thet første icke schaffuis raad och kommis till hielp, och epterdi ditt forleeningsbreff paa for:ne capelle eblant andit nochsom thilholder dig at schulle holde for:ne capelle med residentzen och andit guodtz, ther tilliggendis er, wed guoed heffd och macht, tha bede Wij dig och wille, attu met thet forderligste tiltencker at schaffue raad och middell, att samme capelle wißeligen inden winteren paakommer igien kand bliffue forferdiget och forbedrit epter ditt forleeningsbreffs liudelße och indtholdt. Saaframpt for:ne capelle paa sin heffd, grund och bygning for din forsømmelße schyldt lider ydermere nogen bryst, schade och forderff, eller och anden wbeleiglighet ther aff kunde indtfalde, Wij tha thet icke schulle wiide huoß dig, och dertill wdi saa maade tiltencke andre raad och middell, wed huilcke for:ne capell kand holdis wed sin thilbørlig heffd och magt. Ther med scheer Wor aluorlig wilge och befalning. Thj thag her aldelis ingen forsømmelße fore.

Befalendis dig Gud.
Haffniæ 30. augusti a:o 91.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tyge Brahe of Knudstrup received a letter concerning the building [maintenance] of the aforesaid Chapel of the Three Holy Kings, as follows:

Christian.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, after we came into knowledge how the Chapel of the Three [Holy] Kings at Roskilde Cathedral is said to be much dilapidated and decayed, so that it will plainly fall down and be ruined to its foundations, if remedy be not provided for it and help brought to it at the very first; and since your letter of enfeoffment upon the aforesaid chapel, among other things, sufficiently enjoins you that you are to keep the aforesaid chapel, together with the residence and other property belonging to it, in good upkeep and condition: therefore we ask you, and will [it], that you with all dispatch take thought to provide ways and means, so that the same chapel may surely, before winter come on, be put in good repair again and amended according to the tenor and content of your letter of enfeoffment. If the aforesaid chapel, in its upkeep, foundation, and fabric, should through fault of your negligence suffer any further defect, harm, and ruin, or else any other inconvenience could fall out therefrom, then we shall not let that rest with you, and shall thereupon, in such case, take thought for other ways and means, whereby the aforesaid chapel may be kept in its due upkeep and condition. Herewith is done our earnest will and command. Therefore undertake herein absolutely no negligence.

Commending you to God.
At Copenhagen, 30 August, year [15]91.

Apparatus

- Issuer. Christian IV of Denmark (1577–1648; r. from his father Frederik II's death in 1588). In 1591 the fourteen-year-old king was still a minor under the regency council (Frederik II had died 4 April 1588; Christian was not crowned until 1596), so this chancery missive issues in his name but under the regency's administration. The single-word subscription "Christian." is the king's chancery superscription, not an autograph.
- Recipient. Tycho Brahe, here styled Tyge Brahe till Knudstrup — "Tyge Brahe of Knudstrup," his patrimonial seat in Scania, the form used for him as a nobleman and crown fief-holder rather than as the astronomer of Hven.
- Subject — the chapel and the fief. The Chapel of the Three Holy Kings (Hellige Tre Konger Kapel, the Chapel of the Magi) is the burial chapel attached to Roskilde Cathedral, built under King Christian I (consecrated by 1463) as a royal mausoleum. Its associated prebend/canonry was a crown benefice; Tycho held it (with revenues attached) under a forleningsbrev ("letter of enfeoffment / letter of grant"), which carried the upkeep of the chapel and its residence as a charge upon the holder. The letter is a formal admonition that he is failing that obligation.
- Document type. A chancery-register entry (Sjællandske Tegnelser). The Latin rubric "…HELLIGE THRE KONGER CAPELLES BYGNING ANRØRENDIS ut sequitur" ("concerning the building [maintenance] of the Chapel of the Three Holy Kings, as follows") heads the Danish body — a code-switch preserved by the edition's convention bygning here denotes the upkeep/fabric-maintenance of the building, not new construction; rendered "building [maintenance]."
- Negations and legal force (counted; none added or dropped). The source carries exactly three negations, each translated and none introduced:
1. …saa frampt ther till met thet første icke schaffuis raad och kommis till hielp — "if remedy be not provided for it and help brought to it at the very first": the conditional protasis describing what causes the ruin. Rendered as a negative conditional, mood preserved (subjunctive/passive schaffuis, kommis = "be provided / be brought").
2. Wij tha thet icke schulle wiide huoß dig — "then we shall not let that rest with you." The chancery idiom vide hos (en) = "to leave / let rest with (someone)"; the negation makes this a threat, not a reassurance: should the chapel decay further through Tycho's negligence, the king will not leave the matter in Tycho's hands but will himself "take thought for other ways and means" — i.e. transfer the charge (and by implication the fief). The smoother but wrong reading ("we shall not know it from you") is rejected; the legal sense is removal of trust/responsibility.
3. Thj thag her aldelis ingen forsømmelße fore — "Therefore undertake herein absolutely no negligence." aldelis ingen = "absolutely no" (intensifier + negative), the closing imperative. tage … fore = "to undertake / set about." Kept as a single emphatic negative command.
- Conditional structure. The threat-clause "Saaframpt for:ne capelle … lider ydermere nogen bryst, schade och forderff, eller och anden wbeleiglighet …, Wij tha thet icke schulle wiide huoß dig, och dertill … tiltencke andre raad och middell" is one conditional period: protasis "if the chapel suffers further defect through your negligence," apodosis "then [we] shall not leave it with you, and shall take thought for other means." schulle … tiltencke are modal/future ("shall take thought"); the future-conditional force is preserved without sharpening into a flat present.
- Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1591). WOR gunst thilfornn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula, the edition's convention); Viid = Vid ("Know"); komme wdi forfaringe = kom i forfaring ("came into knowledge / learned"); bygfeldig = "dilapidated, in need of repair" (lit. "fall-prone in fabric"); forfalden = "decayed"; klart will nederfalde = "will plainly fall down"; wdj grunden forderffuis = "be ruined to its foundations"; forleeningsbreff = forleningsbrev ("letter of enfeoffment / letter of grant"); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); eblant andit = iblandt andet ("among other things"); nochsom thilholder = "sufficiently enjoins / obliges"; residentzen = "the residence"; heffd och macht/magt = "upkeep and condition (legal possession and standing)"; forderligste = "most expeditious / with all dispatch"; tiltencker / tiltencke = "take thought, devise"; raad och middell = "ways and means"; wißeligen = "surely, assuredly"; inden winteren paakommer = "before winter comes on"; forferdiget = "completed/repaired"; forbedrit = "amended/bettered"; liudelße och indtholdt = "tenor and content"; bryst = "defect, lack" (brøst); wbeleiglighet = ubelejlighed ("inconvenience"); indtfalde = "fall out, arise"; thilbørlig = "due, proper"; aluorlig wilge och befalning = "earnest will and command"; aldelis = "altogether, absolutely."
- Calendar / place. Haffniae 30. augusti a:o 91 = "At Copenhagen (Hafnia), 30 August, year [15]91" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline date agrees with the edition label (30 August 1591); no in-letter date discrepancy. The dateline place-name is Latin (Haffniae) while the body is Danish — a code-switch preserved. Befalendis dig Gud ("Commending you to God") is the closing formula in place of Actum.
