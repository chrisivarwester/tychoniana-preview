The original

DOCTISSIMO VIRO MAGISTRO ANDREAe SEVERINI WELLEIO, CONCIONATORI IN REGIA HAFFNIENSI, SUO AMICO.
S. P.

DILECTE Magister ANDREA, peto ut iam unâ cum Domino Legato meos lares Huenenses velis invisere prout pollicitus es, et roga meo nomine Dn. Magistrum LAURENTIUM, Amicum nostrum, ut unâ venire velit. Quod si Doctor IOHANNES FRANCISCUS et D. EZOCCUS velint Domino Legato comites esse et ab urbanis molestijs per aliquot dies animum recreare et cum tenui boni consulere, fecerint mihi rem gratam. De Domino Quaestore Regio scripsi Domino Legato. Quod si is me in hac Insula invisere prae negotiis publicis, quibus assidue occupatur, non detrectaverit, fecerit mihi rem gratissimam; non enim diffido ipsum, quamvis pro dignitate et meritis hoc in loco minus sollenniter excipi possit, tamen pro conditione temporis et loci boni consulturum. Vale et significa per puerum meum quid sit futurum.

Iterum vale.
Datae Huenae die 5. Iulij Anno 1579.
T.
Tycho Brahe.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most learned man, Master Anders Sørensen Vedel, preacher at the royal residence in Copenhagen, his friend.
Heartiest greeting.

Beloved Master Anders, I ask that you now, together with the Lord Legate, be willing to visit my home on Hven, just as you promised; and ask in my name our friend Master Laurentius to be willing to come along too. And if Doctor Johannes Franciscus and Master Ezoccus should wish to be the Lord Legate's companions, and for some few days to refresh their minds from the troubles of the city and to take a slender fare in good part, they will have done me a welcome thing. Concerning the Lord Royal Treasurer I have written to the Lord Legate. And if he, despite the public business with which he is constantly occupied, will not decline to visit me on this island, he will have done me a most welcome thing; for I do not doubt that, even though here he can be received less ceremoniously than befits his rank and merits, he will nevertheless take it in good part, according to the circumstance of the time and the place. Farewell, and signify by my boy [the messenger] what is to be.

Again, farewell.
Given at Hven, on the 5th day of July, the year 1579.
Yours,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from his newly-established seat on the island of Hven (Uraniborg, the building of which had begun in 1576). The signature T. / Tycho Brahe and the dateline place this firmly in his first years on the island.
  • Recipient. Andreas Severini Welleius = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), Danish historian, theologian, and ballad-collector, Tycho's close friend (and kinsman by upbringing). The address-line styles him concionator in regia Hafniensi — "preacher at the royal residence in Copenhagen," i.e. court chaplain at Copenhagen Castle, the office Vedel then held.
  • S. P. — salutem plurimam (sc. dicit), "heartiest greeting". Vale … Iterum vale — "Farewell … Again, farewell".
  • lares Huenenses — literally "my Hven household gods," a classicising idiom for "my home / hearth on Hven"; rendered "my home on Hven." Huenae in the dateline = Hven (kept as is by the edition's convention).
  • Dominus Legatus — "the Lord Legate / Envoy." The honorand of the invitation, evidently a high-ranking official or ambassador then in Copenhagen whom Tycho hopes Vedel will escort to Hven; not named in the letter and not securely identified here — rendered by office, not by a guessed name.
  • Magister Laurentius — "Master Laurentius (Lauritz/Lars)," called Amicus noster ("our friend"); a member of Tycho's and Vedel's shared Copenhagen circle. Not further identified in the letter; left in the Latin form with the modern given name flagged.
  • Doctor Iohannes Franciscus and D. Ezoccus — "Doctor Johannes Franciscus" and "Master Ezoccus," two further members of the party. Neither is securely identifiable from this letter alone; both are left in their epistolary forms (D. expanded as Dominus = "Master,"). Crux flagged: Ezoccus is an unusual Latinised form and may be a copyist's spelling in the surviving Afskrift (copy); no normalisation is imposed.
  • Dominus Quaestor Regius — "the Lord Royal Treasurer," the crown financial officer (rentemester / royal treasurer). Identified here by office only; Tycho says he has written separately to the Legate about him.
  • cum tenui boni consulere / boni consulturum — the idiom boni consulere = "to take (something) in good part, to be content with"; here a courteous self-deprecation about the modest hospitality (tenui, "slender [fare/means]") Hven can offer. Rendered "to take a slender fare in good part" / "he will take it in good part," preserving the polite hedge rather than over-translating tenui as a specific noun (bracketed sense added only where English requires a head-noun).
  • per puerum meum — "by my boy," i.e. by Tycho's servant/messenger boy; significa "signify, send word." Rendered "by my boy [the messenger]," the gloss bracketed.
  • urbanis molestijs — "the troubles of the city" (Copenhagen), contrasted with the rural retreat of Hven; the -ij- spelling (molestijs) is the period orthography, kept in the source block.
  • Dateline / calendar. Datae Huenae die 5. Iulij Anno 1579 = "Given at Hven, 5 July 1579" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1579. 5. juli. Hven); no discrepancy.
  • Edition / witness. Edition of record: Dreyer, Opera Omnia (the Epistolae volumes). The sole cited witness is a copy (Afskrift) in Tycho Brahe's private archive, Rigsarkivet, Copenhagen — so the readings (e.g. Ezoccus) descend through a transcript, not an autograph.
  • Correction. Closing valediction corrected from an expansion of Tycho's own name ('T[ycho].') to its actual sense, the epistolary abbreviation 'Tuus' (Yours) — the same convention used elsewhere in TBOO for this abbreviation.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (low). The valediction abbreviation 'T.' immediately above the printed signature 'TYCHO BRAHE' is the standard Latin epistolary abbreviation 'Tuus' (Yours), not an abbreviation of Tycho's own name — the same convention appears elsewhere in TBOO (e.g. Bordingus's letter closes 'T. studiosissimus IACOBUS BORDINGUS' = 'Thy most devoted, Jacob Bording', correctly rendered there). Expanding it as '[Tycho]' produces an odd redundant reading ('T[ycho]. Tycho Brahe.') where the source has a normal 'Yours, Tycho Brahe' closing formula. At: “T[ycho].
Tycho Brahe.”.

Repaired. Verified the letter's structure against Dreyer, TBOO VII p.55, confirming the 'T. / TYCHO BRAHE.' two-line sign-off recurs identically for this and the immediately following Dançay letter on the same page, consistent with the standard Latin epistolary 'Tuus' valediction cited in the audit's parallel (Bordingus) example. Only the closing English clause was touched; the Latin is untouched per the mistranslation repair rule.
