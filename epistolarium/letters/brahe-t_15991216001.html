Vestra potius approbet CAMPANI divisionem,
quæ totum Cœlum ubique respicit et universalis existit. Cui tamen Generos.
Vestra æqualem per Eclipticam, ob planetarum iuxta hanc incessum, partitionem
applicare soleat, imitatione veterum Astrologorum; verum alia atque alia in hac
et illa ordinatione considerando, prout vel ratione totius Cœli vel solius
Zodiaci, requiratur animadversio. Hæc, inquam, etsi satis luculenter indicata

Generositas Vestra mihi unum atque alterum breve schema

Haberem insuper nonnulla alia non aspernanda, videlicet an directio

quæ respectu Poli Æquatoris fit, et an tunc eodem modo mensuratio annorum sit

Vestra censeat, quod si quis per aliquot multos annos in longinqua aliqua

directio Ascendentis ex ascensionibus obliquis, quas Tabula Directionum sub
elevatione Poli regionis nativitatis demonstrat, facienda sit; an vero ex
Ascensionibus obliquis sub elevatione Poli istius loci, sub quo quis degit, ut
exempli gratia natus est aliquis sub elevatione Poli 51 gradus, postea patria
egressus in alia regione, sub elevatione Poli 54 graduum sita, per totos

corpus nati afficiat, quam locus commorationis,
in quo vitam degit? cum tamen fere singulis annis in effectibus Eclipsium, pro

Tertio, quia ex Diario ELIÆ OLAI
Cimbri Octogenariam divisionem Thematum ☌, ☍, et
□ ☉lis et ☾æ,

malos inducere effectus, sed et transitus ♄ni et
♂tis, sive id fiat corpore, sive radijs

Quinto. An malorum planetarum effectus directione inducti per bonos

Sexto. Qui aspectus sive Directio malorum pro morte naturali quam
pernitiosissimus sit observatus, et quæ constitutio malorum vel Directio pro

Septimo. Videri quoque multos infaustas et violentas Genituras

habentes violenter interire. Quæritur, unde hæc operationum sive effectuum
diversitas, Astrologice loquendo, oriatur.

Harum itaque quæstionum Resolutiones, nisi Generositati Vestræ
admodum essem molestus, mihi aliquando etiam communicari submisse peterem. Quia
etiam in Astrologicis absque restitutione motus ☾næ

Vestra mihi significet, anne eiusdem Restitutio publici iuris reddita sit, ut

obnixe rogitans, ne me immeritum favore et benevolentia ex innata heroica erga
me affectione prosequi deserat, et diuturnum illud silentium mihi benigne
ignoscat.

Deus Opt. Max. Generos. Vestram cum
tota inclita familia salvam et incolumem ut conservet, tueatur atque defendat,

[German address] To the right-noble, stern, and highly-learned Lord Tycho Brahe, hereditary [lord] at Knudstrup, but at this time at the Imperial Court at Prague, my greatly-favouring Lord and Preceptor. At Prague.

Heartiest greeting.

Noble and magnificent Hero, my patron and Maecenas, most to be revered with the utmost respect: after the humble offering of my ready services, I again and above all give and render Your Generosity the greatest thanks I can, for each and all the benefits conferred on me, undeserving, out of [your] singular heroic and inborn affection. Next, I cannot conceal from Your Generosity that for this past year I have written no letters. The cause was not only the intolerable mass of my domestic cares in this my solitude, but also a rumour, spread about last Carnival here and at Hamburg, of Your Generosity's departure from this vale of tears — at the hearing of which I was so terrified that I thought it was now all over with the Astrological study, on which I have hitherto most earnestly bent myself, for want of the Tables published by others. But at last, some days ago, from a joyful letter of Lord David Fabricius, I learned (praise God) that Your Generosity, with your whole renowned family, has reached Prague safe and sound, and that the most illustrious, most powerful, and most invincible Emperor, our most clement Lord, most clemently favours You; wherefore, moved both by his letters and by Your Generosity's, I wished to join these mine to Fabricius's.

Since, then, Your Generosity formerly indicated in your "Resolutions" [answers] kindly shared with me, on the question of the method of directing [primary directions]: that there is no more exact method of directing than that handed down by Regiomontanus — [counting] as many degrees crossing the Meridian as [cross] any other circle of position, but rather to be counted on the Meridian, since there they pass directly; and that in the most oblique Sphere the directing-method then holds at once, although the Regiomontanus distribution of houses cannot be administered there; and that for this reason Your Generosity rather approves Campanus's division, which regards the whole Heaven everywhere and is universal — to which, however, Your Generosity is wont to apply an equal partition through the Ecliptic (because of the planets' procession along it), in imitation of the ancient Astrologers, considering different things in this and that arrangement, as the consideration is required either by reason of the whole Heaven or of the Zodiac alone: these things, I say, though indicated clearly enough, I would nonetheless reverently and earnestly ask Your Generosity that you deign to share with me one or two brief schemes of the operation and demonstration.

I should besides have some other things, not to be despised — namely whether direction by the degrees of the Zodiac and its Pole can also induce effects, like that which is done with respect to the Pole of the Equator; and whether then the measurement of years is to be instituted in the same way, by the motion of the Sun [☉] in the Radix of the nativity, as otherwise — or whether a different measurement is to be ordered here.

Then, since in directing the Ascendant, by the unanimous consent of all Astrologers, the oblique ascensions under the Pole-elevation of the Region in which one was born have hitherto been observed: whether Your Generosity also judges that, if someone tarries for many years in some far region much distant from the place of his nativity, the direction of the Ascendant should nonetheless be made from the oblique ascensions which the Table of Directions shows under the Pole-elevation of the birth-region — or rather from the oblique ascensions under the Pole-elevation of the place where he lives? For example: one is born under a Pole-elevation of 51 degrees; afterward, having left his fatherland, he has dwelt, and still dwells, for thirty whole years and more in another region situated under a Pole-elevation of 54 degrees — is his Ascendant-direction to be instituted from the Table of 51, or of 54 degrees of elevation? And what is the reason why the place of nativity, as to the effect of direction, affects the man and the body of the native more than the place of habitation in which he passes his life? — since yet, almost every year, in the effects of Eclipses, the effect follows sooner or later according to the diversity of meridians and places.

Third: since from the Diary of Elias Olai the Cimbrian I understood the Octogenary [eighty-fold] division of charts — by the conjunction [☌], opposition [☍], and square [□] of the Sun [☉] and Moon [☾] — to be approved by him as supported by experience and reasons, I should much like to learn briefly those very reasons of the Octogenary division, and by what experiment the thing was found.

Fourth: since experience teaches that not only the Directions of the malefics induce bad effects, but also the transits of Saturn [♄] and Mars [♂] — whether by body or by unhappy rays — through the chief places of the nativity, especially the Ascendant, sometimes work ill, sometimes not: it is asked whether, from Astrological causes, any certainty can be had by which it may be foreknown when those transits of Saturn and Mars will be harmful, or not; and whether there is any other cause than the agreement of the Direction and the progression, that those transits sometimes operate upon the Ascendant, sometimes not.

Fifth: whether the effects of the malefic planets, induced by direction, can be wholly removed or hindered by the benefic planets — and by what means above all the operations of the malefics are hindered.

Sixth: which aspect or Direction of the malefics has been observed most pernicious for natural death; and which constitution of the malefics, or Direction, has been observed by experience worst of all for a violent death.

Seventh: it is seen also that many having unlucky and violent nativities nevertheless lead a happy life and die a natural death, and contrariwise that very many having the best genesis — or at least a slight signification of violent death — perish violently. It is asked whence this diversity of operations or effects arises, astrologically speaking.

The Resolutions of these questions, then, unless I were quite troublesome to Your Generosity, I would humbly ask to be shared with me sometime. And since in Astrological matters I can do nothing skilfully without the restitution of the motion of the Moon [☾], I would likewise greatly ask that Your Generosity signify to me whether the Restitution of the same has been made public, that I might procure it. And since Your Generosity desired that certain reliable Observations in genitures be shared, I would add these as proven and well-ascertained, and will collect more day by day. In Meteorological [matters] I have nothing but the common sort.

Lastly, again and again reverently and earnestly entreating Your Generosity not to cease pursuing me, undeserving, with favour and benevolence out of [your] inborn heroic affection, and kindly to pardon me that long silence.

I pray from my heart that God, Best and Greatest, may keep, guard, and defend Your Generosity, with your whole renowned family, safe and sound.

Given at Bremen, 16 December, the year [15]99.
Your Generosity's most devoted,
Ludolf Riddershus[en].

[Here follows, appended in the same letter, Riddershusen's dossier of dated Bremen births and deaths, offered as astrological test-cases:]

In the year 1512, on the 22nd of June, between the 17th and 18th hour P.M., was born at Bremen Consul Lord Daniel von Büren — who in the year [15]62 defended the party of Doctor Albert Hardenberg, or of Calvin, against the rest of the consuls, and thereby stirred up the greatest disturbances for the whole City. He was elected in the year [15]39 into the Senatorial order, and afterward in the year [15]47 [elected] Consul; but at the beginning of the year [15]92, resigning the office of the Consulate on account of decrepit age, in the year [15]93, about the 28th of June, seized with sickness, on the 10th of July, after the fifth hour of the morning, he fell peacefully asleep.

In the year 1565, on the 24th of November, between the 10th and 11th hour in the afternoon, was born to the aforesaid Lord Consul a daughter, Margaretha von Büren; but in the year [15]94, on Thursday [♃], the 16th of May, seized by a raging pleurisy, she died on the 23rd of May.

In the year 1539, on the 22nd of July, about the fifth hour of the morning, was born at Bremen Lord Theodoricus von Reden, who in the year [15]76, in autumn, was elected into the Senatorial order; he was a man of great stature and corpulent, and had two wives, with whom he received very great inheritances. This man, in the year [15]97, on the first of June, in the morning, seized with sickness, and taking pills from his own kinsman, Doctor Johann Baptista Servetius, physician, straightway in the Doctor's arms exchanged life for death.

In the year 1551, on the 10th of November, at the 17th hour after noon, was born at Bremen Wichmann Middelstorp. In the year [15]75, on the 16th of February, he collapsed dangerously to the ground, and was compelled to employ the executioner, by name Dionysius, to treat him; in the year [15]86, about the feast of Easter, in the house of Consul Luder von Belmers — [the latter] having piously died — he fell from a height into a ship, and had the ship not been there, he must needs have perished in the Weser. At length, at the beginning of the year [15]92, seized with various kinds of sicknesses, and on the 11th of June again dangerously laid low upon the ground, and so detained by sickness until the year [15]93, on the 8th of October, in the night following, he fell peacefully asleep, though many remedies had been applied in vain.

In the year 1558, on the 25th of August, after the twelfth hour of noon, was born at Frankfurt an der Oder Meimarus von Borken, Secretary of this City, since his parent, Doctor Johann von Borken, was of the patrician stock of our City. This man, in the year [15]83, on Wednesday [☿] in the evening after the feast of the Annunciation of Mary, at Bederkesa, the castle of this City, broke his leg. In the year [15]87, after the feast of Bartholomew the Apostle, he lay sick unto death of a hot [feverish] disease. In the year [15]91, on the 4th of August, from an excessive over-indulgence in wine the day before, spitting blood from his mouth, he died in a frenzy.

In the year 1566, on the 21st of July, at the 7th hour P.M., was born at Bremen Hildemann Deddeken, who in the year [15]90, after the feast of Pentecost, was shot through with a [musket-]ball by pirates at sea.

In the year 1552, on the 7th of February, about the sixth hour in the afternoon, was born at Bremen Lord Gerhard Gerken, a distinguished Musician, no ordinary friend of mine, but an enemy of Astrology. In the year [15]81 he was taken on as Secretary of the City, and in the year [15]93, on the 3rd of July, elected Senator. In the year [15]91, on the Tuesday after Quasimodogeniti [the Sunday after Easter], and again on the fourth of August, he lay dangerously ill; afterward, in the year [15]94, on the 28th of November, again seized [with illness] for some months, and restored to health beyond hope. At length, in the year [15]96, after the feast of Pentecost, seized anew and detained by a long sickness, on the day of St Michael the Archangel, between the 10th and 11th hour before noon, in my presence, he peacefully passed away, though aids had been applied in vain by two Doctors of Medicine of the City. It is a wonder that he did not attain to a decrepit age, since he had the Moon [☾] in conjunction with Jupiter [♃] in its own house, in the eleventh [house], free from the malefics, and the Ascendant unafflicted.

This good man's wife, Brigitta Gröninges, was born at Bremen in the year 1559, on the 11th of May, Thursday [♃] before the feast of Pentecost, between the 7th and 8th hour in the morning; but in the year [15]98, the plague raging, fleeing betimes from the City into a garden, on the 6th of August, in the garden, she was seized by the plague; on the 12th of the same [month], between the 1st and 2nd hour of the night following, she fell peacefully asleep — nor were remedies lacking to her. And this honest matron had the Moon in Cancer [♋] ascending, in Trine to Jupiter [♃], [Jupiter] being in Pisces [♓].

In the year 1555, on the 24th of March, between the 2nd and 3rd hour in the afternoon, was born at Bremen Tibbeke, wife of Alexander Bikkers, who in the year [15]96, on the 26th of February, seized by a raging pleurisy, lost a dear daughter, and was herself restored to health. But afterward, in the same year, on the 4th of September, from grief for her dead daughter, she was reduced to insanity, and most wretchedly detained [in that state] until the feast of Easter of the year [15]97, at which she returned to health. But truly in the year [15]98, about the 10th of September, seized again by the plague, on the 15th of the same [month], at the 2nd hour of the night following, she died.

In the year 1566, on Monday [☾], the 24th of June, between the 1st and 2nd hour of the night, P.M., was born to Lord Johann Schulten, Senator of this City, a son, Fransz. This little son, in the year [15]71, on Wednesday [☿] the 20th of June, at the fifth hour in the afternoon, was pushed by a certain woman, passing by him, from the high eastern bridge into the City moat, but was saved by his Father beyond all hope — who himself very nearly perished along with him. His father having afterward died, in the year [15]86, in autumn, at Amsterdam, on account of squandering his goods with harlots, and in the year [15]90, on the first of November, by the Magistrate — his Mother herself requesting it — for the same cause he was, a second time, cast into prison. The year having elapsed, he set out for Italy; whether he is still living or not is uncertain.

[Postscript:] I, L. R., was born at Bremen in the year 1551, on the 15th of September, after the twelfth hour of the night following; in my boyhood endless misfortunes befell me. In the year [15]73, on the 14th of March in the night following, at Brunswick I was seized with a most burning fever, and in the year [15]78, on the 3rd of November, again with a singular kind of diseases and with quinsy. In the year [15]73, on the 13th of May, I took a wife. In the year 1578, on 16 February, between the 6th and 7th hour of the evening, my son Gregorius was born to me, and he, from his earliest age until the year [15]95, was afflicted with various kinds of diseases. In the year 1579, on the 26th of December, between the 3rd and 4th hour of the night following, my second son Hieronimus was born to me, who enjoys (praise God) better and firmer health. In the year [15]82, on the 15th of July, a little after the 12th hour of the night following, my third son Christianus was born to me. He, in the year [15]83 about the feast of [St] James, and in the year [15]84, on 3 April, lay sick unto death, but afterwards likewise enjoys good health. And since I do not doubt that Your Generosity has the places of the planets restored for my own nativity and my sons', that I might therefore the more make a test of the art's certainty, I again and again reverently and earnestly ask that Your Generosity deign kindly to share with me the Restitutions of the same. And although I do not see that I can repay Your Generosity anything for these and all the other benefits conferred on me, undeserving, nevertheless those who are dear to Your Generosity will find me bound and most ready for every service. The notations of the days are taken from the Julian Calendar.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Ludolf Riddershusen (Riddershusius) — a Bremen astrological student/practitioner who treats Tycho as his "Preceptor" — to Tycho at Prague.
  • Significance — Tycho as astrological authority, and a death-rumour.
  • The "Tycho is dead" rumour. A notable biographical datum: a rumour of Tycho's death circulated at Bremen and Hamburg around Carnival 1599 ("his departure from this vale of tears"), terrifying Riddershusen — corrected only "some days ago" by David Fabricius's letter reporting Tycho safe at Prague and favoured by the Emperor. (Fabricius is the link — cf. his recruitment in brahe-t_15990922001.)
  • A genuine astrological consultation (seven questions). The body is a window on Tycho's reputation as an astrological authority and on late-Renaissance technical astrology. Riddershusen first recapitulates Tycho's earlier "Resolutions" on primary directions — endorsing the Regiomontanus method of directing but preferring the Campanus house-division (as "universal"), with an equal ecliptic partition "in imitation of the ancient astrologers" — then poses seven questions: (1) a worked scheme of the directing operation; (2) zodiacal-pole vs equatorial-pole direction and the time-measure; (3) the relocation problem — whether to direct the Ascendant from the birthplace latitude (51°) or the place of long residence (54°), and why birthplace dominates; (4) the predictability of harmful Saturn/Mars transits; (5) whether benefics can cancel malefic directions; (6) the worst configurations for natural vs violent death; (7) why charts and outcomes so often diverge.
  • The "Octogenary division" and Elias Olai. Riddershusen cites the "Diary of Elias Olai the Cimbrian" (Elias Olsen Morsing, Tycho's former Hven assistant and diarist) for an "Octogenary [80-fold] division" of charts by Sun–Moon conjunction/opposition/square — and asks its rationale. (A trace of Tycho's Hven astrological/meteorological diary tradition.)
  • The lunar tables. Riddershusen says he "can do nothing in astrology without the restitution of the Moon's motion" and asks whether Tycho's lunar Restitution is yet published — echoing the same demand from Magini (brahe-t_16010220001) and the lunar work referenced to Scaliger (brahe-t_16000723001).
  • Cross-references. The Sun/Moon/Saturn/Mars and aspect glyphs (☉ ☾ ♄ ♂ ☌ ☍ □) are reproduced as transcribed. Notes the death-rumour and the Elias Olsen Morsing octogenary-division datum.
  • Persons & places named. Ludolf Riddershusen; David Fabricius (the bearer of the good news); Regiomontanus, Campanus (house systems); Elias Olai / Elias Olsen Morsing (the "Cimbrian," Tycho's diarist); Rudolf II. Places: Bremen, Hamburg, Prague, Knudstrup.
  • Calendar. Bremae 16 Xbris [Decembris] Anno 99 → 16 December 1599 [Old Style] (Bremen reckoning; no stylo marker; label gives no N.S.). Header read Old Style.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing genealogical postscript (Riddershusen's own + his three sons' nativities, with a request to compute their restitutions; "ex Calendario Iuliano"), restored from Dreyer VIII p.221 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • What was recovered. The lost interior passage is Riddershusen's own appended dossier of eleven dated Bremen birth-and-death notices (consuls, a secretary, a musician-senator, and their wives/children), several carrying explicit astrological configurations (Moon–Jupiter aspects, Ascendant condition). It sits between the signed close of the main letter ("Ludolphus Riddershusius") and the already-published autobiographical postscript beginning "Ego L. R. Anno 1551..." — i.e. it is Riddershusen practising exactly what he asks Tycho for earlier in the same letter: "certain reliable Observations in genitures," supplied as raw test-data for the astrological questions posed above.
  • Elias Olai Cimber, identified. The "Diario Eliæ Olai Cimbri" cited for the "Octogenary division" (question three, already-published portion) is almost certainly Elias Olsen Morsing's Diarium astrologicum et meteorologicum anni 1586 — the first book printed at Tycho's own Uraniborg press, by Tycho's own assistant ("Elias Olai Cimber, discipulus Tychonis," per the Opera Omnia index of correspondents). Riddershusen is thus citing back to Tycho a work by a member of Tycho's own household.
  • Two fabricated dates corrected. The previously published partial translation supplied "the 15th of September" (for Riddershusen's own 1551 birth) and "15 July" (for his son Christianus's 1582 birth). The complete source text given for this retranslation has no day-number in either place ("Anno 1551 die Septembris"; "Anno 82 Iulij"). Both are corrected here to reflect the actual source, with the gap marked; the origin of the earlier "15"s is unexplained and should be checked against the manuscript or a critical edition before being reused.
  • Bremen persons. Daniel von Büren, Theodoricus von Reden, Wichmann Middelstorp, Meimarus von Borken, Hildemann Deddeken, Gerhard Gerken and their households are purely local Bremen civic figures (consuls, senators, a City secretary) not otherwise identified in the works consulted (Christianson, Dreyer, Thoren); confirming them would require Bremen municipal/archival sources outside our current holdings.
  • Crux: "in lla" in the Gerken entry, read here as "in the eleventh [house]" (i.e. undecima domo), but the abbreviation is not certain.
  • Crux: "Mirum, quidni ad decrepitam pervenerit ætatem" (Gerken entry) is rhetorically ambiguous; rendered as ironic — Gerken in fact died at about forty-four despite the favourable Moon-Jupiter configuration Riddershusen describes, which is presumably the point being made.
  • Correction. Collated against Dreyer, TBOO VIII pp.215-222. Restored two dropped day-numbers silently missing from the source text: 'die 15 Septembris' (Riddershusen's own birth, p.221) and 'Anno 82 15 Iulij' (birth of his son Christianus, p.221), both translated into the English, removing the 'day not given in the source' hedges. Also corrected three cosmetic corruptions against Dreyer's text with no effect on the English: the German address heading 'HERNNTICHONI' to 'HERRN TICHONI' (p.216), 'locum' to the plural 'loca' (p.221), and 'comæ pensare' to the single word 'compensare' (p.221).

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VIII pp. 215–221; defects the audit noted: text error (5) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.215-222 read as page images in three passes (1-4, 5-8, and a close re-read of p.218 and p.221) and collated the full letter plus its appended Bremen-births/deaths dossier line by line against the transcribed original and the published English. Every name, date, and number in the biographical dossier (Daniel von Büren, Margaretha von Büren, Theodoricus von Reden, Wichmann Middelstorp, Meimarus von Borken, Hildemann Deddeken, Gerhard Gerken, Brigitta Gröninges, Tibbeke/Alexander Bikkers, Johann Schulten/Fransz) checked out exactly against Dreyer, and the astrological-questions section (Regiomontanus/Campanus, the 51/54-degree example, the Octogenary conjunction/opposition/square of Sun and Moon, Saturn/Mars transits) also checked out — the translation of the substantive content is faithful, with no fabrication, omission, or mistranslation found. However, our transcribed original itself departs from Dreyer in five places, all in the autobiographical closing section on p.221: two dropped day-numbers ('15' before Septembris and before Iulij) that silently remove dates Dreyer actually gives (the translator honestly flagged these as 'day not given in the source' rather than inventing them, but the information loss is real and traces to our transcribed original, not the translator), plus three minor corruptions (a glued/transposed address heading, 'locum' for Dreyer's 'loca', and 'comæ pensare' for Dreyer's 'compensare'). Per the grading rule, any text error caps the grade at C regardless of the translation's own fidelity.

5 defects found:
- text error (low). The transcribed original glues and transposes the two words 'HERRN TICHONI' into 'HERNNTICHONI'; Dreyer prints them as two clean words. Cosmetic — English correctly renders 'Lord Tycho Brahe' regardless. Dreyer prints: HERRN TICHONI BRAHE (p.216, address heading).
- text error (medium). Dreyer gives the day as '15 Septembris'; our transcribed original has silently dropped the '15'. The English translator noticed the gap and flagged it honestly ('day not given in the source') rather than fabricating, but the published sentence still omits a date Dreyer actually prints. Dreyer prints: Ego L. R. Anno 1551 die 15 Septembris post horam 12 noctis sequentis Bremæ sum natus (p.221, line 1).
- text error (medium). Same fault pattern as the previous entry: Dreyer reads 'Anno 82 15 Iulij' (15 July); our transcribed original drops the '15'. Again honestly flagged in translation rather than fabricated, but the date is genuinely recoverable from Dreyer and is missing from our text. Dreyer prints: Anno 82 15 Iulij paulo post horam 12 noctis sequentis natus est mihi filius tertius CHRISTIANUS (p.221, line 10).
- text error (low). Dreyer has plural 'loca' (agreeing with the participle 'restituta'); our transcribed original has singular 'locum', a minor grammatical corruption. Does not affect the English rendering, which is correct either way. Dreyer prints: quin Generositas Vestra loca planetarum ad natalem meum et filiorum meorum restituta habeat (p.221, line 14-15).
- text error (low). Dreyer's single word 'compensare' (to repay) is split/garbled into 'comæ pensare' in our transcribed original. The translator correctly inferred the intended sense ('repay') despite the corruption, so the English is unaffected, but the transcribed original itself departs from Dreyer. Dreyer prints: quicquam compensare non videam (p.221, line 18).

Repaired. All five defects were verified directly against page images of Dreyer, TBOO VIII pp.215-222 (the full extract for this letter). Both date defects and the 'loca'/'compensare' corruptions appear on p.221 exactly as the audit's dreyer field states; the address-heading defect is confirmed on p.216. No defect was rejected — the audit's claims all match the printed edition. Nothing outside these five defect sites was touched.
