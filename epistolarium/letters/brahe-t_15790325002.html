The original

HIERONYMUS WOLFIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

EADEM Tabellarij fortuna, TYCHO nobilissime, amice colende, hîc sumus usi, qua tua præstantia. Nam Aurifaber ille, qui tuas reddiderat, hinc abijt. Quis literulas sit perlaturus, nescio, sed sub vesperam demum mihi nuntiatum est, ut rescriberem. Itaque cogor esse brevior et ob temporis brevitatem et propter diem festum. Absentibus WILLERI ministris non possum parare libellos nostrorum, qui de atrocissimo illo Cometa, quem unquam vidi, scripserunt. Et sane puto nec Tabellarium grandiusculos libellos perlaturum et quæ tu elaborâris, propediem nos accepturos. Quæ autem de amplitudine vestræ regionis et fortitudine gentis scribis, satis nota nobis sunt, et Cimbros, Ambrones et Teutones a Martha C. MARIJ maga potius, quam a Romanis esse victos arbitramur. Linguam vero vestram ijs ornavit et antiquitatis et proprietatis et copiæ laudibus IO. GOROPIUS, ut eam vel Hebrææ anteponat, nedum Latinæ et Græcæ. Itaque si hîc esses, Cimbrice a te disceremus. Sed fortasse nobis ætas obstaret, quo minus essemus dociles. Nam mortuo adhibere medicinam, et docere senem idem esse Græco proverbio dicitur. Me quidem senectus et pigrum et obliviosum facit, et multipliciter tam corpus quam animum affligit. Quomodo autem me contra eam, tanquam morbum incurabilem muniam, non video, præsertim deficiente hîc vino bono et incorrupto, quo solo tanquam antidoto moderate sumto uti soleo contra tam invictum hostem, uti scis meum morem. Quare sive me hîc enneaticus climacter, ὁ ἀνδροδάμας, sustulerit, sive 66to tollendum reliquerit: susque deque fero, modo CHRISTUS Servator in Palatio sui patris parvulam aliquam cellulam assignet, in qua beate et æternum requiescam, quod ita fore clementia divina fretus confido.

Respublica nostra in eo statu adhuc est, quem hinc discedens reliquisti. HAINZELIUS filius nuper a me uxoris suæ Genesin confici voluit, cum ego respondi, D. TYCHONEM id in melius præstare posse, si vellet. Sed sane dubito, an velles, cum hîc in talibus difficilimus fueris. Isti homines affectibus suis indulgentes, succensent Astrologis et vera et falsa dicentibus. Itaque Tiresias soli Apollini vaticinandum esse dixit apud SOPHOCLEM, qui hominis indignationem, quippe Deus negligat. Eclipsin Solis in ♓ hîc vidimus pene integram, in qua et CYPRIANUS, sed minus tamen quam COPERNICUS aberravit. Cuius Ephemeridas MICHÆL MÆSTLINUS instrumentis cœlo uti opinor adhibitis ab anno 1577 usque ad annum 1588 correctiores se esse editurum mihi pollicetur, nisi Typographus in mora sibi sit. Sed iam manum de tabula. Nam advesperascit et rhabarbarum scriptionem meam interpellat, quo uti sæpius contra Ischiadem et Chiragram cogor. Tuam vero Excellentiam et valere et florere omni bonorum genere ex animo et spero et opto.

Vale.
25. Martij Anno 1579.

Hieronymus Wolf to Tycho Brahe.

The same fortune with the courier, most noble Tycho, friend to be honoured, have we had here as did your eminence [in writing to me]. For that goldsmith who had delivered your [letters] has gone away from here. Who is to carry these few lines I do not know, but it was only towards evening that word was brought to me that I should write back. And so I am forced to be rather brief, both because of the shortness of time and on account of the feast-day. The servants of Willer being absent, I cannot procure the little books of our people who have written about that most dreadful comet, the [worst] I ever saw. And indeed I think both that the courier will not carry the bulkier little books, and that we shall shortly receive what you have worked out. As for what you write about the extent of your country and the valour of its people, these are well enough known to us; and we hold that the Cimbri, Ambrones, and Teutones were vanquished rather by the sorceress Martha of C[aius] Marius than by the Romans. Your tongue, indeed, Jo. Goropius has adorned with such praises of antiquity, of distinctive character, and of richness, that he sets it even before Hebrew, let alone before Latin and Greek. And so, if you were here, we should learn Cimbric from you. But perhaps our age would stand in the way, so that we should be the less teachable. For to apply medicine to a dead man, and to teach an old man, are said by a Greek proverb to be one and the same thing. Old age, for my part, makes me both sluggish and forgetful, and afflicts in many ways my body as much as my mind. But how I am to fortify myself against it, as against an incurable disease, I do not see — especially when there is lacking here good and unspoiled wine, which alone, as a kind of antidote taken in moderation, I am wont to use against so unconquered an enemy, as you know my custom to be. Wherefore, whether the ninefold climacteric, ὁ ἀνδροδάμας [the man-tamer], shall here have carried me off, or shall have left me to be carried off in my sixty-sixth [year]: I make light of it, provided that Christ the Saviour assign me some little cell in his Father's palace, in which I may rest blessedly and for ever — which, relying on the divine clemency, I am confident will be so.

Our commonwealth is still in the state in which you left it on departing hence. Hainzel the son lately wished his wife's geniture to be drawn up by me, whereupon I replied that Master Tycho could do it better, if he were willing. But I much doubt whether you would be willing, since you were here most difficult in such matters. Those men, indulging their own passions, are angry at astrologers whether they speak the truth or falsehoods. And so Tiresias in Sophocles said that Apollo alone ought to prophesy — he who [being a god] disregards a man's indignation, since he is a god. We saw here the eclipse of the Sun in ♓ Pisces almost total, in which both Cyprian [Leovitius] went astray, yet less so than Copernicus. Michael Maestlin promises me that he will publish ephemerides of the latter, corrected — by using instruments applied to the sky, I suppose — from the year 1577 to the year 1588, unless the printer prove a hindrance to him. But now [I take] my hand from the tablet. For evening draws on, and the rhubarb interrupts my writing, which I am rather often compelled to use against sciatica and gout of the hand. But that Your Excellency may both fare well and flourish in every kind of good things, I both hope and wish from my heart.

Farewell.
25 March, the year 1579.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Hieronymus Wolfius = Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and Greek scholar ("father of Byzantine studies"), librarian of the Fugger collection and rector of the Augsburg gymnasium; a mentor-friend of Tycho's from his Augsburg years. This letter, written within a year of Wolf's death, continues the correspondence whose 1571 instalment (brahe-t_15711113001) shares the same Vienna manuscript volume. The valediction (Vale) and the brevity reflect the haste he describes.
  • Heading / register. HIERONYMUS WOLFIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE is the editor's letter-rubric, not a salutation formula; rendered literally. amice colende = "friend to be honoured," the courtesy register kept. Excellentia Tua = "Your Excellency."
  • Tabellarius — "courier/letter-carrier"; the practical difficulty of finding a bearer frames the whole letter. Aurifaber ille — "that goldsmith," the previous (unnamed) carrier of Tycho's letters.
  • WILLERI ministris — "the servants of Willer": Georg Willer, the prominent Augsburg bookseller-publisher (issuer of the Frankfurt/Augsburg fair catalogues); Wolf cannot obtain the printed pamphlets on the comet because Willer's staff are away.
  • atrocissimo illo Cometa — "that most dreadful comet": the great comet of 1577, the object of Tycho's parallax work and of the contemporary German pamphlet literature Wolf wishes to send. quem unquam vidi is elliptical ("[the worst] I ever saw"); the implied superlative is supplied in [brackets].
  • The Cimbri allusion. Wolf answers Tycho's evident pride in his homeland (amplitudine vestrae regionis et fortitudine gentis, "the extent of your country and the valour of its people"). Cimbros, Ambrones et Teutones — the northern tribes annihilated by Gaius Marius (C. Marii) at Aquae Sextiae and Vercellae (102–101 BC). a Martha … maga — "by the sorceress Martha," the Syrian prophetess Marius reputedly kept in his camp (Plutarch, Marius 17); Wolf wittily credits her divination rather than Roman arms. The Cimbri were popularly identified with the Danish Cimbric peninsula (Jutland), hence the local-patriotic point.
  • IO. GOROPIUS — Johannes Goropius Becanus (Jan van Gorp, 1519–1573), the Antwerp physician-etymologist whose Origines Antwerpianae (1569) argued that the Cimbric / "Brabantic" tongue was the oldest language of mankind, older even than Hebrew — the doctrine Wolf alludes to (vel Hebraeae anteponat) with evident irony. Cimbrice … disceremus — "we should learn Cimbric [from you]," i.e. Danish.
  • The Greek proverb. mortuo adhibere medicinam, et docere senem — "to physic a dead man and to teach an old man [are one and the same]," rendering a Greek commonplace (cf. the proverb γέροντα μὴ παίδευε, "do not instruct an old man"); Wolf paraphrases it in Latin and ascribes it to "a Greek proverb."
  • Wine as remedy. vino bono … tanquam antidoto … contra tam invictum hostem — wine as his "antidote" against the "unconquered enemy" of old age; the self-deprecating tone (drinking moderate, "in moderation") is preserved, not sharpened.
  • The climacteric / Greek. enneaticus climacter = "the ninefold (ennead) climacteric," the astrologically critical year reckoned by nines (here Wolf, aged about 62–63, faces his 63rd — the grand climacteric, 7 × 9); ὁ ἀνδροδάμας = "the man-tamer / man-subduer," a stock epithet of the dangerous climacteric year (lit. andro- "man" + damáō "to tame, subdue"). The Greek is transcribed verbatim and glossed in [brackets]; climacter kept by this chapter's convention (annus climactericus → climacteric year). sive 66to tollendum reliquerit — "or shall have left [me] to be carried off in my sixty-sixth [year]"; 66to = sexagesimo sexto (ordinal abbreviation), expanded in the sense.
  • in Palatio sui patris parvulam aliquam cellulam — "some little cell in his Father's palace," echoing John 14:2 (in domo Patris mei mansiones multae sunt); the consolatory religious turn is kept.
  • HAINZELIUS filius — "Hainzel the son": one of the Hainzel brothers of Augsburg (Johann Baptist and Paul Hainzel, the patrician patrons of astronomy with whom Tycho built his 1569 quadrant); here a younger Hainzel asks for his wife's nativity. Genesin … in melius praestare = "to draw up the geniture better" (Genesis → geniture,). difficilimus (for difficillimus) — "most difficult/reluctant," Wolf recalling Tycho's reluctance over such commissions.
  • The Sophocles allusion. Tiresias … apud SOPHOCLEM — Wolf cites Sophocles (the seer Tiresias, Oedipus Tyrannus / Antigone) for the maxim that prophecy belongs to Apollo alone, "who, being a god, disregards a man's indignation." The syntax is compressed: qui hominis indignationem, quippe Deus negligat is read "[Apollo,] who being a god disregards a man's indignation"; the somewhat elliptical clause is rendered with that supplement and flagged here rather than smoothed.
  • Astronomy. Eclipsin Solis in ♓ … pene integram = "the solar eclipse in Pisces almost total" — the partial solar eclipse observed at Augsburg; the glyph ♓ is kept in the source and named Pisces in the translation. eclipsis (Solis) → eclipse (solar). in qua et CYPRIANUS, sed minus tamen quam COPERNICUS aberravit — "in [predicting] which both Cyprian [Leovitius] (Cyprianus Leovitius, 1524–1574, the astrologer whose tables Wolf used) erred, yet less than Copernicus" — i.e. the eclipse showed the received computations astray.
  • MICHAeL MAeSTLINUS — Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), the Tübingen astronomer (later Kepler's teacher), whose published Ephemerides affirmed the superiority of the Copernican hypotheses, citing the 1577 comet as evidence (so Gingerich, Annotated Census). Ephemeridas … ab anno 1577 usque ad annum 1588 correctiores = "corrected ephemerides from 1577 to 1588." instrumentis coelo … adhibitis — "by instruments applied to the sky," i.e. derived from observation; opinor ("I suppose") is Wolf's own hedge and is preserved. Typographus = the printer.
  • manum de tabula — "[I take] my hand from the tablet/board," a proverbial "I'll stop here" (cf. Pliny / Cicero); the elliptical idiom kept, [I take] bracketed as supplied.
  • Medicine. rhabarbarum = rhubarb (the purgative root); Ischias / Ischiadem = sciatica; Chiragra = gout of the hand (chiragra) — the same complaint as in the 1571 letter; both period terms glossed.
  • Dateline / calendar. 25. Martij Anno 1579 = "25 March 1579." No place is named in the dateline; the witness and content place it at Augsburg (Augusta Vindelicorum), and the catalogue label confirms it. The label "1579. 25. marts. (Augsburg)" agrees with the dateline. Style: O.S. (Julian); the Gregorian reform was still three years off, and Lutheran Augsburg in any case retained the Julian calendar until 1700. No in-letter date discrepancy. propter diem festum — "on account of the feast-day": 25 March is the Feast of the Annunciation (Lady Day), the festival to which Wolf's apology for brevity refers.
