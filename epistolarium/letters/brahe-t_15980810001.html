The original

TAM ANTIQUAe PROSAPIAe QUAM PROPRIA VIRTUTE INPRIMIS NOBILI ET ERUDITIONE SIMUL SOLIDA CLARO VIRO DOMINO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGVINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO INTEGERRIMO.

S. P.

QUAS ante discessum tuum ex ultimis Comitijs Hafniensibus deproperasti, desideratissime mi OLIGERE, Consangvinee, Affinis, et (quod plus reputo) Amice integerrime, his ipsis diebus ob moras, quas isthic nectebat MULIUS, pridie accepi. Erantque utut festinanter scriptae, et ad singula, quae expectabam, propter otij penuriam non respondentes, admodum gratae. Quod literae Electoris et Electorissae Brandenburgensis Regi et Reginae oblatae non sint, parum moror. Neque enim ob [id] eas misi, quod magnopere cuperem, ut exhiberentur. Ideoque (uti scis) liberum id tibi reliqui. Scio eas scatere [politic[is]mis et aulicismis], uti fit apud tales homines. Allene det var anderledes lovet; naar jeg seer til, da colluderer de begge [Canceler] med hverandre, for den eene ravn piller ikke øyet ud paa den anden: quamvis alterum magis integrum et sincerum duxerim, quam vestrum istum NICOLAI MACHIAVELLI Neophitum, qui adhuc quotidie discit a suo [praeceptore contiguo]. Nec tamen id quod tu insinuas, inde inferri posse, si traditae fuissent literae, existimarim. Nam conditio mea nullatenus isthic defletur. Et cur hoc, cum ego ipse hanc praetulerim et etiamnum praeferam priori? Neque commutarem, om man endskiønt vilde give mig Penge til, som naar man bytter. Nec ullis modis reditum captavi per literas illas, utut tam vestratibus quam mihi invisum, plus tamen forte mihi quam illis. Ideoque sollicite excepi apud Principem hunc urgentem, ne tale quid verbo scriptove moveret. Neque eae, quas Bramstedij illi obtuli, quarum exemplar (ni fallor) habes, tale quid prae se ferunt. Recompensationem solummodo expensarum moderate requirebam, suo forte tempore validius. Videbo, quomodo literae illae suis restituantur autoribus, modo id citra offensam per aliqualem excusationem fieri queat, vel (si mavis) praetextum. Quod autem Regi libros destinatos non tradideris, nec tuum nec aliorum probo consilium. Maluissem enim certis de causis mature exhibitos, quam retentos. Neque tamen suspicor, te tenacitate quadam, et amore mei atque librorum eos pro te ipso servare voluisse, ne forte aemuli nostri et malevoli ad se [dono Regis] reciperent. An [Regi] placituri sint nec ne, susque deque fero. Nec sane ob id illi communicandos censeo, quod sic ipsius gratiam aucupari cupiam; cum sat sciam, ne quidem gratiori munere aliquo aut quovis officio ob eos, quibus magis auscultat, id impetrari posse (quod et cordate fero) vel literae meae Rostochio scriptae cum suo responso (necdum uti pollicebaris, critizatae) satis superque testatum relinquunt. Nec si[c] intempestive [supplex] fio, cum nihil petam, nihil deprecer, nihil supplicem, sed potius labores meos quos [ille] cum suis aspernatus est, me Caesari nunc dedicare, aliumque atque adeo summum Imperij caput pro studiorum meorum Maecenate quaerere, posthabitis invidentibus et detrectantibus [Danis] ostendo ostentoque, ut hoc potius (si quid foret insimulandum) culpae imputari posset, quam quod nimis me demitterem. Tu itaque, mi OLIGERE, omnino libros [Regi] trade in manus, sive [volenti] sive [repugnanti], aut si id tibi commodum non fuerit, fac ut per aliquem alium meorum, aut etiam [RAMELIUM] id fiat; ita tamen, ut per quoscunque sive temet ipsum vel alios Regi porrigantur, tibi, siquidem Rex (uti scio) talibus [imo nullis (quod dolendum)] libris delectatur, cedant. Invenies facile rationem. Quod et antea, cum mecum hîc esses, praemonui. Sin [Rex] ipsemet (quod [miraculi] loco duxerim) eos retinere voluerit, aut etiam praeter omnem nostram praevisionem et amolitionem in aliorum, quorum quam minime vellemus, potestatem devenerint, id ferendum erit. Habeant, quod tacite suspiciant atque admirentur, quodque capere aut imitari non possint, palam vero contra conscientiam (uti solent) rodant, extenuent, invertant et cavillentur. Id populus curat scilicet. Faxo autem, si res ita ceciderit, ut tu nova, etiam picta et ornata obtineas exemplaria, quibus hanc iacturam leves, praefixis etiam carminibus, sed in te directis, qualia nuper, dum Duci IOHANNI ADOLPHO hos libros dono mitterem, praeposueram, quorum tenorem in adiuncta scheda habes. Liber de Stellis erat in charta, non membrana scriptus. Et hunc Duci ULDARICO Megapolitano non concessi, cum alterum nuper antepositis etiam suis carminibus dono mitterem. Quanti utrunque fecerit, quam comiter et benigne TENGNAGLIUM meum exceperit, quam gratus erga me fuerit, atque in posterum esse velit, et quam clementi denique favore me prosequatur Sermus Elector Coloniensis, ex ijs, quae ad CRAGIUM nostrum scripsi, cognosces, et aliquatenus etiam ex adiunctis literarum exemplis, quae cum CRAGIO quoque communices, praesertim quae antea non habet, velim, ut et ille nostra tibi. Quae enim alteri vestrum scribo mittoque, utrique scribo et mitto pauculis differentibus. Exemplum scripti istius famosi apud CRAGIUM videbis, quale mihi Helmstadio missum est. Addidit quidam ex meis studiosis annotationes marginales, ubi praecipue fit mei honorifica mentio, ne diu tam memorabilia locum quaerenda forent. Egregius sane liber, qui a Professoribus Hafniensibus tanto applausu, licet tacito murmure inter eos susurrato excipiatur. Gaudent monstris monstrosa ingenia et prava pravis. Ostendet tibi dictus noster communis secreto literas quasdam a [IOANNE STUGGIO] IOANNI Omnivendae, vel potius Ardelioni ex composito scriptas adinstar Beinenkorf, ut ille et ij, quibus hoc scurrile scriptum magni et grati muneris instar congratulatus est, sibi sic assensum et laudem praeberi frustra laetentur; aut si fucum capiunt, sciant alios scire, quam parum veri bonique illi sciant vel sapiant, quamque insulsi invidi et malevoli sint censores, quodque nos eos clam se titillantes et conridentes palam pungamus et rideamus, imo ex alto despiciamus. Librum autem hunc, qui mea Elogia depraedicat et ebuccinat, mihi per hunc ipsum nuncium remitti volo. Nullum enim aliud habeo exemplar. Ubi plura nactus fuero, vobis et alijs communicabo. Eroque adhuc facilior, quam institor iste vester librorum. Nam gratis dabo et aperte faciam, quod is subdole et tecte. Ephemerides istae novae ex novis (si Dijs placet) Tabulis supputatae ne quidem apud Germanos fidem merentur, quam autor in Praefatione venditat, nedum apud [vos Danos], qui terrena potius quam Coelestia curatis. Heu tam aversus Equos vestris Sol iungit ab oris. In Septentrione enim semper habitavit barbaries. Et verendum, ut ne nunc quidem satis extirpata sit, aut postliminio redeat, contemptis ingenuis et humanioribus literis unâ cum virtutis integritate. Quod Cognatus meus [LINDENOVIUS], ea quae ipsemet non invite, antequam disiungeremur, promisit, nunc concedere tergiversetur, licet tunc aeque erat in Politicis prudens atque nunc, non miror. Cimber enim est, cur non Cimbrizet et nippizet? Quamvis (ut dicam quod res est) parum curem, sive communicet, sive non. Potioribus enim intentus sum, quam ut talibus Mundanae scenae frivolis nugis immorer. Volebam saltem memoriae et rei bene gestae causa hanc historiolam penes me habere, att man kunde laese den som et Eventyr om Afftenen. Scripsit is ad me, et quod in diversis locis haereret haec confabulatio, non mitti excusavit. Tum quoque de Comoedia, quae isthic a personato Anglo introducta est, quodque ferme in Tragoediam versa fuerit, indicavit. Kan skee den [gammele] pater conscriptus lader ikke af, furijs sic impellentibus, etiam in defunctum saevire, førend han staar op igien og giør Jertegn. Mones, et quidem recte, ut circumspecte in quibusdam agam. Quod et facio, faciamque. Imo plus taceo, dissimuloque in tantis iniuris, tamque falsis obiectionibus, quam forte opus esset. Sed serviendum est tempori et loco. Quod differtur, non aufertur. Sunt Regibus, fateor, longae manus. At Philosophis et veritatem tuentibus adhuc longiores et fortiores. Nam ipsorum Imperia et Iurisdictiones etiam ultra fata mortalium se extendunt. Caetera momentanea et caduca sunt. Tandem bona causa triumphat. Reges et Dominos habere debent, qui se non habent, et non concupiscunt, quae Reges Dominique concupiscunt. Illi semper metuant, ne fractus labatur axis, et pavidos feriant ruinae. Rex est, qui metuit nihil. Hoc Regnum sibi quisque dat. Sed de his satis.

Aedes meas Hafniacas pro duobus millibus non vendam. Constiterunt mihi, antequam ultimo ibi essem, et quaedam in ijs complevissem, ultra 3000 Ioachimorum. Tunc vero vix intra millenos in eas ulterius erogavi. Satis est constans structura, quae alijs in Civitate lapideis domibus duratione sua non cedet. Nec fundus ille paludosus, quem tanta arte emendavi, ponderosiorem sustinuit. Si vendi iusto precio nequiverint, date saltem operam, mei Amici, ut inquilinum fidelem pro ijs ad Michaëlis festum obtineam, qui id quod aequum erit, inde solvat. Causa PEPLERI etiam invitis (uti existimo) ijs, qui eam promovere satagebant, male illi cessit, et prout revera meritus erat, imo mitius. Si Amstelredami mansisset, neque [HUITFELDIO] auscultasset, sed apud me in Norvegia rursus habitandi veniam unâ cum culpa deprecatus fuisset, siquidem me ad condonandum non futurum difficilem ISAACUS PETRI iam antea illi indicarat, forte rectius sibi consuluisset. Sed confisus meis adversarijs nunc praedominantibus (apud quos ego, etiamsi Geometrice demonstrarem, nihil veri iustique obtineo) maluit hanc Camarinam movere, et maiora subire pericula maioresque miserias. Cum BŒKELIO per MULÆUM uti voluisti, agam, ubi fuerit, nunc enim peregre abest. Quid obtinuero, ex adiuncto responso videbis. Pecuniam totam iam antea tradiderat illi WINSHEMIUS, me inscio, nec tamen, postquam rescivi, multum dissentiente. Erat enim is valde importunus, praesertim vero uxor; et minabantur, at de vilde slaae Skade og Interesse paa. Et nescio, quae alia crocitabant. Quid igitur faceremus? Litem forensem ob id movere tibi forte ingratum fuisset, nec nobis volupe. Et habet is inter iudices sibi addictos, ut vix aliquid obtinuissemus. Nam et hîc iudicia sunt personalia, non realia, ferme ut apud vos, licet paulo minus ob processum Iuris Civilis in cognoscendis causis et non praeiudicandis magis aequum et legitimum. Dabo operam, quantum in me est, ut eos, quos adhuc desideras, nanciscaris. Quod si obtinere nequivero, transeant cum caeteris. Satis habes librorum. Unica SOPHIA omni praevalebit Philosophiae, etiam Platonicae et veriori, nedum Aristotelicae et fucatae, atque in Logimachijs verborum fundatae. Ignosces, quod de Praeceptore tuo id quod res est, dicam. Nam et ipsi Itali, qui alias ei admodum mancipati sunt, illum hoc (uti scis) elogio depraedicare non sunt veriti:

Doctorem calamo ingratus, Dominumque veneno
Perdidit, igne Patrum dogmata, nos tenebris.

Non tamen reprehendenda duxerim, quae in ipso laudabilia et recte constituta sunt, qualia te Lynceis oculis discernere posse non ambigo. Praestabit tamen (uti opinor) vivae illi palpabili et tractabili SOPHIAe operam dare, quae pro libris liberos dabit, pro facundia faecunditatem, ut pulchra faciet te prole parentem. Quod ante revolutionem Phoebi annuam faxit Deus. Cecini tibi breve et fortuito incidens Epigramma loco Epithalamij; quod nescio an acceperis, siquidem nullam in literis eius facis mentionem, nisi serius forte nactus sis, quam eas scriberes. In omnem itaque eventum denuo reliquis, quae mitto, cum alias sit breve, addidi. Restituas mihi velim carmen CRAMERI tui, quod cum caeteris in hoc genere coacervare lubet. Multa enim hinc inde talia ab Eruditis in mei gratiam cuduntur, ut suo tempore videbis. Plura nunc non addo. Ubi tu plura rescripseris et currum promissum, quem indies expeto expectoque, ne iter meum retardetur, miseris, ulterius respondebo. Vide saltem, ne quidpiam eorum silentio praetereas, de quibus antea movi, quaeque memoriae causa denotata habes. Iamque felix et incolumis cum tua costa vale, et matrem tuam officiose ex me saluta. Hancque non neglige, atque non detento hoc Nuncio actutum rescribe. Iterumque vale. Dabantur Wandesburgo die 10. Augusti Anni 1598.

BŒKELIUS hisce diebus domi non fuit, ut responsum eius tibi mittere nunc possem. Quam primum is redierit, omnem hac in re movebo lapidem, ut desiderijs tuis satisfiat.

Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.

To the man as noble in his most ancient lineage as in his own virtue, and renowned withal for solid learning, Master Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm — my kinsman, kinsman-by-marriage, and most upright friend.

Heartiest greeting.

The letter which you dashed off before your departure from the last Diet at Copenhagen, my most longed-for Holger — kinsman, kinsman-by-marriage, and (what I value more) most upright friend — I received only yesterday, in these very days, owing to the delays that Mule was contriving there. And, however hastily written, and not answering — for want of leisure — each of the points I was awaiting, it was most welcome. That the letters of the Elector and Electress of Brandenburg were not presented to the King and Queen, troubles me little. For I did not send them because I greatly desired that they be displayed; and so (as you know) I left that to your discretion. I know they teem with political and courtly turns, as happens with such men. Only, it was promised otherwise; when I look into the matter, the two Chancellors collude with one another, for the one raven does not peck out the other's eye: though I should reckon the one of them more upright and sincere than that Niccolò-Machiavelli novice of yours, who still daily learns from his neighbouring preceptor. Yet I do not think that what you hint at could be inferred from it, had the letters been delivered. For my condition is by no means bewailed there. And why should it be, since I myself have preferred, and still prefer, this present state to my former one? Nor would I exchange it — even if one were to throw in money besides, as when one strikes a bargain. Nor did I in any way angle for a return through those letters — though such a return is hateful both to your countrymen and to me, yet perhaps more to me than to them. And so I anxiously took care, with this Prince who was pressing the matter, that he should raise no such thing by word or in writing. Nor do the letters which I presented to him at Bramstedt — of which (unless I am mistaken) you have a copy — carry any such intent. I was only moderately requesting compensation for my expenses — more forcefully, perhaps, in its own good time. I shall see how those letters may be restored to their authors, provided it can be done without offence, by some excuse — or (if you prefer) pretext.

But that you did not deliver the books intended for the King, I approve neither your counsel nor that of the others. For, for certain reasons, I should have preferred them presented in good time rather than held back. Yet I do not suspect that you wished, out of some tenacity and love of me and of the books, to keep them for yourself, lest perhaps our rivals and ill-wishers should receive them to themselves as the King's gift. Whether they will please the King or not, I take with complete indifference. Nor indeed do I judge that they should be imparted to him because I would thus be eager to court his favour; since I know well enough that not even by some more pleasing gift, nor by any service, could that be obtained — on account of those to whom he gives more ear (which I bear with equanimity) — as my letters written from Rostock, together with their reply (not yet "critiqued," as you were promising), leave more than sufficiently attested. Nor do I thus unseasonably turn suppliant, since I ask nothing, beg off nothing, entreat nothing, but rather I show — and make a show — that I now dedicate to the Emperor my labours, which he with his counsellors spurned; and that, the envious and disparaging Danes set aside, I seek another, indeed the supreme head of the Empire, as the Maecenas of my studies — so that this (if anything were to be charged against me) might rather be imputed as a fault than that I should abase myself too far.

You therefore, my Holger, by all means deliver the books into the King's hands, whether he will it or resist; or, if that is not convenient for you, see that it be done through some other of my people, or even through Ramel — yet so that, by whomsoever they are handed to the King, whether yourself or others, they fall to you, since the King (as I know) takes delight in such books — nay, in no books at all, which is to be lamented. You will easily find a way; as I forewarned you before, too, when you were here with me. But if the King himself should wish to keep them (which I should count a kind of miracle), or even if, beyond all our foresight and contriving, they should come into the power of others into whose hands we should least wish, that must be borne. Let them have something to look up to and marvel at in secret — something they can neither grasp nor imitate — but openly, against their conscience (as is their wont), gnaw at, belittle, pervert, and carp at. The common people care for that, forsooth. But I shall see to it, should the matter so fall out, that you obtain new copies — illustrated and ornamented, too — by which you may relieve this loss, with verses also prefixed, but addressed to you, such as I had lately prefixed when I sent these books as a gift to Duke Johann Adolf; the tenor of which you have in the enclosed slip. The book of the Stars was written on paper, not on vellum; and this one I did not grant to Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg, when I lately sent him the other book as a gift, with its own verses likewise prefixed. How highly he valued both; how courteously and kindly he received my Tengnagel; how grateful he was toward me, and wishes to be hereafter; and finally with how clement a favour the Most Serene Elector of Cologne attends me — you will learn from what I have written to our Krag, and to some extent also from the enclosed copies of letters, which I would have you share with Krag too, especially those he does not already have, just as he shares mine with you. For what I write and send to either of you, I write and send to both, with a few small differences.

At Krag's you will see the copy of that infamous writing, such as was sent to me from Helmstedt. One of my students added marginal notes, especially where honourable mention is made of me, lest such memorable passages should have long to be sought out. A fine book indeed — received by the Copenhagen professors with such applause, though with a muffled murmur whispered among themselves! Monstrous wits delight in monsters, and crooked wits in crooked things. Our said common friend will show you in secret certain letters written by Johann Stuggius to one "John Sell-all" — or rather to some busybody — composed by arrangement, in the manner of a Bienenkorb [beehive-lampoon], so that he, and those to whom he presented this scurrilous writing as though a great and welcome gift, may vainly rejoice that assent and praise are thus offered them; or, if they perceive the trick, let them know that others know how little of truth and goodness they know or savour, and what tasteless, envious, and ill-willed critics they are, and that we, while they tickle and laugh among themselves in secret, openly prick and laugh at them — nay, look down on them from on high. But this book, which proclaims and trumpets my praises, I wish sent back to me by this same messenger; for I have no other copy. When I have got more, I will share them with you and others; and I shall be even more obliging than that book-peddler of yours, for I shall give freely and do openly what he does slyly and covertly.

Those new Ephemerides, reckoned from new Tables (if it please the gods!), do not earn even among the Germans the credit that the author vaunts in his Preface — still less among you Danes, who care for earthly things rather than heavenly. Alas, so turned away from your shores does the Sun yoke his horses! For in the North barbarism has always dwelt; and it is to be feared that even now it is not sufficiently rooted out, or may return by right of postliminy, with liberal and humane letters despised together with the integrity of virtue. That my kinsman Lindenov now shuffles over granting what he himself, not unwillingly, promised before we parted — though he was then just as prudent in political matters as now — I do not wonder. For he is a Cimbrian: why should he not play the Cimbrian, and haggle? Though (to say the truth) I care little whether he imparts it or not, for I am bent on weightier matters than to linger over such frivolous trifles of the worldly stage. I only wished, for memory's sake and the record of a thing well managed, to keep this little history by me — that one might read it like a fairy-tale of an evening. He wrote to me, and excused that the account was not sent, since this story was held up in various places. He also reported about the comedy that was put on there by a masked Englishman, and how it nearly turned into a tragedy. Perhaps the old "conscript father" does not cease — the furies so driving him — to rage even against the dead, before he rises up again and works portents. You advise me — and indeed rightly — that I should proceed circumspectly in certain matters — which indeed I do and shall do. Nay, I keep silent and dissemble, amid such great injuries and such false accusations, more than perhaps is needful. But one must serve the time and the place. What is deferred is not taken away. Kings have long hands, I confess; but philosophers and the guardians of truth have longer and stronger still. For their dominions and jurisdictions extend even beyond the destinies of mortals, while the rest is momentary and perishable. In the end the good cause triumphs. Those who do not possess themselves must have kings and lords — and they do not covet what kings and lords covet. Let those ever fear lest the broken axle-tree slip and the ruins strike them in their terror; he is a king who fears nothing — this kingdom each man gives to himself. But enough of these matters.

My house at Copenhagen I will not sell for two thousand. It cost me, before I was last there and had finished certain things in it, over 3,000 joachimsthalers; and then I spent scarcely a further thousand or so upon it. It is a sound enough structure, which in durability will not yield to the other stone houses in the city; nor has that marshy ground, which I improved with such art, borne any heavier building. If they cannot be sold at a fair price, at least take pains, my friends, that I may obtain a trustworthy tenant for them by Michaelmas, who will pay from it what is fair. The case of Pepler turned out badly for him — even against the will (as I think) of those who were busy promoting it — and as he had truly deserved, nay, more mildly. Had he stayed at Amsterdam and not listened to Huitfeldt, but rather begged leave to dwell with me again in Norway, together with pardon for his fault — since Isaac Petersen had already told him that I would not be hard to forgive — he would perhaps have counselled himself better. But, trusting in my adversaries now in the ascendant (with whom I obtain nothing of truth or justice, even were I to demonstrate it geometrically), he chose to stir up this Camarina, and to undergo greater dangers and greater miseries. As for your wish to make use of Boekel through Mule, I will deal with him when he is back, for he is now abroad; what I shall have obtained, you will see from the enclosed reply. Winsheim had already handed over the whole sum to him, without my knowledge — yet, after I learned of it, not much against my will; for he was very importunate, and especially his wife, and they threatened that they would impose damages and interest, and I know not what else they croaked. What, then, were we to do? To start a lawsuit over it would perhaps have been disagreeable to you, and no pleasure to us; and he has among the judges men devoted to him, so that we should scarcely have obtained anything. For here too the courts are personal, not impartial, much as with you — though somewhat less so, on account of the procedure of the Civil Law, which is more equitable and lawful in trying cases and not prejudging them. I will take pains, so far as in me lies, that you obtain those you still want; but if I cannot obtain them, let them pass with the rest. You have books enough. A single Sophia will outweigh all philosophy — even the Platonic and truer sort, still more the Aristotelian and counterfeit kind, founded on word-battles. You will forgive me for saying of your Preceptor what is the truth; for even the Italians themselves, otherwise quite enslaved to him, have not feared to extol him (as you know) with this epigram:

The ingrate destroyed his Teacher with the pen, his Lord with poison, the Fathers' doctrines with fire, and us with darkness.

Yet I would not have those things blamed which in him are praiseworthy and rightly established — such as I do not doubt you can discern with lynx eyes. Still it will be better (as I think) to devote your effort to that living, palpable, and tractable Sophia, who will give you children for books, fecundity for eloquence, so that she will make you, fair one, a parent with offspring — which may God bring to pass before Phoebus's yearly revolution. I sang you a short epigram, struck off by chance, in place of an epithalamium; whether you received it I do not know, since you make no mention of it in your letter — unless you got it later than you wrote them. And so, against every contingency, I have added it again to the rest of what I send, since it is short anyway. I should like you to return to me the poem of your Cramer, which it pleases me to heap up with the others of this kind; for many such are coined here and there by the learned in my honour, as you will see in due time. I add no more now. When you have written back further, and have sent the promised carriage — which I daily desire and await, lest my journey be delayed — I will answer at greater length. Only see that you pass over in silence none of those matters I raised before, and which you have noted down for memory's sake. And now, happy and unharmed, fare well with your rib, and dutifully greet your mother on my behalf; do not neglect her, and, without detaining this messenger, write back at once. Again, farewell. Given at Wandsbek, the 10th day of August, the year 1598.

Boekel has not been at home these days, so that I cannot now send you his reply. As soon as he has returned, I will move every stone in this matter, so that your wishes may be satisfied.

Yours, Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Edition of record. Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia vol. VIII (Epistolae). MS witness: A = Copenhagen, Rigsarkivet, Langebeks Diplomatarium (an Afskrift / later copy, not Tycho's autograph). Square brackets in the source block reproduce the editor's conjectural readings/insertions (e.g. [id], [Canceler], [praeceptore contiguo], [volenti], [repugnanti], [gammele]); they are kept as printed by the edition's convention
  • Date / place. Dateline Dabantur Wandesburgo die 10. Augusti Anni 1598 — agrees with the catalogue label. Wandsbek (then Wandesburg, the Rantzau estate just east of Hamburg) was Tycho's residence from late 1597 through summer 1598, between his departure from Denmark and his move toward the imperial court. Tycho kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar.
  • Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm (1574–1642), later called "the Learned" — Danish nobleman, theologian, and Tycho's kinsman. The triple address consanguineus, affinis, amicus ("kinsman, kinsman-by-marriage, friend") is literal: the Rosenkrantz and Brahe houses were repeatedly intermarried.
  • The book-gifts — the central thread. "These books" are Tycho's two 1598 presentation pieces, dispatched to princely patrons during the Wandsbek campaign for new patronage: the printed _Astronomiae instauratae mechanica_ (Wandsbek, 1598) and the manuscript star-catalogue (the "Liber de Stellis," the 1000-star catalogue, here noted as "written on paper, not on vellum" — grander recipients received the de-luxe vellum copy). Duke Johann Adolf of Holstein-Gottorp received both; Duke Ulrich III of Mecklenburg received only the other (the Mechanica), not the star-book. This dovetails exactly with brahe-t_15980816001 (16 Aug 1598), where Duke Ulrich, having received Tycho's gift and seen the Hven instruments, recommends Tycho to Rudolf II. The verses Tycho "prefixed" are the dedicatory poems; he promises Holger fresh illustrated copies with verses re-addressed to him.
  • Persons named.
  • Mule (Mulius / Mulaeus) — a Danish intermediary/agent handling Tycho's correspondence and the "Boekel" business; not securely identified here.
  • The Elector and Electress of Brandenburg / the King and Queen — Christian IV of Denmark and his new queen Anne Catherine of Brandenburg (married Nov 1597), daughter of Elector Joachim Friedrich of Brandenburg; hence the Brandenburg-court letters addressed to the Danish royal pair (the bride's family). Identification of the King and Queen as Christian IV and Anne Catherine is secure from the dynastic logic; treat as MEDIUM-HIGH.
  • The "two Chancellors" / the "Machiavelli novice" with his "neighbouring preceptor" — the Danish chancery leadership, satirised: the senior figure (the praeceptor) and his junior pupil (Neophitus). The Danish Rigskansler at this date was Arild Huitfeldt and the Kansler Christian Friis (to Kragelund); Tycho does not name them, and the pairing is left unidentified in the translation rather than forced. Crux — see Confidence.
  • Niels Krag (Cragius) — Danish humanist, historian and diplomat, Tycho's correspondent and the shared conduit ("our said common friend") for the circulating documents.
  • Frans Gansneb Tengnagel (Tengnaglius) — Tycho's assistant (later son-in-law), here received "courteously and kindly" by the Elector of Cologne.
  • The Most Serene Elector of Cologne — Ernst of Bavaria, Archbishop-Elector of Cologne; Tycho courts his favour during the patronage search.
  • Johann Stuggius (Ioannes Stuggius) — author of the anti-Tycho satirical letters circulating from the Copenhagen academic milieu; cf. the hostile "Stygius" letter brahe-t_15980710001. "John Sell-all" (Ioannes Omnivenda) and "the busybody" (Ardelio) are derisive nicknames for the recipients/peddlers of the lampoon, not real names.
  • Lindenov (Lindenovius) — Tycho's kinsman, reneging on a promise; mocked as a Cimber (Jutlander) who would "Cimbrize and haggle."
  • Pepler (Pepleri) — a former associate/servant of Tycho's whose lawsuit went badly; he had once dwelt with Tycho in Norway (Tycho's fief), had moved to Amsterdam, and (against the advice relayed through Isaac Petersen) "listened to Huitfeldt" rather than seeking Tycho's pardon.
  • Henrik Ramel (Ramelius) — German-born Danish statesman, an alternative deliverer of the books to the King.
  • Boekel (Boekelius), Winsheim (Winshemius) — parties to a money matter Holger had asked Tycho to pursue; Winsheim had paid over the disputed sum; Boekel was abroad.
  • Cramer (Crameri) — a poet in Holger's circle whose verse Tycho wishes returned for his collection of complimentary poems.
  • The "Ephemerides … from new Tables." A recently-published set of ephemerides, disparaged by Tycho as over-praised in its own preface; the author is unnamed and not securely identified (a contemporary ephemeris-maker, e.g. of the Magini/Origanus type). Left general in the translation.
  • Code-switches into Danish. The letter repeatedly drops from Latin into Early Modern Danish for sardonic, proverbial, or confidential asides — a hallmark of Tycho's intimate correspondence. The italicised English passages render: "Only, it was promised otherwise … the one raven does not peck out the other's eye"; "even if one were to throw in money besides, as when one barters"; "that they would impose damages and interest" (slaae Skade og Interesse paa, a legal threat); "that one might read it like a fairy-tale of an evening" (et Eventyr om Afftenen); and "before he rises up again and works portents" (førend han staar op igien og giør Jertegn). All are translated, never dropped.
  • Classical and Scriptural allusion.
  • "the one raven does not peck out the other's eye" — the Latin proverb cornix cornici numquam confodit oculum (here in Danish), of those who shield one another.
  • "stir up this Camarina" — the Greek proverb Καμάριναν κινεῖν ("to move Camarina," a marsh best left undisturbed; cf. Virgil, Aen. 3.701), i.e. to invite trouble.
  • "Kings have long hands" — Ovid, Heroides 17.166 (an nescis longas regibus esse manus).
  • The closing sententiae are Senecan: "he is a king who fears nothing — this kingdom each man gives to himself" = Seneca, Thyestes 388–390 (rex est qui metuit nihil … hoc regnum sibi quisque dat); "lest the broken axle-tree slip and the ruins strike them" echoes Horace, Odes 3.3.7–8 (si fractus illabatur orbis, impavidum ferient ruinae).
  • The verse couplet on Aristotle ("The ingrate destroyed his Teacher with the pen…") is a circulating Italian anti-Aristotelian epigram: Aristotle charged with ruining Plato (his Doctor) by his pen, Alexander (his Dominus) by poison, the Church Fathers' doctrines by fire, and posterity by darkness. Tycho cites it to needle Holger's Aristotelianism — "your Preceptor."
  • The Sophia pun. "Unica SOPHIA … vivae illi palpabili et tractabili SOPHIAe" and "cum tua costa" ("with your rib," Gen. 2:21–22 for wife) turn on a double sense: Sophia = Wisdom and a bride named Sophie. Tycho urges Holger to prefer the living Sophia — marriage and children (pro libris liberos, "children for books") — to bookish philosophy, and reports having sent a short epigram in place of an epithalamium (wedding-song). The wife is most plausibly Sophie Brahe (daughter of Axel Brahe), whom Holger Rosenkrantz married in this period; identification MEDIUM — left as the text's pun rather than asserted.
  • Self-positioning. The letter is a key document of Tycho's post-Denmark patronage turn: he insists he is no suppliant ("I ask nothing … I dedicate my labours to the Emperor"), seeks "the supreme head of the Empire" (Rudolf II) as his Maecenas, and frames the rupture with Denmark so that any fault lies with the "envious and disparaging Danes," not with himself. The Copenhagen house he refuses to undersell (>3,000 dalers spent; seeking a tenant by Michaelmas) shows him still managing Danish property in absentia.
  • Correction. Restored 'Mones, et quidem' (dropped from the published Latin) before 'recte, ut circumspecte in quibusdam agam', per TBOO VIII p.90; the English '[The messenger advised me]' was a fabricated supplement filling the resulting gap and is replaced with 'You advise me', matching Dreyer's 2nd-person 'Mones' addressed to Rosenkrantz.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VIII pp. 87–92; defects the audit noted: text error (3), fabrication (2) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.87-93 read as page images. Checked opening/closing lines and every proper name, date, sum of money, and place-name: Rosenkrantz/Rosenholm salutation, 10 August 1598 date and Wandsbek dateline, money figures (2000 / 3000 / c. 1000 Joachimsthalers) all match; place-names Bramstedt, Rostock, Helmstedt, Copenhagen, Mecklenburg, Cologne, Amsterdam, and Norway all confirmed verbatim including the unusual 'apud me in Norvegia' and 'ISAACUS PETRI', which the translation renders correctly. Found one genuine text-fidelity defect: our transcribed original omits 'Mones, et quidem' present in Dreyer p.90, and the English's bracketed supplement for that gap assigns the advice to 'the messenger' rather than to the letter's recipient, whom Dreyer's actual verb addresses in the second person. Also flagged a minor low-confidence mistranslation (not entered as a defect due to uncertainty about the idiom 'fucum capiunt') and a low-severity gender-attachment slip in 'pulchra... te... parentem' (English attaches 'fair one' to the male addressee, which is grammatically impossible for 'pulchra'; correct referent is Sophia or the offspring) — omitted from the defects list as sub-threshold given the text error already determines the grade.

2 defects found:
- text error (high). Our published the transcribed original silently drops the two words 'Mones, et quidem' ('You advise, and indeed') that Dreyer prints, leaving the sentence without its verb or subject. Dreyer prints: ...førend han staar op igien og giør Jertegn. Mones, et quidem recte, ut circumspecte in quibusdam agam. (TBOO VIII p.90, col. 2, ~l.33-36).
- fabrication (medium). The bracketed supplement invents 'the messenger' as the advice-giver; Dreyer's restorable verb 'Mones' shows the advice came from the letter's addressee (Rosenkrantz) himself, not a messenger — a wrong actor, not a neutral fill-in. At: “[The messenger advised me] rightly, that I should proceed circumspectly in certain matters.”.

Repaired. Collated directly against the Dreyer page image, TBOO VIII p.90, which reads '...førend han staar op igien og giør Jertegn. Mones, et quidem recte, ut circumspecte in quibusdam agam. Quod et facio, faciamque.' — confirming both the text error (dropped 'Mones, et quidem') and the fabrication (the invented 'messenger' as advice-giver, where Dreyer's 'Mones' is unambiguously 2nd-person singular, addressed to Rosenkrantz). No crux remains; both defects fully resolved against the edition of record.
