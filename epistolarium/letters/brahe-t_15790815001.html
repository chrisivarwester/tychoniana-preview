The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANDREAM SEVERINI VELLEIUM.
S.

EODEM vesperi quo reversus sum domum, scripsi Μ. MACHABÆO, clarissime Domine Magister ANDREA, de equis, et tuas unâ illi misi literas, et effeci ut eadem nocte literæ transveherentur, ut summo mane sequentis diei Lundam venirent. Ego interea nihil ab eo responsi accepi, si tu aliquid, nescio. Equos si ab ipso non acceperis vide ut Haffniæ habeas, ego stabo promissis. Mitto tibi nunc literas ad Amicos in Germania, Norimbergæ ad CAMERARIUM, cui per te HOMELIJ Gnomonica remitto. Scripsi illi ut earum Bibliothecarum quæ in ipsorum urbe sunt, aliarumque memorabilium rerum tibi copiam faceret. Augustæ ad Consulem PAULUM HAINZELIUM et HIERONIMUM WOLFIUM, cui etiam tuos conatus commendavi, ut eos pro virili iuvarent. Scripsi etiam quasdam literas in ædes hospitis mei LAURENTII DEN et peto, ut si qua ratione fieri poterit, efficias ut Globum meum magnum, quem ipse conservat, ante hyemem habere possim; poteris si quos Augustæ emeris libros, ei includere et bene exterius interiusque compactum meo sumptu huc transferri curare. Norimbergæ etiam effice, ut certo ante hyemem ea quæ ad aquæductum meum requiruntur, a GEORGIO LAUBENWOLF Artifice, cui eo nomine prius per te scripsi, obtineam, et vide quid in Machina quam pro Rege parat, paratum sit, et urge opus tanquam id in mandatis a Rege et me haberes. Augustæ videbis et civitatis et civium instructissimas Bibliothecas, inprimis vero Fuggeranam idque per WOLFIUM consequi poteris. Cum Hospite meo LAURENTIO loquere an quicquam de Fratre meo CANUTO intellexerit, et utrum sciat ipsum Venetias ivisse, et an adhuc ibi commoretur, et si is tibi inciderit, vel de illo quicquam audiveris ubi sit, scribe illi meo nomine, ut sit bono animo: Regem esse placabilem et omnia in melius, quam adversarij putant, cessura. Sed hæc quæ de hoc scribo, in secreto coram omnibus et hîc et alibi habe. Si quos libros invenies in Germania, vel de Cometa præter eos, quos habeo, vel aliquid singulare in Astronomicis, mihi coëmas velim, ego pretium lubens restituam.

Bene vale, charissime Domine M. ANDREA, et Deus te feliciter et incolumem ducat et reducat.
Datæ Huenæ 15. Augusti Anno 1579.
T.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Anders Sørensen Vedel. Greeting.

On the very evening on which I returned home, I wrote to Master Machabæus, most distinguished Master Anders, about the horses, and sent your letter to him along with mine, and arranged that the letters be carried across that same night, so that they should reach Lund at first light the next day. In the meantime I have had no reply from him; whether you have had any, I do not know. If you do not receive the horses from him, see that you have [some] at Copenhagen — I shall stand by my promises. I now send you letters to friends in Germany: at Nuremberg, to Camerarius, to whom through you I am returning Homelius's Gnomonica. I have written to him to put at your disposal the libraries that are in their city, and other things worth seeing. At Augsburg, to Consul Paul Hainzel and to Hieronymus Wolf, to whom I have also commended your endeavours, that they may help them with all their might. I have also written certain letters [to be delivered] to the house of my host Laurentius Den, and I ask that, if by any means it can be done, you bring it about that I may have my great globe — which he is keeping — before winter; and if you buy any books at Augsburg you can enclose them with it, and, well packed without and within, see to it that [it all] be conveyed here at my expense. At Nuremberg, too, bring it about that I certainly obtain, before winter, the things required for my aqueduct, from the craftsman Georg Laubenwolf — to whom on that account I have written earlier through you — and see what has been got ready in the machine he is preparing for the King, and press the work forward as if you had it in charge from the King and from me. At Augsburg you will see the most fully furnished libraries both of the city and of its citizens, but above all the Fugger library; and this you will be able to obtain access to through Wolf. Speak with my host Laurentius [to learn] whether he has understood anything about my brother Knud, and whether he knows that he has gone to Venice, and whether he is still staying there; and if he should fall in with you, or you hear anything of him as to where he is, write to him in my name to be of good cheer: that the King is appeasable, and that everything will turn out for the better than his adversaries suppose. But keep what I write about this matter secret in the presence of everyone, both here and elsewhere. If you find any books in Germany — whether about the comet, besides those I have, or anything notable in astronomy — I should like you to buy them up for me, [and] I shall gladly repay the cost.

Fare you well, dearest Master Anders, and may God lead you forth and bring you back happily and unharmed.
Given at Hven, 15 August, the year 1579.
T[ycho].
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe to Andreas Severini Vellejus = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), the Danish historian, Royal Historiographer and Ribe canon (later the editor of the Hundredvisebog ballad collection); Tycho's close friend from their shared student years. Vedel is here evidently about to set out on a journey through Germany, and the letter is a travel-and-errands brief: Tycho hands him letters of introduction and a list of commissions to execute en route.
  • M. Machabæo — "Master Machabæus," addressed at/near Lund (then Danish) concerning horses. The Macchabæus (Macalpine) family were prominent Danish humanists of Scottish origin descended from the reformer Johannes Machabæus (d. 1557); the individual here is most plausibly a Lund cleric/canon of that family. Identification flagged: the source gives only the surname and the academic title M[agister]; the precise individual is not securely fixable from the letter alone.
  • Camerarius (Nuremberg) — Joachim Camerarius the Younger (1534–1598), the Nuremberg physician and botanist, a learned correspondent through whom Tycho routed introductions and books.
  • Homelii Gnomonica — the Gnomonica (treatise on dialling / construction of sundials) associated with Johann Hommel (Homelius, 1518–1562), the Leipzig mathematician. Tycho is returning (remitto) a copy to Camerarius via Vedel; Gnomonica kept as the work-title.
  • Consul Paulus Hainzelius — Paul Hainzel of Augsburg (one of the Hainzel brothers of the 1571 Wolf letter); Consul here = Bürgermeister (city magistrate) of Augsburg, with whom Tycho had built the great Göppingen quadrant in 1569. Hieronymus Wolfius = Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and librarian (see brahe-t_15711113001).
  • Laurentius Den — Lorenz (Laurentius) Den, Tycho's host (hospes) during his Augsburg residence (1569–70), now the custodian of Tycho's Globus magnus (great globe) awaiting shipment north.
  • Globus meus magnus — "my great globe": Tycho's large celestial globe, ordered/begun in Augsburg in his earlier stay and famously not fully completed and delivered to Hven until years later (it was finished at Uraniborg). The instruction to retrieve it "before winter" fits the period when Tycho was still drawing his Augsburg-made apparatus home.
  • Georgio Laubenwolf Artifice — Georg Labenwolf (Laubenwolf), the Nuremberg bronze-founder and waterworks/fountain engineer (the family responsible for the Nuremberg "Gänsemännchen" fountain). He is independently documented as the maker of the Vandkunsten (waterworks) at Kronborg for Frederik II — which corroborates both the aquaeductus commission for Uraniborg and the reference to "the machine he is preparing for the King." Artifex rendered "craftsman."
  • aquaeductum meum / Machina … pro Rege — "my aqueduct" = the water-supply / fountain works for Uraniborg; the "machine he is preparing for the King" = a parallel hydraulic commission for Frederik II (consistent with Labenwolf's documented Kronborg waterworks). Tycho asks Vedel to chase the Uraniborg parts and to inspect progress on the royal machine, "as if you had it in charge from the King and from me."
  • Bibliotheca Fuggerana — "the Fugger library," the renowned Augsburg book-collection of the Fugger merchant-banking dynasty; access to be arranged through Wolf (who served the Fuggers as librarian).
  • Frater meus Canutus — Knud Brahe (1555–1615), Tycho's younger brother, then reported to have gone to Venice (Venetias). The passage that follows is the letter's confidential core: Knud was at this time in difficulty with the crown, and Tycho wants reassurance passed to him "in my name" — that the King is appeasable (Regem esse placabilem) and matters will turn out better than his adversaries expect — with the express instruction to keep the whole affair secret before everyone, here and elsewhere.
  • de Cometa — "about the comet": Tycho's standing interest in cometary literature (the great comet of 1577, on which he was then at work). He asks Vedel to buy up any comet-books or notable astronomical works he does not already own, against repayment.
  • Epistolary formulae. Opening S. = salutem [dicit], "greeting"; Bene vale = "Fare you well." Domine M[agister] Andrea = "Master Anders" (academic Magister). Subscription T. / TYCHO BRAHE preserved.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Huenae 15. Augusti Anno 1579 = "Given at Hven (the island of Hven, seat of Uraniborg), 15 August 1579" (Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The edition label ("1579. 15. august. Hven") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Witness note. The single recorded witness (A) is a later copy (Afskrift) in Tycho Brahe's private archive at the Rigsarkivet, not an autograph; readings follow Dreyer.
  • Orthography. Source prints Μ. (the Greek capital mu, for Latin M[agister]) before MACHABAeO — retained as printed in the Source block; it stands for the Latin abbreviation and is rendered "Master." Ligature ae (as in MACHABAeO, Vesperi-clause forms) and unâ / hîc circumflexes are kept as printed.
