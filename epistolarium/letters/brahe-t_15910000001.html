The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD GELLIUM SASCERIDEM.

.... Dici non potest quam mihi gratum fuerit, quod intellexerim Excellentissimum et præstantissimum MAGINUM Patavium venisse, illicque æstivum semestre consumere decrevisse, quodque Instrumentum illud Sextantis Astronomici, de quo ad te scripsi, per vos elaboretur. Spero enim vos aliquot Stellarum Observationes hac æstate illo intermediante illic obtenturos. An Organum omni ex parte rite constet, distantiæ et declinationes stellarum transmissæ patefacient. Cuperem Altitudines Meridianas Solis, tum etiam Affixarum Stellarum, præsertim quæ hîc decliviores sunt, eodem diligentius observari, ut Refractionum ratio certius pateat. Eclipses quoque Lunares per Altitudines Fixarum illustrium sedulo notari, ut differentia meridianorum nostrorum innotescat, apprime consultum duxerim. Atque hæc et similia ipsemet MAGINUS, etiam te absente, facile expediet. Si Planetarum quoque loca per distantias a fixis sideribus, ijs præsertim quæ a nobis restituta Catalogus transmissus exhibet, subinde rimatus fuerit, utique quanta sit Cœli et Canonum Astronomicorum differentia experietur. Mars in ultimo Acronycho situ eundem ferme locum (digrediente ad ternos gradus Alphonsinorum calculo) cœlitus designavit, quem rationes Coperniceæ postulant, nisi quantum in simplici motu Solis atque Æquinoctiorum præcessione ab eo aberratum est, cum alias ♂ pernox factus, intra unum vel alterum gradum, nonnunquam etiam plures, calculum Coperniceum eludat. Quod vero nunc tam prope consenserit, id causæ fuit, quod circa Perigæum Solis illi opponebatur; idque nostram Hypothesium cœlestium restitutionem evidenter confirmat; præsertim cum iuxta medias inter Apogæum et Perigæum Solare elongationes ♂ Acronychius factus, plurimum a Coperniceis numeris dissentire deprehendatur, idque in diversas partes, prout Eccentricitatis insinuatio postulat. Idem in reliquis duobus superioribus Planetis usu venit, licet in his ob orbium magnitudinem minus sentiatur. Concurrunt tamen et aliæ quædam causæ, ex commensuratione veterum, adeoque ipsius COPERNICI, non satis accurate instituta; quæ omnia non est huius loci extricare.

In operibus meis perficiendis excudendisque nihil hac æstate laboratum est, ob varia impedimenta et avocamenta minus grata; si hiems tranquillius et exoptabilius otium præbuerit, favente cœlesti Numine, inchoatam telam pertexam, posteaquam cum præstantissimo MAGINO alijsque in Italia eruditis Philosophis id, quicquid erit, lubens communicari curabo. Si interea ipsius opera Privilegia a Pontifice et Duce Mantuæ alijsque Italiæ proceribus, quotquot impetrare licuerit, prout benevole pollicetur, obtinuero, rem præstiterit mihi summopere acceptam omnique gratitudinis significatione compensandam.

Canonem Radicum Quadratarum, quem molitur, avide desidero. Scio equidem tali ratione Tangentium ac Secantium Tabulas excusari posse, an vero promtiori compendio, nondum dixerim. Scripsissem ipsemet nunc MAGINO, sed ad matrem Aulæ magistram (quæ apud Reginam Fridrichsburgi diutius quam sperassem hærens, et proprijs commoditatibus destituta minus prospera fruitur valetudine) avocor, et pleraque alia, quæ me a scriptione retardant, nunc incidunt obstacula. Interim eximium hunc et magni nominis Virum, qui mei, ut non obscure, tum ex ipsius tum etiam tuis litteris perspexi, est amantissimus, meo nomine diligenter resalutare, omniaque grati et benevoli erga ipsum animi officia sincere offerre ne supersedeas. Quamprimum otium et oportunitatem commodiorem nactus fuero, illi scribere et de rebus Astronomicis cum eo ulterius conferre (ex quo videam eum earum tam gnaviter cupidum, quam excellenter peritum) non intermittam.

Tycho Brahe to Gellius Sascerides.

.... It cannot be told how welcome it was to me to have learned that the most excellent and most distinguished Magini has come to Padua, and has resolved to spend the summer half-year there, and that the instrument of that astronomical sextant, about which I wrote to you, is being worked up by you [pl.]. For I hope that this summer, with him as intermediary, you will obtain there observations of several of the stars. Whether the instrument is correctly set up in every part, the distances and declinations of the stars sent over [to me] will reveal. I could wish that the meridian altitudes of the Sun, and also of the fixed stars — especially those that are rather low here — be observed more carefully in the same place, so that the principle of the refractions may be more surely laid open. I should think it especially advisable, too, that lunar eclipses be diligently noted by the altitudes of conspicuous fixed stars, so that the difference of our meridians may become known. And these things and the like Magini himself, even in your absence, will easily dispatch. If he shall also from time to time have investigated the places of the planets by their distances from the fixed stars — especially those that the catalogue sent over presents as restored by us — he will assuredly find by trial how great is the difference between the heavens and the astronomical tables. Mars, in its last acronychal position, marked out from the heavens nearly the same place (the Alphonsine calculation diverging by three degrees) which the Copernican principles require — except in so far as it has erred therefrom in the simple motion of the Sun and in the precession of the equinoxes — whereas at other times Mars, become an all-night star [pernox], by one degree or another, sometimes even more, eludes the Copernican calculation. But that it now agreed so closely, the cause of this was that it stood in opposition to it [the Sun] near the perigee of the Sun; and this evidently confirms our restoration of the celestial hypotheses, especially since, when Mars becomes acronychal near the mean elongations between the solar apogee and perigee, it is found to disagree very greatly from the Copernican numbers — and that on opposite sides, as the working-in [insinuatio] of the eccentricity requires. The same comes about in the case of the remaining two superior planets, although in these, on account of the magnitude of the orbs, it is less perceived. Yet certain other causes also concur, arising from the commensuration of the ancients, and indeed of Copernicus himself, established not accurately enough; all of which it is not the place here to disentangle.

Toward the completing and the printing of my works nothing has been laboured this summer, on account of various hindrances and distractions less than welcome; if the winter affords a calmer and more desirable leisure, then, by the favour of the heavenly Deity, I shall weave through the web I have begun — after which I shall gladly see to it that whatever it may be is communicated to the most distinguished Magini and to the other learned philosophers in Italy. If meanwhile, through his good offices, I shall have obtained privileges from the Pontiff and from the Duke of Mantua and from the other magnates of Italy — as many as it may be allowed to procure, as he kindly promises — he will have rendered me a service most highly acceptable, and to be repaid with every token of gratitude.

The table of square roots, which he is working on, I eagerly long for. I know indeed that by such a method the tables of tangents and secants can be struck off, but whether by a readier shortcut, I would not yet say. I should myself have written now to Magini, but I am called away to my mother, the mistress of the [Queen's] court (who, lingering with the Queen at Frederiksborg longer than I had hoped, and deprived of her own comforts, enjoys less than prosperous health), and very many other obstacles that hinder me from writing now arise. Meanwhile, do not forbear to greet again diligently, in my name, this exceptional man of great renown — who, as I have perceived not obscurely, both from his own and from your letters, is most loving toward me — and to offer him sincerely all the offices of a grateful and well-disposed mind toward him. As soon as I have obtained more convenient leisure and opportunity, I shall not fail to write to him and to confer further with him on astronomical matters (since I see that he is as zealously desirous of them as he is excellently skilled in them).

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe to Gellius Sascerides (Gellius Sascerides Hafnianus, c. 1562–1612), the Danish scholar who had studied and worked at Uraniborg and was then travelling in Italy. Gellius is the intermediary between Tycho and Magini; in 1594 he would become entangled in the notorious broken-betrothal affair with Tycho's daughter Magdalene.
  • Person named. MAGINUS = Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), professor of astronomy at Bologna; the third party in this exchange. Tycho asks Gellius to use Magini, then summering at Padua (Patavium), to procure Italian printing privileges and to make trial observations.
  • Transmission / dating crux. The letter survives only as an excerpt that Gellius Sascerides copied into his own letter to Magini, written at Padua, 14 March 1592 (the date the edition's witness field records). That is the date of the covering letter, not of Tycho's letter. Within that covering letter Gellius states the source explicitly: "Habes, doctissime Magine, quae ad me Tycho suis in litteris, elapso autumno, de te scripsit" — "you have what Tycho wrote to me about you in his letter, last autumn." Tycho's letter is therefore datable to autumn 1591 (O.S.; Denmark used the Julian calendar). Dreyer (Opera Omnia VII, pp. 305–307) accordingly catalogues it under 1591, with no month or day; the edition label "1591. empty" reflects this. The header gives the substantiated autumn 1591 and appends the catalogue year. The leading "...." in the source marks that Dreyer prints only the portion Gellius transcribed, not a complete letter.
  • Place. No place is named in the excerpt; Tycho was resident at Uraniborg on Hven throughout 1591, and the reference to his mother's attendance on the Queen at Frederiksborg fixes the Danish setting. The bracketed [Uraniborg] is editorial.
  • Instruments / observation programme. Instrumentum illud Sextantis Astronomici → the astronomical sextant Tycho had described to Gellius for building in Italy; Organum → the instrument. The verbs patefacient / obtenturos / observari are second-person plural (per vos … vos … obtenturos): Tycho addresses Gellius and his Italian collaborators together. Altitudines Meridianas → meridian altitudes; Affixarum / Fixarum Stellarum → fixed stars; declinationes → declinations; Refractionum ratio → "the principle/account of refractions". The lunar-eclipse-by-fixed-star-altitudes method is for determining the difference of meridians (longitude difference between observing sites).
  • Catalogue of restored stars. ijs … quae a nobis restituta Catalogus transmissus exhibet — the star catalogue Tycho had sent, whose positions he describes as restored (restituta; cf. his programme of astronomia instaurata,). Canonum Astronomicorum → the astronomical tables (Alphonsine and Copernican), tested against the sky.
  • The Mars passage (technical). Mars in ultimo Acronycho situ → "Mars in its last acronychal position" (acronychus / pernox = rising at sunset, i.e. at opposition; rendered "all-night star" where Tycho varies to pernox). digrediente ad ternos gradus Alphonsinorum calculo → "the Alphonsine calculation diverging by three degrees." rationes Coperniceae / calculum Coperniceum / Coperniceis numeris → the Copernican principles / calculation / numbers. simplici motu Solis atque Aequinoctiorum praecessione → "the simple motion of the Sun and the precession of the equinoxes." circa Perigaeum Solis illi opponebatur → Mars "stood in opposition to it [the Sun] near the perigee of the Sun." Apogaeum et Perigaeum Solare → solar apogee and perigee; elongationes → elongations; Eccentricitatis insinuatio → "the working-in [insinuatio] of the eccentricity" — insinuatio is here rendered literally and flagged (see crux). Hypothesium coelestium restitutionem → "the restoration of the celestial hypotheses," Tycho's claim that the close Mars opposition confirms his own (geo-heliocentric) world-system. reliquis duobus superioribus Planetis → "the remaining two superior planets" (Jupiter and Saturn); orbium magnitudinem → "the magnitude of the orbs". commensuratione veterum → "the commensuration of the ancients."
  • Symbol. The Mars glyph ♂ appears twice in the source for Mars; named "Mars" in the translation, glyph retained in the Source block. (Favaro's edition renders the same glyph as the garbled cT / </.)
  • "My works … the web I have begun." In operibus meis perficiendis excudendisque → "completing and printing my works"; inchoatam telam pertexam → "I shall weave through the web I have begun," the loom metaphor for finishing the Astronomiae instauratae programme (the press at Uraniborg). favente coelesti Numine → "by the favour of the heavenly Deity," a piety formula, kept.
  • Privileges. Privilegia a Pontifice et Duce Mantuae → printing privileges (copyright grants) from the Pope and the Duke of Mantua (Vincenzo I Gonzaga), to be sought through Magini's good offices.
  • Mathematics. Canonem Radicum Quadratarum → "the table of square roots" Magini was preparing; Tangentium ac Secantium Tabulas → "tables of tangents and secants"; promtiori compendio → "a readier shortcut." Tycho's hedge is preserved verbatim: an vero promtiori compendio, nondum dixerim → "but whether by a readier shortcut, I would not yet say."
  • "My mother, the mistress of the court." matrem Aulae magistram → Beate Bille (c. 1526–1605), Tycho's mother, who served as hofmesterinde (mistress of the court / chief lady-in-waiting) to the queen dowager Sophie of Mecklenburg. apud Reginam Fridrichsburgi → "with the Queen at Frederiksborg" (the royal castle at Hillerød). minus prospera fruitur valetudine → "enjoys less than prosperous health"; the litotes is kept.
  • Closing courtesies. resalutare … omniaque … officia sincere offerre ne supersedeas → the request that Gellius return Tycho's greetings to Magini and offer every courtesy; de rebus Astronomicis cum eo ulterius conferre → "to confer further with him on astronomical matters." The excerpt breaks off without a subscription, since Gellius transcribed only the substantive body.
  • Calendar. No dateline survives in the excerpt. Tycho's letter = autumn 1591 (O.S.); the covering letter that preserves it = Padua, 14 March 1592 (Gellius writing from Catholic Italy would have used New Style, but no style is internally specified — recorded, not converted). No in-letter date discrepancy beyond the covering-letter/source-letter distinction set out above.
