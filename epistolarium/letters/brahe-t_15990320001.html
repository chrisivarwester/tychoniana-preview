The original

REVERENDO ET CLARISSIMO VIRO D:NO MAGISTRO JONAe JACOBO VENUSINO, AMICO ET PATRONO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO. HAFNIAM.

Reverende domine, amice pl. observande.

CUM post longam peregrinationem superiori aestate patriam et amicos reviserem, multi viri docti, qvis Germaniae, Daniae, et vicinorum regnorum status esset, diligenter ex me qvaesiverunt, inter qvos Johannes Cragius magni apud nostrates nominis astronomus, qvia magnam aetatis partem in Germania consumpserat, qvi nunc ibidem in mathesi excellerent vehementer scire avebat. Cum ego cum multis aliis Tychonem Brahe etiam nominassem, statim ille: fastuosus homo ille, qvi multa profert, sed pavca probat, qvia mecum aliqvando in lite qvadam philosophica minus pro arbitrio certasset, gravissimam de me ad Scotiae regem qverelam detulit, et non ita pridem, inqvit, ab ipso epistolas nonnullas aeditas ajunt, in qvibus me sine nomine acriter perstrinxit, sed nullam inde lavdem reportabit. Proinde isto fumi venditore relicto, dic, ait, anne alios in Dania nosti astronomos insignes. Ego honorificam tui, ut debui, mentionem feci, et post varios de humanitate tua sermones magnetis tui memini. Is qvia se multa de hoc lapide scire existimabat, amicitiam contrahere, et qvicqvid singulare in mathesi experientia didicisset, tecum communicare desiderabat. Certior a me de naturae tuae bonitate et exqvisita eruditione factus, literas meis inclusas ad te misit; qvod serius venerint, effecit turbulentus Svediae status, qvo ego praecipiti avtumno delatus fui. Qvaeso nunc boni consule; adiunctae sunt etiam aliae ad Tychonem, si possis efficere, ut ad ipsum perferantur, cures rogo. Sin minus, ad me remitte; aperire, si placet, poteris meo periculo; hanc mihi potestatem dedit Cragius. Si cras ad me rescripseris, avt ipse ad te veniam avt Svediae meumque statum ad te perscribam, jam avriga properabat. Navigandi Gedanum occasionem hic expectare cogor.

Vale cum toto coetu tuo.
Helsingora xx martii Mdic. T. r. a.
Patricius Gordonius

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the reverend and most distinguished man, Master Jon Jacobsen Venusinus, friend and patron much to be observed. To Copenhagen.

Reverend lord, friend much to be observed.

When, after a long peregrination, last summer I revisited my homeland and [my] friends, many learned men diligently asked of me what was the state of Germany, of Denmark, and of the neighbouring kingdoms; among whom John Craig — an astronomer of great name among our [countrymen], because he had spent a great part of his life in Germany — was vehemently eager to know who now excelled there in mathematics. When I had named, among many others, Tycho Brahe also, at once he [said]: "That arrogant man, who puts forth much but proves little — because he once contended with me, in a certain philosophical dispute, less to [his own] liking, he brought a most grave complaint about me to the King of Scotland; and not long ago, they say, some letters were published by him, in which he sharply censured me without [naming my] name — but he will carry off no praise thereby. Accordingly, leaving that smoke-seller aside," he says, "tell [me], do you know other notable astronomers in Denmark?" I made honourable mention of you, as I ought; and, after various discourses about your kindness, I made mention of your magnet [loadstone]. He, because he thought he knew much about this stone, desired to contract a friendship, and to share with you whatever singular [thing] he had learned in mathematics by experience. Made more certain by me of the goodness of your nature and your exquisite erudition, he sent letters, enclosed in mine, to you; that they have come rather late, the turbulent state of Sweden brought about, whither I was carried in the headlong autumn. I pray [you], now take [it] in good part. There are also adjoined other [letters], to Tycho — if you can bring it about that they be conveyed to him, see to it, I pray. But if not, send [them] back to me; you can open [them], if you please, at my risk — Craig gave me this power. If tomorrow you write back to me, either I myself will come to you, or I will write out to you [the affairs] of Sweden and my own state — [but] now the carter was hastening. I am compelled to await here the occasion of sailing to Danzig.

Farewell, with your whole company.
Elsinore, the 20th of March, 1599. Most devoted to Your Reverence,
Patrick Gordon.

Apparatus

  • A document of the Tycho–Craig controversy. The Scottish traveller Patrick Gordon reports to Jon Jacobsen Venusinus a conversation with John Craig (Johannes Cragius, d. 1620), the Scottish physician-astronomer (later physician to James VI/I), Tycho's antagonist in the long dispute over comets (Craig held comets to be sublunary/optical; Tycho refuted him). Hearing Tycho named, Craig burst out that he is "that arrogant man, who puts forth much but proves little," a "smoke-seller" (fumi venditor — charlatan); complains that, after their "philosophical dispute," Tycho had lodged a grave complaint against him with the King of Scotland, and had "published letters censuring me anonymously" — the anti-Craig matter in Tycho's Epistolae astronomicae (1596). A rare outside witness to how bitterly Craig resented Tycho. (de me … detulit: read with Tycho as subject — Tycho complained to James VI about Craig — per the historical record; the reported speaker is Craig.)
  • Recipient. Jon Jacobsen Venusinus (Jon Jakobsen Venusin, d. 1608), Danish polymath, royal historiographer and professor at Copenhagen, with a known interest in the magnet (magnes — loadstone); Gordon reports that Craig, hearing of Venusinus's magnetic studies, wishes to correspond. The letter forwards Craig's letters to Venusinus and, enclosed, further letters for Tycho — so the piece enters the Tychonic correspondence as a transmittal.
  • Persons & places. nostrates = "our countrymen" (Scots); Scotiae rex = James VI of Scotland; Gedanum = Danzig (Gdańsk); the "turbulent state of Sweden" and Gordon's autumn passage allude to the Swedish succession crisis (Charles vs Sigismund). T. r. a. = Tuae Reverentiae addictissimus ("most devoted to Your Reverence").
  • Calendar. Helsingora xx martii Mdic — Mdic = M D IC = 1000 + 500 + 99 (subtractive IC = 99) = 1599; Elsinore (Helsingør), Denmark = Old Style. 20 March 1599. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. In Craig's reported speech, corrected 'less to [my] liking' to 'less to [his own] liking': the Latin's unstated possessive on 'pro arbitrio' is reflexive to the dispute's subject (Tycho), not to Craig, per the sentence's grammar and Dreyer's printed text (TBOO XIV p.151).

One mistranslation found: the bracketed '[my]' possessive on 'pro arbitrio' misattributes whose satisfaction is at stake, reversing the sense of who came off worse in the Tycho-Craig dispute — a real interpretive point about the anecdote's balance, not a stylistic quibble. Everything else in the letter is faithful.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). The subject of 'certasset' throughout this clause is 'ille' (Tycho), so the unstated possessor of 'pro arbitrio' defaults reflexively to Tycho, not to the first-person narrator Craig — i.e. the dispute went less to TYCHO's own liking (he came off worse), which is what supplies the causal motive for 'he [Tycho] brought a grave complaint about me to the King of Scotland' in the next clause. The English's bracketed '[my]' reassigns the dissatisfaction to Craig, reversing who was bested in the exchange — turning Craig's implicit boast about worsting Tycho into an ambiguous statement about his own disappointment, and breaking the causal logic of why Tycho then complained to the king. At: “he once contended with me, in a certain philosophical dispute, less to [my] liking”.

Repaired. Collated against Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.150-152 (in full); the clause is confirmed verbatim on p.151. The correction itself turns on standard Latin reflexive-possession grammar (unmarked possessives default to the clause's grammatical subject), which the page confirms textually; the interpretation is not contested elsewhere in the sentence, since it alone restores the causal 'because...he complained' logic. Single defect fixed.
