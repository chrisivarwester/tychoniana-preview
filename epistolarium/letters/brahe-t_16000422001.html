The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD VIDUAM HENRICI RANTZOVII].

EDLER vnnd vielthugendsam fraw statthalterin, jnbesonders gunstige gutte freundin. Nechst meines freundtlichen grusses vnd willigen dienstes fermeldung geb ich der frawen gantz freundlich zuwissen, das nachdem ich vernommen, daß mein vertrawtter gutter freunndt vnd blutverwanter in Dennemark, Eske Bilde, nicht zeittlich genung hat zuwissen bekommen von der hauptsumme im land zu Holstein zu bezahlen vnnd meinen brieff wiederumb einzulössen, so hab ich ihm wiederumb davon geschrieben, vnnd alle gelegenheit noch einmahl verstendiget, vnnd danebenst copien von brieffuen darzugehörig geschicht, worauff ehr mir wiederumb zugeschrieben, das er alles auff nechstkumpfftigen vmbslag in diesen lauffenden jahr von meinetwegen will richtigmachen. Bitt darumb gantzfreundtlich, die fraw stathalterin wolt eß alles hierin zum besten auffnehmen, vnnd bei dem herrn Diderich von Dingcklauben handlen, das er wolle zufrieden sein biß auf vorgenante zeit; die zinß aber, welche zu nechstvorgangnen vmbslag sollte ausgelegt werden, vorsihe ich mich sey zu rechter zeitt bezahlt. Ferner wolte ich die fraustathalterin gantz freundtlich gebeten haben, daß sie wolte mit allen den eeissenwerck vnd was anders ich alda zu Wandeßbech vmb mein eigen geldt nöttwendig machen lassen, ein richtig anordnung thun, daß eß mihr möchtte wiederumb erstattet werden, vnd nicht ferner auff dero vielgeliebten sohn Bredo, dem ich davon fleissig geschrieben (vnd er mir dog nichtes geanttuort), schicken, furnemlich dieweill, wie ich vernommen, die fraw willenß ist, gedachtem jhren sohn Wandeßbech abzuhandellen vnd selbst einzuhaben. Jch weß genugk, daß die frauw mein schaden nicht begehret, vnd was zuvor darahn von jhren lieben herrn stathalter, seligen dechtnuß, bezalt ist worden, davon die fraw woll bescheid weiß, kan davon abgeregnit werden, bitt nuhr, daß daß vbrige mihr möchtte bezalht werden, vnd meinem factor zu Hamburgk, Symon von Petkom, zugesthelt, der auch nebenst den herrn zu Hamburgk, smid Jacob Holsten, von allen bescheid weiss zu sagen, vnnd darauff zu quitiren. So fern aber der frawen nicht geliebt die vbrige eisentrallien, vnd waß sonst von eisenwergk da gemacht ist, zu behallten, bitt ich, daß gedachten meinen factor muge vergonnet werden, daß er dasselbige alleß, so fur meinem gehlt ist beköstiget vnd nicht bezalt, muge wiederumb wegknehmen, vnd ein theill davon ihn Hamburgk wiederumb zu gelde machen, daß furnembst aber, so von eissenwergk ist, mihr nebenst denn andern meinen guttern so bald nach ihr röm. kay. may., vnserm gnedigen herrn, schrifftlich an die herrn von Hamburgk begehr, dieselben heraufkommen zu schicken.

Jch vorsihe mich zur frawstathalterin, daß sie mihr diss nicht abslagen wirt, sondern mit dem ehesten, weill eß nun bei anderthalb Jahren gewehrt vnd auffgeschoben ist, ein eigentlichen bescheid hierauff machen. Wohrin ich kan die frawstathalterin oder einigen von jhren kindern nach meinem vermugen wiederumb dienstlich sein, will ich allzeitt gantz guttwillig gefunden werden, vnd thu hiemit die fraw stathalterin sambt allen den ihrigen in schütz vnd schirm deß Allmechtigen treulich befehlen.

Datirt auff dem kayserlichen slotz Benateck in Behemen den 22 thag aprilis stylo nouo anni 1600.

[TYCHO BRAHE TO THE WIDOW OF HENRIK RANTZAU].

Noble and most virtuous Lady governor's-wife, especially favourable good friend. Next [after] the declaration of my friendly greeting and willing service, I give the Lady quite amicably to know that, after I learned that my trusted good friend and blood-relation in Denmark, Eske Bille, did not get to know in time enough about paying the principal sum in the land of Holstein and redeeming my bond, I have written to him again about it, and explained the whole situation once more, and sent besides copies of the relevant letters; whereupon he has written back to me that he will settle everything on my behalf at the next Umschlag in this current year. I therefore ask quite amicably that the Lady governor's-wife would take it all herein for the best, and deal with Lord Diderich von Dincklage, that he would be content until the aforesaid time; but the interest which was to be laid out at the last past Umschlag, I assume has been paid at the right time. Further, I would quite amicably have asked the Lady governor's-wife that she would make a proper arrangement concerning all the ironwork, and whatever else I had necessarily made there at Wandsbek with my own money, that it might be reimbursed to me — and not [be deferred] further upon her much-beloved son Breide, to whom I have written diligently about it (and he has yet answered me nothing) — especially since, as I have learned, the Lady intends to buy out Wandsbek from her said son and hold it herself. I know well enough that the Lady does not desire my loss; and what was previously paid toward it by her dear Lord governor of blessed memory, of which the Lady well knows the account, can be reckoned off from it. I ask only that the remainder might be paid to me, and delivered to my factor at Hamburg, Simon von Petkum — who, together with the Hamburg man, the smith Jacob Holst, knows how to give account of everything and to give a receipt thereupon. But insofar as it does not please the Lady to keep the remaining iron lattice-work, and whatever else of ironwork has been made there, I ask that my said factor may be permitted to take it all away again — that which was paid for with my money and not [re]paid — and to turn part of it into money again at Hamburg; the chief part, however, which is of ironwork, [I ask] to be sent up to me, together with my other goods, as soon as His Roman Imperial Majesty, our gracious Lord, requests of the Lords of Hamburg in writing that they be sent up.

I trust the Lady governor's-wife that she will not refuse me this, but will — since it has now dragged on and been postponed for about a year and a half — make a definite decision hereupon as soon as possible. In whatever I can in turn be of service to the Lady governor's-wife, or any of her children, according to my ability, I will at all times be found quite willing; and I herewith faithfully commend the Lady governor's-wife, together with all hers, to the protection and shelter of the Almighty.

Dated at the imperial castle Benátky in Bohemia, the 22nd day of April, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Christine von Halle (d. 1604), widow of Henrik (Heinrich) Rantzau (1526–1598), the great Holstein humanist and royal Statthalter (lieutenant-governor) of the Duchies — hence Tycho's address Frau Statthalterin ("Lady governor's-wife"). The source header reads simply Ad viduam Henrici Rantzovii ("to the widow of Henrik Rantzau"); the edition supplies her name. The MS is an autograph draft (Kladde).
- Significance — Tycho's unfinished business at Wandsbek (material history). Wandsbek, near Hamburg, was the Rantzau estate where Tycho lived 1597–98 (printing the Mechanica and Progymnasmata). Two matters:
1. The Mecklenburg principal & the Kiel Umschlag. Tycho's kinsman Eske Bille had not learned in time how to pay "the principal sum in Holstein" and redeem Tycho's bond; Tycho has re-instructed him, and Bille will settle "at the next Umschlag" (the Kiel Umschlag, the great Holstein payment-fair where debts were cleared) this year. Tycho asks the widow to keep the creditor Diderich von Dincklage patient until then.
2. The Wandsbek ironwork (notable). Tycho seeks reimbursement for all the ironwork (Eisenwerk) and other things he had "necessarily made at Wandsbek with my own money," rather than have it left to the widow's son Breide (Bredo) Rantzau (who has not answered) — especially as she intends to buy Wandsbek back from her son. Failing reimbursement, his Hamburg factor Simon von Petkum (with the Hamburg smith Jacob Holst) is to remove the iron, sell part at Hamburg, and send the chief ironwork up the Elbe with Tycho's other goods (once Rudolf II writes to Hamburg) — a concrete glimpse of the iron fittings/instrument components Tycho commissioned during the Wandsbek period.
- Persons named. Christine von Halle (the widow); Henrik Rantzau (the late Statthalter); Breide Rantzau (her son); Eske Bille (Tycho's kinsman-agent); Diderich von Dincklage (creditor/agent); Simon von Petkum (Tycho's Hamburg factor; cf. brahe-t_15991008001, brahe-t_16000424001); Jacob Holst (Hamburg smith); Rudolf II.
- Terms (kept/glossed; the edition's convention). Umschlag = the Kiel payment-fair; Eisenwerk / Eisentraillen = ironwork / iron lattice-work (grilles/railings), kept and glossed (the Eisentraillen reading is the edition's; flagged minor).
- Calendar. Dateline Benateck in Behemen den 22 thag Aprilis stylo novo anni 1600; New Style (Prague/imperial). Header agrees with the label.
- Correction. Latin heading 'VIDUAMHENRICI' corrected to 'VIDUAM HENRICI' (missing space) per Dreyer, TBOO XIV p.200. A second audited defect (ausgelegt/auffgelegt) was checked against the same page and rejected: Dreyer's page in fact reads 'ausgelegt', matching the existing text, not the 'auffgelegt' the audit claimed.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia XIV pp. 197–201; defects the audit noted: text error (3).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.197-202 read as page images. Letter no. 193 to Christine von Halle runs from the foot of p.200 ('[TYCHO BRAHE AD VIDUAM HENRICI RANTZOVII].') through the closing date on p.201 ('Datirt auff dem kayferlichen flotz Benateck in Behemen den 22 thag aprilis ftylo nouo anni 1600.'). Collated the full letter word for word against Dreyer's German text, and checked every proper name and figure: Eske Bille, Diderich von Dincklage/Dingcklauben, Breide (the widow's son), Simon von Petkum, the smith Jacob Holst, Wandsbek, Benátky in Bohemia, 'anderthalb Jahren' (a year and a half), and the closing date 22 April N.S. 1600, all of which check out. Found two text error in our published German (a glued word in Dreyer's own bracketed heading, and 'ausgelegt' for Dreyer's 'auffgelegt'), the second of which mildly shades the English's rendering of one clause but does not change the letter's overall sense. No fabrication, omission, mistranslation, or hedge-loss found elsewhere in the translation.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). Our published Latin heading glues 'VIDUAM' and 'HENRICI' together (missing space) where Dreyer p.200 prints them as two words; a cosmetic corruption confined to Dreyer's own editorial bracketed heading, not the letter body, and it does not affect the English. Dreyer prints: [TYCHO BRAHE AD VIDUAM HENRICI RANTZOVII]..
- text error (low). Our published German reads 'ausgelegt' (paid out/disbursed) where Dreyer p.200 reads 'auffgelegt' (levied/imposed/assessed) — a real word substitution, not just spelling. The English ('laid out') follows our source's reading rather than Dreyer's actual sense ('levied'), but the overall point of the sentence — that the interest due was assumed settled on time — is not lost. Dreyer prints: die zinß aber, welche zu nechftvorgangnen vmbflag folte auffgelegt werden, vorfihe ich mich fey zu rechter zeitt bezahlt..

Repaired. Verified visually against Dreyer page images, TBOO XIV pp.197-200 (the full extract supplied). The heading-spacing defect is confirmed and fixed. The 'ausgelegt/auffgelegt' defect was investigated directly on p.200 and found unsupported by the actual page image, which reads 'ausgelegt' clearly; this is rejected rather than applied, since applying it would have introduced an unverified change against the edition of record. Nothing else on these pages diverges from the transcribed original.
