The original

HENRICUS BRUCÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

CUM tuus Typographus, Nobilissime Charissimeque TYCHO, Rostochium venit, abfui, domum vero rediens, literis tuis lectis pecuniam in scattula obsignatam aurigae Witebergensi triduo post tradidi, nolui enim resignare. Nihil itaque pecuniae reservatum, qua auriga satisfieret, aut libri aliqui emerentur. De motuum Solis et Lunae reliquorumque Planetarum emendatione, quae prae manibus habes, expecto ut in lucem prodeant. Ego certe in omnibus Eclipsibus crassis nostris observationibus videor animadvertisse, calculum PURBACHIJ propius accessisse ad tempus apparens, quam COPERNICI. Ad singula autem momenta motus Solis respondere ex tuis observationibus deductos, gaudeo. Idem si in ceteris praestiteris, bene de omnibus mereberis, et aeternam laudem apud posteros consequeris. Et ego ita sentio, tam multa, quae de anni inaequalitate referuntur, ex imperfectis vel falsis observationibus nata esse; motum enim illum coelestem ijsdem perpetuo metis includi, et tam operosis hypothesibus COPERNICI in motu Terrae astruendo non fuisse opus, quas falsas esse dubium non est, et forte non satisfaciunt etiam apparentijs evidentibus. Ob id desiderabam organum, quod mihi ob oculos obiceret totam illam COPERNICI imaginationem, quam ego, ut verum fatear, ex simplici illa delineatione nunquam plene assequi potui. Et quamvis tecum sentiam, non tam in ijs organis laborandum, quae alterius phantasias oculis obijciant, quam quae motus coelestes nobis aperiant: est tamen hominis eruditi, quique aliorum inventa velit explicare, haec intelligere, et quantum fieri potest, oculis obicere. Imo optandum esset, posse sine ullis hypothesibus motus coelestes tradi. Quod ut forte fieri queat, apparentijs tamen satisfieri nunquam poterit. Hypotyposes PROCLI ob nostri Typographi negligentiam non excuduntur. Extant autem, magnae constructioni CL. PTOLEMÆI adiunctae, verum ita corrupte editae, ut autorem suum non agnoscant. Editae sunt alibi opera GEORGII VALLAe Placentini, ijs credo ab eodem autore tralatas Hypotyposes esse adiectas. Quod si hoc opus VALLAe habeas, velim ad me mitti, quo tantum percurram hanc Hypotyposium translationem, remissurus quam primum. Mitto IOSEPHUM SCALIGERUM de emendatione temporum et MÆSTLINI de emendatione Calendarij. MAUROLYCUM hoc tempore, ut neque Gratiopolitanum consequi potui. Si quid in posterum nanciscar, ad te mittam. Quod si velis prius tibi precia perscribi, id quoque facturus sum. Hic tuus servus dum aliquot diebus hîc navem, quae solvat in Daniam, expectat, consumsit solidos 32. Et typos nescio quos maioris notae emit, de quibus mecum actum ut precium penderem. Verum cum huius rei nulla in tuis literis facta sit mentio, et ignorem, utrum hoc tuo iussu factum sit, nihil a me persolutum. Ex ipso plene omnia de typis intelliges.

Bene vale.
Rostochij 12. Iunij Anno Domini 1584.
HENRICUS BRUCÆUS.

Heinrich Brucaeus to Tycho Brahe.

When your printer came to Rostock, most noble and most dear Tycho, I was away; but on returning home, having read your letter, I handed over the money — sealed up in a packet — to the Wittenberg carter three days later, for I did not wish to break the seal. And so no money was held back with which the carter might be paid or any books bought. As for the correction of the motions of the Sun and Moon and of the rest of the planets, which you have in hand, I await their coming forth into the light. For my part, in all the eclipses I have certainly seemed to observe, from our rough observations, that Peurbach's calculation came closer to the apparent time than Copernicus's. And I rejoice that the [positions] derived from your observations answer to the single moments of the Sun's motion. If you shall achieve the same in the rest, you will deserve well of all men, and win everlasting praise among posterity. And this too is my opinion: that the many things which are reported concerning the inequality of the year have arisen from imperfect or false observations; for that celestial motion is enclosed perpetually within the same bounds, and there was no need of Copernicus's so laborious hypotheses in establishing the motion of the Earth — which it is beyond doubt are false, and perhaps do not satisfy even the evident appearances. On that account I desired an instrument that might set before my eyes that whole imagining of Copernicus, which I — to confess the truth — could never fully grasp from that bare diagram. And although I am of one mind with you that one should labour not so much over those instruments which set another's fancies before the eyes, as over those which lay open the celestial motions to us, yet it belongs to a learned man, and to one who would wish to expound the inventions of others, to understand these things and, so far as may be done, to set them before the eyes. Nay, it were to be wished that the celestial motions could be transmitted without any hypotheses at all. But even if that could perhaps be done, the appearances will nevertheless never be able to be satisfied. The Hypotyposes of Proclus are not being printed, owing to the negligence of our printer. They are extant, however, appended to the great construction [Almagest] of the renowned Ptolemy, but so corruptly edited that they do not acknowledge their own author. They were published elsewhere in the works of Giorgio Valla of Piacenza, to which, I believe, the Hypotyposes — translated by the same author — were added. So if you have this work of Valla's, I should like it sent to me, that I might just run through this translation of the Hypotyposes, intending to send it back at the first opportunity. I send Joseph Scaliger's On the Emendation of the Times, and Maestlin's On the Emendation of the Calendar. At this time I have not been able to obtain Maurolico, any more than the Gratiopolitanus [the man / work of Grenoble?]. If I light upon anything hereafter, I will send it to you. And if you would first have the prices written out for you, that too I shall do. This servant of yours, while waiting here some days for a ship to set sail for Denmark, has spent 32 shillings. And he has bought some types or other of a larger sort, concerning which it was settled with me that I should pay the price. But since no mention of this matter was made in your letter, and I do not know whether it was done at your bidding, nothing has been paid out by me. From the man himself you will learn everything about the types in full.

Fare you well.
Rostock, 12 June, in the Year of the Lord 1584.
Heinrich Brucaeus.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Hendrik van den Broeucke, 1531–1598), Flemish-born professor of medicine and (effectively) of astronomy/mathematics at the University of Rostock, and an established correspondent of Tycho's. Mosley (Bearing the Heavens, p. 180) cites this very passage: Tycho, "wanting a Latin copy" of Proclus, had asked Brucaeus for help, and Brucaeus "could only provide … some vague bibliographical help" about Valla's edition.
  • Place. Rostochium / Rostochij = Rostock.
  • The printer / "your servant." tuus Typographus … Hic tuus servus — Tycho's own printer/agent, then travelling between Rostock and Hven, ferrying money, books, and printing type. aurigae Witebergensi = "the Wittenberg carter" (the carrier on the Rostock–Wittenberg run). The closing transaction (32 shillings spent; typos … maioris notae, "types of a larger sort," i.e. a larger type-body, bought on uncertain authority) belongs to the practical business of equipping Tycho's Uraniborg press.
  • solidos 32 — "32 shillings" (solidus, the period accounting/coin unit); kept as "shillings."
  • Astronomy — the technical core.
  • De motuum Solis et Lunae reliquorumque Planetarum emendatione — "the correction of the motions of the Sun and Moon and the rest of the planets," Tycho's restorative programme (emendatio/restitutio; the edition's convention, restoration).
  • calculum PURBACHIJ … quam COPERNICI — Brucaeus reports that Peurbach's calculation (Georg Peurbach, 1423–1461, the Theoricae novae planetarum / Ptolemaic tradition) matched the tempus apparens of eclipses better than Copernicus's. Calculus = "calculation/computation"; tempus apparens = "the apparent time" (kept).
  • anni inaequalitate — "the inequality of the year": the supposed variation in the length of the (tropical/solar) year much debated in the calendar-reform literature; Brucaeus judges the reported variation an artefact of "imperfect or false observations" and holds the celestial motion to be "enclosed perpetually within the same bounds."
  • operosis hypothesibus COPERNICI in motu Terrae astruendo — "Copernicus's so laborious hypotheses in establishing the motion of the Earth." Brucaeus's anti-Copernican stance is stated as his own opinion and is preserved as such (no sharpening): he calls them "beyond doubt false" and says they "perhaps" (forte) fail even to save the appearances — the hedge forte is kept.
  • organum … imaginationem … delineatione — organum = instrument; here Brucaeus wishes for an instrument (a planetary model/sphere) that would render visible "that whole imagining (imaginatio) of Copernicus," which he could not grasp from "that bare diagram (delineatio)." imaginatio rendered "imagining" (the mental construct/representation), not modernised to "model."
  • organis … quae alterius phantasias oculis obijciant … quam quae motus coelestes nobis aperiant — the contrast (kept verbatim in sense) between instruments that "set another's fancies before the eyes" and those that "lay open the celestial motions": Brucaeus agrees with Tycho that the latter matter more, yet defends the former's didactic use.
  • sine ullis hypothesibus motus coelestes tradi … apparentijs … satisfieri — "to transmit the celestial motions without any hypotheses" / "to satisfy the appearances" (satisfacere apparentiis = the sōzein ta phainomena topos). Rendered literally; the pessimistic conclusion ("even if it could be done, the appearances will never be able to be satisfied [thereby]") is preserved with its difficulty intact (see crux below).
  • Books named.
  • Hypotyposes PROCLI — Proclus, Hypotyposis astronomicarum positionum (the elementary exposition of Ptolemaic planetary theory). Hypotyposes kept as a title-word.
  • magnae constructioni CL. PTOLEMAeI adiunctae — "appended to the great construction of the renowned Ptolemy": magna constructio = the Almagest (Greek Megalē syntaxis / Megistē); CL. = Clarissimi ("the renowned/most illustrious"), expanded. Brucaeus notes the appended Hypotyposes there are "so corruptly edited that they do not acknowledge their own author" (i.e. the print is so bad the text is barely recognisable as Proclus's).
  • opera GEORGII VALLAe Placentini — the works of Giorgio Valla of Piacenza (Placentinus = of Piacenza), in whose collection (De expetendis et fugiendis rebus, 1501) a Latin Proclus translation appears; Brucaeus believes the Hypotyposes there were "translated by the same author" (Valla).
  • IOSEPHUM SCALIGERUM de emendatione temporum — Joseph Justus Scaliger, De emendatione temporum (Paris, 1583); a cross-check (Gingerich, Annotated Census) confirms the 1583 Paris edition. Mitto … Scaligerum = "I send [the book of] Scaliger," a common metonymy (the author's name for his book); rendered "I send Joseph Scaliger's …."
  • MAeSTLINI de emendatione Calendarij — Michael Maestlin, De emendatione Calendarii (a tract against the Gregorian calendar reform). Maestlini genitive; rendered "Maestlin's …."
  • MAUROLYCUM — Francesco Maurolico (Maurolyco, 1494–1575) of Messina, mathematician; here the author's name stands for a book of his that Brucaeus could not obtain.
  • Latin formulae. Bene vale = "Fare you well"; Anno Domini = "in the Year of the Lord."
  • Calendar / dateline. Rostochij 12. Iunij Anno Domini 1584 = "Rostock, 12 June 1584." Old Style: a Protestant correspondent in Lutheran Rostock in 1584 wrote Julian. The edition label ("1584. 12. juni. Rostock") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. No silent conversion made.
  • Cruxes.
  • CRUX (Gratiopolitanum). MAUROLYCUM hoc tempore, ut neque Gratiopolitanum consequi potui — "I could not obtain Maurolico at this time, any more than the Gratiopolitanum." Gratiopolitanum (var. Gratianopolitanum) is the Latin adjective for Grenoble (Gratianopolis); as a substantive accusative it parallels Maurolycum and so denotes a person or (more likely) a book "of/from Grenoble" that Brucaeus was likewise trying to procure for Tycho. The precise referent is not determinable from the letter and is not resolved here; "the Gratiopolitanus" is left flagged rather than guessed. This drives the overall grade.
  • Syntactic crux (closing of the hypothesis sentence). Imo optandum esset, posse sine ullis hypothesibus motus coelestes tradi. Quod ut forte fieri queat, apparentijs tamen satisfieri nunquam poterit. The second sentence is logically taut: literally "Although this could perhaps be done, yet the appearances will never be able to be satisfied." The natural reading — adopted above — is concessive ("even granting that hypothesis-free transmission were possible, the phenomena could never be fully saved by it"), i.e. a pessimistic rider to the wish. An alternative reads Quod ut … queat as final-purpose ("in order that this might perhaps come to pass"). The concessive reading is given; the alternative is recorded rather than suppressed.
  • Correction. Latin 'in scapula obsignatam' corrected to 'in scattula obsignatam' per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.85.

The translation sidesteps the corruption with the generic 'packet' rather than reproducing either word literally. Dreyer prints: pecuniam in fcattula obfignatam aurigae Witebergenfi triduo poft tradidi (TBOO VII p.85).
