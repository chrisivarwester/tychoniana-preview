The original

THYGE BRAHE FICK BREFF OM THEND LYCHTE PAA KULDEN ANRØRENDIS.

WOR gunst tilforn. Wiid, att som thu haffuer thend løchte paa Kulden mett huis ther till er forordnitt wdj befalling, saa wille Wij thig naadigst icke forholde, att offte och jdelig klager kommer for Oß aff thend søefarnne mand ther wdj fundit, att samme løchte icke tilbørlig holdis med lius och bluß, och kunde Oß ther offuer icke nogsom forundre, epther att Wij Oß kunde erind[r]e, att thu synderligen haffuer belangitt hoß Oß, att om en ny bequemlige sted, paa huilcken samme lychte kunde bedre standis thend søefaren mand til beste, motte forfaris, och ther om werffuit Wor befallingskriffuelße til Wore gode mend och til tholderne ther sammestedtz, huor aff Wij kunde haffue att formode thin synderlig bewagenhet emod thend søefaren mand och flid til for:ne løchte att holde, huilcke Wore breffue Wij dog forfare icke att were eptherkommen och till affskeed beforderit, end mindre thend gamble løchte, som nu er, siden thend thid att were holdit som thet sig bør, huorfore Wij ere fororsagit thig ther om her mett att belange. Bedendis thig och wille, att thu er forthencktt vdj samme løchte saa att lade holde med liuß och bluß som thet er tilbørligt, paa thet att thend søefaren mand icke, forgefflig sig ther paa forladendis, schulle liide godtz och liffuis fare, skade och forderuelße, saa framt sig nogen ther offuer her epther tilbørligen kand beklage, thu tha icke ther fore wilt stande til rette och Wij fororsagis om samme løchte anderledis att forordne till andre, som Wor befalling och thend søefarne mandz leylighed will bedre bethencke.

Ther meth etc.
Actum Kroneborg 26. martij aar 1585.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tyge Brahe received a letter concerning the beacon on Kullen.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, inasmuch as you hold the beacon on Kullen, together with whatever is appointed thereto, in [your] charge, we will not graciously withhold from you that frequent and continual complaints come before us from the seafaring man, who finds therein that the same beacon is not duly kept with light and flame; and we cannot sufficiently wonder at it, since we are able to call to mind that you in particular had made suit to us that a new, more convenient site — on which the same beacon might be better maintained, to the seafaring man's advantage — should be sought out, and concerning it had procured our letter of command to our good men and to the toll-officers there in that same place, whereby we had cause to expect your particular goodwill towards the seafaring man and your diligence in keeping the aforesaid beacon; which letters of ours we nonetheless learn to have neither been complied with nor carried into effect, much less the old beacon, such as it now is, to have been kept since that time as is fitting — wherefore we are compelled hereby to address you concerning it. Asking you, and willing it, that you be mindful of the same beacon, so as to have it kept with light and flame as is fitting, to the end that the seafaring man, relying upon it in vain, should not suffer peril to goods and to life, harm and ruin: so that, in so far as anyone can hereafter rightfully complain about this matter, you will then not be willing to answer for it [= you will not [be able to] avoid answering for it], and we shall be compelled to appoint the same beacon otherwise, to others, as our charge and the seafaring man's circumstance shall the better warrant.

Therewith, etc.
Done at Kronborg, 26 March, year 1585.

Apparatus

- Issuer. Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), Tycho's royal patron, who in 1576 had granted him the island of Hven. Among Tycho's crown emoluments was the Kullen beacon-light (Kullen fyr), held in fief with its light-dues (fyrpenge) in return for the duty of keeping the light burning for shipping. The grant was a recurring point of friction: Tycho was admonished more than once for neglect, and the king eventually withheld part of the beacon-money over the lapse (cf. the related crown letter on the fyr-penge in Dreyer, Opera Omnia vol. XIV). The present letter is one such reprimand.
- Recipient / addressee form. THYGE BRAHE = Tyge Brahe, the Danish vernacular form of Tycho's name (the Latinised Tycho is the international one). The king addresses him throughout in the familiar 2nd-person singular (thu / thig) — the master-to-servant register of a royal command, not the deferential plural.
- Place / object. thend løchte paa Kulden = "the beacon on Kullen" — the beacon on Kullaberg (the Kullen headland in north-west Skåne, then Danish; modern Kullaberg/Kullen, Sweden), at the entrance to the Øresund. løchte / lychte (mod. Da. lygte) here denotes the navigational beacon-light, i.e. the lighthouse establishment, not a hand-lantern. lius och bluß (mod. lys og blus) = "light and flame" (bluß = the open coal-/wood-blaze of an early beacon). Rendered "beacon … light and flame" throughout.
- Document type. A chancery-register entry (Skånske Tegnelser). The Latin term ANRØRENDIS in the heading is the Danish-Latin chancery participle anrørendes ("concerning"); the heading is otherwise Danish, and the closing register-formula Actum ("Done/Given at") is the conventional Latin chancery tag — both preserved by the edition's convention/. The heuristic da tag is reading-confirmed.
- The petition referred to. thu synderligen haffuer belangitt hoß Oß, att om en ny bequemlige sted … motte forfaris — Tycho had himself earlier petitioned the king to have a new, more convenient site for the beacon sought out (forfaris = "be investigated / sought out"), and had procured a royal befallingskriffuelße ("letter of command") to the local crown officers (Wore gode mend) and toll-officers (tholderne, the Øresund customs men). The king's reproach is that those very letters went unexecuted while the old light was let lapse.
- Negations (legal force — verified count). The body carries five operative negations, each load-bearing; reproduced one-for-one:
1. saa wille Wij thig naadigst icke forholde — "we will not graciously withhold from you," a litotes meaning "we must inform you."
2. samme løchte icke tilbørlig holdis — "the same beacon is not duly kept."
3. kunde Oß ther offuer icke nogsom forundre — "we cannot sufficiently wonder at it" (icke … nogsom = "not … sufficiently").
4. huilcke Wore breffue Wij dog forfare icke att were eptherkommen — "which letters of ours we nonetheless learn to have not been complied with." (Rendered as the English correlative "neither … nor" across eptherkommen … och till affskeed beforderit; the single Danish icke governs both members.)
5. paa thet att thend søefaren mand icke … schulle liide … fare — "so that the seafaring man should not suffer … peril."
A sixth icke stands in the final conditional clause (see crux). No negation is added anywhere — in particular the king's threatened action Wij fororsagis … att forordne till andre ("we shall be compelled to appoint … to others") is affirmative, the substance of the threat, and is rendered without any inserted "not."
- Crux — the closing conditional / threat. saa framt sig nogen ther offuer her epther tilbørligen kand beklage, thu tha icke ther fore wilt stande til rette och Wij fororsagis om samme løchte anderledis att forordne till andre. This is a standard Danish chancery warning, but its exact logical mood is genuinely ambiguous and is flagged, not smoothed. Literally: "in so far as / if anyone can hereafter rightfully complain about this matter, [that] you will then not be willing to answer for it [stande til rette = stand to law, be held to account], and [that] we be compelled to appoint the same beacon otherwise, to others." Two readings are defensible — (a) a consequence clause: should neglect persist and a complaint arise, Tycho will fail/refuse to answer for it and the king will therefore reassign the beacon; (b) a standalone conditional threat: should anyone rightfully complain, Tycho will not [be able to] avoid answering for it, and the king will reassign the beacon. The crux is the force of icke … wilt stande til rette: "will not be willing to answer for it" can mean either "will refuse / fail to answer" or, idiomatically, "will not [be able to] escape answering." The translation keeps the literal wording plus a bracketed gloss and does not collapse the ambiguity into one fluent threat. The king's reassigning the beacon (fororsagis … att forordne till andre) is affirmative and certain in either reading.
- Secondary crux (milder). thend gamble løchte, som nu er admits two readings: "the old beacon, such as it now is" (i.e. in its present neglected state) or "the old beacon, [the one] that now exists" (the existing one, as against a proposed new one). The translation keeps the former, which the surrounding sense favours, but the phrasing is genuinely double.
- Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1585). WOR gunst tilforn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand,"); Wiid = Vid ("know," imperative); huis ther till er forordnitt = hvis dertil er forordnet ("what is appointed thereto"); wdj befalling = udi befaling ("in [one's] charge/commission"); jdelig = idelig ("continual"); erind[r]e — the register drops the r, supplied in brackets = erindre ("call to mind"); belangitt / belange = belange (chancery "to make suit to / address a matter to someone"); motte forfaris = måtte forfares ("might be sought out/investigated"); werffuit = hvervet/værvet ("procured, obtained"); tholderne = toldnerne ("the toll-officers"); bewagenhet = bevågenhed ("goodwill, favourable disposition"); eptherkommen = efterkommet ("complied with"); till affskeed beforderit = til afsked befordret ("carried into effect / brought to a conclusion"); fiden thend thid = siden den tid ("since that time" — printed fiden is a long-s siden, the classic ſ→f trap); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); forthencktt = betænkt/fortænkt ("mindful, take thought"); forgefflig sig ther paa forladendis = forgæves sig derpå forladendes ("relying upon it in vain"); liffuis fare = livsfare ("peril of life"); forderuelße = fordærvelse ("ruin"); saa framt = så fremt ("in so far as / if"); stande til rette = "stand to law, answer at law"; leylighed = lejlighed ("circumstance, occasion, need").
- Calendar / dating crux (flagged, not reconciled). The body dateline reads "Actum Kroneborg 26. martij aar 1585" = "Done at Kronborg, 26 March 1585" (O.S.). The edition's metadata header and label, however, calendar the letter as "1585. 26. maj / 26 May, Kronborg" (and so the identifier …15850526…). The two disagree on the month (March vs. May) by exactly two months. Per the edition's convention the discrepancy is recorded, not silently resolved: the header above leads with the as-written dateline date (26 March) and appends the catalogued date. The likeliest source of the divergence is a martij↔maij (Latin Martii "March" / Maii "May") cataloguing slip, but the witness register reads martij and the source does not decide it. The identifier retains the catalogue date for edition continuity. No conversion to New Style is made (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700).
- Correction. Corrected 'fiden' to 'siden' (a long-s/f misread) in the beacon-neglect clause; Dreyer's page (TBOO XIV p.28) uses the same long-s glyph here as in the adjacent 'som'/'sig'. Translation already correctly rendered 'since' and is unaffected.

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Read Dreyer TBOO XIV p.28 as page image (letter = item 43, entirely on p.28); re-read at close zoom and compared the disputed glyph against the unambiguous long-s letterforms 'ſom' and 'ſig' in the same line to confirm the word is 'ſiden' (siden), not 'fiden'. Aside from this one corrupted word, the transcribed original matches Dreyer verbatim, and the full translation (the neglected Kullen beacon, the prior request for a better site, the warning that the duty may be reassigned) is faithful throughout. Per the grading rule, any text error caps the grade at C even though the translation itself is unaffected.

1 defect found:
