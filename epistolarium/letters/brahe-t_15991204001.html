The original

MAGNIFICO DOMINO, VIRTUTE, ERUDITIONE ET GENERE NOBILISSIMO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE URANIBURGICAe BENACHIAe IN BOHEMIA PRÆSIDI, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO OBSERVANDO.
S. P. D.

MAGNIFICE Domine TYCHO, Domine et amice observande. Verum est quod reversus ex Legatione Anglica binas tuas literas domi repererim, sed quomodo ijs respondere potuerim per tabellarium Witebergensem, mihi non constat. Nam in literis tuis est, te ad festum Pentecostes iturum vocatu Caesaris Pragam. Ego autem, post Dominicam Trinitatis huc appuli; itaque non est quod suspiceris hoc literarum officium intermissum aliquo metu potentiorum, quibus iam pluribus indicijs innotuit mea in amicitia sinceritas et constantia, quam ut novam aliquam offensam mihi pariat. Neque mihi de moribus Anglorum aliquid adhaesit eorum diuturniore convictu et usu, sed tam longe ab ijs vitijs absum, quam te vellem longe abesse a perstringendo et mordendo per iocum et ludum innoxios et fidos tuos amicos: in quorum numero ita me ponas, ut qui nihil unquam passus sim studio et observantiae decedere, etsi fortean non usquequaque tibi officio meo satisfecerim. Res tuas, mi Domine TYCHO, cognovi abunde ex literis tum mihi tum ad ROSÆCRANTIUM datis, magnoque mihi gaudio est eo loco esse, ut tibi tuisque bene sit, parta quiete et invento Maecenate, qui tam munifice tuis studijs et honorifice consultum voluerit. Nec est quisquam eorum, quos tu magnifacis, qui tuae fortunae lingua animoque plus faveat, quam ego, invidiae eo nomine haud expers. Cogor enim aliquando propugnare huius rei veritatem contra eos, qui tuis laudibus nulla unquam a te affecti iniuria obtrectant et tua merita perperam aestimando infra istam fortunam deijciunt. Ac metuebam ab aliquibus male, cum intellexissem huc TYCHONIDEM tuum cum MULÆNO nostro missos ad expetenda instrumenta residua: sed Dei provisu omnes conticuere, nec quisquam contra mutire ausus. Iuvi ego ipsos, quantum in me fuit et res sinebat, auctorque fui, ne literas tuas exhiberent ijs, a quibus sincere auxilium sperari non poterat. Nam aliquando venere quaedam in oblivionem, quae nos ipsos praestat quoque tacita habere, quam dubiam operam aliorum periclitari. Vergit enim quorundam aetas ad extrema, et tamen in sordibus rerum caducarum cogitationes omnes defigimus parum aut nihil de aeternis illis solliciti. Neque dormientes neque vigilantes somniamus aut meditamur aliud quam pecuniam, cui gloriam, honorem et quidquid laudabile est, postponimus. Addimus aliquid de humana severitate (non dicam crudelitate) cuius tristia recens visa in duobus nobilibus et tribus plebeis alijsque exempla. Merito igitur habent quod laetentur, quibus aut favor aulicus hîc affulget, aut res suas ita pararunt, ut migrandi taedium non expavescant.

Mihi, Deo sit gloria, nihil adversi admodum adhuc contigit. Cancellario probantur, quae a me hac legatione gesta sunt omnia, qui vidit, ad fidem et industriam me nihil reliquum fecisse, sed alijs non item, nescio qua de causa. MULÆNUM nostrum quod provehere ad Medicen promittis, facis ut tua virtute et humanitate dignum, nec puto te habuisse vel habiturum unquam, qui te sinceriore animo colat et observet. Verum metuo valde, ne longior apud nos mora ab Italica profectione per hanc hiemem retinuerit. Sed profecto faciendum ita fuit, nec poterunt citius tua negotia expedire. Causas ipsi tibi reddant, quas veras esse non dubitaveris. Quia vero id iter non successit, alia ratione forte tibi fuerit consilium eius iuvandi invenire. Si animus est, ut illum a te dimissurus unquam omni modo, fiat bona cum gratia et aetati mature consulatur. Omnino, si tibi eum non gratum et obsequentem esse voluissemus, alia fuisset ratione prospectum, et prospicietur, Deo propitio, si eum missum feceris. Sin tibi et tuis placet, habebis eum. Cum voluptate audio TEGNALIO tantum beneficij et honoris a te et per te praestari, optoque, ut deinceps sit, qualem tu esse cupis. TYPOTIUS amicus meus unde habuerit, quod me impulsore patriam deserueris, conijcere non possum. Quicunque hoc comminisci ausus est, nec te novit nec intelligit, quid doloris apportet a fidelissimi amici convictu et mutuo adspectu tanta locum intercapedine disiungi. Sed Dominus TYPOTIUS, si recte memini, ipse a me audivit deplorari abitum tuum, tum patriae, tum mea causa, nedum meo sensu hoc factum credere possit, cui eam suspicionem animo exemptam cupio. De libro publicato, quod ad te scripserat ESCHYLUS BILLEUS, nihil mihi exaudire contigit, nec operae pretium te facturum existimo, si tale aliquod procuderes, qui melius horas tuas collocare potes, et verum est quod addis, multos absque tua voluntate eo te labore exonerare. Quaere a TYCHONIDE, quid filius Magistri IOHANNIS MICHAELIJ in mensa patris narravit. Miraberis, sat scio, iuxta atque nos dolemus. Quid mihi etiam in Anglia et Belgio exprobratum, piget referre. Quod de DUNCANO me scribere vis, fateor, quod res est, me non libenter doctorum controversijs velle immisceri, aut ijs nomen meum inseri. Ac fortean in maiore gloria Hypotheseos istius inventio erit, si simpliciter edito sub tuo nomine scripto illustraveris et explicaveris, quam si contentionibus obscuraveris, quod quidem factum non ignoro in Progymnasmatibus, hoc ipso consilio, ne quis eam sibi deinceps vendicaret; sed vellem hoc peculiari scripto fieri, non ita prolixo, sed quod in scholis proponi posset. Tum vix futurum amplius puto, ut quisquam tuae amplius laudi decerpat. DUNKANUS certe quicquid tandem privatim egerit, publice nunquam eam sibi adrogabit, qui ne per somnium quidem de ea cogitavit, antequam ex te hoc tanquam mysterium cognosceret. Quod simul ac patefecisses, memini a te dictum, plus ei communicatum, quam frater fratri edisserere fortean vellet. Atque reperi in paucis hisce diebus inter schedas meas chartam, qua mihi petenti eam signasti, sive ante DUNKANI adventum, sive quo tempore apud te fuit, incertus sum. Sed mi Domine TYCHO, patere te hoc ab amico moneri, ut polemica omnia seponas, et per quae coepisti, vestigia incedas. Nunquam aliquis magnus contentionibus fuit. Semper aliqui sunt, qui iniquae parti etiam favent, nec quicquam tam solide unquam refutatur, quin adversarius inveniat, quod probabiliter oppugnet, et iudicia hominum ex verbis capiuntur, quia res istae subtiles a paucis intelliguntur. Ac ne per alios quidem hoc fieri vellem, nam ijs etiam respondebitur, ac per eorum latus in te tela dirigentur. Inimici et invidi habebunt ex scommatibus, quod rideant et gaudeant, sicuti iam factum in scurrili quodam scripto URSI novisti. Itaque cum CHRISTIANUS tuus ad te redeat, cuius tibi magnus usus esse poterit, pertexe eam telam quam feliciter orsus es, et cude nobis novum coelum denascente natura, et fatiscente industria hominum. Hac via ad immortalitatem homines grassantur.

De honoribus publicis quod est in litteris Rosaekrantianis, et feudo Imperij, mea sententia est, quod prius te vellem in possessionem feudi venire, antequam honores illos ambires, aut oblatos acceptares, alias fortean titulus tibi pro re sufficiet, et imputabitur honor pro beneficio reali. Simul ac vero consecutus es feudum promissum, tunc nollem te honores illos aspernari. Liberis tuis ita melius consultum erit, quorum TYCHO tuus ea est aetate, ut e re foret exteros iam visere, et cupit hoc ipse magnopere. Non dubium, quin ille ingenij bonitate et mansuetudine amicos multos sibi parare possit, sed auctoritas et existimatio ex literis et peregrinatione quaerenda ipsi est. De reliquis etiam non dubito te sollicitum esse, non solum ut haereditatem, sed etiam ut nobilitatem et integra omnia status politici relinquas. Haec ita accipe ut profecta ab eo animo, qui tibi tuisque sincere bene vult. Si praesens essem, plura de his; nam per calamum non ita licet omnia, neque fieri potest, ut quae meditata nobis sunt, veniant in mentem universa, cum pennam movemus. CRAGIUS tuus antiquum obtinet, ita et tu ne desinas TYCHO esse, qui fuisti.

De statu rerum nostrarum pleraque referre TYCHONIDES poterit. Publice nobis pax adhuc est, sed privatim multi male contenti. Variae sunt offensiones multorum de hoc statu rerum, et pauci sunt, qui non male ominantur. Amicitia Anglica ex tenui apta est. Cum CAROLO Sueco neque satis convenit, quia per absentiam Regis naves eius ingressae mare, quod iuris Danici. Apprehenderunt Lubecenses et alios. Adornantur Legati eo mittendi ab reditu PETRI FRANCISCI, quia viam paraturus modo in Sueciam est. Nos arces et oppida in Norvegia et Scania, Hallandia, Blekingia ad fines munimus et reparamus quantum possumus, sed a pecunia non satis instructi sumus. Rex Poloniae misit classem suam cum milite per fretum Oritanum, quae naufragium passa est, nec in Sueciam appulit. CAROLUS iam potitur fere toto regno, postquam hac aestate Finlandiam domuit, et partes illas Livoniae vicinas. De nobilibus captivis in Suecia, qui sunt praecipui ERICUS SPARRE, GUSTAVUS BANNER, ERICUS BANNER, TUROV BIELCKE et GEORGIUS POSE, constans fama supplicium sumendum. Miserunt huc ad Regem petentes eius intercessionem. Exigua spes Polono superest recuperandi regni hereditarij. Non obscurum, quid CAROLUS moliatur, etsi aliud prae se ferat.

De matre tua TYCHONIDES referre poterit, quae vidit et audivit. Amita valde infirma est, et oculi caligaverunt. Quid vero soror agat, et ubi iam sit, ex eius litteris accipies. Faxit aeternus pater, ut sperato tandem coniugio cum gaudio potiatur. Nos ea audivimus de rebus Domini LANGIJ, quae erigunt animos ad expectationem melioris fortunae. De morte IACOBI SEEFELD puto ab alijs ad te perscriptum. Vix duabus vel tribus septimanis a nuptijs duarum filiarum, quae uno die celebratae, in fine comitiorum insperato diem suum clausit. Serenissimus Rex modo in Iutia est, dicitur tamen CHRISTI natalia Haffniae celebrare velle. Utinam te per idem tempus videre tunc possemus. Posses tu gaudia nostra multis modis augere, sed spero nos te brevi visuros. Nec enim committes, quin ad nos aliquando excurras saltem exiguo comitatu, si non omnes adducas. Desiderio isto valde flagro, quod tu ut expleres, tuo cum bono forte fuerit. Iam si tibi prolixitate satisfeci, gaudeo. Si molestiam et taedium ingessi, ipse in causa es, qui ita requisivisti. Utut sit, rogo omnia pro candore tuo boni consulas, et quod occupatus haec sine cura exaraverim. Vale cum coniuge et liberis felicissime, quibus omnibus ita cupio, ut mihi ipsi bene esse velim. Salutat te uxor mea et frater eius LAURENTIUS et parentes. Item MICHAEL Chirurgus, alijque amici nonnulli, nominatim GABRIEL SPARRE et PETRUS BRAHE.

Iterum vale.
Dabantur Haffniae pridie Nonas Decembres anno 1599.
T. T.
NIC. CRAGIUS.

To the magnificent Lord, most noble in virtue, erudition, and birth, Lord Tycho Brahe, Governor of the Uraniborg of Benátky in Bohemia, his Lord and friend to be observed.
Heartiest greeting.

Magnificent Lord Tycho, Lord and friend to be observed. It is true that, returned from the English Legation, I found your two letters at home; but how I could have answered them by the Wittenberg courier, I do not see. For in your letters it stands that you would go to Prague at the feast of Pentecost, by the Emperor's summons; but I arrived here after Trinity Sunday. So there is no [cause] that you should suspect this duty of letters was omitted from any fear of the more powerful — to whom my sincerity and constancy in friendship is now known by many signs, [too much] for it to breed me some new offence. Nor did anything of the English manners cleave to me from a longer association and use of them; but I am as far from those vices as I should wish you far from carping and biting, through jest and play, your innocent and faithful friends — among whose number place me thus: as one who never let study and observance fail, even if perhaps I have not in all things satisfied you with my service. Your affairs, my Lord Tycho, I learned abundantly from the letters given both to me and to Rosenkrantz; and it is great joy to me that you are in such a place that it may be well with you and yours, peace gained and a Maecenas found, who so munificently and honourably wished your studies provided for. Nor is there any of those you esteem who favours your fortune, with tongue and mind, more than I — not free, on that account, of envy. For I am sometimes forced to defend the truth of this against those who detract from your praises (never harmed by you in any injury), and, wrongly estimating your merits, cast them below that fortune. And I feared ill from some, when I had understood that your Tychonides [Tycho the Younger], with our Mule, were sent here to seek the remaining instruments; but by God's providence all kept silent, and none dared mutter against [it]. I myself helped them, as far as was in me and the matter allowed, and was the author [of advice] that they not show your letters to those from whom sincere help could not be hoped. For sometimes certain things have come into oblivion which it is better for us too to keep silent, than to hazard the doubtful aid of others. For the age of some men inclines to its extremity; and yet we fix all our thoughts in the filth of perishable things, little or not at all anxious about those eternal ones. Neither sleeping nor waking do we dream or meditate anything but money, to which we postpone glory, honour, and whatever is praiseworthy. We add something of human severity (I will not say cruelty), of which sad examples were lately seen in two nobles and three commoners and others. Rightly, then, have they cause to rejoice for whom either court favour shines here, or who have so prepared their affairs that they do not dread the tedium of migrating.

To me, glory be to God, nothing very adverse has yet befallen. By the Chancellor all that I did in this legation is approved — who saw that I left nothing wanting in fidelity and industry; but not so by others, for I know not what cause. That you promise to advance our Mule to Medicine, you do as worthy of your virtue and humanity; nor do I think you have had, or will ever have, one who cultivates and observes you with a sincerer mind. But I much fear that a longer stay with us has held [him] back from the Italian journey through this winter. But assuredly it had to be done so, nor could they expedite your affairs sooner. Let them render you the causes themselves, which you will not doubt are true. Since, however, that journey has not succeeded, perhaps by another method you will find counsel for helping him. If [your] mind is ever, in any way, to dismiss him from you, let it be done with good grace, and his age provided for maturely. Altogether, had we not wished him to be agreeable and obedient to you, it would have been provided otherwise — and will be provided (God favouring), if you dismiss him. But if he pleases you and yours, you will have him. With pleasure I hear so much benefit and honour rendered to Tengnagel by you and through you, and I wish that hereafter he may be such as you wish. Whence my friend Typotius got [the notion] that you deserted the fatherland at my instigation, I cannot guess. Whoever dared invent this neither knows you, nor understands what grief it brings to be sundered, by so great an interval of place, from the company and mutual sight of a most faithful friend. But Lord Typotius, if I remember rightly, himself heard from me your departure lamented, both for the fatherland's sake and for mine — much less, then, could he believe this done by my [will] — from whom I wish that suspicion removed from his mind. About the published book of which Eske Bille [Eschylus Billeus] had written you, I happened to hear nothing; nor do I think you would do worthwhile to forge any such [reply], you who can better place your hours; and it is true, as you add, that many would relieve you of that labour without your will. Ask Tychonides what the son of Master Johannes Michaelius narrated at his father's table. You will wonder, I know well, even as we grieve. What was reproached to me, too, in England and Belgium [Holland], it irks me to report.

As to what you wish me to write about Duncan: I confess (as is the case) that I do not gladly wish to be mixed into the controversies of the learned, nor my name inserted in them. And perhaps the invention of that Hypothesis will be in greater glory if you simply illustrate and explain it, in a writing published under your name, than if you obscure it with contentions — which indeed I know was done in the Progymnasmata, with this very plan, lest anyone afterward claim it for himself; but I should wish this done in a special writing, not so prolix, but such as could be proposed in the schools. Then I think it will scarcely happen further that anyone plucks at your praise. Duncan certainly, whatever he may at last have done privately, will never publicly arrogate it [the Hypothesis] to himself — he who did not even in a dream think of it, before he learned this from you as a mystery. Which, as soon as you had revealed [it], I remember was said by you, [that] more was communicated to him than a brother would perhaps wish to expound to a brother. And I have found, in these few days, among my papers, a sheet on which, at my request, you marked it for me — whether before Duncan's arrival, or at the time he was with you, I am uncertain. But my Lord Tycho, suffer yourself to be advised this by a friend: set aside all polemics, and walk in the footsteps by which you began. No one was ever great by contentions. There are always some who favour even the unjust side; nor is anything ever so solidly refuted but that the adversary finds something to attack plausibly; and men's judgements are taken from words, because those subtle matters are understood by few. Nor would I wish this done even through others, for they too will be answered, and through their flank weapons will be aimed at you. Enemies and the envious will have, from the gibes, something to laugh and rejoice at — as you have now seen done in a certain scurrilous writing of Ursus. And so, when your Christianus [Longomontanus] returns to you — who can be of great use to you — weave on that web which you have happily begun, and forge us a new heaven, [while] nature is reborn and men's industry grows weary. By this way men advance to immortality.

As to the public honours that are in Rosenkrantz's letters, and the fief of the Empire: my opinion is that I should wish you first to come into possession of the fief, before you sought those honours, or accepted them offered — otherwise perhaps the title will suffice you for the substance, and the honour will be imputed for a real benefit. But as soon as you have obtained the promised fief, then I should not wish you to spurn those honours. Your children will thus be the better provided for — of whom your Tycho [the Younger] is of that age that it would be to the purpose now to visit foreign [lands], and he himself greatly desires this. No doubt he can, by his good nature and gentleness, gain himself many friends; but authority and reputation are to be sought by him from letters and from travel. About the rest, too, I do not doubt you are solicitous — not only to leave an inheritance, but also nobility, and all things of political status, intact. Take these as proceeding from a mind that sincerely wishes well to you and yours. Were I present, [I would say] more of these; for by the pen not everything is so allowed, nor can it happen that what we have meditated all comes to mind, when we move the pen. Your Cragius holds his old [self]; so do you too not cease to be the Tycho you were.

Of the state of our affairs, Tychonides will be able to report most. Publicly we have peace still, but privately many are ill-content. Various are the offences of many about this state of affairs, and few there are who do not ill-omen. The English friendship hangs by a thin [thread]. With Charles the Swede it agrees not enough, because in the King's absence his ships entered the sea of Danish right [the Sound], [and] seized Lübeckers and others. Legates are being prepared to be sent thither, on the return of Peter Francis, who is now to prepare the way into Sweden. We fortify and repair the castles and towns in Norway and Skåne, Halland, Blekinge, at the borders, as much as we can, but we are not sufficiently supplied with money. The King of Poland sent his fleet, with soldiers, through the Øresund strait, which suffered shipwreck, and did not reach Sweden. Charles now possesses almost the whole realm, after he subdued Finland this summer, and those parts of Livonia nearby. Of the captive nobles in Sweden — the chief of whom are Erik Sparre, Gustav Banér, Erik Banér, Ture Bielke, and Georg Posse — the constant report is that punishment will be taken [they will be executed]. They have sent hither to the King, seeking his intercession. Little hope remains to the Pole of recovering his hereditary realm. It is not obscure what Charles contrives, though he pretends otherwise.

Of your mother, Tychonides will be able to report what he saw and heard. [Your] aunt is very infirm, and her eyes have dimmed [gone blind]. But what your sister [Sophie] does, and where she now is, you will learn from her own letters. May the eternal Father grant that she at last enjoy, with joy, the hoped-for marriage. We have heard such things of Lord Lange's [Erik Lange's] affairs as raise [our] spirits to the expectation of a better fortune. Of the death of Jacob Seefeld, I think it has been written you by others: scarcely two or three weeks from the wedding of [his] two daughters (celebrated on one day), at the end of the Diet, he unexpectedly closed his day. The Most Serene King is now in Jutland, but is said to wish to celebrate Christ's nativity at Copenhagen. Would that we could see you at the same time then! You could augment our joys in many ways; but I hope we shall see you soon. For you will not fail to run over to us sometime, at least with a small retinue, if you do not bring all. With that desire I greatly burn — which, for you to fulfil, may perhaps be to your good. Now, if I have satisfied you by [my] prolixity, I rejoice; if I have brought trouble and tedium, you yourself are the cause, who so required [it]. However it be, I ask that you take all in good part, per your candour, and that, [being] busy, I have scratched these without care. Farewell most happily, with wife and children, to all of whom I wish [well] as I would wish well to myself. My wife greets you, and her brother Laurentius, and [her] parents; likewise Michael the Surgeon, and some other friends — by name, Gabriel Sparre and Peter Brahe.

Again, farewell.
Given at Copenhagen, the day before the Nones of December, the year 1599.
Yours wholly,
Nic. Cragius.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, c. 1550–1602) — the Danish royal historian and diplomat (just back from the English legation), Tycho's steadfast friend — to Tycho.
  • Significance — the source of the Duncan-Liddel testimony, Krag's counsel, and a Baltic bulletin (a keystone letter).
  • ★ The Duncan-Liddel testimony (origin). The passage "Duncan certainly … did not even in a dream think of [the Hypothesis] before he learned this from you as a mystery … and I have found, among my papers, a sheet on which you marked it for me" is the original of the verbatim testimony Tycho quoted as "Krag's letter of 4 December last year" in brahe-t_16000401001 (to Daniel Cramer, 1 April 1600) and the Duncan dossier brahe-t_15990926001 (to Cramer, 26 September 1599). Krag declines to enter the public controversy but affirms, for the record, that Duncan Liddel learned the Tychonic world-system from Tycho — and counsels Tycho to publish the system plainly under his own name "in a special writing … such as could be proposed in the schools."
  • The counsel against polemic (memorable). Krag's wise exhortation: "Set aside all polemics … No one was ever great by contentions"; enemies "will have, from the gibes, something to laugh at, as in Ursus's scurrilous writing." Instead, with Longomontanus returned, "weave on that web you have happily begun, and forge us a new heaven … By this way men advance to immortality."
  • The imperial honours/fief advice. Krag counsels Tycho to secure the promised imperial fief before seeking the offered honours (lest "the title suffice for the substance"); and notes Tycho the Younger is of an age to travel for "authority and reputation."
  • The Mule/Medici and Tengnagel matters. Tycho's wish to place Claus Mule with the Medici (delayed by winter); the honours rendered Tengnagel; and the denial (re Jacobus Typotius) that Krag "instigated" Tycho's departure — "I lamented your departure, for the fatherland's sake and mine."
  • The Danish/Baltic bulletin. Danish discontent and the thin English friendship; tension with Charles of Sweden (his ships in the Sound seizing Lübeckers); the Polish fleet wrecked in the Øresund; Charles's conquest of Finland and Livonia; and the captive Swedish nobles — Erik Sparre, Gustav Banér, Erik Banér, Ture Bielke, Georg Posse — awaiting execution (the Linköping Bloodbath prisoners; cf. brahe-t_16000919002, where Bacmeister reports the executions of March 1600). The sad domestic executions ("two nobles and three commoners") in Denmark.
  • Family news. Tycho's mother (Beate Bille) and aunt (blind); and — poignantly — the hope for Sophie Brahe's long-deferred marriage to Erik Lange, "Lord Lange's affairs" now raising "expectation of a better fortune" (cf.
