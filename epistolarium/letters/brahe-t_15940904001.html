The original

TILL TYGE BRAHE HELLIG THRE KONGERS CAPELL WDI ROSCHILDE DOMBKIRCKE ANRØRENDIS VT SEQUITUR.

Christian 4..

WOR gunst thilfornn. Wiid, epter att Wij thig thilforn haffue thilschreffuit, attu wden forßømmelße schulle lade forethage thend bygning paa thet capell wdi Roschilde domkircke, som kaldis Hellig thre konger capell, och Wore kierre foreldre, salige och hoglofflige ihukommelße, haffue theris begraffuelße wdi, huilckit dog her thill aldelis icke er scheedt, meden samme capell io meere och meere dag fran dag forderffuis och forargis, och forwndre Wij Oß høyligen, attu samme Wor befaling icke anderledis haffuer achtit och epterkommit och samme bygning forferdigit. Tha bede Wij dig och wille, attu aldelis er fortenckt wdi for:ne bygning paa for:ne capell endeligen och wden ald wndschylding at lade forethage och forferdige inden juell førstkommendis wdi thet allersenniste, wdi huis maade thett kunde haffue wdi behoff att byggis och ferdigis paa. Saa frampt thet icke scheer, att for:ne bygning inden for:ne dag och thiid bliffuer forferdigit, tha wille Wij naadigst befale Wor embidtzmand paa Wor gaard wdi for:ne Roschilde for:ne bygning at schulle lade ferdig giørre, och tha strax att forleene en anden med for:ne capell och theß thilliggendis guoedtz och indkompst, och attu thig med thett allerførste her om emoed Oß ercklerer, huad thu er herwdinden thilßindtz att wille giørre, paa thet Wij kunde wiide, huor epter Wij Oß schulle forholde.

Ther met etc.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Tyge Brahe, concerning the Chapel of the Three Holy Kings in Roskilde Cathedral, as follows.

Christian IV.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, since we have heretofore written to you that you should without neglect have undertaken that building-work on the chapel in Roskilde Cathedral which is called the Chapel of the Three Holy Kings, and in which our dear parents, of blessed and most laudable memory, have their burial — which nonetheless has not at all been done up to now, while that same chapel is decaying and being damaged more and more day by day — we are therefore greatly astonished that you have not otherwise heeded and complied with this our command and completed that same building-work. We therefore ask and will of you that you be wholly mindful, finally and without any excuse, to have that aforesaid building-work on the aforesaid chapel undertaken and completed before Christmas next at the very latest, in whatever manner it may have need to be built and finished. So far forth as it does not come to pass that the aforesaid building-work is completed within the aforesaid day and time, then we will most graciously command our official at our manor in the aforesaid Roskilde to have the aforesaid building-work made ready, and then forthwith to enfeoff another with the aforesaid chapel and its appurtenant property and income; and [we will] that you declare yourself to us concerning this at the very first, [as to] what you are minded herein to do, so that we may know how we are to govern ourselves accordingly.

Therewith, etc.
Done at Kolding, 4 September, year [15]94.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Christian IV of Denmark (b. 1577, r. 1588–1648), here writing in his own name at age 17 in the third year after his 1593 accession to personal rule (the regency council had governed 1588–93). The chancery hand subscribes him "Christian 4.." (so written, with the doubled point).
  • Recipient and benefice. Tycho Brahe had held the prebend (canonry) of the Chapel of the Three Holy Kings (Hellig Tre Kongers Kapel) in Roskilde Cathedral since the grant of 1 May 1579 from Frederik II — a sinecure carrying income and the obligation to maintain the chapel fabric and to support two choirboys (tho smaa sincker). The chapel is the burial place of Christian I and later Danish kings; the "kierre foreldre" ("dear parents") here are Frederik II (d. 1588) and Queen Sophie of Mecklenburg — though Sophie was living in 1594, the chancery formula "of blessed and most laudable memory" with foreldre is the dynastic burial-chapel formula and is reproduced as written, not corrected (see crux below).
  • Document type and code-switch. A chancery-register entry (Sjællandske Tegnelser XVIII, 364), not an autograph. The Latin rubric "…ANRØRENDIS ut sequitur" ("concerning… as follows") heads the Danish body — a code-switch preserved by the edition's convention, exactly as in the parallel 1584 Frederik II letter.
  • Legal force — the threat (translated with negation count fixed). The operative clause is a royal ultimatum with three explicit negations / privatives, reproduced one-for-one: (1) "huilckit dog her thill aldelis icke er scheedt" = "which nonetheless has not at all been done up to now"; (2) "attu… befaling icke anderledis haffuer achtit" = "that you have not otherwise heeded"; (3) "endeligen och wden ald wndschylding" = "finally and without any excuse." The conditional "Saa frampt thet icke scheer, att… bliffuer forferdigit, tha wille Wij…" is a negative protasis governing a future apodosis: "So far forth as it does not come to pass that [the work] is completed…, then we will…" — i.e. if you fail to finish it by Christmas, then the king will (a) have it finished by his own Roskilde official and (b) enfeoff another with the chapel and its revenues — a threat to revoke Tycho's benefice. The fourth negation-like privative "wden forßømmelße" ("without neglect," in the recital of the earlier order) is also kept. Total: four privative/negative markers (aldelis icke, icke, wden ald, wden) plus the conditional icke scheer — all five preserved; none added.
  • "bede Wij dig och wille." The doublet bede… och wille couples a courteous "ask" with the binding ville of the royal will ("ask and will of you"); the closing "och attu… ercklerer" depends on this same wille, hence the bracketed "[we will] that you declare." The mood is a command softened by petitionary form, not a request — rendered to keep both registers.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1594). WOR gunst thilfornn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula, the edition's convention); Wiid = Vid ("Know," imperative); attu = at du; thilschreffuit = tilskrevet ("written to"); wden forßømmelße = uden forsømmelse ("without neglect"); forethage = foretage ("undertake"); scheedt = sket ("done, happened"); forderffuis = fordærves ("is spoiled/decays"); forargis = forarges ("is damaged/marred"); forwndre Wij Oß høyligen = forundre vi os højligen ("we are greatly astonished"); achtit = agtet ("heeded, regarded"); epterkommit = efterkommet ("complied with"); forferdigit / forferdige = færdiggjort / færdiggøre ("completed / complete"); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); fortenckt = betænkt ("mindful, intent"); juell førstkommendis = jul førstkommende ("Christmas next-coming"); allersenniste = allersidste ("very latest"); Saa frampt = såfremt ("so far forth as, provided that, if"); embidtzmand = embedsmand ("official, officer"); gaard = gård ("manor, estate"); forleene = forlene ("enfeoff, grant in fief"); theß thilliggendis guoedtz och indkompst = dets tilliggende gods og indkomst ("its appurtenant property and income"); ercklerer = erklærer ("declare"); thilßindtz = tilsinds ("minded, disposed"); huor epter… forholde = hvorefter… forholde ("how we are to conduct/govern ourselves").
  • Calendar / place. Actum Cold[ingae] 4. septembris a:o 94 = "Done at Kolding, 4 September [15]94" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). Actum "Done/Given" kept by the edition's convention; Cold. expanded to the Latin locative Coldingae (Kolding, the royal residence Koldinghus in Jutland). The dateline as written (4 September 1594) agrees with the catalogue label.
