The original

PAULUS HAINTZELIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

NIHIL sane opus erat, amantissime mi TYCHO, tam accurata apud me de intermissione literarum excusatione uti, cum tu me eiusdem negligentiae accusare possis. Verum cum nec ipse tu, quanta ex Latini sermonis paupertate laborem, ignores, et egomet mihi plane sim conscius, quanta difficultate, praesertim ad homines doctos, tuique similes scribam, ne mirere, si vel rariores, vel breviores, quam expectas a me, tibi reddantur. Nisi enim habeam, quod vel te scire tua vel utriusque nostrum intersit, nihil est, quod a me expectes. Illud saltem cave, ne propterea quicquam de meo erga te amore detractum suspicere. De me, mearumque rerum statu scias, me, Dei omnipotentis beneficio, cum tota familia recte valere, filioque (ab hinc anno secundo, nimirum 1577, mense Septembri die 14, hora forte 7 3/4 post meridiem) auctum esse; reliqua veteri adhuc loco sita sunt, nisi quod praeterito anno filiam meam ELIZABETHAM, maiorem natu, cum iuvene huius urbis (cui nomen est MELCHIORI IENISCH) despondi, quorum nuptiae die 13. mensis Octobris, quod felix faustumque sit, celebratae sunt, cum tuae mihi plenae humanitatis literae iam ante mensem essent redditae, quibus ego quod in hunc usque diem non respondi, non mea, sed eius, qui tuas mihi reddidit, culpa factum est. Is namque me inscio hinc discessit, et sine meis literis, secus quam promiserat, ad vos redijt, cuius rei optimus mihi testis esse potest LAURENTIUS, hospes tuus.

Observationes Cometae, quas a me petis, ego sane nullas feci, cum nulla idonea ad observandum instrumenta nunc habeam. Aliorum vero observationes et scripta, quae passim in Germania et alibi de hoc Cometa publicata sunt et ad nos pervenerunt, tibique transmitti expetis (quorum certe magnus est numerus) eorum ego tibi aliquot unâ mitto. Sed mehercule non magnopere lectione, et quae tanto itineris spatio perferantur, digna sunt, exceptis observationibus M. MICHÆLIS MÆSTLINI, CORNELIJ GEMMAe Louaniensis, et ELISÆI RŒSLINI, qui eundem Cometam (quemadmodum etiam tu existimas) non in Elementari, sed in Aetherea mundi regione situm atque generatum esse, certis demonstrationibus testantur, eiusque verum locum, motum, magnitudinem, apparitionem et exstinctionem commonstrare videntur. Sed THADÆI HAGECIJ eiusdem Cometae descriptio, et Landgravij diligens observatio, modo rite in calculum adhibita sit, longe diversum ab illorum et tua opinione et sententia statuunt atque sentiunt. MICHÆLIS MÆSTLINI Epistolas ad Dn. HIERONYMUM WOLFIUM scriptas, et ex his excerpta, item Landgravij observationem, si modo genuina est, hîc unâ mitto. His doctissime, humanissimeque mi TYCHO, valeas, et me, sive loquentem tecum per literas, sive etiam tacentem, manere tamen semper in eodem amore et benevolentia erga te tibi plane persuasum habeas. IOHANNES BAPTISTA, frater meus, salutem tibi plurimam hîc referri voluit.

Iterum etiam atque etiam vale.
Dabantur Augustae 25. Martii An. 79.
T.
Paulus Haintzel.

Has saltem optimæ recordationis viri Pauli Haintzelij, Consulis Augustani,
Astronomicarum rerum amantissimi, in cuius etiam horto Quadrantem illum maximum,

English translation machine translation, Secure

Paul Hainzel to Tycho Brahe.

There was surely no need, my most loving Tycho, to make so careful an excuse to me for the interruption of letters, since you could charge me with the same negligence. But since neither are you yourself unaware how greatly I labour under a poverty of the Latin tongue, and I am for my part fully conscious with what difficulty I write — especially to learned men, and to your like — do not wonder if [my letters] are delivered to you either rarer or briefer than you expect from me. For unless I have something that it concerns either you to know in your interest or both of us, there is nothing for you to expect from me. Only beware of this: do not on that account suspect that anything has been subtracted from my love toward you. As for myself and the state of my affairs, know that I, by the favour of Almighty God, am well together with my whole household, and have been increased by a son (two years ago from now, namely 1577, in the month of September, on the 14th day, at about 7¾ o'clock in the afternoon); the rest still stand in their old place, save that last year I betrothed my elder daughter Elizabeth to a young man of this city (whose name is Melchior Jenisch), whose wedding — may it be happy and auspicious — was celebrated on the 13th day of the month of October, at a time when your letter full of kindness had already a month before been delivered to me; and that I have not until this very day answered it has come about through no fault of mine, but of him who delivered yours to me. For he departed hence without my knowledge, and returned to you without my letters, otherwise than he had promised — of which matter Lorenz, your host, can be my best witness.

The observations of the comet which you ask of me, I have indeed made none, since I now have no instruments suitable for observing. But of others' observations and writings, which have been published here and there in Germany and elsewhere about this comet and have reached us, and which you desire to be sent on to you (of which the number is certainly great), I send you some along [with this]. But, by Hercules, they are not greatly worth reading, nor worth being conveyed over so great a stretch of road, save for the observations of Master Michael Maestlin, of Cornelius Gemma of Louvain, and of Elisaeus Roeslin, who attest by sure demonstrations that this same comet (as you too judge) was situated and generated not in the elementary but in the aethereal region of the world, and who seem to show forth its true place, motion, magnitude, appearance, and extinction. But Thaddaeus Hagecius's description of the same comet, and the Landgrave's careful observation, provided it has been duly brought into the reckoning, establish and hold something far different from both their opinion and judgement and your own. The letters of Michael Maestlin written to Master Hieronymus Wolf, and excerpts from them, likewise the Landgrave's observation, if only it is genuine, I send here along [with this]. With these, my most learned and most kind Tycho, may you fare well, and hold yourself fully persuaded that I — whether speaking with you by letter, or even keeping silent — nonetheless remain always in the same love and goodwill toward you. Johann Baptist, my brother, wished his heartiest greeting to be conveyed to you here.

Again, and again and again, farewell.
Given at Augsburg, 25 March, the year [15]79.
Yours,
Paul Hainzel.

These letters, at least, of Paul Hainzel — a man of most blessed memory, Consul of Augsburg, most devoted to astronomical matters, in whose garden, too, we erected that greatest Quadrant, of which mention is made elsewhere in my writings — these three letters at least it has been possible to append, although he gave more to me, which have perished; just as, likewise, I find no reply among my papers to those things which I wrote to him.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Paulus Haintzelius = Paul Hainzel (1527–1581), patrician of Augsburg, sometime Stadtpfleger (city magistrate), patron of astronomy. With him Tycho had built the great 19-foot quadrant at Göppingen in 1569 (see the Wolf letter of 13 Nov. 1571, where the two Hainzel brothers are named). The closing salutation from Iohannes Baptista, frater meus identifies Johann Baptist Hainzel, his brother.
  • Latini sermonis paupertate — "a poverty of the Latin tongue": Hainzel's self-deprecation about his Latinity; kept literal. (The Latin is in fact competent but heavily Ciceronian-periodic.)
  • The son's birth. filioque … nimirum 1577, mense Septembri die 14, hora forte 7 3/4 post meridiem — a horoscopic-precision timestamp ("on 14 September 1577, at about 7¾ o'clock in the afternoon"), given to a quarter-hour for the casting of a nativity. forte ("about / perhaps") is the author's own hedge on the hour and is preserved, not sharpened. post meridiem = "in the afternoon / p.m." (O.S.).
  • The daughter's betrothal. filiam meam Elizabetham … cum iuvene huius urbis (cui nomen est Melchiori Ienisch) — Elizabeth Hainzel, betrothed to Melchior Jenisch of Augsburg; the wedding die 13. mensis Octobris (13 October [1578], the "last year" of praeterito anno). quod felix faustumque sit — "may it be happy and auspicious," the formulaic Roman nuptial blessing, kept as a parenthetical wish.
  • Laurentius, hospes tuus — "Lorenz, your host": a person (otherwise unidentified here) who had lodged Tycho or acted as his host/agent at Augsburg, blamed for failing to carry Hainzel's reply. hospes = "host" (one who gives lodging), not "guest" in this context.
  • The comet. hoc Cometa = the Great Comet of 1577, the central observational event of these years and the subject of Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588). The whole second paragraph concerns the dossier of printed comet-tracts Hainzel is forwarding to Hven.
  • instrumenta → instruments; Hainzel pleads he has none fit for observing.
  • The cosmological crux of the comet. non in Elementari, sed in Aetherea mundi regione situm atque generatum — "situated and generated not in the elementary but in the aethereal region of the world": the decisive claim that the comet lay above the Moon, in the aether, not in the sublunary (elementary) sphere — the supralunary thesis Tycho himself championed (quemadmodum etiam tu existimas, "as you too judge"). verum locum, motum, magnitudinem, apparitionem et exstinctionem rendered "true place, motion, magnitude, appearance, and extinction" (the comet's first appearance and its fading-out).
  • The observers named.
  • M. Michaelis Maestlini = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), then at Tübingen, later Kepler's teacher; his Observatio et demonstratio cometae (1578) placed the comet supralunary. M. = Magister (academic "Master").
  • Cornelij Gemmae Louaniensis = Cornelius Gemma (1535–1578) of Louvain (son of Gemma Frisius), who likewise treated the 1577 comet in his De prodigiosa specie naturaque cometae (1578).
  • Elisaei Roeslini = Helisaeus Röslin (1545–1616), physician and astronomer of Alsace, author of a 1578 Theoria nova coelestium on the comet.
  • Thadaei Hagecij = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius, 1525–1600), imperial physician at Prague, whose Descriptio cometae (1578) Tycho prized; here said to reach a conclusion longe diversum ("far different") from the supralunary trio and from Tycho.
  • Landgravij … observatio = the observation of the Landgrave — Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel — whose value Hainzel guards twice with explicit hedges: modo rite in calculum adhibita sit ("provided it has been duly brought into the reckoning") and si modo genuina est ("if only it is genuine"). Both qualifiers are preserved.
  • mehercule — "by Hercules," a mild classical oath of asseveration; kept.
  • Dn. = Dominus, "Master / Lord", of Hieronymus Wolf (Hieronymus Wolfius), the Augsburg humanist (1516–1580) and Tycho's correspondent — recipient of Maestlin's letters that Hainzel forwards.
  • Closing formulae. Iterum etiam atque etiam vale — "Again, and again and again, farewell" (an emphatic reduplication of the Vale formula); salutem tibi plurimam … referri voluit rendered "wished his heartiest greeting to be conveyed to you".
  • Dateline. Dabantur Augustae 25. Martii An. 79 = "Given at Augsburg (Augusta Vindelicorum), 25 March, the year [15]79" (O.S.). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1579. 25. marts. Augsburg); no date discrepancy. Dabantur (lit. "they were being given," the epistolary plural for the act of dispatch) rendered idiomatically "Given."
  • What this is — a genre shift, not a continuation. This recovered segment is not part of Hainzel's 1579 letter itself (whose actual close the published tail already gives, signed "Paulus Haintzel"). It is Tycho's own editorial headnote, written in the first person about Hainzel in the third person and in the past tense ("optimae recordationis" — "of blessed/cherished memory", the formula for the deceased), evidently composed when Tycho later assembled this group of letters for his letter-book. A reader could otherwise mistake it for Hainzel continuing to speak of himself, which the content rules out.
  • What it documents. Tycho states plainly that only three of Hainzel's letters to him survived to be included, although Hainzel had sent more, now lost — and that he could find no drafts or copies of his own replies among his papers. This is a rare piece of direct evidence for the compilation history of Tycho's own letter-collection: an admission of gaps, not a claim of completeness.
  • Paul Hainzel identified. Paul Hainzel, patrician and Consul (alderman) of Augsburg, a keen amateur astronomer; in his garden outside the city Tycho built the celebrated "great Quadrant of Augsburg" (1570, radius roughly 543 cm/about 19 feet, the largest instrument Tycho ever built), which Hainzel had commissioned and later undertook to see completed; Hainzel also gave Tycho a newly built sextant. The instrument stood until wrecked by a storm around 1575 and was never repaired [Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (Edinburgh, 1890); Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020); corroborated by Wennberg, Tänk, om det är så!, on the quadrant's dimensions and site]. "Consulis Augustani" in the source matches the title given him in the Progymnasmata's account of the 1572 nova observations.
  • Dating implication. Because the headnote refers to Hainzel as already deceased, it must postdate his death — meaning the note is a later addition to this 1579 letter, not contemporary with it; the works consulted here do not give an exact death year for Hainzel, so this remains a lead rather than a settled fact.
  • Correction. Restored the valediction abbreviation 'T.' (Tuus, 'Yours') on its own line above the signature, dropped from our transcribed original and consequently from the translation, per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII p.50.
