The original

HENRICO RANZOVIO PRODUCI CIMBRICO S. D. TYCHO BRAHE.

QUAMVIS perpaucis abhinc diebus, nobilissime et amantissime Domine RANZOVI, ultimis tuis ad me datis literis nonnihil responderim: facit tamen diutina illa, quæ nobis intercedit, amicitia, tum negotium etiam quoddam postmodum interveniens, ut te alijs insuper compellare nihil addubitem; ex quo illam hanc quoque operam superioribus officijs erga me suis non invite aggregaturam, id vero, quicquid est, tua auctoritate et adiumento promptissime ac ocijssime istic expeditum iri mihi persuadeam. Commodum autem accidit, ut meus olim architectus, quem in quibusdam Astronomicis et Geometricis, dum mecum esset, instruxeram, alias suopte ingenio ad delineationes artis perspectivæ proclivem, ad me imagines quasdam calamo repræsentatas, quas frontispicio et calci meorum librorum hîc excudendorum adiungere constitui, huc ex Hallandia (ubi iam apud fratrem meum Varbergi arcis structuræ, Regis mandato, præest) transmiserit. Quas nunc ad te, mi RANZOVI, mitto, amice rogans, velis tua autoritate et iussu efficere, ut Hamburgi vel Lubecæ ab excellenti quodam artifice quam optime incidantur, mihique prima quaque occasione, quod intra sex vel septem hebdomadas fieri quam maxime cuperem, sartæ tectæ remittantur. Quicquid cœlatori pro labore pendendum sit, certior a te factus lubens solvam. Da quæso operam, ut meis hac in parte votis prompte et mature satisfiat. Præstiteris hoc nomine non solum mihi rem admodum gratam, sed et tibi ipsi studijsque communibus operam (uti spero) dignam et utilem elocaveris. Ego si qua in re tibi vicissim pro his alijsque affatim erga me exhibitis benevolentiæ officijs inservire queam, faxo ne studium et voluntatem de te pariter, quocunque modo possum, bene merendi in me unquam desideres.

Vale.
Uraniburgi 9. Ianuarij An. 93.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Heinrich Rantzau, Governor of the Cimbrian land — Tycho Brahe sends greeting.

Although but a very few days ago, most noble and most loving Master Rantzau, I made some reply to the last letter you sent me, yet that long-standing friendship which subsists between us — and also a certain piece of business which has since come up — makes me not at all hesitate to approach you with further matters besides; whence I persuade myself that you will not unwillingly add this present service too to your earlier kindnesses towards me, and that this matter, whatever it amounts to, will be dispatched there most promptly and most speedily by your authority and assistance. Now it has fallen out conveniently that my former architect — whom, while he was with me, I had instructed in certain matters of astronomy and geometry, being besides by his own native talent inclined to the drawings of the perspective art — has transmitted to me from Halland (where he now superintends, by the King's command, the building works of Varberg castle in the service of my brother) certain images rendered with the pen, which I have determined to attach to the frontispiece and the tail-piece of my books that are to be printed here. These I now send to you, my Rantzau, asking as a friend that you would by your authority and command bring it about that they be engraved at Hamburg or Lübeck, as excellently as possible, by some outstanding craftsman, and be sent back to me safe and sound at the very first opportunity — which I should most exceedingly wish to be done within six or seven weeks. Whatever is to be paid to the engraver for his labour, once informed of it by you I will gladly discharge. Take pains, I beg, that my wishes in this matter be satisfied promptly and in good time. By so doing you will not only have rendered me a most welcome service, but will also (as I hope) have bestowed upon yourself and upon our common studies a labour worthy and useful. As for myself, if in any matter I can in turn be of service to you in return for these and the other offices of goodwill abundantly shown towards me, I will see to it that you never find wanting in me the zeal and the will to deserve well of you likewise, in whatever way I can.

Farewell.
At Uraniborg, 9 January, the year [15]93.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on the island of Hven.
  • Correspondent. Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich Rantzau (also Henrik Rantzau, 1526–1598), royal Statthalter (governor) of the duchies of Schleswig-Holstein for the Danish crown, humanist, astrological enthusiast, patron and bibliophile, and one of Tycho's most active intermediaries to the German printing and engraving trade. The two corresponded frequently, and Rantzau repeatedly used his standing in the Hamburg–Lübeck region to arrange technical and printing services for Tycho.
  • Produci Cimbrico — "Governor of the Cimbrian land": Cimbria is the humanist name for the Cimbrian (Jutland) peninsula / the duchies of Schleswig-Holstein, so the title styles Rantzau as the (vice-regal) ruler of that land. Produx here renders his governorship in elevated humanist diction; "Governor" is given, with the honorific weight noted rather than inflated to a literal "Duke."
  • meus olim architectus — "my former architect": the draughtsman-architect Tycho had earlier employed and trained at Hven, now (1593) directing, by royal command, the building works at Varberg castle in Halland in the service of Tycho's brother. apud fratrem meum = "in the service of my brother" — Tycho's brother Steen Brahe, then lensmand (fief-holder/governor) of Varberg. The architect is most plausibly identified with Hans van Steenwinckel the Elder, who worked at both Hven and Varberg, though the letter does not name him; the identification is left unstated here.
  • delineationes artis perspectivae — "the drawings of the perspective art": the architect's skill in perspective draughtsmanship, which Tycho exploits for the engraved plates.
  • imagines … calamo repraesentatas — "images rendered with the pen": pen-drawn designs (the artwork) to be cut by an engraver.
  • frontispicio et calci meorum librorum — "the frontispiece and the tail-piece of my books": the engravings are destined as the opening and closing plates of the books Tycho was preparing to print at his own Uraniborg/Hven press (hîc excudendorum, "to be printed here"). This places the letter among the documents of Tycho's self-publishing programme of the 1590s.
  • incidantur … coelatori — incido "to engrave / cut (in metal)" and coelator/caelator "engraver, chaser": the technical vocabulary of copperplate engraving, to be executed by a master craftsman at Hamburg or Lübeck.
  • sartae tectae — the legal-idiomatic phrase sarta tecta ("kept in good repair," lit. "patched and roofed"), here used figuratively of the plates being returned "safe and sound / in good order."
  • Internal dating reference. perpaucis abhinc diebus … nonnihil responderim — Tycho notes he had answered Rantzau's previous letter "a very few days ago"; this is a chronological pointer to a slightly earlier letter, not a dating discrepancy.
  • Calendar / place. Uraniburgi 9. Ianuarij An. 93 = "At Uraniborg, 9 January [15]93" — Old Style (Julian; Denmark retained O.S. until 1700). The dateline as written agrees with the catalogue label (1593, 9 January, Uraniborg); no discrepancy.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. D. = salutem dicit, "sends greeting"; Vale = "Farewell."
