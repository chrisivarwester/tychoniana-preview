The original

ANTIQUA NOBILITATE VIRO CLARISS. ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTI D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, URANIEBURGI DOMINO, D. ET AMICO OBSERVANDO.
S.

NOBILISS. Domine et amice observande. Cum intellexissem Legatum Regis vestri D. VINSHEMIUM ad nostrum Imp. venisse, quam primum mihi per valetudinem licuit, statim eum adij et percontatus sum, an aliquid literarum abs te mihi attulisset. Iam enim diu exoptabam tuas, quibus saltem edocerer de meis ad te, num omnes, quas scripsi, aut aliquæ illarum tibi essent redditæ. Cum autem ille negaret se quicquam habere abs te, vellicare animum meum cœpit cogitatio, num mihi succenseres putaresque me data opera ad tuas respondere noluisse. Hoc si ita est, obsecro te, mi TYCHO, depone hanc suspicionem ex animo tuo. Non induo nunc in hoc meo senio, crede mihi, illam barbariem, ut ad amici literas non respondeam. Absit illud longe a me, ita me Deus amet, mi TYCHO, respondi ad singulas tuas semper. Quod si tibi redditæ non sunt, non mihi, quæso, imputa, sed iniquæ sorti, quæ nobis invidet illam voluptatem, quam uterque capturi eramus ex literarum lectione, et hominum infidelitati. Annus est integer, cum per civem quendam Gorliciensem, opera MYLIJ, dedi ad te literas unâ cum opusculo VERNERI, quod aliquando a me expetebas. Hæc cum literis SCULTETI ad te perferri debebant. Quid cum illis factum sit, nescio, postea cum hoc vere missus esset nobilis vestras a Rege ad Imp. cum falconibus, ac ille quoque tuas ad me attulisset literas, ipse eum alloquutus dixi me ipsi literas ad te daturum esse. Sed cum biduo abessem urbe medicinæ faciendæ gratia, ille interea præter spem et nostræ aulæ consuetudinem citius expeditus hinc discessit, quod quam ægre tulerim, dici non potest. Nactus autem mox altero aut tertio die amicum, apud quem deplorabam elapsam mihi occasionem mittendi literas ad te, statim ille suam mihi detulit operam, dixitque se Dresdæ habere amicum, cuius opera fidelissime perferrentur ad te. Dedi itaque illi meas, quibus adhuc alias adiunxi. Si non sunt tibi redditæ, ne mihi culpam adscribas rogo, imo vero hanc meam iustam salva conscientia excusationem eo animo, quo amicum decet, accipias, meque primo quoque tempore de his omnibus edoceas, animumque meum nimis solicitum hanc ob rem tranquillum reddas. Hæc repeto hisce meis, cum ignorem, qualem cursum habuerint priores meæ.

Quid impediverit, quod huc usque primus liber tuus non prodierit, quidve nunc præ manibus habeas, ut me certiorem reddas magnopere peto. Mea Mathemata iam diu quiescunt et quasi in DEMOCRITI puteo abdita latent. Si mihi Deus longius vitæ curriculum concesserit, cogito in meis ædibus extruere aliquam speculam, unde aliquando astra contemplari possem.

Æstate præterita partem meæ domus reparare cœpi. Aliud, quod scribam, non habeo. De rebus Gallicis veriora vos istic habere existimo. Ad nos varia et incerta afferuntur iuxta hominum affectiones varias. Illud certissimum habetur, Regi huc usque feliciter successisse. Genevæ obsidionem solutam narrant, relicta tamen fuisse quædam præsidia in obsidione, quæ Genevenses effusi ex urbe dispulerunt et novum commeatum in urbem invexerunt. Deus suam Ecclesiam contra tyrannos potenti sua manu tueatur et defendat.

B. V. mi amantiss. TYCHO.
Datæ Pragæ die ultima Novemb. veteri stylo Anno 1589.
T. studiosiss.
THAD.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To a man of ancient nobility, most renowned and outstanding in learning, Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Uraniborg, his lord, and friend to be esteemed.
Greeting.

Most noble lord and friend to be esteemed. When I had learned that your King's legate, Master Vinshemius, had come to our Emperor, as soon as my health allowed me I went to him at once and inquired whether he had brought me any letter from you. For I had long been most eager for word from you, by which I might at least be informed about my own [letters] to you — whether all those I wrote, or any of them, had been delivered to you. But when he denied having anything from you, a thought began to gnaw at my mind: whether you might be angry with me and think that I had deliberately refused to answer your letters. If this is so, I beseech you, my Tycho, put this suspicion out of your mind. I am not now, believe me, in this my old age, taking on that barbarousness of not answering a friend's letters. Far be that from me — so help me God, my Tycho — I have always answered each of yours. And if they have not been delivered to you, do not, I pray, charge it to me, but to unkind fortune, which begrudges us that pleasure which we were each to take from the reading of letters, and to the faithlessness of men. It is a full year since, through a certain citizen of Görlitz, by the agency of Mylius, I dispatched a letter to you together with the little work of Werner which you had once been asking of me. These were to be conveyed to you with Scultetus's letter. What became of them I do not know. Afterwards, when this spring a nobleman of yours had been sent by the King to the Emperor with falcons, and he too had brought me your letter, I myself spoke with him and said that I would give him a letter for you. But since I was away from the city for two days for the practice of medicine, he meanwhile — beyond my expectation and contrary to the custom of our court — was dispatched more quickly and departed hence, at which I cannot say how grieved I was. But having soon, on the second or third day, come upon a friend, to whom I was lamenting the lost opportunity of sending a letter to you, he straightway offered me his service, and said that he had a friend at Dresden, by whose agency they would be conveyed to you most faithfully. And so I gave him mine, to which I added yet others. If they have not been delivered to you, I beg you not to ascribe the fault to me, but rather to accept this just excuse of mine — [made] with a clear conscience — in the spirit that befits a friend, and to inform me about all these matters at the very first opportunity, and to render my mind, too anxious on this account, tranquil. I repeat these things in this present letter of mine, since I do not know what course my earlier ones have taken.

What has hindered your first book from coming out hitherto, or what you now have in hand — I greatly ask that you make me more certain of it. My own mathematical studies have long lain at rest and lie hidden, as it were, in Democritus's well. If God grants me a longer course of life, I am thinking of building some little observatory at my house, whence I might one day contemplate the stars.

This past summer I began to repair part of my house. I have nothing else to write. Of French affairs I judge that you there have truer [reports]. To us things are brought various and uncertain, according to men's various dispositions. This is held most certain: that the King has hitherto succeeded happily. They report that the siege of Geneva has been raised — yet that certain garrisons had been left behind in the siege, which the Genevans, pouring out from the city, drove off, and brought a fresh supply of provisions into the city. May God watch over and defend His Church against the tyrants with His mighty hand.

Fare you well, my most loving Tycho.
Given at Prague, on the last day of November, Old Style, in the year 1589.
Most devoted to you,
Thad[deus].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. THAD. / Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer, and personal physician to the Emperor at Prague; one of Tycho's most important Central-European correspondents, and later instrumental in bringing Tycho into Imperial service. The closing subscription contracts his name to THAD.; T. studiosiss. = Tui studiosissimus, "Most devoted to you". Dreyer prints the letter in Opera Omnia VII among Tycho's incoming correspondence.
  • Recipient address. D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, URANIEBURGI DOMINO — "Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Uraniborg." Uranieburgi = Uraniborg on Hven.
  • Salutation. S. = Salutem ("Greeting,").
  • Legatum Regis vestri D. VINSHEMIUM — "your King's legate, Master Vinshemius": an envoy of Frederik II's son and successor Christian IV (a minor; the regency government) to the Imperial court of Rudolf II. The name Vinshemius (Winsheim / Veit Ortel von Winsheim and family) recurs in this same Hagecius–Tycho correspondence (the correspondence preserves a VITUS VINSHEMIUS returning from Wittenberg with Lutheran tracts for Tycho), so the reading is secure as a surname even though the legate's full identity is not fixed by this letter alone.
  • nostrum Imp. = nostrum Imperatorem, "our Emperor" — Rudolf II, Holy Roman Emperor, resident at Prague, whose physician Hagecius was ("our court," nostrae aulae, below).
  • opusculo VERNERI — "the little work of Werner": Johann Werner of Nuremberg, almost certainly his De motu octavae sphaerae (Nuremberg, 1522). Tycho avidly pursued this tract in the years of his prosthaphaeresis-priority quarrel with Ursus (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, on Tycho's "avid pursuit … of the De motu octavae sphaerae of the Nuremberg astronomer Johannes Werner"); the present letter documents Hagecius procuring a copy for him.
  • per civem quendam Gorliciensem … cum literis SCULTETI — "through a certain citizen of Görlitz … with Scultetus's letter": Bartholomäus Scultetus (Bartholomäus Schultz, 1540–1614), the Görlitz mathematician, surveyor, and burgomaster, a mutual friend and a regular relay-point in Tycho's correspondence (Dreyer lists "Scultetus at Leipzig" among Tycho's foreign correspondents). The Görlitz citizen is the carrier; Scultetus's own letter was to travel in the same packet.
  • opera MYLIJ — "by the agency of Mylius": an intermediary, Mylius (the latinised form of Mühl/Müller/Möller); not further specified in the letter and left unidentified rather than guessed.
  • nobilis vestras … cum falconibus — "a nobleman of yours … with falcons": a Danish nobleman sent by the crown to the Emperor bearing falcons — Danish (and especially Icelandic/Norwegian) falcons were a standing diplomatic gift to the Habsburg court. vestras (for vester) is a chancery/period spelling, "of yours" (Danish).
  • in DEMOCRITI puteo abdita latent — "lie hidden, as it were, in Democritus's well": the proverbial Democritus's well (cf. veritas in puteo — truth lies hidden in a well), the saying attributed to Democritus that truth lies sunk in the depths; Hagecius means his mathematical work lies buried out of sight. The classical allusion is kept.
  • speculam … unde astra contemplari possem — "some little observatory … whence I might contemplate the stars": specula in its astronomical sense, a watch-tower / observatory. A poignant aspiration from a man of 64, set against Tycho's Uraniborg.
  • primus liber tuus — "your first book": Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (the first volume of the Astronomical Letters), which Tycho was then printing at his own Uraniborg press; it appeared in 1596. Hagecius asks why it has not yet come out — a securely datable reference to the work's protracted production.
  • French affairs / the King. De rebus Gallicis … Regi huc usque feliciter successisse — the French Wars of Religion: "the King" is Henri IV of Navarre, who had succeeded to the French throne on the assassination of Henri III (1 August 1589) and was campaigning against the Catholic League; Hagecius reports (from a Protestant standpoint) that he has so far prospered.
  • Genevae obsidionem solutam — "the siege of Geneva raised": Protestant Geneva was under pressure from Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy in 1589 (the opening of the Savoy–Geneva war). Hagecius relays a report — explicitly marked as varia et incerta, "various and uncertain" — that the siege had been lifted and the Genevans had driven off the besieging garrisons and revictualled the city. The epistemic hedge is preserved, not sharpened into a flat statement of fact.
  • Deus suam Ecclesiam contra tyrannos … tueatur — "May God … defend His Church against the tyrants": the optative subjunctive tueatur … et defendat is rendered as a prayer ("May God …"), matching the Protestant-confessional register of the war news.
  • Subscription. B. V. = Bene vale, "Fare you well"; Datae Pragae … T. studiosiss. THAD. = "Given at Prague … Most devoted to you, Thad[deus]."
  • Calendar. die ultima Novemb. veteri stylo Anno 1589 = "on the last day of November, Old Style, in the year 1589" = 30 November 1589 (O.S.). Hagecius, writing at the Catholic Imperial court (which had adopted the Gregorian calendar), expressly flags veteri stylo ("Old Style") to align his date with Tycho's Danish reckoning — itself a small monument to the calendar gap discussed in the edition's convention The as-written date agrees with the catalogue label (30 November 1589); no discrepancy. No silent conversion made.
