The original

DEM VESTEN/ VNSERM LIEBEN BESONDERN/ TYCHONI BRAHE, HERRN ZU KNUDSTRUP.
WILHELM VON GOTTES GNADEN/ LANDTGRAUE ZU HESSEN/ GRAUE ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ ETC.

VNSERN gnedigen Gruß zuvor/ Vester lieber Besonder/ Wir mögen euch gnediglich nicht verhalten/ daß wir in vnserm Thiergarten ein Elendsthier haben/ daß sich dan diesen Winter vnd Sommer vber wol gehalten/ vnd fein zugenommen hat/ jm auch sonst nichts mangelt/ alß daß es keinen Gehülffen hat. Weyl wir dan auß ewerm jüngsten Schreiben sub dato den 2. Aprilis verstanden/ daß jr auch ein Elendthier hettet/ vnd vns dasselbige gerne zustehen lassen woltet/ So nemen wir dasselbige von euch zu gnedigen danck an/ vnd haben demnach nicht vnderlassen wollen/ gegenwertigen Botten zu euch abzufertigen/ vnd jm zu befehlen/ daß er bemelt Elendt bey euch abholen/ vnd vns zufüren soli. Woferne jr vns nu dasselbige/ ewerm Zuschreiben nach/ folgen lassen wollet/ wie wir vns gleichwol zu euch versehen thun/ so geschicht vns daran zu angenemen gefallen/ vnd wollen wir es hiernechst mit einem hübschen Zapffenburger gegen euch erwidern/ vnd sonst in Gnaden eingedenck seyn.

Wir zweiffeln auch nicht/ jr werdet in dem Deutschen Exemplari Gesneri de Animalibus Quadrupedibus gelesen haben/ was er fol. 85. von einem Thier/ so Rix, oder wie etliche wollen/ Orix genandt wird/ schreiben thut/ Möchten gerne wissen/ ob jr davon etwas in Erfarung bracht/ oder auch mit Leuten geredt/ die es gesehen/ oder berichten können/ was es vor ein gelegenheit darumb habe/ vbi inveniatur, et si tansæ proceritatis,quantæ a Gesnero pingitur, siet.

Vnser Mathematicus CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS ist bey vns noch nicht widerumb ankommen/ vnd ob wir jn wol zu vns anhero erfordert/ so hat er sich doch seiner Schwacheit halben (darumb wir gleichwol keine eigentliche gelegenheit wissen) entschüldigen lassen.

Die begerte Papiermacher haben wir bißhero vber angewendten fleiß nicht bekommen können/ Wir haben aber vnsern Dienern/ so wir jetzt angehende Franckfurder Meß abgefertigt/ befolen/ daß sie sich daselbst fleissig vmbhören sollen/ ob sie dieselbigen zuwegen bringen mögen/ Was nu dieselbigen außrichten/ davon solt jr hiernechst berichtet werden.

Was wir dißmal von Zeitungen bey handen gehabt/ davon thun wir euch hierneben Abschrifft zufertigen/ vnd werdet jr darauß vernemen/ wie es in Franckreich vnd andern orten jetziger zeit stehet/ vnd daß es miserrimus Status totius Europæ ist. Was jhr dan de Statu Daniæ vnd sonst newes habt/ begeren wir gnediglich/ vns solchs hinwider zu communiciren.

Vnd bleiben euch mit gnedigem guten Willen wolgewogen.
Datum in vnser Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel/ am 31. Augusti, Anno 1591.
WILHELM LANDSGRAFF ZU HESSEN

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI, p. 241; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
POSTSCRIPTA. WAN auch ewere Obseruationes Stellarum Fixarum & erraticarum fertig, möchten wir dieselbige wol haben, mit den vnsern zu rectificiren. Mit dem Orix wollet fleissig nachfragen, ob vnd an was orten man es finde, vnd wie es allenthalben qualificiret. Dan Brieffszeiger ewer Vetter Ascanius Bilde, wil dero im Thiergarten zu Coppenhagen vnd zu Croneburg oder Friderichsburg effictae gesehen haben, Aber bey weitem deserviret er es nicht so groß alß Gesnerus, dan 23. Span ist ein grosse höhe von einem Thier, doch ists dem Creatori nicht vnmöglich.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the honourable, Our trusty and well-beloved, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup.
Wilhelm, by the grace of God, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, etc.

Our gracious greeting beforehand. Our trusty and well-beloved: we would not graciously withhold from you that we have in Our deer-park an elk, which has indeed borne up well through this winter and summer and has put on good condition, and which lacks nothing else save that it has no companion. Since, then, We understood from your most recent letter, dated 2 April, that you too had an elk, and were willing to let Us have it, We accept it from you with gracious thanks; and We have accordingly not wished to refrain from dispatching the present messenger to you, and from charging him to fetch the said elk from you and bring it to Us. If, then, you will now let Us have the same, according to your written undertaking — as indeed We do expect of you — that will be done to Our agreeable pleasure, and We will requite it to you hereafter with a fine [cask of] Zapfenburger, and otherwise remain graciously mindful [of you].

We do not doubt, moreover, that you will have read in the German copy of Gesner On the Four-Footed Animals what he writes at folio 85 of an animal that is called Rix, or, as some will have it, Orix. We should gladly know whether you have learned anything about it, or have spoken with people who have seen it or can give report of it — what the circumstances of it are, where it is found, and whether it is of as great a stature as it is depicted by Gesner.

Our mathematician Christoph Rothmann has not yet come back to Us; and although We did indeed summon him hither to Us, he has nonetheless excused himself on the ground of his ill health (the precise occasion of which We do not, however, rightly know).

The paper-makers you desired We have hitherto, for all the diligence applied, not been able to obtain; but We have charged Our servants, whom We have now dispatched to the coming Frankfurt fair, that they shall diligently make inquiry there whether they may procure the same. What they then accomplish, of that you shall be informed hereafter.

Such tidings as We have had to hand this time, of these We send you a transcript herewith; and from it you will perceive how matters stand in France and in other places at the present time, and that it is a most wretched state of all Europe. What, then, you have concerning the state of Denmark and other news, We graciously desire you to communicate the same to Us in return.

And We remain well-disposed toward you with gracious good will.
Given in Our city and fortress of Kassel, on 31 August, in the year 1591.
Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse.

[Postscript:] Postscript. When, too, your observations of the fixed and the wandering stars are ready, We should gladly have the same, to rectify them with Our own. About the oryx We would have you diligently inquire — whether, and in what places, it is to be found, and how it is everywhere qualified [of what nature it is]; for the bearer of this letter, your kinsman Ascanius Bilde, will have it that he has seen some of the kind in the deer-park at Copenhagen and at Kronborg or Frederiksborg. But by his account it is by far not so large as Gesner [makes it]; for 23 spans is a great height for an animal — yet to the Creator it is not impossible.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Wilhelm von Gottes Gnaden, Landtgraue zu Hessen = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the "astronomer-prince" of Kassel, Tycho's leading princely correspondent and the patron of the Kassel observatory; Graue zu Catzenelnbogen = Count of Katzenelnbogen (an inherited Hessian comital title). The princely Wir / euch register and full titulature are preserved throughout, by the edition's convention
  • Recipient address. DEM VESTEN / VNSERM LIEBEN BESONDERN / TYCHONI BRAHE, HERRN ZU KNUDSTRUP — "To the honourable [vest = fest, the standard chancery honorific for a nobleman], Our trusty and well-beloved [Vnserm lieben Besondern, the fixed princely address-formula, the edition's convention], Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup" (Tycho's ancestral Scanian seat).
  • Opening formula. VNSERN gnedigen Gruß zuvor = "Our gracious greeting beforehand".
  • Subject — the elk exchange. Elendsthier / Elendthier / Elendt = elk (moose) (Frühneuhochdeutsch Elend(t) "elk"); the Landgrave's elk in his Thiergarten ("deer-park, menagerie") lacks a Gehülffe ("companion, mate"), and he takes up Tycho's prior offer (in a letter sub dato den 2. Aprilis, "dated 2 April") of a second elk from Hven.
  • Latin code-switches. sub dato = "dated"; the German text switches into Latin for set phrases, in the chancery manner: miserrimus Status totius Europae = "a most wretched state of all Europe"; de Statu Daniae = "concerning the state of Denmark"; communiciren = the Latinate verb "to communicate."
  • Zapffenburger — a wine. The term denotes a wine of the Zapfenberg type (a sweet/strong wine of the kind dispensed vom Zapfen, "from the spigot"); rendered "a fine [cask of] Zapfenburger," with [cask of] bracketed as an editorial supplement since the source names only the wine, not its vessel. The Landgrave offers it as a Gegengeschenk (return-gift) for the elk.
  • The Gesner zoological query. dem Deutschen Exemplari Gesneri de Animalibus Quadrupedibus = "the German copy of Gesner's On the Four-Footed Animals" — Conrad Gesner's Historia animalium (Liber I, De quadrupedibus uiuiparis, Zurich 1551), in its German translation (the Thierbuch). fol. 85 is cited as written. The animal Rix … oder … Orix is the classical oryx (the antelope of Pliny and Aelian); the Landgrave asks Tycho for natural-historical intelligence on it.
  • Latin natural-history crux. vbi inveniatur, et si tansae proceritatis, quantae a Gesnero pingitur, siet — "where it is found, and whether it is of as great a stature as it is depicted by Gesner." Two printed forms flagged, not silently corrected: tansae is read as tantae ("so great / as great"), and siet as an archaising/poetic form of sit ("it be"). Both readings are adopted in the translation while the printed forms are kept in the Source block; neither alters the sense.
  • Vnser Mathematicus Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician at Kassel (and himself a long-standing Tycho correspondent). He had left Kassel in 1590 — ostensibly to visit Hven — and never returned to his post; here the Landgrave reports that Rothmann has excused himself seiner Schwacheit halben ("on the ground of his ill health"), the cause of which the Landgrave professes not to know.
  • die begerte Papiermacher — "the paper-makers you desired": Tycho had asked the Landgrave's help in recruiting skilled paper-makers (for his paper-mill on Hven); the Landgrave reports he could not get them, but has set his servants to inquire at the Frankfurt fair (Franckfurder Meß, the great autumn book-and-trade fair).
  • Zeitungen — "tidings, news-reports" (here in the early sense of circulated news-sheets / dispatches, not a printed newspaper); the Landgrave encloses a transcript (Abschrifft) reporting the wars in France (the French Wars of Religion).
  • Orthography (chancery German, c. 1591). v/u for u/v (vnserm, vber, vns); jr / jm / jn = ihr / ihm / ihn; daß … daß used for both conjunction and relative das; soli is a printer's setting of soll; thun / thut = tun / tut; Weyl = weil; gnediglich = gnädiglich.
  • Calendar / place. Datum in vnser Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel / am 31. Augusti, Anno 1591 = "Given in Our city and fortress of Kassel, on 31 August, in the year 1591" (O.S.; Hesse-Kassel, a Protestant state, kept the Julian calendar at this date). The dateline agrees with the edition label.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. German closing postscript, restored from Dreyer VI, p. 241 (dropped by the working transcription); the bearer "Ascanius Bilde", the "23 spans", and the oryx reading confirmed against the facing Latin (VI, p. 242). This is the source of the "23 spans" datum the next letter (15910926001) refers back to.
