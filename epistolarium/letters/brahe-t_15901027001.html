The original

PETRO IUNIO TYCHO BRAHE

ETSI nihil ad me tanto tempore dederis literarum, nobilissime et eruditissime D. PETRE IUNI, utut præsens te id sæpius facturum addixeris: ego nihilominus oblata hac oportuna per hunc eruditum iuvenem occasione, intermittere nolui, quin aliquid saltem ad te chartis exararem, teque iamdudum inter nos contractæ amicitiæ atque tuarum promissionum commonefacerem. Cupio autem quam maxime ex te cognoscere, quomodo serenissimus et laudatissimus Rex vester cum suavissima Regina nostrate vivat et valeat, rogoque peramanter, ut ipsius Maiestati meo nomine submisse gratias agere velis, quod meas Uraniæ dicatas ædes in hac insula invisere non sit dedignata, omniaque prospera et ex voto ipsius Maiestati ex me preceris atque exoptes. Si ab ipsius Maiestate privilegium quoddam pro meis typographicis laboribus, quos et præsens spectavit, ne videlicet in Scotia recudantur aut divendantur, absque meo concessu, tua authoritate atque intercessione impetrâris, feceris mihi rem pluribus nominibus gratam. Mitto autem tibi exemplar privilegij Cæsarei mihi nuper procurante amplissimo viro D. IACOBO CURTIO, Imperij Pro-Cancellario, clementer concessi et in membrana conscripti. Addam etiam unâ, si nuntij festinatio feret, ipsius Procancellarij literarum ad me unâ datarum descriptum: quo hac ratione promptius concipies, quomodo privilegium illud, quod expeto, ordinandum veniat. Quamvis Cæsareum illud imitari non sit admodum necesse, tua eloquendi et res ipsas scitis verbis complectendi eximia facultas, facile quid scribendum sit, suggeret. Optarem vero privilegium illud, si non ante hiemem, saltem circa veris primordia, quamprimum huc naves e Scotia solvant, obtinere, ut operibus meis unâ cum Cæsareano atque aliorum quorundam Regum diplomatis præfigatur. Quin et aliud quiddam est, mi IUNI, de quo te commonefacere non supersedendum duxi. Addixisti, cum mecum esses, te non solum BUCHANANI effigiem, sed etiam ipsius vitam a te ipso conscriptam, mihi e Scotia missurum. Effigiem quidem accepi, quam et Serenissimus Rex vester, quamprimum in musæo meo conspexit, hilari vultu agnovit. Vitæ descriptionem adhuc desidero: fac in utroque stes promissis. Rogo insuper, ut D. Doctorem IOHANNEM CRAGIUM, medicinam Edinburgi facientem et præstantissimum, si quis alius in Scotia, Mathematicum, Regi vestro de meliore (ut aiunt) nota commendatum habeas. Est enim dignus, ut ametur et promoveatur. Neque enim ægre fero, quod libro nostro secundo de recentioribus ætherei mundi phænomenis quædam contraria opposuerit eademque ad me perscripserit, quibus etiam apologetice satis luculenter obviavi. Sed hominem eo plus diligo, quod eiuscemodi attentare ausus sit, omnibus in Germania eruditis Mathematicis, suas priores conceptiones, quas dilui, retractantibus, silentibus et quasi obmutescentibus; quemadmodum ipse suo tempore plenius cognosces. Plura nunc non addo, cum et hæc ipsa forte nimia sint, ex quo tu tam diutino silentio distineris. Sin tandem aliqua scripseris, ego crebrius et copiosius; nec sinam me ullo officij genere a te superari.

Vale.
Datæ Uraniburgi die 27. Oct. An. 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE.

Tycho Brahe to Peter Young

Although you have sent me no letter in so long a time, most noble and most learned Master Peter Young — for all that, when you were here, you pledged you would do so more often — I nonetheless, with this opportune occasion offered through this learned young man, was unwilling to let it pass without at least scratching something out to you on paper, and reminding you of the friendship contracted between us this while since, and of your promises. I greatly desire to learn from you how your most serene and most praised King fares and prospers, together with our most charming Queen; and I most affectionately ask that you be willing, in my name, humbly to render thanks to His Majesty, that he did not disdain to visit my house dedicated to Urania on this island, and that on my behalf you pray and wish His Majesty all things prosperous and according to his desire. If from His Majesty you should obtain, by your authority and intercession, a certain privilege for my typographical labours — those which he also viewed while present — to the effect, namely, that they not be reprinted or sold off in Scotland without my consent, you will do me a thing welcome on several counts. I am sending you, moreover, a copy of the Imperial privilege graciously granted to me lately, through the procurement of the most distinguished man Master Jakob Kurtz, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, and written out on parchment. I shall add as well, if the messenger's haste allow, a transcript of the Vice-Chancellor's own letter sent to me along with it: so that by this means you may the more readily grasp how that privilege which I seek ought to be drawn up. Although it is not at all necessary to imitate that Imperial one, your outstanding faculty of expression — of embracing the matters themselves in apt words — will easily suggest what should be written. I should wish, indeed, to obtain that privilege, if not before winter, then at least about the first beginnings of spring, as soon as ships set sail hither from Scotland, so that it may be set at the front of my works together with the Imperial diploma and those of certain other Kings. But there is also another matter, my dear Young, of which I have judged that I ought not to forbear reminding you. You pledged, when you were with me, that you would send me from Scotland not only Buchanan's portrait, but also his life, written by yourself. The portrait, to be sure, I have received — which your most serene King too, as soon as he beheld it in my study, recognized with a cheerful countenance. The account of his life I still want: see that in both you stand by your promises. I ask besides that you should hold Master Doctor John Craig — who practises medicine at Edinburgh and is a most excellent mathematician, if any other in Scotland is — commended to your King on the better footing (as they say). For he is worthy to be loved and advanced. Nor indeed do I take it ill that in our second book on the more recent phenomena of the ethereal world he set certain contrary things in opposition, and wrote the same out to me, which I also met, apologetically, clearly enough; but I esteem the man the more for this, that he dared to attempt something of this kind, while all the learned mathematicians in Germany are retracting their earlier conceptions, which I refuted, falling silent and as it were struck dumb — as you yourself will learn more fully in your own time. I add no more now, since even these very things are perhaps too much, given that you are held back by so long a silence. But if at length you write something, I [shall write] more often and more copiously; nor shall I allow myself to be outdone by you in any kind of courtesy.

Farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, the 27th day of October, the year 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on the island of Hven (Uraniburgum, "house dedicated to Urania," the muse of astronomy — the phrase meas Uraniae dicatas aedes puns on the name of the residence).
  • Recipient. Petrus Iunius = Peter Young (1544–1628), Scottish humanist, former tutor to James VI of Scotland and a royal envoy. He had accompanied (or been part of the circle of) the king's visit to Hven. The Latinized surname Iunius/Iuni (vocative Iuni) renders "Young."
  • The King and Queen. serenissimus … Rex vester = James VI of Scotland (later James I of England), who visited Uraniborg in spring 1590 while in Denmark for his marriage; suavissima Regina nostrate = his queen, Anne of Denmark ("our [own countrywoman]," daughter of Frederik II). Confirmed in the literature consulted (Dreyer, Tycho Brahe; Tycho Brahe and Prague).
  • The royal visit. meas … aedes in hac insula invisere non sit dedignata — the visit of James VI to Hven; Tycho asks Young to convey humble thanks. The deferential third-person ipsius Maiestati / non sit dedignata (singular Majesty) is kept.
  • Scottish privilege. privilegium … pro meis typographicis laboribus … ne … in Scotia recudantur aut divendantur, absque meo concessu — Tycho seeks a royal Scottish printing-privilege forbidding the unauthorized reprinting or sale of his works (printed at his own Uraniborg press) in Scotland, parallel to privileges he held elsewhere.
  • Imperial privilege. privilegium Caesareum … procurante … D. IACOBO CURTIO, Imperij Pro-Cancellario = Jakob Kurtz von Senftenau (Jacobus Curtius), Imperial Vice-Chancellor (Pro-Cancellarius / Procancellarius), through whom Tycho had obtained an Imperial printing-privilege, granted in membrana (on parchment). Caesareum diploma / Caesareano … diplomati rendered "Imperial diploma."
  • Buchanan. BUCHANANI effigiem … ipsius vitam a te ipso conscriptam = a portrait of George Buchanan (1506–1582), the great Scottish humanist and historian (and James VI's tutor), together with a life of Buchanan written by Young himself. Tycho acknowledges receiving the portrait but is still awaiting the vita.
  • John Craig. D. Doctorem IOHANNEM CRAGIUM, medicinam Edinburgi facientem … Mathematicum = John Craig (d. 1620), the Scots Aristotelian physician and mathematician at Edinburgh with whom Tycho conducted an epistolary dispute over whether the comet of 1577 was supralunary; Craig had objected to claims in Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (Uraniborg, 1588). Tycho here speaks warmly of him and asks Young to commend him to the king de meliore nota ("on the better footing," a Ciceronian idiom, flagged with ut aiunt "as they say").
  • libro nostro secundo de recentioribus aetherei mundi phaenomenis — "our second book on the more recent phenomena of the ethereal world" = the second part of Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588), treating the comet of 1577. contraria opposuerit … apologetice … obviavi: Craig raised objections, which Tycho says he answered apologetically (i.e. in defence/refutation), satis luculenter "clearly enough." The author's own hedge is preserved.
  • hunc eruditum iuvenem — "this learned young man," the unnamed bearer of the letter, the occasio prompting it.
  • musaeo meo — "my study" (the Museum, a scholar's workroom), as in the Wolf letter, not a public museum.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading PETRO IUNIO TYCHO BRAHE (dative addressee + nominative sender), with no salutation verb; Vale → "Farewell"; nec sinam me ullo officij genere a te superari keeps the courtesy-competition topos ("nor shall I allow myself to be outdone … in any kind of courtesy").
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgi die 27. Oct. An. 1590 = "Given at Uraniborg, 27 October 1590" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label; no discrepancy.
  • Conditional logic (checked). Two future-more-vivid conditions govern the requests: Si … impetrâris, feceris mihi rem … gratam (Scottish privilege) and Sin tandem aliqua scripseris, ego crebrius (the closing reciprocity). Both are positive (no negation reversed); the single negation in ne … recudantur aut divendantur (the privilege is to forbid reprinting/selling) is preserved as a prohibitive clause.
