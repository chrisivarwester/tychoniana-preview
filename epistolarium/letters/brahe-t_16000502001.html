The original

SERENISSIMO PRINCIPI ET DOMINO, DOMINO MATTHIÆ, ARCHIDUCI AUSTRIÆ, DUCI BURGUNDIÆ, STYRIÆ, CARINTHIÆ, CARNIOLÆ ET WIRTTENBERGÆ, COMITI TIROLIS, DOMINO SUO CLEMENTISSIMO.
Serenissime et Inclyte Princeps, Domine Clementissime.

QUOD Serenissimam Celsitudinem Vestram ignotus et peregrinus ego hisce compellare audeo, id ut pro sua Heroica et congenita Virtute atque clementia candide et benigne accipere dignetur, humilime oro. Impulit me ad hanc audaciam celeberrima fama de Sermæ Celnis Vestræ benignissimo affectu erga quasvis artes ingenuas, præsertim sublimiores illas, quas Mathematicas per excellentiam vocant. Quemadmodum enim Augustissimus et Potentissimus Imperator RUDOLPHUS, Væ Sæ Cnis Dominus Frater, hæc studia plurimi facit atque illis laudabiliter delectatur, sic et Vm Sm Celnem quadam non minus naturæ quam sanguinis conformitate illa pariter in precio habere a plurimis affirmatur: cuius etiam haud obscura τεκμύρια, cum ante annos plusculos, Daniam Patriam meam transeundo, laudatissimæ memoriæ Regem FRIDERICUM Sa Va Co illic invisisset, ad me perlata sunt, quin et paulo post ab Arcis Cronburgicæ Præside mihi insinuatum Sm Cm Vm in Insulam prioris habitationis meæ Uraniburgicam, Instrumentorum, quæ in usum Astronomicæ instaurationis illic construxeram, visendorum gratiâ transfretare constituisse, quod et factum fuisset, nisi maris irrequietudo et glaciei interveniens importunitas propositum hoc impedivissent. Ex eo tempore cœpi Smam Celenm Vm pro meo modulo suspicere et venerari; optassemque, ut consilium illud in Insulam dictam traijciendi successus ratos consecutum fuisset. Cum autem nunc gravibus de caussis, relicta Patria, uti fert rerum Mundanarum vicissitudo, in ditionem Potentissimi Imperatoris Regnumque Bohemiæ ab eius Cæsa Maiestate huc clementissime accitus, transmigrarim, sicque Smæ Celni Væ vicinior redditus sim, eo etiam nunc promptius et confidentius animum mihi sumo aliquid ad Serenissimam Celnem Vam literarum dandi, ut erga eam debitum meum obsequium vel aliqua saltem ratione humilime declarem, præsertim cum adeo oportuna id efficiendi modo sese obtulerit occasio, per hunc Generosum et in literis ac linguis bene versatum Iuvenem FRIDERICUM ROSENKRANTZIUM, Sympatriotam et Consangvineum meum, ad vestram Smam Celnem proficiscentem. Cumque Scra Cæsa Maitas, Dominus meus clementissimus, sua Augusta apud Vam Serm Celnem commendatione et intercessione (quam ROSENKRANTZIUS secum apportat) eum non dedignata sit, supervacaneum et indecens duco quippiam, quod apud Serm Celnem Vm illi promotioni esse possit, ulterius intempestive repetere aut adiungere, hoc saltem dicam, quicquid clementiæ et benevolentiæ Serenissima Celo Va in illum contulerit, id non saltem illi, sed toti Rosenkrantzianæ familiæ, quæ in inclyto Regno Daniæ antiquissima et splendidissima est, tum quoque mihi, qui eiusdem stemmatis aliqua pars sum, beneficio nunquam memoria obliterando et officiosissima mente deprædicando præstituram. Idque de hoc viro polliceri possum, eum Sermæ Væ Celsni fidelem et obsequentissimum futurum, sive privatim in sua Aula, sive publice in bello Ungarico contra Turcam, eius servitiô et qualicunque operâ (est enim ad utrumque humilime paratus) uti voluerit. Deliquit is quidem humanitus et iuvenili quodam lapsu, quod dolendum est, et ipsummet ita contigisse vehementer et ex animo pœnitet, nec suum hoc delictum celare aut excusare præsumit, unde in Regis sui, cui per sexennium in cubiculo fidelissime inservierat, indignationem tantam incidit, ut stimulantibus invidis et osoribus, nullus fuerit veniæ locus, quicquid tandem pro culpæ condonatione et redemptione ipse et illi sanguine iuncti humilime offerrent; attamen sperat se, divina fretus misericordia et bonitate, alibi extra Patriam, potissimum vero in Sermæ Væ Celnis servitio, hanc deplorandæ sortis adversitatem virtute et integritate sua ita superaturum, atque priora illa, fragilitate quadam humana imprudenter commissa, honeste et fortiter gestis diluiturum atque emendaturum, ut tandem, auxiliante divina clementia et Virorum Principum diligenti intercessione apud Regem suum culpæ remissionem submisse impetrare atque in integrum restitui possit, maximo omnium consangvinitate et affinitate ipsi iunctorum, cuiusmodi sunt Nobilissimi quique in Regno Daniæ, gaudio et applausu. Confidimus autem omnes, quotquot eius sortem ex animo dolemus et ad meliora ipsum restitui exoptamus, apud Sam Celnem Vam occasiones et media inventurum, quibus fortunæ asperitatem mitiget atque sermæ Celnis Væ clementissimo favore atque indultu eam vincere et ad potiora emergere possit. Quod faxit misericors et omnis boni largitor Deus.

Cæterum Sermæ Celni Væ per dictum ROSENKRANTZIUM submisse mitto duos libros, quos Sacræ Cæsæ Maiestati, Domino et Mæcenati meo clementissimo, quam primum Pragam veneram, obtuli, et unâ addo globum quendam cælestem e mea inerrantium stellarum accurata et diutino tempore exantlata restitutione apposite conformatum; quæ a tenuitate mea profecta munuscula, ut Sma Celo Va clementi animo accipere, et me meaque studia sibi commendata habere non dedignetur, qua possum reverentia et submissione oro et obsecro. Valeat Serma Celo Va in Reipubæ stabilimentum quam diutissime et florentissime, et successu contra truculentum Christiani nominis hostem per Dei benignitatem felici in posterum quoque utatur.

Dabantur ex Arce Cæsarea Bennaticka die 2. Maij Anni 1600.
Sereniss. Cels. Vestræ addictiss.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most serene Prince and Lord, Lord Matthias, Archduke of Austria, Duke of Burgundy, Styria, Carinthia, Carniola and Württemberg, Count of Tirol, his most clement Lord.
Most serene and renowned Prince, most clement Lord.

That I, unknown and a foreigner, dare to address Your most serene Highness by these [lines], I humbly pray You may deign to take candidly and kindly, according to Your heroic and inborn Virtue and clemency. What impelled me to this boldness was the most celebrated report of Your most serene Highness's most kindly disposition toward all liberal arts — especially those loftier ones which by excellence they call Mathematical. For just as the most august and most powerful Emperor Rudolf, Your most serene Highness's Lord Brother, makes much of these studies and laudably delights in them, so Your most serene Highness too is affirmed by very many to hold them in like esteem, by a conformity no less of nature than of blood — of which no obscure tokens [τεκμήρια] were brought to me when, some years ago, passing through Denmark my fatherland, Your Highness visited there the King Frederick, of most praised memory; nay, and a little after, it was intimated to me by the Governor of Kronborg Castle that Your Highness had resolved to cross over to my Island of Uraniborg, the seat of my former dwelling, to see the Instruments which I had constructed there for the use of the Restoration of Astronomy — which would have been done, had not the restlessness of the sea and the intervening trouble of ice hindered this purpose. From that time I began, according to my small measure, to look up to and venerate Your most serene Highness; and I could have wished that that plan of crossing over to the said Island had met with sure success. But since now, for grave causes — my fatherland left behind, as the vicissitude of worldly things brings — I have migrated, most clemently called hither by His Imperial Majesty into the dominion of the most powerful Emperor and the Kingdom of Bohemia, and am thus made a nearer neighbour to Your most serene Highness, the more promptly and confidently do I now take heart to send some letter to Your Highness, that I may humbly declare my due obedience toward You, at least in some fashion — especially since so opportune an occasion of doing it has offered itself, through this noble Youth, well versed in letters and tongues, Friedrich Rosenkrantz, my compatriot and kinsman, setting out to Your most serene Highness. And since the Sacred Imperial Majesty, my most clement Lord, has not disdained to favour him with His august commendation and intercession to Your most serene Highness (which Rosenkrantz brings with him), I judge it superfluous and unseemly to repeat or add untimely anything further that might further his advancement with Your Highness; this only will I say: whatever clemency and benevolence Your most serene Highness shall confer upon him, it will be conferred not only on him, but on the whole Rosenkrantz family — which in the renowned Kingdom of Denmark is most ancient and most splendid — and also on me, who am some part of the same lineage, by a benefit never to be effaced from memory and to be proclaimed with a most dutiful mind. And this I can promise of this man: that he will be faithful and most obedient to Your most serene Highness — whether privately in Your Court, or publicly in the Hungarian war against the Turk — for whatever service and work You may wish to use him (for he is humbly ready for either). He erred indeed humanly, by a certain youthful lapse, which is to be regretted, and he vehemently and from his heart repents that it so fell out, nor does he presume to conceal or excuse this his fault; whereby he fell into so great an indignation of his King — whom for six years he had most faithfully served in the chamber — that, the envious and the haters goading, there was no place for pardon, whatever he and those bound to him by blood at last humbly offered for the remission and redemption of the fault. Yet he hopes that, relying on divine mercy and goodness, elsewhere outside his fatherland — but above all in Your most serene Highness's service — he will so overcome this adversity of his lamentable lot by his virtue and integrity, and so dilute and amend those former things (imprudently committed by a certain human frailty) by deeds done honourably and bravely, that at last, with divine clemency aiding and the diligent intercession of Princely Men, he may humbly obtain remission of the fault from his King and be restored to his full standing — to the greatest joy and applause of all joined to him by kinship and affinity, such as are all the noblest in the Kingdom of Denmark. And we all — as many of us as grieve his lot from the heart and long for him to be restored to better things — trust that he will find with Your Highness occasions and means whereby he may soften the harshness of fortune, and by Your most serene Highness's most clement favour and indulgence overcome it and emerge to better things. Which may the merciful God, the bestower of all good, bring to pass.

For the rest, I humbly send Your most serene Highness, by the said Rosenkrantz, two books which I offered to the Sacred Imperial Majesty, my Lord and most clement Maecenas, as soon as I had come to Prague; and I add, together with them, a certain celestial Globe, fittingly fashioned from my accurate restitution of the fixed stars, drawn out over a long time. These little gifts, proceeding from my slenderness, I pray and beseech, with what reverence and submission I can, that Your most serene Highness may not disdain to accept with a clement mind, and to hold me and my studies commended to You. May Your most serene Highness fare well, for the establishment of the Commonwealth, most long and most flourishingly, and enjoy hereafter too, by God's goodness, happy success against the truculent enemy of the Christian name.

Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 2nd day of May, the year 1600.
Your most serene Highness's most devoted,
Tycho Brahe, in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Archduke Matthias of Austria (1557–1619), brother of Rudolf II and the future Emperor Matthias — addressed with full titulature (Archduke of Austria; Duke of Burgundy, Styria, Carinthia, Carniola, Württemberg; Count of Tirol).
  • Significance — the Friedrich Rosenkrantz recommendation (resolves the unnamed kinsman of the Rindschadt letter). This is the formal recommendation carried by Friedrich (Frederik) Rosenkrantz — and thereby names the "gentleman of the Rosenkrantz family, my near kinsman," left unnamed in the Danish Kepler-letter brahe-t_16000000001, who was to take Kepler to Vienna and seek Archduke Matthias's service. The two letters are the same spring-1600 episode from two angles.
  • The disgrace (handled with delicacy). Tycho records, without naming the offence, that Rosenkrantz "erred humanly, by a youthful lapse," fell into "so great an indignation of his King" — Christian IV, whom he had served six years in the chamber — that "the envious goading, there was no place for pardon." This is the Frederik Rosenkrantz / Rigborg Brockenhuus scandal (the convicted seduction affair that drove Rosenkrantz into exile; he served and died in the Hungarian war, 1602). Tycho casts the exile as a path to redemption "by deeds done honourably and bravely … in Your service," with hope of eventual royal pardon.
  • Kinship pride. Tycho stresses the Rosenkrantz family is "most ancient and splendid in Denmark" and that he is himself "some part of the same lineage" — a benefit to Matthias's favour would touch the whole house.
  • Biographical gem — Matthias's intended visit to Uraniborg. Tycho recalls that, years before, Matthias passed through Denmark and visited King Frederick II, and that the Governor of Kronborg intimated Matthias had resolved to cross to Hven to see Tycho's instruments — prevented only by "the restlessness of the sea and the trouble of ice." A previously-cherished near-encounter, now invoked.
  • A documented gift — two books + a celestial globe. Tycho sends Matthias, via Rosenkrantz, "two books which I offered the Emperor on coming to Prague" (from the imperial-presentation set — cf. brahe-t_16001110001, the Mechanica / star-Restitutio / Progymnasmata program) and a celestial globe "fittingly fashioned from my accurate restitution of the fixed stars." A datable Tycho celestial-globe and book distribution event (Benátky → Archduke Matthias, May 1600).
  • Persons & places named. Archduke Matthias; Rudolf II (his brother); Friedrich/Frederik Rosenkrantz (the disgraced kinsman, bearer); King Frederick II of Denmark (the late king Matthias had visited); Christian IV (Rosenkrantz's offended King, unnamed as "his King"); the Governor of Kronborg (unnamed). Places: Benátky, Prague, Denmark, Hven/Uraniborg, Kronborg, Hungary.
  • Cross-references. Resolves the unnamed Rosenkrantz of brahe-t_16000000001 → Friedrich Rosenkrantz; register the celestial-globe + two-book gift to Archduke Matthias with the Mechanica/instrument distribution layer and celestial-globe history.
  • Calendar. die 2. Maij Anni 1600 at Benátky (imperial reckoning) → 2 May 1600 [N.S.]; header and label agree.
