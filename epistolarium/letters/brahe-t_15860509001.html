The original

THADDÆUS HAGECIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.
S.

SERO admodum redditæ sunt mihi literæ tuæ, amicorum optime, nihilo minus fuerunt gratissimæ. Sors iniqua fecit, ut neque tuæ ad me tempestive afferri, neque meæ ad te perferri potuerint. Tuæ enim 25. Augusti scriptæ mihi demum 18. Martij redditæ sunt. Aiebat ille, qui eas attulit, tempestate aëris in itinere fuisse remoratum. Ego vero longe ante, quam tuas accepissem, in ipsis ferijs Nativitatis D. nactus quandam oportunitatem et spem perveniendi isthuc D. SEGURIUM, eius servo et meo populari dederam meas ad te unâ cum meis illis lucubrationibus, quæ si habuissent cursum felicem, tuo desiderio tempestive aliqva ex parte satisfactum fuisset. Pervenerant autem meæ hinc Francofurtum usque. Et ecce, accidit quidpiam, quod D. SEGURIUS consilium eundi in Daniam mutare coactus fuerit, et meæ literæ cum lucubrationibus meis illinc sunt ad me remissæ, quemadmodum hæc omnia intelliges ex meis literis hisce nunc adiunctis, quas illo tempore scripseram. Vides, mi TYCHO, quomodo nostri sensus in unum conspiraverint, modo fortuna favere voluisset optatis nostris, cui iam iam, ne amplius nos eluderet, occurrere ac proprium nuncium ad te mittere volui. Mitto igitur ad te bonum et fidelem iuvenem, qui stipendio a me conductus aliquando in mea domo servivit arte sartoria, cuius est peritus. Is me deliberante de Tabellario ad te ablegando et quærendo, num aliqui Rostochienses hîc essent, cum nullum invenirem, et tempus laberetur, suam mihi hic iuvenis obtulit operam, iturum se ad te, si modo sufficiens viaticum essem ei daturus. Accepi conditionem, et eius voluntatem sum amplexus libentissime. Dedi viaticum perveniendi isthuc, tu vicissim eidem ut redeat ad me, prospicies et laborem eius compensabis. Affert autem tibi primum Privilegium impressorium, quale petebas, non absque difficultate impetratum, eo quod non soleant eiusmodi Privilegia ulli concedi, nisi prius manuscripto exemplo in Cancellariam deposito. Nonnullos enim impetrasse eiusmodi Privilegia pro certis quibusdam libris, qui nec inchoati quidem fuerunt et prodierunt nunquam. Evici tamen meo studio, ut tibi in genere tale daretur Privilegium, quale alius habet nemo. Tu eo fruere ad commodum Reipublicæ literariæ. Taxam pro eo dedi in Cancellariam 12½ Fl., quemadmodum ex schedula Taxatoris Privilegio adiuncta cognosces. Affert item tibi meum exemplum TRAPEZUNTIJ PIRCKMANNUS enim Coloniæ cum a me librum magno studio impetrasset, spe facta illum suis typis edendi multis diagrammatis deterritus, quod magnos sumtus requirerent, mutavit sententiam, ac tandem librum ad me remisit; qui mihi profecto semper admodum charus fuit. Ac certe voluissem ex animo illum facere communem omnibus studiosis, nec dubitabam omnibus scholis futurum admodum gratum; imo isthoc interprete Studiosi introducti fuissent ad lectionem operum PTOLEMÆI, inque eo potius quam in SACROBUSTI libello ieiuno versati fuissent. Ac si saltem primi duo libri aut tres ederentur, sufficerent scholis. Primus liber, scis, olim fuit a REINHOLDO editus et scholijs lllustratus; is posset TRAPEZUNTIJ commentarijs inseri. Sed tu de editione statues, quod tibi optimum videbitur. Interea utere et fruere meo libro, et, si tibi placuerit, describi cura. Sin displicuerit, remitte per eundem hominem. Mitto etiam tibi libellum de Directionum Significationibus. CARIONIS esse dicitur; quanquam existimabam, te Ratisponæ illum descripsisse. Libellus WERNERI de motu Octavæ Sphæræ non invenitur amplius venalis. Fuit adiunctus alijs quibusdam WERNERI opusculis, quæ iam nusquam apparent. Hisce diebus accepi literas Francofurto a Nobilibus illis Gallis una cum Diario a tuo ELIA in tua Officina edito. Hoc mihi fuit admodum gratum, non tantum quod ab illis esset mihi donatum, sed quod tuum nomen asscriptum conspicerem, ad cuius conspectum statim verberavit animum cogitatio, tuum hunc genuinum esse partum; quem legens ordine videbar teipsum loquentem audire. In quo, si non fallor, dico prudenter factum esse, quod nomen tuum non sis professus, illudque maioribus tuis operibus reserves. Licet, ut ingenue fatear, non est vulgare illud Diarium, neque conferri ullo modo potest vulgatis illis Diarijs, quæ isti Diarifices annuatim edere consueverunt. Nam et Cometæ proximi, de quo mihi plane constabat nihil, accuratam habet descriptionem; et motus ☉ et ☾ ex tua observatione habet constitutos. Sed vulgus hominum nescit discernere inter aurum et orichalcum. Itaque prudenter eos facere existimo, qui in levioribus istis vulgi iudicio aut nomen suum non profitentur aut suis discipulis illum honorem relinquunt.

Designatione tuæ insulæ admodum sum delectatus. Unum eius exemplum reliqui in Cancellaria Cæsari monstrandum. Armillæ et Sextantis tui picturam sum contemplatus; quam vellem ipsam machinam videre, et si paulo essem iunior, accurrerem ad te.

Quod tam liberaliter de omnibus mecum communicas, magnas tibi ago gratias. Meas lucubrationes de Nova Stella et Cometis duobus mitto ad te. Quæ illis sunt adiuncta, videbis in ipso opere. Si iudicaveris precium operæ fore, ut uno volumine cum tuis, quæ habes, edantur, omnia tuo iudicio relinquo. Sin putaveris commode edi non posse, aut mea aliena esse a tuis, mea rursum ad me remitte. Nam nuper ad me scripsit magnus quidam et doctus vir, in editionem impensas se facere velle. Sed ego cum tuis mea prodire mallem, quæ, ut spero, non sunt futura dissona. Alios scriptores de Cometis proxime præteritis, præter illos, quos meæ Dialexi seu meis opusculis adiunxi, non habeo. MÆSTLINI de Cometa anni 77 scriptum te vidisse existimo. B. V. et Dom. fratrem tuum meis verbis saluta officiosissime, ac etiam eius collegam, quem hîc secum habuit. Pragæ 19. Maij iuxta novum, aut 9 iuxta vetus Calendarium, Anno 1586.

Thaddaeus Hagecius to Tycho Brahe. Greeting.

Your letter was delivered to me very late, best of friends; none the less it was most welcome. Unkind fortune brought it about that neither could yours be conveyed to me in good time, nor mine carried through to you. For yours, written on 25 August, were delivered to me only on 18 March. The man who brought them said he had been held up on the road by foul weather. I, however — long before I had received yours — at the very feast of the Lord's Nativity [Christmas], having got a certain opportunity and a hope that Master Ségur would reach you over there, had given to his servant, a countryman of mine, my letters to you together with those lucubrations of mine; and if these had had a fortunate passage, your wish would have been satisfied in good time, at least in part. My [parcel] had got as far as Frankfurt from here. And lo, something happened whereby Master Ségur was forced to change his plan of going into Denmark, and my letters along with my lucubrations were sent back to me from there — all of which you will understand from this letter of mine, now enclosed, which I had written at that time. You see, my Tycho, how our minds have conspired into one — if only fortune had been willing to favour our wishes; and so now, at once, that she might trick us no longer, I resolved to meet her and to send a messenger of my own to you. I am sending you, then, a good and faithful young man, who, hired by me for wages, served for a time in my house in the tailor's craft, in which he is skilled. While I was deliberating about despatching a courier to you, and was asking whether there were any men of Rostock here — and, finding none, while the time was slipping away — this young man offered me his service, [saying] he would go to you, if only I would give him sufficient travelling-money. I accepted the terms, and embraced his willingness most gladly. I have given him travelling-money to reach you over there; you in turn will see to it that he returns to me, and will recompense his labour. He brings you, first, the printing privilege, such as you were asking for, obtained not without difficulty, in that privileges of this kind are not usually granted to anyone unless a manuscript copy has first been deposited in the Chancery. For some men have obtained privileges of this kind for certain books which were not even begun and never appeared. Yet I prevailed by my efforts that such a privilege be given you in general terms, such as no one else has. Enjoy it for the good of the Republic of Letters. I paid the fee for it into the Chancery, 12½ florins, as you will learn from the assessor's slip attached to the privilege. He brings you likewise my copy of Trapezuntius. For Pirckmann at Cologne, when he had with great eagerness obtained the book from me — a hope being held out that he would publish it in his own type — was frightened off by the many diagrams, because they would require great expense, changed his mind, and at last sent the book back to me; and it has, in truth, always been very dear to me. And assuredly I would from my heart have wished to make it common to all students, nor did I doubt it would be most welcome to all the schools; indeed, with this man as their guide [interpreter], students would have been led on to the reading of the works of Ptolemy, and would have been engaged in that rather than in Sacrobosco's meagre little book. And if at least the first two or three books were published, they would suffice for the schools. The first book, you know, was once published and illustrated with scholia by Reinhold; it could be inserted into Trapezuntius's commentaries. But you will decide about the edition as shall seem best to you. Meanwhile use and enjoy my book, and, if it please you, have it copied. But if it does not please you, send it back by the same man. I am also sending you a little book on the Significations of Directions. It is said to be Carion's — though I had supposed that you had copied it out at Regensburg. Werner's little book on the motion of the eighth sphere is no longer to be found for sale. It was attached to certain other of Werner's small works, which now appear nowhere. In these days I received a letter from Frankfurt from those French noblemen, together with the Diary published by your Elias at your workshop. This was most welcome to me, not only because it had been given to me by them, but because I caught sight of your name appended to it; and at the sight of it the thought at once struck my mind that this was your own genuine offspring; and reading it through in order, I seemed to hear you yourself speaking. In which — if I am not mistaken — I say it was prudently done that you did not declare your name, but reserve it for your greater works. Although, to confess frankly, that Diary is no common one, nor can it in any way be compared with those common Diaries which those diary-mongers are wont to publish year by year. For it has an accurate description of the recent comet, of which I knew plainly nothing; and it has the motions of the ☉ Sun and ☾ Moon established from your observation. But the common run of men knows not how to tell gold from brass. And so I judge that those men act prudently who, in those lighter matters [left] to the judgement of the crowd, either do not declare their own name or leave that honour to their pupils.

I was greatly delighted with the map of your island. I have left one copy of it in the Chancery, to be shown to the Emperor. I have studied the picture of your armillary and sextant; how I wish I might see the machine itself, and were I a little younger, I would hasten to you.

For sharing everything with me so generously, I render you great thanks. I am sending you my lucubrations on the New Star and on the two comets. What is attached to them you will see in the work itself. If you shall judge it worth the trouble that they be published in one volume with your own [writings] which you have, I leave it all to your judgement. But if you shall think they cannot conveniently be published, or that mine are foreign to yours, send mine back to me again. For lately a certain great and learned man wrote to me that he was willing to bear the expenses for the edition. But I would rather that mine came out together with yours, which, I hope, will not prove discordant. Of other writers on the comets just past, besides those whom I have appended to my Dialexis, that is, to my small works, I have none. I suppose you have seen Maestlin's writing on the comet of the year [15]77. Greet B. V. and your lord brother in my words most dutifully, and also his colleague, whom he had here with him. At Prague, 19 May by the new, or 9 [May] by the old Calendar, in the year 1586.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), Prague physician, astronomer, and mathematician; personal physician at the imperial court (Maximilian II, then Rudolf II) and Tycho's most important Bohemian correspondent and conduit to the Imperial Chancery. His 1574 Dialexis on the 1572 nova is the work referred to below.
  • D. SEGURIUS — Master Ségur: Jacques de Ségur-Pardaillan, diplomat of Henri de Navarre (the future Henri IV), engaged in a Protestant-alliance legation through northern Europe in 1585–86. Hagecius had entrusted an earlier packet to his servant in the hope it would reach Tycho via Ségur's planned journey "into Denmark," which was then abandoned.
  • Privilegium impressorium — a printing privilege (imperial copyright), here granted in genere ("in general terms," covering Tycho's works as a class rather than a single named book), which Hagecius stresses is exceptional; the Chancery fee was 12½ florins (Fl.). Taxator = the fee-assessor; schedula = the assessment slip.
  • TRAPEZUNTIJ … exemplum — George of Trebizond (Georgius Trapezuntius, 1395–1486) and his Latin commentary/translation of Ptolemy's Almagest; Hagecius is lending Tycho his own copy. PIRCKMANNUS Coloniae = a printer/publisher Pirckmann at Cologne who had considered printing it but balked at the cost of the many diagrams (diagrammata). Interpres is rendered "guide/interpreter" — the printed Trapezuntius would introduce students to Ptolemy.
  • SACROBUSTI libello ieiuno — the "meagre little book" of Sacrobosco (Johannes de Sacrobosco, De sphaera), the standard elementary university text Hagecius hopes a printed Ptolemy would displace.
  • REINHOLDO … editus — Erasmus Reinhold (1511–1553), Wittenberg astronomer, who had edited and annotated the first book [of the Almagest]; Hagecius proposes it could be combined with Trapezuntius's commentaries.
  • libellum de Directionum Significationibus … CARIONIS — a little book on the significations of [astrological] directions (directio = a predictive astrological technique, glossary), attributed to Johann Carion (1499–1537), court astrologer at Brandenburg; Hagecius notes Tycho may instead have transcribed it himself at Regensburg (Ratisbona).
  • WERNERI de motu Octavae Sphaerae — Johann Werner of Nuremberg (1468–1522), De motu octavae sphaerae (1522), the tract on the motion of the eighth (fixed-star) sphere — i.e. on precession (Copernicus had criticised it); Hagecius reports it can no longer be bought.
  • Diario … a tuo ELIA in tua Officina edito — the Diary (almanac/ephemeris-calendar) issued at Tycho's own Uraniborg workshop (Officina) under the name of Elias Olsen Morsing (Elias), Tycho's assistant; published anonymously of Tycho. The "recent comet" with an accurate description and the ☉/☾ positions "established from your observation" is the comet of 1585, treated in Morsing's Diarium astrologicum et meteorologicum (Uraniborg, 1586).
  • Nobiles Galli — "those French noblemen," the same French (Navarrese) party connected to the Ségur legation, from whom Hagecius received both the letter and the Diary.
  • Symbols. ☉ = Sun, ☾ = Moon (glyphs kept in the Source block, named in the translation by this chapter's convention).
  • Designatio tuae insulae — the map/plan of your island (Hven), one copy of which Hagecius left in the Chancery to be shown to the Emperor (Rudolf II). Armillae et Sextantis tui pictura — the engraved picture of your armillary and sextant (glossary: armillae = armillary, sextans = sextant); machina = the instrument itself, rendered "machine."
  • Meas lucubrationes de Nova Stella et Cometis duobus — Hagecius's own studies on the New Star (the 1572 nova; his Dialexis, named below) and on two comets, which he offers for joint publication with Tycho's works.
  • MAeSTLINI de Cometa anni 77 — Michael Maestlin's tract on the comet of 1577 (Observatio et demonstratio cometae aetherei, 1578).
  • B. V. et Dom. fratrem tuum — the greeting to "B. V." (initials unexpanded in the text — a person Tycho and Hagecius both know; left as given, not guessed) and to "your lord brother and his colleague whom he had here with him." Crux flagged: the referent of B. V. is not determinable from the letter; the brother is unnamed. Rendered literally, not resolved.
  • Calendar / dateline. "Pragae 19. Maij iuxta novum, aut 9 iuxta vetus Calendarium, Anno 1586" = "At Prague, 19 May by the new, or 9 [May] by the old Calendar, 1586." Hagecius, in Catholic Imperial Prague, gives both styles explicitly: 19 May New Style = 9 May Old Style (the 10-day Gregorian gap, this chapter's convention). The identifier and the edition's catalogue date use the O.S. 9 May; the header leads with the as-written date and records both. No silent conversion is made — both are present in the source.
  • In-letter date data. The two transit dates inside the letter ("Tuae enim 25. Augusti scriptae mihi demum 18. Martij redditae sunt" — written 25 Aug., delivered 18 Mar.) are reproduced as written; no style is assigned to them in the source and none is imposed here.
  • MS witness. Recorded as given in the edition: A = Vienna, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66.
