The original

CLARISSIMO VARIOQUE DOCTRINARUM GENERE ERUDITISSIMO EXCELLENTISSIMOQUE VIRO D. THADDÆO HAGGECIO AB HAYCK, PHILOSOPHIAe ET MEDICINAe DOCTORI EXIMIO, AMICO SUO IN PRIMIS COLENDO.
ZU PRAG IM LANDT ZU BEHMEN AN HERN DOCTOR THADDÆUM HAGGECIUM.

SCRIPSI tibi non ita dudum, mi amantissime et doctissime D. D. THADDÆE, atque ad ultimas a te acceptas respondi, solicitudinemque omnem de prioribus tuis, quas suspicabaris non redditas mihi, uti spero, ademi. Nunc et alteras addo, ne semper a te praeveniar aut scriptionem tamdiu, ut antea, procrastinem. Licet enim non magnopere occurrat, quod nunc scribam, tamen idipsum potius indicabo, quam quod nihil mittam, praesertim cum instantibus Francofurtianis iam Nundinis occasionem has ad te mittendi prae foribus esse videam; ut si non recta, saltem inde per ambages meae ad te perferantur. Quas antea circa Novembris initia ad te paulo luculentius exaravi, iamdudum redditas confido. Nam bina exemplaria transscribi curavi seorsimque per diversos internuntios mitti, ne ambae interciderint. Quid te illic voluerim, iam procul dubio cognovisti. Nec dubito, quin in Privilegio Caesareano ampliore procurando, tuam mihi praestiturus sis fidelem operam; quod si impetraveris, fac ut mature habeam; primus enim Tomus Operis nostri, cui illud praefigendum destinavi, iam indies ad Colophonem deducitur. Nam eius tertia et ultima pars, quae aliorum placita, inter quae etiam tua, expendit, iam sub praelo est. Misi etiam tum temporis Apologeticum quoddam Scriptum, quo praestanti cuidam in Scotia Mathematico, Aristotelea tutanti et nostra convellere aggresso, occurri. Hoc si perlegisti, velut te fecisse non dubito, tuum mihi de eo iudicium communica, et si quid desiderari aut minus absolute tractatum censueris, ingenue admone. Fieri enim potest, ut Apologiam hanc secundo Tomo, qui de eodem Cometa, circa quem potissimum movetur controversia, agit, subiungam, quo locupletior reddatur. Si quae igitur inter revidendum atque recognoscendum castigatione aut suppletitione indigere perspexero, eis providebo. Literas meas, quas tibi inde a prima familiaritate Ratisponae contracta inscripsi, si quas forte reservasti, rogavi, ut singularum exemplum transscriptum mihi mitteres, quod et nunc peto, praesertim quantum ad eas attinet, quae aliquid ad Astronomicam vel Cometometricam materiam faciunt. Cur id cupiam, tunc indicavi. Et ut scias, rem ita se habere, mitto aliquam partem libri I Epistolarum Astronomicarum, quae hactenus typis absoluta est; in qua saltem eae, quae ab Illustriss. Principe WILHELMO, Hassiae Landgravio, et eius Celsit. Mathematico, unâ cum mea ad singulas responsione, ultro citroque hactenus missae sunt, continentur. Addam et illas, quas me redeuntibus ab hisce Nundinis mercatoribus (Cassellas enim pertranseunt) recepturum existimo, unâ cum mea responsione, sicque primam Tomi illius Epistolarum partem claudam. In secunda et tertia (nam et hunc trifariam subdividere lubet) aliorum de re Astronomica mecum per literas conferentium Apographa unâ cum mea rescriptione exhibebo. Illic tua quoque, quotquot in meis Archivis reperio, addam. Atque id erat caussae (ut denuo inculcem) cur mearum ad te, quas in promptu omnes non habeo, copiam desiderârim. Habeo alias non paucas Eruditorum de hac materia collationes, quas Astronomiae cupidis lectu non ingratas fore confido. Continent enim multa peculiaria et non ubique obvia ad artis penitiorem illustrationem plurimum conducentia atque rem insuper Cometicam affatim et enucleate excutientia. Licebitque primum Tomum ad tria vel quatuor Alphabeta, prout libuerit, hac ipsa Typorum forma deducere ita, ut iustae reddatur quantitatis, et Neotericorum coeli Phaenomenωn libros singulos magnitudine adaequet. Reliquas literas, quae hoc comprehendi commode non poterint, in alium sequentem digeram, sicque ordine et successive Eruditorum mecum collocutiones Astronomicas certis voluminibus, etiam alia interim agens, complectar, ne prorsus intercidant, cum multa cognitu haud indigna contineant. Has igitur primitias boni consule, tuamque de ijs sententiam mihi perscribe. Invenies vel in his plurima disquisitionem accuratam merentia; nam praeterquam quod de observationibus atque instrumentis aliter quam antea factitatum instituendis quaedam agant, de coeli essentia, stellarum refractionibus, hypothesibus coelestium motuum, tum etiam Cometis atque plerisque eiuscemodi varie inter nos disputatur. Quae omnia tuo solerti iudicio discernenda relinquo. Rogoque peramanter, velis cum Nobilissimo et amplissimo viro D. IACOBO CURTIO de his omnibus vel saltem eorum praecipuis per otij commoditatem conferre mecumque vestram eruditam censuram communicare. Nam et ROTHMANNUS, cum quo de plerisque non ingrata mihi est controversia, ad arbitros provocat, quod et ego non detrecto. Inspicite, legite, dijudicate. Nolim tamen, ut pluribus hos quaterniones communices, antequam totus liber consummatus prodierit. Primus quaternio deest, in quo titulus atque praefatiuncula quaedam extabunt, qui in totius absolutionem, uti fieri solet, differtur. Quamprimum Tomus anterior Ascititiorum coeli Phaenomenon, qui nunc sub praelo est, extremam manum calcographicam senserit, te eius reddam participem. Ne vero te, quem talibus affici scio, nimis diu sine meis detinerem, volui has qualescunque Epistolarum pagellas, etsi non ad destinatam maturitatem perductas, tecum impertiri, ut haberes quo aliquantulum, donec caetera absolvantur, animum per otium, nostra etiam qualiacunque legendo, pasceres. Tuum itaque erit omnia in meliorem partem interpretari et errata typographica, si quae, uti non dubito (necdum enim mihi ipsi ista relegere vacabat) commissa sint, propria industria, qua res ipsas satis intelligis, emendare.

Quin et aliud quiddam nunc occurrit, quod a te hisce expetam. Incidi proximis diebus, dum tuas literas evolvo, in chartam, qua observationes nonnullas a te coelitus habitas mihi denotasti, inter quas ab initio Martis stella mense Septembri et sequent. anni 1578 (licet in tua scheda per incuriam aliquam annus 1579 ponebatur), quando acronychius erat, in distantia a 1a ♈ aliquoties denotata. Quoniam vero ob raras serenitates eius observationem meis organis tunc instituere, uti cupiebam, non licuit, hancque acronychiam eius dispositionem cum pluribus alijs postmodum aliquot sequentibus annis diligenter desuper acquisitis conferre aveam, ipsius motus expendendi restituendique gratia, rogo amanter, ut si quas plures habeas in ♂ tunc praesertim temporis factas animadversiones, quoad distantiam tum ab ea ipsa, tum alijs etiam stellis, et quoad altitudinem meridianam, si eam nactus es, id quicquid erit, in meum usum describi curare velis; et praeterea quotcunque alias observationes Astronomicas in promptu habes. Licet enim sciam, eas ob Radij atque aliorum instrumentorum, quibus usus es, minus exactam perfectionem non satis praecise se habere, tamen qualescunque tandem fuerint, eas cognoscere non inutile duxerim. Nam et nonnunquam has limitare atque, sicubi opus fuerit, ex circumstantijs ipsis corrigere non difficile erit.

Qui de Nova Stella atque Cometis scripserunt, modo plures ad manus habueris, quam me obtinuisse putas, et eorum copiam facias velim. Si mittenda Rostochium ad D. D. HENRICUM BRUCÆUM, aut Lubecam Mercatori meo IACOBO BUCK, ut is nobili viro HENRICO RANZOVIO, Regis nostri in Holsatia Vicario, amico meo singulari, illa transmittat, procurâris, utique ea cito et certo accepturus sum.

Vide, quot nunc scripserim, licet ab initio vix, quae exararem, me habiturum putârim. Talis est inter nos animorum confluentia, ut unum pariat alterum. Da operam, ut brevi luculenter respondeas. Ego posthac in rescribendo maturior ero. Vale et magnificum Dominum CURTIUM meis verbis officiose saluta, a quo etiam responsionem, modo per publica otium illi supererit, indies expecto.

Iterum quam felicissime atque diutissime vale.
Uraniburgi die 23. Februarij 1590.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.
POST SCRIPTA

CUM eo ipso die, quo has literas meo Amanuensi ad mundum describendas tradidissem, circa vesperas Veneris a Sole digressioni maximae observandae intenderem, ecce Cometes quidam ad eandem coeli occiduam plagam ex improviso apparuit, ideoque hasce literas interea mecum reservandas duxi, donec omnes istius ascititij sideris apparentias coelitus per totam eius durationem accurate deductas scrutatus essem, ut mea etiam inventa, ex quo sciam te talibus plurimum affici, unâ transmitterem. Addidi itaque brevem eiusdem Cometae descriptionem, qualem illustrissimo quoque Principi WILHELMO, Hassiae Landgravio, nuper hac de re scribens communicavi. Plenius de eodem tertio nostro Tomo agam. Illic enim hunc prioribus adiungere omnesque eius apparentias, quodque omnino Parallaxi destituebatur, e multiplicibus et certis observationibus Geometrice demonstrare constitui; interim has pauculas de eodem crinito sidere conceptiones, e ratis tamen animadversionibus derivatas, boni consule. Quod autem tamdiu a prima scriptione negotium hucusque distulerim, praeter opinionem meam factum est. Licet enim expectandum erat, donec Cometes ille prorsus evanesceret, quod post sextum diem Martij accidit, et aliquantulum temporis ad eius habitudines ex observationibus rimandas requireretur, accidit insuper, ut statim post Martij medium Sereniss. Scotorum Rex cum splendido suo comitatu me hic inviseret, atque ex eo tempore per aliquot continuos dies pluribus peregrinis hospitibus excipiendis occuparer, quo minus inceptam telam pertexere licuerit. Hincque factum est, quod literas hasce usque in sextam septimanam mecum retinuerim, quod indicandum duxi, ne cur tam diverso tempore haec scribantur, mirareris, sed moram hanc eo citius excusatam haberes. Iterum vale. Haec scribebantur quarto Aprilis.

To the most renowned and, in every varied kind of learning, most erudite and most excellent man, Master Thaddaeus Hagecius of Hájek, distinguished Doctor of Philosophy and of Medicine, his friend to be honoured above all.
At Prague, in the land of Bohemia, to Master Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius.

I wrote to you not so long ago, my most loving and most learned Master Doctor Thaddaeus, and answered the last [letter] received from you, and — as I hope — I removed all your anxiety about your earlier ones, which you suspected had not been delivered to me. Now I add yet another, lest I be always forestalled by you, or, as before, put off writing for so long. For although nothing much comes to mind that I might now write, I shall sooner report this very fact than send nothing — especially since, with the Frankfurt fair now at hand, I see the chance of sending this to you to be right at the door; so that, if not directly, then at least from there, by roundabout ways, my [letters] may be carried to you. Those which earlier, around the beginning of November, I penned to you somewhat more fully, I trust have long since been delivered. For I had two copies transcribed and sent separately by different messengers, lest both should be lost. What I wanted of you over there you have now doubtless learned. Nor do I doubt that, in procuring a more ample Imperial privilege [for me], you will render me your faithful service; and if you obtain it, see that I have it in good time, for the first Volume of our Work, before which I have determined that it be set, is now day by day being brought to its colophon. For its third and last part, which weighs the opinions of others — among which yours too — is now under the press. I also sent at that time a certain apologetic writing, by which I countered a certain distinguished Mathematician in Scotland who, defending Aristotelian doctrines, had set out to demolish ours. If you have read this through — as I do not doubt you have done — share with me your judgement of it, and if you think anything is wanting or treated less than fully, frankly advise me. For it may be that I shall append this Apology to the second Volume, which treats of the same Comet about which the controversy chiefly turns, so that it may be rendered the fuller. If, then, in revising and re-examining, I perceive that any [parts] need correction or supplementation, I shall provide for them. My own letters, which I wrote to you ever since our first acquaintance was formed at Regensburg — if perhaps you have kept any — I asked that you send me a transcribed copy of each, which I ask again now, especially as regards those which bear in any way on Astronomical or Cometometric matter. Why I desire it, I indicated then. And that you may know the matter to be so, I send some part of Book I of the Astronomical Letters, which has so far been completed in print; in which there are contained at least those which, [exchanged] between the Most Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, and His Highness's Mathematician, together with my reply to each, have so far been sent back and forth. I shall add also those which I expect to receive from the merchants returning from this fair (for they pass through Kassel), together with my reply, and so I shall close the first part of that Volume of Letters. In the second and third (for I am pleased to subdivide this too into three) I shall set forth the transcripts of others who confer with me by letter on Astronomical matters, together with my reply. There I shall add yours too, as many as I find in my archives. And this was the reason (to drive it home again) why I desired a supply of my [letters] to you, all of which I do not have ready at hand. I have not a few other collations of the Learned on this subject, which I trust will be not unwelcome reading to those eager for Astronomy. For they contain many things peculiar and not everywhere to be met with, conducing greatly to a deeper illustration of the art, and besides sifting the matter of Comets fully and clearly. And it will be possible to bring out the first Volume, as I please, to three or four Alphabets [of signatures], in this very form of type, so that it be rendered of a just size and match in bulk the individual books of the Moderns on the phenomena of the heaven. The remaining letters, which cannot conveniently be comprised in this [Volume], I shall arrange into another following one; and so, in order and successively, I shall embrace in fixed volumes the Astronomical conversations of the Learned with me — doing other things meanwhile — lest they perish utterly, since they contain much not unworthy to be known. Take these first-fruits, then, in good part, and write me your opinion of them. You will find even in these very many things deserving careful inquiry; for, besides that they treat of certain matters concerning observations and instruments to be established otherwise than was done before, there is varied disputation among us concerning the essence of the heaven, the refractions of the stars, the hypotheses of the celestial motions, and then also Comets and very many things of that kind. All of which I leave to be discerned by your skilful judgement. And I ask you most lovingly: be willing, at your leisure's convenience, to confer with the most noble and most ample man Master Jacob Curtius about all these, or at least the chief of them, and to share with me your learned assessment. For Rothmann too, with whom I have a controversy on very many points that is not unwelcome to me, appeals to arbiters — which I too do not decline. Inspect, read, judge between [us]. I would not, however, have you communicate these quires to more [people] before the whole book has come out complete. The first quire is lacking, in which the title and a certain little preface will stand, which is deferred until the completion of the whole, as is wont to be done. As soon as the earlier Volume on the adventitious phenomena of the heaven, which is now under the press, shall have felt the final engraver's hand, I shall make you a partaker of it. But, lest I detain you — whom I know to be affected by such things — too long without anything of mine, I wished to share with you these few pages of Letters, of whatever sort, though not brought to their intended maturity, so that you might have something on which, for a little while, until the rest are finished, you might feed your mind at leisure, by reading even these such-as-they-are [works] of mine. It will therefore be for you to interpret everything in the better part, and the typographical errors — if any have been committed, as I do not doubt (for I had not yet had leisure to read these over myself) — to amend by your own industry, with which you understand the matters themselves well enough.

But yet another thing now comes to mind, which I would ask of you by this [letter]. In recent days, while unrolling your letters, I came upon a paper on which you noted down for me some observations taken by you from the heaven, among them — from the beginning — the star of Mars, in the month of September and following of the year 1578 (although in your sheet, by some carelessness, the year 1579 was put), when it was acronychal, [its distance] noted several times from the 1st [degree] of ♈ Aries. But since, owing to the rare clear spells, I was not then able, as I wished, to set up an observation of it with my instruments, and I long to compare this acronychal disposition of it with several others diligently acquired thereafter in some following years, for the sake of weighing and restoring its motion, I ask lovingly that, if you have several more observations of ♂ Mars made especially at that time — as to distance both from that same [star] and from other stars too, and as to meridian altitude, if you have obtained it — whatever it may be, you would be willing to have it written out for my use; and besides, as many other Astronomical observations as you have ready at hand. For although I know that, on account of the less than exact perfection of the cross-staff and the other instruments you used, they are not held with sufficient precision, yet whatever sort they may finally have been, I should reckon it not useless to know them. For it will sometimes be no hard matter to bound these and, wherever there be need, to correct them from the circumstances themselves.

Those who have written on the New Star and on Comets — if only you have more at hand than you think I have obtained — I would wish that you make a supply of them too. If you arrange for them to be sent to Rostock to Master Doctor Heinrich Brucaeus, or to Lübeck to my merchant Jacob Buck, that he may transmit them to the noble man Heinrich Rantzau, my King's Vicar in Holstein, my especial friend, I shall in any case receive them quickly and surely.

See how much I have now written, though at the outset I scarcely thought I should have anything to set down. Such is the confluence of minds between us, that one [thing] begets another. Take pains to answer clearly soon. I, hereafter, shall be more prompt in writing back. Farewell, and greet the magnificent Master Curtius dutifully in my words, from whom too I daily await a reply, if only leisure from public affairs be left to him.

Again, fare you well most happily and most long.
At Uraniborg, the 23rd day of February 1590.
Yours, Tycho Brahe.
POSTSCRIPT

On the very day on which I had handed these letters to my amanuensis to be copied out fair, towards evening I was intent on observing the maximum elongation of Venus from the Sun, when behold! a certain Comet appeared unexpectedly in that same western quarter of the heaven; and so I judged that this letter should meanwhile be kept back with me, until I had scrutinized all the appearances of that adventitious star, accurately derived from the heaven throughout its whole duration, so that I might transmit my own findings too along with it — since I know you to be much affected by such things. I have therefore added a brief description of the same Comet, such as I lately communicated to the most illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, when writing to him on this matter. I shall treat of it more fully in our third Volume. For there I have determined to join this [comet] to the earlier ones, and to demonstrate geometrically, from manifold and sure observations, all its appearances, and that it was altogether without parallax; meanwhile take in good part these few conceptions about the same hairy star, derived nevertheless from sound observations. But that I have put off the business so long, until now, from the first writing, has happened beyond my intention. For although it had to be waited until that Comet vanished entirely — which happened after the sixth day of March — and some little time was required for tracing out its conditions from the observations, it happened besides that, straight after the middle of March, the Most Serene King of the Scots, with his splendid retinue, visited me here, and from that time, for several continuous days, I was occupied in receiving many foreign guests, so that I was the less able to finish weaving the web I had begun. And hence it came about that I have kept this letter with me right up to the sixth week — which I judged should be indicated, lest you wonder why these things are written at such different dates, but might rather hold this delay the more readily excused. Again, farewell. These [last lines] were written on the fourth of April.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, at Uraniborg on Hven, to Thaddaeus Hagecius (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku, Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), the Bohemian court physician, astronomer and humanist at Prague — a long-standing correspondent of Tycho's since the De nova stella exchanges of the 1570s. The acquaintance "first formed at Regensburg" (Ratisponae) refers to their meeting at the 1575 Imperial Diet.
  • Address line (German). ZU PRAG IM LANDT ZU BEHMEN AN HERN DOCTOR THADDAeUM HAGGECIUM = "At Prague, in the land of Bohemia, to Master Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius" — the cover/direction in chancery German over the Latin salutation and body; the code-switch is preserved by the edition's convention Behmen = Böhmen (Bohemia).
  • Other persons. D. Iacobus Curtius / Dominus Curtius = Jacob Curtius (Jakob Kurtz von Senftenau), Imperial Vice-Chancellor at the court of Rudolf II, an astronomically literate patron whom Tycho cultivated (and through whom the imperial privilege was sought). Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, the Hesse-Kassel mathematician, with whom Tycho carried on his great cosmological controversy; Illustriss. Princeps Wilhelmus, Hassiae Landgravius = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel; D. D. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus of Rostock; Iacobus Buck = Jacob Buck, Tycho's merchant-agent at Lübeck; Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), royal Statthalter (Vicar) of Holstein, the great Schleswig-Holstein humanist and Tycho's friend and conduit. Sereniss. Scotorum Rex = James VI of Scotland (later James I of England), who visited Hven in March 1590 during his stay in Denmark for his marriage to Anne of Denmark.
  • The "Scottish Mathematician." praestanti cuidam in Scotia Mathematico, Aristotelea tutanti et nostra convellere aggresso — the unnamed Scot defending Aristotle is John Craig (Craig of Edinburgh), with whom Tycho conducted the comet-parallax dispute; the Apologeticum Scriptum is Tycho's reply to him.
  • The Work in press. primus Tomus Operis nostri … Tomus anterior Ascititiorum coeli Phaenomenon refers to Tycho's Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata / De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis programme: the volume "on the adventitious (ascititia) phenomena of the heaven" = the comet volume (De mundi … recentioribus phaenomenis, 1588, of which the third part on others' opinions was still under the press); liber I Epistolarum Astronomicarum = the Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (Uraniborg, 1596), here being printed in instalments, opening with the Wilhelm/Rothmann correspondence.
  • Technical / printing terms. Privilegium Caesareanum = an Imperial privilege (copyright/printing protection). Colophon kept as printed. Alphabeta (ad tria vel quatuor Alphabeta … deducere) = "Alphabets" of printer's signatures (gathering-sequences A–Z); rendered "[of signatures]". quaterniones / quaternio = quires (gatherings of four sheets); primus quaternio deest = "the first quire is lacking" (title-leaf + preface deferred to last, normal practice). sub praelo = "under the press"; extremam manum calcographicam = "the final engraver's/printer's hand." Apographa = transcript-copies. Nundinae Francofurtianae = the Frankfurt [book] fair.
  • Astronomy. acronychius / acronychia dispositio = acronychal (rising at sunset / in opposition to the Sun) — kept as the technical term. digressio maxima (a Sole) = maximum elongation (here of Venus from the Sun); by the audit's rule the interpretive sense is the glossary's elongatio → elongation, and "maximum" renders maxima without bracketing since it stands in the source. altitudo meridiana = meridian altitude. refractiones stellarum = refractions of the stars. hypotheses coelestium motuum = hypotheses of the celestial motions. Parallaxis = parallax — Tycho's point that the comet "was altogether without parallax," i.e. supralunary. organa = instruments; Radius = the cross-staff (radius astronomicus). Cometometrica materia = "Cometometric matter" (cometary measurement) — a coinage kept and glossed. Nova Stella = the New Star (the 1572 nova). crinitum sidus / Cometes = "hairy star / Comet"; the 1590 comet was observed at Uraniborg from late February to 6 March.
  • Symbols. ♈ = the zodiac sign Aries (a 1a ♈ = "from the 1st [degree] of Aries"); ♂ = the planet Mars. Per the edition's convention the glyphs are kept in the Source block and named in English in the translation.
  • In-letter date discrepancy (flagged, not reconciled). Within the Mars-observation request, Tycho records that Hagecius's own sheet was dated anni 1579 but that the observations in fact belong to September 1578 — "licet in tua scheda per incuriam aliquam annus 1579 ponebatur." This is Tycho's own note of an error in his correspondent's document; reproduced as written, not resolved.
  • Calendar / dating. Datelined Uraniburgi die 23. Februarij 1590 (Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700), with the postscript expressly dated quarto Aprilis ("the fourth of April") — Tycho explaining that he held the letter back roughly six weeks to append his observations of the comet (vanished "after the sixth day of March") and because of James VI's mid-March visit. The catalogue label dates the letter "1590. 23. februar." (the body date); the header leads with that as-written dateline and notes the postscript date. No conversion is performed; both internal dates (23 February; 4 April) are O.S. as the body gives them.
  • Greek lettering. Phaenomenωn — the genitive plural is written with a Greek omega (ω) embedded in the otherwise Latin word (Phaenomen-ων), a learned typographic affectation; the glyph is preserved in the Source block.
  • Formulae. S. in the address = salutem ("greeting"); Vale / Iterum … vale / Iterum vale = "Farewell / Again … fare you well / Again, farewell"; D. D. = Domine Doctor ("Master Doctor"); Tuus = "Yours."
