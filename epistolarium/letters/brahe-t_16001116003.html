The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MARTINUM BRASCHIUM..

LITERIS tuis, eruditissime et amantissime Domine BRASCHI, etsi vix otium suppetat, ut nunc quæ vellem respondeam (sum enim plurimis alijs negotijs distractus) malui tamen potius hæc pauca quam nulla ad te dare, ne amicitiam nostram negligere viderer. De carminibus tam MELISSI quam tuis iam satis inter nos convenit, et par stat sententia.

Quæ Dominus D. DAVID CHYTRÆUS optimæ memoriæ, dum adhuc in vivis erat, de mea e Dania emigratione annuæ suæ historiolæ, me consentiente, inserere voluit, si ea istic interciderunt, Magnificum Saxonici Electoratus hîc Legatum agens, Dominus D. GÖDELLMANNUS, qui vos nunc accedit, in alio exemplo ea monstrare poterit, modo res sic postulet. Sed non multum interest, sive hæc fiant, sive non, quemadmodum hac de re ad Dominum D. LUCAM BACKMEISTERUM et Dominum D. STURTZIUM, historicum vestrum, plenius perscripsi. Carmen illud tuum, cuius antea meministi, iam accepi, quod mihi perplacet. Canisium, quod Hungariæ Camisium aliqui vocabant, siquidem eo detracto pateret hostibus in Stiriam et Austriam facilior aditus, ante paucas hebdomadas in Turcarum potestatem, quod dolendum est, pervenit, quo occupato mox Papam castrum obsederunt, quod ijs vix resistere poterit. Cœtera a Domino D. GÖDELLMANNO cognosces.

Iamque quam optime cum omnibus tuis vale.
Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum 16 Novembris Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Martin Brasch.

To your letter, most learned and most loving Master Brasch — although leisure scarcely suffices for me to answer now as I would wish (for I am distracted by very many other affairs) — I preferred nonetheless to send you these few [words] rather than none, lest I should seem to neglect our friendship. About the poems, both Melissus's and yours, enough is now agreed between us, and [our] judgement stands equal [we are of one mind].

The things which Master Dr. David Chytraeus, of best memory, while he was yet among the living, wished — with my consent — to insert about my emigration from Denmark into his annual little history, if these have been lost there, Master Dr. Gödelmann — the Magnificent [man] acting here as Legate of the Saxon Electorate, who now comes to you — will be able to show in another copy, if the matter so require. But it does not much matter whether this be done or not, as I have written more fully about this matter to Master Dr. Lucas Backmeister and to Master Dr. Sturtz, your historian. That poem of yours, which you mentioned earlier, I have now received, and it pleases me greatly. Kanizsa — which some called the "Camisium of Hungary," seeing that, with it removed, an easier approach to Styria and Austria would lie open to the enemy — a few weeks ago came (which is to be lamented) into the power of the Turks; and this taken, they soon besieged the fortress of Pápa, which will scarcely be able to resist them. The rest you will learn from Master Dr. Gödelmann.

And now fare most well, with all your [people].
Given at Prague of the Bohemians, 16 November, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Tie to brahe-t_16010308001. Tycho's reply to Martin Brasch (see that letter); the source-material and poem business referred to there. Carmina Melissi = poems by Paul Schede Melissus (1539–1602), the leading German neo-Latin poet, evidently also commemorating Tycho; "we are now of one mind" about both Melissus's and Brasch's verses.
  • David Chytraeus. David Chytraeus (1530–1600), the great Rostock theologian and historian, "of best memory" (just dead — June 1600). With Tycho's consent he had meant to insert an account of Tycho's emigration from Denmark into his annual chronicle (historiola — his Chronicon / Saxonia annals). If the passage was lost at Rostock, Gödelmann can supply another copy. Tycho says he has written about it more fully to Lucas Backmeister (Rostock theologian) and to Dr. Sturtz, "your historian" (the Rostock historiographer).
  • The Turkish war. Canisium … Camisium Hungariae = (Nagy)kanizsa, the great Hungarian frontier-fortress, whose fall to the Ottomans (October 1600, "a few weeks ago") opened the road toward Styria and Austria; "this taken, they soon besieged the fortress of Pápa" — the same Hungarian front on which Friedrich Rosenkrantz was serving (cf. brahe-t_16000802001). A precise, datable war-news note.
  • Calendar. Pragae Bohemorum 16 Novembris 1600 = 16 November 1600; Prague (imperial) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
