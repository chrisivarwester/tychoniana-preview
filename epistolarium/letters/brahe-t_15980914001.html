The original

NATALIUM SPLENDORE AeQUE AC PROPRIA VIRTUTE AC ERUDITIONE INPRIMIS CONSFPICIO VIRO DOMINO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGUINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO INTEGERRIMO.

VIX octiduum elapsum est, Nobilissime et Charissime OLIGERE, ex quo per Studiosum quendam NICOLAUM ALBINUM nomine ad te de rerum mearum statu perscripsi, nunc vero, cum nihil addubitem, quin eae literae perlatae sint, tamen cum inopinata se haec occasio offerret per Nobilissimum Virum FRANCISCUM RANZOVIUM, qui iam e Belgio redux rursus iter isthuc parabat, denuo ad te perscribendum ratus sum, quo de curru, quem me accepisse innueras, te denuo admonerem, ut eum actutum huc procurares, quippe qui maxima ex parte iter meum remoraretur. Lubecam enim eum necdum allatum certo mihi constat. Caeteroquin non intermisisset IOACHIMUS BUCK (pro ut semel atque iterum ab ipso expetij) quin te per literas ilico de eo fecisset certiorem. Rogo itaque etiam atque etiam, ne longiores hac in re morae nectantur, sed ut sequenti ad summum hebdomada huc ad me perferatur (Uden saa er, att I vilde holde Jytske Louff, det er at beholde hvad mand loffuer, saa at en anden faar intet deraff. Da maatte man endda i det mindste lade en vide det, saa man skulle ikke vente det thill forgieffues); nam diutius praestolari non possum. Sed quamvis tum iter ingressus fuero, tamen non ambigo, quin is nihilo serius ad me perveniat, pro ut, antequam discessero, disponam.

Plura iam non vacat, cum de caeteris in prioribus satis superque tuis responsum existimem.

Vale mi OLIGERE quam diutissime cum matre tua et coniuge SOPHIA a me meisque diligenter salutatus.
Raptim Hamburgi die 14. Septembris Anno 1598.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man conspicuous especially by splendour of birth, as also by his own virtue and erudition, Master Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, kinsman, relative by marriage, and most upright friend.

Scarcely a week [and a day] has elapsed, most noble and dearest Holger, since I wrote to you — through a certain student named Nicolaus Albinus — about the state of my affairs; but now, although I doubt not at all that those letters have been delivered, yet, since this unexpected occasion offered itself through the most noble man Frans Rantzau — who, now returned from Belgium, was again preparing the journey thither — I have judged that I should write to you again, to remind you once more about the wagon which you had hinted you had received, that you procure it hither at once, since it for the most part delays my journey. For I am certain it has not yet been brought to Lübeck. Otherwise Joachim Buck would not have failed (as I sought of him once and again) to make you certain of it at once by letter. I ask, therefore, again and again, that no longer delays be woven in this matter, but that it be conveyed hither to me by next week at the latest (Unless it be that you wish to keep Jutish Law — that is, to keep what one has promised, so that another gets nothing of it. In that case one might, at the very least, let a man know it, so that he should not wait for it in vain); for I cannot wait any longer. But although I shall by then have set out on my journey, yet I do not doubt that it will reach me none the later, as I shall arrange before I depart.

There is no more leisure now, since I reckon that enough and more than enough has been answered, about the rest, in [reply to] your earlier [letters].

Farewell, my Holger, as long as possible, [you] being diligently greeted by me and mine, together with your mother and your wife Sophia.
Hastily, at Hamburg, the 14th of September, in the year 1598.
Your Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), Tycho's consanguineus, affinis et amicus (kinsman, relative by marriage, and friend); cf. the Cramer/Liddel letters (brahe-t_15980331001, …0524001). His wife Sophia = Sophie Axelsdatter Brahe; his mother is also greeted.
  • Occasion. A homely, impatient note about a wagon (currus) that Rosenkrantz was to procure and forward to Tycho at Hamburg — it is holding up Tycho's onward journey (south, toward the Empire, in his 1597–98 exile). Tycho is sure it has not yet reached Lübeck, since Joachim Buck (his Lübeck contact) would otherwise have written. Couriers: Nicolaus Albinus (a student, the earlier letter) and Frans Rantzau (Franciscus Ranzovius, back from Belgium/the Low Countries and travelling that way again).
  • ⚠ Danish code-switch (kept and translated). Mid-sentence, Tycho drops from Latin into Danish for a proverbial joke: "Uden saa er, att I vilde holde Jytske Louff, det er at beholde hvad mand loffuer, saa at en anden faar intet deraff. Da maatte man endda i det mindste lade en vide det, saa man skulle ikke vente det till forgieves" — "Unless it be that you wish to keep Jutish Law — that is, to keep what one promises, so that another gets nothing of it; [then] one might at least let a man know it, so that he should not wait for it in vain." The phrase holde jysk lov ("keep Jutland law") is a Danish proverbial pun: to keep for oneself what one has promised to another. Preserved verbatim in the Source block and rendered in italics (by this chapter's convention); a deliberate bilingual joke between kinsmen, not smoothed away.
  • ⚠ Dateline. Raptim Hamburgi die 14 — the dateline gives only "the 14th," without month or year. The edition (identifier …0914… / label "14. september") supplies 14 September 1598; the internal "scarcely a week has elapsed since…" and the Hamburg exile context are consistent. Header dates 14 September 1598, the month/year flagged as editorially supplied. Hamburg (Lutheran) = Old Style.
  • Correction. Two restorations against Dreyer (TBOO VIII pp.113-114): removed an unsupported 'tuto ad te' from the opening paragraph (with a matching trim to the translation), and restored the full closing dateline 'die 14. Septembris Anno 1598.', replacing the translation's bracketed guess '[of September]'.
