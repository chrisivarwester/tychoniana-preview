The original

VNSERM MATHEMATICO VND LIEBEN GETREWEN CHRISTOPHORO ROTHMANNO.
WILHELM VON GOTTES GNADEN LANDTGRAUE ZU HESSEN/ GRAUE ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ DIETZ/ ZIEGENHAIN VND NIDDA/ ETC.

LIEBER Getrewer/ Wir mögen dir in gnaden nicht verhalten/ das Zeiger dieses D. Nicodemus Frischlinus vorhabens ist/ eine Reyse in Daniam zuthun. Dieweil er dann sonder zweiffel zu dem TYCHONI de BRAHE kommen wirdet/ Als ist an dich vnser befelich in gnaden/ das du etwa ein sein schreiben an jhnen thuest/ vnd von jhme zuwissen begerest/ was er jtzo gutes vorhabe/ vnd wann er seine vorgehabte Arbeit dermahls eins werde außgehen lassen/ Dann vns verlange sehr darnach solchs zusehen. Das thun wir vns zu dir mit gnaden versehen.

DatumHombergk am 26. Iulij, Anno 89.
WILHELM LANDTGRAFF ZU HESSEN.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To Our Mathematician and trusty well-beloved Christoph Rothmann.
Wilhelm, by the grace of God Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda, etc.

Trusty well-beloved. We would not graciously withhold from you that the bearer of this [letter], Master Nicodemus Frischlin, has it in mind to undertake a journey into Denmark [Daniam]. Since, then, he will without doubt come to Tycho de Brahe [TYCHONI de BRAHE], it is therefore Our gracious charge upon you that you write him some letter of yours to him, and desire to learn from him what good thing he now has in hand, and when he will at length let his earlier-undertaken work go forth; for We greatly long to see such [a thing]. This We graciously look for from you.

Given at Homberg, the 26th of July, in the year [15]89.
Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince of Kassel and Tycho's principal German correspondent. The titulature — Landgraf zu Hessen, Graf zu Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain und Nidda — is the standard Hessian style; preserved in full by the edition's convention
  • Recipient. Christoph Rothmann (c. 1550 – c. 1600), the Landgrave's resident mathematician/astronomer at Kassel from late 1584. Here addressed as the prince's subordinate — Vnserm Mathematico vnd lieben getrewen ("Our Mathematician and trusty well-beloved") — in the princely Wir/du register.
  • The bearer. D. Nicodemus Frischlinus = Nicodemus Frischlin (1547–1590), the Neo-Latin poet, dramatist, and philologist (here poeta laureatus of the Count Palatine). Mosley (Bearing the Heavens) confirms the occasion exactly: Wilhelm, hunting at Homburg in July 1589 and lacking recent news of Tycho's work, learned that Frischlin intended to travel to Denmark and induced him to go via Kassel carrying these instructions for Rothmann. Zeiger dieses ("the bearer of this [letter]") is the chancery formula for the courier carrying the document.
  • Code-switches (German → Latin). The letter switches into Latin for the proper-name material the chancery treats as fixed: in Daniam ("into Denmark," accusative of motion); zu dem TYCHONI de BRAHE (dative Tychoni, the Latin name retained whole inside the German clause); the heading Christophoro Rothmanno and Datum ("Given"). The code-switch is preserved by the edition's convention; Datum rendered "Given."
  • Negations / legal force. Two negation-bearing constructions, both reproduced exactly. (1) Wir mögen dir … nicht verhalten is a courtly litotes — literally "We may not withhold from you," i.e. "We would not keep from you / We are pleased to inform you"; rendered "We would not … withhold," keeping the single negation rather than flattening it to a plain positive. (2) sonder zweiffel = "without doubt," rendered as such. The operative clause ist … vnser befelich … das du … is a gracious command ("it is Our charge upon you that you …"), not a request; the three governed verbs — ein schreiben … thuest (write a letter), zuwissen begerest (seek to learn), and the indirect questions was er … vorhabe / wann er … außgehen lassen [werde] — are kept in their subordinate, reported-question mood.
  • was er jtzo gutes vorhabe / seine vorgehabte Arbeit … außgehen lassen. "What good thing he now has in hand" and "let his earlier-undertaken work go forth [= publish it]": the Landgrave is asking Rothmann to draw from Tycho, through Frischlin, news of Tycho's current projects and especially when he will at last publish the work already undertaken — außgehen lassen in the period sense "issue, bring out (in print)." The phrasing is kept neutral; no specific Tychonic title is named in the source, so none is supplied.
  • Place / dateline crux. The dateline reads DatumHombergk am 26. Iulij, Anno 89 (the 1596 print runs Datum and Hombergk together without a space; transcribed verbatim). The place is Homberg, a Hessian landgravial seat where Wilhelm was hunting — not Kassel, which the edition label field gives. Mosley's "hunting at Homburg in July of that same year" corroborates the dateline against the catalogue label; the header therefore leads with the as-written "Homberg" and appends "(catalogued Kassel)." Homberg (Efze), north of the Schwalm hunting grounds, is the most probable referent for a Hessian Hombergk hunting dateline, though the source does not disambiguate it from other Hessian Hombergs; flagged rather than asserted. Date 26. Iulij Anno 89 = 26 July 1589, Old Style (Hesse keeping the Julian calendar; the edition's convention); not silently converted.
  • Edition of record. The letter is printed in Tycho's own Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I, Uraniborg 1596) — the volume Tycho printed at Hven — whence Dreyer's Opera Omnia VI. No manuscript witness is recorded in the edition.
