The original

MAGNIFICO ET GENEROSO TYCHONI BRAHÆ, DOMINO IN KNUSTORPH ET HUENA, DOMINO ET FAUTORI SUO, OMNI OFFICIJ GENERE COLENDO.

LITERIS tuis, Magnifice et Generose Do[mine] TYCHO, instructus, in cognitionem ex CHYTRÆO et alijs præsentis tui status et conditionis veni, de quo me certiorem factum esse summopere gaudeo. Gratulor etiam tibi de tanto Mœcenate, opere et studio tuo digno, quo non solum tibi prospectum, sed toti posteritati plurimum inserviri intelligo. Chymicus Silesius, quo de antea ad te perscripsi, Sittæ in Silesia degit, vocaturque IACOBUS KINDLER, olim ibidem vel in vicinia scholæ Cantor. Artificium, quod illum celebrem facit, describitur in lib. Vexationum PARACELSI sub titulo Hæc est ars, cursumque sphæræ Saturninæ vocant, quo omnes planetæ terrestres, mediante Saturno et uno minerali adiecto, exiguo negocio in lapides vertuntur, qui deinceps nulla vi ignis aut vulcano quantumvis torvo, postliminium perveniunt, ↄ: in metalla redeunt. Huius revolutionis spectator ipse fui atque dirigere scio, sed finem, quem author ibidem promittit, assequi ignoro. Literas tuas FRANCISCO SCHRODERO Sundensi inscriptas, quotquot ad me pervenerunt, summa fidelitate isthic misi, nec non ad responsionem illum adhortatus sum, ac suam taciturnitatem illi exprobravi, ad quod mihi nuper respondit, tibi quoque suis literis Witebergam delatis responsum esse. Sero forte, quod tuus discessus illinc prævenerit illud, aut quicquid intervenerit.

Nova, quæ hîc habemus, ex adiuncto scripto cognosces. Alias forma Academiæ et Reipub. eadem est, quam reliquisti; discursum Suecicum tibi quoque misissem, sed nulla hîc exemplaria, ut qui Stockholmiæ impressus est, prostant.

Vale, nec non qui tibi curæ sunt, et me inter tuos familiares retineto.
Data Rostochij 19. Decembris anni 1599.
T. Magnif. studiosiss.
Christopho. Henniochus,
Medicus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and generous Tycho Brahe, lord in Knudstrup and Hven, his lord and patron, to be honoured with every kind of office.

Instructed by your letter, magnificent and generous Master Tycho, I came into knowledge of your present status and condition — from Chytraeus and from others — about which I greatly rejoice to have been made more certain. I also congratulate you on so great a Maecenas [patron], worthy of your work and study, by which I understand that not only have you been provided for, but the whole of posterity is greatly served. The Silesian chemist, about whom I wrote to you before, lives at Zittau [Sitta] in Silesia, and is called Jacob Kindler, formerly Cantor of the school there or in the vicinity. The art which makes him famous is described in the Book of Vexations of Paracelsus, under the heading "This is the art," and they call it the course of the Saturnine sphere, by which all the terrestrial planets [the metals], by means of Saturn [lead] and one mineral added, are with little trouble turned into stones — which thereafter, by no force of fire, or by however grim a Vulcan, "come back from exile," that is, return into metals. Of this revolution I was myself a spectator, and I know how to direct it; but the end which the author there promises, I know not how to attain. Your letters inscribed to Franz Schröder of Stralsund, as many as reached me, I sent thither with the utmost fidelity, and also exhorted him to a reply, and reproached him with his silence; to which he lately answered me that an answer had also been [given] to you, by his letters carried to Wittenberg — late, perhaps, because your departure thence forestalled it, or [because] something intervened.

The news which we have here, you will learn from the enclosed writing. Otherwise the form of the Academy and the commonwealth is the same as you left it; I would also have sent you the Swedish discourse, but there are no copies here — being one that was printed at Stockholm, [where such things] are on sale.

Farewell — and likewise those who are a care to you — and keep me among your familiars.
Given at Rostock, 19 December, in the year 1599.
Most zealous of Your Magnificence,
Christoph Henniochus,
Physician.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Christoph Henniochus (Medicus, a physician) — the alchemical correspondent of brahe-t_16000329003 (Tycho's reply, 29 March 1600). This letter (19 Dec 1599) is the one Tycho there acknowledges as "brought by my son at the beginning of this year," and it is here that Henniochus identifies the Silesian alchemist Tycho asked after.
  • The alchemist identified. Chymicus Silesius … Sittae in Silesia degit, vocaturque Iacobus Kindler, olim … scholae Cantor — the man is Jacob Kindler, living at Zittau (Sitta; in Upper Lusatia, near Silesia), a former school Cantor. His "art" is the [pseudo-]Paracelsian Liber Vexationum process called the cursus sphaerae Saturninae ("course of the Saturnine sphere"): the metals (planetae terrestres — the seven "planets" = the seven metals of alchemy) are, "by means of Saturn [= lead] and one added mineral," turned into "stones" and then back into metals (postliminium = "[returning] from exile," a Roman-law term for restored status). Henniochus witnessed the operation and can run it, but cannot reach the finis (the promised goal). The symbol ↄ: = id est ("that is"). Reinforces the Tychonic alchemy thread (cf. brahe-t_15860301001, …16000329003).
  • Maecenas = Tycho's new imperial patron (Rudolf II); Henniochus had learned of Tycho's new footing "from Chytraeus and others" (David Chytraeus of Rostock, then in his last year). Franciscus Schröderus Sundensis = Franz Schröder of Stralsund, the delinquent correspondent of brahe-t_16000329003. discursus Suecicus = a "Swedish discourse" (a political pamphlet on the Swedish succession war) printed at Stockholm.
  • ⚠ Place of writing. The dateline reads Data Rostochij = Rostock; the edition catalogues the letter under Wittenberg. The internal references are mixed — Henniochus forwarded Tycho's letters via Wittenberg and reports that "the form of the Academy … is the same as you left it" (Tycho had wintered at Wittenberg in 1599) — which may be why the catalogue says Wittenberg; but the as-written dateline (Rostock) governs the header, with the discrepancy flagged. Either way the place is Lutheran = Old Style.
  • Calendar. 19. Decembris anni 1599 = 19 December 1599, Old Style. Header agrees with the label's date (the place differs, as noted).
