The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD SIMONEM DE PETKUM]

MEIN freundtlichen grues mit wunschung alles gutts zuuor, erbar vnd furnemer Symon von Petkum, inbesonders gutter freundt. Eur schreiben hab ich, zu Hamburgk den 12 februarij diesen jhars datirt, bekommen, vnd daneben die beide von magistro Dauide Fabricio vnnd herrn Ritherhausen zu Brehmen. Was nun anlanget die brieffe an mir hero zu bestellen, gilt es mir gleich, ob ihr sie gehn Leipzig vnd von dannen auff Dressden an mein wirt, oder auch auff Nurrenbergk vnd von dannen hieher, oder auff Prag ahn herrn doctor Heller, auff die kleine zeitte wonhafftig, dirigirt, so werde ich sie wohl bekommen, so ferne ihr nicht gelegenheit habt, von dannen dieselbige brieffue an mir directe zu schicken.

Der her burgmeister Vincentius Mullerus schreibt, das jhre key. may. ahn die herrn von Hamburgk gethanes schreiben jhnen noch nicht geliebert sey, aber wen das gescheen vnd er daraus vernummen, was jhr may. von meinetwegen allergnedigst begerren thutt, will ehr, so vill ihme ihmmer meuglig, helffen die sache promouiren, worfur ich mich gegen ihme thu bedancken, vnd hab ihme selbst weitern bescheidt dauon zugeschrieben, welchen brieff ihr ihme von meinetwegen könnet vberantwortten. Das dieselbige literae caesareae noch nicht alda den herrn sein vberliebert, kan ich gedencken, das die vhrsache sey, das mein factor zu Lübeck, Jochum Boch, den selbigen brieff bej sich behelt, bis er die jnstrumenten neben dem andern gutt gehn Hamburgek schicke, vnd wan das mitlerweill nicht gescheen ist, so wirt ehr es halt thun, wovon ich ime auch geschrieben hab, welchen brieff ihr alßbalt könnet zu ihm schicken. Vnd so balt die instrumenten vnd das ander gutt, so ich zu Lubch hab, gehn Hamburg ist ankommen, so bitt ich freundtlich ihr wollet bei den herren alda soliciterern, das sie wollen die furordnung thun, das man kuntte schiffe dazu bekommen, das es alles sambt was ihr alda habt, so vhnverkaufft ist, köntte ihr key. may. zum gnedigsten gefalln hirauff in Bohemen gefurt werden, vnd so die herren wollen ein von ihren eygenen diener dabey haben, der auff köntte sehen, das alle ding richtig zuginge, wehre es mir sehr angenem, vnd so sie mülstein oder andern wahren, welche alhie leichter alß da bey euch zu bekommen, wieder hin ab füren zu lassen gesint, auff das die schiff nicht ledich zu hauß kemen, köntte derselbige auch darauf auffsehn haben, vnd ich woltte auch gehrn meuglichen fleiß anwenden, das solchs mit dem ehesten vnd mit vortheill ausgericht werde.

Vnd wie wohl ihr meinet, das nutzlich kuntte sein, das ihr may. noch ein schreibent ließ an den erbarn rath alda der sachen wegen abgehen, so achte ich es vhnvonnötten, dieweill das vorrige schreibent noch verhanden oder villeigt nun schon vberliebert. Ich vorsiehe mich, das die hern von Hamburgk werden sich wohl wissen gehorsamblich nach jhr key. may. ehrsten schreiben wissen zu richten, das es keines mehreren bedarff, vnd das sie keineswegens thun werden wie die Magdaeburger, ahn welchen jhr key. may. zuo mahl von meinetwegen geschrieben, vnd sie gleichwohl subterfugia gefunden vnd die schuere belegerung, so sie vor ein halbhundert jahr gelitten, praesendirt, das sie solchs nicht thun köntten. Welchs jhr may. hart hatt verdrossen, vnd sie den kop darvber schuttelt, vnd zu vhnwillen auffgenohmen. Die rehtte wahren auch gahr spötsch darüber, das sie durfften jhr may. so ein ringes abslagen, vnd hat einer von ihnen, welcher teglich mehr alß andere zu jhr may. zutritt hat, diesse formalia verba darzu gesagt: Wir hetten gemeint, die von Magdeburgk wehren hoffleutte, nuhn vormercken wihr, daß sie bauren sein, es kan wohl die zeit kommen, das sie jhre may. wiederumb bedarffen, alsdan kan mahn wohl ein bessere vnd mehr glaublich enthschuldung vnd praetext finden, alß sie itzunder thun, das sie also repulsam bekommen. Haec ille, audientibus quibusdam allijs, tum quoque meo sollicitatore, dixit, vnd hat alßbalt jhr may. gnedigst befolen, das ein ander brieff wurde geschrieben ad reuerendum capittulum vnd die ehrwerdige dumbherrn zu Magdaeburgk, daß sie solchs jhr may. zum gnedichsten gefallen thun wolten, welchs sie auch sich darzu gantz gehorsamlich erbotten. Vnd ich thu den brieff itzunder zu jhnen verschicken, mallet jam renatus Magdaeburgensis se id officij Caesari non denegasse et nunc libenter, si liceret, obtemperaret, sed sero sapiunt Phryges, Caesar etiam non uult amplius, ut per ipsos id fiat et potius opera reuerendi capituli uti dictum. Wan etzliche brieffue von hern Josepho Scaligero oder sunst auß Niederlant ankommen, oder bescheit von dem ephemeridum compilatore, so last mihr dieselbige mit ehester gelegenheit bekommen, vnd sendet diesen beigelegten brieff ahn magistrum Dauidem Fabricium nebenst dem andern ahn herrn Ritterhusium zu Brehmen; wehn derselbe den ahn Fabricium bekommet, so wirt er in wohl alßbalt gehen Resterhaffuen vorschaffuen. Ich vormercke auß des heern Dauidis Fabrici schreibend das so ehr einige gutte gelegenheit alhie köntte bekomen, das er nicht vhngneigt wehre allhie zu sein, worauff ich im gutten bescheit geschrieben, wolte gehrn, das ehr eigentlich den brieff köntte bekommen, dan so es ihm also gefellig wehr, köntte ehr mit seiner frauwen vnd kinder vnd nöttichstem gerette hir mit schiffen von Hamburgk her auffkommen; vnd was er dazu fur nöttwendig viaticum bedarff, köntt ihr ihm von meinetwegen vorstrecken, vnd so ihr keins von meinem gehlt habt, damit es wider kan erlegt werden, so will ich es euch zu danck lassen wiederumb mit den leutten, so von Hamburgk hjerauff kommen, zustellen, vnd geburlich interesse davon geben, das ihr ohne schaden sollt sein.

Das ihr meiner lieben schwester, fraw Sophia Brahe, habt einen von meinen kutschwogen sambt deme, was dazu gehörig, nach ihren begehr folgen lassen, daran habt ihr recht gethan, vnd ich trage darahn kein missgefallen, es kumbt vmb so ein gering ding nicht zuischen ihr und mihr, ich wolte ihr alß meiner hertzlieben schwester viel ein grösserß zugefallen geschehen lassen.

Was ihr von meinem übrigen gutt könnet alsbalt zu gelte machen, das thutt, aber daß positiff vnd die kleine geschutz von kupfer, eeissen oder waß sönsten metallisch ankombt, das schicket hierauff nebenst dem, so von Lybch geschicket wirt, darvnter auch thunnen, vnd stellt es alles, es sei was es wolle, auff ein klar register, dass ich richtigen bescheid davon wisse. Schreibt mihr auch zu, wie lang mein schwester ist alda zu Wandesbech gewessen, vnd wo sie hingezogen, dan ich kan nicht glauben, das sie noch alda sey, sönsten hette ich ihr wohl geschrieben. Last mihr auch wissen, ob Erich Lange ist zu Hamburgk gewesen, vnd wie es mit seinen sachen stehet, vnd woh er itzunder sein wohnung hat; wan ehr alda in die nahent ist, vnd mihr etwas schreiben will, so könt ihr mihr dasselbe zuschicken.

Was ahnlanget, das die frau stathalterin euch geschrieben hat von den 5000 thallern, wan sie dieselbige solten gewertigk sein, so ist es an dehm, das mein gutter freundt vnd blutsvörwanter ihn Dennemark, Eske Bilde, solte von meinetwegen dasselbige gelt in vorgangen vmbslag erleget haben, vnd meinen brieff wieder gefodert, aber er hat nicht zeittlich genug bescheit davon krigen können, sondern hatt sich erbotten zu nechstkumbstigen vmbslag alles richtig zu machen, vnd ich vormeine, das ehr die 300 thaller zum interesse schon erlegt hat.

Von wegen meiner vhnkostung, so ich ahm gebau vnd eissenwerch zum Wandesbech gethan, vorsihe ich mich, das die frau stathalterinn mihr nicht lenger aufhalten wirt, vnd meinen schaden nicht begehren. Vnd dieweill sie jhren sohn Bredo Wandesbech hatt abgehandelt, kan sie woll es selbst am besten richtig machen; will sie auch nicht die trallien vnd eeissenwerch, vnd waß ich sonst da hab machen lassen, behallten, vnd zahlen das, so zuvohr nicht davon bezalt ist worden, so könt jhr es alles wiederumb wegknehmen, vnd alles das eeisien wegenlassen vnd mit dem andern gutt hierauffschicken. Es sein auch noch etzliche schlosser, welche ich alda hab machen lassen, vnd zwo kachellofen, nebenst anders, so ihr wohl wirt können erfaren. Vorsihe ich mich, das ihr in diesen vnd allen anderen werdet von meinetwegen thun was billig vnd recht sein kan, dasselbige vmb euch vnd den eurigen wiederumb zu vorschulden will ich stetz geflissen sein. Vnd thu vnß hiemit semtlichen in schutz des Allmechtigen befehlen.

Was ihr von meinetwegen zu böttenlohn errleget oder sönsten, das das gutt mit brettern vnd mätten wohl vorwart werde, oder in andern wegen beköstiget, sol euch zu danch wiederumb bezallt werden.

[TYCHO BRAHE TO SIMON VON PETKUM]

My friendly greeting, with wishing of all good, first — honourable and distinguished Simon von Petkum, especially good friend. Your letter, dated at Hamburg the 12th of February this year, I have received, and with it the two from Master David Fabricius and Mr. Ridderhusen at Bremen. Now as concerns the forwarding of letters to me here: it is all one to me whether you direct them via Leipzig and thence to Dresden, to my host; or via Nuremberg and thence hither; or to Prague, to Dr. Heller, dwelling on the Lesser Town [Kleine Seite] — so [that] I shall well get them — unless you have a chance to send the same to me directly from there.

Burgomaster Vincent Möller writes that the Imperial Majesty's letter to the lords of Hamburg has not yet been delivered to them; but when that has happened, and he has learned from it what His Majesty most graciously desires on my behalf, he will help promote the matter as much as ever he can; for which I thank him, and have written him further instruction myself, which letter you can deliver to him on my behalf. That the imperial letter is not yet delivered to the lords there, I think the cause is that my factor at Lübeck, Jochum Boch [Joachim Buch], keeps that letter by him until he sends the instruments, with the other goods, to Hamburg; and if that has not meanwhile happened, he will do it — about which I have also written him, which letter you can send him at once. And as soon as the instruments and the other goods I have at Lübeck have arrived at Hamburg, I ask you kindly to solicit the lords there to arrange that ships could be got for it, so that all of it — together with whatever you have there unsold — could be brought up here to Bohemia, to the Imperial Majesty's most gracious pleasure; and if the lords will have one of their own servants along, who could see that everything goes properly, it would be very agreeable to me; and if they are minded to ship millstones or other wares (easier got here than there with you) back down, so the ships do not come home empty, the same [man] could oversee that too, and I would gladly apply all possible diligence that it be done quickly and with advantage.

And although you think it might be useful for His Majesty to let another letter go to the honourable Council there about the matter, I deem it unnecessary, since the previous letter is still on hand, or perhaps already delivered. I expect the lords of Hamburg will know how to comply obediently with His Majesty's first letter, so that no more is needed — and that they will by no means do as the Magdeburgers: to whom His Majesty wrote twice on my behalf, and they nonetheless found subterfuges and pleaded the harsh siege they suffered a half-hundred years ago, [as excuse] that they could not do such. Which His Majesty took grievously ill, and shook his head over it, and took as displeasure. The councillors too were quite mocking that [the Magdeburgers] dared refuse His Majesty so trifling a thing; and one of them — who has daily more access to His Majesty than others — said these formal words to it: "We had thought those of Magdeburg were courtiers; now we perceive they are peasants. The time may well come when they need His Majesty again; then one can well find a better and more credible excuse and pretext than they do now — so that they get a rebuff." This he said, certain others hearing, and my solicitor too; and His Majesty at once most graciously ordered another letter be written to the reverend Chapter and the venerable canons at Magdeburg, that they would do such to His Majesty's most gracious pleasure — which they also quite obediently offered to do. And I now send the letter to them. The Magdeburger, as it were reborn, would now wish that he had not denied that service to the Emperor, and would now gladly obey if it were permitted — but too late are the Phrygians wise [sero sapiunt Phryges]; the Emperor too no longer wishes it be done through them, but rather that the service of the reverend Chapter be used, as said.

When any letters from Mr. Joseph Scaliger, or otherwise from the Low Countries, arrive, or word from the ephemerides-compiler, let me get the same at the earliest occasion; and send this enclosed letter to Master David Fabricius, along with the other to Mr. Ridderhusen at Bremen; when the latter gets the one to Fabricius, he will soon convey it to Resterhafe [Fabricius's Frisian parish]. I perceive from Mr. David Fabricius's letter that, if he could get some good situation here, he would not be disinclined to be here; whereupon I have written him a good answer, [and] would gladly that he actually got the letter — for if it so pleased him, he could come up here, with his wife and children and most necessary gear, by ship from Hamburg; and what necessary travel-money he needs for it, you could advance him on my behalf; and if you have none of my money to repay it with, I will gladly have it conveyed back to you with the people who come up here from Hamburg, and give due interest on it, so that you shall be without loss.

That you let my dear sister, Lady Sophia Brahe, have one of my coach-wagons, with what belongs to it, at her request — in that you did right, and I bear no displeasure at it: it does not come to such a trifle between her and me; I would let a much greater favour befall her, as my heartily-dear sister.

What of my remaining goods you can quickly turn to money, do that; but the positive [organ], and the small cannon of copper, iron, or whatever else is metallic, ship up here, along with what is sent from Lübeck — barrels among them — and put it all, whatever it be, on a clear register, so that I know proper account of it. Write me also how long my sister was there at Wandsbek, and whither she has moved — for I cannot believe she is still there, else I would have written her. Let me also know whether Erik Lange was at Hamburg, and how his affairs stand, and where he now has his dwelling; if he is near there, and will write me something, you could send it to me.

As concerns the Statthalterin [Governess] writing you about the 5000 thaler — whether she should expect them: the matter is that my good friend and blood-relation in Denmark, Eske Bille, should have paid that money on my behalf at the past settlement-term [omslag], and demanded my bond back; but he could not get word of it in time, but offered to make all right at the next settlement — and I suppose he has already paid the 300 thaler interest.

As for my expenses on building and ironwork at Wandsbek, I expect the Statthalterin will not hold me up longer, nor desire my loss. And since she has bargained Wandsbek [back] from her son Breide [Rantzau], she can best make it right herself; [but] if she will not keep the grilles and ironwork, and what else I had made there, and pay what was not paid for before, then you could take it all away again, and remove all the iron and ship it up here with the other goods. There are also several locks I had made there, and two tiled stoves, besides other things, which you will well be able to learn. I expect you will, in this and all else, do on my behalf what is fair and right; the same, toward you and yours, I will always be diligent to requite. And herewith I commend us all to the protection of the Almighty.

What you advance on my behalf for messenger-fees, or otherwise — that the goods be well protected with boards and mats, or in other ways provisioned — shall be repaid you with thanks.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Tycho's reply to Simon von Petkum (his Hamburg/Wandsbek agent), answering brahe-t_16000212001 (12 February 1600) point by point. A draft (Kladde), breaking off unsigned.
  • Significance — Tycho's northern logistics and the great Magdeburg anecdote.
  • Forwarding routes. Letters via Leipzig→Dresden, Nuremberg, or Prague (Dr. Heller, on the Lesser Town).
  • The instrument-ship. Via Vincent Möller (Hamburg burgomaster) and Jochum Boch (Joachim Buch) at Lübeck (who holds the imperial letter until the Lübeck goods reach Hamburg); the plan to bring all the instruments and unsold goods up to Bohemia by ship, with a Hamburg overseer, the ships returning with millstones.
  • The Magdeburg anecdote (a vivid court scene). Magdeburg twice refused the Emperor's request to ship Tycho's stored goods, pleading "the harsh siege they suffered a half-hundred years ago" (the 1550–51 Siege of Magdeburg) — Rudolf II "shook his head" in displeasure; and a councillor's jibe: "We had thought those of Magdeburg were courtiers; now we perceive they are peasants … the time may come when they need His Majesty again." The Emperor redirected the order to the Magdeburg cathedral Chapter (cf. brahe-t_16000405001); Tycho caps it with the Latin tag "sero sapiunt Phryges" ("too late the Phrygians grow wise") — the now-willing Magdeburgers are too late.
  • David Fabricius's recruitment. Tycho invites Fabricius to come to Prague "with wife and children" by ship from Hamburg, with travel-money advanced by Petkum — the next step from the death-rumour aftermath (cf. brahe-t_16000212001, brahe-t_15991216001). Routed via Ridderhusen at Bremen → Resterhafe (Fabricius's parish).
  • Sophie Brahe & Erik Lange. Tycho approves the coach-wagon given his sister Sophie Brahe ("a much greater favour I would let befall her"); asks where she has gone from Wandsbek; and inquires after Erik Lange — the alchemist nobleman (Sophie's betrothed/husband, ruined by alchemy and living in exile-debt) — "how his affairs stand, where he now dwells."
  • The remaining goods. A striking inventory to ship: "the positive [organ]" (a chamber pipe-organ), the small cannon (of copper/iron), barrels, grilles, locks, two tiled stoves, and ironwork from Wandsbek — all to be put "on a clear register." A rare glimpse of Tycho's household furnishings.
  • Finances. The 5000-thaler bond to be redeemed by Eske Bille at the next omslag (cf. brahe-t_15991029001), the 300-thaler interest likely already paid; the Wandsbek building costs and the Statthalterin / Breide Rantzau estate matter (cf. brahe-t_16000212001).

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). 'mallet... non denegasse' (would prefer/wish that he had NOT refused) is a counterfactual wish about the past, implying the Magdeburger DID refuse and now regrets it — which is exactly what the following idiom 'sero sapiunt Phryges' (the Phrygians grow wise too late) comments on. The English instead has him 'now claim that he did not deny' the service, turning an admission-with-regret into a present-tense denial of fact — the opposite posture toward his own past conduct. At: “The Magdeburger, as it were reborn, would now claim that he did not deny that service to the Emperor, and would now glad”.

Repaired. Verified against the Dreyer page image, TBOO XIV p.197 (letter spans pp.195-199; pages 195-198 examined). The Latin 'mallet... non denegasse... et nunc libenter, si liceret, obtemperaret, sed sero sapiunt Phryges' is grammatically a regretful counterfactual wish (perfect infinitive under 'mallet'), consistent with the following proverb about too-late wisdom; the published English had converted this into a present-tense denial, which the fix corrects. The Latin itself is left untouched as instructed for a mistranslation defect.
