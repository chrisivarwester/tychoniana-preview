TYCHO BRAHE AD DANIELEM CRAMERUM

S. P.

CLARISSIME et Praestantissime Vir, Amice plurimi mihi semper faciende: Literae tuae, pari eruditione et benevolâ erga me affectione affatim certantes, quas circa initia Decembris superioris Anni ad me dedisti, paulo post huius labentis primordia mihi redditae sunt, unâ cum ijs, quas communis noster Amicus, Dominus Doctor LUCAS BACKMESTERUS, per filium natu maiorem, quem in Daniam ablegaram pro auferendis residuis Instrumentis, huc redeuntem miserat. Fuerunt eae mihi admodum gratae, inprimis vero Oda illa ad Caesam Matem, elegans sane et arguta, et quae vel sola lauream te mereri testari poterit, pro quâ etiam ab ipsius Mate obtinendâ ego meo calculo tibi non sum defuturus, arrepta occasione idoneâ. Ipsius Matas per semestre Pragae non fuit, sed in urbe Pilsna, 10 milliaribus inde remotâ, ob luem Epidemicam, quae totam Bohemiam superiore Anno graviter afflixit, commorata est; interea paucissimis patuit ad ipsius Matem accessus; speramus eam brevi Pragam reversuram.

Gratissimum insuper mihi est, quod insolentem istum Scotulum redarguere eiusque inverecundam arrogantiam compescere non detrectes: dignum se reddidit, cui vaniloquum et in alios scurriliter mordax obtruderetur os. Adfuit mihi Uraniburgi in mensa Dominus Doctor NICOLAUS KRAGIUS, Professor Haffniacus et Historicus Regius, cum DUNCANO tunc simul praesenti meas Hypotheses aperirem; et licet idem KRAGIUS non libenter omnia, quae hac de re comperta habet, fateatur, quod Scotis, qui de ipso bene meriti sunt, contradicere nolit (nam Rex Scotiae, cum ibi Legatum ageret, eum nobilitavit et insigni aureâ torque donavit) attamen in literis, quas pridie Nonarum Decembris Anni superioris ad me dedit, haec verba suapte manu adscripsit: »DUNCANUS certe quicquid tandem privatim egerit, publice nunquam eam (loquitur de Hypothesi nostra) sibi arrogavit, qui ne per somnium quidem de eâ cogitavit, antequam ex te hanc tanquam mysterium cognosceret. Quod simul ac patefecisses, memini a te dictum, plus ei communicatum quam frater fratri edisserere fortean vellet. Atque reperi in paucis hisce diebus inter schedas meas chartam, quâ mihi petenti eam signâsti; sive ante DUNCANI adventum, sive quo tempore apud te ille incertus sum.« Haec ille nolens volens fatetur; quo Anno id fuerit, ex literis ipsius DUNCANI ad me, postquam Rostochium redierat, quibus Hypothesin illam meam sibi apprime placere scribit, eamque Ptolemaicae et Coperniceae praefert, quarum te exemplum habere existimo, facile colliges. Est mihi ipsius DUNCANI autographum ad manus, quod inficiari non potest, etiamsi in mille se verteret formas; et quantum subsidij habeat ex meis, de quibus tam frivole gloriatur, literis, vel lippis oculis loci peristasin consideranti discernere facillimum. Rem apprime gratam nec indecentem praestiteris, si quae contra eum veritatis asserendae caussâ meditatus es, compleveris, mihique, prius quam publicentur, transmiseris, ut si quae praeterea eo facientia ad manus habeam, annotem, tibique mox restituam.

Existimo te vidisse scurrile quoddam et supra modum calumnijs atque convitijs scatens, omninoque famosum scriptum cuiusdam efferi Dithmarsi, Ursi potius quam hominis (uti sese vocat) contra quod, si per otium, quae restitutionis loco tibi in mentem venerint, consignare non degravatus fueris, quae ego postea locupletabo, cum nihil sit in eo, quod non minimo negotio refelli queat, etiam ubi invictis Geometriae demonstrationibus triumphum agere praesumit, singulare mihi praestiteris officium, et maiori quam antea beneficio me tibi devinxeris, meque vicissim tui nominis atque honoris studiosum et gratum experiere. Ego contra tam rabiosum allatratorem quidpiam sub meo nomine, dilutionis loco, edere indecorum existimo, sed potius legibus istud contra Imperij constitutiones inverecundum facinus castigari suo tempore permittam. Hactenus rem omnem dissimulavi, partim propter aulam longe dissitam et pro maiori parte distractam, partim quod gravioribus occupationibus distinerer; sed quod differtur, non aufertur. Feceris OLIGERO nostro rem inprimis acceptam, si et hac in parte desiderijs nostris morem gesseris: dici etenim vix potest, quam moleste is tulerit, cum primum illi de putido illo libro innotuit; et fustibus ac plagis excipiendum authorem censuit, sed praestat legibus. Verum desino te serioribus occupatum talibus nugis remorari: extorsit ista mea erga te confidentia sincerusque et mutuus amor. De me vero vicissim tibi quidvis audacter pollicearis. Vale. Dabantur ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ pridie Paschatis Anno 1600, qui Romae habetur Iubilaeus.

Quae in Oda tua per se satis concinne elaborata paulo aliter legi malui, in subiuncta charta notavi, quod licet tuâ bonâ veniâ admitti posse mihi persuadeam, tamen indicandum duxi, quo conscius esses.

TYCHO BRAHE TO DANIEL CRAMER

Heartiest greeting.

Most renowned and excellent Man, a friend always to be highly valued by me: your letter — abundantly vying in equal erudition and benevolent affection toward me — which you sent me about the beginning of December last year, was delivered me a little after the beginning of this [year], together with those our common friend Lord Doctor Lucas Backmeister had sent through my elder son (whom I had dispatched into Denmark to bring away the remaining Instruments), returning hither. They were very welcome — but especially that Ode to the Imperial Majesty, elegant and pointed, which even alone could attest you deserve the laurel; for the obtaining of which from His Majesty I too will not fail you with my vote, a fit occasion seized. His Majesty was not at Prague for half a year, but stayed at Pilsen, 10 miles distant, on account of the epidemic plague which gravely afflicted all Bohemia last year; meanwhile access to His Majesty lay open to very few; we hope he will soon return to Prague.

It is most welcome to me, besides, that you do not decline to refute that insolent little Scot and curb his shameless arrogance: he has made himself deserving that a vain-speaking, scurrilously biting muzzle be thrust upon him. Lord Doctor Nicolaus Krag, Professor at Copenhagen and Royal Historian, was present with me at table at Uraniborg, when I opened my Hypotheses to Duncan, then also present; and although Krag does not willingly confess all he knows of this (because he will not contradict the Scots, who have deserved well of him — for the King of Scotland, when Krag was Legate there, ennobled him and gave him a distinguished gold chain), yet in the letter he sent me on 4 December last year he added these words in his own hand: "Duncan certainly — whatever he may at last have done privately — never publicly arrogated it [he speaks of our Hypothesis] to himself, who did not even in a dream think of it before he learned it from you as a mystery. As soon as you had revealed it, I remember it was said by you that more was communicated to him than a brother would perhaps wish to expound to a brother. And I found, in these few days, among my papers, a sheet on which you marked it for me at my request — whether before Duncan's arrival, or while he was with you, I am uncertain." These things he confesses, willing or not. In what year it was, you will easily gather from Duncan's own letter to me (after he had returned to Rostock), in which he writes that that Hypothesis of mine pleased him exceedingly, and prefers it to the Ptolemaic and Copernican — a copy of which I think you have. I have Duncan's own autograph at hand, which he cannot deny, even if he turn himself into a thousand shapes; and how much support he has from my letters — about which he so frivolously boasts — it is most easy to discern, even with bleary eyes, for one considering the circumstances. You will do a thing exceedingly welcome and not unbecoming, if you complete what you have meditated against him for the sake of asserting the truth, and transmit it to me before it is published, that, if I have anything else conducing to it, I may annotate and soon return it.

I think you have seen a certain scurrilous writing, teeming beyond measure with calumnies and insults, altogether infamous, of a certain savage Dithmarsian — a Bear [Ursus] rather than a man (as he calls himself) — against which, if at leisure you would set down what comes to your mind by way of refutation (which I will afterward enrich) — since there is nothing in it that cannot be refuted with the least trouble, even where he presumes to triumph with "invincible demonstrations of Geometry" — you will do me a singular service, bind me by a greater benefit than before, and find me in turn zealous and grateful for your name and honour. For myself, I think it unbecoming to publish anything under my own name, by way of refutation, against so rabid a barker — but I will rather let that shameless crime against the Empire's constitutions be punished by the laws in its time. Hitherto I have dissembled the whole matter, partly because of the court being far off and mostly scattered, partly because I was held by weightier occupations; but what is deferred is not taken away. You will do our Holger a thing exceedingly welcome, if in this part too you comply with our wishes: for it can scarcely be said how ill he took it when that putrid book first became known to him — he judged the author should be received with cudgels and blows, but it is better [left] to the laws. But I cease to delay you, occupied with more serious things, with such trifles: my confidence toward you, and our sincere and mutual love, extorted these. Of me, in turn, promise yourself anything boldly. Farewell. Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the day before Easter, the year 1600, which at Rome is held as the Jubilee.

What in your Ode — in itself quite elegantly wrought — I preferred to read a little otherwise, I have noted on the appended sheet; which, though I persuade myself it can be admitted with your good leave, I thought should be indicated, that you might be aware.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Daniel Cramer (1568–1637), the Pomeranian (Stettin) Lutheran theologian, poet, and polymath — here a literary and polemical ally of Tycho's, who had sent an Ode to Rudolf II (Tycho promises to help him to the imperial laurel) and is willing to write refutations on Tycho's behalf.
- Significance — a principal document of Tycho's priority/plagiarism disputes (with the verbatim Krag testimony). Three intertwined controversies:
1. John Craig ("the insolent little Scot"). Tycho welcomes Cramer's refutation of Craig (the Scotulus; cf. the cometary Capnurania dispute, brahe-t_15990321001, brahe-t_15981231002).
2. The Duncan affair (priority of the Tychonic hypothesis). Tycho recounts that, at his table at Uraniborg, he revealed his world-system hypotheses to "Duncan" (a Scot of Craig's circle), in the presence of Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, Copenhagen professor and Royal Historian). Krag — reluctant to testify against the Scots, who had honoured him (ennobled and gold-chained by James VI of Scotland when Krag was Danish legate) — nonetheless wrote Tycho (4 Dec 1599) the verbatim words quoted here: that "Duncan … never publicly arrogated the Hypothesis to himself, who did not even in a dream think of it before he learned it from you as a mystery," and that Krag still has the sheet on which Tycho diagrammed it for him. Tycho adds that he holds Duncan's own autograph letter preferring the Tychonic hypothesis "to the Ptolemaic and Copernican" — documentary proof of priority.
3. Ursus. The "scurrilous, infamous writing of a certain savage Dithmarsian — a Bear (Ursus) rather than a man" — i.e. Nicolaus Reimers Bär's De astronomicis hypothesibus (Prague 1597; cf. brahe-t_16000328001, brahe-t_15990321001). Tycho will not answer under his own name "so rabid a barker," but pursue it by law ("that shameless crime against the Empire's constitutions … punished by the laws"); he enlists Cramer for a refutation, to vet before publication. Holger Rosenkrantz's fury at the book ("he judged the author should be received with cudgels and blows, but it is better [left] to the laws") is reported.
- Notable phrases. quod differtur, non aufertur — "what is deferred is not taken away"; Ursi potius quam hominis (uti sese vocat) — the standing pun on Ursus = Bear; vel lippis oculis — "even with bleary eyes" (the obvious). The closing notes that Tycho emended Cramer's Ode (as he did Brasch's, Fischer's verses).
- Persons named. Daniel Cramer; Lucas Backmeister; John Craig (the Scotulus); Duncan (the Scot who learned the hypothesis); Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, quoted; ennobled by James VI); Nicolaus Reimers Bär (Ursus); Holger Rosenkrantz; Rudolf II.
- Calendar. Dateline pridie Paschalis Anno 1600, qui Romae habetur Iubilaeus — "the day before Easter 1600, which at Rome is the Jubilee" = 1 April 1600 [New Style] (Gregorian Easter 2 April 1600). Header agrees with the label.
- Correction. Latin closing date 'pridie Paschalis' corrected to 'pridie Paschatis' (Dreyer TBOO VIII p.292, where the word is itself marked with an editorial obelus in Dreyer's own apparatus); no change to the English, which already read correctly.
