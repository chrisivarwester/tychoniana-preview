The original

DOCTISSIMO VIRO MAGISTRO ANDREÆ WELLEIO, CANONICO RIPENSI, AMICO SUO DENTUR.
S.

SI vales bene est, ego cum meis (quod Deo acceptum refero) mediocriter me habeo. Miror, te multo tempore nihil ad me dedisse literarum neque cum HELIA meo quidpiam scripsisse. Sicut prorsus mei immemor factus? Da operam, ut exarationem quandam eorum habeam, quæ in Privilegio Danico commode continebuntur, de quibus aliquoties tecum egi, sed Iutica fides in verbis quidvis etc. Cuperem hac hyeme habere, nam primus Tomus mei operis iam sub prelo est. Moletrina papyro conficiendæ destinata unâ cum annexis iam parata est, et aqua adest in sufficienti copia non saltem hyberno, sed etiam æstivo tempore, omniaque rite se habent, saltem chartopæus defideratur. Nam is, qui ERICO inservierat, quem Lubecam pro veteramentis conquirendis misi, nusquam postea comparuit. Si talem Artificem illic expiscari poteris, mitte illum huc mature, pro suo labore debite remunerandum. Distantias omnium locorum in Cymbria et alibi in Dania, quotquot hactenus adeptus es, mihi describi cura et quamprimum huc mitti. Rectius erit, si unius loci a duobus alijs, qui triangulum ferme effecerint, intercapedinem denotaveris, quoties id fieri queat; tum etiam sylvæ, flumina, portus, littora, anfractus et similia, qua fieri potest diligentia, designanda veniunt. Effice ut cito habeam, nam præterquam quod Daniæ chartam aliquando per ocium emendate conficiendam mediter, in hoc etiam quem nunc imprimi curo libro, in tabella quadam, ubi longitudines et latitudines diversorum Europæ locorum correctiores, quoad eius fieri potest, ob meridianorum differentias discernendas comprehenduntur, Patriæ etiam nostræ insigniora quædam loca secundum longum et latum verificata apponere animus est. Fac itaque, ut nulla sit in te mora, quin propediem quæ desidero obtineam, et per hunc ipsum nuncium vel quemvis alium obvium rescribe. Vale et METTAM tuam unâ cum utroque Parente cæterisque Ripensibus amicis saluta.

Datæ Uraniburgi die 20. Novemb. 89.
Tuus
TYCHO BRAHE
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To be delivered to the most learned man Master Anders Vedel, canon of Ribe, his friend.
Greeting.

If you are well, it is good; I, with my household — for which I give thanks to God — am moderately well. I wonder that for a long while you have sent me no letters, and have written nothing even by way of my [servant] Elias. As though you had become quite forgetful of me? See to it that I may have some draft of those things which can conveniently be contained in the Danish Privilege, about which I have dealt with you several times — but "Jutish good faith in words [is] anything you please," etc. I should like to have it this winter, for the first volume of my work is now under the press. The mill destined for the making of paper, together with its appurtenances, is now ready, and water is at hand in sufficient supply not only in the winter but also in the summer season, and everything is in good order; only a papermaker is wanting. For the man who had served Erik — whom I sent to Lübeck to seek out old rags [for paper] — has nowhere appeared since. If you can fish out such a craftsman thereabouts, send him here in good time, to be duly rewarded for his labour. Take care to have the distances of all places in the Cimbrian peninsula [Jutland] and elsewhere in Denmark, as many as you have so far obtained, written out for me and sent here as soon as possible. It will be more correct if you note the interval of one place from two others which form roughly a triangle, as often as that can be done; then too the woods, rivers, harbours, shores, windings [of the coast] and the like ought to be marked down, with what diligence may be possible. Bring it about that I may have it quickly, for — besides that I am planning at some time, at leisure, to make a corrected map of Denmark — in this very book also, which I am now having printed, on a certain table, where the longitudes and latitudes of various places of Europe are set down more correctly (so far as that can be done) for distinguishing the differences of the meridians, it is my intention to add certain of the more notable places of our homeland too, verified according to longitude and latitude. See to it, therefore, that there be no delay on your part, but that I shortly obtain what I desire; and write back by this same messenger or by any other that comes to hand. Farewell, and greet your Mette together with both her parents and the rest of your friends at Ribe.

Given at Uraniborg, the 20th day of November, [15]89.
Yours,
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Magister Andreas Wellejus, canonicus Ripensis = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), the Danish historian and royal historiographer, canon of Ribe (Lat. Ripa); Tycho's old friend from their student years. Tycho's request for distance-measurements supports both Vedel's and his own cartographic and historical projects.
  • Place. Uraniburgum = Uraniborg, Tycho's observatory-castle on the island of Hven. Ripa = Ribe, in Jutland; Cymbria = the Cimbrian peninsula, i.e. Jutland (the humanist name from the Cimbri). Lubeca = Lübeck.
  • Persons named. Helia meo — "my Elias," evidently a servant or messenger of Tycho's (the diminutive-affectionate meus); not further identified here. Erico — "Erik," likewise unidentified; the man Tycho mentions had formerly been in this Erik's service. Both are flagged as unidentified rather than guessed at.
  • Salutation. S. = salutem (dicit), "greeting". The address-line …AMICO SUO DENTUR ("let it be given to … his friend") is the exterior direction/superscription.
  • Privilegium Danicum — "the Danish Privilege": a printing privilege (royal licence/copyright) for Tycho's works to be issued under Danish authority; Tycho wants Vedel to draft the text of what it should cover. Rendered literally "Danish Privilege" and glossed.
  • Iutica fides in verbis quidvis — "Jutish good faith in words [is] anything you please": a proverbial gibe at the unreliability of Jutlanders' promises (Vedel was a Jutlander, of Ribe), Tycho twitting his friend for his long silence. The clause is elliptical (no main verb); rendered with a bracketed copula and the etc. kept, the proverbial force flagged rather than smoothed.
  • Papermaking. primus Tomus mei operis iam sub prelo est — "the first volume of my work is now under the press": the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (begun printing at Uraniborg c. 1588–89). Moletrina papyro conficiendae destinata — "the mill destined for the making of paper," Tycho's own paper-mill at Hven. chartopaeus (= chartopoeus, Gr. χαρτοποιός) — "papermaker," the craftsman who makes the sheets; rendered "papermaker." veteramenta — "old rags / cast-off cloth," the linen rag-stock from which hand-made paper is beaten; rendered "old rags [for paper]." Artifex — "craftsman."
  • Cartography / surveying. Distantias … describi … unius loci a duobus alijs, qui triangulum ferme effecerint, intercapedinem — "the distances … the interval of one place from two others which form roughly a triangle": Tycho is requesting triangulation data for a map of Denmark. The phrasing describes the triangulation principle without using a single technical term; rendered descriptively. anfractus — "windings, bends [of the coastline]." Daniae chartam … emendate conficiendam — "a corrected map of Denmark." tabella … ubi longitudines et latitudines … ob meridianorum differentias discernendas — "a table … where the longitudes and latitudes … for distinguishing the differences of the meridians": a geographical coordinate table planned for the book under the press. secundum longum et latum verificata — "verified according to longitude and latitude" (longum/latum here = the long/lat coordinates).
  • Mettam tuam — "your Mette": Mette, Vedel's wife (rendered with the Danish given-name form, since the Latin accusative Mettam simply Latinises the Danish name). utroque Parente — "both her parents" (taken as Mette's parents, the natural antecedent of tuam); the Latin uterque Parens could in principle mean Vedel's own two parents, but the possessive attaches to Mette — the reading is noted.
  • manu propria — "in his own hand," the autograph subscription formula.
  • Calendar. Datae Uraniburgi die 20. Novemb. 89 = "Given at Uraniborg, 20 November [15]89" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline date agrees with the catalogue label (1589. 20. november. Uraniborg); no discrepancy.
  • Textual note. defideratur (line: saltem chartopaeus defideratur) is a printer's/copyist's spelling of desideratur ("is wanting/lacking"); kept as printed in the Source block, rendered "is wanting." MS witness A is a later copy (Afskrift) in Tycho's private archive, not an autograph original.
