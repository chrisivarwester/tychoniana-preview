The original

OS ELSCHELIGE ERLIGE OG VELBIURDIG MAND TYGE BRAHE TIL KNUDSTRUP, VOR MAND OG TIENER.

CHRISTIAN den 4., med Guds naade Danmarkes, Norges, Wendis og Gottis konning. Vor gunst tilforn. Vid, at thin skriffvelße, som du Os tilschreffvet haffver sub dato Rostoch den 10. dag julij sidst forleden, er Os nu i thenne uge underdanigst tilstillet, udi hvilken iblant andet opregnes, først at du ingen leilighed havde at tale med Os, førend du drogst her af riget, ey heller du vidste, om sligt var Os angenehm eller icke, derfor haffde du Os underdanigst skrifftlig villid lade forstaa, og at Os uden tuiffl er bevist, at du est af med huis du til den astronomische konstes underholdning her til dags haver været med benaadet, ogsaa at [Vi] samme kunst icke ville holde ved magt, sampt andet meere, som wformodendes sig tildraget og uthen thin skyld og brøde thig er hendet og vederfaret, som du meener. Thernest at thu icke hafver sielff then formue, at du samme konst kand med thin bekostning fuldkomme; og om thu endskiønt haffde thine forrige forlenninger, tha estu dog saa kommen til achters, at du nødes til at afhende thit godtz. Och effterthi thu af forberørdte aarsage est trengt til at søge paa andre stæder, hos fremmede potentater og herrer, hielp, middel og raad thend astronomische kunst at forfremme oc thit naffn hos udlændische ved macht at holde, tha beder thu, at Vi saadan thin reyße icke vilde tage til nogen wnaade, besynderligen forthi thu icke end aldeeles vilt forlade thit fæderneland. Vijdere formelder thu, at thersom thet kand bliffve thig forgyndt, at thu her uthi rijget maa thit foretagne arbeyd forfølge, tha vilde thu thet icke forsijge, men Os og thit fæderne rijge then ære gierne for nogen anden forunde, om thet kand schee med lijdelige vilkor oc uden thin skade, efftersom thin vijdløfftig skriffvelße derom vijdere indholder. Tha ville Vi thig therpaa naadigst icke forholde, først hvad anlanger, at thu icke haffver hafft leilighed at tale med Os, førend du drogst her af riget, oc thig var vbewist, om sligt kunde være Os angenehm eller ey, saa vedstu thig vel at erindre, at du udi nogle uger laae udi Vor kiøbsted Kiøbenhaffn, førend du drogst af rijget, oc icke allene icke forloff af Os begierede, at thu maatte af rijget bort-reijße, som thig haffde burd at giøre, men aldrig engang thalede med Os, undertagendes then ene gang, the Huensche bønder og thu varst for Os udi rette, oc thig bleff paalagt oc befalet at møde for Os paa slottet. Oc enddog thu icke blues thin undschyldning her om at giøre, saaleedes som thu varst Vor jeffnlijge, tha ville Vi dog vdi thenne Vor skriffvelße thig lade vijde, at Vi sligt forstaae, ocsaa at Vi effter thenne dag ville anderleedes være af thig respecteret, saafrembt thu i Os skalt befinde en naadig herre oc konning. Angaaende attu icke tuiffler, at Os er bevist thig at være opschreffvet noget, som thu varst med forlænet, og dog formelder sligt at være scheed uden thin skyld og brøde, saa vedst thu thig sielff at erindre, huad klagemaal Vore fattige undersaatter oc bønder paa Huæn imod thig hafft hafver. Huorledis thu med kircken ther sammesteds handlet haffver, hvis indkomst og tiender thu udi mange aar til dig haffver annammet, oc ingen forordnet til kircke-verge, men ladet den bygfeldig staa, wdi lijge maade taget jorden fra p[ræ]stegaarden, husene enpart aftaget, oc præsten, som ther udi boe skulde og jorden bruge, sig og sin hustru til ophold, hannem haffver thu giffvet nogle β om ugen, och spißet hannem hos thine arbeyds folk, saa ther paa nogle faa aars tid haver været mange praester, og dog icke haffver bekommet kald af meenigheden effter ordinantzen, ey heller ere dømbte derfra, som thet sig burde. Uthi huad maade daabens ord ere i langsommelig thiid udeladt, imod disße rigers vedtagne brug, med thit vidschab, sligt er huer mand ald for vitterligt. Hvilket forberørte med andet meere, som sig paa thet forberørte lille og ringe land tildraget haver oc Os en god rum tijd, førend thet er kommen offentlig for hver mand, til denne dag haffver været bevist, haver aarsag givet, Vore og kronens bønder andre at forlæne, som thennem ved low, ret og landsens vedtagne brug holde ville. Hvad belanger, at thu ikke est saa formuendes, attu kanst af dit eget fuldkomme den foretagne astronomische konst, om thu skiønt solte thit fæderne-gods oc haffde enda thine forlæninger, men er draget af riget fremmede potentater om hielp at besøge, og icke i then meening, at thu thit fæderneland plat vilde forlade, og beder underdanigst, at Vi slig thin reyße icke ville tage til wnaade, saa bæres stor tvifl, at du de penninge, som du haver bekommet for thit soldte godtz, hafver anlagt paa the astronomische instrumenter, effterthi ther sijges, at thu i mange tusind thal haffver thennem til herrer og førster at vdlaane, thine børn til bæste oc icke riget til nogen ære eller konsten til forfremmelße. Ocsaa er Os aldeeles ukiært at forfare, at du hos andre herrer og førster uthi the maader hielp søger, lijgesom Wi oc riget ware saa wformuendes, at Vi sligt ikke af sted kunde komme, mindre thu med qvinde og børn skulle uddrage og slig penninge hos andre bettle. Thog effterthi thet nu giord er, tha nødes Vi sligt at lade skee, og icke Os i nogen maade anmode, enten thu drager af landet eller bliver derudj. Sidst, efftersom thu underdanigst formelder, at dersom thet thig maa forloffves, thit forretagne arbeid her i riget at fuldende, tha vilde du det ikke forsige, oc at thet skee maatte uden thin skade, faa ville Vi thig herpaa naadigst hafve svaret: at saafremt thu thiene vil for en mathematico og giøre thet, hand bør at giøre, tha skaltu først underdanig tilbiude thin tieniste og therom bede som en tienere bør at giøre, og icke med slig fortagdte ord (at thu thet icke vilt forsijge) fremføre thin meening. Naar thet skeer, tha ville Vi Os siden therpaa tilbørligen vide at erklære. Och efftersom thin skriffvelße thilmed noget seltzom er stijlet, oc icke uden stor wforstand oc dristighed, ligesom Vi thig meere schulde giøre regnschab, huorfor og af huad aarsag Vi paa Vort og kronens godtz giorde forandring, og Vi therhos Os erindrer, hvorleedes thu adschillige snach om Vor kiære her fader haffver iblant thine e[pistolæ] ladet udgaae Hans Kierlighed saavelsom thig self til forvid og forkleinering, tha ville Vi thet med thette Vort breff forbødet haffve, attu samme thit breff, som thu Os nu haffver tilschreffvet, icke lader udgaae paa prent, saa frembt thet skeer, thu icke af Os therfor vilt tiltales og straffes, som thet sig bør. Befalendes thig Gud.

Skreffvet paa Vort slot Kiøbenhaffn thend 8. octobr: a:o 1597. Vnder Vort signet.
Christian.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To our beloved, honourable and well-born man Tyge Brahe of Knudstrup, our man and servant.

Christian the Fourth, by the grace of God King of Denmark, Norway, the Wends and the Goths. Our favour beforehand. Know that your letter, which you wrote to us under date of Rostock, the 10th day of July last past, has now this week been most humbly delivered to us; in which, among other things, the following are set out: first, that you had no opportunity to speak with us before you departed from this realm, nor did you know whether such [a departure] was agreeable to us or not, and that you therefore had wished most humbly to make [it] known to us in writing; and that it is doubtless made known to us that you are deprived of that wherewith you have hitherto been graced for the maintenance of the astronomical art, and also that [we] would not keep up that same art, together with more besides, which (as you maintain) has befallen and come upon you unlooked-for and without your fault and offence. Next, that you do not yourself have the means to complete that same art at your own expense; and that even though you still had your former enfeoffments, you have nonetheless fallen so far into arrears that you are compelled to dispose of your estate. And since, for the aforesaid reason, you are constrained to seek in other places, from foreign potentates and lords, help, means and counsel to advance the astronomical art and to keep up your name among foreigners, you therefore ask that we would not take such a journey of yours in any ill grace, especially because you will not yet altogether forsake your fatherland. Further you state that, should it be granted you that you may here within the realm pursue your undertaken work, you would not refuse it, but would gladly grant to us and your native realm that honour before any other, if it can be done on tolerable terms and without harm to you, as your prolix letter further sets out concerning this.

Therefore we will most graciously not withhold from you our answer thereon. First, as concerns [the claim] that you had no opportunity to speak with us before you departed from this realm, and that it was unknown to you whether such [a departure] could be agreeable to us or not: you well know how to recall that you lay for some weeks in our market-town of Copenhagen before you departed from the realm, and not only did you not request leave of us that you might travel away out of the realm — as you ought to have done — but you never once spoke with us, save that one time when the Hven peasants and you stood before us at law, and you were enjoined and commanded to appear before us at the castle. And although you are not ashamed to make your excuse on this matter, as if you were our equal, yet we will nonetheless in this our letter let you know that we understand such [conduct], and also that after this day we will be otherwise respected by you, if so be that you are to find in us a gracious lord and king. As regards [the point] that you do not doubt it is made known to us that something has been entered against you concerning that wherewith you were enfeoffed, and yet you state that such [a thing] has happened without your fault and offence: you yourself know how to recall what complaint our poor subjects and peasants on Hven have had against you. [Namely] how you have dealt with the church in that same place, whose income and tithes you have for many years taken to yourself, and have appointed no one as churchwarden, but let it stand in disrepair; likewise [how you] took the land away from the parsonage, demolished part of the buildings, and [as for] the priest, who was to dwell therein and use the land for the support of himself and his wife — to him you have given a few shillings [β] a week, and fed him among your workfolk, so that within the space of a few years there have been many priests there, and yet [they] have not received a call from the congregation according to the ordinance, nor have [they] been [duly] dismissed therefrom, as ought [to have been done]. In what manner the words of baptism have for a long time been left out, contrary to the accepted usage of these realms, with your knowledge — such [a thing] is all too well known to every man. Which aforesaid [matters], together with more besides, that have come to pass on the aforesaid small and mean land, and have been made known to us a good long while before it came openly before every man, to this day — [these] have given cause [for us] to enfeoff others, our and the crown's peasants, who will maintain them by law, justice and the accepted usage of the land.

As concerns [the claim] that you are not so well provided that you can of your own [resources] complete the undertaken astronomical art, even if you sold your patrimonial estate and still had your enfeoffments, but [that you] have departed from the realm to seek out foreign potentates for help, and not with the intent that you would utterly forsake your fatherland, and [that you] humbly ask that we would not take such a journey of yours in ill grace: great doubt is borne [as to whether] you have laid out the money you have received for your sold estate upon the astronomical instruments, since it is said that you have them out on loan to lords and princes in many thousands [of dalers], for the benefit of your children and not for any honour to the realm or for the advancement of the art. Also it is altogether displeasing to us to learn that you seek help from other lords and princes in those ways, as if we and the realm were so unprovided that we could not bring such [a thing] to pass — much less that you, with wife and children, should travel away and beg such money from others. Yet since it is now done, we are obliged to let such [a thing] happen, and not to importune us in any way [as to] whether you depart from the country or remain therein. Last, since you most humbly state that, if it be permitted you to complete your undertaken work here in the realm, you would not refuse it, and that it might be done without harm to you, we will most graciously have answered you hereon: that if you will serve as a mathematician and do that which he ought to do, then you shall first humbly offer your service and request it thereon, as a servant ought to do, and not put forward your meaning with such veiled words (that you "would not refuse it"). When that is done, we will thereafter know how to declare ourselves fittingly thereon. And since your letter is moreover couched in a somewhat strange [manner], and not without great want of understanding and boldness — as though we should render you the more account of why and for what reason we made a change upon our and the crown's estate — and since we therewith recall how you have, among your letters [epistolae], let go forth divers gossip about our dear lord father, to His Love's reproach and disparagement as well as your own: we will therefore by this our letter have forbidden [it], that you do not let that same letter, which you have now written to us, go forth in print; and if that happens, [know] that you will be arraigned and punished by us therefor, as is fitting. Commending you to God.

Written at our castle of Copenhagen, the 8th of October, in the year 1597. Under our signet.
Christian.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Christian IV of Denmark (r. 1588–1648), then aged 20 and newly in personal rule after the regency that followed Frederik II's death (1588). This is the famous letter in which the young king repudiates Tycho's appeal, itemises the Hven tenants' and church grievances against him, and refuses to recall the patronage — the document that effectively confirmed Tycho's self-exile.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe (Tyge Brahe) of Knudstrup (1546–1601), styled Vor mand og tiener ("our man and servant") — the feudal-vassal formula, deliberately deflating the standing Tycho had claimed.
  • Occasion / the cited letter. Tycho's own appeal, written sub dato Rostoch den 10. dag julij sidst forleden = "under date of Rostock, 10 July last past" — i.e. from Rostock, where Tycho had gone after leaving Hven in spring 1597. This is an in-letter date (the date of the document being answered), not the date of this letter; reproduced as written, not reconciled, by the edition's convention
  • Structure. The king's reply tracks Tycho's appeal point by point: he first summarises Tycho's four heads (no chance to speak; loss of endowment; lack of private means; the request not to be held in disgrace, and the conditional offer to stay), then answers each in turn. The translation preserves this summary-then-rebuttal structure and the heavy chancery subordination.
  • Editorial insertion [Vi]. The source as transcribed reads …ogsaa at [Vi] samme kunst icke ville holde ved magt — the bracketed [Vi] ("we") is the edition's supplied subject; kept in brackets in both Source and Translation. The clause reports Tycho's grievance that the crown would not maintain the astronomical establishment.
  • Negation check (load-bearing). Several clauses turn on stacked negations and are rendered with the exact count of the source:
  • icke allene icke forloff af Os begierede … men aldrig engang thalede med Os = "not only did you not request leave of us … but you never once spoke with us" — a double-negative emphatic ("not only not … but never"), reproduced as three negations, not collapsed.
  • thu icke blues thin undschyldning her om at giøre = "you are not ashamed to make your excuse on this matter" (i.e. you are shameless enough to excuse yourself) — a single negation; the sense is reproachful, the negation kept.
  • icke ville holde ved magt / ikke ville tage til nogen wnaade — the requests that the king would not keep up the art / would not take the journey in ill grace are Tycho's reported pleas; the king's own refusal is voiced separately. Kept distinct so the negations are not transferred between speakers.
  • icke … forsijge ("would not refuse [it]") — Tycho's conditional offer to stay, which the king mocks as fortagdte ord ("veiled / disguised words"); the negation is central to the king's complaint and is preserved verbatim, including in the parenthetical quotation.
  • Conditional / mood (flagged). The closing prohibition is a conditional with chancery legal force: tha ville Vi thet … forbødet haffve, attu … icke lader udgaae paa prent, saa frembt thet skeer, thu icke af Os therfor vilt tiltales og straffes — literally "we will have forbidden [it], that you do not let [the letter] go forth in print; if so be it happens, [that] you will not be arraigned and punished by us therefor." The Danish is elliptical: the saa frembt thet skeer ("if it happens") clause, with its own icke, is best read as an apodosis of consequence — if you do print it, you will (not escape being) arraigned and punished — i.e. the icke governs an implied "[think] not [that you will escape]". The transcription's literal "you will not be arraigned" would invert the threat; the king is plainly threatening punishment (cf. som thet sig bør, "as is fitting"). The translation renders the evident legal force ("[know] that you will be arraigned and punished") while this note records the elliptical construction and the syntactic crux. This is the principal crux of the letter — see the confidence grade.
  • The peasants' suit. the Huensche bønder og thu varst for Os udi rette = "the Hven peasants and you stood before us at law" — the 1590s litigation between Tycho and his Hven tenants over labour-services and the parsonage, heard before the king. Corroborated by the king's itemised rebuttal (Christianson, On Tycho's Island / Lord of Uraniborg: Tycho took the church's incomes and tithes without maintaining it, removed glebe land from the parsonage, etc.).
  • β (the shilling sign). nogle β om ugen — the glyph β is the early-modern Danish abbreviation for skilling (shilling); rendered "a few shillings [β]," the symbol kept in the Source block by the audit's rule. The complaint is that Tycho paid the resident priest a pittance and boarded him among the labourers.
  • Church / liturgical charge. daabens ord ere … udeladt = "the words of baptism have … been left out," imod disße rigers vedtagne brug "contrary to the accepted usage of these realms" — the grave accusation that the baptismal formula was omitted (a Reformation-confessional offence), med thit vidschab "with your knowledge." effter ordinantzen = "according to the [Church] Ordinance" (the 1539/1542 Danish Kirkeordinans); kald af meenigheden "a call from the congregation."
  • mange tusind thal — "many thousands [of dalers / in tale]"; thal (= tal, "tale/count, reckoning") is here the money-of-account; "[of dalers]" supplied in brackets as the implied unit. The charge is that Tycho lent out large sums to princes for his children's benefit rather than spending on the realm's honour.
  • The late king and the Epistolae. adschillige snach om Vor kiaere her fader … iblant thine e[pistolae] ladet udgaae = "let divers gossip about our dear lord father go forth among your letters [epistolae]" — a reference to Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596), the published correspondence in which passages touched the late Frederik II; Hans Kierlighed ("His Love") is the chancery honorific for the deceased king. The prohibition on printing the present letter follows directly from this grievance.
  • Latin code-switches. sub dato ("under date of"); en mathematico (Latin ablative mathematico, "[as] a mathematician," used inside the Danish syntax); e[pistolae] (abbreviated in the source, expanded in brackets). Preserved by the edition's convention
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1597). Vor gunst tilforn = the royal address-formula ("Our favour beforehand,"); Vid = vid ("know!"); thin/thig/thu = din/dig/du; huis = hvis (here relative "that which"); est af med = "are deprived of / done with"; forlenninger/forlaeninger = forleninger ("enfeoffments, fiefs"); til achters = til agters ("into arrears, behindhand"); afhende thit godtz = "dispose of your estate/goods"; wnaade = unåde ("disgrace, ill grace"); forsijge = forsige ("refuse, decline"); lijdelige vilkor = "tolerable terms"; vijdløfftig = vidtløftig ("prolix, long-winded"); vbewist/bevist = ubevidst/bevidst ("unknown / known"); jeffnlijge = jævnlige ("equal, peer"); bygfeldig = "in disrepair, dilapidated"; p[rae]stegaarden = præstegården ("the parsonage," with the prae- abbreviation expanded in brackets); enpart = "in part"; fortagdte = fordægte/fortagne ("veiled, dissembling"); seltzom = sælsom ("strange, odd"); wforstand = uforstand ("want of understanding"); forkleinering = "disparagement, belittling"; Befalendes thig Gud = "Commending you to God."
  • Calendar / place. Skreffvet paa Vort slot Kiøbenhaffn thend 8. octobr. a:o 1597 = "Written at our castle of Copenhagen, the 8th of October 1597" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline and the edition label (8 October 1597, Copenhagen) agree; no date discrepancy in the header. The only date inside the letter belonging to a different document is Tycho's 10 July appeal from Rostock (above).
