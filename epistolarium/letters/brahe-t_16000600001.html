The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD IOANNEM KEPLERUM.

EGO TYCHO BRAHE de Knudstrup et Uraniburg in Regno Daniæ, nunc vero Sacræ Cæsæ Matis cliens in arce Bohemiæ Bennaticha habitans, notum facio omnibus et singulis hasce litteras inspecturis, cuiuscunque status, dignitatis et conditionis fuerint, earundem exhibitorem, ornatissimum et eruditissimum virum M. KEPLERUM, Ill. Ducatus Styriæ Statuum Mathematicum, hoc labente Anno Epochæ Christianæ 1600 ab initio mensis Februarij usque ad finem Maij, per quatuor nimirum intercedentes menses pro maiori parte hîc in dicta Cæsaris arce Bennaticha mecum fuisse, seque interea in meo convictu honeste gessisse et apprime diligentem operam in exercitijs et studijs Astronomicis præstitisse, intermediantibus meis iustæ magnitudinis et conformationis instrumentis et observationibus multiplicibus atque diutinis, mutuaque inter nos de talibus atque alijs ad Philosophiam facientibus collocutione: ut omnino mihi persuadeam, illum id temporis non minus frugifero hîc collocasse, quam si interea domi desedisset, imo si absque invidia id affirmare liceret, longe utilius. Quo nomine etiam eius præsentia et conversatio mihi imprimis grata extitit, multoque adhuc gratior futura, si eadem in posterum sæpius et diutius concedi poterit.

Quapropter omnibus illustribus, præstantibus et cordatis viris prænominatum M. KEPLERUM de meliore nota quam commendatissimum esse cupio, utque eius laudabile propositum et conatus egregios in hac sublimi et a vulgi captu semota scientia ulterius et plenius excolenda adiuvent et promoveant, quibus possum precibus obnixe et officiose rogo atque obtestor: fecerint sic meo equidem iudicio Reipubl. litterariæ, præcipue vero divinæ illius Astronomiæ totiusque eruditæ Matheseos alumnis rem admodum utilem atque acceptam, quin et non saltem ipsi M. KEPLERO commodam, sed et mihi ob conformia studia longe gratissimam, ac quocunque per me licuerit officio vicissim compensandam et obsequiosa mente perpetuo recolendam. In quorum omnium evidentiorem et pleniorem testificationem sigillum meum apposui et propria manu subscripsi.

Actum in arce Cæsarea Bennaticha inchoante mense Iunio Anno 1600.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe, [concerning] Johannes Kepler.

I, Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup and Uraniborg in the Kingdom of Denmark, but now client of His Sacred Imperial Majesty, dwelling in the Bohemian castle of Benátky, make known to each and all who shall inspect this letter, of whatever status, dignity and condition they may be, that the bearer of the same — the most accomplished and most learned man, Master Kepler, Mathematician of the Estates of the Illustrious Duchy of Styria — in this passing Year of the Christian Era 1600, from the beginning of February until the end of May, that is, through the four intervening months, was for the greater part here with me in the said Imperial castle of Benátky; and that he meanwhile conducted himself honourably in my company, and rendered exceptionally diligent work in astronomical exercises and studies — with the mediation of my instruments of fitting size and construction and of my manifold and long-continued observations, and of our mutual conversation about these matters and others conducing to Philosophy — so that I am wholly persuaded that he spent that time here no less fruitfully than if he had meanwhile sat at home, nay (if it might be affirmed without envy) far more usefully. On which account his presence and converse was especially welcome to me, and will be much more welcome still, if the same may in time to come be granted more often and for longer.

Wherefore I desire that the aforenamed Master Kepler be most warmly commended, in the best regard, to all illustrious, excellent and sensible men; and with what prayers I can I earnestly and dutifully ask and beseech them to aid and promote his laudable purpose and his outstanding endeavours toward the further and fuller cultivation of this sublime science, set apart from the grasp of the common crowd. They will thus, in my judgement, do a thing very useful and welcome to the Republic of Letters, but especially to the nurslings of that divine Astronomy and of all learned Mathematics; nay, a thing not only of advantage to Master Kepler himself, but also — on account of our kindred studies — far most pleasing to me, to be repaid in turn by whatever service shall lie in my power, and perpetually cherished with an obliging mind. In clearer and fuller testification of all which, I have affixed my seal and subscribed with my own hand.

Done in the Imperial castle of Benátky, at the beginning of the month of June, in the year 1600.
TYCHO BRAHE,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Genre. This is not an ordinary letter but an open testimonial / letter of recommendation (litterae testimoniales) — a public certificate, drawn in the notarial first person (Ego Tycho Brahe … notum facio omnibus et singulis hasce litteras inspecturis), sealed and autograph, attesting Kepler's residence and conduct and commending him to "all illustrious men." The heading ad Ioannem Keplerum in the source marks the subject; the document was given to Kepler to carry (earundem exhibitorem, "the bearer of the same").
  • Occasion. It closes Kepler's first stay with Tycho — from the beginning of February to the end of May 1600, the very months of their first collaboration at Benátky (Kepler had arrived 4 February). Tycho here certifies that period and, by commending Kepler to patrons, smooths his position as he returned to Graz amid the Styrian Counter-Reformation expulsions. Within months Kepler would join Tycho's household permanently; on Tycho's death (24 October 1601) he succeeded him as Imperial Mathematician and inherited the observations this document describes.
  • Self-titling. Tycho Brahe de Knudstrup et Uraniburg in Regno Daniae, nunc vero Sacrae Caesareae Maiestatis cliens — Tycho styles himself by his Danish patrimony (Knudstrup) and his lost observatory (Uraniborg on Hven,), "but now client [retainer] of His Sacred Imperial Majesty" (Rudolf II), resident at Benátky.
  • Kepler's title. M. Keplerus, Ill. Ducatus Styriae Statuum Mathematicus = Johannes Kepler, Magister (academic Master,), mathematician to the Estates of the Duchy of Styria (his Graz appointment as Landschaftsmathematiker).
  • Astronomy. mea iustae magnitudinis et conformationis instrumenta et observationes multiplices atque diutinae = Tycho's instruments (instrumenta/organa,) "of fitting size and construction" and his "manifold and long-continued observations" — the working capital of the Benátky establishment that Kepler had been set to use (notably on the Mars data). exercitia et studia Astronomica rendered "astronomical exercises and studies."
  • Formulae. Respublica litteraria → "the Republic of Letters"; Astronomiae … alumni → "the nurslings of Astronomy"; Actum → "Done"; manu propria → "with his own hand" (autograph + seal attestation). The humility/"without envy" topos (absque invidia) is preserved.
  • Calendar & dating crux. The subscription reads inchoante mense Iunio Anno 1600 — "at the beginning of June 1600" — and the body's own span (ab initio mensis Februarij usque ad finem Maij … per quatuor intercedentes menses) requires a date after the end of May. The header therefore leads with the as-written date (beginning of June 1600); the edition's catalogue label "1600. april" disagrees and is recorded as (catalogued April 1600) by the edition's convention, not silently adopted. The April label is most likely a catalogue mis-entry, since the text cannot have been written before the residence it certifies had ended. No calendar-style marker (O.S./N.S.) is given; in Imperial service Tycho was by now dating New Style (cf. the surrounding 1600 letters), but the testimonial states no style, so none is asserted here.
  • Witnesses. Two Vienna manuscripts (ÖNB cod. lat. 10703 and 10686) preserve the text within the Tychonic/Keplerian Nachlass — the same Vienna codices that hold much of the observational archive (cf. the KB↔Vienna collation work).
