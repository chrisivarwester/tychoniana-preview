The original

GENEROSO MAGNIFICO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, EQUITI DANO, S. CÆS. MAIESTATIS CONSILIARIO PRÆCIPUO ET MATHEMATICO LONGE CELEBERRIMO, DOMINO AC PATRONO OBSERVANDO.
PRAGA.

S.

RECTE perlatas esse meas ad te literas, generose et nobilissime vir, index scida superiore Decembri me docuit. Volui, uti suggestum fuerat, rem denuo et statim prehendere; sed avocarunt morbus uxoris, negotia clientum, sexcenta alia. Nunc paullo expeditior, et amanter a vidua illa CUNONIS filia invitatus, libenter ei hoc operæ dare volui, ut, quod bono etiam publico futurum providerem, sollicitatoris partes susciperem, quo opus illud horologicum, multis, magnis ac diuturnis laboribus ac vigilijs elucubratum ac perfectum, lucem tandem sub tuo, aut cuiuscunque videatur nomine, aspicere possit. Superioribus addo hoc quoque, esse apud eandem viduam opus, secundum præcepta ista inchoatum, atque adeo semicompletum, quod morte præventus absolvere auctor non potuit. Qui librum emat paullo liberalius, facile unâ idipsum quoque impetret; cui summam manum peritus in arte et libro isto instructus sine negotio imponat. Sumptum credo facile libenterque ferat pietate, sapientia, virtute et meritis in rempub. maximus, Divus Cæsar RUDOLPHUS, mathematicis disciplinis ipse quoque et supra conditionem principum excultus, quem non ALPHONSUM alterum, sed et illo potiorem non immerito prædicamus. Neque dubium mihi est, quin monitu suggestuque tuo res procedat melius: quod ut facias, sive viduæ illius pauperculæ nomine, sive boni publici respectu, sive quod hanc operam haud difficulter te præstiturum virtus et humanitas mihi tua spondet, quam possum officiose submisseque rogo. Qua de re literas tuas, vel si ijs non vacat, saltem nutum tuum expecto. Si quid ad me velis, et occasio alia non sit, Bratislaviam ad clarissimum Dn. MONAVIUM mittere possis, qui, qua est humanitate et virtute, id porro haud gravate curet. Vale, vir generose et optime, meque et studia mea, quibus ipsis quoque MÆCENATE opus sit, apud Sacram Cæsaream Maiestatem, cui vitam ac felicitatem perpetuam precor, tibi commendata habe.

Vale iterum.
Francofurto cis Viadrum a. d. XII Martij Anno MDCI.
Generostis ac magnif. t. observantiss.
BALTHASAR CAMINAÆUS I. V. D. et professor publ.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the generous, magnificent and most noble man, Lord Tycho Brahe, Danish knight, principal councillor of His Imperial Majesty and by far the most celebrated mathematician, his lord and patron to be revered. Prague.

Greeting.

That my letters were duly delivered to you, generous and most noble man, a note informed me last December. I wished, as had been suggested, to take the matter up anew and at once; but the illness of my wife, the affairs of clients, and six hundred other things called me away. Now a little freer, and kindly invited by that widow, the daughter of Cuno, I have willingly wished to devote this effort to her: that — foreseeing it would be for the public good as well — I might take up the part of advocate, so that that horological work, elaborated and perfected by many, great and long-continued labours and vigils, might at last see the light under your name, or whosever's may seem [best]. To the above I add this too: that there is with the same widow [another] work, begun according to those precepts, and indeed half-completed, which the author, forestalled by death, could not finish. Whoever should buy the book a little more liberally would easily obtain that very thing along with it; on which an expert, skilled in the art and instructed in that book, might without trouble lay the finishing hand. The cost, I believe, would be easily and gladly borne by the Divine Emperor Rudolph — greatest in piety, wisdom, virtue, and services to the commonwealth, himself too cultivated in the mathematical disciplines beyond the condition of princes — whom we proclaim, not undeservedly, not a second Alphonso, but even greater than he. Nor do I doubt that by your advice and prompting the matter will go forward the better; and that you would do this — whether in the name of that poor little widow, or out of regard for the public good, or because your virtue and humanity assure me you will render this service without difficulty — I ask as dutifully and submissively as I can. On which matter I await your letter, or, if you have no time for that, at least your nod. If you should want anything from me, and there be no other occasion, you could send it to Breslau, to the most renowned Lord Monavius, who, being of the humanity and virtue he is, would see to it further without grudging. Farewell, generous and excellent man; and hold me and my studies — which themselves too have need of a Maecenas — commended to you with the Sacred Imperial Majesty, to whom I pray perpetual life and happiness.

Farewell again.
From Frankfurt-on-the-Oder, the 22nd of March, in the year 1601.
Most observant of Your Generosity and Magnificence,
Balthasar Caminaeus, Doctor of Both Laws and public professor.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound letter to Tycho at Prague. Balthasar Caminaeus (Caminäus), Iuris utriusque doctor et professor publicus — doctor of both laws and public professor at Frankfurt an der Oder (Francofurtum cis Viadrum, "Frankfurt on this side of the Oder," to distinguish it from Frankfurt-am-Main).
  • Purpose. Caminaeus acts as advocate (sollicitator) for a widow seeking to get a deceased author's horological work (opus horologicum — a treatise on, or design for, time-measuring / astronomical clockwork) into print, plus a second, half-finished work in the same vein. He asks Tycho to lend his name and influence, and floats Emperor Rudolf II as the natural patron to bear the cost.
  • Persons named.
  • vidua illa Cunonis filia = "that widow, the daughter of Cuno" — the heir pressing for publication; neither she nor her late husband (the auctor of the horological work) is further identified in the source.
  • Divus Caesar Rudolphus = Emperor Rudolf II, praised as himself learned in mathematics and likened to Alfonso X of Castile ("the Wise," patron of the Alfonsine Tables) — non Alphonsum alterum, sed et illo potiorem, "not a second Alfonso, but greater than he."
  • clarissimus Dn. Monavius = Jacob Monau (Jacobus Monavius) (1546–1603), the Breslau humanist and patron of letters, proposed as a relay for any reply.
  • Maecenas invoked generically as the type of the literary patron.
  • Place. Francofurtum cis Viadrum = Frankfurt an der Oder; Bratislavia here = Breslau / Wrocław (the Neo-Latin name Vratislavia, spelled Bratislavia), Monau's city — not modern Bratislava.
  • Epistolary formulae & titles. S. = salutem; Vale … Vale iterum → "Farewell … Farewell again"; subscription Generosit[at]is ac magnif[icentiae] t[uae] observantiss[imus] → "Most observant of Your Generosity and Magnificence"; I. V. D. = Iuris Utriusque Doctor ("Doctor of Both Laws," civil and canon).
  • Calendar & dating crux. The working transcription's dateline reads a. d. XII Martij Anno MDCI — literally "the 12th of March 1601," which does not match the edition's placement. Read as Roman reckoning, the intended form is a. d. XI Kal. Apriles (ante diem undecimum Kalendas Apriles) = 22 March, and Dreyer/the catalogue date the letter accordingly; the transcribed "XII Martij" is a corruption of that Roman dateline. Following Dreyer by the edition's convention, the header gives 22 March 1601 (New Style) — Frankfurt an der Oder being in Brandenburg, but the letter directed to Catholic-Imperial Prague and dated New Style in the edition. The source form is preserved in the block; the reading is explained here rather than silently emended.
