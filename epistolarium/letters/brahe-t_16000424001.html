The original

MAGNIFICO ET PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO D:NO VINCENTIO MOLLERO, AMPLISSIMÆ REIPUB. HAMBURGENSIS CONSULI PRUDENTISSIMO ET V. I. LICENTIATO EXIMIO, AMICO SUO HONORANDO.

MAGNIFICE et excellentissime do:ie Vincenti Mullere, amice honorande, pro nupera euectione ad dignitatem consularem in amplissima vestra republica E:æ T. ex animo congratulor, optoque, ut fausta et salutaris sit. Ago vero gratias, quod me humanissimis tuis literis inuisere non degrauatus sis, et negocium apud reliquos dignissimos consules et senatores vrbis vestræ, de quo cæs:a ma:tas, d:nus noster clementissimus, meo nomine perscripsit, promoturum polliceris. Spero autem, literas ipsius m:tis iam ad vos peruenisse, aut cum instrumentis et rebus meis, quæ Lubecæ sunt, breui allatum irj. Quare E:m T:m diligenter et peramice rogatam cupio, ut sua authoritate et intercessione procurare velit, quo per omnia c:æ m:tis clementissimæ voluntatj meæque expectationi satisfiat. De quibus Simon de Petkum te plenius instruet, ejus enim fidej et curæ rem omnem commisj. Scripsi etiam denuo illi de ijs, quæ Wandesburgj meo ære parata sunt et nondum persoluta, ut compensationem urgeat, in quo etiam negocio Præstantia Tua suo consilio et auxilio, præsertim quo ad vestræ vrbis fabrum et cæteros opifices, qui sciunt, quantum exposuerim illisque numerauerim, quam optime mihi subueniet, nec diffido eam, vt id ingenue fateantur, lubenti animo effecturam. Si quid in his oris, quod T. P:æ gratum erit, a me præstari poterit, inueniet me vicissim ad illi gratificandum quam paratissimum.

Valeat P:a T:a cum tota domo sua quam optime et diutissime.
Dabantur properanter ex arce Cæsarea Benatika 24 aprilis stylo nouo anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and most excellent man, Master Vincent Moller, most prudent Consul of the most ample Commonwealth of Hamburg, and distinguished Licentiate of Both Laws, his friend to be honoured.

Magnificent and most excellent Master Vincent Moller, friend to be honoured: I congratulate Your Excellency from my heart on [your] recent elevation to the consular dignity in your most ample commonwealth, and wish that it be auspicious and salutary. And I give thanks that you did not find it burdensome to visit me with your most kind letter, and that you promise to promote — with the rest of the most worthy consuls and senators of your city — the business about which His Imperial Majesty, our most clement lord, wrote in my name. I hope that His Majesty's letters have now reached you, or will shortly be brought, together with my instruments and goods, which are at Lübeck. Wherefore I desire Your Excellency diligently and most amicably asked, that you be willing, by your authority and intercession, to procure that satisfaction be made in all respects to the most clement will of His Imperial Majesty and to my expectation. About which Simon von Petkum will more fully instruct you, for I have committed the whole matter to his good faith and care. I have also written to him again about those things which were prepared at Wandsbek at my expense and not yet paid for, that he urge the compensation; in which business, too, Your Excellency will best aid me by your counsel and help — especially as regards your city's smith and the other craftsmen, who know how much I laid out and paid to them; nor do I doubt that you will, with willing mind, bring it about that they candidly confess it. If anything can be performed by me in these regions which will be pleasing to Your Excellency, you will find me, in turn, most ready to gratify [you].

May Your Excellency fare as well and as long as possible, with your whole house.
Given hastily from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 24th of April, new style, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Vincent Moller (the Elder), now Consul (Bürgermeister) of Hamburg and V[triusque] I[uris] Licentiatus (Licentiate of Both Laws) — Tycho replies to his letter (brahe-t_15991008001), congratulating him on his elevation and pressing the same Hamburg business.
  • The business. Tycho asks Moller to use his authority with the Hamburg consuls and senators to satisfy the Emperor's letters written "in Tycho's name," and hopes those imperial letters have reached Hamburg "together with my instruments and goods, which are at Lübeck" (the Baltic-leg consignment — cf. brahe-t_15991205001). Simon von Petkum has the full brief.
  • The Wandsbek debts. de ijs, quae Wandesburgi meo aere parata sunt et nondum persoluta — Tycho seeks compensation/repayment for things made at his own expense at Wandsbek and not yet settled, naming "your city's smith and the other craftsmen" who know the sums he paid — i.e. instrument-fittings and work done during his Wandsbek stay (cf. the ferramenta Wandesburgi of brahe-t_15991008001). He trusts Moller can get the craftsmen to "candidly confess" the amounts.
  • Calendar. ex arce Caesarea Benatika 24 aprilis stylo nouo … 1600 = 24 April 1600, New Style (stylo novo explicit); from the Imperial castle of Benátky. Header agrees with the label. (A draft, Kladde.)
