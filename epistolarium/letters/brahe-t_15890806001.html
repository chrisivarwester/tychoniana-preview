The original

LITERÆ CHRISTOPHORI ROTHMANNI AD TYCHONEM BRAHE, AD ANNVM OCTUAGESIMUM NONUM POTIUS PERTINENTES. S. P.

QVID superioribus tibi literis scripserim, et qua ratione syncere voluntati tuæ morem gesserim, tibique meum qualecunque, nec fortassis vsque adeo magni momenti iudicium candide explicarim, Nobilissime Excellentissimeque Dn. TYCHO, mihique longe charissime, tibi est notissimum. Etsi autem in proxime præteritis Nundinis Francofurdensibus responsum non sine desiderio expectaremus, tamen nihil prorsus, quod a te esset, nobis exhibitum est. Cuius rei causam, cum a me impense requireret Illustrissimus Princeps noster, nihil aliud respondere potui, quam aut te non habuisse cui recte literas ad nos committere posses, aut alijs negotijs impeditum fuissent. Hoc vero tempore, de nouo, ab Illustrissimo Principe meo, iam extra munitionem Cassellanam, in Comitatu Hombergico, venationibus vacante, literas mihi attulit Dn. D. NICODEMVS FRISCHLINVS, vir ex eruditis suis scriptis tibi forte notissimus, et Com. Palat, ac Poët. Laure, quibus iubeor, vt a te simul et responsum flagitem, ac te insuper admoneam, ne Opus tuum de Recentioribus mundi ætherei Phænomenis absolutum, Illustriss. ipsius Celsitudini mittere, vlterius differas. Se enim maximo eius videndi desiderio teneri, vt ipsa verba, quæ in dictis Illustriss. ipsius Celsitudinis literis continentur, referam. Scis me superioribus tibi literis significasse, quod spem fecerim Illustriss. ipsius Celsit. de Opere illo tuo â te huc mittendo. Id itaque iam Illustrissimus Heros adeo vrget. Quod igitur superioribus feci literis, illud adhuc a te vehementer etiam atque etiam maiorem in modum peto contendoque, vt fidem meam redimas. Deinde etsi Illustriss. Princeps noster superiori Autumno nihil literarum ad te dedit, varijs modis impeditus, tamen nihilominus aliquas iam a te expectat, præsertim de statu tuo quid agas, et quid iam tibi sit de Astronomicis laboribus præ manibus: Nec ipsi gratior vlla est, quam sibi quæ BRAHÆ præscripsit pagina nomen. Sed non dubito, quin in his omnibus Illustriss. ipsius Celsit. sis cumulatissime satisfacturus. Cum item Illustriss. et Clementiss. Princeps meus mihi mandarit, vt per hunc doctissimum sane virum tibi scriberem, facile per te intelligere poteris, Illustriss. ipsius Celsit. hoc suo ad me mandato hunc tibi eo commendatiorem reddere voluisse. Sed tamen id per te propter studia Philosophica facturus es, vt non ignoro. Cognoui etiam ex literis fratris mei Iohannis, quod disputationes nostras ad Dn. D. PEVCERVM miseris, quodque Dn. PEVCERVS alia consilia proferat, nec mihi aut tibi suffragari velit. Sed hæc quo sint habenda loco tu intelligis. Nihil consilium hîc, nihil humana valet authoritas. Doctrinam Triangulorum qui intelligit, sua sponte in veram pedibus ibit sententiam. Tu fac responsione tua, vt ad dulcissimas nostras Philosophicas disputationes redeamus. Sed fortasse plus quam sat erat.

Tu igitur Nobiliss. et Excellentiss. Dn. TYCHO quam optime vale, quæque scripsi in optimam partem accipe.
Cassellis, 6. Calendas Augusti, Anno 1589.
T. T.
CHRISTOPHORVS ROTHMANNVS.

English translation machine translation, Probable

A LETTER OF CHRISTOPH ROTHMANN TO TYCHO BRAHE, BELONGING RATHER TO THE YEAR EIGHTY-NINE. Heartiest greeting.

What I wrote to you in my last letter, and in what spirit I sincerely complied with your wish, and frankly set out for you my own judgement — such as it was, and perhaps not of any very great moment — is very well known to you, most noble and most excellent Master Tycho, and to me by far the dearest of men. But although at the Frankfurt fair just past we were awaiting a reply not without longing, yet nothing at all that came from you was delivered to us. When our Most Illustrious Prince pressed me earnestly for the cause of this, I could answer nothing else but that either you had had no one to whom you could properly entrust a letter to us, or that you had been hindered by other business. But at this time, anew, from my Most Illustrious Prince — who is now outside the fortifications of Kassel, in the County of Homberg, taking his leisure at the hunt — a letter has been brought to me by Master Doctor Nicodemus Frischlin, a man perhaps very well known to you from his learned writings, Count Palatine and Poet Laureate, by which I am ordered both to demand a reply of you and, moreover, to admonish you not to defer any longer the sending of your completed Work on the recent phenomena of the aethereal world to His Most Illustrious Highness himself. For he is held by the greatest desire to see it — to report the very words contained in the said letter of His Most Illustrious Highness. You know that in my last letter I indicated to you that I had raised His Most Illustrious Highness's hope of that Work of yours being sent here by you. This, therefore, the Most Illustrious Hero now presses so much. What I did, then, in my last letter, that same I still ask and strive for from you vehemently, again and again, in even greater measure: that you redeem my pledge. Next, although our Most Illustrious Prince sent no letters to you last autumn, hindered in various ways, yet he nonetheless now awaits some from you, especially concerning your situation — what you are doing, and what you now have in hand of your astronomical labours; nor is any page more welcome to him than one that has set the name of BRAHE at its head. But I do not doubt that in all these matters you will give His Most Illustrious Highness the most abundant satisfaction. Since my Most Illustrious and Most Clement Prince has likewise charged me to write to you through this man — most learned indeed — you will easily understand for yourself that His Most Illustrious Highness, by this command of his to me, wished to render this man the more commended to you on that account. Yet you will do this on your own account, for the sake of philosophical studies, as I am well aware. I have learned, too, from the letters of my brother Johannes, that you sent our disputations to Master Doctor Peucer, and that Master Peucer puts forward other opinions, and is unwilling to side with either me or you. But in what regard these things are to be held, you understand. Here counsel avails nothing, human authority avails nothing. Whoever understands the Doctrine of Triangles will, of his own accord, go on foot into the true opinion. Do you, by your reply, bring it about that we return to our most delightful philosophical disputations. But perhaps [I have said] more than was enough.

Do you therefore, most noble and most excellent Master Tycho, fare as well as may be, and take in the best part the things I have written.
At Kassel, the sixth day before the Kalends of August [27 July], in the year 1589.
Yours wholly,
Christoph Rothmann.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann (c. 1555–c. 1600), mathematician and astronomer to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel; Tycho's principal scientific correspondent of the late 1580s. He writes here on the Landgrave's instruction. The exchange is one printed by Tycho himself in the 1596 Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (the edition of record).
  • Recipient / addressee titulature. Nobilissime Excellentissimeque Dn. TYCHO — "most noble and most excellent Master Tycho" (Dn. = Dominus, this chapter's convention).
  • The Prince. Illustrissimus Princeps noster / Illustriss. ipsius Celsit. / Illustrissimus Heros — Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592). Celsitudo → "Highness" and Princeps → "Prince" by this chapter's convention; Heros ("Hero") is kept as Rothmann's honorific flourish. munitio Cassellana … in Comitatu Hombergico = "the fortifications of Kassel … the County of Homberg" (Homberg an der Efze, in Hesse).
  • Nicodemus Frischlinus = Nicodemus Frischlin (1547–1590), the Württemberg humanist poet and dramatist, Comes Palatinus (Count Palatine) and Poeta Laureatus (Poet Laureate) — here the bearer of the Landgrave's letter, whom Rothmann commends to Tycho. Com. Palat, ac Poët. Laure. expanded accordingly.
  • Opus tuum de Recentioribus mundi aetherei Phaenomenis — Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (the second volume of his projected trilogy, the comet/world-system book printed at Uraniborg), here named as the work the Landgrave is impatient to receive. Rendered "[your] Work on the recent phenomena of the aethereal world."
  • Nundinae Francofurdenses — "the Frankfurt fair" (the Frankfurt book fair), the standard early-modern channel for transmitting scholarly letters and books.
  • disputationes nostras … ad Dn. D. Peucerum miseris — "you sent our disputations to Master Doctor Peucer." Caspar Peucer (1525–1602), Wittenberg physician-astronomer, whom Tycho was using as an arbiter; Rothmann learned, via his brother Johannes Rothmann, that Tycho had circulated their private debate to Peucer, who would side with neither. Mosley (Bearing the Heavens) quotes this very sentence and dates the letter 27 July 1589, corroborating the dateline below.
  • Doctrina Triangulorum — the Doctrine of Triangles (trigonometry); Rothmann's point that the dispute will be settled by mathematics, not authority. sua sponte … pedibus ibit sententiam renders an idiom of formally "going over" to an opinion (as a senator crosses the floor pedibus in sententiam); kept as "will … go on foot into the true opinion."
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = salutem plurimam → "heartiest greeting". quam optime vale → "fare as well as may be" (, Vale). T. T. = Tuus Totus → "Yours wholly," the subscription.
  • Heading. AD ANNVM OCTUAGESIMUM NONUM POTIUS PERTINENTES — "belonging rather to the year eighty-nine": this is the editor's (Tycho's own 1596) note correcting the letter's placement to 1589, printed as part of the running head. It is translated, not dropped, because it is itself the textual evidence resolving the date.
  • Dateline / calendar. Cassellis, 6. Calendas Augusti, Anno 1589 = "At Kassel, ante diem VI Kalendas Augusti, 1589." Counting inclusively from the Kalends of August (1 Aug.): pridie = 31 July, III Kal. = 30, IV = 29, V = 28, VI Kal. Aug. = 27 July. The date is 27 July 1589 (O.S. — Hesse-Kassel kept the Julian calendar at this period; the Protestant German states did not adopt the Gregorian reform until 1700). Roman-calendar idiom is given with the civil gloss by this chapter's convention.
  • Date crux — catalogue vs. source (RESOLVED, flagged). The edition label reads "1587. 17. januar. Kassel." This is contradicted by two internal witnesses: (1) the letter's own running head, ad annum octuagesimum nonum … pertinentes ("belonging rather to the year eighty-nine"), and (2) the dateline, Anno 1589. The internal content also fixes 1589: the reference to De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis as a completed work the Landgrave is awaiting, the prior-autumn silence, and the circulation of the disputations to Peucer all belong to mid-1589. Mosley independently dates the letter 27 July 1589. The header therefore leads with the as-written date (27 July 1589) and notes the catalogue date; the "17 January 1587" label is a catalogue error and is not adopted. Per this chapter's convention, the discrepancy is recorded and not silently reconciled in the body.
  • Edition of record. The edition field cites Tychonis Brahe Dani epistolarum astronomicarum libri … primus (Uraniborg, 1596), the volume Tycho printed himself; this corresponds to Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI (the Epistolae of the Hesse-Kassel exchange). No manuscript witness is listed in the source.
