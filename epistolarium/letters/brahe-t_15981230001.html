The original

CLARISSIMO ET DOCTISSIMO VIRO M. ANDREAe WELLEIO, CANONICO RIPENSI, AMICO SUO ........ ET DILECTO.
S. P.

CUM hic Tabellarius sponte se mihi [of]ferret, qui in Cimbriam ire et Ripas transire constituit, non intermittendum duxi, amantissime Domine Magister ANDREA, quin aliquid saltem literarum ad te darem. Fecissemque idipsum, antequam Wandesburgo discessi, si negotiorum mole et internuncij penuriâ non fuissem praepeditus. De statu autem mearum rerum sic habe. Cum statim a Michaëlis festo inde abijssem Pragam versus cogitans, a Caesare per Dominum CORRADUCIUM Imperij Procancellarium, intermediante etiam D. THADDÆO HAGGECIO, aliquoties eo accitus, accidit ut cum Dresdam venissem, certo intelligerem, omnia Pragae ob luem Epidemicam isthic uti et alibi in Bohemia ingravescentem, conturbata esse, Caesarem inde discessisse, atque in tutiorem locum se recepisse, consiliarios autem hinc inde dispersos, aulamque totam dissipatam ideoque consilia et iudicia publica feriari et iustitium esse. Quare illic paulisper subsistens, misi proprium Tabellarium cum literis, me Dresdam advenisse indicantibus, et quid in tali rerum statu faciendum sit sciscitantibus. Quarum exemplo a CORRADUCIO Caesari, ubi tunc degebat, transmisso, responsum postea est a Domino BARVITIO, supremo Secretario et Caesari ab intimis consiliis, Caesaream Matem perlibenter de meo adventu intellexisse, utque Dresdae vel alibi in vicinia subsistam, donec aula recolligatur, cupere; interea de loco, ubi commode collocari possim, benigne cogitare. Quo responso accepto (cui etiam lubens acquievi, cum periculosum alias foret Bohemiam ingredi) me huc Wittebergam cum meis recepi. Ubi Deo volente hybernare constitui, vel saltem donec a Caesare avocer, quem scio mihi et meis rebus clementer ac serio favere, idque maiore studio et desiderio quam ...nia factum est, spero etiam constant ... Interim vero dum hîc dego, nonnulla quae in operibus meis nondum Typograph[ico] labore absoluta sunt, complere animus est, unâ cum ijs quae Caesari speciatim destinavi. Habito autem hîc Wittebergae in aedibus, quae olim erant optimae recordationis PHILIPPI MELANCHTONIS, nunc PEUCERI, inquilino earum me excipiente Domino Doctore IOHANNE IESSENIO Philosophiae et Medicinae Doctore. Qui ut est vir syncerus et humanus, meique amantissimus, plusquam dimidiam aedium partem pro me et mea familia inhabitandam benigne concessit, ita ut mediocri hîc fruar oportunitate, donec res meae providentia divina uberiorem commoditatem sortitae fuerint. Curo autem nunc adduci Wandesburgo familiam meam muliebrem proprijs curribus, per filium Tychonem, pro qua ......... donec amplior volente Numine a Caesare nobis omnibus concessus fuerit. Cuius in arbitrium totus mi... clauditur orbis. Haec brevibus significare volui ob veterem inter nos necessitudinem, ne rerum nostrarum inscius esses, aut me tui immemorem suspicareris. Forte tamen similia aut his plura a Mag. CHRISTIERNO SEVERINO LONGOMONTANO, meo aliquande domestico, qui etiam me Magdeburgum comitatus est et Gynaecaeum meum idem Wandesburgum reduxit, intellexisti. Relatum enim mihi est eum Ripis nuper fuisse, et meas isthuc ad eum perlatas esse literas, quas illi, quam primum huc veni, destinavi. Scripsi nunc et alteras eidem, sive isthic sive alibi sit, tradendas. Quod si per alios expeditum non fuerit, a te fieri cuperem, ubicunque eum morari nosti. Vale cum tota domo, salutata a me tua coniuge peramanter.

Per hunc vero Tabellarium ........tius, cum non raro Witebergam [lite]ras mittendi detur occasio, rescribe. Iterumque vale et Praesidi arcis vestrae de me salutem nuntia unâ cum aliis amicis, si quos forte apud vos habeo residuos. Precor vobis omnibus Annum hunc instantem auspicatum et salubrem.

Iamque tertium et pluries vale.
Dabantur Witebergae die penultima Anni 1598.
TYCHO BRAHE

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned man, Master Anders Vedel, Canon of Ribe, his friend ........ and beloved.
Heartiest greeting.

Since this courier offered himself to me of his own accord — one who has resolved to go into Cimbria [Jutland] and pass through Ribe — I thought I should not omit, most loving Lord Master Anders, to give you at least some letter. And I would have done this very thing before I departed from Wandsbek, had I not been hindered by the mass of business and the scarcity of a messenger. But of the state of my affairs, take it thus. When, immediately after Michaelmas, I had set out thence, intending [to go] toward Prague — being several times summoned thither by the Emperor through Lord Coraduz, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, with Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius also mediating — it happened that, when I had come to Dresden, I learned for certain that everything at Prague was thrown into confusion on account of the epidemic plague growing worse there and elsewhere in Bohemia; that the Emperor had departed thence and withdrawn to a safer place; and that the councillors were scattered here and there, and the whole court dispersed, and therefore the public councils and courts on holiday, and there being a cessation of business. Wherefore, staying there a while, I sent my own courier with a letter indicating that I had come to Dresden, and inquiring what should be done in such a state of affairs. A copy of which, transmitted by Coraduz to the Emperor where he then was, was answered afterward by Lord Barvitius, the chief Secretary and [one] of the Emperor's intimate councils: that the Imperial Majesty had learned of my coming most gladly, and desired that I stay at Dresden, or elsewhere in the neighbourhood, until the court reassembles, meanwhile kindly considering a place where I might conveniently be settled. Which answer received (in which I gladly acquiesced, since it would otherwise be dangerous to enter Bohemia), I withdrew hither to Wittenberg with my [household]. Where, God willing, I have resolved to winter — or at least until I am summoned by the Emperor, whom I know favours me and my affairs clemently and seriously, and that with greater zeal and longing than [……], I hope also constant[ly ……]. But meanwhile, while I dwell here, it is my mind to complete some things in my works not yet finished by typographic labour, together with those I have specially destined for the Emperor. And I dwell here at Wittenberg in the house which was once [the house] of Philip Melanchthon, of best memory — now [of] Peucer — its tenant receiving me, Lord Doctor Johann Jessenius, Doctor of Philosophy and Medicine. Who, as he is a sincere and humane man, and most loving of me, kindly granted more than half the house for me and my household to dwell in, so that I enjoy here a moderate convenience, until my affairs, by divine providence, obtain a more abundant accommodation. But I am now having my womenfolk brought from Wandsbek in my own carriages, through my son Tycho, for [whom ……], until a larger [dwelling], God willing, be granted us all by the Emperor — in whose decision my whole world is enclosed. These things I wished to signify briefly, on account of the old bond between us, lest you be ignorant of my affairs, or suspect me forgetful of you. Yet perhaps you have learned similar, or more, from Master Christen Sørensen Longomontanus, formerly my servant, who also accompanied me to Magdeburg, and brought my womenfolk back to Wandsbek. For it has been reported to me that he was lately at Ribe, and that my letters were brought thither to him, which I sent him as soon as I came hither; I have now written another to the same, to be delivered whether he be there or elsewhere. And if it be not dispatched through others, I should wish it done by you, wherever you know him to be staying. Farewell, with your whole house, your wife being most lovingly greeted by me.

But by this courier ……, since opportunity of sending letters to Wittenberg is given not rarely, write back. And again farewell; and announce a greeting from me to the Governor of your castle, together with the other friends, if I happen to have any remaining among you. I pray for you all that this coming Year be auspicious and salubrious.

And now a third time, and many times, farewell.
Given at Wittenberg, the penultimate day of the year 1598.
TYCHO BRAHE

Of the earthquake which shook these shores of Germany twice, on the 16th of December between 6 and 7 in the morning, both here and at Magdeburg and Leipzig and in various other places, I suppose you have heard. If you have experienced anything of the kind among yourselves, or elsewhere in Denmark, tell me. For I judge that tremor to have come from the North or the West. Which, however, by divine clemency, brought no damage in this region.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Anders Sørensen Vedel (Andreas Severini Vellejus, 1542–1616), the Danish historian and Canon of Ribe — Tycho's old friend (the addressee also of brahe-t_15710518001, 1571). The MS is a Langebek excerpt-copy, which accounts for the lacunae.
  • Significance — the 1598 Wittenberg winter (a key biographical letter). Tycho narrates the interrupted journey to Prague:
  • Summoned to the imperial court "immediately after Michaelmas [29 Sept 1598]" through Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor) with Tadeáš Hájek (Hagecius) mediating, he got as far as Dresden, where he learned the plague had thrown Prague into confusion — the Emperor withdrawn to safety, the court dispersed, public business suspended (iustitium).
  • From Dresden he wrote ahead; Barvitius (the chief imperial secretary) answered that Rudolf II welcomed his coming but wished him to wait "at Dresden or nearby until the court reassembles," meanwhile pondering where to settle him.
  • So Tycho wintered at Wittenberg — "in the house once Philip Melanchthon's, now Peucer's," whose tenant, Johann Jessenius, gave him more than half the house for his household. (A celebrated detail: Tycho lodged in Melanchthon's former Wittenberg house — now Caspar Peucer's, Melanchthon's son-in-law — as Jessenius's guest; cf. brahe-t_15981231001, the same-period Longomontanus letter.)
  • He is completing unfinished printed works and those "specially destined for the Emperor," and having his womenfolk (gynaeceum) brought from Wandsbek by his son Tycho, awaiting "a larger dwelling … in whose [the Emperor's] decision my whole world is enclosed."
  • Longomontanus at Ribe. Tycho notes that Longomontanus — "formerly my servant, who accompanied me to Magdeburg and brought my womenfolk back to Wandsbek" — was lately at Ribe, and asks Vedel to forward an enclosed letter to him (cf. brahe-t_15981231001, brahe-t_15990111001).
  • Persons named. Anders Sørensen Vedel; Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor); Tadeáš Hájek (Hagecius); Barvitius (chief secretary); Philip Melanchthon (the late owner of the house); Caspar Peucer (its present owner); Johann Jessenius (Tycho's host); Christen Sørensen Longomontanus; Tycho's son (Tycho the Younger); Rudolf II; the Governor of [Ribe] castle and Vedel's wife (greeted).
  • Textual flags (the witness is a Langebek excerpt). Several lacunae/corruptions are preserved with ellipses and bracketed editorial supplements: the address-line gap (amico suo ……); "si … non fusilem praepedita" (read as "had I not been hindered"); "maiore studio … quam …nia … spero etiam constant …"; "pro qua ………"; "totus mi… clauditur orbis"; "Per hunc … ……tius … rescribe." None obscures the overall sense; left marked, not conjectured.
  • Calendar. Dateline die penultima Anni 1598 = 30 December 1598; Wittenberg/Saxony kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar. The edition label ("1598. 30. december. Wittenberg") agrees.
  • What this is. An added paragraph of news preceding the letter's closing courier-and-greetings section (which is what the published tail actually preserves) — Tycho reporting a recent German earthquake to Anders Sørensen Vedel and asking whether it was felt in Denmark too. Written from Wittenberg, where Tycho was then encamped in transit from Wandsbek toward Prague.
  • Crux — "inaudivisse." The verb is inaudio, a later/Neo-Latin near-synonym of audio ("to hear, hear tell of"), not a privative ("not to hear"); so "te inaudivisse opinor" reads "I suppose you will have heard [of it]" rather than its surface-tempting negative. Rendered accordingly, but flagged since the in- prefix invites misreading.
  • Source note. The source text has a doubled conjunction, "tam hîc quam Magdeburgi et et Lipsiæ" — reproduced here verbatim and untouched per the house rule; almost certainly a transcription duplication rather than an authorial repetition, but not corrected.
  • Context. The letter is one of several from this period showing Tycho relaying naturalia (comets, earthquakes) to his correspondents as a matter of course alongside astronomical and personal business; no other source consulted specifically corroborates a 16 December 1598 earthquake at Wittenberg/Magdeburg/Leipzig, so the event itself is reported here only on Tycho's own authority.
  • Correction. Latin corrected from 'non fusilem praepedita' to 'non fuissem praepeditus' per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.131. (A second flagged instance, the doubled 'et et' at the pp.132–133 page-break, was checked against Dreyer and found to be genuinely doubled in the edition of record; left unchanged.)

Found two text error where the transcribed original departs from Dreyer's printed Latin — a corrupted verb phrase ('fusilem praepedita' for 'fuissem praepeditus') and a duplicated conjunction ('et et') — both low-severity and, in the first case, not propagated into the English, which correctly renders Dreyer's actual sense. No fabrication, omission, mistranslation, or hedge-loss found. Per the grading rule any text error caps the grade at C; verdict is minor since neither defect changes what a scholar would take away.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). Source_text's 'non fusilem praepedita' is a corrupted transcription of Dreyer's grammatical 'non fuissem praepeditus' (pluperfect subjunctive passive, 'had I not been hindered'). The published English matches the correct Dreyer sense, so the corruption did not propagate into the translation, but the Latin as published departs from the edition of record. Dreyer prints: fi negotiorum mole et internuncij penuriâ non fuiffem praepeditus (p.131).
- text error (low). Source_text doubles the conjunction 'et et' where Dreyer has a single 'et' before the page break. A trivial duplication, silently and correctly resolved in the English translation. Dreyer prints: tam hîc quam Magdeburgi et [page break] Lipfiæ atque in alijs hinc inde locis (pp.132-133).

Repaired. Collated both defects against the Dreyer page images (TBOO VIII pp.131-133). The first is a confirmed corruption and was fixed; the published English already carried the correct sense and needed no change. The second defect was rejected on direct inspection: Dreyer's own text carries 'et' at the very end of p.132 and again 'et Lipfiæ' at the start of p.133, so the doubled conjunction in the current file is not an error relative to the edition of record. No crux remains.
