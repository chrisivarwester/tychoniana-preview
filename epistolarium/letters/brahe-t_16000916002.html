The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD LUCAM BACMEISTERUM.

S.

CLARISSIME et Præstantissime Vir, Amice inprimis honorande, etsi per M. CHRISTIERNUM nostrum hinc discedentem et in Daniam cogitantem, siquidem Rostochium transire et ibi forte aliquantisper subsistere constituerat, literas ad te dederim, partim de rebus meis, partim de ijs, quæ historiolæ annuæ optimæ memoriæ Domini Doctoris DAVIDIS CHYTRÆI, prout ipsemet, etiam dum in vivis erat, proposuerat, inseri averem, commonefacientes; tamen oblata per hunc tabellarium ad Vos euntem et mox huc revertentem tam commoda oportunitate, non intermittendum duxi, quin denuo aliqua tibi scriberem, nec tamen priora multis repetam, id saltem ijs superaddam, me etiamnum desiderare, ut ea, de quibus prius rogâram, historiolæ annuæ præfati Domini D. CHYTRÆI usque ad huius Anni exitum ab aliquo Vestrorum, uti opinor, professore continuandæ, competenti loco inserantur. Video enim in ijs chartis, quas mihi misit Poëta vester Dn. BRASCHIUS, quæ in solstitio æstivo ob Autoris mox subsequentem obitum, narrationem interrumpunt, id omissum esse. Quare pluribus hac de re ad dictum Dn. BRASCHIUM scripsi, qui tibi meas facile ostendet literas, unde plura et particulariora hauries; neque tamen hîc quippiam urgeo, sed omnia cum vestro stabunt cadentque libero iudicio.

Quantum vero ad res meas attinet, sunt eæ adhuc in eodem statu, quo erant, cum ultimas ad te mitterem. Maneo etiamnum hîc Pragæ cum tota familia ex Cæsaris voluntate hîc retentus, donec de loco habitationis commodiore et propinquiore, ubi exercitijs meis Astronomicis vacare possim, mihi clementissime prospexerit, qua de re etiamnum agitur, nam lente in hac Aula ob negotiorum molem omnia procedunt. Supellex mea unâ cum Instrumentis Astronomicis, quæ Magdeburgi aliquamdiu relicta erant, in ea Albis parte, quæ Dresdam et hunc locum intercedit, iam, uti ante biduum inaudivi, navigio appropinquant, ita ut sperem ea instante septimana hîc in Bohemia adfutura. Quare de loco, ubi disponenda sint (siquidem arx Benatica nimis hinc remota sit, nec admodum oportuna) iam eo maturius deliberamus, et negotium hoc acceleretur.

Non dubito, quin literas adfini tuo Magnifico Dn. BORDINGO inscriptas, quas prædictus M. CHRISTIERNUS secum attulit, expedivisse, et contenta earundem, quantum in te erat, promovisse. Si nihil adhuc in re illa pecuniaria, de qua aiebam, effectum est, nec aliqua isthic suppetit commoditas id tuto exequendi, poterit usque in adventum Dn. D. IOHANNIS GÖDELMANNI (qui se quatuordecim abhinc diebus Rostochium profecturum heri mihi indicavit) deferri, ut per ipsum quam optime fiat. Qua de re etiam ad prænominatum adfinem tuum latius perscripsi, quas literas, si tabellarius hic Gustrowium transiens id non præstiterit, actutum dirigas quæso, atque ut idem tabellarius ratum secum adferat responsum, operam da, quin et tu ipsemet cum eodem mihi rescribas, simulque indices, quid rerum in vicina Dania et Suecia geratur. Narratum est hîc ante paucos dies, incerto tamen rumore, dissidium quoddam subortum inter Regem Daniæ Regnique Nobilitatem, quod tamen irrita fama prolatum spero. Deus avertat a Patria mea intestina mala, quæ plurimum secum damni trahere solent. Turca in Ungaria est cum maximo exercitu, cui nostri Imperatoris vix pro quarta parte par est: pugnet pro nobis armipotens Deus, cui etiam te tuosque omnes commendo et bene valere precor.

Dabantur properanter Pragæ Bohemorum 16 die Septembris stylo novo Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO LUCAS BACKMEISTER.

Greeting.

Most renowned and most excellent Man, friend especially to be honoured: although, through our Master Christen [Longomontanus] — departing hence and intending [to go] into Denmark, since he had resolved to pass through Rostock and perhaps stay there a while — I gave you a letter, partly about my affairs, partly reminding [you] of those things I desired inserted into the annual little history of Lord Doctor David Chytraeus, of best memory (as he himself, while still alive, had proposed): nevertheless, so convenient an opportunity being offered by this courier going to you and soon returning hither, I thought I should not omit to write you something again. Yet I will not repeat the earlier things at length; this only I add to them: that I still desire that the matters about which I asked before be inserted in a fitting place in the aforesaid Lord Chytraeus's annual little history — to be continued, as I think, by some professor of yours, up to the end of this year. For I see, in those sheets your Poet Lord Brasch sent me — which break off the narration at the summer solstice, on account of the Author's soon-following death — that this is omitted. Wherefore I have written more on this matter to the said Lord Brasch, who will easily show you my letter, whence you will draw more and more particular things; yet I press nothing here, but everything will stand and fall by your free judgement.

As far as concerns my affairs, they are still in the same state as when I sent you my last. I still remain here at Prague, with my whole household, retained here by the Emperor's will, until he most clemently provides me a more convenient and nearer dwelling-place, where I may be free for my astronomical exercises — about which the matter is still in hand, for in this Court all things proceed slowly, on account of the mass of business. My furniture, together with the astronomical instruments which had been left a while at Magdeburg, are now (as I heard two days ago) approaching by ship in that part of the Elbe which lies between Dresden and this place, so that I hope they will be here in Bohemia in the coming week. Wherefore, concerning the place where they are to be set up (since the castle of Benátky is too remote from here, and not very convenient) we now deliberate the more maturely, and this business is being hastened.

I do not doubt that you have dispatched the letter addressed to your kinsman the Magnificent Lord Bording, which the aforesaid Master Christen brought with him, and have furthered its contents as far as was in you. If nothing has yet been effected in that money-matter of which I spoke, nor is any convenient means at hand there of carrying it out safely, it can be deferred until the arrival of Lord Doctor Johann Gödelmann (who indicated to me yesterday that he would set out for Rostock fourteen days hence), that it may best be done through him. About which I have also written more fully to your aforenamed kinsman; which letter, if this courier passing through Güstrow does not deliver, please direct at once, and take pains that the same courier bring back a definite reply — nay, that you yourself write back to me with the same, and at the same time indicate what is going on in neighbouring Denmark and Sweden. It was reported here a few days ago — by uncertain rumour, however — that some dissension had arisen between the King of Denmark and the Nobility of the Realm, which I hope was put about by an empty report. May God avert from my fatherland internal evils, which are wont to bring much damage with them. The Turk is in Hungary with a very great army, to which our Emperor's is scarcely equal for a fourth part: may God, mighty in arms, fight for us — to whom I also commend you and all yours, and pray that you fare well.

Given in haste at Prague of the Bohemians, the 16th day of September, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Lucas Backmeister (Bacmeister, 1530–1608), Superintendent of Rostock and theologian — the kinsman of Jacob Bording who handled the Mecklenburg interest-money (cf. brahe-t_16001115001, brahe-t_16000919001), here also Tycho's contact on the Chytraeus history.
  • The Chytraeus annual-history thread (cross-link to Brasch). Tycho repeats his wish that his migration narrative be inserted into David Chytraeus's annual history (the Saxonia / Chronicon), now "to be continued by some professor of yours up to the end of this year." He notes that the sheets Brasch sent break off at the summer solstice — Chytraeus having died then — so the desired matter is omitted; he refers Backmeister to his fuller letter to Brasch (the reply to which is brahe-t_16000920002, where Brasch reports the search failed and the Chronicle cannot easily be extended). "Yet I press nothing … everything will stand and fall by your free judgement."
  • Tycho's affairs (autumn 1600). Still retained at Prague with his household by the Emperor's will, awaiting "a more convenient and nearer dwelling." His furniture and the Magdeburg-stored instruments are now coming up the Elbe between Dresden and Bohemia (expected "in the coming week") — the success of the Magdeburg leg (cf. brahe-t_16000404001) — and he is deliberating where to set them up, "since Benátky is too remote and not very convenient" (anticipating the move into the Prague Curtius house).
  • The debt-matter & news. The 600-thaler interest business via Bording: if not yet done, to await Gödelmann's departure for Rostock (in 14 days). Tycho asks for news of Denmark and Sweden, mentioning an "uncertain rumour" of dissension between Christian IV and the Danish nobility (which he hopes false), and reports the Turk in Hungary "with a very great army, to which our Emperor's is scarcely equal for a fourth part."
  • Persons named. Lucas Backmeister; M. Christiernus = Christen Longomontanus (departed to Denmark via Rostock; cf. brahe-t_16000920002); David Chytraeus (the late historian); Martin Brasch; Jacob Bording; Johann Georg Gödelmann; Rudolf II; Christian IV and the Danish nobility.
  • Calendar. Dateline Pragae Bohemorum 16 die Septembris stylo novo Anno 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
