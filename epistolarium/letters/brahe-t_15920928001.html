The original

CLARISSIMO, VARIOQUE DOCTRINARUM GENERE ERUDITISSIMO EXCELLENTISSIMOQUE VIRO, DOMINO THADDÆO HAGGECIO AB HAYCK, PHILOSOPHIAe ET MEDICINAe DOCTORI EXIMIO, AMICO SUO IN PRIMIS COLENDO.

QUAMVIS mi amantissime Domine D. THADDÆE, nihil a te literarum tota hac elapsa aestate acceperim, te tamen incolumi, Deo vitam tibi feliciter prorogante, adhuc esse valetudine spero et ex animo exopto. Ego diutinam hanc literarum intermissionem nequaquam inde accidisse reor, quod vel tu praeter solitum tam diu a scribendo plane cessaveris aut ad meas, quas interea ad te destinâram, nihil omnino responderis; sed (uti alias contigit) ob Tabellariorum perfidiam et incuriam, si quas ad me (prout nihil ambigo) dederis, intercidisse, aut in tanta, quibus disiungimur, locorum intercapedine alicubi interceptas fuisse suspicor; praesertim cum in proximis meis, quas te accepisse non dubito, tuam de quibusdam illic contentis responsionem quam primum ad me perferendam, unâque iudicium de libro nostro ad te ablegato, mihi per opportunitatem communicandum, serio desiderârim. Quorum tamen neutrum tanto temporis intervallo praestitum fuisse antedictis causis ascribendum censeo. Rogo itaque peramanter ut idonea oblata commoditate, tum de praecipuis superiorum ad te literarum momentis, me certiorem reddere, tum etiam de primo nostrorum Progymnasmatum Tomo tibi transmisso censuram tuam interponere mihique impertire velis. Quem iam dudum integrum ad te misissem, nisi varijs exacto hoc semestri negotijs praepeditus fuissem, quo minus hunc typographico labore ad colophonem, uti constitueram, perducere potuerim. Quam primum autem eundem absolvere licuerit (id quod instante nunc hyeme, Deo conatus nostros feliciter adiuvante, futurum confido) mox omnibus numeris absolutum istuc transportari curabo.

Sed quas iam, praestantissime vir, bullatas naenias ad te adduco? En CRAIGI istius scriptum admodum insolens ac scopticum, quo se omnia, quae in libro nostro secundo de Aethereis sunt Phaenomenis, atque Apologia illi hoc nomine destinata, refutare atque infirmare perfricta fronte iactârit; cum tamen ne minimum quidem eorum apud eos, qui non caeco praeiudicio, sed solidis rationibus haec metiuntur, vacillare faciat. Usqueadeo vir ille Aristotelis de Cometarum generatione fumositatibus, suisque praeiudicatis opinionibus occaecatus est, ut non modo nostra penitus improbare, invertere, distrahere, male accommodare, et scommatis, oblocutionibus, dicterijs, fallacibus cothurnis et sophisticis elenchis turgido ac temerario quodam ausu passim excipere non vereatur: sed etiam ipsi veritati certissimis Observationibus et invictis Demonstrationibus assertae vim et fucum facere, suisque ut obtemperet pomposis sophismatis, invitam eam cogere non erubescat. Hem egregiam ab hoc CRAIGO pro tot non modo assiduis in hac palaestra Astronomica exantlatis laboribus, sed quod tam studiose ipsum Regi Scotorum, cum hîc mecum praesto esset, et Cancellario (ut ex ipsius ad me data Epistola, cuius descriptum unâ ad te mitto, plenius cognosces) tum coram tum per literas iterum atque iterum commendârim, gratiam nunc reporto. At ingratus, qui bene merita non reddit, ingratior, qui dissimulat aut negat; sed omnium ingratissimus, qui beneficium maleficio recompensat. Verum de hoc novo Prometheo et vanissimo Capnuranicae restinctionis compilatore satis.

A te autem, mi exoptatissime THADDÆE, in primis desidero, ut scriptum hoc magnifico Domino CURTIO, siquando per alias graviores quibus vacat occupationes tantum temporis concedatur, legendum exhibeas, ut inde liquido perspiciat Peripateticum hunc Athletam non propterea tam fastuoso et pertinaci subinde certamine manus mecum conserere, quasi hoc pacto veritatem plenius inveniri, stabiliri aut illustrari omnibus nervis contenderet; sed ut inventam potius calumnijs et cavillis labefactare, et suarum opinionum ac errorum laqueis irretitam in pristinas ignorantiae tenebras reducere queat. Peto insuper ut Capnuranicas hasce subtilitates vel futilitates verius cum ijs, quae in Tomo nostro secundo et Apologia de hoc negotio Cometico pertractata sint, conferre non degravere. Et quae in hasce Scoticas affanias et captiosas argutias animadvertenda censueris, ea in charta denotare et ad me quam primum transmittere velis oro. Interea autem antilogiam istam sarctam tectam apud vos conservari, nec ad alios propalari cupio, mihique, ut ita fiat, omnino persuadeo. Ego, si tantum mihi per alias, quibus operam impendo, lucubrationes otij suppetat, has arrogantes et insulsas strophas ac calumnias facile retundam, vel alijs forte potius affatim discutiendas et diluendas relinquam. Tu, si quid ad me (id quod magnopere a te expeto) antequam navigatio ob frigoris inclementiam interclusa fuerit, rescripseris, id commode Rostochium ad D. D. BRUCÆUM vel Lubecam ad D. D. IOHANNEM SAGERUM communem nostrum amicum dirigere poteris. Inde postea ad me per occasionem tuto et certo facile perferetur.

Vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 28. Septembris Anno 1592.
Quaeso ut salutes meo nomine magnificum Dominum CURTIUM quam officiosissime.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and, in every various kind of learning, most erudite and most excellent man, Master Thaddaeus Hagecius of Hájek, distinguished Doctor of Philosophy and of Medicine, his friend above all to be honoured.

Although, my most loving lord Master Thaddaeus, I have received no letter from you throughout this whole summer now past, I nevertheless hope, and from my heart wish, that you are still safe and in good health, God prospering your life happily. I in no way reckon that this long interruption in our correspondence has come about because either you, contrary to your custom, have for so long altogether ceased from writing, or have given no reply at all to the letters of mine that I had meanwhile sent to you; rather (as has happened on other occasions) I suspect that, through the faithlessness and carelessness of the couriers, any [letters] you may have given to be carried to me (as I do not at all doubt you did) have been lost, or have been intercepted somewhere in so great a distance of places as separates us — especially since in my most recent [letter], which I do not doubt you received, I earnestly desired that your reply concerning certain matters contained therein be conveyed to me as soon as possible, and together with it your judgement of our book dispatched to you, to be communicated to me as opportunity allowed. Yet neither of these, I judge, having been rendered in so great an interval of time, is to be ascribed to the causes aforesaid. I therefore most lovingly ask that, when a suitable occasion offers, you would both make me more certain about the chief points of the earlier letter to you, and also lend and impart to me your assessment of the first volume of our Progymnasmata transmitted to you. This [volume] I would long since have sent to you complete, had I not been hindered by various pieces of business in the course of this past half-year, so that I could not, as I had resolved, bring it through the labour of the press to its colophon. But as soon as I am permitted to complete it (which, with winter now pressing on, and God prospering our efforts happily, I am confident will be) I shall at once see to it that it be conveyed thither, finished in every particular.

But what bullae-sealed dirges [bullatas naenias] do I now bring to you, most excellent man? Behold the writing of that fellow Craig, exceedingly insolent and scoffing, in which with brazen brow he has boasted that he refutes and overturns everything that is in our second book on the Ethereal Phenomena and in the Apology directed to him under this same head; whereas in fact he makes not the least of these things waver in the eyes of those who measure these matters not by blind prejudice but by solid reasonings. So far has that man been blinded by Aristotle's smoke-fumes about the generation of comets, and by his own preconceived opinions, that he does not fear not only to disapprove utterly, to invert, to distort, to misapply our [arguments], and everywhere to carp at them with gibes, contradictions, taunts, fallacious buskins [cothurni], and sophistical refutations [elenchi], with a certain swollen and reckless audacity; but he does not even blush to do violence and false colouring to the truth itself — established by the most certain observations and unconquerable demonstrations — and to drive it, unwilling, into compliance with his own pompous sophisms. Ha — a fine return I now reap from this Craig, in exchange for so many labours not only unceasingly drained dry in this astronomical wrestling-school, but also for having so zealously commended him, again and again, both in person and by letter, to the King of the Scots himself (when he was here present with me) and to the Chancellor — as you will learn more fully from his own letter sent to me, a copy of which I send to you along [with this]. But ungrateful is he who does not repay good deserts; more ungrateful, he who dissembles or denies them; but most ungrateful of all, he who repays a kindness with an injury. But enough about this new Prometheus and most empty compiler of a Capnuranic extinction.

From you, however, my most longed-for Thaddaeus, I desire above all that you exhibit this writing to be read by the magnificent Master Curtius — whenever, amid the other weightier occupations he is engaged in, so much time may be granted — so that from it he may clearly perceive that this Peripatetic Athlete does not for this reason engage hand to hand with me in so haughty and obstinate a contest, time and again, as though by this means he were striving with all his sinews that truth be more fully found out, established, or illustrated; but rather that he may undermine truth once found, by calumnies and cavils, and, having ensnared it in the toils of his own opinions and errors, lead it back into the former darkness of ignorance. I ask, moreover, that you not think it too burdensome to compare these Capnuranic subtleties — or futilities, more truly — with the matters treated in our second volume and Apology concerning this cometary business. And whatever you may judge worth remarking upon in these Scottish ravings and captious quibbles, I beg that you would set down on paper and transmit to me as soon as possible. Meanwhile, however, I desire that that counter-argument [antilogia] of his be kept whole and under cover among you, and not divulged to others; and I altogether persuade myself that it will be so done. I, if only I have leisure enough amid the other lucubrations on which I expend my effort, shall easily beat back these arrogant and insipid tricks and calumnies — or rather, perhaps, shall leave them to others to be amply dispelled and dissolved. You, if you write back anything to me (which I greatly request of you) before the sailing-passage be closed off through the inclemency of the cold, will be able conveniently to direct it to Rostock to Master Doctor Brucaeus, or to Lübeck to Master Doctor Johannes Sagerus, our common friend. From there it will afterwards easily be brought to me, safely and surely, as occasion offers.

Farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 28 September, the year 1592.
I pray that you would greet the magnificent Master Curtius in my name as dutifully as possible.
Yours, Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, at Uraniborg on Hven, to Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer, and imperial court physician at Prague; Tycho's chief scientific correspondent in the Empire and the man who had supplied him manuscript material used in the Progymnasmata. The standing salutation register ("most loving lord," "most longed-for Thaddaeus") matches their long friendship.
  • Progymnasmatum … primo Tomo — the first volume of Tycho's Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata (the "Preliminary Exercises," on the nova of 1572). The work was printed slowly at Uraniborg and not published until 1602/1603; here in 1592 the volume is still incomplete — Tycho says he could not "bring it through the labour of the press to its colophon" (typographico labore ad colophonem … perducere). colophon kept as the printer's-term it is.
  • libro nostro secundo de Aethereis … Phaenomenis — the second book of Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588), on the comet of 1577. Apologia illi hoc nomine destinata — the apologia (defence) Tycho addressed to Craig "under this same head," i.e. answering Craig's objections to that book.
  • The Craig polemic. Craigus / Craigi istius = John Craig (d. 1620), Scottish physician (later physician to James VI/I), an Aristotelian who disputed Tycho's claim that the comet of 1577 lay in the supralunary (ethereal) region rather than being a sublunary exhalation. The two had exchanged sharp letters (Craig's of 9 July 1589 survives in this same correspondence). Tycho calls him a "Peripatetic Athlete" (Peripateticum Athletam — a follower of Aristotle's Peripatetic school, cast as a brawler in the "astronomical wrestling-school," palaestra Astronomica) and "this new Prometheus."
  • bullatas naenias — literally "bubble-/bulla-dirges": rendered "bullae-sealed dirges." naeniae are funeral-dirges, hence worthless droning; bullatae puns on bulla (a sealed document / an empty bubble), so the phrase doubly belittles Craig's tract as a pompously sealed yet hollow lament. The double sense is flagged rather than collapsed to one reading.
  • Capnuranicae restinctionis … Capnuranicas … subtilitates — Capnuranica is from Greek καπνός (kapnós, "smoke") + οὐρανός (ouranós, "heaven/sky"): "smoke-in-the-heaven," Tycho's mocking coinage (or echo of Craig's own title) for Craig's Aristotelian doctrine that the comet was a smoky exhalation. restinctio = "extinction / quenching" (of the cometary fire/fumes). Kept transliterated and glossed; Capnuranic italicised as a retained technical coinage. Crux flagged (see Confidence).
  • Aristotelis … Cometarum generatione fumositatibus — "Aristotle's smoke-fumes about the generation of comets": the Aristotelian theory (Meteorologica) that comets are dry terrestrial exhalations ignited in the upper air — the very doctrine Tycho's parallax measurements overturned.
  • fallacibus cothurnis et sophisticis elenchis — "fallacious buskins and sophistical refutations." cothurnus (the tragic actor's high boot) = bombast/inflated style; elenchi sophistici alludes to Aristotle's Sophistici Elenchi (fallacious refutations) — Tycho turns the Aristotelian's own logical apparatus against him. Both kept as the rhetorical/technical terms they are.
  • Domino CURTIO / magnificum Dominum CURTIUM — Jacob Kurtz von Senftenau (Jacobus Curtius, d. 1594), imperial Vice-Chancellor at the court of Rudolf II in Prague, a learned patron of astronomy; Tycho wants Curtius to read Craig's tract and see its bad faith. The closing postscript renews the greeting to him.
  • Regi Scotorum … et Cancellario — the King of the Scots = James VI (later James I of England), whom Tycho had hosted on Hven in 1590; and his Chancellor (John Maitland of Thirlestane). Tycho stresses he had repeatedly recommended Craig to both.
  • D. D. BRUCAeUM — Heinrich Brucaeus (1530–1593), professor of medicine and mathematics at Rostock; a forwarding address. D. D. = Domino Doctori, "Master Doctor." D. D. IOHANNEM SAGERUM — Johannes Sager (Sagerus), a Lübeck contact and "our common friend," a second forwarding node. The two are given as the reliable mail-relay before the sea-passage closes for winter (antequam navigatio ob frigoris inclementiam interclusa fuerit).
  • antilogiam istam sarctam tectam … conservari — "that counter-argument of his be kept whole and under cover": antilogia = a counter-/contrary-argument; sarcta tecta is the legal-administrative formula "in good repair, intact," here "whole and under cover." Tycho asks that Craig's tract be held privately, not circulated.
  • Scoticas affanias — "Scottish ravings": affaniae = idle nonsense, twaddle (Craig being Scottish).
  • novo Prometheo — "this new Prometheus": an ironic flourish — Craig as a would-be fire-bringer who in fact only deals in smoke (tying back to the Capnuranica / "extinction" jibe).
  • Editorial note on the source. The verbatim block reproduces the printed forms as given, including responionem (for responsionem), the æ-ligature spellings rendered Ae (THADDAeO, AHaycк→ab Hayck, HAGGECIO, CRAIGO etc.), and j for consonantal i (varijs, negotijs, sophismatis); these are not normalised in the Source block.
  • Calendar. Dateline Datas Uraniburgi 28. Septembris Anno 1592 — 28 September 1592, Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). No in-letter date discrepancy; the catalogue label agrees with the dateline (place "Uraniborg"). No conversion made.
  • Correction. Latin corrected against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Opera Omnia VII, pages 342 and 343: "responionem" corrected to "responsionem"; the closing "Datas" corrected to "Datae". The English translation already read correctly at both sites and required no change.
