The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD GEORGIUM ROLLENHAGIUM.

S. P.

QUAS die 23 Februarij huius Anni stylo vestro ad me dedisti literas, Eruditissime et amantissime Domine ROLLENHAGI, eæ mihi redditæ sunt, cum nuper Pragæ essem, unâ cum alijs quibusdam in fine Augusti superioris Anni pariter a te mihi scriptis; aderant quoque aliæ ad Doctorem RHODIUM, quas statim in ipsius hospitium misi, sed ipse peregre aberat; polliciti nihilominus sunt inquilini eas recte procuraturos. Ad priores mihi destinatas nihil nunc respondeo, siquidem earum præcipua contenta repetuntur in posterioribus, ad quas sic habe.

Civem illum Hamburgensem, quantum potui, per meos homines, qui tunc in Aulam abituri erant, iuvi, tam tuâ, quam Domini Doctoris VITI de caussa, sed lente isthic omnia, uti scis, et difficulter procedunt ob negotiorum multitudinem.

Pro supellectile meâ huc advehenda, non opus est, ut amplius sollicites apud Senatum vestrum. Cum enim Cæsa Matas, Dominus noster Clementissimus, a Domino BARVITIO rescivisset (me tamen id non cupiente, sed aliquantulum adhuc temporis Magistratui vestro rem rectius dispiciendi, uti me facturum ad te scripsi, relinquente) Magdæburgenses, quos bis per literas hac de re clementissime compellavit, officium illud detrectasse, invite id ipsum tulit, et statim ad Reverendum Capitulum vestrum de hoc eodem negotio perscribi iussit, quas literas nunc eo per quendam ex meis studiosis natione Danum mitto. Exemplum earum hîc adiunctum habes, nec dubito, quin Reverendum Capitulum, cui ego etiam scripsi, in gratiam Cæsæ Matis effecturum, ut Instrumenta mea et omnes res isthic relictæ huc per Albim suis sumptibus, quamprimum fieri poterit, adferantur. Confido etiam illud de asseribus prospecturum, quibus ea omnia ab iniuria aëris et pluviarum muniantur, quos Nautæ secum recipere poterint. Et si navem vinis Bohemicis, quæ admodum generosa superiori anno creverunt, onerari voluerit, aut alijs mercibus, quæ hîc minoris quam isthic venduntur, poterunt asseres illi ad hoc denuo usui esse; conduceret vero, ut fidus aliquis minister unâ mitteretur, qui ea, quæ hîc coëmenda essent, procuraret, et in accessu curam mearum rerum simul gereret, pro qua inveniar erga eundem gratus. Quod si factum fuerit, non opus erit, ut studiosus hic meus huc cum supellectile illa, quam Magdæburgi habeo, revertatur, sed is potius rectâ Hamburgum eat, ut pro reliquis Instrumentis inde huc pariter advehendis operam Hamburgensium sollicitet; sed tam hæc quam alia tuo consilio disponenda relinquo. Et si conduxeris pro certo precio hominem, qui cum rebus meis huc accedat, atque earum inspector sit, modo Capitulum aliquem adiungere nolit, solvam ei quidquid promiseris. In cæteris Emissarium meum ita instrue, ut quid sibi faciendum vel omittendum sit, sciat, daque operam, ut omnia mature et diligenter executioni mandentur. Habebo te in omnibus indemnem, si quid pro me exposueris, vel fidem interposueris.

Polliceris in ultimis, te reliquis scriptorum meorum postulatis per primum quemque Tabellarium responsurum, quod ut brevi facias, rogo. ROTHMANNO quod ulterius scribam, non habeo, siquidem is meis benevole et humaniter ad ipsum datis quippiam respondere gravatus est, et videtur is ita morosus atque impatiens esse, ut vix seipsum ferre queat, necdum alios. Quæ de me in suis ad te literis conqueritur, facile replicare possem, sed nolo cum homine, uti recte ais, impatientissimo et sibi ipsi præ animi impotentia vix constante contendere, aut quidpiam commertij habere. Si ipsemet sibi deesse vult, quomodo ego eum tueri aut iuvare potero, quin potius propria agam, neglectis alienis. Si existimat eius Epistolas non candide et ingenue esse a me editas, habet proculdubio exemplaria, monstret, quo hîc loco adulteratæ sint, aut pudeat ipsum de mea integritate tale quid citra occasionem suspicari. De confessione eius mihi iudicium non sumam, neque enim eam ob prolixitatem sufficienter introspicere meæ occupationes hactenus permiserunt, neque ego me Theologicis disputationibus libenter immisceo, ut mirer eum meam censuram in talibus tam sæpe tamque serio exposcere, quæ ad ea studia, quæ prius inter nos ventilata sunt, non pertinent. Duriusculum mihi videtur, quod ab initio asserit et definit, Deum nos per sacramenta salvos facere. Id sane indiget bona glossa et interpretatione sobria, si idolatriæ suspicio abesse debet, ad quam plurimi cæteroquin homines iam dudum hac præsertim in re (proch dolor) admodum proclives sunt. Quin et multa sunt præterea in eodem scripto, tam de Baptismo quam de cæna, quæ non ita invicte demonstravit, atque existimat. Sed viderit ipse et alij, si quos habet, suæ sententiæ astipulatores; meum non est tantas componere lites. Deus liberet ROTHMANNUM ab ijs, quibuscum conflictatur, miserijs, et det illi patientiam atque constantiam. Ego si illi adessem, ipsius paupertati, quantum res meæ ferrent, non defuturus essem, quemadmodum etiam antea, quando in Dania mecum fuit, ipsum non inanem demisi, sed aliquot Roseis nobilibus, pro quibus mihi tamen vix gratias agere dignatus est (adeo tunc erat insolens) donavi, cum tamen ipsius olim Princeps me longe ditior meos homines, quos aliquando ad ipsum misi, ne obulo quidem, nec etiam eos interea aluerit, quemadmodum ego per 5 vel 6 hebdomadas ROTHMANNUM, cum in Dania esset. Sed ista nihil ad rhombum, [ne]que ea, quæ parvi [no]n duco momen[ti]. De fratre eius IOHANNE, qui Doctor medicinæ est, cuperem scire, an is adhuc in vivis sit; si sic, optarem, ut me aliquando accederet; erit id illi forte non inutile.

Intellexi nuper, esse quendam in ditione Brunsvicensi nomine IOHANNEM KRABBEN, de Munden, quem præteritorum aliquot annorum Eclipses in utroque Luminari diligenter observasse aiunt; si itaque ea, quæ is circa has explorata habet, mihi procuraveris, rem apprime gratam præstiteris. Sed non suffecerint mihi nuda tempora, verum potius media scire averem, per quæ in eorum cognitionem pervenit, sive sint Altitudines stellarum sive alia huc conducentia. Cupio autem eius observata ea potissimum de caussa habere, ut cum meis in ijsdem Ecclipsibus conferre possem, atque hinc meridianorum differentiam rectius eruere. Est etiam aliquando in diversis locis dispar serenitas. Sed de observationum fide certus esse non possim, nisi compertum habuero, qua ratione, uti dixi, acceptæ sint.

Apud Cancellarium vestrum ea, de quibus antea [ad] te perscripsi, spero te effecturum, quo desiderijs nostris mature satisfiat.

Vale.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsareana Benatica Die 5 Aprilis Anno 1600.

TYCHO BRAHE TO GEORG ROLLENHAGEN.

Heartiest greeting.

The letter you sent me on the 23rd of February of this year, by your style [Old Style], most learned and most loving Lord Rollenhagen, was delivered me when I was lately at Prague, together with certain others likewise written to me by you at the end of August last year; there were also others, to Doctor Rhodius, which I at once sent to his lodging — but he was away abroad; the residents nonetheless promised they would duly see to them. To the earlier ones addressed to me I now answer nothing, since their chief contents are repeated in the later, to which take this.

That Hamburg citizen I helped, as much as I could, through my men who were then about to go to court — both for your sake and for that of Lord Doctor Vitus [Winsheim]; but everything there proceeds slowly, as you know, and with difficulty, on account of the multitude of business.

As for the transport of my goods hither, there is no need to solicit your Senate further. For when the Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, had learned from Lord Barvitius (though I did not wish it, but was leaving your Magistracy a little more time to consider the matter more rightly, as I wrote you I would) that the Magdeburgers — whom He had twice clemently addressed by letter on this — had declined that office, He took it ill, and at once ordered a letter written about this same business to your Reverend Chapter — which letter I now send thither by one of my students, a Dane by nation. A copy of it you have here enclosed; nor do I doubt that the Reverend Chapter (to whom I too have written) will, for the Imperial Majesty's sake, bring it about that my Instruments and all the things left there be carried hither by the Elbe at their own expense, as soon as can be. I trust too they will provide for the boards by which all these may be protected from the injury of weather and rains, which the sailors can take with them. And if they should wish to load the ship with Bohemian wines (which grew very generous last year), or with other goods sold cheaper here than there, those boards can again serve for this. It would be useful that some faithful servant be sent along, to procure what is to be bought here and at the same time take care of my things on the way — for which he will find me grateful. If this be done, there will be no need for this my student to return hither with the goods I have at Magdeburg; but rather let him go straight to Hamburg, to solicit the labour of the Hamburgers for the remaining Instruments to be carried hence hither likewise; but both these and other matters I leave to be arranged by your counsel. And if you hire, for a fixed price, a man to come hither with my things and be their overseer — should the Chapter be unwilling to appoint one — I will pay him whatever you promise. For the rest, instruct my emissary so that he knows what he must do or leave undone, and take care that all be put into execution maturely and diligently. I will hold you indemnified in all things, if you lay out anything for me, or pledge your credit.

You promise, in your last, that you will answer the rest of my writings' requests by the first courier; which I beg you do shortly. To Rothmann I have nothing further to write, since he was loth to answer anything to my letters, sent him benevolently and courteously; and he seems to be so morose and impatient that he can scarcely bear himself, much less others. What he complains of me in his letters to you, I could easily refute; but I am unwilling to contend, or have any commerce, with a man — as you rightly say — most impatient, and from incapacity of mind scarcely consistent with himself. If he wishes to fail himself, how shall I be able to protect or help him? — rather let me mind my own affairs, neglecting others'. If he thinks his Letters were not candidly and honestly published by me [in the Epistolae astronomicae], he doubtless has the originals — let him show in what place they were adulterated, or let him be ashamed to suspect such a thing of my integrity without occasion. Of his "confession" I will take no judgement, for my occupations have not hitherto permitted me to examine it sufficiently, by reason of its prolixity; nor do I gladly meddle in Theological disputes — so that I wonder he so often and so seriously demands my censure in such things, which do not pertain to those studies formerly ventilated between us. It seems to me a little harsh, what he asserts and defines from the start: that God saves us through the sacraments. That indeed needs a good gloss and a sober interpretation, if the suspicion of idolatry is to be absent — to which very many men are otherwise, alas, already much inclined, especially in this matter. And there are besides many things in the same writing, on Baptism as on the [Lord's] Supper, which he has not so invincibly demonstrated as he thinks. But let him see to it, and others, if he has any supporters of his opinion; it is not mine to settle such great quarrels. May God free Rothmann from the miseries with which he struggles, and give him patience and constancy. If I were with him, I would not fail his poverty, so far as my means allowed — even as before, when he was with me in Denmark, I did not send him away empty, but gave him several rose-nobles, for which, however, he scarcely deigned to thank me (so insolent was he then) — whereas his own Prince, far richer than I, gave my men (whom I once sent to him) not even an obol, nor even fed them meanwhile, as I fed Rothmann for 5 or 6 weeks when he was in Denmark. But these things are nothing to the purpose, nor those which I reckon of [no] small moment [text partly damaged]. About his brother Johann, who is a Doctor of medicine, I should wish to know whether he is still among the living; if so, I should wish that he visit me sometime — it will perhaps be not unprofitable for him.

I have lately understood that there is a certain man in the Brunswick territory, by name Johann Krabbe of Münden, who they say diligently observed the eclipses of these past years in both Luminaries [Sun and Moon]; if, then, you procure for me what he has explored about these, you will do a most welcome thing. But the bare times will not suffice me; rather I should wish to know the means by which he came to their knowledge — whether they be Altitudes of stars, or other things conducing hither. I desire to have his observations chiefly for this reason: that I may compare them with my own in the same eclipses, and thence more rightly draw out the difference of meridians [longitude]. There is also sometimes unequal clearness [of sky] in different places. But I cannot be sure of the observations' reliability unless I have ascertained, as I said, by what method they were taken.

With your Chancellor, I hope you will accomplish those things about which I wrote you before, that our wishes may be maturely satisfied.

Farewell.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 5th day of April, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), rector of the Magdeburg gymnasium and poet (the Froschmeuseler) — Tycho's agent for the instruments stored at Magdeburg, and the relay through whom Tycho learned Rothmann was alive (cf. brahe-t_15990922001).
  • Significance — the Magdeburg instrument-transport and the Rothmann estrangement.
  • The transport (imperial muscle). The Magdeburg city Senate had twice refused to ship Tycho's stored library and instruments up the Elbe; Rudolf II "took it ill" and ordered a letter to the Magdeburg cathedral Chapter instead — carried now by a Danish student of Tycho's, with a copy enclosed. Tycho lays out the logistics: shipment by the Elbe at the Chapter's cost, protective boards (reusable to load Bohemian wine or cheap goods on the return), a trusted overseer; if arranged, the student goes on to Hamburg for the remaining instruments. A vivid picture of moving Uraniborg's apparatus to Bohemia. (Cf. the same store at Magdeburg in brahe-t_16000124001.)
  • The Rothmann rupture. A revealing, bitter passage on Christoph Rothmann (the former Hesse-Kassel mathematician, Tycho's great correspondent of the 1580s): Rothmann, now "morose and impatient," complains of Tycho to Rollenhagen and demands Tycho's verdict on a theological "confession" he has written. Tycho declines — he "does not meddle in Theological disputes" — but cannot resist noting his unease at Rothmann's thesis "that God saves us through the sacraments" (needing "a good gloss" lest it smack of "idolatry"), and at his arguments on Baptism and the Supper. On the charge that Tycho did not faithfully print Rothmann's letters in the Epistolae astronomicae (1596), Tycho retorts: let him show where they were "adulterated." Tycho recalls hosting Rothmann 5–6 weeks in Denmark and gifting him rose-nobles (scarcely thanked), contrasting Rothmann's own Prince (Landgrave Wilhelm IV), who gave Tycho's men "not even an obol." A poignant late-stage souring of a famous scientific friendship.
  • A new observer for longitude. Tycho seeks the eclipse observations of Johann Krabbe of Münden (Brunswick) — but insists on knowing the method (star-altitudes etc.), since he wants them to compare with his own in the same eclipses and so derive the meridian-difference (longitude). A clean statement of the eclipse-timing method for longitude.
  • Persons named. Georg Rollenhagen; Doctor Rhodius (a Magdeburg/Wittenberg correspondent, unidentified-detail); Doctor Vitus [Winsheim]; Barvitius; the Magdeburg cathedral Chapter; Christoph Rothmann and his brother Johann (a physician); Landgrave Wilhelm IV ("his own Prince," unnamed); Johann Krabbe of Münden; the Hamburg citizen (unnamed); "your Chancellor" (unnamed). Places: Benátky, Prague, Magdeburg, the Elbe, Hamburg, Brunswick/Münden, Denmark.
  • Text note. A short damaged stretch near the end — "Sed ista nihil ad rhombum, [ne]que ea, quae parvi [no]n duco momen[ti]" — is partly editorially reconstructed (brackets); rendered "these things are nothing to the purpose, nor those which I reckon of [no] small moment," with the damage flagged. (nihil ad rhombum = "nothing to the point," a proverb.)
  • Calendar. Die 5 Aprilis Anno 1600 at Benátky (imperial reckoning) → 5 April 1600 [N.S.]; header and label agree. (Rollenhagen's incoming letter is dated "23 Feb … stylo vestro" = Old Style, the Magdeburg reckoning.)
