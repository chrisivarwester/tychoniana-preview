The original

CLARISSIMO ET DOCTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO THADÆO HAGECIO, MEDICINÆ ET PHILOSOPHIÆ DOCTORI CELEBERRIMO, AMICO SUO PLURIMUM COLENDO.

CLARISSIME et charissime D. D. THADDÆE. Binas a te recepi literas, ex quo Ratisbonæ simul fuimus, et quamdiu licuit, illic familiaritatem contraximus. Quod autem prioribus nihil interea a me responsum sit, scias propterea minime factum esse, quod amicitiæ nostræ immemor essem, et tui ob locorum et temporis intervalla oblitus. Nam nulla Horizontis vel locorum quantumvis magna intercapedo, vel temporis diuturnitas tui memoriam et amorem mihi adimet, sed inopia tabellariorum et commoditatum ad te ferendi meas literas effecit, ut hactenus nihil rescripserim, donec nunc tandem PAULUS WITTICHIUS alteras a te mihi attulerit, in quibus eum mihi multis commendas, iuvenem sane qui in Mathematicis exercitijs adeo sedulam hactenus operam navavit, ut non saltem dignus sit, qui ab omnibus bonis et doctis commendetur et ametur, sed etiam, qui ipsa commendatione maior sit. Fuit is mecum aliquandiu hîc in mea Astronomica Insula, et in triangulorum compendijs plura quam antea, communicatâ operâ, extricabimus. Nam et ego talibus insudavi atque in posterum ulterius, volente Numine, insudabo, quo hæc ratio, quæ per προσθαφαίρεσιν procedit absque tædiosa multiplicatione et divisione, plenius excolatur et locupletetur, in quibus tamen ille necdum satis est versatus, sed assuescet successive. Visus est enim is, quod et sponte fatetur, saltem initia quædam hîc iecisse admonitus ijs verbis, quæ se a me audivisse, dum semel Wittebergam ipso illic studente transirem et me horum studiorum causa convenisset, licet ego eorum recordari non potuerim. Verum is nunc ne aliquid damni in re familiari capiat, patriam et suos revisere constituit, brevi huc ad me, veluti sponte dixit, reversurus, ut simul observationes instrumentis meis, quæ varia et insigni magnitudine atque diligenti elaboratione nec parvis sumtibus et laboribus adorno, iucunde et utiliter exerceamus. Exstruo etiam insignem domum, quæ instrumentorum varij generis commodam dispositionem admittet, unâ cum laboratorio peculiari pro chymicis exercitijs, præsertim quantum ad Medicinam Spagiricam spectat, continuandis. Perduxi autem non sine magno sumtu et constanti labore, multis exantlatis molestijs et difficultatibus, eo usque opus totum, ut Deo feliciter auspicante futura proxime septimana sim migraturus in novas illas Ædes Uraniæ perpetuo, modo astra Borealia sic tulerint, dicatas, et illic nactus maiorem commoditatem tranquillitatemque, Deo volente, per otium hybernum observationes et demonstrationes nostras, quas habui varias et certas tam in ultimo maiori Cometa quam hoc minori, qui nunc conspicitur, absolvam, easque tibi et alijs eruditis communicabo; ubi inter alia certo ex diversis observationibus et rationibus demonstraturus sum, neutrum horum Cometarum exstitisse in Elementari regione, sed longe super Lunam in ipso æthere sedes et motum suum obtinuisse. Nam prior etiam Horizonti vicinissimus nunquam admisit parallaxin maiorem tertia parte unius gradus, sed semper minorem, uti multis rationibus infallibiliter demonstraturus sum. Huius, qui nunc durat, Cometæ parallaxes adeo sunt exiguæ, ut sint insensibiles pene, ut plane non dubitem eum esse æthereum. Tuæ observationes in ultimo Cometa ob instrumentorum penuriam non fuerunt exactæ neque intervallo 18 minutorum temporis per REGIOMONTANI rationem parallaxes ita variabantur, ut discerni et indagari poterant, etiamsi cætera, quæ ad observationem requirebantur, quæ sane non levia erant nec facile dabilia, recte se habuerunt. Neque Cometa unquam cum Aquila visus est culminare, nec si factum esset, parallaxis inde dari potuit, ut in Dialexi tua præ occupationibus minus considerate docuisti et in illo libro de Cometa exercuisti. Distantiæ vero a stellis diversis horis factæ et insensibiliter mutatæ, ut ipsemet asseris, convincunt minime tantam fuisse parallaxin, quantam asseris, imo ne sensibilem quidem; sed de his alibi latius. SCULTETI demonstrationes falsis nituntur fundamentis et erroneis observationibus, ut ipse WITTICHIUS, a quo eas desumsit, nunc fatetur; ideo nihil probat, quod eandem fere tecum posuerit parallaxin. Sed brevi, Deo volente, habebis meas observationes et demonstrationes in utroque Cometa a rei veritate, quatenus in sensus cadit, non aberrantes. Addam etiam instrumentorum meorum formam et constructionem et Solaris cursus exquisitam restitutionem, quam præcedentibus annis inquisivi, adeo ut cum observationibus indies conveniat, veluti WITTICHIUS mecum sæpe expertus est, a quibus calculus tam ALPHONSI quam COPERNICI nonnunquam semisse unius gradus, interdum paulo plus, aberrat. Longe enim alius est motus centri eccentrici Solis in suo circello, quam antecessores nostri deprehenderunt, vel ipse etiam COPERNICUS constituit, adeo ut eccentricitas Solis nunc sit 2p 5m scrupulis tredecim maior opinione COPERNICI. Apogæum vero Solis iuxta 5p ♑ hæret longe anterius quam habent COPERNICI Hypotheses. Aliter enim apparentiæ Solares non respondent, ut ex multis observationibus demonstravi, et brevi, Deo volente, communicabo eruditis. Deprehendi etiam celeriorem longe nunc esse motum octavæ sphæræ, quam COPERNICUS constituit, adeo quod æquinoctia ferme quarta parte unius gradus celerius anticipârit, quod per Spicam facta observatione eadem via qua COPERNICUS multoties observavi et demonstravi. Sed de his et alijs data maiori commoditate et otio ad te latius perscribam.

Nunc unum addam, quod tua interesse et in tuum commodum ac rei familiaris incrementum spectare confido. Rettulit mihi PAULUS noster, quomodo a Machiavellicis istis invidis et osoribus stipendio in aula Cæsaris privatus sis, neque interea certam et commodam vivendi rationem denuo assecutus es. Suaserim itaque, ut si res tuæ ita ferant, huc in Daniam ad nos conferas, visurus quales hîc commodidates adipisci queas; certo enim tibi polliceor me effecturum apud Regiam Maiestatem, et apud primarios Regni nostri nobiles, ut si hîc Medicinam exercere velis, liberale et sufficiens salarium a Rege et a cetera nobilitate pro opera medica amplam remunerationem obtineas, et in honore et existimatione, uti par est, apud nostrates magno cum rei familiaris emolumento degas, modo hisce meis consilijs morem gesseris, qua in re, si ipsemet tam cito venire nequeas, mihi tuam voluntatem significes rogo; ego interea procurabo ut omnia, quæ in tuum commodum spectare possint, apud nos copiosius et melius adipiscaris, quam in patria tua vel usquam alibi in reliqua Germania hactenus contigerunt. Tute igitur quid faciendum tibi sit, tecum delibera, et mihi, ut dixi, mature significa. De meis pollicitationibus non est quod quicquam dubites; efficiam enim ut experiaris in ipso effectu maiora his et omnia tibi Deo felicitante ex voto cessura. Deus Optimus Maximus te conservet, et in omni bono et utili proposito consilio et auxilio sit.

Vale.
Datæ in Insula nostra Huena 4. Novembris Anno 1580.
T. TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned man, Master Thaddaeus Hagecius, most celebrated Doctor of Medicine and of Philosophy, his friend much to be honoured.

Most renowned and most dear Master Doctor Thaddaeus. I have received two letters from you since we were together at Regensburg, and there, for as long as we were allowed, struck up a close acquaintance. But that nothing has meanwhile been answered by me to the earlier of them — know that this came about by no means because I was unmindful of our friendship and had forgotten you through the intervals of place and time. For no gap of horizon or of places, however great, nor any length of time, shall take from me the memory and the love of you; rather it was the lack of letter-carriers and of opportunities of conveying my letters to you that brought it about that hitherto I have written nothing in reply — until now at last Paul Wittich has brought me a further letter from you, in which you commend him to me at length: a young man indeed who has up to now devoted such diligent effort to mathematical exercises that he is not merely worthy to be commended and loved by all good and learned men, but is even greater than the commendation itself. He was with me here for some while on my astronomical island, and in the shortcuts of triangles we shall, by shared labour, work out more than before. For I too have toiled at such things, and shall in future toil at them yet further, God willing, so that this method, which proceeds by prosthaphaeresis [προσθαφαίρεσιν], without tedious multiplication and division, may be more fully cultivated and enriched — in which, however, he is not yet sufficiently versed, but will grow accustomed to it by degrees. For he seems — as he himself freely admits — to have laid at least certain beginnings here, prompted by those words which [he says] he had heard from me, when once I was passing through Wittenberg while he was studying there and he had met me on account of these studies, although I myself could not recall them. But now, lest he take some loss in his domestic affairs, he has resolved to revisit his homeland and his own people, intending — as he said of his own accord — soon to return here to me, so that together we may pleasantly and usefully carry out observations with my instruments, which I am furnishing of various and remarkable size and with careful workmanship and at no small expense and labour. I am also building a notable house, which will allow a convenient arrangement of instruments of various kinds, together with a special laboratory for the pursuit of chemical exercises, especially so far as they concern Spagyric Medicine. And I have, not without great expense and constant labour, and after many troubles and difficulties endured, brought the whole work so far forward that — God prospering the venture — next week I am to move into those new lodgings dedicated to Urania forever (provided the Northern stars so allow), and there, having gained greater convenience and tranquillity, I shall, God willing, complete during the winter's leisure our observations and demonstrations — which I have had, various and sure, both on the last, greater Comet and on this lesser one now in view — and shall communicate them to you and to other learned men; where, among other things, I am going to demonstrate with certainty, from diverse observations and reasonings, that neither of these Comets existed in the Elementary region, but obtained its seat and motion far above the Moon, in the aether itself. For the earlier one, even when nearest the horizon, never admitted a parallax greater than a third part of one degree, but always less, as I shall demonstrate infallibly by many arguments. The parallaxes of this Comet now lasting are so small as to be almost imperceptible, so that I quite do not doubt it to be aethereal. Your observations on the last Comet were, owing to the scarcity of instruments, not exact; nor, over an interval of 18 minutes of time, did the parallaxes by Regiomontanus's method vary in such a way that they could be discerned and traced out — even granting that the rest of what was required for the observation (things by no means slight, nor easily supplied) were rightly disposed. Nor was the Comet ever seen to culminate with Aquila; and even if it had been, no parallax could be derived from that — as in your Dialexis you taught, less considerately, on account of your other occupations, and as you argued in that book on the Comet. But the distances from stars, taken at different hours and changed imperceptibly (as you yourself assert), prove that the parallax was by no means so great as you assert — nay, not even perceptible; but of these things more fully elsewhere. Scultetus's demonstrations rest on false foundations and erroneous observations, as Wittich himself — from whom he took them — now admits; therefore it proves nothing that he posited nearly the same parallax as you. But shortly, God willing, you shall have my observations and demonstrations on both Comets, not erring from the truth of the matter, so far as it falls within the senses. I shall add too the form and construction of my instruments, and an exquisite restoration of the Sun's course, which I have investigated in the preceding years, so that it agrees with the observations from day to day — as Wittich has often found with me — from which the calculation both of the Alphonsine [Tables] and of Copernicus sometimes errs by half of one degree, occasionally by a little more. For the motion of the centre of the Sun's eccentric in its little circle is far other than our predecessors detected, or than Copernicus himself laid down, so that the eccentricity of the Sun is now greater than Copernicus's estimate by 2 parts [degrees], 5 minutes, [and] thirteen seconds. And the Sun's apogee stands fixed near 5 degrees of ♑ Capricorn, far earlier than Copernicus's Hypotheses have it. For otherwise the solar appearances do not answer, as I have demonstrated from many observations, and shall shortly, God willing, communicate to the learned. I have also detected that the motion of the eighth sphere is now far swifter than Copernicus laid down, so that it anticipates the equinoxes by nearly a fourth part of one degree more swiftly — which, by an observation made through Spica, in the same way as Copernicus, I have many times observed and demonstrated. But of these and other matters I shall write to you more fully when greater convenience and leisure are given.

Now I shall add one thing, which I am confident concerns your interest and looks to your advantage and the increase of your estate. Our Paul has reported to me how you have been deprived of your stipend at the Emperor's court by those Machiavellian enviers and haters, and that you have not meanwhile secured anew any sure and convenient means of living. I would therefore advise that, if your affairs so allow, you betake yourself hither into Denmark to us, to see what advantages you may obtain here; for I promise you with certainty that I shall bring it about, with His Royal Majesty and with the foremost nobles of our Kingdom, that — if you are willing to practise Medicine here — you may obtain a liberal and sufficient salary from the King, and ample remuneration for your medical service from the rest of the nobility, and may live in honour and esteem, as is fitting, among our countrymen, with great profit to your estate — provided you comply with these counsels of mine; in which matter, if you yourself cannot come so quickly, I ask that you signify your will to me; I meanwhile will see to it that you obtain among us, more abundantly and better, everything that can look to your advantage, than has hitherto fallen to you in your homeland or anywhere else in the rest of Germany. Deliberate, then, with yourself what you ought to do, and signify it to me, as I have said, in good time. There is no reason for you to doubt anything of my promises; for I shall see to it that you experience, in the very effect, greater things than these, and that all things — God prospering it — fall out for you according to your wish. May God, Best and Greatest, preserve you, and be your counsel and aid in every good and useful purpose.

Farewell.
Given on our Island of Hven, 4 November, in the year 1580.
Yours, Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe writing from Hven; the recipient Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer and personal physician at the Imperial court in Prague. This is Tycho's first letter to Hagecius (corroborated by Solc, in Tycho Brahe and Prague: Crossroads of European Science), opening a correspondence that ran to Tycho's Bohemian years. The two had met in person at Regensburg (Ratisbonae) — the 1575 Imperial Diet, where Maximilian II was crowned.
  • Place. Insula nostra Huena = the island of Hven (Ven); mea Astronomica Insula ("my astronomical island"). The novae Aedes Uraniae … dicatae ("new lodgings dedicated to Urania") = Uraniborg, into which Tycho says he will move "next week."
  • Paul Wittich. Paulus Wittichius = Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586) of Wrocław (Breslau / "the Wroclavian"), the mathematician who visited Hven in 1580 and carried Hagecius's letter of recommendation. The passage is the locus classicus of the prosthaphaeresis priority dispute: Tycho claims Wittich "freely admits" he had merely "laid certain beginnings here," prompted by words Wittich said he had heard from Tycho at Wittenberg — a claim Tycho hedges carefully (licet ego eorum recordari non potuerim, "although I myself could not recall them"; veluti sponte dixit, "as he said of his own accord"). The hedges are preserved, not sharpened; cf. Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, on Tycho's self-positioning as "originator" of the method.
  • Technical — prosthaphaeresis. haec ratio, quae per προσθαφαίρεσιν procedit absque taediosa multiplicatione et divisione — the additive-subtractive computing method (using trigonometric product-to-sum relations) that replaces laborious multiplication and division in spherical-triangle reduction (triangulorum compendia, "the shortcuts of triangles"). The Greek προσθαφαίρεσιν (accusative of προσθαφαίρεσις, prosthaphaíresis, lit. "addition-and-subtraction") is kept in the Source block and rendered prosthaphaeresis per glossary — never reduced to "trig identity."
  • Comets. ultimus maior Cometa = the great comet of 1577; hoc minori, qui nunc conspicitur = the comet of 1580, in view as Tycho wrote. Tycho's central claim: neutrum horum Cometarum exstitisse in Elementari regione, sed longe super Lunam in ipso aethere — neither comet lay in the sublunary Elementary region but far above the Moon, in the aether — established by the smallness of their parallax (1577: never more than ⅓ of a degree; 1580: "almost imperceptible," insensibiles pene).
  • Critique of Hagecius's Dialexis. in Dialexi tua … et in illo libro de Cometa = Hagecius's Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt 1574, on the 1572 nova) and his tract on the comet (Descriptio cometae … anni 1577, Prague 1578). Tycho faults the parallax method as too crude given instrument scarcity (instrumentorum penuria) and an 18-minute observing interval, faults the culminating with Aquila derivation, and notes the distances "changed imperceptibly" between hours — flagging Hagecius's claimed parallax as not even perceptible. The criticism is professional, softened by prae occupationibus minus considerate ("less considerately, on account of your other occupations").
  • Scultetus. Sculteti demonstrationes = Bartholomäus Scultetus (Schultz, 1540–1614) of Görlitz, mathematician; Tycho says Scultetus's cometary demonstrations rest on Wittich's erroneous data, which Wittich "now admits."
  • Solar theory (technical figures — verbatim). eccentricitas Solis nunc sit 2p 5m scrupulis tredecim maior opinione COPERNICI = the solar eccentricity is greater than Copernicus's value by 2 parts, 5 minutes, thirteen seconds (i.e. 2°5′13″); here p (partes) = degrees, m (minuta) = arc-minutes, scrupula = arc-seconds. Apogaeum … iuxta 5p ♑ = the solar apogee (apse) near 5° of Capricorn (♑ kept in Source, named in translation by this chapter's convention). The Alphonsine and Copernican calculation is said to err by up to ~½° (semisse unius gradus, interdum paulo plus). motus centri eccentrici Solis in suo circello = the motion of the centre of the solar eccentric on its "little circle" (the Copernican small-circle device for the variable eccentricity). Note: the figures are reproduced exactly as written; "2°5′13″ greater than Copernicus" is Tycho's stated difference, not an absolute value — not silently reconciled to any modern figure.
  • Precession. celeriorem … motum octavae sphaerae … aequinoctia ferme quarta parte unius gradus celerius anticipârit = the motion of the eighth sphere (precession) is now swifter, the equinoxes anticipated by nearly ¼° faster than Copernicus laid down; verified per Spicam (by observation of the star Spica, α Virginis, Copernicus's own precession reference star), eadem via qua Copernicus ("in the same way as Copernicus").
  • Medicine / alchemy. laboratorio … pro chymicis exercitijs … quantum ad Medicinam Spagiricam spectat = the laboratory for chemical work, "especially as concerns Spagyric Medicine" (the Paracelsian alchemical pharmacy) — the period term kept, not modernised.
  • The invitation to Denmark. a Machiavellicis istis invidis et osoribus stipendio in aula Caesaris privatus sis = Hagecius had lost his court stipend to "those Machiavellian enviers and haters" — Tycho offers to secure him a salaried medical post under Regia Maiestas (Frederik II) and the Danish nobility. Solc notes the invitation concerned Hagecius as a physician, not as an astronomer.
  • Authorities named. Regiomontani ratio = the parallax method of Regiomontanus (Johannes Müller, 1436–76); calculus … Alphonsi = the Alphonsine Tables; Copernicus / Copernici Hypotheses = Copernicus and the planetary models of De revolutionibus (1543).
  • Epistolary formulae. Salutation Clarissimo et doctissimo viro … amico suo plurimum colendo and Clarissime et charissime D. D. (= Domine Doctor) Thaddaee rendered by this chapter's convention; subscription Vale → "Farewell"; Datae … 4. Novembris Anno 1580 in the dateline.
  • Calendar. Dateline 4. Novembris Anno 1580 — Denmark was on the Julian calendar (Old Style) throughout Tycho's career; the date is O.S. The edition label ("1580. 4. november. Hven") agrees with the as-written dateline; no date discrepancy.
  • Editorial marks. The superscript sigla in the working transcription (printed as raised letters keyed to the apparatus of the edition of record) have been silently dropped from both the Source block and the translation, as they are editorial reference marks, not part of the letter text. DD, D. expanded as Dominus/Domine by this chapter's convention
