English translation machine translation, Probable

To the worshipful, our trusty and well-beloved Tycho Brahe, lord of Knudstrup. Wilhelm, by the grace of God Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, etc.

Our trusty and well-beloved: That you gave us to understand, under date of Uraniborg, 2 April, that you now have it in hand to bring out in print the first volume On the More Recent Phenomena of the Heavens [de Recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis], and have offered to communicate the same to us — this has been so much the more welcome to us to learn, inasmuch as in the said volume you treat, as it were professedly [quasi ex professo], of the Fixed Stars (for which we have so long had a particular longing), and not only that, but also recount several hundred stars amended out of your observations [Obseruationibus]. We shall accordingly look forward to that work [Operis], to requite it with [our] grace. As concerns our mathematician [Mathematicum], he did indeed, some months ago, complain to us of his bodily sickness; but, notwithstanding that he gave us to understand [the same] in writing, and that we graciously commanded him thereto, he has nonetheless not arrived. That he does not come back, we cannot sufficiently wonder at, and we almost form the thought, or suspicion [Suspition], that he may have some grave and harmful sickness; [and] the longer he now keeps it by him, the worse it will become with him. As concerns the reindeer [Riens], there is a Danish lad with us, of the Bilde [Bille] family, who has told us of it, that there ought to be some of them in the menagerie at Frederiksborg, likewise at Copenhagen. We also asked him whether they be the reindeer; he said no, but [that] it is quite a tall beast. So we have, in Gessner's German book on animals [libro Germanico … de Animalibus], printed at Zürich in the year 1563, on folio 85, found the image and description [Iconem et descriptionem] of the same; but from those who were out here at Wolfenbüttel with the Queen we could get no account of it. The reindeer-bucks, or beasts, we have had fetched out of Sweden, on two occasions, to nearly a hundred head, which also held up quite well in the winter season and drew the sledge upon the ice after their custom; but as soon as the warmth set in, they all died, so that we thus see that in summer, and when the warmth comes on, they cannot keep [alive] here in [this] land. But as concerns the elk, we have one in our menagerie at Zapfenburg; it has held up well from Martinmas until now, runs and leaps and is of good cheer, and especially when we drive about in our menagerie in our little green cart, it runs straight up to us and waits for us as if it were a dog; but its antlers will not grow much for it, and to this day are not above a finger's length. Whether this be perhaps a sign [Signum] that they will not well take to breeding in this land — that one will see. We have sent again into Sweden [to ask] whether we might get more of them; but if you could send us one or two, especially such as were tame, you would thereby do us a particularly agreeable favour. We shall also acknowledge it again in grace, and graciously reimburse you the costs and expenses laid out. As, then, concerns the requested papermakers, we may not graciously conceal from you that we have among us a single one; the same is only just now setting up, and makes very coarse work, as is to be seen from the present paper and from the letter dispatched to you. But we will have inquiry made for us at Frankfurt, [to see] whether we might not move one or two thereto, that they should be willing to betake themselves to you; yet we must first know in what manner you would keep and maintain them: we will then help to further their dispatch as soon as possible. This we would not graciously conceal from you, to whom we are by [our] grace especially well disposed. Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on 22 May, in the year 1591. Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), "the Wise," the observing prince of Kassel whose astronomers Christoph Rothmann and Joost Bürgi corresponded with Tycho; the long Tycho–Hesse exchange (vols VI–VII of Dreyer) is the documentary core of late-16th-c. observational astronomy. The titulature Graue zu Catzenelnbogen (Count of Katzenelnbogen) is the standard Hessian style.
  • Recipient address. Dem Vesten — "to the worshipful/steadfast," the chancery honorific for an untitled nobleman (vest/fest "firm, steadfast"); vnserm lieben Besondern = "our trusty and well-beloved" (the edition's convention, Vester lieber besonder). Herrn zu Knudstrup — lord of Knudstrup (Knutstorp), the Brahe family seat.
  • Date crux (catalogue vs. dateline). The edition label reads "1587. 22. maj."; the letter's own dateline reads "am 22. Maij, Anno 1591." The header follows the dateline by the edition's convention The internal evidence corroborates 1591, not 1587: the Landgrave is replying to Tycho's letter of "2 April" announcing that he now has the first volume "de Recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis" in Druck (in press) — i.e. the volume on the new-star/comet phenomena and the fixed-star catalogue (the work issued from Uraniborg as the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata / De mundi … recentioribus phaenomenis), whose printing was under way in the early 1590s, not 1587. The "1587" label is treated as a catalogue mis-dating and recorded, not silently corrected. The letter is printed in Tycho's own Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596), the edition of record. Date is Old Style (Hesse, like Denmark, kept the Julian calendar).
  • vnsern Mathematicum — "our mathematician," Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's astronomer, who had travelled to Hven in 1590 and, pleading illness, never returned to Kassel; the Landgrave's puzzlement ("we cannot sufficiently wonder … that he does not come back") fixes the situation in 1590–91. Identification of Mathematicum with Rothmann is secure on context; named here on first occurrence.
  • The natural-history matter (reindeer / elk). The bulk of the letter answers a request from Tycho for live northern animals. Riens / Rhenen = reindeer (Rentier); Reiner likewise the reindeer; Elendt = the elk / European moose (Elch). Thiergarten = menagerie / animal park. Places: Friderichsburg = Frederiksborg; Koppenhagen = Copenhagen; Zapffenburg = Zapfenburg (the Landgrave's game park near Kassel); Wolffenbüttel = Wolfenbüttel. die Königin — "the Queen": almost certainly Sophie, the Hessian-born consort/dowager moving between the Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel and Hessian courts (her exact identity not made explicit in the letter; left as "the Queen").
  • Gessner's German book on animals … Zürich 1563, folio 85. = Conrad Gessner, Thierbuch (the German Historiae animalium), Zürich 1563; the Landgrave cites Iconem et descriptionem ("the image and description") on fol. 85 — a code-switch into Latin for the bibliographic reference.
  • deß Geschlechts ein Bilde. Resolved: Dreyer's facing 1596 Latin (Opera Omnia VI p. 247) renders it ex familia BILDORVM, 'of the family of the Bildes' — Bilde is the surname of the Danish noble Bille/Bilde family (here a young Bille in Hessian service), not 'image-maker'. Rendered 'of the Bilde [Bille] family.'
  • The papermakers. Papiermacher — Tycho was equipping his own paper mill on Hven; the Landgrave reports that his single papermaker "makes very coarse work" (sehr schro[ff] Werck), evidenced by "the present paper," and offers to recruit one or two from Frankfurt for Tycho — conditional on first knowing welcher gestalt jhr sie halten … wollet ("in what manner you would keep and maintain them"). The conditional is preserved (the dispatch is promised only alßdan, "then," after that condition is met); no royal-style command here, a princely offer with a stated precondition.
  • Latin code-switching (chancery German over a learned-Latin technical lexicon, the edition's convention). Technical and bibliographic nouns are kept in Latin in the source and italicised on first use in the translation: den ersten Tomum de Recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis, quasi ex professo, Stellarum Fixarum, Obseruationibus, Operis, Mathematicum, Suspition, Iconem et descriptionem, Signum. The closing Datum … Anno 1591 is the Latin dating formula.
  • The superscript "A" markers scattered through the source (ETC.A, KranckheitA, DenischerA, GeschlechtsA, gedrucktA, LXXXV.A, keinenA, SchwedenA, wennA, einesA, alsoA, am 22.A, VestungA, final …HESSEN.A) are not part of the text: they are the anchor-marks of the 1596 printed edition's marginal sidenotes/apparatus, carried over as artefacts of extraction. They are reproduced as-printed in the Source block by the edition's convention(2) and carry no lexical content; the translation ignores them. Likewise fsondert (for sondern) and Datumin / .WILHELM (lost word-spacing) are extraction run-ons, read as sondern and Datum in / . Wilhelm.
  • Princely register. The plural-of-majesty Wir/vns/euch–jhr and the recurring gnediglich(en) ("graciously") are preserved throughout; Wolten wir euch … gnediglich nicht verhalten = "this we would not graciously conceal from you" (a standard chancery close). Two negations occur in that clause-pair (nicht … nicht verhalten across the final two sentences); both are the idiomatic litotes "we would not conceal" and are reproduced exactly, not collapsed.
  • Correction. 'befohlen' (from 'befehlen', to command) had been rendered as 'graciously granted him this'; corrected to 'graciously commanded him thereto', consistent with the sense of the following sentence expressing surprise at his non-return.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). German 'befohlen' (from 'befehlen') means 'commanded/ordered', not 'granted [permission]'. The next sentence — 'that he does not come back, we cannot sufficiently wonder at' — only makes sense if Wilhelm had ordered the mathematician to return and is now surprised he hasn't; 'granted him this' instead implies Wilhelm permitted him to stay away, which would make the following expression of surprise incoherent. At: “notwithstanding that he gave us to understand [the same] in writing, and that we graciously granted him this, he has non”.

Repaired. No Dreyer extract exists for this letter (no page reference); the fix rests entirely on internal evidence — the German the transcribed original's own word 'befohlen' and the logical coherence of the surrounding sentence, which only makes sense as a command to return, not a grant of leave to stay away. No text error claims were present to require Dreyer verification. Grade B: sound but uncollated against the edition of record.
