The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

INTELLEXI, Vir nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile Princeps, ex Agente rerum Serenissimi Principis mei in aulâ Caesareâ, ALEX. PRENDTL, qui ad negotia sua quaedam propria expedienda hâc transijt, literas illas, quas D. Doctori HELLER ad me dirigendas committis, praeposito seu Magistro supremo equorum dispositorum Pragae consignari. Coeterum novi et expertus sum eorum, quibus cura Postae seu equorum dispositorum incumbit, negligentiam et desidiam, atque adeo oscitantiam quoque miram, ita ut literas aliô dirigendas aliorsum nonnunquam transmittant. Tenet itaque haec res me admodum et forte plus nimio solicitum, ne forte ea, quae ad me mittis, intereant. Quare rogo te etiam atque etiam, ut in posterum, si quid literarum ad me dederis, eas praefato Agenti rerum Principis mei, vel in absentia illius IOANNI MENHARDO S. Caes. M. Consilij aulae Imperialis Secretario Latino, qui aulam Imperatoris sequitur, quorum titulos his subscripsi, consignes; curabunt enim illi sedulo, ut sub coperto, uti vocant, Serenissimi mei Principis tuto ad me transferantur.

Novissime literas tuas pridie huius anni labentis datas unâ cum dono partis primae Progymnasmatum restauratae Astronomiae (id quod gratitudinis ergo commemoro) accepi, nullas autem recentiores. Quod si interea temporis nullas ad me dederis, bene se res habet, et omni solicitudine vaco: si quas vero dederis, timeo, ne alicubi delitescant, et obsecro summopere, ut eas reiterari cures, et vel hac ratione solicitudinem alleves.

Atque hisce tibi officia et studia mea paratissima quaeque polliceor.
Datae Monachij die 11 Apr. A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

I have understood, most noble man and easily Prince of all mathematicians, from the Agent of my Most Serene Prince's affairs at the Imperial court, Alexander Prendtl — who passed this way to expedite certain affairs of his own — that those letters which you entrust to Doctor Heller, to be directed to me, are consigned to the prefect, or supreme Master, of the relay-horses [the post] at Prague. But I know, and have learned by experience, the negligence and sloth — and indeed the wondrous somnolence too — of those to whom the care of the Post, or of the relay-horses, is committed, so that they sometimes send letters that ought to be directed one way off in another direction. And so this matter holds me very anxious — perhaps more than is fitting — lest the things you send me should perchance perish. Wherefore I ask you, again and again, that in future, if you give any letters to me, you consign them to the aforesaid Agent of my Prince's affairs, or, in his absence, to Johann Menhard, Latin Secretary of the Aulic Imperial Council of His Imperial Majesty, who follows the Emperor's court (whose titles I have subscribed below); for they will diligently see to it that, "under cover" (as they call it) of my Most Serene Prince, they be safely conveyed to me.

Most recently I received your letter, dated the day before [the beginning of] this passing year, together with the gift of the first part of the Progymnasmata of restored Astronomy (which I mention out of gratitude); but none more recent. So if, in the meantime, you have given me none, the matter stands well, and I am free of all anxiety; but if you have given any, I fear lest they be lying hidden somewhere, and I most earnestly beseech that you see to their being repeated, and by this means at least relieve my anxiety.

And with these I promise you my most ready services and zeal.
Given at Munich, the 11th day of April, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (the Munich correspondent); here on the practical problem of postal security.
  • The postal routing. Herwart warns that letters routed through the Prague post-master (praepositus seu Magister supremus equorum dispositorum — the prefect of the relay-horses, i.e. the imperial post) go astray, and asks Tycho instead to consign letters to Alexander Prandtl (the Bavarian ducal agent at the imperial court — cf. brahe-t_16000923001), or, in his absence, to Johann Menhard (Menhardus), Latin Secretary of the Aulic Imperial Council — so they travel "under cover" (sub coperto — enclosed in the Bavarian diplomatic packet) and arrive safely. Doctor Heller = Tycho's Prague forwarding contact (cf. brahe-t_15991214001).
  • The book-gift. Herwart acknowledges Tycho's gift of "the first part of the Progymnasmata of restored Astronomy" = the first volume of the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata — received with a letter dated pridie huius anni labentis, "the day before [the start of] this current year" (i.e. c. 31 December 1599 / 1 January 1600).
  • Calendar. Monachij die 11 Apr. 1600 = 11 April 1600; Munich (Catholic Bavaria) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Corrected the agent's name in translation from 'Alexander Prandtl' to 'Alexander Prendtl', matching both the transcribed original and Dreyer's print (TBOO VIII p.300) exactly.
