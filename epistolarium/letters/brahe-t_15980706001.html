FRANCISCUS TENGNAGEL AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

S. P.

NOBILISSIME et præstantissime Domine TYCHO. Rebus Mæ Tæ ex animi sententia confectis, officij mei esse ratus sum (siquidem incertus sim, utrum priores meæ raptim Coloniæ ad te exaratæ tibi redditæ sint nec ne) particulatim nonnulla et fusius referre, cum tunc otium singula attingendi mihi non suppeteret, omissis tamen nunc ingratis, quæ in itinere mihi evenerunt, siquidem illibenter talia te audire norim. Aquisgranum cum appulissem relicta 3 die Iunij Colonia, ibi Serenissimum Principem Electorem Coloniensem ERNESTUM iam itineri Leodium versus, quod postero die ingressus est, accinctum reperi. Qui quamprimum de eo, quod cum literis tuis adessem, inaudivit, confestim me accersitum humanissime compellavit, gratissimum sibi munus Ma Tæ literarium significans, et daturum se operam, ut Ma Ta reipsa se insignem personæ suæ et rei Astronomicæ fautorem comperiat. Quem suum affectum iam pridem antea conceptum, in posterum etiam magis magisque se declaraturum pollicebatur. Interim velle se, ut Leodium curru suo proprio me conferam. Omnem enim fere se eo comitatum unâ translaturum, et rebus suis intra triduum in Limburgensi propinquo Ducatu expeditis actutum subsequuturum. Id quod et factum est. Ibi singulis fere diebus in Cameram ad secretum Rmæ eius Celnis colloquium per aliquot nonnunquam integras horas vocatus fui, humanissime exceptus, tandem etiam (quod tam subito ob innumeras, quibus Leodij quotidie distringeretur occupationes, fieri non posse prolixe excusavit) liberalissime dimissus, donata mihi aurea sua effigie, unâ cum equo, quem centum et viginti Coronatis a Magistro Palatij sui coëmerat. Quæ vero Rmæ eius Celni de negotijs Mæ Tæ in scriptis tradidi, et simul oretenus exposui, summa cum voluptate legit et audivit, Danos rem se indignam in eo, quod Mam Tam non in eo (uti decuit) precio habuerint, atque tam inconsiderate dimiserint, vel potius amiserint, magno suo opprobrio commisisse perhibens vix ulla ætate apud cordatos et intelligentes expiandam. Et aiebat insuper, eorum officij fuisse sponte sua omni conatu in hoc eniti, quo te beneficijs atque officijs liberaliter præstitis apud se in Regis et Regni honorem retinerent, tantum abest, ut tuæ abitioni, velut inaudierat, causas tam evidentes et intolerabiles præbere, atque voluntatem et decretum Optimi Regis FRIDERICI post fata rescindere, Senatorumque omnium literas tibi (prout compertum habebat) in membrana statim ab obitu FRIDERICI Regis concessas exauthorare ipsis fas esset; cumque me semel interrogasset, in quantam pecuniæ summam excrescere possent, si computarentur omnia, quæ a Rege FRIDERICO et Senatu Regni ad Astronomiæ instaurationem Mæ Tæ concessa erant, me certo illud dicere non posse referente, tantum tamen Mæ Tæ suppetijsse, ut omnes e quibuscumque tandem oris adventantes, Reges, Principes, eorundem Legatos, Doctores, Studiosos, et cuiusvis ordinis homines splendide semper et munifice tractârit, præterquam quod tot et tantos sumptus in Astronomiæ redintegrationem, quales nemo ante ipsum, erogasset, et alias copiosam aleret familiam. An vero beneficis Regijs, an etiam pro parte proprijs bonis hæreditarijs, illos haberet reditus, mihi non satis constare: Existimare tamen summam eorum, quæ ex Regno quotannis habebat, quatuor thalerorum millia mea opinione non multum excessisse, mox Rma eius Celdo in hæc formalia verba prorupit: Was? Solte man ein Solchen Man vmb so ein geringes geldt auß dem Land ziehenn lassen. Schande ist es. Ein Herr verspielt mehr auf einen abendt. Goldt bekompt man alzeitt aber solche leutte nicht alzeitt. Sed quandoquidem ita iam evenerit, quocunque tandem Danorum fato et oscitantia, ut in Germaniam veniens Ma Ta sibi propior reddita sit, magnopere se ea de causa lætari, et avide hanc commoditatem in posterum arrepturum. Placere sibi, quod nulli Principi Germaniæ, aut Regioni exteræ suam operam addicere velit, antequam de Imperatoriæ Maiestatis clementissimæ voluntate erga se et ea quæ tractat studia, certo constaret. Et omnino censuit, hanc Regiam artem Imperatori, tanquam præcipuo Europæ capiti, omnium primo offerendam esse, ac neutiquam se ambigere, imo multa depositurum, Cæsaream Maiestatem pro eo quo esset erga omnes liberales disciplinas, inprimis vero Mathematica et inter hæc Astronomica studia singulari favore et amore, Mam Tam libentissimo animo excepturam, munifice cum familia et toto apparatu Astronomico sustentaturam, nec commissuram, ut Mam Tam migrationis huius e Dania unquam pœniteat. Qua etiam de re se non saltem commendatitias, sed pariter congratulatorias ad Imperatoriam Maiestatem daturum, tum quoque intimo eius Secretario BARVITIO, de quo ipse bene meritus esset, quique illi aliquo modo obstrictus, unâ scripturum. Et si præter omnem suam opinionem Cæsa Maiestas Astronomica studia in totius Imperij nunquam intermoriturum honorem et decus conservare et sustentare ab alijs forte dissuasus gravaretur (quod tamen ipsam sponte sua non admissuram certe sciret) multos fore, qui hanc gloriam sibi et suis ditionibus accedere summopere desiderent, nec eam occasionem negligant. Imo se ipsum suis impensis id præstiturum, nec ullis nisi soli Imperatori, cui hic honor primum debetur, hac in parte cessurum. Hæc et plura eiuscemodi diversis temporibus ad me dixit, quæ referre nimis nunc prolixum foret. Latius D. V. de his coram. Unicum tamen præterire non possum, quod mihi discessuro confirmationis eorum omnium loco sub ipsum valedictionis tempus inculcavit; ferme his formalibus verbis: Saget dem guttenn ehrlichenn Mann viel guts von meinet wegenn, und das ich hernacher also bey ihm handeln werde, als wan er mein leiblicher bruder wehr, und ich ihn von jugendt auf gekant hette, dessen er sich gentzlichenn zu mir versehenn soll. Saget ihm auch daneben alles was ihr sonstenn von mir gehöret habt, und bitet ihn das er mir seinenn zustandt zueschreibe, so offt ihm gelegenheit vorfelt. Wan ich vormercke, das er bey Hamburg etwas verziehenn wirdt, ehe er zur Kay. Ma. ziehet, bin ich willens einenn von meinenn nobilibus, deme ich in pyronomicis exercitijs vertraue, zu ihm abzufertigenn, von diesem und anderem meinetwegenn mit ihm zu conferiren. Und so baldt ich, ob Gott will, zu ihr Kay. Mt. komme, will ich ihm TYCHONEM, wo er alda in des Kaisers gebiet anzutreffen, besuchenn und weiter mit ihm kuntschaft machen. Nam plane nihil dubitavit, se alicubi in Imperatoriæ Matis ditionibus te reperturum. Sicque me clementer et liberaliter (uti prius innui) dimisit. Pericula et damna, quæ in hoc itinere perpessus sum, quæque in prioribus meis aliquatenus attigi, hîc utpote ingrata, non lubet iterare.

Venio nunc ad Holandos, ad quos in Patria aliquantisper moratus me contuli, quo commoditatem promptiorem et tutiorem redeundi nanciscerer. Ibi cum Hagam, ubi iuris suprema est administratio, quam vel in transcursu cognoscere avebam, pervenissem, Illustrissimus Princeps MAURITIUS, qui Mam Tam novit et magni facit, benignum et clementissimum se mihi exhibuit. Cui libros quosdam Mæ Tæ secreto ostendi. Quos cum illi apprime arridere viderem, eos consulto ipsi tanquam ex tua voluntate dono dedi, atque illud inscripsi. Qui sane nihil [magis] in votis habuit, quam te in Hollandia novas Uraniæ sedes figere et stabilire, seque id Ordinibus sedulo propositurum, modo de tua voluntate certior redderetur, aiebat. Neque enim ambigere, quin et Ordinibus hoc perplaciturum sit. Existimavit etiam, non reperturam Mam Tam ullum locum Genio et studijs suis aptiorem. Quod ut ad Mam Tam suo nomine cum insigni animi sui et amicitiæ declaratione, donec ipsemet istic comparuero, perscriberem, iussit, et has quoque inclusas ad eam primo quoque tempore mittendas dedit. Neque tantum ab Illustrissimo Principe hoc ipsius et Ordinum notum percepi; sed et IOHANNE BARNEFELDIO, qui nomine quidem Advocatus supremus, sed reipsa quasi Comes ceu Regulus est Hollandiæ. Nam ab eius nutu et voluntate ferme pendent omnia. Cumque et ipsum non minus quam MAURITIUM (qui brevibus ob innumeras suas occupationes Mæ Tæ gratias, pro libris actis, rescripsit, particularia ut Mæ Tæ oretenus exponerem iubens) in nostras partes inclinare animadverterem atque te eo accersendum et benigne tractandum censere, subiunxi, me de tua voluntate hac in parte nihil certi referre posse, siquidem intellexerim, te Imperatoriæ Maiestati omnium primo tua studia demisse offerre decrevisse, tunc ille mox obmutuit et nihil amplius hac de re ingessit, sed quasi alio declinans orationem, aiebat, se esse in procinctu itineris Anglici, ubi legatum acturus esset nomine Ordinum. Ideoque nunc pluribus mecum loqui non posse. Ubi reversus fuerit, sique res ita postularit, et de tua voluntate sibi quidpiam constiterit, hoc negotium se apud Ordines peracturum. Inaudivi etiam in Hollandia, Angliam magno tui teneri desiderio, quod et ad te perscriptum esse quidam referebat: verene an falso, me fugit. Sed tu rectius dispicies, quid tibi faciendum sit. Prævides enim quantis tumultibus Regnum illud obnoxium sit, quæ tunc primum gliscent, ubi Regina ferme decrepita fatis concesserit. Habes, si sic luberet, Scotiæ Regem in magna illa Britannica Insula, non saltem affinitate Danis coniunctissimum, sed et tui amantissimum, vel ex ea consuetudine, quæ vobis intercessit, ex quo illum cum nobili suo Comitatu te in tua Insula invisentem magnifice excepisti, deque rebus eruditis (uti est Princeps doctus) cum eo affatim contulisti.

Hæc Mæ Tæ, donec ipsemet Wandesburgi me stitero, præsignificanda habui, quantum nunc inter peregrinandum licuit, ut, quid in posterum sibi faciendum sit, eo citius et rectius statuat. Vale vir nobilissime et præstantissime, ac me propediem expecta.

Amstelodamo die 6. Iulij Ao. 1598.
Mæ Tæ perpetuo devinctus
FRANCISCUS G. TENGNAGEL, manu propria.

FRANS TENGNAGEL TO TYCHO BRAHE.

Heartiest greeting.

Most noble and excellent Lord Tycho. Your Magnificence's affairs being accomplished according to [my] mind, I thought it my duty (since I am uncertain whether my earlier [letter], hastily written to you at Cologne, was delivered you or not) to report some things in detail and more fully — for then I had no leisure to touch each point — omitting, however, now, the unpleasant things that befell me on the journey, since I know you hear such things unwillingly. When I had come to Aachen (leaving Cologne on the 3rd of June), I found there the most serene Prince Elector of Cologne, Ernst [of Bavaria], already girded for a journey toward Liège, which he entered the next day. As soon as he heard that I was present with your letters, he at once had me summoned and most kindly addressed me, signifying that Your Magnificence's literary gift was most welcome to him, and that he would take pains that Your Magnificence find him a signal patron of his person and of the Astronomical cause — an affection conceived long before, which he promised to declare more and more hereafter. Meanwhile he wished me to go to Liège in his own carriage; for he would transfer almost his whole retinue thither, and, his affairs in the neighbouring Duchy of Limburg dispatched within three days, would follow at once — which was done. There, almost daily, I was called into the Chamber for secret conversation with His Reverend Highness, sometimes for several whole hours, most kindly received; at last too (he excusing at length that it could not be done so soon, on account of the innumerable occupations by which he was daily distracted at Liège) most liberally dismissed, a golden effigy of himself given me, together with a horse which he had bought from his Palace-Master for a hundred and twenty Crowns. What I gave His Reverend Highness in writing about Your Magnificence's affairs, and at the same time expounded by mouth, he read and heard with the greatest pleasure — declaring that the Danes had committed a thing unworthy of themselves, in not holding Your Magnificence in the esteem they ought, and so inconsiderately dismissing — or rather losing — you, to their great reproach, scarcely to be expiated in any age among the sensible and intelligent. And he said besides that it had been their duty, of their own accord, to strive with all effort to retain you with them, by benefits and offices liberally given, to the honour of the King and Realm — far from giving such evident and intolerable causes of your departure (as he had heard), and rescinding the will and decree of the best King Frederick after his death, and annulling all the Senators' letters granted you on parchment immediately after King Frederick's death. And when he once asked me to how great a sum of money [all] could grow, if everything were computed that had been granted Your Magnificence by King Frederick and the Realm's Senate for the instauration of Astronomy — and I answered that I could not say that for certain, but that Your Magnificence had had enough always to treat splendidly and munificently all comers from whatever shores (Kings, Princes, their Legates, Doctors, Students, and men of every rank), besides laying out such great costs on the restoration of Astronomy as no one before, and otherwise feeding a numerous household; [and] whether you had those revenues from royal benefices, or partly from your own hereditary goods, was not sufficiently clear to me; yet I reckoned the sum you had yearly from the Realm did not, in my opinion, much exceed four thousand thalers — His Reverend Highness at once burst into these formal words:

> "What? Should one let such a man leave the country for so paltry a sum of money? It is a shame. A lord gambles away more in one evening. Gold one always gets — but such people, not always."

But since it has now so happened — by whatever Danish fate and negligence — that Your Magnificence, coming into Germany, is made nearer to him, he greatly rejoices on that account, and will eagerly seize this convenience hereafter. It pleases him that you will pledge your service to no Prince of Germany, nor to any foreign Region, before it be certain of the Imperial Majesty's most clement will toward you and the studies you pursue. And he wholly judged that this Royal art ought to be offered, first of all, to the Emperor, as the chief head of Europe; and he in no way doubts — nay, would wager much — that the Imperial Majesty, given his singular favour and love for all liberal disciplines (and especially Mathematics, and among these the Astronomical studies), would receive Your Magnificence most gladly, munificently sustain you with your family and your whole Astronomical apparatus, and not permit you ever to repent this migration from Denmark. About which he would give the Imperial Majesty not only commendatory but congratulatory letters; and likewise write to his intimate Secretary Barvitius (whom he had himself benefited, and who is in some way bound to him). And if — beyond all his expectation — the Imperial Majesty (perhaps dissuaded by others) should be reluctant to preserve and sustain the Astronomical studies, to the never-dying honour and ornament of the whole Empire (which he was certain the Emperor would not admit of his own accord), there would be many who would greatly desire this glory to accrue to themselves and their dominions, and would not neglect the occasion. Nay, he himself would provide it at his own expense, and yield to none in this part but the Emperor alone, to whom this honour is first owed. These and more such things he said to me at different times, which it would be too prolix now to report. More of these to Your Lordship in person. Yet one thing I cannot pass over, which, as I was leaving, he inculcated to me, in place of a confirmation of all, at the very time of farewell, almost in these formal words:

> "Say much good to the good, honest man on my behalf — and that I will hereafter deal with him as if he were my own brother, and I had known him from youth; of which he should wholly rely upon me. Tell him also, besides, everything else you have heard from me; and ask him to write me his condition, as often as occasion offers him. When I perceive that he will tarry a while near Hamburg before he goes to the Imperial Majesty, I am minded to dispatch to him one of my nobles, whom I trust in pyronomic [alchemical] exercises, to confer with him about this and other matters on my behalf. And as soon as I, God willing, come to the Imperial Majesty, I will visit Tycho — wherever he is to be met there, in the Emperor's territory — and make further acquaintance with him."

For he plainly did not doubt that he would find you somewhere in the Imperial Majesty's dominions. And so he dismissed me kindly and liberally (as I indicated before). The perils and losses I suffered on this journey, which I touched on somewhat in my earlier [letter], I do not care to repeat here, as being unpleasant.

I come now to the Hollanders, to whom — having stayed a while in my fatherland — I betook myself, to get a readier and safer means of returning. When I had come there to The Hague (where the supreme administration of justice is, which I longed to see even in passing), the most illustrious Prince Maurice [of Nassau], who knows Your Magnificence and esteems you highly, showed himself kind and most clement to me. To him I secretly showed some of Your Magnificence's books. Seeing them please him greatly, I deliberately gave them to him as a gift, as from your will, and inscribed it [so]. He indeed wished nothing more than that you fix and establish new seats of Urania in Holland, and said he would diligently propose it to the States [Estates], if he were made certain of your will; nor does he doubt it would greatly please the States. He reckoned, too, that Your Magnificence would find no place fitter for your genius and studies. He ordered me to write this to Your Magnificence in his name, with a signal declaration of his mind and friendship, until I myself appear there; and gave these enclosed [letters] too, to be sent you at the first opportunity. Nor did I perceive this intent — his and the States' — only from the most illustrious Prince, but also from Johan van Oldenbarnevelt, who is by title indeed Supreme Advocate, but in fact a kind of Count or petty-king of Holland; for upon his nod and will almost everything depends. And when I noticed him too, no less than Maurice (who, on account of his innumerable occupations, wrote Your Magnificence brief thanks for the books, ordering me to expound the particulars to you by mouth), inclining to our side, and judging that you should be summoned thither and kindly treated, I added that I could report nothing certain of your will in this part, since I understood you had decided humbly to offer your studies, first of all, to the Emperor; then he soon fell silent, and brought forward nothing more on this — but, as if turning the discourse elsewhere, said that he was on the point of an English journey, where he would act as legate in the States' name, and so could not speak more with me now; [but] when he had returned, if circumstances so required and he were certain of your will, he would accomplish this business with the States.

I also heard in Holland that England is held by a great desire for you — which someone said had been written to you; whether truly or falsely, escapes me. But you will better see what is to be done. For you foresee to how great tumults that Realm is liable, which will first swell when the Queen, almost decrepit, has passed away [Elizabeth I]. You have, if it so pleased, the King of Scotland [James VI] in that great British Island — not only most closely joined to the Danes by affinity, but most loving of you, even from that acquaintance which passed between you, since you magnificently received him, visiting your Island with his noble retinue, and conferred abundantly with him on learned matters (for he is a learned Prince).

These things I had to pre-signify to Your Magnificence — until I myself shall present myself at Wandsbek — so far as is now allowed amid travelling, that you may the more quickly and rightly determine what is to be done hereafter. Farewell, most noble and excellent man, and expect me shortly.

From Amsterdam, the 6th day of July, the year 1598.
Perpetually bound to Your Magnificence,
Franciscus G. Tengnagel, in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Frans (Franz) Tengnagel (Tycho's noble assistant, later son-in-law) → Tycho at Wandsbek — reporting his diplomatic embassy through Germany and the Low Countries (June–July 1598) on the exiled Tycho's behalf. (Complements his Italy leg: the books to Pinelli/Magini/the Doge, brahe-t_16000103001, brahe-t_16000103002, brahe-t_16000124001.)
  • Significance — the patronage "auction" for the exiled Tycho, and his strategic choice of the Emperor. A vivid first-hand record of the courts competing for Tycho in 1598:
  • The Elector of Cologne (Ernst of Bavaria). Met at Aachen/Liège; days of secret audiences; gifts (a golden portrait-medal and a 120-Crown horse). He denounces Denmark's loss of Tycho ("scarcely to be expiated in any age"), and bursts out in German: "Should one let such a man leave the country for so paltry a sum? … Gold one always gets — but such people, not always." He endorses Tycho's plan to offer himself first to the Emperor ("the chief head of Europe"), promising commendatory letters to Rudolf II and to Barvitius, and pledging — if the Emperor faltered — to support Tycho at his own expense. His farewell charge (also in German): to treat Tycho "as my own brother," to send "one of my nobles whom I trust in pyronomic [alchemical] exercises" to confer, and to visit Tycho in person once at the imperial court.
  • Maurice of Nassau & the Dutch States. At The Hague, Maurice (who "knows and esteems" Tycho) received Tengnagel, took Tycho's books as a gift, and offered to propose to the States that Tycho "fix new seats of Urania in Holland" — "no place fitter for your genius." Johan van Oldenbarnevelt (the "petty-king of Holland") inclined the same way — but fell silent on learning Tycho meant to offer himself to the Emperor first, then left on an English embassy.
  • England & Scotland. Rumour that England desires Tycho; but Tengnagel warns of the coming succession tumults at Elizabeth I's death, and points to James VI of Scotland — "most loving of you" — recalling James's 1590 visit to Hven (when Tycho hosted him and "conferred abundantly on learned matters").
  • The strategic upshot. The letter documents that, by mid-1598, Tycho had resolved to offer his studies "first of all to the Emperor" — the choice that led to Benátky/Prague (cf. the exile narratives, brahe-t_15980607001, brahe-t_16000103001).
  • Cross-references. A keystone of the patronage-search / exile thread (pair with the Grynaeus and Pinelli letters); a books-distribution record (gift copies to the Elector of Cologne — cf. the Mechanica list at brahe-t_15980909001 — and to Maurice of Nassau); and the Tengnagel-embassy thread (the Italy leg).
  • Persons & places named. Frans Tengnagel; Ernst of Bavaria, Elector of Cologne; King Frederick II of Denmark (the late); Barvitius; Rudolf II; Maurice of Nassau; Johan van Oldenbarnevelt; Elizabeth I ("the decrepit Queen"); James VI of Scotland (the Hven visitor). Places: Amsterdam, The Hague, Cologne, Aachen, Liège, Limburg, Wandsbek, Hamburg, Holland, England/Scotland.
  • Two German passages (the Elector's outburst and his farewell charge) are reproduced verbatim and translated; pyronomic exercises = alchemy.
  • Calendar. Amstelodamo die 6. Iulij Ao. 1598 — Holland was New Style, but the source carries no stylo marker and the label gives none; dated 6 July 1598 (style left as given; Holland's N.S. noted). Header and label agree.
