The original

NOBILITATE ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTI IUVENI, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE.
S. P. D.

DICERE non possum, Nobilissime et amantissime mi TYCHO, quanta voluptate me affeceris, non saltem eruditissimis literis, ad me nuper datis, sed et contemplatione tua vere Mathematica, de miraculosa illa ad Cassiopeiam stella, quæ ante paucas hebdomadas plane conspici desijt, quam ego quidem Cometam appellare solitus sum, secundum receptam ex ARISTOTELIS schola opinionem, licet ut fatear id, quod res postulat, propriæ meæ observationes, Quadrante illo maximo, ex tuo præscripto hîc fabrefacto, obtente convincant, id, quicquid erat iubaris, non infra Lunam fuisse, sed in altissimo æthere, siquidem nullæ proveniant parallaxes ex meis diligenter institutis observationibus, et tamen non ausus fui dicere, supra Lunam extitisse, ne contra Philosophum, cuius præcipua est autoritas, quippiam affirmarem, qui Cœlum nova non admittere corpora indubitanter statuit. Sed prævaleat autoritati experientia, præsertim cum tu accuratissimis tuis Instrumentis et Observationibus idem, quod ego, deprehenderis: nimirum nullam prorsus adfuisse huic φαινομένῳ parallaxin, veluti etiam satis evidenter in hoc tuo eruditissimo scripto demonstrasti, quod sane longe antecellit omnium aliorum hactenus de eadem stella in publicum editas conceptiones, ita ut vix cum hac conferri mereantur: et optarem quam maxime, me id prius obtinuisse, ut descriptum eius cum doctis hinc Mathematicis, cum duraret adhuc ea stella, præsertim MÆSTHLINO, qui idem tecum sentire videtur, licet non tam evidenter atque dilucide, sed per ambages quasdam demonstret, communicare potuissem. Sed spero, me plura exemplaria a te obtenturum, eademque, uti polliceri videris, auctiora reddita, tum demum alijs ea impertiar. Interea hoc meum, uti rem pretiosam, reservabo, et fratri meo atque WOLFIO nostro ostendam. Quamprimum igitur plura eiuscemodi publicaveris, me eorum facere participem ne supersedeas quæso. Adiunxi hisce observationes meas in hac ipsa stella, et quibusdam alijs, factas eodem Quadrante permagno, tuo instinctu hîc elaborato, quas examinare poteris, et tuam postea mihi de ijs indicare censuram. Plura nunc scribere non vacat, ob negotiorum molem in hoc officio, quod sustineo, publico. Tu itaque vale, optime mi TYCHO, et sæpius me tuis eruditis literis invisere ne omittas.

Iterumque etiam atque etiam vale.
Datæ Augustæ Vindelicorum, Anno 1574 die 10. Martij.
T.
Paulus Haintzel.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the young man pre-eminent in nobility and learning, Master Tycho Brahe — sends heartiest greeting.

I cannot tell you, my most noble and most loving Tycho, with what delight you have filled me — not only by the most learned letter lately sent to me, but also by your truly mathematical reflection on that miraculous star by Cassiopeia, which a few weeks ago entirely ceased to be seen, [the star] which I, for my part, have been accustomed to call a comet, in accordance with the opinion received from the school of Aristotle — although, to confess what the matter requires, my own observations, obtained with that great quadrant built here after your prescription, prove that the brightness, whatever it was, was not below the Moon, but in the highest aether, seeing that no parallaxes whatever arise from the observations I diligently set up; and yet I did not dare to say that it had stood above the Moon, lest I affirm anything against the Philosopher, whose authority is paramount, who lays it down beyond doubt that the heaven admits no new bodies. But let experience prevail over authority, especially since you, with your most accurate instruments and observations, have found the same as I — namely, that there was present to this phenomenon [φαινομένῳ] no parallax at all, just as you have also demonstrated quite evidently in this most learned writing of yours, which indeed far surpasses the conceptions about the same star hitherto published by all others, so that they scarcely deserve to be compared with this one. And I would wish as much as possible that I had obtained it earlier, so that I might have been able to share a description of it with the learned mathematicians hereabouts, while that star yet lasted — especially with Maestlin, who seems to hold the same view as you, although he demonstrates it not so evidently and clearly, but by certain roundabout ways. But I hope that I shall obtain more copies from you, and these — as you seem to promise — rendered fuller; and only then shall I impart them to others. Meanwhile I shall keep this [copy] of mine as a precious thing, and show it to my brother and to our Wolf. As soon, therefore, as you publish more of this kind, do not, I beg, forbear to make me a sharer of them. I have attached to this [letter] my observations made upon this very star, and upon certain others, with the same very great quadrant elaborated here at your prompting, which you will be able to examine, and afterwards to convey to me your judgement upon them. There is no leisure to write more now, on account of the mass of business in this public office which I sustain. So farewell, my excellent Tycho, and do not omit to visit me more often with your learned letters.

And again, again and again, farewell.
Given at Augsburg, in the year 1574, on the 10th day of March.
Yours,
Paul Hainzel.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Paulus Haintzel = Paul Hainzel (1527/8–1581), patrician of Augsburg, town councillor (Stadtpfleger / magistrate — hence hoc officio … publico, "this public office which I sustain") and patron of astronomy. With Tycho during the latter's Augsburg years (1569–70) he built a great quadrant at his suburban estate at Göggingen, the Quadrans maximus repeatedly invoked here. One of the Hainzel brothers of the editorial glossary; the frater meus of this letter is his brother Johann Baptist Hainzel.
  • Recipient / superscription. Domino Tychoni Brahe — Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), then 27 and freshly the author of De nova stella. The salutation S. P. D. = salutem plurimam dicit, "sends heartiest greeting"; the subscription T. (here likely Tuus, "Yours") + signature.
  • Occasion — the writing in hand. hoc tuo eruditissimo scripto / descriptum eius is Tycho's De nova stella (Copenhagen, 1573), his account of the new star (SN 1572) that appeared in Cassiopeia in November 1572. Hainzel acknowledges receiving a copy, praises it above all rival accounts, and asks for further, auctiora ("fuller") copies — Tycho apparently having promised an enlarged treatment.
  • The phenomenon. miraculosa illa ad Cassiopeiam stella … quae ante paucas hebdomadas plane conspici desijt — the nova faded from naked-eye visibility in early 1574, consistent with "ceased to be seen a few weeks ago." Hainzel records that he had habitually called it a comet (Cometam appellare solitus sum), per the Aristotelian doctrine that placed all change in the sublunary realm; his own parallax measurements, however, forced the contrary conclusion.
  • Astronomy — technical terms. parallaxes / parallaxin → parallax; Quadrante illo maximo … permagno → "that great / very great quadrant" (Tycho's instrument-type, here the Göggingen quadrant built ex tuo praescripto / tuo instinctu, "after your prescription / at your prompting"); Instrumenta et Observationes → instruments and observations; infra / supra Lunam … in altissimo aethere → "below / above the Moon … in the highest aether" (the crux of the cosmological dispute: a body with no measurable parallax must lie beyond the Moon, in the supposedly immutable heavens). censura rendered "judgement" (a scholar's verdict on submitted work).
  • Greek. φαινομένῳ (dative sing. of φαινόμενον, phainómenon) = "to this phenomenon." Transcribed as printed and translated; the iota-subscript spelling of the dative is retained. Hainzel uses the technical Greek noun for the celestial appearance rather than committing to "comet" or "star."
  • Philosophus — "the Philosopher," i.e. Aristotle (ARISTOTELIS schola named earlier in the sentence), whose dictum that the heavens admit no new bodies (Coelum nova non admittere corpora) Hainzel is reluctant to contradict openly: praevaleat autoritati experientia, "let experience prevail over authority" — a notable methodological declaration.
  • Persons named. MAeSTHLINO = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), then a young Tübingen mathematician, whose own 1573 tract on the nova likewise argued for its supralunary, parallax-free position — Hainzel notes Maestlin reaches the same view per ambages quasdam ("by certain roundabout ways"). WOLFIO nostro = Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist, "our Wolf." frater meus = Hainzel's brother (Johann Baptist Hainzel).
  • Place / dateline. Datae Augustae Vindelicorum, Anno 1574 die 10. Martij = "Given at Augsburg (Augusta Vindelicorum,), in the year 1574, on the 10th day of March" (O.S.; Denmark and the Empire were on the Julian calendar in 1574, the Gregorian reform being still eight years off). The as-written dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1574.10.marts.Augsburg); no discrepancy.
  • Closing formula. Iterumque etiam atque etiam vale = "And again, again and again, farewell" — an intensified Iterum vale (: Again, farewell), the doubled etiam atque etiam kept as the affectionate redundancy it is, not smoothed.
  • Reading note. id quicquid erat iubaris — iubar (poetic, "radiance, brightness, a heavenly light") is read as a partitive genitive after quicquid: "the brightness, whatever it was." The hedge (quicquid erat, "whatever it was") is preserved rather than resolved to "the star" / "the comet," matching Hainzel's deliberate refusal to pre-judge the object's nature.
