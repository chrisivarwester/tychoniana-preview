The original

GENEROSO TYCHONI
BRAHEO, MATHEMATICO PRINCIPI, COGNATO ET AFFINI
DESIDERATISSIMO.

GENEROSE mi TYCHO, cognate affinis desideratissime, amicorum intime. Iam
inde quo literæ tuæ desideratissimæ pridie Non. Decembris reddebantur, medijs
his turbis nuptialibus, laboriosissimis districtus præpedimentis distineor, ne
quod valde exoptâram, responsum usque huc festinare licuerit. In præsens vero,
ubi liberior aliquanto adspirat aura, rarissimam hanc felicitatem, qua cum
amicis ingenuæ mentis tuto communicare conceditur, flagrantissime cupitam,
adlubentius eo excipio, quo rariores eius census, multivarios inter, ut nosti
ingenia, habeam et æstimem. Utut autem facile sciam nil præter spem, haud multa
præter voluntatem, exuli iam pridem tibi, modo libero, accidisse, doleo nihilo
etiamdum minus, non tui equidem, sed mei ipsius, sed patriæ immerentis, sed
bonorum in patria omnium, quibus integrior mens et animus, caussa, quos paucorum
quorundam livor, alia obtentui sumpta caussa, suo desiderio, sua gloria, suo
privavit amore et gaudio; alias equidem, ut nihil est certe, quod, natura sic
imperante, in rebus caducis apparere solentius
autumem dissimilium inter se haud interrupta unquam contentione et pugna, quam
non abstrusis modo, sensusque latentibus Naturæ laboribus familiarem, sed et
concitatis destinati humanarum rerum dispensatoris Mentis facinoribus pene
domesticam indies ostentari suspicimus, ita et nihil est, cur eundem hunc Naturæ
morem demirarer, si vel maxime hoc ex circo etiam Musæ iunctim omnes recentibus
æmulationibus proscriptæ exularent, tecumque castra moventes, se in universum
hinc, ubi nihil prisci et integri moris, subducerent; magis est vel tandem
experiendo discere, perversam eiuscemodi, non utique alias insolentem, scena hac
tripudiantis invidentiæ, caute suspectare inconstantiam, prudenterque provisis, quæ tempus commonet, subductas sui rationes non stolidis
ac evanidis, ast solidis et ævo dignis firmare munimentis, ne alienis quam
proprijs innixi mage virtutibus, ad aleatorios quosvis fortunæ iactus * cernimus
nutare, quin et concidere penitus eveniat. Tu vero nobis, tu inquam TYCHO generose facem hîc prætulisti, tu
tuo nobis præluxisti exemplo, tu veræ virtutis ipsi sibi ex asse undique
sufficientis, vestigia posuisti, quibus insistere, quis ad hanc metam adspirans
non adniteretur? Ipse ego certe (qui licet illustri huic consessui accersiri non
merear, in spem tamen secundam aut tertiam saltem acciri indies adnitor) multa
nec levia in me malevolentiæ indicia, utut premantur, erumpere sentisco, ast σὺν
δαίμονι ἐσϑλῷ quod spero incassum, nec enim ad hosce ventus ego mea distendi
vela, neque enim importuoso isti me commiserim vado. Sed de me coram ipse
præsens, ne adfectu et præcipitantia, quorum caussas procul habeo, imprudens
nimis in literas intutas, relatrices, rationum mearum secreta conijcere videar,
quæ non aliter constant, quam si nulli, nisi alteri mihi reddantur. Ad diem
itaque anni adproperantis quintum decimum, favente Numine, aliud vitæ meæ
facturus initium, tædiosissimo huic renunciaturus ergastulo, rogationi tuæ
amicissimæ vel non rogatus sponte adsentiri iam pridem est animus, nec vero ad
ullius unquam æmulationes et odia me in his, quæ recta sunt, comparaturus sum
unquam. Et vero quid mage dedeceret, te, quem non affinitate modo, sed et
sanguine, sed et necessitudinis vinculis omnibus peculiari sorte, iure primum
æstimo, colo et observo, te inquam post tot benevolentissimi animi in immerentem
indicia, insalutatum præterire? Age ergo, num circa dictum tempus sat commodus
tibi meus videatur accessus, litera per hunc tabellarium
adsignifices una aut altera; faxo ego hîc interim, ne, quod in me desideres,
præter vires forte, siet quidquam; alia præsenti adloquio reservanda, quo et si
quæ nova sunt, quæ referre interest, relata velim, nisi quod hoc unicum in
præsens referre intersit, communem nostrum CRAGIUM in Poloniam brevi
ablegandum, ad 1. Ianuarij litteris huc esse
advocatum, quem, itinere per Holsatiam
sumpto, te invisurum nullus dubito, cui et quo me adiungere queam, modo non
festinantior abierit, enixe studebo. Ego vero si non studio me continerem, finem
vix habiturum esset hoc meum per litteras adloquium, ut et potiora, imo vero,
quod ego sentio potissima, Epistolij tui capita commodiori opportunitati lubens
reservem. Ludant interea meæ Musæ, qua valent, ad hæc; aperte equidem, ast suo
sub velo. Interea te præpotenti rerum nostrarum gubernatori, Trinuno Numini, cum
universa tua familia, consilijs et actionibus, commendo. Vale felicissime.

Te nulli secundus integerrimo colens animo

OLIGERUS
ROSÆCRANTZ.
Perscribebam

Haderslebiæ XVI Decemb.
CIↃ CI
IIIC.

Salutari te suo nomine officiosissime voluit IACOBUS ULFELDIUS, quem te
sincere etiam maioribus renitentibus amare et suspicere scias.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the noble Tycho Brahe, prince of mathematicians, kinsman and most longed-for relative by marriage.

My noble Tycho, most longed-for kinsman and relative, most intimate of friends. Ever since your most-longed-for letter was delivered on the day before the Nones of December [4 December], I have been held fast, hemmed in by these wedding tumults and their most laborious encumbrances, so that I have not been able to hasten until now the reply I had so greatly desired. But at present, when a somewhat freer breeze breathes upon me, I take up the more gladly this rarest happiness — so ardently desired, the happiness of being permitted to share safely with friends of a free and honest mind — the more so since I hold and prize its returns as the rarer, amid temperaments as varied as you know them to be. But although I readily know that nothing beyond [your] hope, and not much beyond [your] wish, has befallen you — long since an exile, now a free man — I grieve nonetheless not at all the less, not on your account indeed, but on my own, but on that of an undeserving fatherland, but on that of all good men in the fatherland, men of a sounder mind and spirit; for the spite of certain few, taking up some other cause for a pretext, has robbed [the fatherland] of its desire for [you], of its glory in [you], of its love and joy in [you]. For the rest, indeed — just as there is surely nothing which, nature so ordaining, I would judge to appear more habitually in transient things than the never-once-interrupted strife and contention of unlike things among themselves, a strife we see displayed daily not only as familiar to the hidden, sense-eluding labours of Nature, but as almost domestic in the violent doings of the appointed Mind that dispenses human affairs — so too there is no reason why I should marvel at this same way of Nature, even if to the very utmost [it should come to pass that] the Muses, all together, proscribed by these recent rivalries, should likewise go into exile out of this arena, and, striking camp along with you, should withdraw from here altogether, where nothing of the ancient and uncorrupted way remains. Rather is it better, at last and by experience, to learn to be cautiously wary of so perverse an inconstancy — not, in truth, otherwise unwonted [than] on this stage of capering envy — and, with prudent provision for what the time admonishes, to fortify one's withdrawn resources with bulwarks not foolish and fleeting but solid and worthy of the age; lest, leaning more on others' virtues than on our own, we be seen to totter at any chance throws of fortune's dice — nay, that it even come about that we collapse utterly. But you indeed — you, I say, noble Tycho — have carried the torch before us here; you have lit our way by your own example; you have set down the footprints of true virtue, sufficient in full to itself on every side, which footprints — what man aspiring to this goal would not strive to tread? I myself, to be sure (who, though I may not deserve to be summoned to this illustrious assembly, yet strive daily to be called at least into the second rank, or the third), feel that many and no light tokens of ill-will against me are bursting forth, however they be suppressed; but, with a kindly genius* [σὺν δαίμονι ἐσϑλῷ], I hope it is in vain — for I have not spread my sails to these winds, nor would I have entrusted myself to that harbourless shoal. But of myself [I shall speak] in person, present face to face, lest, through emotion and rashness — the grounds of which I keep far off — I should seem too imprudently to cast the secrets of my counsels into unsafe, tale-bearing letters, secrets which hold good no otherwise than if they be rendered up to none but a second self. And so, on the fifteenth day of the year now hastening on, the Deity favouring, being about to make another beginning of my life, about to renounce this most wearisome house of toil, it has long been my mind to assent — even unasked — of my own accord to your most friendly request; nor shall I ever, in those things that are upright, set myself against anyone's rivalries and hatreds. And truly, what would be more unbecoming than for me to pass you by unsaluted — you whom, by a singular lot, I esteem, honour, and regard as first by right, [bound to me] not by affinity only, but by blood, and by every bond of intimacy — you, I say, after so many tokens of a most kindly disposition shown toward one who did not deserve them? Come then: signify to me by one line or another, through this courier, whether about the said time my coming seems sufficiently convenient to you; I shall see to it here meanwhile that there be nothing, of what you might desire in me, [lacking] beyond my powers perhaps; other matters [are] to be reserved for conversation in person, in which I should also wish reported whatever there is of news worth relating — save that this one thing it is worth relating at present: that our common friend Cragius, soon to be dispatched into Poland, has been summoned hither by letters for the first of January, and that he, taking his route through Holstein, will visit you, I do not doubt at all; to whom, and whither, I shall earnestly endeavour to attach myself, provided he has not departed too hastily. But truly, did I not restrain myself on purpose, this my converse with you by letter would scarcely have an end; so that I gladly reserve for a more convenient opportunity the weightier — nay rather, what I deem the weightiest — heads of your little letter. Meanwhile let my Muses sport, as best they can, at these matters; openly indeed, yet under their own veil. Meanwhile I commend you, with your whole household, your counsels and your actions, to the all-powerful governor of our affairs, the Triune Deity. Fare most happily.

Honouring you with a most upright mind, second to none,

Holger Rosenkrantz.
Written out
at Haderslev, 16 December
1597. Jacob Ulfeldt wished most dutifully to send you greeting in his own name — one whom you may know sincerely loves and looks up to you, even with his elders opposing [it].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Oligerus Rosaecrantz = Holger Rosenkrantz — addressed, and signing, as Tycho's cognatus et affinis ("kinsman and relative by marriage"). Tycho's reply of ten days later (Hamburg, 26 December 1597; Opera Omnia) speaks of Cragius noster in the same terms, confirming the pairing. (The signatory is one of the several contemporaneous Holger Rosenkrantzes of the high Danish nobility; the writer here is a younger kinsman about to "make another beginning" of his life, not the elder statesman "the Rich.")
  • Address / titulature. MATHEMATICO PRINCIPI — "prince of mathematicians," an honorific (not an office), rendered literally. Generose … cognate affinis desideratissime, amicorum intime: the piled-up vocatives of high epistolary courtesy are kept.
  • "exuli iam pridem tibi, modo libero." "you, long since an exile, now a free man." Tycho had left Hven and Denmark in 1597; the letter's whole consolatory frame turns on his self-imposed exilium and on the livor ("spite, envy") of "certain few" at court who, alia obtentui sumpta caussa ("having taken up some other cause for a pretext"), drove him out. Rosenkrantz's modo libero ("now free") and the praise of Tycho as having "carried the torch before us" cast the departure as liberation, not defeat — the author's framing is preserved, not editorialised.
  • Greek. σὺν δαίμονι ἐσϑλῷ — transcribed as printed (with the medial ϑ form of theta); "with a kindly genius / good spirit," i.e. "with good fortune / God willing." A learned tag (cf. the Homeric/proverbial σὺν δαίμονι); transliterated and glossed, not flattened.
  • The asterisk (iactus ). An editorial mark stands in the source after fortunae iactus ("fortune's dice"). It is reproduced verbatim in the Source block; its referent (a footnote or crux* sign in the copy/edition) is not recoverable from the text and is not silently deleted. The clause is translated as it stands; the mark is flagged here.
  • "Ad diem … anni adproperantis quintum decimum." Rendered "on the fifteenth day of the year now hastening on" — i.e. about 15 January [1598], the date Rosenkrantz sets for the "new beginning" of his life and his proposed visit; non rogatus … adsentiri … animus ("my mind is to assent even unasked") answers a request (rogatio) Tycho had made in the letter Rosenkrantz received on 4 December. Crux flagged: the syntax compresses date, intention, and the renunciation of the ergastulum into one clause; the reading "fifteenth day of the [coming] year" is the natural one but is not beyond doubt.
  • taediosissimo huic … ergastulo — "this most wearisome house of toil"; ergastulum (literally a slaves' workhouse/prison) used metaphorically of Rosenkrantz's present situation, exactly as Wolf used it of his own life (cf. brahe-t_15711113001). Rendered consistently "house of toil."
  • Cragius = Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, c. 1550–1602), Danish humanist, historian, and diplomat, soon to be dispatched into Poland (in Poloniam brevi ablegandum) — a reference to his 1598 embassy. itinere per Holsatiam sumpto — "taking his route through Holstein," i.e. past Tycho's then station in the Hamburg/Holstein region. Tycho's 26 December reply (Utinam Cragius noster tecum sit vel praecedat) shows the planned rendez-vous was real.
  • Epistolij tui capita — "the heads [chief points] of your little letter" (epistolium, diminutive of epistola); Rosenkrantz defers reply to Tycho's weightier points until they meet.
  • Ludant … meae Musae … suo sub velo — "let my Muses sport … under their own veil," i.e. he will express more (perhaps in verse) but veiled; the figure is kept.
  • Trinuno Numini — "to the Triune Deity," the closing commendation to the Holy Trinity; praepotenti rerum nostrarum gubernatori ("the all-powerful governor of our affairs") in apposition.
  • Place. Haderslebiae = Haderslev (Hadersleben), in Schleswig; Perscribebam ("I was writing out / I write out," epistolary imperfect) kept.
  • Dateline & calendar. XVI Decemb. CIↃ CI IIIC = "16 December 1597." The numeral is the apostrophus form: CIↃ = 1000, CI (= D) = 500, IIIC = 97 (the reversed-C "97" idiom), giving M D 97 = 1597; O.S. (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). This matches the catalogue label (1597-12-16); no date discrepancy. pridie Non. Decembris in the body = "the day before the Nones of December" = 4 December (Nones of December = the 5th), glossed in the translation by the edition's convention
  • Register / style note. Rosenkrantz writes in a deliberately mannered, hyper-periodic late-humanist Latin: very long suspended periods, heavy hyperbaton, accumulated comparatives and rare adverbs (solentius, adlubentius, sentisco). The translation keeps the periodic architecture rather than chopping it into modern short sentences, at some cost in fluency — faithful to the author's evident self-display.
  • Names Jacob Ulfeldt as a covert admirer of Tycho. The postscript reports that the Danish nobleman Jacob Ulfeldt (til Ulfeldtsholm) asked to be remembered to Tycho, and states that Ulfeldt sincerely loves and looks up to him even though his elders/family oppose it ('etiam maioribus renitentibus') — a telling detail for late 1597, when much of the Danish nobility had already turned against Tycho.
  • Corroboration. A genuine prior friendship is independently attested: Tycho addressed one of his own occasional Latin poems directly to 'Jacob Ulfeldt til Ulfeldtsholm' ('TIL MIN GODE VEN... HR. JACOB ULFELDT TIL ULFELDTSHOLM' — 'To my good friend... Lord Jacob Ulfeldt of Ulfeldtsholm'), per Grinder-Hansen (ed.), Tycho Brahes Verden (Nationalmuseet, 2006). The 'elders resisting' detail is new information not otherwise recorded in that source.
