The original

NOBILISSIMO ET VIRTUTE AC ERUDITIONE VIRO INPRIMIS CONSPICUO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGVINEO, AFFINI, ET AMICO SUO INTEGERRIMO.

S. P.

CHARISSIME OLIGERE, scripsi tibi semel atque iterum; nihil tamen adhuc responsi accepi. Quod nihilominus occupationibus propter nuptias tuas tribuo. Expectavit te amanuensis meus Hafniæ per triduum, sed cum interea pro ut constitueras, non accederes, forte vento præpeditus, is noluit diuturniores isthic moras nectere, sciens, te facile per alium Hafnia Lubecam versus literas posse dirigere. At cum ne hoc quidem factum sit, rogo, velis mihi nunc absque diuturniore procrastinatione ad singula, quæ priores meæ literæ continebant, respondere, et eas Lubecam mittere, si expeditior non datur ratio. Nihil iam adeo remoratur iter meum ac currus, quem promisisti, quem scio Arhusiæ paratum navem expectare, ut Lubecam transvehatur. Quod et Scotus ille (licet et mihi id promiserit) et quidam alij neglexerunt, cum nemo in tua absentia adesset, qui negotium serio urgeret. Fac, ut eum quam citissime Lubecæ habeam, vel saltem indica, cur id fieri nequeat, ne frustra illum diutius expectem, sed potius de alio mihi provideam. Instat enim hyems, nec peregrinationem diutius differre commodum est. Quæ Coloniensis mea de causa Imperatori scripsit, puto te accepisse. Quin et illustrissimus Dux Megapolensis ULDARICUS pariter ad Imperatorem meo nomine commendatitias dedit, quarum hîc Exemplum habes. Quorsum eæ mihi usui esse possint, facile intelligis. Serva id tibi soli; neque nisi fidelibus amicis (si qui sunt) inspiciendum concede; uti nec priora. Addidi alterum exemplar versuum BRASCHIJ Poëtæ Rostochiensis, quod rectius est castigatum atque revisum. Cupio mihi remitti Carmen Cramerianum, quod tamen lima indiget; et de singulis alias, quorum mea interesse putaveris, admoneri, tum quoque de libris, an Regi oblati sint, quidque responderit, et an tibi postea cesserint nec ne. Plura nunc non vacat. Sed has saltem ad te deproperare lubuit, nactus tam commodam opportunitatem per hunc Studiosum Cimbrum hac ad vos transeuntem.

Vale, salutata a me tua matre et coniuge, mea nepte, quam diligentissime.
Raptim Wandesburgo 5. Septembris Anno 1598.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man, especially conspicuous in virtue and erudition, Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, his kinsman by blood, kinsman by marriage, and most upright friend.

Heartiest greeting.

Dearest Holger, I have written to you once and again; yet I have so far received no reply. Which nonetheless I set down to your occupations on account of your wedding. My amanuensis waited for you at Copenhagen for three days; but when in the meantime you did not come, as you had arranged — perhaps held back by the wind — he was unwilling to weave longer delays there, knowing that you could easily direct letters from Copenhagen toward Lübeck through another. But since not even this has been done, I ask you: be willing now, without further procrastination, to answer me on each of the matters my earlier letters contained, and to send them to Lübeck, if no more expeditious means presents itself. Nothing now so holds up my journey as the wagon you promised — which I know is ready at Aarhus, awaiting a ship to be carried across to Lübeck. Both that Scot (although he too promised it to me) and certain others have neglected this, since no one was at hand, in your absence, to press the business seriously. See to it that I have it at Lübeck as quickly as possible, or at least indicate why it cannot be done, lest I wait for it longer in vain, and rather provide myself with another [conveyance]. For winter is at hand, and it is not convenient to defer the journey longer. What the [Archbishop] of Cologne wrote to the Emperor on my behalf, I think you have received. Indeed the most illustrious Duke of Mecklenburg, Ulrich, has likewise given commendatory letters to the Emperor in my name, a copy of which you have here. To what use these can be to me, you easily understand. Keep it to yourself alone, and allow it to be seen by none but faithful friends (if any there be) — as also the earlier [papers]. I have added another copy of the verses of Brasch, the Rostock poet, which is more correctly amended and revised. I should like the Cramer poem sent back to me — it still needs the file [of polishing]; and to be advised on particular matters elsewhere, in which you judge my interest lies; then too about the books — whether they have been presented to the King, and what he replied, and whether they were afterward granted to you or not. No time for more now. But I was glad at least to hurry these to you, having got so convenient an opportunity through this Cimbrian student passing this way to you.

Farewell — your mother, and your wife, my niece, being most diligently greeted from me.
In haste, from Wandsbek, 5 September, in the year 1598.
Your Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender & moment. Tycho Brahe at Wandsbek (Wandesburgum, near Hamburg), in the months after his 1597 departure from Denmark — actively courting imperial patronage and preparing the onward journey that would lead, via Wittenberg, to Bohemia. The letter is a logistics-and-patronage dispatch to his closest Danish kinsman-ally.
  • Recipient. Oligerus Rosaekrantzius de Rosenholm = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), lord of Rosenholm, addressed as consanguineus, affinis et amicus — Tycho's kinsman by blood and by marriage and his trusted agent in Denmark. His silence is excused by his recent wedding (nuptiae tuae): he had married Sophie Brahe (1593), here named as coniunx … mea neptis, "your wife, my niece" — establishing the affinity. (Holger is the Oligerus whose news recurs in the 1600 letters, e.g. brahe-t_16000420001, brahe-t_16000813001.)
  • The logistics. Tycho is stranded for want of a wagon (currus) Holger had promised, "ready at Aarhus awaiting a ship" to be carried across to Lübeck; unnamed agents — "that Scot" among them — have let it lapse. With winter coming he presses for the wagon or a clear refusal, so he can arrange other transport. Correspondence is to be routed Copenhagen → Lübeck.
  • The patronage papers.
  • Quae Coloniensis … Imperatori scripsit = the letter the Elector-Archbishop of Cologne (Ernst of Bavaria) wrote to the Emperor (Rudolf II) on Tycho's behalf.
  • illustrissimus Dux Megapolensis Uldaricus = Ulrich, Duke of Mecklenburg (Megapolis = Mecklenburg), who likewise gave commendatory letters to the Emperor in Tycho's name — a copy enclosed. (The same Duke Ulrich figures, two years later, in the debt thread, brahe-t_16010809001.) Tycho enjoins strict confidentiality: show only to "faithful friends, if any there be."
  • versus Braschij Poëtae Rostochiensis = verses by Brasch, the Rostock poet (a revised copy enclosed); Carmen Cramerianum = a poem by Cramer, which Tycho wants returned for further polishing (lima indiget, "needs the file" — the Horatian limae labor). de libris, an Regi oblati sint = books presented (or to be presented) to the King of Denmark (Christian IV).
  • Courier. Studiosus Cimbrus = a Jutland student (Cimber = Cimbrian/Jutlander) passing through to Denmark, the bearer of this letter.
  • Witness note. The text survives as an afskrift (copy) in Langebek's Diplomatarium at the Copenhagen Rigsarkiv, not as the autograph original.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting"); Vale → "Farewell"; Raptim → "in haste"; manu propria → "with his own hand."
  • Calendar. Dateline 5. Septembris Anno 1598 at Wandsbek; no style marker. Wandsbek/Holstein and Denmark were on the Julian (Old Style) calendar in 1598; reproduced as written, not converted.
