The original

... MAGINVS per totam ferme æstatem hîc PATAVII et VENETIIS moratus est. Quâ de caussâ, non satis constat.

Interea GALLILÆVS de GALLILÆIS FLORENTINVS Professionem Mathematicam hîc adeptus est, qui suarum lectionum septimo Decembris initium fecit. Exordium erat splendidum in magnâ auditorum frequentiâ. A Dno. PINELLO is liberaliter commendatur, quem, si posset, perlibenter in Dni. TYCHONIS amicitiam insinuaret. Tu, qui animum TYCHONIS novisti, poteris, quod ex re erit, in hisce disponere. MAGINVS êdidit nuper librum, cui Titulum fecit [Tabula Tetragonica] sub TYCHONIS patrocinio. Exemplar mihi ad te mittendum dedit, quod primâ occasione transmittam. Retulit etiam, Illustrissimos VENETOS in consilio rogatorum deliberasse, ut aliquis Matheseως peritus stipendio 300. Coronatorum in ÆGYPTVM ablegaretur, qui pro TYCHONE isthic observaret. Tantæ enim hîc TYCHO certe est celebritatis, quantæ nemo eorum, qui nunc vivunt.

Datæ Patavii 28. Decembris Anni 1592.

English translation machine translation, Secure

... Maginus stayed here, at Padua and at Venice, for almost the whole summer. For what reason, it is not sufficiently clear.

Meanwhile Galileo de' Galilei of Florence has obtained the Chair of Mathematics here, who made the beginning of his lectures on the seventh of December. The opening was splendid, before a great throng of hearers. He is generously recommended by Master Pinelli, who, if he could, would most gladly insinuate him into the friendship of Master Tycho. You, who have come to know Tycho's disposition, will be able to arrange matters in this affair as shall be fitting. Maginus has lately published a book, to which he gave the title [Tabula Tetragonica], under Tycho's patronage. He gave me a copy to be sent on to you, which I shall forward at the first opportunity. He reported, too, that the Most Illustrious Venetians had deliberated in the Council of the rogati that some man skilled in mathematics [Matheseōs] should be dispatched to Egypt at a salary of 300 coronati, to observe there on Tycho's behalf. For so great, assuredly, is Tycho's renown here as belongs to none of those who are now living.

Given at Padua, the 28th of December, the year 1592.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent / addressee. Both are unidentified in the catalogue: an Unknown doctor of medicine writing from Padua to an Unknown Danish student who is in personal contact with Tycho ("you, who have come to know Tycho's disposition"). The writer is evidently a member of the Paduan academic-medical orbit reporting Italian astronomical news northward; the addressee functions as an intermediary to Tycho. No firmer identification is asserted.
  • Fragment. The leading ellipsis ("...") is in the source: this is an excerpt, the closing portion of a longer letter; only the final movement and dateline survive in the edition. Translated as the partial text it is.
  • Maginus = Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), professor of mathematics at Bologna and a correspondent of Tycho's. The librum cui Titulum fecit Tabula Tetragonica is Magini's Tabula tetragonica (Venice, 1592), a table of squares for computation; that it appeared sub Tychonis patrocinio ("under Tycho's patronage") is confirmed by the surrounding scholarship.
  • Galileo de' Galilei of Florence = Galileo Galilei (1564–1642). He took up the chair of mathematics at the University of Padua in late 1592; the letter's notice that he initium fecit septimo Decembris ("made the beginning [of his lectures] on the seventh of December") is a near-contemporary witness to the start of his Paduan inaugural — here said to have drawn magna auditorum frequentia ("a great throng of hearers").
  • Pinellus = Gian Vincenzo Pinelli (1535–1601), the Paduan humanist whose library and circle were the hub of Veneto learning; he is reported as eager to bring Galileo into Tycho's friendship. insinuaret ("would insinuate / introduce") kept in its period sense of effecting an introduction, not pejorative.
  • Greek. Matheseως — the Greek genitive Mathéseōs ("of mathematics / of the science"), the Greek ending -εως grafted onto the Greek stem within an otherwise Latin sentence (aliquis Matheseōs peritus = "someone skilled in mathematics"). Transcribed as printed and rendered; the code-switch is flagged, not silently Latinised to Matheseos.
  • consilio rogatorum — the Venetian Council (Consiglio) of the Rogati, i.e. the Senate (the Pregadi); the senators were the rogati ("those summoned/invited"). Rendered "the Council of the rogati" with the technical Venetian term kept.
  • Coronatorum — coronati, the gold scudo/corona coin; "300 coronati" kept as the named coin, not converted.
  • in Aegyptum ablegaretur … qui pro Tychone isthic observaret — "should be dispatched to Egypt … to observe there on Tycho's behalf." The notice of a proposed Venetian-funded astronomical mission to Egypt observing pro Tychone is the letter's testimony to Tycho's Italian celebrity (Tantae … celebritatis, quantae nemo eorum qui nunc vivunt — "of such renown as belongs to none now living").
  • Reading note. The working transcription reads isthic ("here/there"); the Dreyer Opera Omnia page image (vol. VI) prints istinc ("from there / thence," idiomatically "there"). Both yield "observe there"; the translation follows the sense common to both. The bracketed [Tabula Tetragonica] reproduces the editor's square brackets in the source.
  • Edition of record. The source's edition field names the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica; the letter is, however, an epistola printed in Dreyer's Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the Epistolae volumes), and the matching page image is in vol. VI. The field is reported as given and the actual printed locus noted; no MS witness is recorded in the edition.
  • Calendar / place crux. Dateline: Datae Patavii 28. Decembris Anni 1592 = "Given at Padua, 28 December 1592." The catalogue label reads "1592. 28. december n.s. Bologna." The dateline says Padua, not Bologna; the header therefore leads with Padua and notes the catalogued place. The n.s. of the label is consistent with a writer in the Veneto (Catholic territory, on the Gregorian calendar since 1582); the date is preserved as written and tagged New Style — not converted. The 7 December lecture-date is likewise N.S. as written.
