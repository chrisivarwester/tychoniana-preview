The original

AMPLISSIMO ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO DOMINO HENRICO RANZOVIO DE BREDENBERGA, SERENISSIMI REGIS DANIÆ IN DUCATIBUS HOLSATIÆ ET SCHLESWIGÆ VICARIO ILLUSTRI, AFFINI AC AMICO SUO INPRIMIS COLENDO.

AMPLISSIME et nobilissime vir, affinis et amice honorande, binas iamnunc interea, dum hîc Hafniæ paulisper commoror, a te recepi literas, quarum priores te ea, quæ per tabellarium tuum remisi, sat perfecta accepisse, atque hinc inde, prout destinata erant, per certos internuncios dirigi curasse innuerunt. Quod et lubenter accipio, tibique hoc nomine gratias ago. Et quam de correctione quorundam verborum in diplomate Hispanico licentiam impetrandam amice polliceris, brevi expecto. Alteræ, quibus exemplum literarum Reverendiss. et Illustriss. Domini ERNESTI Archiepiscopi Coloniensis et Electoris Imperij adiunctum erat, de quibusdam cum dicto Illustriss. Principe iuxta ipsius petitionem in utraque Astronomia, tam superiori quam inferiori (sic enim nobis loqui lubet) communicandis me adhortantur. Quin et ulterius ex ipsius Celsitudinis ad te datis literis non sine animi oblectatione percipio, ipsam non saltem Mathematicis, sed et Pyronomicis exercitijs (quæ etiam nostra est voluptas) adeo impense delectari, ut hæc propriâ industriâ proprijsque manibus tractare, inter alias Reipub. procurandæ occupationes, non degravetur. In utrisque igitur cum ipsius Celsit, ingenue conferre et continuatam servare correspondentiam (uti vocant) nequaquam tergiversabor. Agoque ipsius Celsit, reverenter gratias, quod me tam clementer ad hanc mutuam communicationem invitet. Ubi autem is, quem ad me hoc nomine ablegare decrevit, huc accesserit, et ea, de quibus ipsius Celsit. mecum agere constituit, speciatim indicaverit, libere et lubenter ad illa, quantum in me erit, respondebo. In Mathematicis et Astronomicis satis aperte, quatenus ea ante operum nostrorum plenariam editionem ulli patefacere commodum fuerit, in Spagiricis vero tecte, ne, si ea in aliorum pervenirent manus, indigne quis ijs abuti possit. Satius itaque iudico, aut per circumlocutiones quasdam arcanas, aut mediante alphabeto quodam peculiari, nobis solis invicem cognito id præstari, vel quacumque aliâ ratione. Qua de re ipsemet Illust. Princeps, prout voluerit, constituat, et in literis, quas se mihi cum suo illo nuntio brevi perscripturum pollicetur, quibus medijs atque indicijs id fieri volet, indicet, et si alphabetum quoddam secretum vel transpositionem literarum usitatarum elegerit, id qualecunque erit, a se ipso tamen non alijs excogitatum et absque ulla propalatione suapte manu in schedâ conscribat, literisque actutum includat, et mox ipsemet consignet. Idem ego vicissim facturus sum, omniaque ut utrinque in silentio arcano contineantur, necessum et admodum erit consultum. Libenter intellexi ipsius Celsit. ultra 22 annos huic eximio et arcano studio Spagirico solerter impendisse, in quo et ego 28 iam annos inde ab ætatis anno 22 versatus sum, quemadmodum 16imo Astronomicis assuescere incepi. Scripsissem hoc tempore ipsius Celsit. atque in ijs, quæ exoptat, sponte prævenissem, si non modo hîc Haffniæ varijs curis et occupationibus distraherer, dum nonnullam partem ex mea supellectile unâ cum familia huc transferre satago, aliquandiu hîc certis de causis commoraturus. Quin et Instrumenta Astronomica ex meis nonnulla hîc in turri quadam nostris ædibus vicinâ admodumque observationibus accommodâ disponere animus est, unâque laboratorium quoddam Spagiricum hîc adornare, cum quodam insuper Typographeo, quibus omnibus exequendis nunc intentus sum. Constitui insuper hodie V. D. in Insulam nostram traijcere, ut plura ex meis huc transvehi facerem, et cætera, quæ istic relinquo, disponam, interea dum ea, quæ hîc fieri demandavi, absolvantur, adeo ut animus iam in multas distractus partes, scriptioni tam sublimium et secretarum rerum ad tantum præsertim Principem, commode vacare vix queat. Fiet id nihilominus, quamprimum ipsius huc adventârit minister. Tunc enim me opportuniori quieti restitutum fore confido, eritque id tum non saltem commodius, sed et tutius propter caussas aliquatenus indicatas. Quod autem ipsius Cels. in suis ad te datis literis scribit de Privilegio Hispanico ob id retardato, quod vererentur, ne sub specie rerum Mathematicarum religionis negotia, quæ nunc varie trahuntur, tractarem, id caret periculo. Ego enim me illis immiscere nolo. Satis habemus hæresium et confusionum, non opus est, nec mearum partium, aut novas condere, aut priores stabilire. Misereatur nostri cœli et terræ autor, qui universaliter omnia digerit et prospicit, nec cum præiudicio, uti homines, rem discernit. Interim pro hoc ipso, quod obtinui, Privilegio tam clementer impetrato ipsius Celsit. summas demisse habeo gratias. Apprime etiam mihi gratum est, quod ipsius Celsit. primum tomum Epistolarum de Astronomicis per te illi communicatum tam diligenter perlegere inter tot alias curas non supersederit, quodque ipsi tam illustri et in his rebus exercitato atque iudicioso Principi eius contenta admodum placuerint, non possum non mihi ipsi congratulari. Reliquos tomos nunc typis adorno. Inprimis autem Progymnasmatum nostrorum in Astronomiâ instauranda volumen primum atque alterum, typographico labore absolvere et mature publici iuris facere enixe laboro. Quæ ubi absoluta fuerint, quod propediem futurum confido, ipsius Gestit.ni tum quoque tibi eorundem quædam exemplaria transmittam.

Miratus Illustriss. ille ERNESTUS in dictis ad te literis, quod præstantissimi illius Mathematici CHRISTOPHORI CLAVIJ Bambergensis in transmissis epistolis Astronomicis nullam faciam mentionem, cum is ob tot præclara in Mathematicis edita opera celebris existat; atque hanc nominis ipsius intermissionem, ob locorum nimiam intercapedinem, quodque scripta illius Romæ utplurimum impressa ad nos forte perlata non sint, clementer et candide excusat. Atqui ego omnia eruditissimi illius CLAVIJ opera fortassis non vidi, ea nihilominus quæ in EUCLIDEM ingeniose et in IOHANNEM DE SACROBUSCHO luculenter, et de Gnomonicis copiose conscripsit, in promptu habeo, et iamdudum introspexi, tum quoque Apologiam Calendarij novi contra MÆSTHLINUM, in qua is mei alicubi mentionem facit, observationem nostram æquinoctij verni, anni ni fallor 76 allegando, ut probet ex ea recte factum, quod decendij ablatio in Calendario Gregoriano admissa sit, quod et convenienter se habet, licet summa in his præcisio non sit animadversa, quæ etiam forte in tali negotio, quoad Ecclesiasticum et Politicum usum esse poterit ociosa, quemadmodum MÆSTHLINO, qui scrupulositatem calculi Prutenici urget, non citra causam subinde obijcit CLAVIUS. Verum si liceat id quod res est affirmare, uterque tam CLAVIUS quam MÆSTHLINUS de cœlestibus, cœlo, quod horologium est temporis, inconsulto sententiam protulit. CLAVIUS enim vix ullam observationem cœlestem, nisi eam unicam, de qua dixi, e nostris petitam, quæ sola tam arduo negotio non sufficit, in medium protulit, nullasque Lunarium motionum, quæ magis intricatæ sunt, quam hactenus creditum est, denotationes, prout operæprecium fuit, in consilium adducit. MÆSTHLINUS nullam plane, quod sciam, e cœlestibus assumpsit probationem. Et si id ex suis observatis fecisset, nihilo plus fidei mereretur. Quæ enim ipsius (si quas absolvit) in correctione motus Solis et Lunæ animadversiones produnt, frustranea sunt, uti consignatio Eclipsium ipsius Ephemeridibus præfixa, cœlesti harmoniæ non magis quam antea, si modo non minus congrua, liquido patefecit. Inhæret enim is nimium fidenter calculo Prutenico, et quantitati annuæ inde desumptæ, quæ nequaquam talem sortitur inæqualitatem, prout magnus ille alias COPERNICUS voluit; sed propius ad Alphonsinam accedit supputationem, licet ne hanc quidem satis scrupulose attingat, veluti hæc a nobis convenientiore loco reserantur. Non igitur tanta disconvenientia est in Calendario neoterico a cœlesti norma, prout voluit MÆSTHLINUS, quicunque tandem eius autor existat, sive hic CLAVIUS, sive ALOYSIUS ille, de quo pontifex in proœmio publicationis eius mentionem facit. Licet vero huius excellentissimi Mathematici, CLAVIJ videlicet in Epistolis hisce non meminerim, quod nulla fuerit istic occasio id faciendi, præsertim cum is Astronomica ex ipso cœlo non tractârit, de quibus utplurimum ibi agitur, tamen circa finem primi nostri Tomi Progymnasmatum antedictorum, cum ea, quæ de Nova Stella in ultimâ editione Commentariorum Sphæricorum attexuit, sub disquisitionem libertate Philosophicâ voco, eius luculentam et honorificam, prout præcipua eius in his artibus doctrina meretur, mentionem facio. Si quæ præterea ex ipsius Minervâ Mathematicæ lucubrationes in lucem prodierunt, vel adhuc prodituræ sunt, cuperem eas meis sumptibus mecum communicari. Porro si is mecum per literas correspondentiam habere atque de his excellentibus studijs, quibus utrimque dediti, iucunde conferre voluerit, erit mihi idipsum longe gratissimum, præsertim si autoritate et suasu tanti Principis, uti is in literis innuit, id fiat. Addet enim idipsum nobis calcar, eritque utrique nostrum eo honorificentius. Hæc si dicto Illustrissimo Principi cum submissa ex me salutatione per literas indicaveris, recte feceris et me tibi eo devinctiorem reddideris.

Vale, et prolixitati huic ignosce.
Dabantur Haffniæ die 22. Febr. Anno 1597.
Tycho Brahe,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most noble man, Lord Heinrich Rantzau of Breitenburg, illustrious viceroy of the Most Serene King of Denmark in the duchies of Holstein and Schleswig, his kinsman and friend to be honoured above all.

Most distinguished and most noble man, kinsman and honoured friend: by now, meanwhile, while I tarry here a little at Copenhagen, I have received two letters from you. The earlier of them gave to understand that you had received those things which I sent back by your courier sufficiently intact, and that you had had them directed hither and thither, as they were destined, by trusty intermediaries. This too I gladly accept, and for it I give you thanks. And the leave you kindly promise to procure regarding the correction of certain words in the Spanish patent [diploma] I shortly expect. The other letter, to which was joined a copy of the letter of the Most Reverend and Most Illustrious Lord Ernst, Archbishop of Cologne and Elector of the Empire, urges me to share certain matters with the said Most Illustrious Prince, at his own request, in both kinds of astronomy, the higher as well as the lower (for so it pleases us to speak). And further, from His Highness's letter written to you, I perceive — not without delight of mind — that he takes such intense pleasure not only in mathematical but also in pyronomic [fire-working, i.e. alchemical] exercises (which is our own delight too) that, among his other occupations of administering the commonwealth, he does not think it beneath him to handle these by his own industry and with his own hands. In both [pursuits], therefore, I shall by no means shrink from conferring frankly with His Highness and keeping up a continued correspondence (as they call it). And I render reverent thanks to His Highness that he so graciously invites me to this mutual exchange. But when the man whom he has resolved to send to me on this account shall have come here, and shall have set out in particular the matters which His Highness has determined to treat of with me, I shall freely and gladly answer them so far as in me lies: in mathematical and astronomical matters openly enough, in so far as it shall be convenient to disclose them to anyone before the full edition of our works; but in spagyric [alchemical] matters covertly, lest, if they should come into the hands of others, someone might unworthily abuse them. I judge it better, therefore, that this be accomplished either by certain secret circumlocutions, or by means of some private alphabet known to us two alone, or by whatever other method. Concerning this let the Most Illustrious Prince himself determine as he shall wish, and in the letter which he promises shortly to write to me with that messenger of his, let him indicate by what means and signs he wishes it to be done; and if he should choose some secret alphabet or a transposition of the customary letters, let him — whatever it shall be, but devised by himself and not by others, and without any disclosure — write it out in his own hand on a sheet, and at once enclose it in the letter, and forthwith seal it himself. I shall do the same in return, and it will be needful and very advisable that everything be kept on both sides in secret silence. I have gladly learned that His Highness has assiduously devoted more than 22 years to this excellent and arcane spagyric study, in which I too have now been engaged for 28 years from the 22nd year of my age — just as in my 16th I began to grow accustomed to astronomical matters. I should have written to His Highness at this time, and of my own accord have anticipated him in the things he longs for, were I not just now distracted here at Copenhagen by various cares and occupations, while I busy myself transferring hither some part of my household goods together with my family, intending to stay here for a while for certain reasons. Indeed it is my mind to set up here some of my astronomical instruments in a certain tower neighbouring our house and very well suited to observations, and at the same time to fit out here a certain spagyric laboratory, with, besides, a certain printing-house — to the carrying out of all which I am now intent. I have besides resolved this day, God willing, to cross over to our island, so as to have more of my things conveyed hither and to dispose of the rest that I leave behind there, meanwhile, until those things which I have ordered to be done here are completed — so that my mind, now distracted into many parts, can scarcely conveniently be free for the writing of such lofty and secret matters, especially to so great a Prince. Nonetheless it shall be done, as soon as his minister shall have arrived here. For then I trust I shall be restored to a more opportune repose, and it will then be not only more convenient but also safer, for the reasons in some measure indicated. But as for what His Highness writes in his letter to you concerning the Spanish privilege having been delayed on this ground — that they feared lest, under the appearance of mathematical matters, I might treat of the affairs of religion which are now variously drawn this way and that — that is without danger. For I am unwilling to meddle with those things. We have heresies and confusions enough; there is no need, nor is it my part, either to found new ones or to establish the old. May the author of heaven and earth have mercy on us, who universally orders and provides for all things, and discerns the matter not with prejudice, as men do. Meanwhile, for this very privilege which I obtained, so graciously procured, I humbly render the greatest thanks to His Highness. It is also exceedingly gratifying to me that His Highness has not, among so many other cares, forborne to read through so diligently the first volume of the Letters on Astronomical matters communicated to him through you; and that its contents have so well pleased a Prince so illustrious and so practised and judicious in these matters, I cannot but congratulate myself. The remaining volumes I am now setting up in type. But above all I labour earnestly to complete by the printer's work, and in good time to make public, the first and second volume of our Progymnasmata on the restoration of astronomy. When these shall have been completed — which I trust will be shortly — I shall then also send certain copies of them both to His Highness and to you.

That Most Illustrious Ernst, in the said letter to you, marvelled that I make no mention of that most excellent mathematician Christopher Clavius of Bamberg in the astronomical letters I sent, since he is renowned for so many distinguished works published in mathematics; and he graciously and candidly excuses this omission of his name on the ground of the too great distance of places, and because his writings, mostly printed at Rome, have perhaps not reached us. And yet — though perhaps I have not seen all the works of that most learned Clavius — those which he wrote ingeniously on Euclid and lucidly on John of Sacrobosco, and copiously on gnomonics, I have at hand, and have long since looked into; and also the Apology for the New Calendar against Maestlin, in which he makes mention of me somewhere, citing our observation of the vernal equinox, of the year — if I am not mistaken — [15]76, to prove from it that it was rightly done that the removal of ten days was allowed in the Gregorian calendar — which is indeed fittingly so, although the utmost precision in these matters has not been observed (which perhaps too in such a business, as far as ecclesiastical and political use is concerned, may be idle), just as Clavius from time to time, not without cause, objects to Maestlin, who presses the niceness [scrupulosity] of the Prutenic computation. But, if it be permitted to affirm the thing as it is, both Clavius and Maestlin alike have delivered a verdict about the heavenly bodies without consulting the heaven, which is the clock of time. For Clavius brought forward scarcely any celestial observation — except that single one of which I spoke, taken from ours, which alone does not suffice for so arduous a business — and adduces in counsel no determinations of the lunar motions (which are more intricate than has hitherto been believed), as it would have been worth the while [to do]. Maestlin, so far as I know, assumed no proof at all from the heavenly bodies; and if he had done it from his own observations, he would deserve no more credence. For whatever observations of his (if he completed any) his corrections of the motion of the Sun and Moon betray are vain — just as the register of eclipses prefixed to his Ephemerides has plainly shown [itself] no more agreeable to the celestial harmony than before, if indeed not less so. For he clings too confidently to the Prutenic computation, and to the annual quantity derived from it, which by no means receives such an inequality as that great Copernicus, for his part, would have it; but it comes nearer to the Alphonsine reckoning, although it does not touch even this with sufficient nicety — as these things are unlocked by us in a more fitting place. There is not, therefore, so great a discrepancy in the modern [neoteric] calendar from the celestial standard as Maestlin would have it — whoever, after all, be its author, whether this Clavius, or that Aloysius of whom the pontiff makes mention in the preface to its publication. But although I have not mentioned this most excellent mathematician — namely Clavius — in these Letters, because there was no occasion to do so there (especially since he has not treated of astronomical matters from the heaven itself, of which there is mostly question there), nevertheless toward the end of the first volume of our aforesaid Progymnasmata, when I call under examination, with philosophical liberty, the things he appended concerning the New Star in the last edition of his Commentary on the Sphere, I make lucid and honorific mention of him, as his pre-eminent learning in these arts deserves. If, besides, any [other] mathematical lucubrations from his Minerva have come to light, or are yet to come forth, I could wish them to be shared with me at my expense. Furthermore, if he be willing to keep up a correspondence with me by letter, and to confer pleasantly about these excellent studies, to which we are both devoted, that very thing will be far the most gratifying to me — especially if it be done by the authority and persuasion of so great a Prince, as he intimates in his letter. For that very thing will add a spur to us, and will be the more honourable for each of us. If you signify these things to the said Most Illustrious Prince by letter, with a humble greeting from me, you will have done rightly and will have made me the more bound to you.

Farewell, and pardon this prolixity.
Given at Copenhagen, the 22nd day of February, the year 1597.
Tycho Brahe,
in his own hand.

[Postscript:] Most loving Henry Rantzau, if with the same diligence by which the Spanish [privilege] was sent last, you will in good time — and that before the feast of Pentecost, if it can be done — procure for us the Gallic and English Privilege (of which I wrote to you before, too, and which I know to be granted there), you will have rendered a service useful in this respect not only to me but also to our common studies; and that you do so (as I trust you will gladly do) I would have you adjured by the mysteries of Urania, to whom we both sacrifice.

Apparatus

  • Sender / dateline. Tycho Brahe, signing manu propria ("in his own hand"). The dateline reads Dabantur Haffniae die 22. Febr. Anno 1597 — "Given at Copenhagen, 22 February 1597" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The body confirms the place repeatedly: hîc Hafniae … commoror ("I tarry here at Copenhagen"), the household and instruments being moved huc ("hither," i.e. to Copenhagen), and the plan hodie … in Insulam nostram traijcere ("to cross over today to our island," i.e. Hven). Date/place crux flagged: the edition label reads "1597. 22. februar. Uraniborg." The source's own dateline and internal evidence place the writing at Copenhagen, not Uraniborg — at the very moment Tycho was beginning to move off Hven. The header follows the as-written place (Copenhagen) by this chapter's convention and notes the catalogue's "Uraniborg" as the discrepant label; the date (22 Feb 1597) is not in dispute.
  • Recipient. Henricus Ranzovius de Bredenberga = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598/99), royal Statthalter (viceroy) of the Danish king in the duchies of Holstein and Schleswig, humanist, polymath, and astrology patron, of Breitenburg (Bredenberga); a kinsman (affinis) and friend of Tycho, and the host who would shelter Tycho at Wandsbek in 1597–98 where the Mechanica was printed.
  • affinis — rendered "kinsman" (a relation by marriage/affinity), preserving the connection without overstating it as blood kinship.
  • The Prince. ERNESTUS Archiepiscopus Coloniensis et Elector Imperij = Ernst of Bavaria (1554–1612), Archbishop-Elector of Cologne, a noted patron of alchemy; the "Most Illustrious Prince"/"His Highness" (Illustriss. Princeps / ipsius Celsitudo) throughout. Per Mosley, Tycho enclosed a copy of Ernst's letter to Rantzau when he wrote to Clavius on 5 January 1600 — the correspondence channel this letter inaugurates.
  • in utraque Astronomia, tam superiori quam inferiori — "in both kinds of astronomy, the higher as well as the lower (for so it pleases us to speak)": Tycho's coy formula for astronomy proper (superior, of the heavens) and alchemy / "terrestrial astronomy" (inferior) — the Paracelsian conceit that alchemy is the astronomy of the earth. The hedge sic enim nobis loqui lubet is preserved.
  • Pyronomicis exercitijs — "pyronomic exercises," literally "fire-management/fire-working" practices = alchemical/chemical laboratory work; glossed in brackets. Spagiricis / studio Spagirico / laboratorium Spagiricum — "spagyric" (Paracelsian alchemical-chemical) matters / study / laboratory; the period term is kept and glossed "[alchemical]" on first use.
  • Secrecy apparatus. alphabetum quoddam peculiari / secretum vel transpositionem literarum usitatarum — a private cipher-alphabet or a transposition of the ordinary letters: Tycho proposes a cipher for the alchemical exchange, the prince to devise and seal it in his own hand. Rendered literally; "secret silence" for silentio arcano.
  • operum nostrorum plenariam editionem — "the full edition of our works": Tycho's planned complete edition, of which the Progymnasmata and the Epistolae are parts.
  • diplomate Hispanico / Privilegio Hispanico — "the Spanish patent / privilege": a printing/publishing privilege Tycho was seeking through Spanish/Habsburg channels (Rantzau acting as intermediary); its delay is blamed on fears that Tycho might smuggle religious controversy under cover of mathematics — a charge he disclaims.
  • primum tomum Epistolarum — the first volume of the Epistolae astronomicae (Epistolarum astronomicarum libri, Liber I, Uraniborg 1596), which Tycho had sent to Ernst via Rantzau.
  • Progymnasmatum … in Astronomiâ instauranda volumen primum atque alterum — the first and second volumes of the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata ("preliminary exercises in the restoration of astronomy"). Astronomia instauranda / instaurata = the restoration of astronomy, Tycho's programme. Rendered "restoration of astronomy."
  • Clavius. CHRISTOPHORUS CLAVIUS Bambergensis = Christoph Clavius (1538–1612), the Jesuit mathematician of Bamberg, then at the Collegio Romano; chief architect of the Gregorian calendar. Named works: his edition of Euclid; his commentary In Sphaeram Ioannis de Sacro Bosco; his works de Gnomonicis (Gnomonices libri octo); the Apologia of the new calendar against Maestlin; and his Commentary on the Sphere (last edition), where he appended remarks on the New Star (the 1572 nova) — which Tycho says he treats "with philosophical liberty" toward the end of Progymnasmata I.
  • Minervâ — "from his Minerva," i.e. from Clavius's [own] mind/genius (Minerva as goddess of wisdom = native talent); rendered idiomatically.
  • Maestlin. MAeSTHLINUS = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), the Tübingen astronomer (Kepler's teacher) and a Copernican; here criticised over the calendar dispute and his Ephemerides.
  • Aloysius … de quo pontifex in prooemio … mentionem facit = Aloysius Lilius (Luigi Lilio / Aloysius Lilius, d. 1576), whose scheme underlay the Gregorian reform and whom Gregory XIII (pontifex) names in the bull's preface; Tycho raises him as the calendar's possible true author over against Clavius.
  • Calendar / technical. Calendario Gregoriano / neoterico = the Gregorian (modern/neoteric) calendar; decendij ablatio = "the removal of ten days" (the 1582 excision of 10 days); aequinoctij verni … anni 76 = the vernal equinox observation "of the year [15]76," cited by Clavius. calculus Pruteniciʼs — the Prutenic computation (the Prutenic Tables of Erasmus Reinhold, 1551, based on Copernicus); Alphonsina supputatio = the Alphonsine reckoning (the Alphonsine Tables). quantitas annua = "the annual quantity" (length of the tropical year); inaequalitas = the (claimed) inequality/variation in the year's length. coelo, quod horologium est temporis — "the heaven, which is the clock of time" — kept as the sententia it is. libertate Philosophicâ = "with philosophical liberty" (freedom of philosophical inquiry).
  • Biographical datum. Tycho dates his alchemy "28 years … from the 22nd year of my age" and his start in astronomy to "my 16th [year]" — i.e. astronomy from c. 1562 and alchemy from c. 1568, consistent with a 1597 letter (Tycho b. 1546). Figures rendered exactly; no reconciliation imposed.
  • V. D. (in hodie V. D. … traijcere) = volente Deo, "God willing"; expanded in the translation.
  • Negations checked. The disclaimer cluster — id caret periculo ("that is without danger"), Ego enim me illis immiscere nolo ("for I am unwilling to meddle with those things"), non opus est, nec mearum partium, aut novas condere, aut priores stabilire ("there is no need, nor is it my part, either to found new ones or to establish the old") — is rendered with its negations counted and preserved; Tycho denies any intent to engage religious controversy, neither inventing nor entrenching heresies. The double construction nec … aut … aut … is kept as "nor … either … or."
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (before the signature), restored from Dreyer VII, p. 382 (dropped by the working transcription); the request that Rantzau procure the Gallic and English printing-privileges before Pentecost.
