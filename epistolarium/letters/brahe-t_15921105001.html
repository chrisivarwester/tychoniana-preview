The original

NOBILISSIMO CLARISSIMOQUE VIRO D. TYCHONI BRAHE, MATHEMATICO EXIMIO, DOMINO ET AMICO PLURIMUM OBSERVANDO. S. P.

EGI cum MYLIANDRO de Typographico ministro, verum in præsentia de nullo ipsi constat. Mathematicarum disciplinarum studiosi pauci hodie, plerique enim Theologiæ studio vacant. Certum et præsens viaticum inquiram diligentius. De CRAIGO miror, nec scio quomodo paralaxin refellere queat, qua moti veteres ARISTOTELIS interpretes viam lacteam in cœlo statuerunt. Velim mihi proximis literis indicari rationes quibus eam evertere conatur, nisi forte observationem culpet. Nam vidi nonnullorum scripta, qui hunc eundem Cometam infra Lunam statuant. Ego certe parallaxi aut nulla aut exigua et minore quam in Luna in hoc Cometa concessa, non video quomodo suam sententiam tueri queat. Si editus est, dabitur aliquando copia legendi. Si autem non est editus, iudico illum hæc scripsisse, ut magis excitet ingenij tui acumen, et cogites quæ contra dici possint. Homo est eruditus, ut ex DUNCANO intellexi, maiorem modestiam in eo requirerem, si cavillando et detorquendo tua infringere conatur. Attamen non pæniteat commendasse, decet enim bonum virum iuvare plurimos, obesse nemini. Futuram eclipsim, si conspicietur, accuratius observabo: Globum tuum vel Sphæram armillarem satis affabre confectam vidi, verum parum meis oculis aptam, qui iam incipiunt obtusiores fieri. Non satis autem intelligo cur Terræ polos detorseris a polis mundi et Zodiaci.

Bene vale.
Rostochij Va Novembris A.° Domini 1592.
HENRICUS BRUCÆUS
plane tuus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most renowned man, Master Tycho Brahe, distinguished mathematician, his lord and friend much to be respected — heartiest greeting.

I have dealt with Myliander concerning a printer's assistant, but at present he knows of none. Few today are students of the mathematical disciplines, for most give themselves over to the study of theology. I shall inquire more diligently after a sure and ready means of support [for him]. About Craig I wonder, and I do not see how he can refute the parallax by which the ancient interpreters of Aristotle were moved to place the Milky Way in the heaven. I should like to have set out for me, in your next letter, the grounds by which he attempts to overturn it — unless perhaps he finds fault with the observation [itself]. For I have seen the writings of certain men who place this very same comet below the Moon. For my part, certainly, once a parallax either nil or slight, and less than that in the Moon, is granted in this comet, I do not see how he can maintain his opinion. If it has been published, the chance to read it will be given at some time. But if it has not been published, I judge that he wrote these things rather to rouse the keenness of your intellect, and that you might consider what can be said against [them]. He is a learned man, as I have understood from Duncan; I should require greater modesty in him, if it is by quibbling and twisting that he attempts to undermine your [arguments]. Yet let it not be regretted that I commended him, for it befits a good man to help very many and to harm no one. The coming eclipse, if it shall be visible, I shall observe more accurately. Your globe, or the armillary sphere, I have seen, made skilfully enough — but little suited to my eyes, which are now beginning to grow duller. I do not, however, sufficiently understand why you have turned the poles of the Earth aside from the poles of the world and of the Zodiac.

Fare you well.
At Rostock, the 5th of November, in the Year of the Lord 1592.
Henricus Brucaeus,
wholly yours.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Hendrik van den Broeke, c. 1530/31–1593), born at Alost (Aalst) in Flanders, professor of medicine and of mathematics at the University of Rostock, and an established astronomical correspondent of Tycho's. Writing late in life (he died the following year), he refers to his failing eyesight.
  • Recipient / place. Tycho is at this date at Uraniborg on Hven; Brucaeus writes from Rostock (Rostochium; the edition's convention).
  • Myliander — a Rostock contact (most plausibly connected to the local book trade / university milieu) through whom Brucaeus sought to recruit a compositor for Tycho's private press on Hven. Identification crux: the name is not securely fixed here; not resolved beyond the printing-recruitment context the letter itself supplies.
  • Typographicus minister — rendered "printer's assistant" (a journeyman compositor / press-hand for Tycho's Uraniborg printing office); viaticum "means of support / provision" — i.e. the wages or maintenance that would secure such a man. Latin technical-of-trade nouns kept in sense by the edition's convention
  • The Craig controversy (the core of the letter). Craigo = John Craig (Craigius, d. 1620), the Scottish physician (later physician to James VI/I) who disputed Tycho's doctrine that comets are supralunary. Tycho had sent Craig an "apology" defending his comet book; Craig in turn composed a refutation (the Capnuraniae restinctio, against Tycho's "smoke-in-heaven"), upholding the Aristotelian doctrine that comets are sublunary exhalations. Brucaeus's whole technical paragraph is his reaction to Craig's argument. Brucaeus does not know whether Craig's piece is yet editus ("published") — at the time of writing it apparently was not — and hedges that, if unpublished, it may be a private provocation to sharpen Tycho's wits; the hedge is preserved, not resolved.
  • paralaxin / parallaxi — parallax; spelled paralaxin (acc.) once and parallaxi (abl.) once in the source, both retained as printed. Brucaeus's argument: Aristotle's ancient interpreters placed the Milky Way (via lactea) in the heaven (i.e. as a celestial, not sublunary, phenomenon) precisely on a parallax argument; he cannot see how Craig overturns parallax. The decisive clause — parallaxi aut nulla aut exigua et minore quam in Luna … concessa ("a parallax either nil or slight, and less than that in the Moon, being granted") — is the standard Tychonic test: a comet showing no measurable parallax, or one smaller than the Moon's, must lie beyond the Moon.
  • observationem culpet — "he finds fault with the observation": Brucaeus's alternative supposition that Craig attacks not the inference but Tycho's observational data; rendered without sharpening.
  • Cometam infra Lunam statuant — "place this very same comet below the Moon," i.e. as sublunary. hunc eundem Cometam ("this very same comet") implies a specific comet under discussion; the letter does not name it, so it is left unspecified rather than identified.
  • Duncano — Duncan, a Scot (the period form Duncanus) cited as Brucaeus's informant on Craig's learning; not further identified here.
  • cavillando et detorquendo … infringere — "by quibbling and twisting … to undermine"; the contemptuous pair is kept. tua ("your [arguments / positions]") is supplied in brackets as the understood object.
  • Instruments. Globum tuum vel Sphaeram armillarem — "your globe, or the armillary sphere" (the edition's convention: globus → globe, armillae/sphaera armillaris → armillary sphere). Brucaeus has seen one of Tycho's instruments but finds it hard on his ageing eyes.
  • Futuram eclipsim … accuratius observabo — "the coming eclipse … I shall observe more accurately" (eclipsis → eclipse,); the specific eclipse is not named in the letter.
  • The terrestrial-poles question. cur Terrae polos detorseris a polis mundi et Zodiaci — "why you have turned the poles of the Earth aside from the poles of the world and of the Zodiac." This records Brucaeus's puzzlement at a feature of Tycho's cosmological/diagrammatic model (the poles of the terrestrial axis displaced from the poles of the celestial sphere mundi and from the ecliptic poles Zodiaci). polos mundi rendered "poles of the world" (the celestial poles). The exact construction Brucaeus is reacting to is not elaborated in the letter and is not reconstructed here.
  • Salutation / subscription. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting,"); Bene vale → "Fare you well"; plane tuus → "wholly yours."
  • Dateline. Rostochij Va Novembris A.° Domini 1592 = "At Rostock, the 5th of November, in the Year of the Lord 1592." Va is the ordinal quinta (5th); the as-written date agrees with the catalogue label ("1592. 5. november. Rostock"). Old Style: Rostock (Mecklenburg, Lutheran) and Denmark both kept the Julian calendar in 1592; the date is O.S.
