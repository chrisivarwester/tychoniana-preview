The original

GENIO, INGENIO, GENERE VIRO EXIMIE DECORATO ET CONSPICUO OLIGERO ROSENKRANTZIO, AMICO CONSANGVINEO, AFFINI INTEGERRIMO. ROSENHOLM.

S. P.

SCRIPSI tibi, desideratiss. mi OLIGERE, quamprimum huc Witebergam veni, et de statu rerum mearum te certiorem reddidi, quas literas te accepisse existimo, ideoque ijs indicata repetere nolo. Cum vero commodum sese obtulisset hic Tabellarius, qui se etiam Arhusiam venturum aiebat, non intermittendum duxi, quin aliquas saltem denuo ad te darem. Cupio autem per eundem ex te cognoscere, quomodo vivas et valeas, et quid rerum agas, tum quoque, si quid mea interest scire, ut fideliter et amice significes. Ego hîc volente Numine hybernabo, cum Bohemiam nunc ingredi lues Epidemica illic magis magisque invalescens non sinat, Cæsare ipso vix ibi locum satis huius contagij convenientem inveniente, ut ob id eum inde excedere animum induxisse nonnulli referant. Familia mea muliebris necdum advenit, pro qua adducenda misi TYCHONEM filium cum equis et curribus meis. Quam primum advenerit, quod intra paucos dies futurum existimo, equos in nundinis Lipsensibus distrahere constitui, ne mihi sint, interea dum hîc subsisto, oneri. Facile, uti spero, alios instante vere recepturus. Interea Typographicum laborem urgeo, ut compleantur Libri mei, in Dania non absoluti, nec etiam prorsus Wandesburgi. Paro adhuc alios pro Cæsare, quantum otium hoc ferre poterit.

Cæterum hoc insuper incidit, quod antea exciderat, ut te interrogarem de duobus illis libris Regi vestro offerendis, an id factum sit, necne. Si exhibiti sunt, cupio scire, per quem, et quomodo excepti, et a quo postea accepti, cum is, uti constat, talibus non afficiatur, quæ tamen omnia parum moror, veluti etiam nullam inde gratiam aut affectavi aut speravi, uti neque antea ex tot non sine meo damno meritis ullam promereri potui, quod pariter ex alto negligo. Sin vero hi libri adhuc penes te sunt, cuperem eos bene ab iniuria aëris et pluviarum munitos (quod per lineum cera imbutum facile præstatur) per hunc Tabellarium mihi remitti, quo in quibusdam eos recognoscere et locupletare queam, præsertim quoad stellarum restitutionem, et addito deinde tertio quodam eiusdem formæ et magnitudinis, quem etiam Cæsari dedico; omnes enim tres, præfixo singulis peculiari Epigrammate, Regi in Comitijs proximæ æstatis exhiberi, Deo volente, procurabo, sive per te sive per alium quempiam ex meis, si tibi id non fuerit commodum. Adiungentur, uti spero, quædam alia ex meis operibus, hisce maiora, ut videant vestrates, si quos ea cura tangat, me isthic non fuisse otiosum, nec tempus inutiliter collocasse, nostrosque labores non ob id negligi aut interire, quod quidam ex ijs eos pessundare conati sint. Habeo etiam alias rationes, cur sic fieri velim, nec censuris contrarijs, undecunque petitis, afficior.

Cum Dresdæ essem, adfuit mihi IVARDUS FRISIUS, fratrem Regis comitatus; is forte tibi nonnulla de me rettulit, aut alijs. Vellem eum satis attentum fuisse, ut adhuc plura referre potuisset, non tam tibi quam ijs. Si memoriam, vel potius facundiam eius inaudivisti, indicatione pande, et quomodo illa intellecta acceptaque fuerint. Scis quid velim, de pluribus etiam, uti et prius rogavi, me (cum tam certus adsit internuntius) commonefacias; Exemplaria omnium librorum meorum, tam alibi quam hîc absolutorum, brevi, favente Deo, obtinebis, ut duorum illorum, quos habuisti, iactura cum fœnore resarciatur. Salutavi nuper ex te, quamvis non rogatus, Doctorem HUNNIUM, cum simul in arce, unâ cum alijs Professoribus, a Præside invitati essemus. Is te vicissim resalutat. Quin et alii hîc Professores, qui te norunt et plurimi faciunt, salutem tibi dicunt. Inter quos etiam est Hospes meus Dominus D. IOHANNES IESSENIUS. Vale cum Coniuge tua, mea Nepte, quam felicissime, et instantem annum, feliciter inchoatum, quam prosperrime etiam absolvite, multosque insuper alios. Et his proximis det Deus, ut vobis ludat ROSENKRANTZIUS [in] aula Parvulus. Saluta Matrem, coniugem, et amicos meos, si quos absentia mei adhuc memores esse admittit.

Dabantur properanter Witeberga, pridie novi Anni 1599.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man eminently adorned and conspicuous in character, talent, and birth, Holger Rosenkrantz, [my] most upright friend, kinsman, [and] relative. [At] Rosenholm.

Heartiest greeting.

I wrote to you, my most longed-for Holger, as soon as I came hither to Wittenberg, and informed you of the state of my affairs — which letter I think you have received, and so I will not repeat what was indicated in it. But since this courier offered himself conveniently, who said he would also come to Aarhus, I thought I should not omit to give you at least some [letter] again. I desire, by the same [courier], to learn from you how you live and fare, and what you are doing; and also, if there be anything it concerns me to know, that you faithfully and amicably signify it. I will here, God willing, winter, since the epidemic plague — growing ever stronger there — does not now allow me to enter Bohemia, the Emperor himself scarcely finding there a place sufficiently free of this contagion (so that some report he has resolved to depart thence). My womenfolk have not yet arrived, for whose bringing I sent my son Tycho with my horses and carriages; as soon as they arrive, which I think will be within a few days, I have resolved to sell the horses at the Leipzig fair, lest they be a burden to me while I stay here — hoping easily to get others in the coming spring. Meanwhile I press the typographic labour, that my books may be completed (not finished in Denmark, nor quite at Wandsbek); and I am still preparing others for the Emperor, as far as this leisure will allow.

For the rest, this moreover occurs to me, which had earlier slipped [my mind]: that I should ask you about those two books to be offered to your King — whether it was done or not. If they were presented, I desire to know by whom, and how received, and by whom afterward taken — since he, as is well known, is not affected by such things; all which, however, I little regard, just as I neither sought nor hoped for any favour thence (as neither before could I earn any from so many merits, not without my loss) — which I likewise disregard from on high. But if these books are still with you, I should wish them — well protected from the injury of air and rains (which is easily done with linen soaked in wax) — sent back to me by this courier, so that I may revise and enrich them in certain [respects], especially as regards the restitution of the stars; and then, a third of the same form and size being added — which I also dedicate to the Emperor — for all three, a special Epigram prefixed to each, I will, God willing, have presented to the King at the Diet of next summer, whether through you or through some other of mine, if it be not convenient for you. There will be added, as I hope, certain other of my works, greater than these, that your countrymen — if any be touched by that care — may see that I have not been idle abroad, nor spent my time uselessly, and that our labours are not neglected or perishing because some of them tried to ruin them. I have also other reasons why I wish it so done; nor am I moved by contrary censures, sought from wherever.

When I was at Dresden, Ivar Friis was with me, accompanying the King's brother; he perhaps reported some things about me to you, or to others. I could wish he had been attentive enough to report yet more — not so much to you as to those [others]. If you have heard of his memory — or rather his eloquence — disclose it by some indication, and how those things were understood and received. You know what I wish; about more things too, as I asked before, remind me (since so sure a messenger is at hand). You will soon, God favouring, obtain copies of all my books, finished both elsewhere and here, so that the loss of those two you had may be repaid with interest. I lately greeted Doctor Hunnius on your behalf, though unasked, when we were together invited to the castle, with other Professors, by the Governor; he greets you back. Nay, other Professors here too, who know you and esteem you greatly, send you greeting — among whom is also my host, Lord Doctor Johann Jessenius. Farewell, with your wife, my niece, most happily; and complete the coming year, happily begun, as prosperously as may be, and many others besides. And in these next [years] may God grant that a little Rosenkrantz may play for you in the hall. Greet your mother, wife, and my friends, if my absence allows any to be still mindful of me.

Given in haste from Wittenberg, the day before the new year 1599.
Your Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient & date. Holger Rosenkrantz at Rosenholm, addressed with the alliterative flourish Genio, Ingenio, Genere ("in character, talent, and birth"). Dated pridie novi Anni 1599 = 31 December 1598 — one of the three same-day Wittenberg letters (with brahe-t_15981231001 to Longomontanus and brahe-t_15981230001 to Vedel, 30 Dec). The MS is a later Langebek afskrift.
  • The Wittenberg winter. Tycho confirms he is wintering at Wittenberg because the plague bars Bohemia (even the Emperor "scarcely finding a place free of contagion," reportedly about to leave); his womenfolk are being brought by his son Tycho with horses and carriages (to be sold at the Leipzig fair); he presses the printing of his unfinished books and prepares "others for the Emperor."
  • Significance — the two books the Danish King kept, and the three-book Diet plan (cross-ref to brahe-t_15990920001). Tycho asks whether the two books offered to King Christian IV were ever presented, and how received — noting drily that the King "is not affected by such things," and that he himself "neither sought nor hoped for any favour." (This is the same pair of books that, per Henrik Ramel's letter to Sophie Brahe, brahe-t_15990920001, the King retained "because your dear brother had given them" though he cared little for them.) If the books are still with Holger, Tycho wants them sent back (wrapped in wax-soaked linen against the weather) so he can revise and enrich them — "especially as to the restitution of the stars" — add a third of the same format dedicated to the Emperor, prefix a special epigram to each, and have all three presented to the King at next summer's Diet, alongside greater works — "that your countrymen … may see that I have not been idle abroad … and that our labours do not perish because some tried to ruin them" (a pointed allusion to his Danish detractors).
  • Persons & news. Ivardus Frisius = Ivar Friis, who at Dresden had accompanied the Danish King's brother and may have reported on Tycho; Doctor Hunnius = Aegidius Hunnius (1550–1603), the Wittenberg theologian, greeted at a castle dinner given by the Governor; Johann Jessenius, Tycho's host at Wittenberg (cf. brahe-t_15981230001, brahe-t_15981231001). The closing wish — "may a little Rosenkrantz play for you in the hall" — hopes for a son for Holger and his wife (Tycho's niece, Sophie Brahe, Axel Brahe's daughter).
  • Calendar. Dateline Witeberga, pridie novi Anni 1599 = 31 December 1598; Wittenberg/Saxony kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar. (The edition label "1598. 31. december" agrees.)
