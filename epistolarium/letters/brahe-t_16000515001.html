The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM.

ILLUSTRIS ET GENEROSE VIR, DOMINE OBSERVANDISSIME.

DE ijs, quæ nuper ad Illustrem Dominationem Vestram per meum, quem isthuc ablegavi, domesticum perscripsi et referri volui, nunc pluribus non agam, ne eadem repetendo ei nimium molestus videar, sperans nihilominus, Illustrem Domem Vestram desiderijs meis lubenti animo annuituram, atque ut res optatum finem sortiatur, effecturam. Verum aliud quid incidit, de quo nunc Dominationem Vestram reverenter et obnixe rogandam duxi. Venit hisce diebus huc e Dania quidam Generosi Stemmatis Iuvenis, e splendidissima et antiquissima ROSENKRANTZIORUM familia isthic oriundus, FRIDERICUS nomine, qui Regis per sexennium Cubicularius fuit. Is militiam aliquandiu sequi et in ea se exercere in animum induxit, et primum Serenissimo Principi, Archiduci Austriæ, Domino MATTHIÆ, Sacræ Cæsareæ Maiestatis, Domini nostri clementissimi, fratri, aliquantisper in aula ipsius Celsitudinis humilime inservire cupit, ut paulatim addiscendo mores et consuetudinem earum regionum in bello Ungarico postea inter equitatus exercitum locum oportunum pro se et sibi inservientibus aliquot equitibus obtinere possit. Quare cum dictus ROSENKRANTZIUS mihi non saltem consanguinitatis iure iunctissimus existat, sed et ab intimis in patria nostra eius de caussa per literas rogatus sim, ut, quantum in me est, conatus ipsius promovere laborem, idcirco ad Illustrem Generositatem Vestram confugio atque ab ea quam officiosissime peto, ut illi literas ad Serenissimum Austriæ Archiducem, Dominum MATTHIAM, intercessorias procurare non degravari velit, quo eo facilius et promptius in eius Celsitudinis aulicum servitium recipi et postea in Ungaria contra Turcas militare et suam fortunam experiri queat, sicque virtutis bellicæ laude, ad quam strenue aspirat (utut aliâs non saltem genere clarus sit, sed et in literis bene versatus, simulque Latinæ, Italicæ, Gallicæ et Hispanicæ linguæ, præter Teutonicam et vernaculam, gnarus) ornatior ad suos aliquando redeat, et Patriæ eo magis in posterum usui esse possit. Sed quia de Illustris Domis Væ erga me singulari benevolentia, quam aliâs satis perspectam habeo, nihil addubitem, eam etiam hac in parte meæ expectationi non defuturam confido. Ideoque illam aliâs occupatissimam ulterius remorari desino. Deus Opt. Max. Illustrem Domem Vestram quam diutissime sospitem conservet.

Dabantur ex arce Cæsarea Benaticka die 15 Maij Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to an anonymous [correspondent].

Illustrious and generous man, most-to-be-revered lord.

Of those matters which I lately wrote out in full to Your Illustrious Lordship through my servant, whom I sent thither, and wished to be reported, I will not now treat at more length, lest by repeating the same I seem too troublesome to you — hoping nonetheless that Your Illustrious Lordship will assent to my desires with willing mind, and will bring it about that the matter reach its wished-for end. But another thing has come up, about which I have now thought to ask Your Lordship, reverently and earnestly. There came hither these days, from Denmark, a certain youth of generous lineage, sprung there from the most splendid and most ancient family of the Rosenkrantzes — Frederik by name — who was for six years Chamberlain to the King. He has resolved to follow soldiering for a time and to train himself in it, and first desires most humbly to serve a while in the court of His Highness the Most Serene Prince, Archduke of Austria, Lord Matthias, brother of His Sacred Imperial Majesty our most clement Lord; so that, gradually learning the manners and usage of those regions, he may afterward, in the Hungarian war, obtain a suitable place among the cavalry, for himself and for several horsemen serving under him. Wherefore, since the said Rosenkrantz is most closely bound to me not only by right of blood-kinship, but I have also been asked by letters, by [my] intimates in our homeland, on his account, to labour — so far as in me lies — to advance his endeavours: I therefore take refuge with Your Illustrious Generosity, and ask of it most dutifully, that you would not find it a burden to procure for him letters of intercession to the Most Serene Archduke of Austria, Lord Matthias, whereby the more easily and readily he may be received into His Highness's court service, and afterward soldier in Hungary against the Turks and try his fortune; and so, with the praise of martial valour to which he strenuously aspires (though otherwise he is not only illustrious by birth, but also well versed in letters, and at the same time skilled in the Latin, Italian, French and Spanish tongues, besides German and his vernacular), he may someday return the more adorned to his own people, and the more be of use to the Fatherland hereafter. But since I doubt nothing of Your Illustrious Lordship's singular benevolence toward me, which I have otherwise sufficiently perceived, I trust that it will not fail my expectation in this part either. And so I cease to detain you, otherwise most busy, any further. May God, best and greatest, preserve Your Illustrious Lordship in safety as long as may be.

Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 15th day of May, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe at Benátky.
  • Recipient. Anonymous in the source (ad anonymum): an illustrious imperial patron with access to the court of Archduke Matthias — possibly the same well-placed official Tycho addresses in brahe-t_16000306002 (also ad anonymum, also re a recommendation). Identity not recoverable from the text.
  • Purpose & connective tissue. A letter of intercession for Frederik Rosenkrantz — and a striking node in the edition. Fridericus … Regis per sexennium Cubicularius = Frederik Rosenkrantz (1571–1602), formerly Chamberlain to King Christian IV of Denmark for six years. Disgraced in the 1599 court scandal (the affair with Rigborg Brockenhuus) and stripped of honour — the very man whose condemnation ("caussa cecidisse et infamem pronuntiatum") Tycho's nephew reports in brahe-t_16000512001, and whose later "happier breeze" Winshemius notes in brahe-t_16000813001. Here, in May 1600, his kinsman Tycho is actively engineering his rehabilitation by soldiering: a place at the court of Archduke Matthias of Austria (Rudolf II's brother, the future Emperor Matthias), as a step toward a cavalry command in the Hungarian war against the Turks (the Long Turkish War). Rosenkrantz did serve, and died in that war in 1602 — so this letter documents the turning-point. Tycho notes Rosenkrantz is consanguinitatis iure iunctissimus — closely bound to him by blood — and that "intimates in our homeland" had written asking Tycho to help.
  • Persons named.
  • Fridericus Rosenkrantzius — see above; praised as learned and fluent in Latin, Italian, French, Spanish, German and his Danish vernacular.
  • Serenissimus Archidux Austriae Dominus Matthias = Archduke Matthias (1557–1619), brother of Rudolf II, governor in the Habsburg lands and a commander in the Hungarian war; later Emperor Matthias (1612).
  • Rex = Christian IV of Denmark, whose chamberlain Frederik had been.
  • Place. arx Caesarea Benaticka = Benátky.
  • Epistolary formulae. Dabantur (epistolary) → "Given"; Deus Opt. Max. … sospitem conservet → the closing benediction.
  • Calendar — RESOLVED. The dateline day is 15 May 1600 (N.S., consistent with the Benátky series). Dreyer (Opera Omnia VIII, p. 319; running-head 5/15 Mai.) prints die 15 Maij, so the earlier "blank/lacuna" reading was a defect of the working transcription, now corrected in header, Source, and translation.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: the dateline day is not a lacuna — Dreyer (Opera Omnia VIII, p. 319; running-head 5/15 Mai.) prints die 15 Maij. Restored to header, Source, and translation; the lacuna flag is withdrawn.
