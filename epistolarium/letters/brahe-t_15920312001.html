The original

CLARISSIMO DOCTISSIMOQUE VIRO M. BARTHOLOMÆO SCULTETO, GORLICENSI, RERUM MATHEMATICARUM PERITISSIMO, VETERI SUO AMICO PLURIMUM COLENDO. S.

EN! habes mi SCULTETE, operum nostrorum, diu a te atque alijs desideratum , primum Quaternionem, inscriptionem quatuor subsequentium tomorum eorumque contenta breviter complectentem. Hinc cætera ratiocinare. Addidi MYLIJ vestri succinctum Epigramma, sed in plerisque a me immutatum, eo quod prius illud nimis affectuosum atque insolens quibusdam videri posset; nec tali me dignor honore. Agas vero illi gratias pro suo candido et benevolo erga me meaque studia amore. Si mihi scripserit, ego par pari referre, et amicitiam cum isto inposterum colere, non tergiversabor. Monstra quoque vestratibus Bibliopolis hanc operum nostrorum inscriptionem, et, an aliquot exemplaria iusto pretio obtinere satagant, exquire, quidque pro singulis Alphabetis aut quaternionibus numerandum sit, cum illis transige, posteaque me, et de pretio et de exemplarium numero, quæ habere velint, certiorem effice. Continebit quodlibet horum voluminum, quæ quatuor omnino erunt, circiter sesquina Alphabeta, in charta quam regalem vocant, suntque plurimis Schematis atque Tabulis hinc inde conferta. Tuum itaque erit, nostris desiderijs morem gerere, negotium hoc fideliter, ac si tuum esset (quod et tibi confido) agere, meque per primam oblatam occasionem de singulis commonefacere. Vale, et præstantissimum illum MYLIUM meo nomine diligenter saluta. Iamque ambo feliciter vivite, bibite, valete. Propino vobis hoc die ☉, quo et hîc et isthic alibique potare solent fere omnes (rem scilicet Bacho sacram gratamque facientes) unum alterumque cantharulum, eiusdem Liæi liquore usque ad exuberantiam refertum, idque haustu etiam Bachico, licet non Philosophico, multo minus Theologico, quamvis dies Christianis sacer esse deberet: Ebibo autem pro vestra salute, quod et magnus BARTHOLINUS, nunc mihi domesticus, proxime assidens, lubenter satisfecit. Non crediderim illum artem hanc apud vos didicisse, cum in Patria eius Seelandia rudes ipsius non sint. Vestrum est, nobis iugiter et gurguliter par pari referre, etiam adhibita Musica, quemadmodum nos non sine buccinarum, tibiarum, fidium clangore et suavi concentu, hæc pocula vobis vovimus. Iterum atque iterum vivite, bibite, valete, literasque has ad MONAVIUM Vratislaviam destinate.

Datæ Uraniburgi d. 12. Martij Anno 1592.
TYCHO BRAHE, manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most learned man, Master Bartholomaeus Scultetus of Görlitz, most skilled in mathematical matters, his old friend to be greatly honoured — greeting.

Look! you have here, my Scultetus, the first gathering [Quaternion] of our works — long desired by you and by others — which sets out the title of the four volumes that follow and briefly comprehends their contents. From this reckon the rest. I have added the terse epigram of your Mylius, but in most places altered by me, because that earlier version might seem to some too effusive and out of the ordinary; nor do I deem myself worthy of such an honour. Do, however, render him thanks for his frank and kindly affection toward me and my studies. If he writes to me, I shall not shrink from returning like for like and cultivating a friendship with him hereafter. Show this title of our works also to your booksellers there, and find out whether they are eager to obtain a number of copies at a fair price; and settle with them what is to be paid for each Alphabet or gathering, and afterward make me the more certain both as to the price and as to the number of copies they wish to have. Each of these volumes — which will be four in all — will contain about one and a half Alphabets [sheets], on what they call royal paper, and they are crammed throughout with very many diagrams and tables. It will therefore be your part to humour our wishes, to conduct this business faithfully, as if it were your own (which I also trust to you), and to keep me apprised of each point at the first occasion offered. Farewell, and diligently greet in my name that most excellent Mylius. And now, both of you, live happily, drink, and be well. I drink your health on this day of the Sun [☉, Sunday] — on which, both here and there and elsewhere, nearly all are wont to drink (performing, that is, a thing sacred and pleasing to Bacchus) — one cup and another, brimful to overflowing with the liquor of the same Lyaeus, and that too with a Bacchic draught, though not a Philosophical one, much less a Theological, even though the day ought to be holy to Christians: but I drink it down for your health, which the great Bartholinus too, now of my household, sitting close beside me, has gladly performed. I would not believe that he learned this art among you, since in his homeland Zealand they are not unschooled in it themselves. It is yours to return like for like to us, continuously and throat-deep, with music brought in too — just as we, not without the blare and sweet concord of trumpets, pipes, and strings, have pledged these cups to you. Again and again, live, drink, and be well; and send this letter on to Monavius at Breslau.

Given at Uraniborg, the 12th day of March, in the year 1592.
TYCHO BRAHE, by his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. M. Bartholomaeus Scultetus, Gorlicensis = Bartholomäus Scultetus (Bartholomäus Schultz, 1540–1614), mathematician, cartographer, and astronomer of Görlitz (Latin Gorlicium, in Upper Lusatia), where he served as mayor; a long-standing correspondent and friend of Tycho's. The title M. (Magister) is the academic "Master".
  • Occasion. The "first Quaternion" (primum Quaternionem) is the printed specimen gathering — the title-leaf-cum-prospectus announcing Tycho's projected four-volume opera: the great programme that would issue (in part) as the Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata. Tycho is using Scultetus as his book-trade agent in Görlitz/Lusatia to gauge demand among the booksellers and to set a per-gathering price.
  • Persons named.
  • Mylius vester — (Caspar) Mylius, a Görlitz man in Scultetus's circle, author of a laudatory epigramma on Tycho's works which Tycho says he has toned down. Identification of the praenomen is not secured by the letter; "Mylius" is kept as written.
  • magnus Bartholinus, nunc mihi domesticus — "the great Bartholinus, now of my household": a member of the Danish Bartholin family then resident with Tycho on Hven, said to be from Zealand (Seelandia). Crux flagged: the famous Caspar Bartholin the Elder (1585–1629) was a child of seven in 1592 and cannot be meant; the person here is an older Bartholin (the epithet magnus, "the great/elder," and artem hanc — the drinking — are jocular). The precise individual is not determined from the text and is left un-asserted.
  • Monavius, Vratislaviam — Jacob Monau (Jacobus Monavius, 1546–1603), humanist of Breslau (Latin Vratislavia, modern Wrocław), another of Tycho's correspondents; Tycho asks Scultetus to forward the letter (and presumably the prospectus) on to him. Vratislaviam is the accusative of destination, "to Breslau."
  • Bibliographical / book-trade terms.
  • Alphabeta — an "Alphabet" is the bibliographer's unit of 23 gathering-signatures (A–Z, excluding J, U, W); circiter sesquina Alphabeta = "about one and a half Alphabets," i.e. roughly 34–35 sheets per volume. Rendered "Alphabet(s)," glossed.
  • quaternio — strictly a quire of four folded sheets (a quaternion); here used both for that codicological unit and, in the opening, for the specimen prospectus-gathering. Kept as "gathering [Quaternion]."
  • charta quam regalem vocant — "what they call royal paper" (charta regalis, a large-format premium paper); Tycho's own hedging phrase quam … vocant preserved.
  • Schematis atque Tabulis — "diagrams and tables," the figures of the astronomical text.
  • Symbol. hoc die ☉ — the glyph ☉ is the symbol of the Sun; dies Solis = Sunday. Per the edition's convention the glyph is kept in the source block and named in English ("this day of the Sun [Sunday]"). Tycho's wit turns on its being the Lord's Day, on which he nonetheless drinks a "Bacchic, not Philosophical, much less Theological" draught.
  • Classical allusions (kept as metaphor). Bacho … Bachico — Bacchus, god of wine; eiusdem Liaei liquore — Lyaeus ("the Loosener," Lyaios) is a cult-epithet of Bacchus, here "the liquor of the same Lyaeus," i.e. wine. par pari referre — the proverbial "to return like for like." iugiter et gurguliter — a jingling pair, "continuously and throat-deep" (gurguliter, from gurgulio, the gullet, is a mock-coinage matching iugiter); the playful rhyme is signalled rather than smoothed.
  • Epistolary formulae. Opening S. = salutem ("greeting"); the triple sign-off vivite, bibite, valete ("live, drink, and be well") and Vale ("Farewell") by the edition's convention; subscription manu propria = "by his own hand."
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgi d. 12. Martij Anno 1592 = "Given at Uraniborg [on Hven], the 12th day of March, year 1592" — Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The catalogue label date (1592.12. marts. Uraniborg) agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. The edition of record is the Singularia historico-literaria Lusatica (the Lusatian literary-historical miscellany in which the letter was printed); no manuscript witness is recorded in the edition.
