The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTOPHORUM STURCIUM.
NOBILIS ET CLARISSIME VIR, AMICE HONORANDE.

EX ijs quas paulo post æquinoctium Autumnale ad me dedisti literis, quo in statu res tuæ sint, et cur rarius ad me scripseris, intellexi. Ego vero tibi quietem illam et studiorum commoditatem congratulor, ac literarum intermissionem lubens excusatam habeo.

De rebus Suecicis et Livonicis similia ijs, quæ scribis, imo adhuc plura hîc cognovimus — quem das finem, pater alme, furorum?

Negotium illud de ijs, quæ Historiolæ huius anni Domini D. CHYTRÆI laudatæ memoriæ inseri ipse, dum in vivis esset, volebat, nec ego abnui, siquidem eius continuatio a te, uti etiam scribis, pertexenda erit, tuæ prudentiæ relinquo, quid in eo faciendum vel omittendum duxeris. Habet secum gener eius Dominus D. GÖDELMANNUS, qui hîc Legatum agit Saxonicum, amicus meus singularis, quæ hac de re inter Dominum D. CHYTRÆUM et me ventilata sunt, tibi monstranda, si forte priora isthic interierunt; is etiam plenius hac de re tecum loquetur, neque tamen in his quippiam vobis præscribo aut urgeo, sed relinquo liberum agere prout lubuerit. Perinde enim apud me erit, quacunque ratione id fiat, nec ægre feram, etiamsi plane omittatur.

In privilegio hîc obtinendo pro libris tuis in publicum edendis, lubens tibi meam navabo operam, sed scire prius oportet, quales illi sint libri, et quid contineant; si enim quippiam habent, quod religioni, quam Catholicam vocant, adversatur, aut eos, qui hanc non amplectuntur, commendet, vix quippiam impetrabimus.

Nova quæ hîc narrantur, a prædicto Domino GÖDELLMANNO percipies, qui etiam nonnulla ad continuationem historiæ huius anni facientia tecum facile communicabit. Plura non addo, sum enim ita alias occupatus, ut calamum hîc sistere oporteat.

Tu itaque cum omnibus tuis quam optime et felicissime vale.
Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum 16to IXbris Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Christoph Sturtz.
Noble and most distinguished man, friend to be honoured.

From those letters which you sent me a little after the autumnal equinox, I understood in what state your affairs are, and why you have written to me more rarely. But I congratulate you on that rest and convenience for [your] studies, and gladly hold the interruption of letters excused.

About Swedish and Livonian affairs, we have learned here things similar to what you write — nay, still more: "what end of the furies do you grant, kind father?"

That business about the things which Master Dr. Chytraeus, of praised memory, himself wished — while he was alive — to be inserted into this year's Historiola (nor did I refuse), since its continuation will, as you also write, have to be woven by you, I leave to your prudence: what you judge ought, in it, to be done or omitted. His son-in-law, Master Dr. Gödelmann — who acts here as Saxon Legate, my singular friend — has with him the things which were ventilated about this matter between Master Dr. Chytraeus and me, to be shown to you, if perchance the earlier ones perished there; he will also speak with you more fully about this matter. Nor, however, do I prescribe or urge anything on you in these [matters], but leave [you] free to act as you please. For it will be all the same with me by whatever method it be done, nor will I take it ill even if it be entirely omitted.

In obtaining a privilege here for publishing your books, I will gladly do my service for you; but it is necessary first to know what kind of books they are, and what they contain. For if they have anything which is opposed to the religion which they call Catholic, or which commends those who do not embrace this [religion], we shall scarcely obtain anything.

The news which is told here, you will learn from the aforesaid Master Gödelmann, who will also easily share with you some things bearing on the continuation of this year's history. I add no more, for I am otherwise so occupied that I must here stop the pen.

You, therefore, with all yours, fare as well and as happily as possible.
Given at Prague of the Bohemians, the 16th of November, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Tie to brahe-t_16010310001 and …16001116003. Tycho's reply to Christoph Sturtz (the Rostock historian), answering Sturtz's autumn letter; written the same day (16 Nov 1600) as the parallel letter to Brasch (brahe-t_16001116003), with which it shares the Chytraeus Historiola business and Gödelmann as conduit. Sturtz's own side is brahe-t_16010310001 (March 1601).
  • The Virgilian tag. quem das finem, pater alme, furorum? — "what end of the furies [madnesses] do you grant, kind father?" — Tycho's witty adaptation of Virgil, Aeneid I.241 (Venus to Jupiter: quem das finem, rex magne, laborum?), applied to the Swedish/Livonian war (the Charles-vs-Sigismund conflict; cf. brahe-t_16000329002, …16010310001). Rendered as the quotation it is.
  • The Chytraeus autobiography. Tycho leaves it to Sturtz's judgement whether, and how, to insert into the continuation of David Chytraeus's annual chronicle the account of Tycho's life that Chytraeus had wanted (cf. brahe-t_16001116003); Gödelmann — here explicitly named as Chytraeus's son-in-law (gener) and "Saxon Legate" — holds the relevant material. Tycho is studiedly indifferent ("nor will I take it ill even if it be entirely omitted").
  • The book-privilege caveat. Tycho will help obtain a printing privilege at Prague for Sturtz's books, but warns that, in Catholic imperial Prague, "if they have anything opposed to the religion they call Catholic, or commend those who do not embrace it, we shall scarcely obtain anything" — a candid note on Counter-Reformation censorship.
  • Calendar. Pragae Bohemorum 16to IXbris 1600 = 16 November 1600 (IXbris = November, the ninth month from March); Prague (imperial) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
