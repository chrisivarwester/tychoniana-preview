The original

IACOBUS BONGARSIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

NOBILISSIME ET AMPLISSIME DOMINE.

TRES iam septimanæ abierunt, ex quo redditæ mihi sunt illæ, quas ad me dedisti IX Martij. Sed neque eas accepi, quas memoras datas sub finem superioris anni, neque FRANCISCUM TENGNAGLIUM vidi, quem a te, tanto viro, commendatum omnibus fuisse officijs proferebas. Non gravaberis itaque repetere et quæ illis litteris erant explicata, et quæ huius fidei commiseras. Me certe, magno tuo merito, habebis ad omnia, quæ quidem sunt meæ potestatis, semper paratissimum. Nec addo plura; sed mandata tua exspecto cupide. Tuas vero tute poteris committere bibliopolæ, qui CLAUDIJ MARINI WECHELIANI officinæ præest Pragæ.

Francofurti 1. Maij 1599.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Jacques Bongars to Tycho Brahe.

Most noble and most ample Master.

Three weeks have now passed since those letters were delivered to me which you sent me on 9 March. But I have neither received those which you mention as sent toward the end of last year, nor have I seen Frans Tengnagel, whom you declared to have been commended — by you, so great a man — with every good office [i.e. recommended to receive every courtesy]. You will not, therefore, find it burdensome to repeat both what was set out in those letters, and what you had entrusted to his good faith. Me certainly, by your great merit, you shall always have most ready for everything that is indeed within my power. Nor do I add more; but I eagerly await your instructions. Your own [letters] you can safely entrust to the bookseller who is in charge, at Prague, of the office of Claude de Marne the Wechelian.

Frankfurt, 1 May 1599.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Jacques Bongars (Jacobus Bongarsius, 1554–1612), French humanist, philologist, and diplomat in Imperial service; a learned correspondent and book-conduit for Tycho. The letter survives in his papers at Bern (the Bongars collection, source of the Codices Bongarsiani).
  • Franciscus Tengnaglius = Frans Gansneb genaamd Tengnagel van de Camp (c. 1576–1622), Tycho's noble assistant, later (1604) his son-in-law; here being recommended to Bongars but not yet arrived.
  • Claudij Marini Wecheliani officina = the printing-and-bookselling house of Claude de Marne (with Jean Aubry, the heirs of Andreas Wechel) — the great Frankfurt scholarly firm; Bongars names its agent at Prague as the safe relay for letters to him.
  • Near-duplicate. This letter recurs almost verbatim a year later as brahe-t_16000501001 (Frankfurt, 1 May 1600, to Tycho at Benátky) — evidently Bongars re-sent the same unanswered text; the two share the Tengnagel and Wechel-agent matter with only minor verbal variants (sunt/erunt, proferebas/prosecutus).
  • Calendar. Frankfurt am Main, a predominantly Protestant Imperial city, kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar until 1700; the date is taken as O.S. (the edition gives no n.s. tag). Flagged as a mild assumption, since the writer is a Catholic-service Frenchman; nothing in the letter turns on the style.
