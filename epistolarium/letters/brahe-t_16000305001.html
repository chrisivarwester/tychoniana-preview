The original

ILLUSTRI ET PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO TYCHONI BRAHE, IMPERATORIS RUDOLPHI II ASTRONOMO, AMICO S. S. IN AULA IMPERATORIS VEL BENATICA 5 MEILEN VON PRAG.

SÆPIUS de te mihi percontanti, BRAHEE nobilissime, nihil certi potuit significari a gentili tuo, qui heic commoratur studiorum gratiâ. Nam sese intellexisse aiebat, fore ut in Galliam te penetrares, nisi res tuæ in aulâ Cæsareâ optatissimum sortirentur exitum. Nunc autem vehementer gaudeo, Uranien tuam repperisse locum speculationi commodissimum, idque beneficio munificentissimi Imperatoris RUDOLPHI II. TAUBMANNUS et BRASCHIUS, Poëtæ laureati, me submonuerant de quibusdam carminibus in Tychen tuam componendis, quæ nunc cum hisce litteris accipies, et cum reliquis amicorum lucubrationibus, si placuerint, coniunges. Antea quoque a DAVIDE CHYTRÆO v. cl. reddita mihi fuerant exempla nonnulla epistolarum inter te et cancellarium Danicum commutatarum, ex quibus statum (vel potius ruinam) rerum tuarum, cum etiamnum in patriâ degeres, plus satis intellexi. Ea CHYTRÆO remisi, ita illo volente.

De exemplari Astronomiæ instauratæ, in quo plurimæ coloribus depictæ machinæ visuntur, magnas tibi et ago et habeo gratias: ut et de fasciculo elegantissimorum carminum unâ coniunctorum, quæ sanê libenter legi: sed tamen vehementer indolui, rem eo pervenisse, ut sponte exilium susciperes: quod nihilominus tibi ex animi sententiâ successit, fruituro meliore commoditate quam ipsâ in patriâ. Nuper fui adsiduus in manuscriptis quibusdam codicibus, ut viderem, nunquid reperirem antiqui de lapide illo Philosophico, ad quem inveniendum tot labores hactenus frustra desudavere. Investigabo etiam, si forte quid ad nos pervenerit a veteribus de rebus Astronomicis. Hoc enim te maximê expetere video. Fasciculo, quem TAUBMANNUS iussu tuo ad me transmiserat, epistola tua non fuit addita. Hanc unicam nuperrimê abs te exaratam a Dano quodam nobili accepi, cui vicissim hanc meam concredidi. Rectê fecisses, si Pragæ mihi quempiam nominasses, cui fasciculum committerem, tibi porro transmittendum. Amicus illic est mihi summus WENCESLAUS LAVINIUS ab OTTENFELDO, medicus excellentissimus, cum quo Lutetiæ familiarissimê vixi; ubi tum simul erat illustris Baro CAROLUS a ZEROTINO Moravus; Norimbergæ autem est CÆSAR CALANDRINUS, vir ornatissimus, quem adhuc vivere arbitror. Hic litteris transmittendis operam nobis suam haudquaquam denegaturus erit.

Scripsi hæc festinabundus, cum Oda quæpiam ZEROTINO Baroni inscripta ad prelum adornanda esset.

Tu bene vale et me ama.
Dat. Haidelbergæ die V. Martij veteris, anno MDC.
P. MELISSUS.

CRAMERUM vellem in Senarijs suis observare
quarto loco Iambum, qui etiam in secundo requiritur necessario. Corriget igitur,
ubicumque spondeus positus est, si vult imitari HORATIUM et SENECAM in Trag.
Produxit syllabam no in Pannoniæ et ge corripuit in Indulge. Et hoc limandum.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious and most excellent man Tycho Brahe, astronomer of Emperor Rudolf II, [his] friend, greeting. At the court of the Emperor, or at Benátky, 5 miles from Prague.

When I have rather often inquired about you, most noble Brahe, nothing certain could be told me by your kinsman who is staying here for the sake of his studies. For he said he had understood that you would press on into France, unless your affairs at the imperial court should obtain a most longed-for outcome. But now I greatly rejoice that your Urania has found a place most convenient for observation, and that by the bounty of the most munificent Emperor Rudolf II. Taubmann and Brasch, laureate Poets, had prompted me about certain poems to be composed upon your Tyche; these you will now receive with this letter, and will join, if they please, with the other lucubrations of [your] friends. Earlier too, by the renowned David Chytraeus, some copies of letters exchanged between you and the Danish chancellor had been delivered to me, from which I understood more than enough the state — or rather the ruin — of your affairs, while you still lived in your homeland. These I returned to Chytraeus, he so wishing.

For the copy of the Astronomia instaurata, in which very many engines [instruments] are seen depicted in colours, I both give and owe you great thanks; as also for the bundle of most elegant poems joined with it, which I read very gladly. Yet I grieved deeply that the matter had come to this, that you should of your own will take up exile — which has, none the less, turned out for you according to your heart's wish, you being about to enjoy a better convenience than in your homeland itself. Lately I have been assiduous over certain manuscript codices, to see whether I might find anything of the ancients about that Philosopher's Stone, to find which so many labours have hitherto sweated in vain. I will also investigate whether perhaps anything has come down to us from the ancients on Astronomical matters; for I see you seek this above all. To the bundle which Taubmann had transmitted to me by your order, your letter was not added. This one [letter], very lately written by you, I received from a certain Danish nobleman, to whom in turn I entrusted this of mine. You would have done rightly had you named me someone at Prague to whom I might commit a bundle, to be passed on to you. I have there a great friend, Wenceslaus Lavinius von Ottenfeld, a most excellent physician, with whom I lived on the most familiar terms at Paris — where at that time was likewise the illustrious Baron Karl von Žerotín, the Moravian; while at Nuremberg is Caesar Calandrinus, a most distinguished man, whom I think to be still living. He will by no means deny us his service in forwarding letters.

I have written this in haste, since a certain Ode inscribed to Baron Žerotín had to be made ready for the press.

Fare you well, and love me.
Given at Heidelberg, the 5th day of March, Old [Style], in the year 1600.
P. Melissus.

I could wish that Cramerus, in his Senarii, would observe an Iamb in the fourth place, which is also necessarily required in the second. Let him correct, then, wherever a spondee has been set, if he wishes to imitate Horace and Seneca in [his] Tragedy. He has lengthened the syllable "-no" in "Pannoniae," and shortened "-ge" in "Indulge." And this must be filed smooth.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound humanist letter to Tycho. Paulus Melissus (Paul Schede Melissus) (1539–1602), the celebrated German Neo-Latin poet, imperial poeta laureatus and librarian of the Bibliotheca Palatina at Heidelberg — writing to congratulate Tycho on his settlement under Rudolf II and to send commendatory verse.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, "astronomer of Emperor Rudolf II"; the address carries a German routing-tag — am Hof des Kaisers oder zu Benátky, 5 Meilen von Prag ("at the Emperor's court, or at Benátky, 5 miles from Prague").
  • The poems & the verse-economy.
  • Taubmannus = Friedrich Taubmann (1565–1613), Wittenberg poet and professor; Braschius = Brasch, the Rostock poet (cf. brahe-t_15980905001) — both Poetae laureati who prompted Melissus to compose verses in Tychen tuam ("on your Tyche" — a pun: Greek Týchē "Fortune"/the Muse, ≈ Tycho's name), enclosed here for Tycho's collection of friends' poems.
  • exemplar Astronomiae instauratae … machinae coloribus depictae = Melissus thanks Tycho for a colour-illustrated copy of the Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica (Wandsbek 1598), with its hand-coloured instrument plates — a notable detail on the deluxe presentation copies.
  • Persons named.
  • gentilis tuus = a kinsman of Tycho's (one of the Brahe nephews then studying in Germany) at Heidelberg.
  • David Chytraeus = David Chytraeus (1530–1600), the Rostock theologian-historian, who had shown Melissus copies of Tycho's letters with the Danish chancellor documenting "the ruin of your affairs" before the exile.
  • Wenceslaus Lavinius ab Ottenfeldo = Václav Lavín z Ottenfeldu, Bohemian physician, Melissus's intimate from his Paris (Lutetia) days, proposed as a Prague relay.
  • Carolus a Zerotino Moravus = Karel starší ze Žerotína (Karl von Žerotín the Elder, 1564–1636), the Moravian magnate, also known to Melissus from Paris and the dedicatee of an Ode now at press.
  • Caesar Calandrinus = Cesare Calandrini, at Nuremberg, another possible courier.
  • The alchemy/antiquity note. Melissus offers to search the Palatina's manuscript codices for ancient lore on the Philosopher's Stone (lapis Philosophicus) and on astronomy — "for I see you seek this above all," a glance at Tycho's alchemical and source-gathering interests.
  • Place. Heidelberga = Heidelberg; Lutetia = Paris; Norimberga = Nuremberg.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. S. in the address ≈ salutem [plurimam]; bene vale et me ama → "fare well, and love me"; Dat. = Datum → "Given."
  • Calendar (the edition's convention — explicit Old Style). Dateline die V. Martij veteris = "5 March, Old Style" — the Calvinist Palatinate kept the Julian calendar; marked expressly veteris [styli], to be contrasted with Tycho's New-Style Prague datelines. Year MDC = 1600. Reproduced, not converted.
  • What this is. A closing paragraph of technical literary criticism, added just before the letter's actual farewell (which the published tail preserves) — the humanist poet Melissus giving Tycho a metrical critique to pass on (or simply reporting his own editorial work) on verses in senarii, the six-foot iambic line used in Latin comedy and tragedy.
  • The sender. Paulus Melissus (Paul Schede, 1539–1602), the leading German Neo-Latin poet of his generation, court librarian at Heidelberg; his closing lines (in the published tail) mention preparing an ode to Baron Karl von Žerotín for the press at the very moment of writing — i.e., this is a working poet's letter, and the Cramerus critique belongs to that same professional register.
  • The metrical point, unpacked. In the iambic senarius, feet 2, 4, and 6 traditionally require a true iamb (short-long) rather than a substitutable spondee (long-long); Melissus is telling Cramerus that his fourth foot needs the same strictness already expected of the second, on the model of Horace and Seneca's tragic verse — plus two specific scansion faults (an incorrectly long "-no" in "Pannoniae"; an incorrectly short "-ge" in "Indulge").
  • "Cramerus" — not identified in the works consulted. No figure of this name surfaced in the Tycho-circle biographical sources searched; he was presumably a minor Neo-Latin poet in Melissus's or Tycho's orbit whose verses (perhaps addressed to Tycho, given the letter's destination) needed polishing before publication. Flagged as an open identification rather than guessed at.
  • Register note. "Diatribam"/technical-prosody vocabulary aside, this passage is a useful reminder that Tycho's correspondence network extended into pure literary humanism — verse composition and correction as a shared court accomplishment, not merely astronomy.
