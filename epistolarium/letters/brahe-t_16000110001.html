The original

CLARISSIMO ET EXCELLENTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO IACOBO MONAVIO, PATRITIO VRATISLAVIENSI, ILLUSTRISSIMI DUCIS SILESIAe CONSILIARIO DIGNISSIMO, AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO.

S. P.

PAUCIS diebus ex quo Pragam veneram, Excellentissime et amantissime Domine MONAVI, schedam quandam a te e vestra Silesia mihi asportavit Dominus D. SCHWALMIUS, si recte memini: quae nihil aliud quam de Carmine quodam Domini TOBIAe FISCHERI Poëtae vestratis, quâ occasione id in eum modum concinnatum sit, continebat. Grata mihi fuit tua chartula ipsiusque Carmen, in quo paucula verba mutata sunt iuxta censuram criticam, prout in adiunctis pagellis videre est, quibus etiam nonnulla alia, praeter ea, quae prius habes, adscripta sunt. Velim, ut tam haec quam priora cum eodem communices. Scripsissem ipsemet iam illi, si literas eius ad me humanissimas tam cito invenire potuissem. Nescio enim, ubi illas cum quibusdam alijs, interea dum res meae huc Pragâ transferuntur, reposuerim. Quando eas reperire dabitur, rescriptionem non ulterius intermittam. Interim ipsum meo nomine officiose saluta, et pro benevolo suo erga me animo gratias age.

Scripsi tibi aliquoties antea de libris Witichianis a sorore haerede coëmendis. Quod si iam tua operâ praestari possit, ut iusto precio eos obtinere queam, praesertim quoad Exemplaria quae habuit tria COPERNICI a se marginalibus quibusdam illustrata de Revolutionibus Coelestibus, et quae de Triangulis conscripserat, atque de Hypothesibus Coelestibus si quae inveniuntur, feceris mihi rem apprime gratam. Precium indicatum confestim solvam. Relatum quoque ad me est, quendam apud vos ante aliquot Annos fuisse Astronomiae peritum, qui observationes nonnullas Coelestes suo tempore perfecerat; quas filium ipsius adhuc conservare aiunt. Habitat autem is (si rite audita recordor) in plateâ quam vocant die Stockgasse, nomen eius excidit; tu (opinor) facile id conijcies atque inquires, rogoque peramanter, ut ab ipso impetrare velis earundem observationum, quot et qualescunque fuerint, descriptum mihi concedi. Inveniar vicissim erga illum gratus. Indica insuper, ubi rescripseris (quod te mature facturum confido) an aliqua apud vos sit Typographia, quae tales stylos habeat, qualibus liber primus Epistolarum Astronomicarum e nostris, quem existimo te habere, excusus est. Constitui enim alterum Tomum, in quo 12 primi quaterniones, antequam res meae in Daniâ conturbarentur, absoluti sunt, complere, in eâdem formâ papyri et characteris, quâ prior confectus est. Et transige simul cum Typographo idoneo, quid pro quolibet quaternione, ita ut Exemplaria imprimantur 1500, illi numerandum sit. Et simul papyri valerem indica, quidque correctori gnaro in singulas septimanas dandum. Si intellexero, omnia isthic rite succedere posse, mittam unâ cum Exemplari ad mundum scripto pecuniam, pro quâ papyrus coëmatur, et coetera persolvantur. Ignoscas vero mihi, mi MONAVI, quod te alias occupatissimum meis negotijs sic molestem. Ego quidvis vicissim tuâ caussâ faciam, quâcunque in re mea opera tibi unquam usui esse poterit.

Vale.
Dabantur ex Arce Caesareanâ Benaticâ, ubi nunc cum meis habito, die 10 Ianuarij stylo renovato Anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most excellent man, Lord Jacob Monau, patrician of Breslau, most worthy Councillor of the Most Illustrious Duke of Silesia, his friend especially to be honoured.

Heartiest greeting.

A few days after I had come to Prague, most excellent and most loving Lord Monau, a certain note from you, from your Silesia, was brought to me by Lord Doctor Schwalm, if I remember rightly — which contained nothing other than [matter] about a certain Poem of Lord Tobias Fischer, a poet of your parts, on what occasion it had been composed in that fashion. Your note was welcome to me, and his Poem, in which a few little words have been changed according to critical judgement, as may be seen in the appended leaves, to which some other things, besides those you already have, have also been added. I should wish you to share both these and the earlier [pieces] with the same [poet]. I would have written to him myself already, could I have found his most courteous letters to me so quickly; for I know not where I have put them, with certain others, while my things are being moved hither [to and from] Prague. When it shall be granted me to find them, I will delay a reply no further. Meanwhile greet him dutifully in my name, and give thanks for his benevolent disposition toward me.

I have written to you several times before about the Wittich books, to be bought from the sister-heir. And if it can now be brought about by your effort that I may obtain them at a just price — especially as regards the three Copies he had of Copernicus, On the Celestial Revolutions, annotated by himself with certain marginal notes, and what he had written On Triangles, and On the Celestial Hypotheses, if any are found — you will have done me a thing exceedingly welcome. The price named, I will pay at once. It has also been reported to me that there was among you, some years ago, a man skilled in Astronomy, who had made some celestial observations in his time, which they say his son still keeps. He lives (if I rightly recall what I heard) in the street they call die Stockgasse; his name escapes me. You, I think, will easily guess and inquire it; and I most lovingly ask that you would obtain from him that a copy of those observations — however many, and of whatever kind, they were — be granted to me; I will in turn be found grateful toward him. Indicate moreover, when you reply (which I trust you will do in good time), whether there is any printing-house among you that has such types as those by which the first book of our Astronomical Letters — which I think you have — was printed. For I have resolved to complete the second Tome, of which the first 12 gatherings were finished before my affairs in Denmark were thrown into confusion, in the same form of paper and type as the former was made. And settle at the same time with a suitable Printer what is to be paid him for each gathering, such that 1,500 copies be printed; and at the same time indicate the cost of paper, and what is to be given weekly to a skilled corrector. If I understand that everything can proceed properly there, I will send, together with a fair copy, the money with which paper may be bought and the rest paid. But forgive me, my Monau, that I trouble you — otherwise most occupied — thus with my business; I will in turn do anything for your sake, in whatever matter my effort may ever be of use to you.

Farewell.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, where I now dwell with my household, the 10th day of January, in the renewed [New] style, of the year 1600.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Jacob Monau (Jacobus Monavius) (1546–1603), Breslau humanist, patricius Vratislaviensis (patrician of Breslau / Wrocław) and Councillor of the Duke of Silesia — a noted literary patron and Tycho's Silesian agent (cf. brahe-t_16010322001, where Monau is proposed as a letter-relay). Vratislaviensis = of Breslau/Wrocław (not Bratislava).
- The Tobias Fischer poem. A commendatory Carmen by Tobias Fischer, a Silesian poet, sent via Dominus D. Schwalmius (Doctor Schwalm); Tycho returns it lightly emended "according to critical judgement," with additions, for Monau to pass back to the poet — the routine traffic of the friends'-verse collection.
- The Wittich books (the historically significant request). Tycho repeats a standing request that Monau buy for him, "from the sister-heir," the library of the late Paul Wittich (the Breslau mathematician, d. 1586, who had visited Tycho at Hven in 1580 and exchanged computational methods — prosthaphaeresis; cf. the Tycho–Hagecius correspondence, e.g. brahe-t_15820923001). He names specifically:
- the three copies of Copernicus's De revolutionibus orbium coelestium annotated in Wittich's own hand (marginalibus … a se illustrata);
- Wittich's writings On Triangles (de Triangulis — trigonometry); and
- On the Celestial Hypotheses (de Hypothesibus Coelestibus), "if any are found."
The Wittich-annotated volumes. These are the Wittich-annotated Copernicus copies central to the modern "Wittich-not-Tycho marginalia" question — surviving exemplars include the Apianus–Wittich volume at the University of Chicago and a Copernicus at Prague; marginalia of this group were later misattributed to Tycho. This letter is Tycho's own documented attempt to acquire them.
- The Breslau astronomer's observations. Tycho also seeks, via Monau, a copy of the celestial observations of an unnamed deceased Breslau astronomer, kept by his son, who lives "in the street called die Stockgasse" — Tycho's habitual hunt for predecessors' observational records. (Name not recovered — flagged.)
- Printing the second Tome of the Epistolae in Silesia (printing history). Tycho asks whether a Breslau press has type matching the first book of his Astronomical Letters (Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus, Uraniborg 1596), so as to complete the second Tome — of which the first 12 gatherings were printed "before my affairs in Denmark were thrown into confusion" — in matching paper and type. Concrete figures: a press-run of 1,500 copies, with per-gathering printer's fee, paper cost, and weekly pay for a skilled corrector all to be negotiated. A valuable datum for the production economics of Tycho's books.
- Persons named. Jacob Monau; Schwalmius (Doctor Schwalm, the courier); Tobias Fischer (the poet); Paul Wittich (the late mathematician); the unnamed Breslau astronomer and his son.
- Calendar. Dateline die 10 Ianuarij stylo renovato Anni 1600 — stylo renovato = the renewed (New/Gregorian) style; imperial usage. Header agrees with the label.
- Correction. Latin corrected: 'PATRITO' to 'PATRITIO' in the salutation, and 'molestam' to 'molestem' (subjunctive), per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII pp.236, 237.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VIII pp. 235–237; defects the audit noted: text error (2).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Read Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.236-237 in full (within the 235-238 extract; the Monau letter itself runs from mid-236 to mid-237, flanked by the Scultetus and Bochazius letters). Body content, names (Schwalmius, Tobias Fischer, die Stockgasse), numbers (three annotated Copernicus copies, 12 gatherings, 1500 copies), and the closing date all match Dreyer exactly, and the translation is accurate throughout. Two single-letter corruptions survive in our Latin transcription versus Dreyer's print (PATRITO/PATRITIO; molestam/molestem); per the grading rule any text error caps the grade at C even though both are low severity and the translation itself is sound.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). Our Latin drops the -I- in PATRITIO (patrician) versus Dreyer's print; a single-letter corruption in the source, not in the translation, which renders the sense correctly regardless. Dreyer prints: PATRITIO VRATISLAVIENSI.
- text error (low). Dreyer prints the grammatically required present subjunctive 'molestem' (that I may trouble); our transcribed original has 'molestam', an ungrammatical corruption. Translation sense is unaffected. Dreyer prints: sic molestem.

Repaired. Both defects verified against Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.236-237 (page images, not the digitised text layer); both are single-letter corruptions in the Latin with no effect on the already-correct English. No other part of the letter was touched.
