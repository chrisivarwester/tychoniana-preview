The original

DEM GESTRENGEN, EDLEN VND ERNVESTEN HERREN TYCHO BRAHEN ITZO ZU WANDESBECK, MEINEM BESONDERN GUNSTIGEN LIEBEN HERREN VNDT FORDERER.

GENEROSE et Magnifice D. TYCHO, omni cum observantia colende mihi Domine. Negotium commissum apud D. IOANNEM BŒKELIUM, quomodo confecerim, a M.V. ex scheda inserta apparebit. Duci OTTONI Brunsvicensium et Luneburgensium de sententia Μ.V. diligenter scripsi. C. ipsius omnino acquiescit et..quod M. Vestræ commodum futurum est. De accessu Reginæ in hanc civitatem nihil ad me perfertur, sicut et ex filio nihil tale accipio. Verum is, quæ alias publice et notorie sparguntur, confirmat. M.V. et mihi quoque ubi vacaverit a negotijs, ...dabo operam, ut illam invisam. Reliqua committo prudentiæ Μ.V. Quæ ut cum suis optime valeat, toto pectore opto. Datæ raptim Hamburgæ XI. Martij An. 1598.

Μ.V. omni cum observantia
qui colo
VITUS WINSHEMIUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the stern, noble, and honourable Lord Tycho Brahe, now at Wandsbek, my especially gracious, dear lord and patron.

Noble and Magnificent Master Tycho, my lord to be honoured with all respect. How I have carried out the business entrusted [to me] with Master Johann Bokel will appear to Your Magnificence from the slip [of paper] enclosed. I have written diligently to the Duke of Brunswick and Lüneburg, [Otto], concerning Your Magnificence's view. His Highness [C(elsitudo)] entirely concurs, and ⟨…⟩ what will be to Your Magnificence's advantage. Concerning the Queen's arrival in this city nothing is reported to me, just as I get nothing of the sort from [my] son either; he does, however, confirm the things that are otherwise being put about publicly and notoriously. When it shall be free of business [time permitting] both for Your Magnificence and for me as well, ⟨…⟩ I shall take pains to visit her. The rest I commit to Your Magnificence's prudence. That you, with yours, may fare most excellently, I wish with my whole heart. Given in haste at Hamburg, 11 March, the year 1598.

Your Magnificence's, with all respect,
[he] who honours [you],
Vitus Winshemius.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Vitus Winshemius = Vitus Winshemius (Veit Oertel/Ortel von Windsheim), the younger — here a Hamburg agent/intermediary of Tycho's during his Wandsbek exile; he acted as Tycho's local man of affairs in the city (the Latin epistolary register and the D. Doctor address in Tycho's reply imply a learned physician/lawyer of the Hamburg circle). Tycho's reply survives, dated 28 March 1598, Wandsbek (identifier brahe-t_15980328001), opening "Excellentissime Domine Doctor Vinshemi…". The name is shared with the better-known Wittenberg Greek scholar Veit Oertel von Windsheim (1501–1570), who is too early to be this writer.
  • Recipient / place. Tycho Brahe, then resident at Wandsbek (Wandesbeck / Wandsburg), the estate of Heinrich Rantzau just outside Hamburg, where Tycho lived in 1597–98 after leaving Denmark and where he completed and printed the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (1598). The letter is sent from Hamburg (Hamburgae) into Wandsbek.
  • D. Ioannem Boekelium = Johann Bökel (Johannes Bokelius, 1535–1605), the leading Hamburg physician of the day, professor and city physician (Stadtphysicus). Some "business" (negotium) of Tycho's had been transacted through him; the details were sent on a separate enclosed slip (scheda inserta), now lost.
  • Duci Ottoni Brunsvicensium et Luneburgensium = the Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, Otto — i.e. of the house of Braunschweig-Lüneburg. (Tycho's circle of ducal dedicatees of the Mechanica included Duke Otto II of Braunschweig-Lüneburg, 1528–1603, recipient of a presentation copy; the identification of this Otto with that duke is plausible but not made explicit in the letter, so the given name is bracketed in the translation.)
  • Regina — "the Queen": in the Hamburg–Danish context of 1598 most naturally Sophie of Mecklenburg, queen dowager of Denmark (Tycho's sometime visitor), but the letter does not name her; the rumour of a royal "arrival in this city" is reported only as hearsay (publice et notorie sparguntur) and the writer keeps it expressly unconfirmed. Identification left open per the source's own reserve.
  • ex filio — "from [my] son": Winshemius's own son, evidently a source of city news; unnamed.
  • Abbreviations. M.V. / Μ.V. = Magnificentia Vestra, "Your Magnificence" (the standard honorific answering the address Magnifice D. Tycho). The source alternates the Latin majuscule M.V. with a Greek capital Mu (Μ.V.) — a scribal/typographic variation in Langebek's copy, here reproduced as printed (source block) and rendered uniformly "Your Magnificence." C. (in C. ipsius … acquiescit) is expanded as C(elsitudo), "His Highness" (i.e. the Duke just named); the expansion is editorial and so the rendering is supplied with the bracketed source letter.
  • Lacunae in the witness. The copy-text carries two breaks, reproduced verbatim in the source block: (1) "et..quod M. Vestrae commodum futurum est" — a short gap after et, here marked ⟨…⟩; the syntax around the gap is incomplete and the exact connective sense ("and [will do] what will be to Your Magnificence's advantage") is reconstructed, not secure; (2) "…dabo operam, ut illam invisam" — a gap before dabo, also marked ⟨…⟩. Both are gaps in Langebek's Afskrift, not editorial cuts; they are flagged, not smoothed.
  • scheda inserta — "the enclosed slip [of paper]" (a separate sheet sent with the letter), now not preserved with the text.
  • Honorific / closing formulae. Address Gestrengen, edlen vnd ernvesten Herren — the German chancery courtesy stack "stern (i.e. authoritative), noble, and honourable lord," kept in register; Forderer = "patron, promoter." Subscription "Μ.V. omni cum observantia qui colo" = "Your Magnificence's, with all respect, [he] who honours [you]" — the relative qui colo left slightly elliptical in the Latin and rendered with a bracketed antecedent.
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae raptim Hamburgae XI. Martij An. 1598 = "Given in haste at Hamburg, 11 March, the year 1598" (Old Style: Denmark and the north-German Lutheran cities kept the Julian calendar in 1598). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1598. 11. marts. Hamborg); no discrepancy. raptim ("in haste") kept.
