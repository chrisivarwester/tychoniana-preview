The original

CLARISSIMO EXCELLENTISSIMOQUE VIRO D. THADDÆO HAGGECIO AB HAYCK, PHILOSOPHIÆ ET MEDICINÆ DOCTORI ATQUE ADMINISTRATORI EXIMIO, SUO IN PRIMIS COLENDO AMICO.
S.

ACCEPI iam nunc tuas literas, Pragæ ultima Novembris datas, quas mihi Hamburgo misit clariss. D. D. VITUS WINSHEMIUS. Intelligo autem ex ijs, te admodum solicitum esse, an priores tuæ mihi redditæ sint, vererisque, ut eas receperim; ideoque scriptione tamdiu supersedeam præsertim cum nihil per WINSHEMIUM ad vos proficiscentem scripserim, ex quibus omnibus satis perspicio benevolum tuum erga me affectum et amoris sinceri non obscura indicia. At ignorabam, mi amantissime THADDÆE, D. WINSHEMIUM ad Imperatoriam Maiestatem hinc Legati munus sustinuisse, alias non intermisissem, quin tibi scriberem. Rarius nunc Aulam nostram accedo, quam antea, vivente optimæ et laudatiss. memoriæ Rege nostro FREDERICO, qui me sæpius accersiri curabat, ut nolens volens tunc crebro Aulæ adesse cogerer; at nunc lubentius et quietius otio fruor philosophico meisque studijs vaco, nec quid rerum in Aula geratur, magnopere quæro. Facile itaque evenire potuit, ut WINSHEMIUS me inscio vos accesserit. Scripseram tibi nihilominus iam antea circa Novembris initia, et quaternis tuis unicis luculenter respondi, unâque Apologiam, quam ijsdem pene diebus ad insignem quendam in Scotia Mathematicum, IOANNEM CRAIGUM nomine, pro ijs, quæ Tomo secundo nostro, ab illo in dubium vocata, continentur, adornâram, tibi inspiciendam misi. Et ne literæ facile interciderent, præsertim ob hyemis difficultates, binas conscribi, unasque Hamburgo, alteras Lubecâ, opera Nobiliss. viri et rerum Astronomicarum amantiss. Domini HENRICI RANZOVIJ, Regis nostri in Holsatia Locumtenentis, amici mei singularis, ad te transmitti curavi. Addidi quoque ad Nobilissimum et doctiss. virum D. IACOBUM CURTIUM, Imperij Procancellarium, te admonente, quasdam, etiam bis eâdem de caussa transcriptas. Spero autem, vos alteras saltem harum, si non utrasque, iam nunc accepisse, ideoque te ea, qua detinebaris, suspicione exemtum. Ego de tua fide atque integritate, tum etiam erga me constanti amicitia nihil prorsus addubito, mi THADDÆE, ut ea, quam adfers, excusatione opus non fuisset. Ego potius quam tu in culpa sum, qui tamdiu tibi respondere procrastinârim. Optabam quidem tota præterita æstate, mihi aliquem offerri certum internuntium, per quem tibi rescriberem, sed cum id ex voto non contingeret, præter voluntatem responsionem usque in hyemis initia differre coactus sum, quemadmodum ultimis me hoc nomine apud te purgabam, ex quibus etiam satis percepisti, me omnes tuas sartas tectas unâ cum WERNERI libro accepisse, ut hac te solicitudine iam satis liberatum existimem. Ijsdem etiam indicavi, cur primus Tomus nostri Operis, quod præ manibus habeo, nondum prodierit. Chartopæus, quem Lubecam in æstate miseram, moletrinæ meæ (de qua etiam scripsi, me pro papyro conficienda quandam extrui curasse) necdum reversus est. Sed pro alio conquirendo hincinde in Germaniam scripsi. Et nisi hyemis asperitas portus congelasset, iam aliquem eiuscemodi artificem obtinuissem, quem et me brevi habiturum spero, ut papyrus impressoria in maiori copia paretur. Interim tamen eam, quæ in promptu est, non feriari sino. Est enim nunc sub prælo primus ille Tomus, quem desideras, et duo Alphabeta iam impressa sunt, restat sesquialterum. Curo etiam Epistolarum Astronomicarum volumen alio prælo excudi, quod non minus iustæ erit magnitudinis. Sicque duo præla indies urgeri iubeo, quæ non parum papyri absumunt, ut nisi mature chartopæum acceperim, aut aliunde papyrum sufficientem acquisiverim , quod difficulter hyeme fit, vereor, ne Opus ipsum remoram aliquam sentiat, quod tamen, quantum in me est, ne fiat, præcavebo. Ad Nundinas Francofurtianas vix primum librum mittere dabitur, quod desiderem adhuc Privilegium nuperi Galliarum Regis, mihi ab eius Legato, qui brevi nobiscum fuit, ad Pascatis festum promissum. Sic etiam Anglicanum per ROGERSIUM procurandum adhuc expecto. Interim et tertium Tomum adornabo, ac conabor, ut et is, quam citissime fieri possit, expediatur. Si quid Deus votis nostris faverit, efficiam, ut instante æstate aliquando horum reddaris particeps, unâ cum Epistolarum libro, qui plurima rem Astronomicam illustranda et negotium etiam hoc Cometicum, de quo nunc agimus, disquirentia continebit. Adduntur et tuæ, quotquot de Astronomicis mecum conferentes hactenus obtinui. Rescriptiones meas alicubi desidero, quod exemplum non ad omnes in promptu sit, si tu reservasti, cura describi mihique mature mitti. Id etiam et ultimis, si recte memini, petij. Quod speculam pro observationibus Astronomicis in novis tuis ædibus extruere decreveris, in primis laudo. Deus concedat tibi prosperam et diuturnam senectam, ut his et alijs rebus eximijs diu vacare queas. Si aliæ curæ, tum etiam senij gravedo te nonnunquam averterint, ut tibi ipsi cœlestibus invigilare minus commodum fuerit, facile unum vel alterum iuvenem harum rerum cupidum sustentabis, qui observationes per opportunam serenitatem expediat, modo Instrumenta idonea non defuerint. Da operam, ut Sextantem quendam ad instar eius, cuius ideam iam dudum (ni fallor) misi, habeas, eo enim distantias exactas et altitudines etiam, si sic lubet, satis scrupulose rimari datur. Radius Astronomicus eam, quam pollicetur, præcisionem non præstat, quomodocunque subdivisus et compositus. Vide etiam, ut Quadrantem aliquem Azimuthalem solide elaboratum unâ obtineas, qui ad minimum singula minuta suppeditet. Quod si Armillas quasdam Æquatorias ad instar mearum, præsertim earum, quæ sesquialteram solummodo Armillam requirunt, unâ confieri curaveris, his tribus organis, quicquid in observationibus Astronomicis desideras, facile et tuto absolves, et si quod supervenerit, ut proximis annis, ascititium sidus, illud certius et accuratius quam antea demetiri dabitur. Ubi hi duo Tomi, de quibus dixi, publicati fuerint, plurium Machinarum Astronomicarum in ijs designationem invenies, ut eligere possis, quæ tibi commodior visa fuerit. Divisionem Instrumentorum per puncta transversalia fac ut subtiliter perficias, et Dioptra rimulas parandas priori pinnacidio habentia adhibeas, prout in fine libri secundi hæc aliquo modo exprimuntur. Plenariam de omnibus his conceptionem obtinebis, quando Opus nostrum de Mechanica Astronomiæ parte absolutum fuerit, in quo 24 ad minimum varia ratione adinventa constructaque Instrumenta Astronomica, quoad eorum fabricam et usum, ubertim exponere constitui et plæraque alia observationibus inservientia adiungere. Apologiam, quam misi, quæso diligenter perlustres, et si quid inveneris, ubi satisfactum non esse putas, admone. Ego illam inter plurimas alias occupationes meo Amanuensi subito, prout in buccam venerunt, dictavi, adeo ut vix otium superesset relegendi. Si quid deesse intellexero, per occasionem supplebo. Et si tibi ita consultum videtur, Apologiam hanc secundo Tomo de eodem Cometa agenti subiungam, quo locupletior redditus priorem adæquet. Habeo siquidem adhuc ferme omnia exemplaria non distracta. Nolui enim vulgaria fieri, antequam omnes Tomi typis excusi essent. De rebus Gallicis, quæ addis, perceptu grata sunt, quin et nos similia his percipimus, Regem videlicet novum felici uti successu, det Deus, ut indies magis ac magis prosperetur. Genevenses obsidione liberatos gaudeo. Deus pacem et tranquillitatem tam Ecclesiæ quam Rebuspublicis largiri dignetur.

Scotorum Rex his ipsis diebus ad vicinam Helsingoram Arcemque Croneburgum advenit unâ cum sua nova nupta Regina, Regis nostri feliciss. memoriæ filia, magnoque e Regni Primoribus comitatu. Cum enim illa circa finem æstatis in Scotiam appellere nequiret, classe nostra, qua vehebatur, semel atque iterum adversa tempestate in Norvegiam repulsa tandemque ob ingruentem hyemem navigationem intermittente, sereniss. ille Rex conscensis suis navibus sponsam (quid non cogit Amor?) inter rupes Norvegicas quærens adinvenit, petensque a sereniss. Regina Regnique Gubernatoribus Consiliarijs nos accedendi copiam fieri, ex quo in nostras ditiones advenisset, rogatus, hacque lubentiss. impetrata, multo et difficili, hyberno præsertim tempore, itinere huc accessit atque hîc cum suis hybernaturus, donec libera concedatur navigatio, nunc Croneburgi honorifice excipitur. Quod tibi indicandum duxi, ut et quid novarum rerum in publicis apud nos agatur, cognosceres. Vale, et si ante Pasca Privilegium denuo impetratum, de quo ultimo scripsi, tum etiam MUNOSIJ libellum et quos præterea habes, de re Cometica agentes, miseris, rem pergratam præstiteris.

Iterum quam felicissime vale, mi amantissime exoptatissimeque THADDÆE.
Uraniburgi 25. Ian. Anno 90.
Tui amantiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

POST SCRIPTA. CUM eo ipso die, quo has literas meo Amanuensi ad mundum describendas tradidissem, circa vesperae Veneris a Sole digressioni maximae observandae intenderem, ecce Cometes quidam ad eandem occiduam plagam ex improviso apparuit, ideoque hasce literas interea mecum reservandas duxi, donec omnes istius ascititij sideris apparentias coelitus per totam eius durationem accurate deductas scrutatus essem, ut mea etiam inventa, ex quo sciam te talibus plurimum affici, una transmitterem. Addidi itaque brevem eiusdem Cometae descriptionem, qualem illustrissimo quoque Principi WILHELMO, Hassiae Landgravio, nuper hac de re scribens communicavi. Plenius de eodem tertio nostro Tomo agam. Illic enim hunc prioribus adiungere omnesque eius apparentias, quodque omnino Parallaxi destituebatur, e multiplicibus et certis observationibus Geometrice demonstrare constitui; interim has pauculas de eodem crinito sidere conceptiones, e ratis tamen animadversionibus derivatas, boni consule. Quod autem tamdiu a prima scriptione negotium hucusque distulerim, praeter opinionem meam factum est. Licet enim expectandum erat, donec Cometes ille prorsus evanesceret, quod post sextum diem Martij accidit, et aliquantulum temporis ad eius habitudines ex observationibus rimandas requireretur, accidit insuper, ut statim post Martij medium Serenissimus Scotorum Rex cum splendido suo comitatu me hic inviseret, atque ex eo tempore per aliquot continuos dies pluribus peregrinis hospitibus excipiendis occuparer, quo minus inceptam telam pertexere licuerit. Hincque factum est, quod literas hasce usque in sextam septimanam mecum retinuerim, quod indicandum duxi, ne cur tam diverso tempore haec scribantur, mirareris, sed moram hanc eo citius excusatam haberes. Iterum vale. Haec scribebantur quarto Aprilis.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the most renowned and most excellent man, Master Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, Doctor of Philosophy and of Medicine and distinguished Administrator, his friend to be honoured above all — greeting.

I have just now received your letter, dated at Prague on the last day of November, which the most renowned Master Doctor Vitus Winshemius sent to me from Hamburg. I gather from it that you are quite anxious as to whether your earlier letters were delivered to me, and that you fear I may [not] have received them, and that for that reason I have so long held off from writing — especially since I wrote nothing by Winshemius as he was setting out to you; and from all this I perceive well enough your kindly disposition toward me and no obscure tokens of a sincere affection. But I did not know, my most loving Thaddaeus, that Master Winshemius had undertaken from here the office of Legate to His Imperial Majesty; otherwise I would not have failed to write to you. I now go to our Court more rarely than before, in the lifetime of our King Frederick, of best and most praised memory, who often had me summoned, so that, willing or not, I was then frequently compelled to be present at Court; but now I more gladly and more quietly enjoy philosophical leisure and give myself to my studies, nor do I greatly inquire what is being done in the affairs of the Court. So it could easily have come about that Winshemius came to you without my knowledge. I had nonetheless written to you already before, around the beginning of November, and replied fully to your single sheet [of four leaves], and at the same time I sent for you to inspect an Apologia, which in those same days, almost, I had prepared for a certain distinguished mathematician in Scotland, John Craig by name, in defence of the things contained in our second Tome which had been called in doubt by him. And lest the letters should easily go astray, especially because of the difficulties of winter, I had two [copies] written out, and arranged for one to be sent to you from Hamburg, the other from Lübeck, by the agency of the most noble man and most devoted to astronomical matters, Master Henrik Rantzau, our King's Lieutenant in Holstein, my especial friend. I also added — at your reminding — certain [letters] to the most noble and most learned man Master Jacob Curtius, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, likewise transcribed twice for the same reason. I hope, however, that you have by now received at least one set of these, if not both, and that you are therefore freed from the suspicion in which you were held [back]. As for me, I have no doubt at all about your faith and integrity, and also about your constant friendship toward me, my Thaddaeus, so that there was no need of the excuse you bring forward. It is I rather than you who am at fault, in having so long put off replying to you. Indeed I wished throughout the whole past summer that some sure messenger might be offered me, by whom I might write back to you, but since this did not happen as I wished, against my will I was compelled to defer my reply right up to the beginning of winter — just as in my last [letter] I cleared myself with you on this score, from which too you understood well enough that I had received all your [letters] safe and sound together with Werner's book, so that I reckon you are now sufficiently freed from this anxiety. In the same [letter] I also indicated why the first Tome of our Work, which I have in hand, has not yet appeared. The papermaker, whom I had sent to Lübeck in the summer, has not yet returned to my mill (concerning which I also wrote that I had had a certain one built for the making of paper). But to seek out another I have written here and there into Germany. And had not the harshness of the winter frozen the harbours, I would by now have obtained some craftsman of this kind, whom I hope to have shortly, so that printing-paper may be procured in greater quantity. In the meantime, however, I do not let what is at hand lie idle. For that first Tome which you desire is now under the press, and two gatherings [alphabets] are already printed; one and a half remain. I am also having the volume of Astronomical Letters struck off at another press, which will be of no less due magnitude. And so I order two presses to be driven forward daily, which consume not a little paper, so that, unless I receive the papermaker in good time, or acquire sufficient paper from elsewhere — which is done with difficulty in winter — I fear lest the Work itself feel some delay; which nevertheless, so far as in me lies, I shall take precaution against, that it not happen. Scarcely will it be granted to send the first book to the Frankfurt fair, because I still want the Privilege of the recent King of the French, promised me by his Legate, who was lately with us, by the feast of Easter. So too I still await the English [Privilege], to be procured through Rogers. In the meantime I shall also prepare the third Tome, and shall try that it too may be dispatched as quickly as can be done. If God shall favour our prayers at all, I shall bring it about that, with the coming summer, you shall at some point be made a partaker of these, together with the book of Letters, which will contain very many things to illuminate astronomical matters and others inquiring into this Cometary business too, of which we are now treating. Yours too are included, as many as I have hitherto obtained of those conferring with me on astronomical matters. My replies I miss in one place or another, since a copy is not at hand for all of them; if you have kept them, see that they are transcribed and sent to me in good time. This too I asked, if I rightly remember, in my last [letter]. That you have resolved to build a watch-tower [observatory] for astronomical observations in your new house, I praise above all. May God grant you a prosperous and long old age, that you may long be free to devote yourself to these and other excellent matters. If other cares, and also the heaviness of age, should now and then turn you aside, so that it be less convenient for you yourself to keep watch over the heavens, you will easily support one or another young man eager for these things, who may carry out observations through favourable clear weather — provided suitable instruments be not wanting. Take pains that you have a certain Sextant after the pattern of the one whose design I sent long ago (unless I am mistaken), for by it exact distances, and altitudes too, if so it please, may be searched out scrupulously enough. The cross-staff (radius astronomicus) does not furnish the precision it promises, in whatever way it be subdivided and constructed. See also that you obtain, along with it, some Azimuthal Quadrant solidly wrought, which may yield single minutes at the least. And if you arrange to have made along with these certain Equatorial Armillae after the pattern of mine — especially those that require only one and a half armillary [rings] — with these three instruments you will easily and surely accomplish whatever you desire in astronomical observations; and if some adventitious star should come upon [you], as in recent years, it will be granted to measure it out more surely and more accurately than before. When these two Tomes of which I have spoken are published, you will find in them the delineation of very many Astronomical Machines, so that you may choose the one that shall seem more convenient for you. See that you carry out the division of the Instruments by transversal points finely, and that you employ Sights [dioptra] having little slits to be made [in addition to] the foremost pinnule, just as these things are in some manner expressed at the end of the second book. You will obtain a full conception of all these matters when our Work on the Mechanical part of Astronomy has been completed, in which I have resolved to set forth abundantly at least 24 Astronomical Instruments, devised and constructed by various method, as to their fabrication and use, and to add most other things serving observations. The Apologia which I sent, I ask you to peruse diligently, and if you find anything where you think satisfaction has not been given, advise me. I dictated it amid very many other occupations to my amanuensis on the sudden, just as the words came to my lips, so that scarcely any leisure remained for reading it over. If I understand that anything is lacking, I shall supply it as occasion offers. And if it seems to you advisable so, I shall append this Apologia to the second Tome treating of the same Comet, that, rendered fuller, it may match the former [in extent]. For I still have almost all the copies unsold. For I did not wish them to become common before all the Tomes were struck in type. Concerning French affairs, which you add, they are pleasing to learn of; indeed we too learn of things similar to these — namely that the new King meets with happy success; may God grant that day by day he prosper more and more. I rejoice that the Genevans have been freed from the siege. May God deign to bestow peace and tranquillity upon the Church as well as upon the Commonwealths.

The King of the Scots has, in these very days, arrived at neighbouring Helsingør and the castle of Cronborg, together with his newly-wedded Queen, daughter of our King of most happy memory, and with a great retinue of the Realm's chief men. For since she could not put in to Scotland about the end of summer — our fleet, in which she was being carried, having been driven back into Norway by adverse tempest once and again, and at last, because of the onset of winter, breaking off the voyage — that most serene King, having boarded his ships, seeking his bride (what does Love not compel?) among the Norwegian crags, found her out; and having asked of the most serene Queen and of the Realm's Governors and Councillors that leave be granted him to come to us, once he had arrived in our dominions — being asked, and this most gladly obtained — by a long and difficult journey, especially in winter-time, he came hither, and intending to winter here with his people until free navigation be granted, he is now being received honourably at Cronborg. Which I thought should be indicated to you, that you might learn what is going forward of new matters in public affairs among us. Farewell; and if before Easter you shall have sent the Privilege obtained anew, of which I wrote last, and also Muñoz's little book and whatever others you have treating of the Cometary matter, you will have rendered a most welcome service.

Again, fare you most happily, my most loving and most longed-for Thaddaeus.
At Uraniborg, 25 January, the year [15]90.
Most loving toward you,
Tycho Brahe.

[Postscript:] POSTSCRIPT. On the very day on which I had handed this letter to my amanuensis to be copied fair, while toward evening I was intent on observing Venus's greatest elongation from the Sun, behold, a certain Comet appeared unexpectedly in that same western quarter; and therefore I thought I should keep this letter by me in the meantime, until I had examined all the appearances of that adventitious star, accurately deduced from the heavens throughout its whole duration, so that I might send my own findings too along with it — knowing, as I do, that you are greatly affected by such things. I have therefore added a brief description of that same Comet, such as I lately communicated, when writing on this matter, to the most illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse. More fully I shall treat of it in our third Tome. For there I have resolved to join this one to the earlier [comets] and to demonstrate geometrically, from manifold and certain observations, all its appearances, and that it was altogether destitute of Parallax; in the meantime take in good part these few notions concerning that same hairy star, derived nonetheless from settled observations. That I have so long deferred the business hitherto, from the time of first writing, has come about beyond my intention. For although it was needful to wait until that Comet wholly vanished — which happened after the sixth day of March — and some little time was required to search out its dispositions from the observations, there befell besides that, immediately after the middle of March, the most serene King of the Scots visited me here with his splendid retinue, and that from that time I was for several days together taken up with receiving many foreign guests, whereby I was the less able to finish the web I had begun. And hence it came about that I retained this letter by me right up to the sixth week, which I thought I should indicate, lest you wonder why these things are written at so different a time, but might rather hold this delay the more readily excused. Again, farewell. This was written on the fourth of April.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Haggecius ab Hayck = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius, 1525–1600), the Bohemian physician, astronomer and imperial body-physician at the court of Rudolf II in Prague; Administrator refers to his medical/court administrative office. He was Tycho's longest-standing learned correspondent and the principal channel through which Tycho's cometary controversies (and, later, his move to Bohemia) passed. S. = salutem (dicit), "greeting".
  • Sender / dateline. Tycho Brahe, from Uraniborg on Hven, "25 January, the year [15]90" (O.S.). The header date matches the dateline; the edition label ("1590. 25. januar. Uraniborg") agrees. Denmark was on the Julian (Old Style) calendar.
  • Vitus Winshemius = Veit Winsheim (Vitus Winshemius), of the Wittenberg academic family; here acting as imperial legate (ad Imperatoriam Maiestatem … Legati munus) en route through Hamburg.
  • Henricus Ranzovius = Henrik Rantzau (1526–1598), royal Statthalter (Lieutenant, Locumtenens) of Holstein, humanist patron and astrological enthusiast, who frequently forwarded Tycho's correspondence into Germany.
  • Iacobus Curtius = Jacob Kurtz von Senftenau (Jacobus Curtius), Imperial Vice-Chancellor (Imperij Procancellarius) and a participant in the cometary discussions.
  • Ioannes Craigus = John Craig (d. 1620), Scottish physician and mathematician (later physician to James VI/I), who had disputed the parallax arguments of Tycho's second Tome. Tycho's Apologia answers him.
  • The "Tomes" of the Opera. Tomus secundus / secundo Tomo de eodem Cometa agenti = the second volume, on the comet = Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588, on the comet of 1577). Primus … Tomus / primus ille Tomus "now under the press" = the first volume, the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (on the new star of 1572 and solar/lunar theory; published only posthumously, 1602). Tertium Tomum = a projected third volume. The "Epistolarum Astronomicarum volumen" / "Epistolarum libro" = the Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I printed at Uraniborg, 1596).
  • Apologia. Tycho's defence-piece against Craig's objections to the cometary parallax; he dictated it subito … prout in buccam venerunt ("on the sudden, just as the words came to his lips"), proposing to append it to the second Tome. Apologia is kept as a title (italicised).
  • Werneri liber — "Werner's book," a work of Johann Werner (1468–1522), Nuremberg mathematician; sent to Tycho among Hagecius's enclosures.
  • Munosij libellus = the little book of Jerónimo Muñoz (Hieronymus Munyos/Munosius, c. 1520–1591), the Valencia mathematician whose tract on the 1572 new star bore on the cometary/parallax debate; Tycho asks Hagecius to send it.
  • Rogersius = Daniel Rogers (c. 1538–1591), the English diplomat, through whom Tycho hoped to procure the English printing Privilege (Anglicanum … Privilegium). The "Privilegium nuperi Galliarum Regis" is the French royal printing-privilege promised by the legate of the new King of the French (Henri IV, "the new King" Regem … novum, acceded 1589). Privilegium = printing privilege/monopoly.
  • The paper-mill. moletrina mea … pro papyro conficienda = Tycho's paper-mill (built on Hven); Chartopaeus = papermaker (Greek-Latin coinage, χάρτης + facere); papyrus impressoria = printing-paper. The whole passage documents the supply bottleneck for Tycho's private press.
  • Printing units. duo Alphabeta iam impressa sunt, restat sesquialterum — "two alphabets are already printed; one and a half remain": an alphabetum is a printer's unit of gatherings signed through the alphabet (≈23 sheets/gatherings); sesquialterum = one-and-a-half (the ratio 3:2), here "one and a half [alphabets]." Rendered with "[gatherings]" glossed.
  • Frankfurt fair. Nundinae Francofurtianae = the Frankfurt book fair, the chief distribution event.
  • Astronomy — adventitious star. ascititium sidus … ut proximis annis = "an adventitious star, as in recent years" — i.e. a new star/nova or comet such as those of 1572 and 1577. demetiri "measure out" (its parallax/position).
  • Instruments. Sextans → sextant; Radius Astronomicus → cross-staff (radius astronomicus) — Tycho explicitly disparages its real precision; Quadrans Azimuthalis → azimuthal quadrant ("yielding single minutes at the least"); Armillae Aequatoriae → equatorial armillae ("those that require only one and a half armillary [rings]," sesquialteram … Armillam — Tycho's simplified equatorial armillary); Machinae Astronomicae → astronomical machines/instruments; organa → instruments; puncta transversalia → transversal points (Tycho's transversal-dot subdivision of scales); Dioptra … rimulas … priori pinnacidio → sights with little slits / the foremost pinnule (pinnacidium/pinnula → sight/pinnule,). The number 24 instruments anticipates the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (1598). speculam … extruere = "to build a watch-tower / observatory."
  • Opus … de Mechanica Astronomiae parte — Tycho's projected work "on the Mechanical part of Astronomy," realised as the Mechanica (1598); rendered descriptively, the instrument-count (24+) flagged as a forecast.
  • Royal-marriage report. Scotorum Rex = James VI of Scotland (later James I of England); his bride the nova nupta Regina … Regis nostri … filia = Anne of Denmark (Anna, daughter of the late Frederik II). The historical episode is accurate: Anne's autumn 1589 crossing was driven back by storms into Norway; James sailed in person to fetch her and wintered in Denmark, being received at Cronborg (Kronborg) by Helsingør (Helsingør/Elsinore). quid non cogit Amor? — "what does Love not compel?", a rhetorical aside, kept. sereniss. Regina Regnique Gubernatoribus = the queen-dowager (Sophie) and the regency Council governing during Christian IV's minority.
  • French / Genevan affairs. Rex … novus = Henri IV (see above); Genevenses obsidione liberatos = the lifting of the Savoyard siege of Geneva — current-events glosses Hagecius had relayed.
  • A possible negation. vererisque, ut eas receperim — early-modern Latin vereor ut = "I fear that … not"; rendered "you fear I may [not] have received them," consistent with Hagecius's anxiety that the letters miscarried. The bracketed "[not]" marks the idiom rather than a word in the text; flagged for transparency. (Classical vereor ut indeed means "fear that … not," so the sense is secure; the bracket is editorial caution, not a crux.)
  • Salutation/subscription. Vale … Iterum quam felicissime vale → "Farewell … Again, fare you most happily"; Tui amantiss. (amantissimus) → "Most loving toward you."
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing comet postscript (the letter held to the 6th week for the Scottish King's visit; "scribebantur quarto Aprilis"), restored from Dreyer VII p.228 (dropped by the working transcription).
