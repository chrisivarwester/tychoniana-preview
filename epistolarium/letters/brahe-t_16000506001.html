TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTIANUM SECUNDUM, ELECTOREM SAXONIÆ.

ILLUSTRISSIME ET INCLYTE PRINCEPS, DOMINE CLEMENTISSIME.

CUM Illustrissima Celsitudo Vestra me, licet peregrinum et de facie ignotum, suas Ditiones, quas administrat, transeuntem, Clementissimo alloquio dignata sit, et torque aureâ cum suâ effigie donârit atque denuo Torgæ, dum in itinere essem Bohemico, ad se in Arcem invitârit, non possum non hanc Illustrissimæ Celsitudinis Vestræ erga me, nihil eiuscemodi promeritum, benignissimum favorem humilime agnoscere atque recolere. Cumque me imparem fatear ad quippiam, quod tanto Principi vicissim gratum sit, præstandum, id saltem submisse polliceri possum, me semper huius Clementissimæ erga me voluntatis et beneficentiæ, quam apud ipsam expertus sum, quoad vixero, memorem futurum, atque apud alios deprædicaturum. Verum enimvero ne prorsus meo officiolo defuisse videar, licet, uti dictum, longe inferior sim, quam ut remunerationis loco pro benignissimis suis erga me meritis retribuere quid valeam, ne tamen omninô ingratus atque horum immemor videar, ex tenui penu, quæ pro meo modulo possum, depromo atque ad Illustrissimam Vestram Celsitudinem per Dominum Doctorem IESSENIUM, in inclyta Vestræ Celnis Academia Wittebergæ Professorem, meum, dum aliquamdiu isthic morarer, hospitem, me hîc in Bohemiâ proximis diebus invisentem, humilime mitto, nimirum de Inerrantium Stellarum accurata restitutione librum, plurimorum Annorum studio et labore partum, atque unâ Globum Cœlestem, in quo Affixæ Stellæ iuxta hanc ipsam a nobis exantlatam verificationem operâ cuiusdam artificiosi Batavi in Insula prioris meæ habitationis Uraniburgica, Regni Daniæ, Patriæ nostræ, solerter et accurate, quantum hæc fert Mechanicæ Sphæræ magnitudo, repræsentatæ sunt. Oroque Illmam Vram Celnem debitâ submissione et reverentiâ, ut hæc munuscula ab homine Illmæ Vræ Celnis observantissimo profecta non aspernari velit, sed Clementissimo animo suscipere atque me in posterum etiam sibi commendatum habere non dedignari. Pluribus illmam Vram Celnem, gravissimis suarum Ditionum administrandarum curis occupatam, interpellare nolo, neque enim decere arbitror de privatis meis rebus ad Illmam Vram Celnem quippiam perscribere, quas tamen si Illma Vra Celdo aliquatenus cognoscere non degravatur, a prænominato Doctore IESSENIO percipiet, quantâ Clementiâ et benignitate Augustissimus et Potentissimus Imperator RUDOLPHUS, Dominus noster Clementissimus, me subiectissimum suum clientem hîc susceperit, et studia mea, quæ diu multumque molior, Astronomica, præ suspiciendâ et nunquam satis laudatâ ipsius Sacræ Cæsæ Matis erga omnes liberales Artes, inprimis vero Mathematicas, affectione et favore Clementissimo, fovere et promovere dignata est, rem hanc a summo, quod gerit in terris, officio non alienam neque se indignam censendo.

Deus opt. Max. Universi author, ipsius Cæsam Matem in totius Reipæ Christianæ et bonarum literarum tutelam atque propagationem quam diutissime nobis incolumem conservet, cui etiam æviterno Numini Divino Illmam Vestram Cnem unâ cum Illma coniuge et liberis inclytis toto pectore pariter commendo: in quo etiam ut Ia Co Va vivat, valeat et floreat diutissime, optime, prosperrime, ex animo precor.

Dabantur ex Arce Cæsareâ Benaticâ Die 6 Maij stylo novo Anni 1600.

TYCHO BRAHE TO CHRISTIAN II, ELECTOR OF SAXONY.

Most illustrious and renowned Prince, most clement Lord.

Since Your Most Illustrious Highness deigned to address me most clemently — though a stranger and unknown by face — as I passed through the Dominions you govern, and presented me with a gold chain bearing your effigy, and again, at Torgau, while I was on my Bohemian journey, invited me to you in the Castle: I cannot but humbly acknowledge and recall this most kindly favour of Your Most Illustrious Highness toward me, who have deserved nothing of the kind. And although I confess myself unequal to performing anything that might in return be pleasing to so great a Prince, I can at least humbly promise that, as long as I live, I will ever be mindful of this most clement goodwill and beneficence toward me which I have experienced with you, and will proclaim it among others. But truly, lest I should seem to have failed altogether in my little duty — though, as I said, I am far too lowly to be able to repay anything by way of recompense for your most kindly merits toward me — yet, lest I seem wholly ungrateful and forgetful of these things, from my slender store I bring forth what I can, according to my measure, and humbly send to Your Most Illustrious Highness, through Lord Doctor Jessenius, professor in Your Highness's renowned Academy at Wittenberg — my host while I stayed there a while, and now, in these next days, visiting me here in Bohemia — namely, a book on the accurate restitution of the Fixed Stars, won by the study and labour of very many years; and together with it a Celestial Globe, on which the Fixed Stars are represented according to this very verification accomplished by us, by the work of a certain skilled Dutchman, on the island of my former dwelling, Uraniborg, of the Kingdom of Denmark, our fatherland — skilfully and accurately, so far as the size of this mechanical sphere allows. And I beg Your Most Illustrious Highness, with due submission and reverence, that you would not disdain these little gifts, proceeding from a man most respectful of Your Most Illustrious Highness, but receive them with most clement mind, and not disdain to hold me also commended to you hereafter. I do not wish to detain Your Most Illustrious Highness, occupied with the weightiest cares of governing your Dominions, with more words; nor do I think it fitting to write anything of my private affairs to Your Most Illustrious Highness — which, however, if Your Most Illustrious Highness does not find it burdensome to know in some measure, you will learn from the aforenamed Doctor Jessenius: with how great clemency and kindness the most august and most powerful Emperor Rudolf, our most clement Lord, has here received me, his most submissive client, and has deigned to foster and promote my astronomical studies, on which I have long and greatly laboured, by the admirable and never sufficiently praised most clement affection and favour of His Sacred Imperial Majesty toward all the liberal Arts, but especially the Mathematical — judging this matter not alien to the highest office he holds on earth, nor unworthy of himself.

May God Best and Greatest, author of the Universe, preserve His Imperial Majesty safe for us as long as may be, for the protection and propagation of the whole Christian Commonwealth and of good letters; to which everlasting Divine Power I likewise commend with my whole heart Your Most Illustrious Highness, together with your most illustrious consort and renowned children — in whom [God] I pray from my heart that Your Most Illustrious Highness may live, fare well, and flourish, as long, as well, and as prosperously as possible.

Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 6th day of May, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Christian II, Elector of Saxony (1583–1611), then a young prince (the Electorate under the Administrator's regency at this date; cf. brahe-t_16000324001). Tycho had passed through Electoral Saxony on his way from Wandsbek/Wittenberg to Bohemia.
- Significance — a presentation letter (gifts of book + globe). Tycho thanks the Elector for two marks of favour — a gold chain (torques aurea) bearing the Elector's effigy, and an invitation to the castle at Torgau during Tycho's Bohemian journey — and reciprocates with two gifts, sent through Jessenius (Wittenberg professor and Tycho's recent host, then visiting him in Bohemia):
1. the book on the accurate restitution of the Fixed Stars (the fixed-star catalogue, "won by the study and labour of very many years" — the same work presented to Duke Ulrich, brahe-t_15980914002, and carried to Magini, brahe-t_15991104001); and
2. a Celestial Globe carrying his restored star-positions, made by "a certain skilled Dutchman" at Uraniborg on Hven — i.e. one of the celebrated Tychonic globes produced in the Uraniborg workshop. A notable material-history datum (logged for census/material cross-reference).
- The Torgau connection. The Torgau-castle invitation links to the contemporaneous Saxon-court correspondence: Tycho's letter to Georg von Wolframsdorf, the Elector's chamber-junker at Torgau (brahe-t_16000508001), dispatched — like this one — through Jessenius.
- Rudolf II's patronage. Tycho refers the Elector to Jessenius for the fuller account of how Rudolf II "received me, his most submissive client," and fosters his astronomy "by … favour toward all the liberal Arts, but especially the Mathematical" — the standard framing of the imperial establishment (cf. brahe-t_16000106001, brahe-t_16000324001).
- Persons named. Christian II of Saxony; Johannes Jessenius (courier/host); the unnamed Dutch globe-maker at Uraniborg; Rudolf II; the Elector's consort and children.
- Abbreviations. Heavy honorific contraction (Illma Vra Celdo/Celnem = Illustrissima Vestra Celsitudo/Celsitudinem; Ia Co Va = Illustrissima Celsitudo Vestra; Caesae Matis = Caesareae Maiestatis) silently expanded; the source block keeps them.
- Calendar. Dateline Die 6 Maij stylo novo Anni 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label. (Same date-cluster as the Galileo/Concini letters of 4 May 1600.)
