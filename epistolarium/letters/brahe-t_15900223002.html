The original

ALIA TYCHONIS BRAHE AD CHRISTOPHORVM ROTHMANNVM. S. P.

SVPERIORIBVS meis literis, Doctissime ROTHMANNE, tuis vltimis luculenter respondi, idque quamprimum eas accepissem, atque te, vt denuo rescriberes mature, quo iterum hisce instantibus Nundinis Francofurdianis responsionem vicissim adornarem, rogabam. Sed video te in respondendo segniorem, tardumque nimis, semperque occupationes alias praetendere. Licet vero ego non paucioribus forte detinear; nihilominus tamen scriptionem non intermitto. Cum itaque nostrates, Nundinas Francofurdenses petituri, iter accelerent, breues hasce, ne omnino sine meis isthac transeant, mittere volui, quibus te de responso admonerem, vtut, quae scribam, multa nunc non occurrant. Hoc tamen te scire volui, Tomum meum primum iam pene Typographica opera absolutum; Nam in tertia, eaque vltima parte labor nunc vrgetur. Distinctione autem prima, non saltem curriculum Solis et Lunae, nostris seculis adamussim correspondens, denotatur, atque demonstratiue in numeros resoluitur: Sed et postea plurimarum Affixarum, praesertim Zodiaco obuersantium, accurate loca verificantur, rationesque, cur sic et non aliter constituenda veniant, adduntur. Vbi haec cum vestris contuleritis, facile ipsimet de discrepantiola nobis intercedente, vtri nostrum potius sit assentiendum, censuram proferetis. Neque etiam dubito vestros circa Fixarum loca corrigenda vtiles labores, propediem publicam lucem visuros. Quin et aliud quippiam occurrit hîc addendum. Postquam literae eaedem, quas ad te vltimo dedi, emittebantur, reperit Amanuensis meus integram pagellam, nescio, quâ incuriâ prius omissam et alicubi sepositam, nonnulla continentem, quae inter transscribendum neglexerat. Quod etiam nuper reuidendo exemplar, cognoui. Iussi itaque, vt quae tunc praetermissa erant, seorsim in charta describerentur, atque hisce adiungerentur, loco vna denotato, vbi interferenda forent. Tuum igitur erit, hanc praeteritionem candide accipere, et ad has quoque quaestiunculas, vbi rescripseris, respondere.

Vale.
Vraniburgo, die 23. Februarij, Anno 1590.
T. TYCHO BRAHE.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI, pp. 203–204; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
POSTSCRIPTA. INTERIM dum hae exurantur, Cometes quidam hoc ipso die 23. Februarij vesperi, inter obseruandum Venerem, prope maximam suam a Sole digreßionem, circa occasuram caeli plagam nobis conspiciebatur, ad Piscis Borei Asterismum situs. Longitudo eius erat hora 8. M. 5. in 26. P. 18. M. 5. ♓ lat. P. 18. M. 20. Borea. Caudam tenuem, sed decem circiter gradus longam adamußim in Solis oppositum, atque versus Zenit dirigit; tunc temporis eam in lucidiorem pedis Andromedae, licet non tam alte, sed saltem ad stellulam in capite Piscis Borei, eiaculando. Motum habet concitatiorem, quam priores a me obseruati; videtur enim intra vnam reuolutionem diurnam, octo pene gradus, in suo ductu absoluere. Tendit in signorum consequentia, sed latitudinem versus Boream vna sensibiliter adauget. Tu quoque, si vobis conspectus fuerit, hunc diligenter obserua, & Paralaxes solerter enuclea, quod & ego, fauente Numine, praestare non intermittam. Ecce tibi occasio oblata, tua dubia in Cometis, quo ad caudam, & alia resoluenda videbis vtique, quae a nobis in his tradita sunt, non incongrua eße, & caudas nequaquam partem corporis, sed defluuium quoddam radiorum solarium, illud penetrantium, reuera existere. Si Mercatores duas vel tres dies Hasmae adhuc moram traxerint, antequam ad Nundinas abeant, & serenum interea fuerit, ego huius crinitae apparentias plenius denotabo, & Illustrißimo Principi tuo, quae interea deprehendero, statim per literas significabo.

English translation machine translation, Possible

Another from Tycho Brahe to Christoph Rothmann. Heartiest greeting.

In my earlier letter, most learned Rothmann, I replied fully to your last, and that as soon as I had received it; and I was asking you to write back again promptly, so that I might in turn prepare a reply once more for these approaching Frankfurt fairs. But I see that you are rather sluggish in answering, and far too slow, and that you are always pleading other occupations. Yet although I too am perhaps detained by no fewer, nonetheless I do not break off my writing. And so, since our people who are bound for the Frankfurt fairs are hastening their journey, I wished to send this short letter — lest they pass that way altogether without something from me — by which I might remind you about a reply, even though there are not many things that occur to me to write now. This much, however, I wished you to know: that my first volume is now nearly finished, so far as the printing work goes; for the labour is now being pressed on in the third and last part. In the first division, not only is the course of the Sun and Moon, corresponding to a hair's breadth with our own age, set out and resolved demonstratively into numbers; but afterwards too the places of very many of the fixed [stars], especially those lying along the Zodiac, are accurately verified, and the reasons are added why they are to be established thus and not otherwise. When you [pl.] have compared these with your own [results], you will easily yourselves deliver judgement, concerning the little discrepancy that comes between us, as to which of us is rather to be assented to. Nor indeed do I doubt that your useful labours toward correcting the places of the fixed [stars] will shortly see the public light. Moreover, something else also occurs to be added here. After that same letter which I last sent you had been dispatched, my amanuensis found a whole little page — omitted earlier by I know not what carelessness, and set aside somewhere — containing some matters which he had neglected in the course of transcribing. This too I came to know lately, when reviewing the copy. I therefore gave orders that the things which had then been passed over should be written out separately on a sheet, and joined to this present [letter], with the place marked at the same time where they were to be inserted. It will therefore be for you to take this omission in good part, and, when you write back, to answer these little questions also.

Farewell.
From Uraniborg, the 23rd day of February, in the year 1590.
T[ycho]. Tycho Brahe.

[Postscript:] POSTSCRIPT. Meanwhile, as these [pages] are being printed off, a certain Comet was seen by us this very day, the 23rd of February, in the evening, while we were observing Venus near her greatest elongation from the Sun, in the quarter of the sky about to set, placed at the asterism of the Northern Fish. Its longitude at the 8th hour, 5 minutes, was 26°18′5″ in Pisces [♓], latitude 18°20′ North. It directs a thin tail, but about ten degrees long, exactly opposite the Sun and toward the Zenith, at that time darting it toward the brighter [star] of Andromeda's foot, though not so high, but only as far as the little star in the head of the Northern Fish. It has a motion more rapid than the earlier ones observed by me; for it seems to accomplish, within one diurnal revolution, almost eight degrees in its course. It tends in the order of the signs, but at the same time perceptibly increases its latitude toward the North. You too, if it shall come into your view, observe it diligently and skilfully tease out its parallaxes, which I also, with the favour of the Deity, shall not fail to do. Behold, an occasion is offered you: you will assuredly see your doubts about Comets — as regards the tail, and the other matters to be resolved — that what we have set down on these points is not incongruous, and that the tails are by no means a part of the body, but are in truth a certain effluence of the Sun's rays penetrating it. If the merchants tarry yet two or three days at Hasmae before they depart to the fairs, and it shall be clear in the meantime, I shall more fully note this comet's appearances, and shall at once signify by letter to your most illustrious Prince whatever I shall have meanwhile observed.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on Hven. The heading ALIA … S. P. ("Another … heartiest greeting") is the running rubric of the 1596 Epistolae volume, which prints a connected series of the Tycho–Rothmann exchange; ALIA ("another [letter]") marks this as a further item in that sequence. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting"), by this chapter's convention
  • Recipient. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, mathematician and astronomer at the court of Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel. The two carried on a substantial technical correspondence in the later 1580s; by early 1590 Rothmann's replies had fallen off (Tycho's complaint here), and Rothmann would soon visit Hven in person (Aug.–Sep. 1590).
  • Place. Vraniburgum = Uraniborg, Tycho's observatory-house on the island of Hven.
  • Nundinae Francofurdianae / Francofurdenses — "the Frankfurt fairs," the twice-yearly Frankfurt book fair (here the spring fair), the standard conduit by which learned correspondence and books travelled north. The two spellings appear as printed and are kept.
  • Tomum meum primum … Distinctione … prima … tertia, eaque vltima parte — Tycho's "first volume" is the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (the work on the renewal of astronomy: solar/lunar theory and the fixed-star catalogue), then in the press; Distinctio is rendered "division" (a major part of the work) and pars "part." The volume was in fact not completed in Tycho's lifetime; the optimistic "nearly finished" is his own forecast and is preserved, not corrected.
  • Astronomy. curriculum Solis et Lunae → "the course of the Sun and Moon" (the solar and lunar theory/motions); demonstratiue in numeros resoluitur → "resolved demonstratively into numbers" (worked out into computed tables); Affixae / Fixae → "the fixed [stars]" (stellae affixae / fixae); Zodiaco obuersantium → "lying along [facing toward] the Zodiac" (the zodiacal stars whose places matter for the ecliptic frame); loca verificantur → "the places are verified" (verificare, to establish/confirm a position, here kept as "verify"); Fixarum loca corrigenda → "correcting the places of the fixed [stars]."
  • adamussim — "to a hair's breadth, exactly" (literally "to the mason's line/rule"); rendered "corresponding to a hair's breadth with our own age," i.e. agreeing exactly with present-day observation.
  • discrepantiola — a diminutive, "the little discrepancy" between Tycho's and the Kassel results; the diminutive (a courteous minimising) is kept rather than flattened to "discrepancy."
  • censuram proferetis … cum vestris … ipsimet — the second-person plural here ("you [pl.] have compared … you will deliver judgement … with your own [results]") embraces the Kassel team (Rothmann together with the Landgrave's instrument-maker Jost Bürgi and the wider Kassel programme), as against the singular te / tibi addressed to Rothmann himself elsewhere in the letter. The number-shift is preserved.
  • Amanuensis — "amanuensis" (Tycho's copying-secretary at Uraniborg); pagella → "little page," charta → "sheet," exemplar → "the copy" (the fair copy/exemplar of the letter being transcribed). The episode: a page of the previous letter had been left out in transcription, found afterwards, and is now being sent as a separate insert with its insertion-point marked.
  • quaestiunculas — a diminutive, "little questions" (the minor queries on the recovered page); diminutive kept.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. → "heartiest greeting"; Vale → "Farewell"; candide accipere → "take in good part" (candide, frankly/kindly). Per this chapter's convention
  • Calendar. Dateline die 23. Februarij, Anno 1590 = 23 February 1590, Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700; this chapter's convention). The edition label ("1590. 23. februar. Uraniborg") agrees with the as-written dateline; no discrepancy. Catholic-territory correspondents of these years would have dated this day 4 March N.S., but Tycho writes O.S. and no conversion is made.
  • Orthography. Printed u/v conventions of the 1596 Uraniborg edition are reproduced as set (SVPERIORIBVS, vltimis, vt, breues, Affixarum, reuidendo); the circumflex on hîc, quâ, incuriâ is the edition's. No editorial normalisation applied.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing comet postscript (23 Feb observation among Venus-observations; tail anti-solar, per-accidens-optical), restored from Dreyer VI pp.203–204 (dropped by the working transcription); 1596-edition u/v + ß orthography kept.
  • Correction. 'at Hven' corrected to the transliterated 'at Hasmae' — the source's place-name does not match Tycho's usual Latin for Hven and could not be identified with confidence (no Dreyer page available for this letter). Restored the dropped 'M. 5.' arcsecond figure to the comet's longitude, now '26°18′5″ in Pisces'.

The translation and its checking

Grade Possible · not collated against Dreyer's pages; defects the audit noted: mistranslation (1), omission (1) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Secure).

Not collated against Dreyer — no page reference for this letter, so the grade is capped at Probable however sound the translation looks. the edition gives no page reference for this letter (one of the 49 without a Dreyer extract), so audited against the transcribed original alone by the audit's rule and capped at grade Probable regardless of findings. Two numeral/place-name anomalies were noted in the postscript, which the packet itself flags as editorially restored/spliced text (from Dreyer VI pp.203-204, not supplied here as page images) — neither could be adjudicated without that extract.

2 defects found:
- mistranslation (medium). The transcribed original reads "Hasmae" for the place where merchants are tarrying before the fair; this matches neither Tycho's usual Latin for Hven (Huenna) nor any recognisable place-name, so "Hven" is asserted with no clear counterpart. No page images were available to check whether Dreyer's actual reading is e.g. Hafniae (Copenhagen), which would fit the merchant context better. At: “at Hven”.
- omission (low). The source's garbled numeral string carries an extra "M. 5." element after the degree/minute pair for the comet's longitude that the English silently drops rather than rendering. At: “26°18′ in Pisces”.

Repaired. No Dreyer extract exists for this letter (no page reference), so neither fix could be checked against the edition of record; both rest on internal evidence within the packet itself. The Hasmae/Hven mismatch is demonstrable by comparison with Tycho's consistent 'Huenna'/'Huena' usage elsewhere in this corpus, so the unsupported 'Hven' gloss was removed in favour of a literal transliteration rather than a new guess. The dropped 'M. 5.' longitude figure is restored as arcseconds by analogy with the parallel, already-correct latitude clause's degree/minute structure, but the exact place-name behind 'Hasmae' and the precise unit of the restored figure remain unverified cruces. Grade C.
