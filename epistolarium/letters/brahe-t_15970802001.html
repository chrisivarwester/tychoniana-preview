The original

IACOBUS BORDINGUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

S.

REDDITÆ mihi sunt literæ tuæ, Vir Nobilissime, quibus significas, te patrium solum vertere coactum, et nunc Rostochij subsistere, quod etiam ex alijs iam pridem intellexeram. Doleo profecto non modo vices tuas, sed ingens quoque illud rerum Astronomicarum dispendium, quod ob hasce rerum tuarum inexpectatas conturbationes eventurum, unâ prævideo. Sed quid facias? cum hæc nulla tua culpa aut merito acciderint, nullumque in eo studio laborem, quantumvis gravem aut molestum, detrectâris, omnibus æquis censoribus facile excusatus eris. Verum de his aliquando coram plura. De Illustrissimi Principis mei in te clementi favore nihil est quod dubites. Literas tuas C. E. reverenter obtuli, ac curabo, ut non tantum ad illas responsum, sed etiam commendatorias ad Sereniss. Daniæ Regem intra biduum vel triduum accipias. Nunc enim tantum otij mihi non fuit, ut confici potuerint, nec etiam iusto diutius illos detinere volui, qui tuas attulerint. Quod reliquum est, officia mea tibi defero promptissima.

Vale.
Gustrovij die 2. Augusti Anno 1597.
T. studiosissimus
IACOBUS BORDINGUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Jacob Bording to Tycho Brahe.

Greeting.

Your letter has been delivered to me, most noble Sir, in which you make known that you have been forced to leave your native soil and now reside at Rostock — a thing I had also long since learned from others. I grieve, indeed, not only over your own fortunes, but also over that immense loss to astronomical affairs which, together [with them], I foresee will come to pass on account of these unexpected disturbances of your circumstances. But what are you to do? Since these things have befallen through no fault or deserving of yours, and since you have shirked no labour in that pursuit, however heavy or troublesome, you will easily be excused in the eyes of all fair-minded judges. But of these matters, more at some time face to face. As to my Most Illustrious Prince's gracious favour towards you, there is nothing for you to doubt. I have reverently presented your letter to His Princely Grace [C. E.], and I shall see to it that within two or three days you receive not only a reply to it, but also a letter of recommendation to the Most Serene King of Denmark. For just now I had not so much leisure that they could be completed, nor did I wish to detain longer than was right those who brought your [letter]. For the rest, I tender you my most ready services.

Farewell.
At Güstrow, the 2nd day of August, the year 1597.
Most devoted to you,
Jacob Bording.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iacobus Bordingus = Jacob Bording (the Younger), physician (Leibarzt) at the Mecklenburg ducal court at Güstrow. He is the son of the better-known Jacob Bording the Elder (1511–1560), the Antwerp-born professor of medicine at Copenhagen and royal physician to Christian III of Denmark; the family thus had standing connections at the Danish court, which is precisely the leverage this letter exploits — Bording undertakes to procure a ducal letter of recommendation addressed to the Danish king on Tycho's behalf.
  • Recipient / situation. Tycho Brahe, by August 1597 had left Hven and Denmark after the breakdown of his relations with the young Christian IV and the new royal council, and was lodging at Rostock (Rostochij subsistere) before moving on toward the Imperial court. patrium solum vertere coactum — "forced to leave [literally: to turn from] your native soil"; solum vertere is the classical legal idiom for going into exile, and Bording's diplomatic choice of it (rather than a blunter word) is preserved.
  • Illustrissimi Principis mei — "my Most Illustrious Prince": Bording's master, the Duke of Mecklenburg resident at Güstrow, i.e. Ulrich III (1527–1603). Named only by title in the letter; the identification is supplied here.
  • C. E. — the abbreviated honorific Celsitudini Eius / Celsitudo Eius, "His Highness / His Princely Grace," to whom Bording presented Tycho's letter. Expanded in the translation as "His Princely Grace [C. E.]," the abbreviation flagged rather than silently filled (cf. the edition's convention, Celsitudo → "Highness"). The reading is secure in sense but the exact case-form behind the two letters is editorial.
  • commendatorias (sc. literas) — "a letter of recommendation / commendatory letter," the ducal letter to the Danish king that is the practical object of the whole note.
  • Sereniss. Daniae Regem — "the Most Serene King of Denmark," i.e. Christian IV (r. 1588–1648; assumed personal rule 1596). Title only; identified here.
  • ingens illud rerum Astronomicarum dispendium — "that immense loss to astronomical affairs": Bording's foreboding that Tycho's exile will damage the science itself (dispendium = loss, detriment), not merely Tycho's personal fortune.
  • Salutation / subscription. S. = salutem (dicit), "greeting". Vale = "Farewell." T. studiosissimus = Tui studiosissimus, "Most devoted to you" (the addressee being a private nobleman, not an "Excellency," the form is the plain Tui rather than Excellentiae Tuae; rendered "Most devoted to you" accordingly).
  • Calendar / place. Gustrovij die 2. Augusti Anno 1597 = "At Güstrow, the 2nd day of August, the year 1597" (O.S.; Mecklenburg, a Lutheran territory, still kept the Julian calendar in 1597). The dateline agrees exactly with the edition label (1597. 2. august. Güstrow); no date discrepancy. biduum vel triduum = "two or three days" (the Latin idiom for a span of two / three days), kept as the natural English.
  • Text witness. The surviving authority is a later transcript (Afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium (Copenhagen, Rigsarkivet), not an autograph; ordinary copyist orthography (-ij for -ii, otij, alijs; the printed â/û circumflexes mark ablatives/contractions). No reading affects the sense.
