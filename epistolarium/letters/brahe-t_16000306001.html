The original

ILLUSTRI ET GENEROSO DOMINO, DOMINO IOHANNI FRIDERICO HOFFMANNO, LIBERO BARONI IN GRUENPUECHEL ET STRECHAU, PRÆFECTO PROVINCIÆ DUCATUS STYRIÆ ET ARCHIDUCATUS AUSTRIÆ, MARSCHALCHO UTROBIQUE HÆREDITARIO, BURGRAVIO IN STEYER NEC NON SAC. CÆS. MAI. AULÆ CONSILIARIO ET SERENISSIMI ARCHIDUCIS MAXIMILIANI CUBICULARI PRIMARIO, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO.

ILLUSTRIS ET GENEROSE VIR, DOMINE ET AMICE HONORANDISSIME.

QUOD Illris Dn° tua Nobilem et Eruditissimum Adolescentem FRANCISCUM TENGNAGLIUM et filium meum TYCHONEM non solum adeo comiter et benevole exceperit (neque enim Illris Dnis tuæ eximiam humanitatem sibi exhibitam huc reversi satis deprædicare poterant) sed et cultissimis suis literis sincera erga me benevolentiâ refertissimis mihi rescribere non degravata sit, pro eo Illi Dni tuæ gratias ago quas possum maximas: si quâ in re mea opella illi unquam usui esse possit, quam lubentissime pro virili relaturus.

Quæ M. KEPLERUS (cuius ingenium et consuetudo mihi perplacent, ut dignum eum censeam, qui ametur et ad optima quæque promoveatur) oretenus ad me retulit, percepi. Et quantum ad primum attinet, de studioso illo hîc alendo et informando, actutum respondi, me non saltem in hoc, sed longe maioribus Illri Dni tuæ pro meo modulo gratificaturum: alterum satis prævidi confici non posse, prius quam expressum ab Aulâ Cæsareâ decretum in Cameram mitteretur; quod iam conscriptum esse intellexi. At ubi hæreat, me latet. Quia vero a dicto TENGNAGLIO cognovi, quod, cum ante quatriduum Brandeisium concessisset, ut cum Domino Capitaneo quædam meo nomine loqueretur, eumque illic non repertum Pragæ quæsivisset, Illrem quoque Dnnem tuam pauculis alloquutus sit, quodque consilium illud de KEPLERO atque illo alio, cum illud Illi Dni tuæ paucis exponeret, eidem placuerit, deque eo ipsamet mecum ulterius loqui velit, dabo operam, ut quamprimum nonnulla, quæ pro Cæsareâ Mate, Domino nostro Clementissimo, elaboro, confecta fuerint (nam prius hinc abire vix ausim) assumpto mecum M. KEPLERO Pragam excurram, quo apud Illrem Dnnem tuam hac de re, prout ea suaserit, deliberemus et certi aliquid statuamus. Neque enim ipse KEPLERUS Illri Dnone tuâ inconsultâ quicquam concludere voluit. Quod et ego probo, siquidem absque eiusdem consensu et suasu nihil nobis faciendum censeam. Intellexi quidem quodammodo, nonnullas difficultates sese insinuare, quibus tamen (uti spero) ex Illris Dnis tuæ prudenti provisu facile occurremus, ijsque medelam adhibebimus. Quare omnia circa hoc negotium, donec cum Illri Dne tuâ præsentes de eo egerimus, differenda arbitramur.

Quæ de Mechanico illo Artifice ab Illri Dne tuâ accersendo antedictus TENGNAGLIUS pariter mihi exposuit, cognitu grata erant. Si eum Illris Dn° tua, ubi accesserit, ad me (uti constituit) amandârit, efficiam, ut in Instrumentis ad observationes ratas idoneo modo fabricandis componendisque instructior ad ipsam redeat. Excogitavi iam nuper quoddam ad varios usus minimo negocio conducens, quodque simul per longinqua itinera commodum sit portatu: idque eam præsertim ob causam, quam TENGNAGLIUS noster Illri Dni tuæ secreto aperiet. Præbebit et hoc circuli Sextantem, et non saltem Altitudines et Siderum distantias etiam ultra sextam Cœli partem usque in Quadrantem, imo etiam eo ampliores, quoad intercapedines, solerter et satis subtiliter rimabitur, atque insuper longitudines ac latitudines Planetarum per totum Zodiacum minimo labore et absque tædiosâ supputatione; tum quoque ascensiones rectas et declinationes Stellarum non longe ab Æquatore utrinque dissitarum promet. Quin et, quod plus est, earundem a Meridiano Æquatorias distantias pro temporis momento inter observandum perquirendo et subinde examinando suppeditabit: ut de alijs quibusdam minoribus utilitatibus nihil addam. Ubi vero artifex ille advenerit, illi structuram eius proponam, et si confectioni absolvendæ idoneus (uti existimo) fuerit, cum Illri tuâ Dnne id, quicquid est, lubens communicabo, atque succinctum et multiplicem eius usum eidem reserabo. Plura nunc non addo, ne Illrem Dnem tuam nimium remorer. Si quæ hîc desiderantur, a TENGNAGLIO uberius, si vacârit, percipiet.

Valeat itaque Illris Dn° tua quam optime et diutissime.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsareanâ Benaticâ die 6 Martij A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious and generous Lord, Lord Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, Free Baron in Grünbüchel and Strechau, Prefect of the Province of the Duchy of Styria and of the Archduchy of Austria, hereditary Marshal of both, Burgrave in Steyr, and also Aulic Councillor of the Sacred Imperial Majesty and First Chamberlain of the Most Serene Archduke Maximilian, his Lord and friend especially to be honoured.

Illustrious and generous man, Lord and most honoured friend.

That Your Illustrious Lordship received the noble and most learned young man Franz Tengnagel and my son Tycho not only so courteously and kindly (for, returning hither, they could not sufficiently proclaim the remarkable humanity shown them by Your Illustrious Lordship), but also did not find it burdensome to write me back with your most cultivated letter, most full of sincere benevolence toward me — for this I give Your Illustrious Lordship the greatest thanks I can; and if in any matter my poor service can ever be of use to you, I will most gladly repay it to the best of my power.

What Master Kepler — whose genius and company please me greatly, so that I judge him worthy to be loved and promoted to every best thing — related to me by word of mouth, I have understood. As to the first point, about that student to be maintained and instructed here, I answered at once that I would gratify Your Illustrious Lordship not only in this, but in far greater things, according to my measure. The other [point] I sufficiently foresaw could not be accomplished before an express decree from the Imperial Court were sent to the Chamber — which I understand has now been drawn up, but where it sticks, escapes me. And since I learned from the said Tengnagel that, when four days ago he had gone to Brandeis to speak with the Lord Captain in my name, and, not finding him there, had sought him at Prague, he also addressed Your Illustrious Lordship in a few words — and that that plan about Kepler, and about that other [person], when he set it out to you in few words, pleased you, and you wish to speak with me further about it yourself — I will take pains that, as soon as certain things I am elaborating for the Imperial Majesty are finished (for I would scarcely dare to leave hence before), I will, taking Master Kepler with me, run over to Prague, that we may deliberate with Your Illustrious Lordship about this, as you shall advise, and settle something certain. For Kepler himself was unwilling to conclude anything without consulting Your Illustrious Lordship — which I too approve, since I judge nothing should be done by us without your consent and advice. I have indeed understood, in a way, that some difficulties insinuate themselves, which however (I hope) we will easily meet by Your Illustrious Lordship's prudent provision, and apply a remedy to them. Wherefore I think all things concerning this business should be deferred until we treat of it, present, with Your Illustrious Lordship.

What the aforesaid Tengnagel likewise set out to me about that mechanical craftsman to be summoned by Your Illustrious Lordship was pleasant to learn. If Your Illustrious Lordship, when he has come, sends him to me (as you have determined), I will see that he returns to you better instructed in fabricating and assembling, in a suitable manner, instruments for sure observations. I have lately devised something serving various uses with minimal trouble, and at the same time convenient to carry on long journeys — and that especially for the reason which our Tengnagel will secretly disclose to you. This will furnish a Sextant of the circle, and will skilfully and subtly enough search out not only altitudes and the distances of stars even beyond the sixth part of the heaven, up to a Quadrant — nay, even greater than that, as to the intervals — and moreover the longitudes and latitudes of the planets through the whole Zodiac, with minimal labour and without tedious computation; and will also yield the right ascensions and declinations of stars set not far from the Equator on either side. Nay, and (what is more) it will supply their equatorial distances from the Meridian for the moment of observation, by searching out and then examining — to say nothing of certain other smaller utilities. When that craftsman has arrived, I will propose its structure to him; and if he be fit (as I think) to complete the making, I will gladly share it, whatever it is, with Your Illustrious Lordship, and unfold to you its compact and manifold use. I add no more now, lest I detain Your Illustrious Lordship too much. If anything is wanting here, you will learn it more fully from Tengnagel, if you have leisure.

May Your Illustrious Lordship therefore fare as well and as long as possible.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 6th day of March, the year 1600.

Apparatus

- Recipient. Johann Friedrich Hoffmann, Freiherr von Grünbüchel und Strechau (Landeshauptmann of Styria, Imperial Aulic Councillor; full titulature as in brahe-t_16000126001) — Kepler's patron, in whose Prague house Kepler first lodged.
- Significance — the negotiation of Kepler's employment with Tycho. A key letter in the Tycho–Kepler convergence of spring 1600: Tycho responds to proposals Kepler had brought "by word of mouth," brokered through Hoffmann (to whom Tengnagel had spoken at Prague). Two points:
1. A student to be maintained and taught by Tycho — which Tycho readily grants ("not only this, but far greater things").
2. "That plan about Kepler" — the terms of Kepler's own attachment, requiring an imperial decree to the [Court-]Chamber. Tycho promises that, once his current imperial work is done, he will come to Prague with Kepler to settle matters with Hoffmann — "for Kepler himself was unwilling to conclude anything without consulting" his patron. The phrase "some difficulties insinuate themselves" discreetly registers the early friction (the quarrel that produced Kepler's apology, brahe-t_16000400001). Tycho's warm verdict — "Kepler's genius and company please me greatly … worthy to be loved and promoted" — is notable on the eve of their collaboration.
- A new portable instrument (technical; the edition's convention). Hoffmann is to send Tycho a mechanical craftsman to be trained in instrument-making. Tycho describes a newly devised compact, travel-portable instrument — "a sextant of the circle" — that measures altitudes and star-distances up to a quadrant (and beyond), planetary longitudes and latitudes through the zodiac "without tedious computation," right ascensions and declinations near the equator, and meridian (hour-angle) distances at the moment of observation. The reason for it Tengnagel will "secretly disclose." A valuable record of Tycho's late instrument-invention for the Bohemian establishment.
- Persons named. Hoffmann; Johannes Kepler; Franz Tengnagel; Tycho's son (Tycho the Younger); the Captain of Brandeis (Brandýs); the imperial Chamber; Archduke Maximilian (in the titulature); Rudolf II.
- Calendar. Dateline die 6 Martij A. 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
