The original

NOBILISS: VIRO, VIRTUTE ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTI D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO IN KNUDSTORPFF ET URANIEBURGO ETC DOMINO ET AMICO OBSERVANDO. S.

NOBILISS. D. TYCHO et amice observande. Quod tam diu ad tuas binas non responderim, multa fuerunt quae me impediverunt: tum valetudo adversa, quae me per integram propemodum Quadragesimam afflixit, tum etiam curae domesticae ac crebrae profectiones medicinae faciendae gratia. Huc accesserunt alia plurima, quae saepe praeter spem incidere consueverunt. Etiamsi vero aliquod otium habuissem, non tamen aliquid nostrae illi disputationi adijcere potuissem, vel nunc quoque possem; adeo exacte omnia abs te excussa, examinata, meaque argumenta diluta sunt, ut me veritate et evidentia demonstrationum victum esse fateri necesse sit, nisi manifestae veritati repugnare velim. Exempla aliquot tuae effigiei accepi, quae inter amicos distribui. D. CURTIUS noster quoque unam accepit, misitque in Bavariam ad quendam sibi bene notum et celebrem Poëtam, qui arguto aliquo epigrammate eam ornaret. Sed nondum accepit responsum. Hîc Pragae hoc tempore nullum eiusmodi Poëtam, quod sciam, habemus, qui meo desiderio satisfacere, et quomodo pictor faciem et lineamenta corporis, sic ille mentem et vim eius depingere posset. NICOLAUS RAYMARUS, quem tu plagij accusaveras, ante septimanas aliquot huc venit et innotuit D. CURTIO qui illum Caesari ita commendavit, ut pro Mathematico, tenui satis, in his initijs, stipendiolo sit receptus. Hodie hinc Francofurdum pro advehenda sua suppellectili discedit, mox huc rediturus. Aliud quod scribam nunc nec habeo, nec vacat.

B. V.
Datae Pragae die 29 Augusti novo stylo Anno 1591.
T. studiosiss.
THAD.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 305; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
POST SCRIPTA. HERI venit ad me quidam artifex, eius nomen ignoro, vir aetate grandeva, et narravit se vixisse aliquandiu tecum, saepeque multa de me audivisse: cum igitur venisset in hanc urbem, salutandi et alloquendi gratia me accessisse. Mira quaedam et Geometrica profitetur, potissimum currum quendam admirabilem, cuius rotae emensa milliaria sonitu quodam indicent, et nescio quae alia; nam plenius cum illo colloqui non licuit et aliorum interventu abruptum fuit nostrum colloquium. Electori Saxoniae, Principi Pomeraniae et quibusdam alijs eiusmodi currum se confecisse aiebat. Intellexi Caesari quoque sua servitia offerre velle.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man, outstanding in virtue and erudition, Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup and Uraniborg etc., his lord and friend to be honoured — greeting.

Most noble Master Tycho and friend to be honoured. That I have been so long in replying to your two letters, there were many things that hindered me: both ill health, which afflicted me through almost the whole of Lent, and also domestic cares and frequent journeys for the sake of practising medicine. To these were added very many other things, such as often tend to befall beyond expectation. But even had I had some leisure, I could not on that account have added anything to that disputation of ours, nor can I even now: so exactly has everything been sifted and examined by you, and my arguments dissolved, that I must needs confess myself vanquished by the truth and the evidence of the demonstrations, unless I were willing to fight against manifest truth. I have received several copies of your portrait, which I have distributed among friends. Our Master Curtius too received one, and sent it into Bavaria to a certain poet well known to him and celebrated, that he might adorn it with some witty epigram. But he has not yet received a reply. Here at Prague at this time we have no poet of that kind, so far as I know, who could satisfy my desire and could depict — as a painter [depicts] the face and the lineaments of the body, so he — his mind and his power. Nicolaus Raymarus, whom you had accused of plagiarism, came here some weeks ago and became known to Master Curtius, who so commended him to the Emperor that he has been taken on as Mathematician, on a little stipend slight enough, in these beginnings. Today he departs hence to Frankfurt to fetch his household goods, intending soon to return hither. Anything else to write I now neither have, nor is there leisure.

Fare you well.
Given at Prague, the 29th day of August, New Style, in the year 1591.
Most devoted to you,
Thad[daeus].

[Postscript:] POSTSCRIPT. Yesterday there came to me a certain craftsman — his name I do not know — a man advanced in age, and he related that he had lived with you for some time, and had often heard much about me; and so, when he had come into this city, he had approached me for the sake of greeting and conversing. He professes certain wondrous and Geometrical things, above all a certain marvellous cart, whose wheels indicate by a kind of sound the miles measured out, and I know not what else; for it was not granted to converse with him more fully, and our conversation was broken off by the intervention of others. He said he had made a cart of this kind for the Elector of Saxony, for the Prince of Pomerania, and for certain others of the like. I understood that he wishes to offer his services to the Emperor too.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. THAD. / Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek) (1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer and personal physician to Emperor Rudolf II at Prague; an early and central observer of the 1572 nova, a long-standing correspondent of Tycho and the principal conduit through which Tycho's later relations with the Imperial court ran. The subscription THAD. abbreviates his Christian name.
  • Recipient titulature. D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO IN KNUDSTORPFF ET URANIEBURGO — "Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup and Uraniborg": Knudstorpff = Knudstrup (the Brahe family seat in Scania), Uranieburgo = Uraniborg on Hven.
  • tuas binas — "your two letters" (binas sc. literas); rendered "your two letters."
  • Quadragesimam — Lent (the forty-day fast); here a time-marker for the duration of Hagecius's illness, kept as the liturgical season.
  • nostrae illi disputationi … meaque argumenta diluta sunt … victum esse fateri — "that disputation of ours … my arguments dissolved … to confess myself vanquished." This refers to the running cosmological controversy between the two over Tycho's geo-heliocentric world-system; Hagecius here concedes the point. diluta ("watered down, dissolved, refuted") is rendered "dissolved"; the concession is preserved at its full force without sharpening Tycho's claim beyond what Hagecius grants ("vanquished by the truth and the evidence of the demonstrations").
  • Exempla aliquot tuae effigiei — "several copies of your portrait." The reference is to printed/engraved likenesses of Tycho being circulated among the learned; effigies = likeness/portrait.
  • D. CURTIUS noster — Jacobus Curtius (Jakob Kurz von Senftenau) (d. 1594), Imperial Vice-Chancellor at Rudolf II's court and himself an amateur astronomer in correspondence with Tycho; noster ("our [Curtius]") marks the shared acquaintance.
  • in Bavariam ad quendam … celebrem Poëtam … arguto … epigrammate — Curtius sent a portrait into Bavaria to a celebrated poet to furnish a "witty epigram" (argutum epigramma) — a learned verse-eulogy to accompany the likeness. The poet is not named.
  • quomodo pictor faciem … sic ille mentem et vim eius depingere posset — "as a painter [depicts] the face … so he [could depict] his mind and power": the painter–poet topos (the painter renders the body, the poet the soul). The elliptical Latin (the verb depingit is supplied from depingere) is rendered with a bracket-free but explicit parallel; no editorial word is added beyond the supplied verb signalled by "[depicts]".
  • NICOLAUS RAYMARUS, quem tu plagij accusaveras — Nicolaus Reimers Bär, "Reimarus Ursus" (c. 1551–1600), the Dithmarschen mathematician whom Tycho had accused of plagiarism (plagium) for publishing in his Fundamentum astronomicum (1588) a geo-heliocentric world-system Tycho held to be stolen from Hven — the origin of the bitter Tycho–Ursus priority dispute. Hagecius reports, neutrally, Ursus's arrival at Prague and his appointment. plagium accusare = "to accuse of plagiarism"; kept as the period term.
  • innotuit D. CURTIO qui illum Caesari ita commendavit, ut pro Mathematico … stipendio sit receptus — Ursus "became known to Master Curtius, who so commended him to the Emperor [Caesar = Rudolf II] that he was taken on as Mathematician [i.e. Imperial Mathematician], on a stipend slight enough, in these beginnings." tenui satis … in his initijs ("slight enough … in these beginnings") is Hagecius's own hedge on the smallness of the initial salary; preserved, not smoothed.
  • Francofurdum pro advehenda sua suppellectili — "to Frankfurt to fetch his household goods" (suppellex = furniture/household stuff); advehenda future-passive (gerundive of purpose), "for the bringing-up of."
  • B. V. — Bene vale, "Fare you well".
  • T. studiosiss. — Tui studiosissimus, "Most devoted to you" (subscription formula).
  • Calendar. The dateline reads die 29 Augusti novo stylo Anno 1591 — 29 August 1591, New Style (Gregorian), explicitly so marked by the writer; the catalogue label likewise gives "n.s." Hagecius, in Catholic Imperial Prague, dates New Style; the corresponding Old Style date (which Tycho on Hven kept) would be 19 August 1591, but the letter is not silently converted: the header carries the as-written N.S. date by the edition's convention, and the 10-day gap is noted here only.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (a visiting artisan's mileage-measuring odometer-cart), restored from Dreyer VII p.305 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. Three small corrections against Dreyer (TBOO VII pp.303-306): the postscript's "aliquando" restored to "aliquandiu" (a stay of some duration, not a single occasion); "stipendio" restored to the diminutive "stipendiolo"; and the closing dateline's inserted "Hîc" removed (Dreyer reads simply "Datæ Pragæ").

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VII pp. 303–305; defects the audit noted: text error (4).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO VII pp.303-306 read as page images (the Hagecius letter runs from '[E codice Vindobonensi lat. 10686 58]' on p.304 through the postscript on p.305; pp.303 and 306 belong to the flanking Maginus letters and were read only to confirm the boundaries). Collated the whole body - the Lenten-illness excuse, the portrait-distribution and Curtius/Bavarian-poet passage, the Nicolaus Raymarus/plagiarism paragraph, and the full postscript about the unnamed elderly craftsman and his sounding-wheel cart offered to the Elector of Saxony, the Prince of Pomerania, and the Emperor - against the printed page, word by word. Found three genuine divergences between the transcribed original and Dreyer's print (listed above); the 'aliquando/aliquandiu' one is substantive enough to change a scholar's read of the craftsman's prior relationship to Tycho, so the letter is graded C and verdict serious despite the translation itself being a faithful rendering of the (corrupted) source it was given.

3 defects found:
- text error (medium). Dreyer prints the durative adverb 'aliquandiu' (for some time) but our transcribed original has 'aliquando' (once, at some point) - a dropped syllable that changes a duration of residence into a single occasion, a fact about the unnamed craftsman's relationship to Tycho that a scholar would take away differently. Dreyer prints: et narravit se vixisse aliquandiu tecum, saepeque multa de me audivisse (TBOO VII p.305, postscript, l.2).
- text error (low). Dreyer prints the diminutive 'stipendiolo' (a paltry little stipend); the transcribed original drops the diminutive ending to plain 'stipendio'. Minor, since 'tenui satis' already conveys smallness, but a genuine divergence from the printed text. Dreyer prints: ut pro Mathematico, tenui satis, in his initijs, stipendiolo sit receptus (TBOO VII p.305, l.10).
- text error (low). the transcribed original inserts 'Hîc' (here) into the closing dateline; Dreyer's closing formula reads simply 'Datae Pragae' without it. Low-impact (the place is already named), but the English 'here at' is an artifact of the corrupted source, not of Dreyer's text. Dreyer prints: Datae Pragae die 29 Augusti novo stylo Anno 1591 (TBOO VII p.305, l.13-14).

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.303-306. Page 305 confirms all three text error: line 10 reads "ftipendiolo"; lines 13-14 read "Datæ Pragæ" with no "Hîc"; and the postscript (headed POST SCRIPTA on the same page) reads "vixiße aliquandiu tecum". All three fixed in the Latin, with light corresponding adjustments to the English. No other defects found across the four pages.
