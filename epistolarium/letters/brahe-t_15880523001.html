The original

TYCHONI BRAHE.

IAM optimo Regi vestro laudationem pango, cuius exemplum IUNIO ad te mittam: qua re cum sim occupatus, neque diligenter scribo, neque uberius. Gratificor autem huic familiari meo HAKEN, Equiti Marchico, iuveni probo, nec abhorrenti a litteris, quarum gratia apud nos vivit. Omnino voluit te videre: facies gratum, si facies ut intelligat, te mihi peramicum; nam et alioqui te humanissimum virum sentiet.

Vale.
Rostochio X. Kalend. Iun. Anno 1588.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Tycho Brahe.

I am even now composing a panegyric of your excellent King, a copy of which I shall send you in June; and since I am occupied with this work, I write neither carefully nor at any length. But I am doing a kindness to this member of my household, Haken, a knight of the March [of Brandenburg], an upright young man, and no stranger to letters — for the sake of which he lives here among us. He very much wished to see you: you will do [me] a favour if you bring it about that he understands you to be a great friend of mine; for in any case he will find you a most courteous man.

Farewell.
From Rostock, on the tenth day before the Kalends of June [23 May], in the year 1588.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Johannes Caselius = Johannes Caselius (Johann Kessel/Chessel, 1533–1613), the leading humanist professor of rhetoric and the studia humanitatis, then at the University of Rostock (he moved to Helmstedt in 1589). The name is not in the editorial glossary; identification supplied here. Caselius is independently attested in Tycho's orbit: Dreyer notes a Tycho letter alluding to "Professor Caselius" at Rostock, and a Caselius dedication to Christian IV is recorded among the Royal Library's holdings.
  • Place. Rostochio → Rostock (the Hanseatic university town in Mecklenburg), by the edition's convention; Rostochio is the ablative of origin ("from Rostock").
  • optimo Regi vestro laudationem pango — "I am composing a panegyric of your excellent King." Regi vestro ("your King") is the Danish crown, addressed to Tycho as a Dane. Contextual note (not asserted in the text): Frederik II of Denmark had died on 4 April 1588 (O.S.); a laudatio of the "best King" in late May 1588 is most naturally a funeral panegyric on the late Frederik II, though the letter neither names the king nor states that he is dead — the identification with Frederik II is left as the apparatus reading and not introduced into the translation. pango (lit. "I set down / fasten," used esp. of composing verse or song) is rendered "composing"; the work may well be in verse.
  • cuius exemplum IUNIO ad te mittam — "a copy of which I shall send you in June." IUNIO is the month Iunius (June) in the ablative of time, not a personal name despite the source's capitalisation; the dateline (23 May) makes "in June" coherent. exemplum here = "a copy" (of the text), not "an example."
  • Gratificor … huic familiari meo HAKEN, Equiti Marchico — "I am doing a kindness to this member of my household, Haken, a knight of the March." familiaris = a member of one's household / a close associate (here apparently a young man lodging with or studying under Caselius), rendered "member of my household." Haken is the young man's name (left as written; unidentified). Eques Marchicus = "a knight of the March," i.e. a nobleman of the Margraviate (March) of Brandenburg — Marchicus "of the March/Mark"; rendered "knight of the March [of Brandenburg]," the bracketed gloss marking the editorial expansion.
  • nec abhorrenti a litteris, quarum gratia apud nos vivit — "and no stranger to letters, for the sake of which he lives here among us." non abhorrere a litteris = not to shrink from / not to be averse to learning; the litotes is preserved ("no stranger to"). quarum gratia apud nos vivit = he resides at Rostock for the sake of his studies.
  • facies gratum, si facies ut intelligat, te mihi peramicum — "you will do [me] a favour if you bring it about that he understands you to be a great friend of mine." facies gratum ("you will do a welcome thing / a favour"): the beneficiary is unexpressed and supplied as "[me]" in brackets. peramicum (intensive per- + amicus) = "a very great friend"; te … peramicum sc. esse mihi.
  • nam et alioqui te humanissimum virum sentiet — "for in any case he will find you a most courteous man." humanissimus in the rhetor's idiom = most cultivated/courteous/kindly; sentiet = "he will perceive/find [you to be]."
  • Salutation / subscription. The letter opens with a bare address (TYCHONI BRAHE, "To Tycho Brahe") and no greeting-formula, and closes with Vale — Farewell.
  • Dateline / calendar. Rostochio X. Kalend. Iun. Anno 1588 = "From Rostock, ante diem decimum Kalendas Iunias, in the year 1588." The tenth day before the Kalends of June, counted inclusively in the Roman manner (the Kalends = 1 June counts as day 1), falls on 23 May (O.S.; Denmark and Lutheran Germany were on the Julian calendar in 1588). The Roman-calendar idiom is rendered with both the idiom and the civil date, by the edition's convention. The catalogue label ("1588. 23. maj. Rostock") agrees with the as-written dateline; no discrepancy.
