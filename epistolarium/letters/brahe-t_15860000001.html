The original

POTENTISSIME IMPERATOR D. D. CLEMENTISSIME.

CUM ex liberalitate et optimo erga me studiaque astronomica animo serinissimus rex Daniæ, dominus meus clementissimus, astronomiæ instaurandæ gratia insulam Huenam mihi addixerit, in qua meis sumptibus extruxi arcem in varia domicilia ad literarum studia, exercitia astronomica, chymica, opificia, oblectamenta et ad alios usus distinctam et distributam; cui arci Uranieburgo a studio astronomico nomen indidi. Ut igitur et voluntati regis nostri et multorum de me expectationi satisfacerem, ab annis pluribus omne meum studium et operam in instauranda astronomia posui, iamque nonnulla in ea arte elaborata habeo, cætera sub incude, ut dicitur, versantur, adornantur et expoliuntur. Quæ omnia in reipub: literariæ commodum omnibus communia facere vellem, nostris prælis in eadem arce excusa. Verum plurimum vereor ne maximis meis impensis, quas tum in typographiam, tum in opifices feci, et in excudendis operibus mathematicis adhuc facturus sum, defrauder, si labores mei maximis sumptibus comparati ab alijs typographis in Germania et sub ditione Maiestatis Vestræ recudantur et divenduntur. Quare Maiestatem Vestram cum omni submissione rogo, quæcumque ex mea officina in mathematicis studijs proditura sint, sive à me elaborata, sive illustrata, ne ea a quoquam typographo recudantur, clementer privilegio ad decennium cavere dignetur, idque sub mulcta transgressoribus irrogari solita. Cavebo vicissim, ne libri a me publicandi quidpiam, quod vel religionis controversiis vel reipubl: constitutioni offensioni siet, ullo in loco contineant. Et si quid Cæs: Ma: V: in hisce mathematicis studijs quidpiam à me postulaverit, aut ego intellexero qua in re gratum servitium eidem præstare possem, me semper promptum et paratum habebit, cui omne meum studium, operam, officium et obsequium humiliter defero. Et ut Cæs: Ma: V: rectius intelligat et ædificium nostræ arcis et totum animi nostri propositum mitto eidem eiusdem arcis totiusque nostræ insulæ designationem.

Cum his me Cæs: Ma: V: commendo.
S: Cæs: Ma: V: addictissimus
Tycho Brahe Danus Dom: de Knudstrup.

English translation machine translation, Secure

MOST POWERFUL EMPEROR, MOST MERCIFUL LORD AND MASTER.

Whereas, out of his generosity and his most excellent disposition toward me and toward astronomical studies, the most serene King of Denmark, my most merciful lord, has assigned to me the island of Hven for the sake of the renewal of astronomy [astronomia instauranda] — on which, at my own expense, I have raised a castle laid out and divided into various dwellings for literary studies, for astronomical and chemical exercises, for workshops, for recreations, and for other uses; to which castle I gave the name Uraniborg, from the astronomical study [pursued there] — therefore, that I might satisfy both the will of our King and the expectation many have of me, for several years now I have set all my zeal and labour upon the renewal of astronomy [instauranda astronomia], and I now have a number of things in that art worked out, while the rest are, as the saying goes, [still] on the anvil, being furnished and polished. All of which I should wish to make common to all, for the benefit of the republic of letters, printed off at my presses in that same castle. But I greatly fear that I may be defrauded of the very great expenses which I have incurred, both on the printing-house and on the workmen, and which I am still going to incur in printing off mathematical works, if my labours, procured at very great cost, should be reprinted and sold off by other printers in Germany and under the dominion of Your Majesty. Wherefore I ask Your Majesty, with all submission, that whatever shall issue from my workshop in mathematical studies, whether worked out by me or illustrated [by me], may not be reprinted by any printer; [and that] you would mercifully deign to provide for this by a privilege for ten years, and [that] this [be enforced] under the penalty customarily imposed upon transgressors. I, for my part, will take care that the books to be published by me contain nothing, in any place, that may give offence either to the controversies of religion or to the constitution of the commonwealth. And if Your Imperial Majesty should require anything of me in these mathematical studies, or if I should understand in what matter I might render some welcome service to the same, you will always have me ready and prepared, to whom I humbly offer all my zeal, labour, duty, and obedience. And that Your Imperial Majesty may the more rightly understand both the structure of our castle and the whole purpose of our mind, I send to the same a drawing [designatio] of that same castle and of our whole island.

With these [words] I commend myself to Your Imperial Majesty.
Most devoted to Your Imperial Majesty,
Tycho Brahe, the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup.

Apparatus

  • Sender and recipient. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), then resident on Hven, writing to Rudolf II (1552–1612), Holy Roman Emperor (r. 1576–1612), seeking the imperial printing privilege that would protect his self-published works against unauthorized reprinting in the Empire. The literature confirms this is Tycho's first printing privilege, "a ten-year Imperial charter awarded in 1586" (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, 2007); "by May 1586 Tycho had his ten-year copyright" (Christianson, On Tycho's Island / Lord of Uraniborg). The privilege is the same one later cited (alongside regum quorundam) in the apparatus of the 1602 Mechanica.
  • Document type / no dateline. The letter is preserved as the enclosure (Beilage) to the Imperial Registry (Reichsregistratur) at Vienna, filed under 28 March 1586. The body carries no dateline: it ends with the subscription only. The edition label ("1586. empty") records exactly this — a 1586 letter with an empty (absent) in-text date. Per the edition's convention, the header therefore does not assert a date the source substantiates; it gives the year the text supports (1586) and appends the archival/registry date "(catalogued 28 March 1586)", flagged here rather than presented as an authorial dateline. The "28 March" is the registry filing date, not necessarily the date of composition or dispatch.
  • Calendar. Denmark and the Imperial chancery were both on the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1586; the filing date 28 March is O.S. No conversion is performed.
  • astronomia instauranda / instauranda astronomia — "the renewal of astronomy," Tycho's programmatic phrase (astronomia instaurata, "astronomy restored/renewed"), kept consistent per glossary. The verb recurs twice in the source and is rendered identically both times.
  • arx / Uranieburgum — "castle"; the name Uraniborg ("citadel of Urania," the Muse of astronomy), Tycho's observatory-house on Hven, here spelled Uranieburgo in the source. insula Huena = the island of Hven.
  • Dom: de Knudstrup — "Lord of Knudstrup," Tycho's ancestral seat in Scania (kept as is,).
  • sub incude … versantur — "are on the anvil," a proverbial idiom (incudi reddere, Horace) for work still being hammered into final form; the metaphor is kept.
  • reipublica literaria — "the republic of letters," the commonwealth of learning; rendered literally.
  • designatio — here a drawing / plan (a depiction of Uraniborg and the island enclosed with the letter); rendered "a drawing," with the Latin flagged, since the term can also mean "design/layout."
  • Editorial / orthographic notes. serinissimus (for serenissimus) and reipubl: constitutioni offensioni siet are reproduced as transmitted; siet is the archaic subjunctive sit ("may be"). The chancery contractions are expanded silently only in translation: Caes: Ma: V: = Caesarea Maiestas Vestra ("Your Imperial Majesty"); D. D. in the address = Domine ac Domine ("Lord and Master"); S: before the subscription = servus / subditus ("servant/subject," here taken with addictissimus). The penalty clause sub mulcta transgressoribus irrogari solita ("under the penalty customarily imposed upon transgressors") supplies no figure; "[be enforced]" is bracketed as an editorial supplement to the elliptical Latin.
  • Negation check. The source contains the negations ne … recudantur, ne … defrauder (governed by vereor ne, "I fear that"), ne ea a quoquam typographo recudantur, and ne libri … quidpiam … contineant. Each is reproduced once in the translation; none added or dropped. Note that vereor ne defrauder is the standard Latin verb-of-fearing construction — "I fear that I may be defrauded" (not "I fear that I may not be"); the ne is the fearing-clause marker, not a negation of the verb, and is rendered accordingly.
