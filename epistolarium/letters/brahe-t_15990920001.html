The original

ERLIG OCH WELBIURDIGE FRU SOPHIA BRAHE THILL ERICKSSHOLMB, MINN KIERRE SWOGERSKE OCH BESSØNDERLIG GUODE WENN, GANDSCH WENLIGENN THILSCHREFFUENN.

MINN gandsch wenlig hilßenn nu och altid forßent med Wor Herre. Kierre fru Sophia Brahe, suogerske och beßønderlig guode wenn. Nest en wenlig tacksigelße for megen beuist guode, i mig altid giortt och beuist haffuer, huilckett att forskiulde jeg altid will findis gandsch guoduilligenn med huis i kand haffue ehre och gott aff.

Giffuendis ether gandsch wenligen att wide, att eders guode skriffuelße och quitantze er mig tilhende kommenn. Anlangendis thre hunder daller och om de beuiste bøger saa kand jeg eder venligen icke forholde, at jeg nu nogen tiidlang hafuer hafft samme 300 daller till rede, och werrit eders bud foruentendis, och eptherdj det icke føre kommen er, haffuer jeg dennom paa andere steder wdgiffuitt. Dog paa det i icke paa eders foretagne reiße therudoffuer schulle forhindris, saa sender jeg min egen thienere till Sten Malteßenn med samme penge, att hand eder skall betalle, naar hand eders nøiagtige quitantz ther paa bekommer, thi det icke alleniste mig widkommer, mens mine suogere och medarfuinger. Derfor bleffue ocsaa en quitantz concipierit paa Widtschøffle, wi ware ther tilsammen siste gang, epther alles woris sambtøcke, och eder tilskickitt, thend jeg gierne war begierendis att motte bliffue aff eder forsiglett och wnderschreffuenn; jeg mig icke heller fordriste kandt, en anden quitantz allene att annamme wden de andere medarffuingers widskaff och sambtøcke.

Sammeledis wdi eders quitantz, i mig forßent haffuer, formeldis om it breff paa itt thußind daller och rente penge ther aff, och att her till inted schulle werre der paa bethalledt end 500 daller, med slig andere mehr omstendighedt, huilckett mig for min perßonn aldelis er wbeuift, saawelsom brudschat och andit mehre, bønderne schulle wdgifue.

Setter det derforre i Sten Malteßens handt, att hand paa mine wegne bestiller, att jeg motte faa quitantz paa det breff, salig Henrick Brahe ther om wdgiffuit hafuer, och paa altt andet, som i haffuer hafft att kreffue aff salig fru Lene och nu deris arffuinger. Kierre fru Sophia Brahe, beder jeg gandsch wenligen och gierne, attj hermed ville haffue mig wfortencktt, och at jeg saa bemøder ether med thenne min skriffuelße, thiufflendis icke, att jeg io i slig, ia end vdi større maade hois eder och alle eders med ald tacksom och behagelig tieniste att beuiße will lade mig findis guoduilligenn och wspartt.

Huad bøgerne erre anlangendis, haffuer jeg epther woris affscheid paa Widschiøffle loffuit att wille eder dennom tilskicke, dog med thend beschedenn, att jeg først wille thalle med konng. maytt. der om, om hans maytt. ware dem begierendis, saa giffuer jeg ether wenligen tilkiende, att jeg wdi chantzelers neruerelse haffuer tilforne adsport hans maytt., da haffuer hand suarett, hand icke haffde sønderlig forstandt paa dennom, eller paste der megit om. Endog wille hans maytt. dennom foruare, eptherdj eders kierre broder haffde hannom samme bøger foreritt; haffde det icke werritt, schulle i dennom retteligens gierne och med en guod wilge bekommedtt. Hermed eder och altt dett i well will Gud almegtigste beffallitt, med mange m. guode netter, jegh och Elße ønscher eder och Thage. Actum Helsingøer thend 20 septemb. a:o 1599.

Eders quitantz, eders tiener haffde med sig, sender jeg eder och med min thienere.

Henrich Ramell
m: propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the honourable and well-born Lady Sophie Brahe of Eriksholm, my dear kinswoman and especial good friend, [this] most amicably written.

My most friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with [the blessing of] Our Lord. Dear Lady Sophie Brahe, kinswoman and especial good friend. Next [after] a friendly thanks for the much kindness you have always done and shown me — which to repay I shall always be found quite willing, in whatever you may have honour and good from.

Giving you quite amicably to know that your good letter and quitance have come to my hand. Concerning the three hundred dalers, and about the said books, I cannot amicably withhold from you that I have now for some time had those same 300 dalers ready, and have been awaiting your messenger; and since it did not come sooner, I have paid them out in other places. Yet, so that you may not be hindered on your undertaken journey on that account, I send my own servant to Sten Maltesen with the same money, that he shall pay you when he receives your exact quitance thereon — for it concerns not me alone, but my brothers-in-law and co-heirs. Therefore a quitance was also drafted at Vidskøvle, where we were together last time, by the consent of us all, and sent to you, which I gladly desired might be sealed and signed by you; nor can I venture to accept any other quitance alone, without the other co-heirs' knowledge and consent.

Likewise in your quitance, which you have sent me, mention is made of a bond for one thousand dalers and the interest-money thereon, and that hitherto only 500 dalers should have been paid on it, with sundry further particulars — which, for my own person, is entirely unknown to me, as is also the bride-tax and other more [dues] which the peasants are to pay.

I therefore put it into Sten Maltesen's hand, that he arrange on my behalf that I may get a quitance for the bond which the late Henrik Brahe issued thereon, and for all else you have had to claim from the late Lady Lene and now from their heirs. Dear Lady Sophie Brahe, I ask quite amicably and gladly that you would herewith hold me unblamed — and [pardon] that I trouble you thus with this my letter — not doubting that I, in such, yea in even greater measure, will let myself be found quite willing and unsparing toward you and all yours, with all grateful and pleasing service.

As concerns the books: I had, after our parting at Vidskøvle, promised to send them to you, yet on this condition — that I would first speak with the Royal Majesty about it, [to learn] whether His Majesty desired them. So I give you amicably to know that, in the Chancellor's presence, I previously asked His Majesty; and he answered that he had no special understanding of them, nor cared much about them. Yet His Majesty would keep them, since your dear brother had presented him these same books; had it not been [so], you should rightly, gladly, and with good will have obtained them. Herewith I commend you, and all that you wish well, to God Almighty — with many a thousand good nights, which I and Else wish to you and Tage. Done at Helsingør, the 20th of September, the year 1599.

Your quitance, which your servant had with him, I send you also with my servant.

Henrik Ramel,
with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender & recipient. Henrik Ramel (c. 1550–1610), the Pomeranian-born Danish royal councillor — Tycho's affinis and friend (cf. brahe-t_16000331001, brahe-t_15980628001) — writing to Sophie Brahe (1559–1643), Tycho's astronomer-sister, of Eriksholm (her seat from her first marriage to Otte Thott). Ramel addresses her as swogerske ("kinswoman / sister-in-law"), the family-by-marriage tie. The MS is the original letter.
  • Significance. A Danish family business letter within the Brahe kin-network, on an estate / inheritance settlement in which Sophie and Ramel's group of medarvinger (co-heirs) are jointly involved. It connects to the same circle as the letters to/about Sophie and her son Tage Thott (brahe-t_16000217001, brahe-t_16000115001) — indeed Ramel and his wife Else close by wishing "many good nights" to Sophie and Tage.
  • The money & the co-heirs' quitance. Ramel had 300 dalers ready for Sophie but, her messenger being late, paid them out elsewhere; he now sends them via his servant to Sten Maltesen against Sophie's exact receipt — stressing that the matter "concerns not me alone, but my brothers-in-law and co-heirs," so a joint quitance drafted at Vidskøvle (a Brahe-family estate) "by the consent of us all" must be sealed and signed; he will not accept a separate one without the co-heirs' consent. Further items: a 1,000-daler bond (only 500 paid), the bride-tax (brudskat) and peasant dues, and quitances for a bond issued by the late Henrik Brahe and for claims on the estate of the late Lady Lene and her heirs.
  • The books and the King (notable). Sophie had wished Ramel to send her certain books; he had promised to, but first asked Christian IV (in the Chancellor's presence) whether the King wanted them. The King "had no special understanding of them, nor cared much about them," yet would keep them — "since your dear brother [Tycho] had presented him these same books." A telling vignette: books Tycho had given Christian IV, which the King retained out of form rather than interest, blocking Sophie's claim. (Provenance-relevant — books from the Brahe orbit in the royal collection.)
  • Persons named. Henrik Ramel (sender); Sophie Brahe (recipient); Sten Maltesen (the intermediary holding the money); the medarvinger (co-heirs); the late Henrik Brahe; the late Lady Lene and her heirs; Christian IV and his Chancellor (Christian Friis); "your dear brother" = Tycho Brahe; Else (Ramel's wife) and Tage (Thott, Sophie's son).
  • Calendar. Dateline Helsingøer thend 20 septemb. a:o 1599; Old Style (Denmark). Header agrees with the label.
