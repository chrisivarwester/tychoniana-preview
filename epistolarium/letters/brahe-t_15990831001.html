TYCHO BRAHE AD IOH. G. HERWARTUM AB HOHENBURG.

S.

SIC est, Vir Nobilis et magnifice Amice, licet de facie mihi ignote, plurimi tamen ob præclaras ingenij tui dotes semper faciende, quod a Sacra Cæsarea Maiestate Pragam clementissime vocatus ab ipsius Cæsarea Maiestate in ipsius clientem clementissime sim acceptus, et liberaliter prout ferunt hæc tempora mihi et meis studijs ab ipsius Cæsarea Maiestate provisum. Concessus etiam est benignissime habitationi meæ locus hic in arce Benachia 5 miliaribus Pragâ distante, ubi commodius et tranquillius quam in urbe, consuetis meis exercitijs Astronomicis et Spagyricis vacare licebit, et opus Astronomicum, quod diu multumque molior, favente Cœlestium Opifice in ipsius Cæsareæ Maiestatis et Imperij honorem (siquidem ob quorundam inscitiam et invidiam in patria mea id fieri nequiverit) absolvere et consecrare, qua etiam de caussa totum meum apparatum Astronomicum et supellectilem omnem, quam Magdeburgi reliqui, huc transferri curo.

Cæterum ut ad . T. eruditas quæstiones pro virili respondeam, sic habeat. Veneris stellam anno ante CHRISTUM 4 inchoante cœlitus non conspectam, magni miraculi loco ducerem, si res ea indubie constaret: sit fides apud Autorem, qui cum Poëta fuerit, Privilegio forsitan Poëtico usus est, cum ne quidem Historici, qui magis veraces haberi volunt, et certe esse debebant, in cœlestibus ostentis recte indicandis, experientiæ consona semper proferant, adeo ut interdum Chasmata quædam et ignitas aëris inflammationes, varias formas sæpe exhibentes, pro Cometis habeant, quos tamen ipso in Cœlo locum obtinere, illicque cursum suum absolvere, e septem, quos intra 22 Annos conspexi, ratis observationibus demonstrative comprobavi. Concedatur vero ♀ris stellam tunc miraculose delituisse: ☿ij tamen interventu id fieri potuisse, ut sic Eclipsin passa sit, verisimile non videtur, neque enim ☿ij stella in cœlo tantæ apparet magnitudinis, ut ♀ris stellam, quæ multo maior conspicitur, abscondere possit. Adde quod ☿ij stella luminosa sit, nec opaco corpore prædita ut ☾, ideoque si quando centraliter ♀ri et visui nostro interponeretur, lumen suum illius lumini potius coniungeret, sicque illud augeret magis quam imminueret. Taceo quod ob motus celeritatem diu sub ♀ris stella ☿ consistere nequeat, nisi in easdem partes æquarentur ambarum motus, quod rarissime evenit. Præterea cum ipsemet ex calculo Prutenico adinveneris, stellam ☿ij tunc fuisse 2¾ G. ♀re borealiorem, satis partiliter et exacte pro Eclipsatione non iungebatur. Licet enim in latitudine non saltem horum duorum Planetarum, quos inferiores vocant, sed et cæterorum magna diversitas in cœlo ab utrisque tabulis deprehendatur, tamen adeo enormiter ☿ij stellam quoad latitudinem exorbitare non animadverti. Observavi quidem ♀rm, cum esset circa medietatem ♓ium iuxta Perigæum Epicycli (ut communiter loquuntur) latitudinem Boream assequi posse partium 9 Min. 50, tribus gradibus plus quam fert Alphonsinorum calculus, licet is Coperniceum unico circiter excedat, ita ut in 10um G. completum excrescere possit eius ab Ecliptica in Boream digressio; idque bis denotavi, utpote anno 1587 et 1595 interiecto (uti requiritur) octennio, quibus etiam temporibus ♀rem intra triduum et quatriduum tam vespertinam quam eoam meis instrumentis observavi et commutationem illius (uti vocat COPERNICUS) hinc promptius restitui. Sic etiam in ☿io et tribus superioribus Planetis non modicam adinveni in eorum latitudinibus (veluti innui) a cœlesti norma exorbitationem, adeo ut ne quidem in tribus illis, ubi simplicior ratio est, limites maximarum latitudinum rite prædefiniti sint a PTOLEMÆO, cuius placitis nimis secure æquievit COPERNICUS, nec nodi intersectionum circuituum Planetarum cum Ecliptica ita se habent, respectu Apogæorum, uti ex PTOLEMÆO assumpsit idem COPERNICUS. Et si ea quæ de his tradidit PTOLEMÆUS, ipsius ævo cœlo consona erant, oportebit nodos hosce simulque limites maximæ latitudinis longe alium sensibiliter per se habere motum, quam is est, qui ab Apogæorum mutatione procedit, differentia nunc intercedente multorum graduum. Quin et in ipsa ☾ non minima conspicitur in latitudine varietas atque a traditione antecessorum diversitas. In novilunijs siquidem et plenilunijs latitudinem maximam obtinere potest 4 partium et 58', quæ Ptolemaicum limitem intra 2' attingit. At in □tis eandem tertia gradus parte iuxta limitem boream et austrinum adauget, et quod magis mirum est, nodorum insuper motum exhibet inæqualem singulis mensibus hinc inde reciprocantem, idque sensibili intervallo, quod gradum 1 35' attingit. Ex modo dictis itaque (de quibus tamen alias latius et particularius volente Deo agam) satis liquet, quod . T. latitudinis Planetarum calculum e tabulis consuetis erutum, non citra rem suspectum habeat; nihilominus circa ea tempora, quando in ☿ij latitudine e tabulis Prutenicis supputatio est instituta, usque adeo magnum insinuari potuisse errorem vix crediderim, quod PTOLEMÆI temporibus, quibus ille astrorum observationibus invigilavit, decies magis quam nunc sumus propiora fuerint. Et canones Prutenici illo ævo Ptolemaicis quam proxime subscribunt. At si in observatis PTOLEMÆI error aliquis subrepsit, quod non raro factum haud sine causa suspicor, frustra super hoc fundamentum ædificamus; quare circumspecte admodum, nec nisi limitatione competenti adhibita eius placitis utendum.

De Eclipsibus, quæ scribis, ita se habent quod Prutenicæ tabulæ ijs, quæ PTOLEMÆI ætate contigerunt, quam proxime consentiant, nec tamen singulis satis præcise adaptatæ sint; constat enim COPERNICUM ad Ptolemaicas Eclipses, quatenus in nonnullis fieri potuit, tanquam Epochas suos numeros applicuisse. In omnibus tamen accuratam amussim invenire non sustinuit, ob aliam quandam inæqualitatis lunaris nec a PTOLEMÆO nec ipso animadversam implicationem. Neque enim tres Eclipses prout illi factitarunt, sufficiunt ad primam inæqualitatem ☾æ enucleandam, ut de altera adhuc intricatiore hîc nihil dicam; sed pluribus in diversis Eccentrici seu maioris Epicycli locis opus est ijsdemque accuratissime denotatis. Ut vero hæc aliquatenus in uno saltem exemplo dispicias, mitto deliquij ☾æ in principio huius anni pragmatiam, tam in triangulari demonstratione ex hypothesi nostra, quam numeris correspondentibus ab eximio Mathematico Wittebergensi M. IOSTELIO, dum ibi aliquamdiu ob luem in Bohemia grassantem et alijs de causis subsisterem, me concedente factitatam et typis excusam. Ubi vero meæ tabulæ, favente divina ope, de motu luminarium (quas cæteris Planetis præmittere animus est) sub auspicijs Romani Imperatoris RUDOLPHI Secundi in lucem prodierint, testabitur experientia, eas non saltem nostro seculo adamussim quadrare, sed et vetustissimis illis Eclipsibus sufficienter congruere, quatenus veterum observata ipsissimis Cœli apparentijs præcise consona fuêre. Et quid quæso vel in ☾a vel cæteris Planetis ab omni vitio immune præstare poterant, cum stellas fixas minus fideliter correctas adhibuerint, quemadmodum mille affixarum stellarum accuratissima restitutio in ipso minuto adeoque nonnunquam eius semisse a me præteritis aliquot annis exantlata et nuper Cæsareæ Maiestati in membrana manuscripta subiectissime exhibita, abunde testari poterit, collatione cum consuetis abacis instituta. Obtuli etiam eidem Cæsareæ Maiestati in alio libro manuscripto Solis et Lunæ restitutionem ad singulos dies duorum proximorum annorum, apposita simul discrepantia utriusque calculi Alphonsini et Copernicei, quæ subinde non exigua est. Quin et in tertio quodam eiusdem formæ et magnitudinis libro Mechanicam Astronomiæ partem Instrumenta mea, quæ hactenus elaboravi repræsentantem, eumque typis, postquam Dania excessi, impressum, ipsius Cæsaris Maiestati pariter humillime dedicavi. De hoc ultimo misissem nunc tria exemplaria, duo pro illustrissimis Ducibus Bavariæ GUILHELMO seniore ac patre et MAXIMILIANO filio regente, tertium pro te ipso, sed nullum iam ad manus habeo quod coloribus rite illuminatum (uti vocant) existit. Verum id Pragæ modo fieri procuro, quo peracto, quod modo dixi præstabo: et forte plura etiam mittam, quæcunque tam cito describi curare potero. Interim pauculis verbis indicare non degraveris, an dicti Illustrissimi Principes vestri talibus afficiantur. Valeat T. M. quam felicissime et diutissime, meque literis suis, uti benevole incepit, eruditis sæpius invisere, atque amicitiam semel ijs, cum aliter vix liceat, inchoatam ijsdem continuare et conservare pergat; inveniet me ad idem præstandum quam paratissimum, ut nunquam sic se certasse priorem pœniteat, omniaque ei studia mea et quæcunque a me proficisci possunt officia, lubens polliceor atque offero. Cæterum quod non prius rescripserim, varijs occupationibus, quibus Pragæ præpediebar, et migrationi in hanc arcem nuperæ imputet, et candide excusatum habeat. Volui istam tarditatem in rescribendo ea, quam vides, prolixitate compensare, quæ ut accepta sit rogo.

Iamque iterum et sæpius T. M. quam optime valeat.
Dabantur ex arce Cæsareæ Benachia die 31. Augusti Anno 1599.
. T. studiosissimus
TYCHO BRAHE.

TYCHO BRAHE TO JOH. G. HERWART VON HOHENBURG.

Greeting.

So it is, noble and magnificent Friend — though unknown to me by face, yet always to be highly valued for the eminent gifts of your genius — that, clemently called to Prague by the Sacred Imperial Majesty, I have been most clemently received as His client, and liberally (as these times allow) provided for, with my studies, by His Imperial Majesty. A place for my dwelling has also most kindly been granted here, in Benátky castle, 5 miles distant from Prague, where I shall be free — more conveniently and tranquilly than in the city — to pursue my accustomed Astronomical and Spagyric [alchemical] exercises, and to complete and consecrate the Astronomical work I have long and much laboured at, to the honour of His Imperial Majesty and the Empire (since, through certain men's ignorance and envy, it could not be done in my fatherland) — for which cause too I am having my whole Astronomical apparatus, and all the furnishings I left at Magdeburg, transferred hither.

But, to answer Your Magnificence's erudite questions to my power: thus hold it. That the star of Venus was not seen in the heaven at the beginning of the 4th year before Christ, I would count in the place of a great miracle, if the thing were undoubtedly established. Let the credit rest with the Author, who, since he was a Poet, perhaps used a Poetic licence — since not even the Historians (who wish to be held more truthful, and certainly ought to be) always report, in correctly indicating celestial portents, things consonant with experience; so that sometimes they take certain chasmata [sky-cleavings] and fiery inflammations of the air (often showing various forms) for Comets — which, however, hold a place in the heaven itself, and there complete their course, as I have demonstratively proved, by reliable observations, of the seven I beheld within 22 years. But grant that the star of Venus [♀] then miraculously hid itself: that it could have happened by the intervention of Mercury [☿] — that it thus suffered an eclipse — does not seem likely; for Mercury's star does not appear in the heaven of such magnitude as to be able to hide Venus's star, which is seen much larger. Add that Mercury's star is luminous, and not endowed with an opaque body like the Moon [☾]; and so, if it were ever centrally interposed between Venus and our sight, it would rather join its light to that light, and so augment it more than diminish it. I am silent that, on account of the speed of its motion, Mercury cannot long abide under Venus's star, unless both their motions were equalised in the same direction, which most rarely happens. Besides, since you yourself found, from the Prutenic calculation, that Mercury's star was then 2¾° more northern than Venus, it was not partilely and exactly enough joined for an eclipse. For although a great diversity of latitude is detected in the heaven, from both tables — not only of these two Planets, which they call the lower, but of the rest too — yet I have not noticed Mercury's star to deviate so enormously as to latitude.

I have indeed observed Venus, when it was about the middle of Pisces [♓], near the Perigee of the Epicycle (as they commonly speak), able to reach a Northern latitude of 9° 50′ — three degrees more than the Alphonsine calculation gives (though that exceeds the Copernican by about one), so that its digression from the Ecliptic into the North can grow to a full 10°; and this I noted twice, namely in the years 1587 and 1595, with the eight-year interval interposed (as is required) — at which times too I observed Venus, both evening and morning, within three and four days, with my instruments, and thence more readily restored its "commutation" (as Copernicus calls it). So too in Mercury and the three superior Planets I found no small deviation in their latitudes from the celestial norm (as you hint) — so that not even in those three (where the reckoning is simpler) are the limits of the maximum latitudes rightly predefined by Ptolemy (whose tenets Copernicus too securely accepted); nor do the nodes of the intersections of the planets' circuits with the Ecliptic stand, with respect to the Apogees, as Copernicus assumed from Ptolemy. And if what Ptolemy transmitted of these was consonant with the heaven in his age, these nodes, and the limits of maximum latitude, must have a far different, perceptible motion of their own than that which proceeds from the change of the Apogees — a difference of many degrees now intervening. Nay, in the Moon itself no small variety of latitude is seen, and a diversity from the predecessors' tradition. For in new and full moons the maximum latitude can be 4° 58′, which reaches the Ptolemaic limit within 2′; but in the quadratures [□] it augments the same by a third of a degree, on the north and south limit; and — what is more wonderful — it shows besides the motion of the nodes unequal, reciprocating back and forth each month, and that by a perceptible interval, which reaches 1° 35′. From what has just been said, then (of which, however, I will treat more fully and particularly elsewhere, God willing), it is clear enough that Your Magnificence not without cause holds suspect the planet-latitude calculation drawn from the usual tables. Nonetheless, around those times when the computation in Mercury's latitude was made from the Prutenic tables, I should scarcely believe that so great an error could have crept in — because Ptolemy's times, when he watched the star-observations, were ten times nearer [to his epoch] than we now are; and the Prutenic canons subscribe most closely to the Ptolemaic for that age. But if some error crept into Ptolemy's observations (which I suspect, not without cause, has not rarely happened), we build upon this foundation in vain; wherefore his tenets are to be used very circumspectly, and only with a competent limitation applied.

Concerning the Eclipses of which you write, it is thus: that the Prutenic tables agree most closely with those which happened in Ptolemy's age, yet are not precisely adapted to each; for it is established that Copernicus applied his numbers to the Ptolemaic Eclipses (so far as could be done in some) as Epochs. Yet in all he could not attain an accurate exactness, on account of a certain other implication of the lunar inequality, noticed by neither Ptolemy nor himself. For three eclipses, as they practised, do not suffice to work out the Moon's first inequality — to say nothing here of the second, yet more intricate one; but more are needed, in different places of the Eccentric or greater Epicycle, and those most accurately noted. That you may consider this somewhat, in at least one example, I send the practical working [pragmatia] of the lunar eclipse at the beginning of this year — both in the triangular demonstration from our hypothesis, and in the corresponding numbers, worked out (with my leave) and printed by the eminent Wittenberg Mathematician M. Jöstel, while I tarried there a while, on account of the plague raging in Bohemia and other causes. But when my tables of the motion of the luminaries (which I purpose to publish before the other Planets) shall, by divine help, come to light under the auspices of the Roman Emperor Rudolf II, experience will testify that they fit not only our age exactly, but also sufficiently agree with those most ancient Eclipses — so far as the ancients' observations were precisely consonant with the very appearances of the heaven. And what, pray, could they furnish, in the Moon or the other Planets, immune from all fault, when they used the fixed stars less faithfully corrected — as the most accurate restitution of a thousand fixed stars, [done] to the very minute, and sometimes to its half, drawn out by me over the past years, and lately most submissively exhibited to the Imperial Majesty in a manuscript on parchment, will abundantly testify, by a comparison set up with the usual tables. I also offered the same Imperial Majesty, in another manuscript book, the restitution of the Sun and Moon for each day of the next two years, with the discrepancy of both calculations — the Alphonsine and the Copernican — set down at once (which is sometimes not small). Nay, and in a third book of the same form and size — the Mechanical part of Astronomy, representing my Instruments which I have hitherto elaborated, and printed after I left Denmark — I likewise most humbly dedicated to His Imperial Majesty. Of this last I would have sent now three copies — two for the most illustrious Dukes of Bavaria, William the elder and father, and Maximilian the son, [now] reigning; the third for yourself — but I have none at hand now that is properly illuminated with colours (as they call it). But I am now having this done at Prague; which finished, I will do what I just said, and perhaps send even more — whatever I can have copied so quickly. Meanwhile do not find it a burden to indicate, in a few words, whether your said most illustrious Princes are affected by such things. May Your Magnificence fare most happily and most long, and continue often to visit me with your erudite letters, as you have benevolently begun, and to continue and preserve the friendship once begun by them (since otherwise it can scarcely be); you will find me most ready to do the same, so that you may never repent of having thus striven first; and I gladly promise and offer you all my zeal and whatever services can proceed from me. For the rest, that I did not write back sooner, impute it to the various occupations by which I was hindered at Prague, and to my recent migration into this castle, and hold it candidly excused. I wished to compensate that tardiness in writing back by the prolixity you see — which I beg may be welcome.

And now again, and oftener, may Your Magnificence fare most well.
Given from the imperial castle of Benátky, the 31st day of August, the year 1599.
Most zealous of Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Tycho → Hans Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (Munich) — the letter Herwart answers in brahe-t_15990917001 (17 September 1599), where he cites "your letter from Benátky on the last day of last month." Read the two as a pair.
  • Significance — Tycho's reply on the Venus-star and his planetary/lunar discoveries (a major scientific letter).
  • The Benátky establishment. Tycho confirms his clemently-granted seat at Benátky, "5 miles from Prague," for his "Astronomical and Spagyric [alchemical] exercises," with his apparatus being moved from Magdeburg.
  • The Venus-star debunked (physical refutation). Against Herwart's theory that a reported non-appearance of Venus "at the beginning of 4 BC" was a Mercury-occultation (cf. brahe-t_15990917001; the chronology question Scaliger also attacked, brahe-t_16000310001), Tycho marshals physical arguments: the "Author" was a poet (poetic licence); Mercury is far too small to hide the much larger Venus; Mercury is luminous, not opaque like the Moon, so it would add light, not eclipse it; Mercury moves too fast to stay under Venus; and it was 2¾° off in latitude (Herwart's own Prutenic figure) — far from a partile conjunction. A model of Tychonic empirical reasoning. (He also defends, in passing, the supra-lunar place of comets — "seven in 22 years.")
  • Planetary-latitude discoveries. Tycho reports observing Venus reach 9° 50′–10° N latitude (3° beyond the Alphonsine, c. 1° beyond the Copernican), noted twice (1587 & 1595, the required 8-year interval); and deviations of Mercury and the three superior planets from Ptolemy/Copernicus — concluding that the maximum-latitude limits and the nodes have an independent motion, not derivable from the apogees, differing now "by many degrees."
  • The lunar discoveries (the core, celebrated in Herwart's reply of 17 September 1599). Max lunar latitude 4° 58′ at syzygy (within 2′ of Ptolemy's limit), but +⅓° at the quadratures; and — "more wonderful" — the nodes' motion is unequal, reciprocating monthly by up to 1° 35′. This is Tycho's variation of the inclination and libration of the nodes (cf. the same to Scaliger, brahe-t_16000723001).
  • On eclipses and tables. The Prutenic tables fit Ptolemy-era eclipses (Copernicus used them as epochs) but not precisely — for "three eclipses do not suffice to work out the Moon's first inequality," still less the second; more, in varied epicycle positions, are needed. Tycho sends the worked lunar eclipse of early 1599 (triangular demonstration + numbers printed by Melchior Jöstel at Wittenberg, where Tycho waited out the Bohemian plague), and promises his luminary tables "under the auspices of Rudolf II" (the future Rudolphine work) — superior because the ancients "used the fixed stars less faithfully corrected."
  • The three books offered to Rudolf II (a precise inventory): (1) the 1000-fixed-star restitution "to the minute, sometimes the half," on parchment; (2) a Sun–Moon restitution for each day of the next two years, with Alphonsine/Copernican discrepancies; (3) the Mechanica (printed since leaving Denmark). And a Mechanica gift plan: three coloured/illuminated copies — for Duke William V of Bavaria ("the elder, father") and Duke Maximilian I ("the son, reigning") and Herwart — to follow once illuminated at Prague. (A datable Mechanica distribution arrangement.)
  • Cross-references. Pair with brahe-t_15990917001 (Herwart's reply, 17 September 1599); the lunar-theory thread (brahe-t_16000723001); the Venus-star/chronology thread (brahe-t_16000310001); and the Mechanica-distribution layer (the Bavarian-duke copies).
  • Persons & places named.
