The original

NOBILISSIMO ET ERUDITIONE NON VULGARI PRÆEMINENTISSIMO VIRO D. OLIGERO ROSENKRANTZIO DOMINO HÆREDITARIO IN ROSENHOLM, COGNATO, AFFINI ET AMICO MEO INTEGERRIMO.
S.

NOBILISSIME OLIGERE, cognate et affinis charissime, redditæ mihi quidem sunt literæ tuæ postremæ, eruditæ illæ et amoris ac constantiæ erga me plenissimæ. Sed aberam tunc Wandesburgo in oppidulo quodam sex miliaribus inde distante, quod Brambsted vocatur, ut cum Domino Produce isthic mihi obvio de quibusdam loquerer. Reperi autem ibidem Illustm Dominum Administratorem et ipsius Illmam coniugem, qui me singulari favore clementer complexi sunt. Domum autem reversus pridie Natalitiorum hasce tuas accepi, quas una e filiabus meis reddidit, nuntij, qui eas apportarat, nulla facta mentione, cum ea, ubi is esset, ignoraret. At cum postmodum statim ad currum conscenderem huc Hamburgum cum meis iturus, ut festos dies hîc transigeremus, mihi indicabatur, nuntium tuum adhuc adesse; non quidem retrospicere licuit, sed data scheda, quæ illum suo officio perfunctum testaretur, redire eum permisi, cogitans hîc cum Nobilibus Danis, qui D. Administratorem domum deduxerunt, tuis respondere velle, quod etiam sufficienter præstarem, nisi illi hodie præter opinionem, incidente quadam ingrata necessitate, abitum festinassent, cum alias me unâ cum BALTHASARE ALFELDIO Wandesburgi invisere constituissent. Cogor igitur premere ea, quæ latius ad te scribere animum induxeram, illa in adventum tuum ad me Wandesburgum, quem tam amice polliceris, procrastinaturus. Ego itaque te desideratissime OLIGERE isthic, volente Numine, præstolabor, et avidissime cum alacritate excipiam. Utinam CRAGIUS noster tecum sit vel præcedat; tunc inter nos coram sermones miscebimus de ijs, quæ nunc scribere importunitas vetat, quæque per se calamus non satis tuta sustinet. Fac igitur mature stes promissis, de quo etiam nihil addubito, utque queas nobis salvus adesse.

Vale.
Dabantur Hamburgo, postridie Natalitiorum Anno 97.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 18; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Resaluta meo nomine quam officiosissime D. IACOBUM ULFELDIUM, de cuius fide et constanti in me amore satis certus sum; neque enim levitatibus et nugis est obnoxius. Scripsissem illi, nisi temporis penuria præpedirer. Saluta etiam BROCKENHUSIUM illum, qui se meum vocat discipulum; eius literis respondere iam non suppetit otium; habeat itaque me excusatum.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man, pre-eminent in no common learning, Master Holger Rosenkrantz, hereditary lord of Rosenholm, my kinsman, kinsman-by-marriage, and most upright friend — greeting.

Most noble Holger, dearest kinsman and kinsman-by-marriage, your latest letter has indeed been delivered to me — that learned letter, brimful of your love and constancy towards me. But I was at that time away from Wandsbek, in a certain little town some six miles distant from there, called Bramstedt, in order to confer about certain matters with the Lord Provost [Produx], who met me there. And I found in that same place the Most Illustrious Lord Administrator and his Most Illustrious consort, who graciously embraced me with singular favour. But having returned home on the eve of Christmas [pridie Natalitiorum], I received this letter of yours, which one of my daughters delivered, no mention being made of the messenger who had brought it — since she did not know where he was. Yet when soon afterwards I was at once mounting the carriage to go here to Hamburg with my household, that we might pass the festal days here, it was reported to me that your messenger was still at hand; I could not, to be sure, look back [to deal with it then], but, giving him a note that should attest he had discharged his duty, I let him return — intending here to wish to answer your letter together with the Danish noblemen who had escorted the Lord Administrator home, which I should indeed do sufficiently, had they not today, beyond expectation, an unwelcome necessity having intervened, hastened their departure — whereas otherwise they had resolved to visit me, along with Balthasar Alefeldt, at Wandsbek. I am therefore compelled to suppress those things which I had set my mind to write to you at greater length, putting them off until your coming to me at Wandsbek, which you so amiably promise. And so, most longed-for Holger, I shall await you there, God willing, and shall receive you most eagerly and with alacrity. Would that our Cragius were with you, or came ahead; then between ourselves, face to face, we shall exchange conversations about those matters which importunity now forbids to write, and which of itself the pen cannot bear safely enough. See to it, then, that you stand by your promises in good time — of which I have no doubt at all — and that you may be able, safe and sound, to be present with us.

Farewell.
Given at Hamburg, the day after Christmas [postridie Natalitiorum], the year [15]97.
Yours, TYCHO BRAHE,
with my own hand.

[Postscript:] Greet again on my behalf, most dutifully, Master Jacob Ulfeldt, of whose faith and constant love towards me I am sufficiently sure; for he is not given to levities and trifles. I should have written to him, were I not hindered by want of time. Greet too that Brockenhus who calls himself my disciple; there is now no leisure to spare to answer his letter; let him therefore hold me excused.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, here in his post-Hven exile: having left Denmark in 1597, he was residing at the castle of Wandsbek (Wandesburgum), near Hamburg, as guest of Heinrich Rantzau. The letter is signed manu propria ("with my own hand").
  • Recipient. Oligerus Rosenkrantzius, dominus haereditarius in Rosenholm = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), the young Danish nobleman and theologian of Rosenholm (Jutland). Tycho addresses him as cognatus et affinis — kinsman and kinsman-by-marriage — and a sequel letter of 22 January 1598 (also from Wandsbek) continues the same exchange about Rosenkrantz's promised visit.
  • cognato, affini — cognatus = a blood-relative/kinsman; affinis = a relative by marriage. Both retained distinctly rather than collapsed into "relative," since the pairing is a deliberate courtesy formula.
  • Domino Produce — Produx (acc./abl. Producem/Produce) is an unusual title; rendered "the Lord Provost [Produx]" with the Latin flagged. It denotes a local official (the Propst/provost of Bramstedt or a comparable administrative head) whom Tycho met at Bramstedt; the exact office is not specified in the text. Flagged, not silently resolved.
  • Illustm Dominum Administratorem … ipsius Illmam coniugem — Illustm and Illmam are abbreviations for Illustrissimum / Illustrissimam (expanded in the translation as "Most Illustrious"). The Administrator is the Lutheran Administrator of the Prince-Archbishopric of Bremen, Johann Adolf of Holstein-Gottorp (Administrator of Bremen 1585–96 and of Lübeck, then Duke of Holstein-Gottorp from 1590), here with his consort — the regional prince in whose orbit Tycho was then moving. (Identification from the regional and chronological context; the text gives the office, not the name.)
  • Brambsted = Bad Bramstedt in Holstein, north of Hamburg; Wandesburgo = Wandsbek, now a district of Hamburg. sex miliaribus = "six miles" (German miles, hence a substantial distance).
  • Balthasar Alfeldio = Balthasar (von) Alefeldt (Ahlefeldt), of the Holstein noble family; named as one of the company who had meant to visit Tycho at Wandsbek.
  • Cragius noster = Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, c. 1550–1602), the Danish humanist, historian, and diplomat; "our Cragius" marks him as a mutual friend. The 22 January 1598 sequel reports that Cragius would pass straight on to Lübeck rather than come to Wandsbek as hoped.
  • quae … calamus non satis tuta sustinet — "matters which … the pen cannot bear safely enough": Tycho's discretion about confidential matters (his standing with the Danish crown and his exile) is preserved as written, not paraphrased into a definite topic. The reticence is itself the point.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting"; Vale = "Farewell"; manu propria = "with my own hand."
  • Calendar / dateline. Dabantur Hamburgo, postridie Natalitiorum Anno 97 = "Given at Hamburg, the day after Christmas, the year [15]97" (O.S.; Denmark and the Lutheran north kept the Julian calendar). postridie Natalitiorum = the day after the Nativity = 26 December, which agrees with the catalogue label (1597. 26. december). No in-letter date discrepancy: the body's pridie Natalitiorum ("the eve of Christmas," 24 December) is the date Tycho received Rosenkrantz's letter at Wandsbek, a distinct earlier event from the postridie Natalitiorum (26 December) dateline of writing at Hamburg; the two are consistent with the narrated sequence (return home 24 Dec → set out for Hamburg → write 26 Dec). The Anno 97 is given as written, not expanded to 1597 except in brackets.
  • Witness. A = Copenhagen, Rigsarkivet, Langebeks Diplomatarium, a later copy (Afskrift), per the source's witness field; the autograph is not the surviving witness.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (greetings to Jacob Ulfeldt + Brockenhus), restored from Dreyer VIII, p. 18 (dropped after "manu propria").
