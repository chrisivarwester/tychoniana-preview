The original

MAGNIFICO ET GENEROSO DOMINO D. TYCHONI BRAHAe, DOMINO HÆREDITARIO IN KNUSTORPFF ET HEERESWART, CÆSAREAe MAIESTATIS CONSILIARIO DIGNISSIMO, AMICO SUO ET DOMINO SUMMA OBSERVANTIA COLENDO.

S. P.

OCCASIONE oblata intermittere non debui, quin aliquid literarum ad te, Magnifice Dom. TYCHO, darem, et quidem per eundem, quem ipse praesentem aliquoties cupivisti, quo etiam hisce in locis exercitatiorem sphagirum nescimus. Novissimae tuae literae sunt quas misisti per NICOLAUM MAUL quondam domesticum tuum, in quibus etiam quae ad grammatophorum expedienda praecepisti exequutus sum.

Quoad Politicum statum omnia apud nos sunt tranquilla, nihilominus tamen multae gliscunt inter magnos dominos, principes et reges simultates, quae bellorum semina et initia esse solent. Inter CAROLUM Sueciae nunc protectorem et urbem Lubecam quomodo convenit, te non latet, quod negocium nondum est consopitum, licet Lubecenses multum in eo laboraverint. Comitia Polonica nuper Warsoviae, quo et Brandenburgicus Elector, adiunctis in adsistentiam Regis Daniae, nostri principis et nonnullorum aliorum, petitum successionem in Feudum Brutenicum, legatos miserat, celebrabantur, quod denegatum illi esse intelligo, nisi regem Poloniae, Suecia nunc privatum, postliminum regem fieri opitulentur. In dictis etiam Comitijs Poloni hostes suos, ut fertur, declararunt, CAROLUM Sueciae, qui iam dudum bellum in Livonia Polonis intulit, et eousque progressus est in Livonia, ut Polonis ibidem nihil restet praeter Rigam. Et meo quidem iudicio hoc bellum non solum ad CAROLUM pertinere videtur, nisi temeritatis sibi hocipso vitium attrahat. Alter hostis Polonorum dictus est Magnus dux Moscoviae, tertius vero Walachiae Waiwoda. Utrum tot hostibus Poloni simul stare poterint, eventus docebit. In Belgico magnus est apparatus iterum belli praefixi: ALBERTUS dux Austriae exercitum ducet in Hollandiam. Hollandici status non segnius vim eius aversari moliuntur. Aestas futura absque Tragedijs magnis non praeteribit, uti praesens est forma rerum. Rex Portugaliae SEBASTIANUS in conflictu Africano amissus, nunc redivivus, ab Hispano non agnoscitur, a suis vero affectatur, qui exutus regno apud Regem Galliae hospitio fruitur. Proh deum, quanta Metamorphosis ista! EXSECCIUS Comes Angliae et successor Reginae in regno hactenus muscitatus, ante biennium a Regina in Irlandiam cum Classe Nautica missus, ob rem non satis fideliter, ut accusatur, gestam, in Parlamento damnatus, et captivus de vita periclitatur. In Patria tua Regno Daniae cura et expensis Regis nonnulla fortalitia et Castella, ut vocant, exstruuntur, nec non in Gothlandia idem fit. Ungarica a nobis non expectabis, quin potius quid rerum ibi agitur, a te perdiscere cupimus. Haec per occationem ad te pervenire volui.

Vale, ut bene etiam sit tuis.
Datae Rostochij 21 Martij anni 1601.
T. Mag. observantiss.
CHRISTOPHO. HENNIOCHUS Med.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 408; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Dux Brunsuiccensis civitati denuo commeatum intercipit, nec ulla ratione placabilis dicitur, inter quos, missis et datis a Caesarea Maiestate commissarijs, nuper frustra de pace tractatum esse, notum est.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and generous Lord, Lord Tycho Brahe, hereditary Lord in Knudstrup and Heereswart, most worthy Councillor of the Imperial Majesty, his friend and Lord to be honoured with the utmost respect.

Heartiest greeting.

An occasion being offered, I ought not to omit to send you some bit of a letter, magnificent Lord Tycho — and that by the very man whom you yourself several times wished present, than whom we know no more practised spagyrist in these parts. Your most recent letters are those you sent through Nicolaus Maul, formerly your servant, in which I also carried out what you directed for despatching the letter-carrier.

As to the political situation, with us all is tranquil; nevertheless many enmities are swelling among the great lords, princes, and kings, such as are wont to be the seeds and beginnings of wars. How matters stand between Charles, now Protector of Sweden, and the city of Lübeck does not escape you; that business is not yet laid to rest, though the Lübeckers have laboured much over it. The Polish Diet was lately held at Warsaw, to which the Elector of Brandenburg, too, had sent envoys — with those of the King of Denmark, of our own prince, and of some others, joined in support — to seek the succession to the Prussian fief; which I understand was refused him, unless they help the King of Poland, now deprived of Sweden, to be restored as king by right of return. In that same Diet the Poles also, as is reported, declared their enemies: Charles of Sweden, who long since carried war into Livonia against the Poles, and has advanced so far in Livonia that nothing remains to the Poles there but Riga. And in my judgement this war seems not to concern Charles alone — unless by this very act he draws upon himself the charge of rashness. The second enemy of the Poles was named the Grand Duke of Muscovy, and the third the Voivode of Wallachia. Whether the Poles will be able to stand against so many enemies at once, the outcome will show. In the Low Countries there is again great preparation for an appointed war: Albert, Archduke of Austria, will lead an army into Holland; the Dutch States are contriving no less actively to repel his force. The coming summer will not pass without great tragedies, such as the present shape of things is. The King of Portugal, Sebastian, lost in the African battle, now come back to life, is not acknowledged by the Spaniard, but is favoured by his own people; he, stripped of his kingdom, enjoys hospitality with the King of France. Good God, how great a Metamorphosis is that! Essex, Earl of England and reputed successor of the Queen in the kingdom — hitherto kept quiet — sent two years ago by the Queen into Ireland with a naval fleet, [and] for the affair not faithfully enough conducted (as he is charged), condemned in Parliament, is a captive and in peril of his life. In your fatherland, the kingdom of Denmark, by the King's care and expense certain fortresses and castles (as they call them) are being built, and the same is being done in Gotland. Hungarian news you will not expect from us — rather, we desire to learn from you what is going on there. These things I wished to reach you, as occasion served.

Farewell, and may it be well with your people too.
Given at Rostock, 21 March, the year 1601.
Most respectful of Your Magnificence,
Christophorus Henniochus, physician.

[Postscript:] The Duke of Brunswick is again cutting off the city's provisioning, and is said to be by no means appeasable; it is known that, commissioners having lately been sent and appointed by the Imperial Majesty, peace was treated of between them, but in vain.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Christophorus Henniochus (Christoph Henioch), a physician (Med. = Medicinae Doctor) at Rostock, already in correspondence with Tycho (cf. Tycho→Henniochus, brahe-t_16000329003). A learned medical/alchemical contact of Tycho's Rostock circle.
  • Significance — a European news-letter (March 1601). The body is a compact survey of European affairs, the kind of manuscript "avviso" exchanged among the learned. Its sweep:
  • Sweden/Lübeck. Carolus … nunc protector Sueciae = Duke Charles (later Charles IX), then Protector/Regent of Sweden (he took the crown only in 1604); his unsettled quarrel with the Hanseatic city of Lübeck.
  • The Polish Diet at Warsaw & the Prussian fief. The Elector of Brandenburg (Joachim Friedrich), backed by envoys of Denmark, of "our prince" (the Duke of Mecklenburg — Rostock's overlord; Henniochus writes "our"), and others, sought the succession to the Feudum Brutenicum (the Ducal Prussian fief, held of the Polish crown) — refused, conditional on aiding Sigismund III Vasa, "King of Poland, now deprived of Sweden," to recover the Swedish throne postliminio (by right of return).
  • The Polish-Swedish war in Livonia. The Poles' declared enemies: Charles of Sweden, who had pushed so far into Livonia that "nothing remains to the Poles there but Riga"; the Grand Duke of Muscovy; and the Voivode of Wallachia.
  • The Low Countries. Archduke Albert of Austria (sovereign of the Spanish Netherlands) preparing to lead an army into Holland, resisted by the Dutch States — "the coming summer will not pass without great tragedies."
  • The false Sebastian of Portugal. King Sebastian, "lost in the African battle" (Alcácer-Quibir, 1578) and "now come back to life" — one of the Sebastianist pretenders — unacknowledged by Spain but favoured by his partisans, sheltering "with the King of France."
  • Essex. Exseccius Comes Angliae = Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex, rumoured successor to Queen Elizabeth I; sent to Ireland (1599), charged with mismanaging the campaign, "condemned in Parliament … in peril of his life."
  • Denmark/Gotland. Christian IV building fortresses in Denmark and Gotland.
  • Hungary. Henniochus expects Tycho (at the imperial court) to be his source for news of the Ottoman–Habsburg "Long War."
  • News-lag (worth noting). By 21 March 1601 (O.S.) Essex had already been executed (25 February 1601); Henniochus reports him only as "a captive in peril of his life" — the news had not yet reached Rostock. A clean illustration of contemporary information-latency, preserved (not "corrected") by this chapter's convention
  • Persons named. Christophorus Henniochus (sender); Nicolaus Maul, "formerly your [Tycho's] servant," the prior courier; the unnamed spagyrist (sphagirus — an alchemical physician) carrying this letter, whom Tycho had wished to engage; Charles of Sweden; the Elector of Brandenburg; Sigismund III of Poland; Archduke Albert; King Sebastian (the pretender); the Earl of Essex; Queen Elizabeth I; Christian IV.
  • Address crux (flagged). Tycho is styled Dominus hereditarius in Knustorpff et Heereswart. Knustorpff = Knudstrup (his hereditary seat); Heereswart is uncertain — most likely the sender's garbling of a second estate-name (possibly Herrevad, where Tycho lived early, though he did not hold it hereditarily). Rendered as written and flagged; not resolved.
  • Lexical flags. muscitatus (of Essex, "hitherto kept quiet/silent") is non-standard — apparently a coinage for "kept mute/inactive"; rendered by sense. The sentence hoc bellum non solum ad Carolum pertinere videtur, nisi temeritatis … vitium attrahat is slightly compressed; read as "this war concerns more than Charles alone — unless he is simply being rash," and flagged.
  • Greek/technical. grammatophorus (Greek, "letter-carrier"); sphagirus (= spagyrus, a Paracelsian alchemical physician) — kept and glossed.
  • Calendar. Dateline Rostochij 21 Martij anni 1601 — Rostock kept the Julian calendar (O.S.). The edition label ("31 marts") disagrees with the dateline and the identifier (16010321001, the 21st); the label is in error and is rejected in favour of the source's own date, by this chapter's convention

- §1 Dreyer collation. News-postscript (Brunswick's renewed blockade + the failed imperial peace mediation), restored from Dreyer VIII, p. 408 (this is the Christophorus Henniochus→Tycho letter; the postscript follows Henniochus's signature).
