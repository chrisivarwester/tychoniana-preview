The original

CLARISSIMO VARIOQUE DOCTRINARUM GENERE ERUDITISSIMO VIRO D. THADDÆO HAGECIO AB HAYK, PHILOSOPHIAe ET MEDICINAe DOCTORI EXIMIO, AMICO MEO IN PRIMIS COLENDO.
S.

SCRIPSI ad te proxime elapso mense Maio, optime et amantissime D. D. THADDÆE, per Studiosum quendam Silesium, qui me cum quibusdam alijs tunc temporis inviserat, unâque librum secundum operis nostri de recentioribus aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis transmisi, quem non dubito te iamdudum unâ cum literis illis accepisse. Quia vero nihil interea responsi a te receperim, et post paucos menses hyems ingruens commoditatem huc scribendi praecludet non intermittendum duxi, quin te hisce brevibus denuo compellarem, ut rescriptionem maturare non intermitteres, cupio autem tuam sententiam cognoscere de ijs, quae liber iste tibi missus continet, utque ingenue eam de singulis, ubi opus videbitur, mihi perscribas, rogo. Feceris mihi hac in parte rem gratam. Non enim mea ita mihi placent, quin aliorum de his libenter sufferam iudicium, quemadmodum ego et de tuis et aliorum quorundam scriptis circa hanc materiam, fretus libertate Philosophica et veritatis inquirendae studio, libere quod sentiam profero, absque tamen ullius laesione mordaci. Primum tomum, qui de Stella illa nova luculenter agit, et tertium, qui de tribus alijs minoribus Cometis postmodum visis e certis observationibus Mathematice rem omnem tractat, spero me per hyemale otium, favente divina Clementia, absoluturum, ut ea omnia ad Colophonem deducta vernalibus Nundinis Francofurti venundentur. Prioris tomi editionem Gallici privilegij defectus, quod Caesareo per te mihi procurato in operis frontispicio adiungere decrevi, hactenus retardavit, prout (ni fallor) in ultimis etiam literis tibi indicavi. Verum cum nunc Gallicanum hoc adeptus sim, et insuper Anglicanum indies expectem, nihil obstabit, quo minus telam inchoatam pertexere omnique ex parte absolvere elaborem. Tu interim intermediae illius partis tibi transmissae penitiorem disquisitionem non intermitte, tuumque de hac iudicium (quod prius etiam expetivi) ante hyemem prima quaque occasione sufficienter et ingenue perscribe. Commodius autem erit Rostochium eas mittere literas ad Doctorem HENRICUM BRUCÆUM, vel in aulam Illustriss. Principis Ducis ULDARICI Megapolitani, ad ipsius Celsis Cancellarium, D. D. IACOBUM BORDINGUM, aut etiam Lubecam ad FRANCISCUM KNOCKERT, Patricium et Senatorem urbis, sive ibidem ad quendam Nauclerum, ALBERT GRUBBE nomine, habitantem in loco appellato Lubecensi Angelo, ut aliquis horum easdem Hafniam ad civem in aedibus meis habitantem, DIDERICUM FULLONEM nomine, transmitti procuret; quacunque enim ratione ex iamdictis (nisi ipsemet commodiorem adeptus fueris) hoc negotium expediveris, spero me literas tuas satis certo et mature obtenturum, quibus ego etiam confestim respondere non intermittam. Vale; plura enim nunc non vacat scribere, et haec saltem obiter ad te perarata Francofurtum transmisi, si forte inde per certum hominem tibi perferantur, qua in parte siquidem aliquid dubij subesse queat, copiosius de alijs rebus nunc scribere non duxi operaepretium. Amplissimum et nobilissimum virum ANDREAM DUDITIUM, si illic in vicinia est, velim meo nomine vel verbis praesens, vel per literas absens officiose salutes. Quod si eius etiam iudicium de libro meo sibi transmisso scriptis mandatum ante hyemem obtinuero, rem mihi praestiteris tam ille quam tu longe acceptissimam.

Iterum felicissime vale.
Datae Uraniburgi die 17. Augusti Anno 88.
Tui amantiss.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned man, most learned in every kind of knowledge, Master Thaddaeus Hagecius of Hájek, distinguished Doctor of Philosophy and of Medicine, my friend to be honoured before all others.
Greeting.

I wrote to you this past May, most excellent and most loving Master Doctor Thaddaeus, by way of a certain Silesian student who at that time had visited me along with certain others, and at the same time I sent the second book of our work on the more recent phenomena of the aethereal World, which I do not doubt you received long since together with that letter. But since in the meantime I have had no reply from you, and within a few months the onset of winter will cut off the convenience of writing hither, I thought I should not fail to address you again with these few lines, that you might not put off hastening a reply; and I desire to learn your opinion of the matters that the book sent to you contains, and I ask that you write it out to me frankly on the several points, where there shall seem to be need. You will herein do me a welcome thing. For my own [findings] do not so please me that I do not gladly bear the judgement of others upon them — just as I too, both upon your writings and upon those of certain others concerning this subject, relying on the liberty of philosophy and on zeal for the inquiry into truth, freely set forth what I think, yet without any biting injury to anyone. The first volume, which treats luminously of that new Star, and the third, which treats the whole matter mathematically, from sure observations, concerning three other lesser Comets seen afterwards, I hope, by the favour of the divine Clemency, to finish during my winter leisure, so that the whole, brought to the colophon, may be put up for sale at the spring Fair at Frankfurt. The publication of the earlier volume has hitherto been delayed by the want of the French privilege, which I have resolved to add on the title-page of the work to the Imperial one procured for me through you — as (unless I am mistaken) I also indicated to you in my last letter. But since I have now obtained this French one, and moreover expect the English one any day, nothing will stand in the way of my labouring to weave out the web I have begun and to finish it in every part. You meanwhile, do not put off a deeper examination of that intermediate part sent to you, and write out your judgement upon it (which I asked for before, too) fully and frankly before winter, at the very first opportunity. It will, however, be more convenient to send those letters to Rostock, to Doctor Henricus Brucaeus; or to the court of the Most Illustrious Prince Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg, to His Highness's Chancellor, Master Doctor Jacobus Bordingus; or also to Lübeck, to Franciscus Knockert, patrician and senator of the city; or, in the same place, to a certain shipmaster by the name of Albert Grubbe, dwelling in the place called the Lübeck Angel — so that one of these may see to it that they are forwarded to Copenhagen to a townsman dwelling in my house, by the name of Diderik Fuller. For in whatever way among those just named you shall dispatch this business (unless you yourself shall have found a more convenient one), I hope to obtain your letter surely enough and in good time, to which I too shall not fail to reply at once. Farewell; for there is no leisure now to write more, and I have sent these lines — penned to you only in passing — to Frankfurt, in case they may be carried thence to you by some sure man; since in that respect some doubt might lie hidden, I have not thought it worthwhile to write now more copiously about other matters. The most ample and most noble man Andreas Dudith, if he be there in the neighbourhood, I should wish you to greet dutifully in my name — in person by word of mouth, or, if absent, by letter. And if I shall obtain his judgement too, set down in writing, upon my book sent to him, before winter, you will have rendered me a thing most welcome by far, both to him and to you [alike].

Again, most happily, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, the 17th day of August, the year [15]88.
Most loving toward you,
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender / programme. Tycho writes from Uraniborg on Hven. The "work on the more recent phenomena of the aethereal World" is his De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis — the three-volume project of which, in this letter: the second book (liber secundus / tomus / intermedia pars) is the volume on the comet of 1577, already printed at Hven and being circulated (sent to Hagecius the previous May); the first volume (primum tomum, qui de Stella illa nova … agit) is the planned treatise on the new Star of 1572 — eventually the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (completed posthumously, 1602); and the third (tertium … de tribus alijs minoribus Cometis) the projected treatment of three lesser comets. Both first and third Tycho still hopes to "finish during my winter leisure" and sell at the next Frankfurt spring fair — an expectation not in fact met.
  • Recipient. Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayk = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius / Hagecius) (1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer, and imperial counsellor at Prague; one of Tycho's closest scholarly correspondents and his chief broker for imperial favour. His own Apodixis physica et mathematica de cometis (1581) is part of the comet literature in view here.
  • Persons named as forwarding agents.
  • Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (1530–1593), professor of mathematics and medicine at Rostock, a regular intermediary in Tycho's correspondence.
  • Illustriss. Princeps Dux Uldaricus Megapolitanus = Ulrich, Duke of Mecklenburg (1527–1603); Megapolitanus is the Latin for Mecklenburg. eius Celsis = "His Highness's" (genitive of Celsitudo, this chapter's convention).
  • Iacobus Bordingus = Jakob Bording (the younger), the Duke's chancellor.
  • Franciscus Knockert — Franz Knokert (Knokart), named "patrician and senator" (city councillor) of Lübeck.
  • Albert Grubbe — a Lübeck shipmaster (nauclerus), "dwelling in the place called the Lübeck Angel" (in loco appellato Lubecensi Angelo) — apparently an inn or house sign, "the Angel," at Lübeck; kept as named, no firmer identification asserted.
  • Didericus Fullonem (acc.) = Diderik Fuller (Diderik Fullo), a townsman lodging in Tycho's house [at Copenhagen], the final relay point for letters reaching Hven.
  • Andreas Duditius = Andreas Dudith (András Dudith / Dudithius) (1533–1589), the Hungarian humanist, diplomat, and former bishop, then living at Breslau (Wrocław) in Silesia near Hagecius's orbit; author of De cometarum significatione, hence Tycho's wish for his iudicium on the comet book. (Dudith in fact died the following February.)
  • Place / sign. Hafnia = Copenhagen; Rostochium = Rostock; Lubeca = Lübeck; Francofurtum = Frankfurt (am Main), site of the great book fair (Nundinae Francofurtenses).
  • Technical / bibliographic terms. Caesareo … privilegio / Gallicum / Anglicanum — the Imperial, French, and English printing privileges Tycho was assembling for the work; frontispicium = the title-page (here the place where the privileges would be announced), not a building front. ad Colophonem deducta — "brought to the colophon," i.e. carried through to completion of printing. vernalibus Nundinis Francofurti — the spring Frankfurt [book] fair. telam inchoatam pertexere — "to weave out the web begun," a classical metaphor (Cicero) for finishing an undertaking; kept as metaphor.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading abbreviations D. D. = Domine Doctor ("Master Doctor"); D. (Dominus) = "Master/Lord". Closing Iterum … vale → "Again … farewell"; Tui amantiss[imus] → "Most loving toward you"; manu propria → "in his own hand" (an autograph subscription).
  • Hedges preserved. ni fallor ("unless I am mistaken"), non dubito … te accepisse ("I do not doubt you received"), si … in vicinia est ("if he be there in the neighbourhood"), si forte … perferantur ("in case they may be carried"), and siquidem aliquid dubij subesse queat ("since some doubt might lie hidden") are all rendered with their qualifiers intact, by the audit's rule (no silent sharpening).
  • Calendar. Dateline die 17. Augusti Anno 88 = 17 August 1588, Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The edition label ("1588. 17. august. Uraniborg") agrees; no discrepancy. No Roman-calendar idiom in the dateline.
  • Greek / symbols. None in this letter.
  • Correction. Restored the standalone salutation 'S.' (dropped by the working transcription) between the address block and 'SCRIPSI', per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.122; added the corresponding English 'Greeting.' line.
