The original

NOBILISSIMO ET ERUDITISSIMO VIRO DOMINO OLIGERO ROSENKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGUINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO INTIMO, MIHIQUE CHARISSIMO.

S. P.

CHARISSIME OLIGERE, Amice, Consangvinee et Affinis integerrime, de statu mearum rerum cognoscendo te esse sollicitum, id sic suadente eo, quo me prosequeris amore, et ego vicissim te, non dubito. Sic itaque brevibus habe: Wandesburgo statim a Michaëlis festo discedens Pragam versus ad Caesarem iturus, Magdeburgi primum per octiduum ob Ducem Brunsvicensem, tum quoque ERICUM LANGIUM substiti, postea Dresdae per mensem integrum, quo responsum a Caesare, an illis temporibus, cum Praga abesset ob luem Epidemicam, accedendus et compellandus foret, expectarem; quale vero tandem receperim, ex altera pagina cognosces. Caetera exemplum literarum ad KRAGIUM nostrum, quod adiunxi (neque enim nunc vacat eadem ad te repetere) edocebit. Ingressurus est hac ipsa hora filius meus TYCHO cum quibusdam comitibus iter Wandesburgum, ut familiam meam muliebrem huc meis curribus adducat; constitui enim, favente Deo, hîc Wittebergae hyemare, aut saltem tam diu manere, donec denuo a Caesare in Bohemiam vocer. Plura non addo, quia pluribus otium deest. Tu vero, mi OLIGERE, copiosius rescribe, et de singulis, quae scire mea interesse nosti, affatim instrue, meque saepius, interea dum hîc sum, tuis literis invise. Invenies me ad par pari referendum minime segnem. Habebis brevi plura carmina ad Caesarem de me, tum quoque ad me a varijs Poëtis concinnata, quae nunc describere non licuit. Tu interim, ut Dania Germaniae respondeat, effice, pro ut in te sponte recepisti, si modo Iutorum sit pollicitis aliquando stare. Si ego Schanus tale quid addixissem, certe fidem meam dudum redemissem, utut fuerim non minus quam tu occupatissimus. Sed si adhuc promissis steteris, erit veniae locus. Cum HUNNIO hîc necdum familiaritatem contraxi, pridie enim primum huc veni, sed fiet id facile in posterum, uti etiam cum reliquis huius Academiae Eruditis. POLYCARPUM decumbentem, cum Dresdae essem, bis invisi, humanissime ab illo exceptus; is te salutari voluit. Salutem etiam tibi dicit Hospes meus Dnus Doctor IOHANNES IESSENIUS, qui mihi in Philippaeis olim aedibus commodam habitationem pro me et meis lubenter et benevole concessit. Vale, et brevi plura a me expecta. Sed da operam, ut praevenias. Saluta coniugem SOPHIAM Neptem meam, utque alteri SOPHIAe praevaleat, indulge.

Dabantur properanter Wittebergae die 5. Decembris Anni 1598.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 126; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
POSTSCRIPTA. IMPURUM istud et calumnijs scatens Dithmarsi istius scriptum, quod tibi inspiciendum misi, necdum recepi, utut in literis ultimis, quas, cum Wandesburgi essem, accepi, indicaveris te illud mihi remisisse; fac igitur, ut mature habeam, neque enim eo carere possum propter illa, quae in margine adscripta erant, ut inde dem occasionem alteri cuipiam ista maledicta in suum authorem retorquendi; sed luet is brevi quae meritus est. Retulit de eo ad me Doct. IOHANNES GŒDELMANNUS, Consiliarius Saxonicus et CHYTRÆI Gener, quem proculdubio nosti, quod URSUS iste plane contemptus sit et despectui habitus ab omnibus cordatis viris et ijs, qui in aula sunt Caesaris, postquam tam insulsum et temerarium scriptum publicavit, esseque ab ipso Caesare neglectum et vili pensum.

To the most noble and most learned man, Lord Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, kinsman by blood, kinsman by marriage, and most intimate friend, and most dear to me.

Heartiest greeting.

Dearest Holger — friend, kinsman by blood, and most upright kinsman by marriage — that you are anxious to learn the state of my affairs, the love with which you attend me (and I you in turn) so persuading, I do not doubt. So have it thus, in brief: leaving Wandsbek right after the feast of Michael, to go toward Prague to the Emperor, I halted first at Magdeburg for eight days, on account of the Duke of Brunswick, and also of Erik Lange; afterward at Dresden for a whole month, there to await a reply from the Emperor — whether at that time, since he was away from Prague because of the epidemic plague, he was to be approached and addressed. What [reply] I finally received, you will learn from the other page. The rest, the copy of the letter to our Krag, which I have appended (for there is no time now to repeat the same to you), will teach you. This very hour my son Tycho is to set out on the journey to Wandsbek, with some companions, to bring my womenfolk hither with my wagons; for I have resolved, God favouring, to winter here at Wittenberg, or at least to remain so long until I am called again by the Emperor into Bohemia. I add no more, because for more there is no leisure. But do you, my Holger, write back more fully, and instruct me amply on the particulars which you know it concerns me to know, and visit me the more often with your letters while I am here. You will find me by no means slow to render like for like. You will shortly have several poems to the Emperor about me, and also to me, composed by various Poets, which I could not now transcribe. You, meanwhile, see to it that Denmark answers Germany [in correspondence], as you spontaneously undertook — if only it be in Jutlanders to stand by their promises sometime. Had I, a Scanian, pledged such a thing, I should certainly have redeemed my word long ago, however much I have been no less busy than you. But if you will yet have kept your promises, there will be room for pardon. With Hunnius I have not yet struck up acquaintance here, for I came hither only yesterday; but it will easily happen hereafter, as also with the rest of the learned of this Academy. Polycarp, lying ill, I visited twice while I was at Dresden, and was most kindly received by him; he wished to be remembered to you. Greeting to you, too, is sent by my host, Lord Doctor Johannes Jessenius, who has freely and kindly granted me convenient lodging, for myself and mine, in the house that was once Philip's. Farewell, and shortly expect more from me — but take pains to forestall me. Greet your wife Sophie, my niece, and indulge her, that she may outdo the other Sophie.

Given hastily at Wittenberg, the 5th day of December, in the year 1598.
Your Tycho Brahe.

[Postscript:] POSTSCRIPT. That impure writing of the Dithmarsian, teeming with calumnies, which I sent you to inspect, I have not yet received back — although in your last letter, which I received while I was at Wandsbek, you indicated that you had sent it back to me; see to it, then, that I have it in good time, for I cannot do without it on account of those things which were noted in the margin, that I may thence give occasion to some other man of turning those slanders back upon their author; but he shall shortly pay what he has deserved. Doctor Johann Gödelmann, Saxon Councillor and Chytraeus's son-in-law — whom you doubtless know — reported to me concerning him, that this Ursus is plainly despised and held in contempt by all sensible men, and by those who are at the Emperor's court, after he published so insipid and rash a writing, and that he is neglected and lightly esteemed by the Emperor himself.

Apparatus

  • Sender & moment. Tycho Brahe at Wittenberg, December 1598, having just arrived ("only yesterday") — a chatty progress-report to his closest Danish kinsman during the slow approach to imperial service. It pairs with brahe-t_15980905001 (Wandsbek, Sept 1598) as the two main Tycho→Holger letters of the migration.
  • Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" of Rosenholm — consanguineus, affinis et amicus intimus (kinsman by blood and marriage; cf. brahe-t_15980905001).
  • The itinerary. From Wandsbek, just after Michaelmas (29 Sept), toward Prague; halted a week at Magdeburg (on account of the Duke of Brunswick and Erik Lange), then a whole month at Dresden, awaiting the Emperor's word on whether to approach him (Rudolf II being absent from Prague ob luem epidemicam — because of the plague). Tycho now resolves to winter at Wittenberg until summoned into Bohemia; his son is being sent back to Wandsbek to fetch the womenfolk and wagons.
  • Persons named.
  • Dux Brunsvicensis = the Duke of Brunswick (Heinrich Julius of Brunswick-Wolfenbüttel).
  • Ericus Langius = Erik Lange (d. 1613), Danish nobleman and alchemist, Tycho's friend and prospective brother-in-law (betrothed to Tycho's sister Sophie Brahe) — chronically in debt and abroad.
  • Kragius noster = Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius) (c. 1550–1602), Danish humanist, historian and diplomat — recipient of a parallel letter Tycho encloses in copy.
  • filius meus Tycho = Tycho Brahe the Younger, sent to fetch the household.
  • Hunnius = Aegidius Hunnius (1550–1603), the leading Wittenberg Lutheran theologian, not yet met.
  • Polycarpus = Polycarp Leyser (Leiser) the Elder (1552–1610), Saxon court preacher, visited (ill) at Dresden.
  • Dnus Doctor Iohannes Iessenius = Johannes Jessenius, Tycho's host at Wittenberg, who lodged him in Philippaeis aedibus — "in the house that was once Philip's," i.e. Melanchthon's former house (Philippus = Philipp Melanchthon) — a resonant detail (cf. Jessenius across the 1600 letters).
  • coniunx Sophia, neptis mea = Sophie Brahe, Holger's wife and Tycho's niece; altera Sophia = the other Sophie — Tycho's learned sister Sophie Brahe (the wordplay: may the niece "outdo" the aunt).
  • The Scanian/Jutlander jest. si modo Iutorum sit pollicitis … stare … Si ego Schanus … — Tycho teases Holger (a Jutland landowner) about Jutlanders keeping promises, contrasting himself as a Scanian (Schanus, of Skåne); rendered straight as the friendly regional jab it is.
  • Witness note. Survives as an afskrift (copy) in Langebek's Diplomatarium, Copenhagen.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = salutem plurimam; Dabantur properanter → "Given hastily"; Vale → "Farewell"; ad par pari referendum → "to render like for like."
  • Calendar. Dateline die 5. Decembris Anni 1598 at Wittenberg; no style marker. Lutheran Saxony was on the Julian (Old Style) calendar; reproduced, not converted.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (the unreturned Ursus book + Gödelmann's report that Ursus is despised at court), restored from Dreyer VIII p.126 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. English corrected from 'a week' to 'eight days', matching 'per octiduum' (Latin and Dreyer agree; only the translation was at fault), per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII p.125.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (low). 'octiduum' denotes a specific eight-day period, not 'a week' (seven days); source and Dreyer agree, but the English rounds the number down. At: “I halted first at Magdeburg for a week”.

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO VIII p.125 (page image). The Latin 'octiduum' is unambiguous and undisputed between our source and Dreyer; only the English rounding was corrected. No other part of the letter was touched.
