The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MELCHIOREM JŒSTELIUM..

VENIT ad me Dresdâ hisce diebus M. AMBROSIUS RHODIUS, et nonnulla, quæ circa Hypotheses tentasti, clarissime et præstantissime D. D. IŒSTELI, secum a te attulit. Utrumque mihi gratum fuit. Faciam, quantum ad illum attinet, prout mones. Hypotheses autem cuperem accuratius enodari, et magis perspicue explicari, ita ut nihil desit aut sit superfluum, atque ordine debito sese omnia insequantur. Neque quæstio est, ut loca Planetarum, per Hypothesin quamcunque inventa, cum meis observatis conferantur, quibus nulla ratio hactenus præsupposita consentiet, sed sufficit, ut ea, quæ secundum PTOLEMÆUM deducuntur, calculo respondeant Alphonsino, ita tamen ut Apogæa, Eccentricitates et dimensiones orbium adhibeantur, prout Alphonsina fert methodus, a PTOLEMÆO potissima ex parte derivata, nisi quod Apogæa hisce temporibus aliter applicantur. Quæ vero iuxta COPERNICUM fiunt, ea oportet ipsius pariter datis et assumptionibus, in ijs quæ modo dixi, inniti, et calculo Prutenico convenire, idque sive iuxta ipsius speculationem Solem ad centrum Universi quiescentem, in annuo orbe, eius loco revoluta Terra, statuemus, sive immutata hac, uti ego ostendi, Hypothesi, Terra ibidem immota permanente, et assumptis Eccentroepicyclis more Ptolemaico, cum circello circa centrum, loco æquantis, uti recte imitatus es. Sed hæc non est proprie inversio, sed potius permutatio quædam et cum Ptolemaicis hypothesium COPERNICI conciliatio. Nostra autem innovatio, quæ Epicyclos non minus quam ille negligit, Terra nihilominus immota permanente, etiam aliud adhuc particulare requirit, ut apparentijs satisfiat, quam a te iuxta COPERNICI proportiones factum est. Ostendunt siquidem apparentiæ, Planetarum circuitus proprios non medium, sed verum, adeoque ipsum corpus Solis, respicere, ita ut Martiæ stellæ, quæ præcipuam hîc parit differentiam, phænomena, etiam extra Acronychios situs, accurate et multipliciter considerata, nos ad ipsius Solis Apogæum iuxta initia ♋cri manuducant, ut de Eccentricitate nunc nihil dicam. Recte tamen a te factum est, quod nostram hypothesin Coperniceæ collatam, et assumptis ijsdem dedomenis, eundem præbere sideris locum, quem eius exigit numeratio, demonstrâsti. De his vero per oportunius ocium deliberatius cogita, et quæ vel hactenus perfectius elaborata habes, quam ea sunt, quæ misisti, vel inposterum elaborare dabitur, mecum communica.

Cœterum de Typographo LEMANNO sic habe. Precium quod annotavit pro Balla papyri 15 fl. nimium est, venditur enim hîc, ubi omnia longe cariora sunt quam Lipsiæ aut Witebergæ, una eiuscemodi Balla 8 Ioachimicis. Et cum Witebergæ essem, potui eam a cartopœis pro decem fl. si recte memini, obtinere. Verum quantum ad mercedem attinet, quam pro labore suo Typographico in imprimenda una Balla postulat, facile inter nos conveniret, si papyrum æquiore precio adhibuerit, ita ut decem fl. vel ad summum totidem Ioachimicis (quod tamen pluris est, quam hîc una Balla venit) comparetur. Qua de re ut cum ipso diligenter meo nomine agas, te peramanter rogatum habeo, utque promptius intelligat, hanc esse meam sententiam, idipsum Germanicis verbis contignatum illique tradendum, uti voluisti, adiungo. Exemplar autem, quod ea continebit, quæ de Luna in 6 illis quaternionibus excudenda erunt, quam primum ad mundum (in quo nunc laboratur) descriptum fuerit, mittam, modo prius de voluntate Typographi certior a te factus fuero, quod ut mature facias, etiam atque etiam peto. Vale et saluta amicos Witebergenses, quibus scribere nunc non vacat, sed fiet id, volente Deo, per oportunius otium, iterumque vale.

Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum 18 Novembris Anno 1600.

TYCHO BRAHE TO MELCHIOR JÖSTEL.

Master Ambrosius Rhodius came to me from Dresden these days, and brought with him from you, most renowned and excellent Lord Doctor Jöstel, some of what you have attempted concerning the Hypotheses. Both were welcome to me. As far as concerns him [Rhodius], I will do as you advise. But the Hypotheses I should wish to be more accurately unfolded and more perspicuously explained, so that nothing be lacking or superfluous, and everything follow in due order. Nor is the question that the places of the Planets, found by any Hypothesis whatever, be compared with my observations — with which no method hitherto presupposed will agree — but it suffices that what is deduced according to Ptolemy answer to the Alphonsine calculation, yet so that the Apogees, Eccentricities, and dimensions of the orbs be employed as the Alphonsine method bears (derived for the most part from Ptolemy, except that the Apogees are differently applied in these times). But what is done according to Copernicus must likewise rest on his own data and assumptions, in the things I have just mentioned, and agree with the Prutenic calculation — and that, whether we set the Sun at rest at the centre of the Universe according to his speculation, the Earth being revolved in its place in an annual orb; or whether, this Hypothesis being changed (as I have shown), the Earth remaining there immobile, and eccentric-epicycles being assumed in the Ptolemaic manner, with a little circle about the centre in place of the equant — as you have rightly imitated. But this is not properly an inversion, but rather a certain permutation, and a reconciliation of the Copernican hypotheses with the Ptolemaic. Our own innovation, however — which neglects the Epicycles no less than he does, the Earth nonetheless remaining immobile — requires yet one further particular thing, that the appearances be satisfied, beyond what has been done by you according to Copernicus's proportions. For the appearances show that the planets' own circuits respect not the mean, but the true [Sun] — indeed the very body of the Sun — so that the phenomena of the star Mars (which here produces the chief difference), even outside the acronychal [opposition] positions, accurately and manifoldly considered, lead us by the hand to the Apogee of the Sun itself, near the beginning of Cancer ♋ — to say nothing now of the Eccentricity. Yet rightly was it done by you, that you demonstrated that our hypothesis, compared with the Copernican — the same data being assumed — yields the same place of the star which its computation requires. But about these things think more deliberately at a more convenient leisure, and share with me what you have hitherto elaborated more perfectly than what you sent, or what it shall hereafter be granted you to elaborate.

For the rest, concerning the printer Lehmann, take it thus. The price he noted for a bale of paper — 15 florins — is too much; for here, where everything is far dearer than at Leipzig or Wittenberg, one such bale sells for 8 joachimsthalers; and when I was at Wittenberg I could get it from the paper-makers for ten florins, if I rightly recall. But as for the fee he demands for his typographic labour in printing one bale, we would easily agree, if he employed paper at a fairer price — say, procured for ten florins, or at most as many joachimsthalers (which is yet more than one bale sells for here). Wherefore I lovingly ask you to deal diligently with him in my name; and, that he may the more readily understand this to be my view, I add the same framed in German words, to be handed to him, as you wished. The copy containing what is to be printed concerning the Moon in those 6 quaternions I will send as soon as it has been fair-copied (on which work now proceeds) — provided I have first been informed by you of the printer's willingness, which I ask again and again that you do in good time. Farewell, and greet the Wittenberg friends, to whom there is now no leisure to write, but it shall be done, God willing, at a more convenient leisure; and again, farewell.

Given at Prague of the Bohemians, 18 November, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient & context. Melchior Jöstel, Tycho's Wittenberg computational collaborator — this is the follow-up to brahe-t_16000327001 (27 March 1600), where Tycho had set Jöstel the planetary-hypotheses task for the second Book of the Epistolae. Jöstel's results were carried from Dresden by Master Ambrosius Rhodius (Ambrosius Rhode, 1577–1633, mathematician, later professor at Wittenberg).
  • Significance — a cornerstone statement of the Tychonic planetary theory. One of the clearest concise expositions of Tycho's system and its computational reconciliation with its rivals:
  • The reconciliation programme. The hypotheses are not to be tested against Tycho's own observations ("with which no method hitherto presupposed will agree"), but made to agree (a) with the Alphonsine calculation on the Ptolemaic side — using the apogees, eccentricities, and orb-dimensions the Alphonsine method (itself "derived for the most part from Ptolemy") supplies — and (b) with the Prutenic calculation on the Copernican side.
  • The Tychonic transformation. Either set the Sun at rest at the centre with the Earth in an annual orb (Copernicus), or — "this Hypothesis being changed, as I have shown" — keep the Earth immobile and use eccentric-epicycles in the Ptolemaic manner, with a little circle (circellus) about the centre in place of the equant. Tycho insists this is "not properly an inversion, but a permutation and a reconciliation of the Copernican hypotheses with the Ptolemaic."
  • The distinctive feature — orbits about the true Sun, proved from Mars. "Our innovation … neglects the Epicycles no less than Copernicus … the Earth nonetheless remaining immobile," but "requires one further particular thing": the appearances show the planets' circuits respect not the mean but the true Sun — the very body of the Sun. The decisive evidence is Mars ("which produces the chief difference here"): its phenomena, even outside the acronychal (opposition) positions, "lead us by the hand to the Apogee of the Sun itself, near the beginning of Cancer." (This — that planetary orbits are referred to the true Sun, and Mars as the critical case — is precisely the line later so consequential for Kepler.)
  • Jöstel's correct result: he had demonstrated that the Tychonic and Copernican hypotheses, on the same data, yield the same planetary place — confirming their geometric equivalence.
  • Technical terms (kept;). Hypotheses (the planetary models); Apogaeum → apogee; Eccentricitas → eccentricity; Eccentroepicyclus → eccentric-epicycle; aequans → equant; circellus → little circle; Acronychus situs → acronychal position (opposition to the Sun); medium / verum Solem → mean / true Sun; calculus Alphonsinus / Prutenicus → the Alphonsine / Prutenic Tables; ♋ = Cancer (glyph kept in source). dedomena (Greek δεδομένα, "givens/data") rendered "data."
  • The printing business. The printer Lehmann (Lemannus, a Leipzig/Wittenberg printer) quotes 15 florins per bale of paper — too much (a bale sells for 8 joachimsthalers at Prague, 10 fl. at Wittenberg); Tycho will agree the printing fee if paper is reckoned fairly, and encloses his terms "in German" for Jöstel to hand over. He will send the fair-copy of the 6 lunar quaternions (the lunar-theory text; cf. brahe-t_15981231001, brahe-t_16000327001) once the printer's willingness is confirmed.
  • Persons named. Melchior Jöstel; Ambrosius Rhodius; Ptolemy, Copernicus (and the Alphonsine/Prutenic tables); the printer Lehmann; the Wittenberg friends.
  • Calendar. Dateline Pragae Bohemorum 18 Novembris Anno 1600; New Style (Prague). The edition label gives only the place ("Prague"); date supplied from dateline/identifier.
