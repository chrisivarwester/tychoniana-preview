The original

DEM DURCHLEUCHTIGEN/ HOCHGEBORNEN FÜRSTEN VND HERRN/ HERRN MORITZEN, LANDTGRAFFEN ZU HESSEN/ GRAFFEN ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ DIETZ/ ZIEGENHAIN VND NIDDA: MEINEM GNEDIGEN HERRN.

DVRCHLEUCHTIGER/ Hochgeborner/ Gnediger Fürst vnd Herr/ Nechst erbietung meiner vntherthenigen willigen Dienste/ sol Ew. F. G. ich vnterthenigklich nicht vorhalten/ Wie dan ohne das sonder zweiffel E. F. G. gnedige wissenschafft haben werden/ Welcher gestalt der auch Durchleuchtiger Hochgeborner Fürst vnd Herr/ Her WILHELM, Landtgraff zu Hessen/ Graff zu Catzenelnbogen/ Dietz/ Ziegenhain vnd Nidda/ Hochloblicher gedachtnus/ zu vnterschiedlichen mahlen vnter anderen/ etlicher Ellende halben aus Norwegen seiner F. G. zum besten zu bestellen ghar fleissigk an mich geschrieben. Ob ich nuhn wol mir zum höchsten angelegen sein lassen/ Das seiner F. G. ich darinne meinen vnterthenigen geflissenen Willen im Werck bezeigen mochte/ auch etliche deroselbigen Thiere zu wege gebracht; So habe ich doch das Glück nicht haben mügen/ das dieselbigen lebendich an seine F. G. angebracht wurden. Als er aber endtlich Zeigern dieses an mich gnedichlich abgefertigt/ mit befellich/ er solte nitt zu rücke kommen/ ehe er deroselben Thiere mit sich brächte/ hab ich denselbigen Botten von hinnen ab in Norwegen abgefertiget/ vnd allen müglichen fleiss angewendet/ das mihr fünff sülcher Ellende geschafft würden/ Wie mir von Kuniglicher Majett. in Norwegen Stadthalteren/ meinem Blutsverwanten AXEL GVLDENSTERN zugesagt vnd gehalten ist worden. Von denselbigen fünffen/ die er da wol zu passe bekommen/ Hat er mir Zwei/ ein Weiblein vnd ein Menlein lebendig alhie angebracht. In mittelst aber ist alhie die trawrige Zeittunge von Hochgemelter Ihrer F. G. tödtlichen Abgang aus dieser Welt erschollen. Darob ich nit allein (weil er mir alzeit in Gnaden gewogen gewesen) sondern auch alle fromme Hertzen hohes vnd nidriges standes/ billich ein betrüptes mitleiden gehabt. Vnd were zwar wol zu wünschen gewesen/ da es dem Allerhöchsten also gefallen/ das sein F. G. noch ein lange zeitt der Cristlichen Kirchen/ auch sönst dem gantzen Vatterlandt Teutscher Nation zum trost vnd besten/ leben hette mügen. Wan aber seiner Gödtlichen Allmacht in dero weisen gericht nit zu greiffen/ so muß man auch hierinne sich desselbigen vnwandelbaren willen gehorsamlich vntergeben. Der getrewe Gott wolle seine Rutte/ vnd woluerdiente straffe von vns gnedichlich abwenden/ vnd hohe Hâupter vnd Fürsten/ so noch vbrich vnd lebendig geblieben/ erhalten/ Damit seine Kirche vnd Glidtmassen bei denselbigen dürch gnedige Göttliche hülffe/ Rhat/ Trost/ Schütz vnd Schirm haben mügen. Weil dan Gnediger Fürst vnd Herr/ ich es billich dafür halte/ das Hochgemeltes Ew. F. G. löblichen Herrn Vattern befellich zu folge/ Dennoch diese Thiere/ so vbrig/ vnd lebendig geblieben/ an bestimpten Ordt/ vnd an statt seiner löblichen F. G. itzo E. F. G. zugefertiget werden/ Also thue deroselbigen ich sie hiemit bei dero eignen Botten vnthertenigk vbersenden/ Mit vntertheniger Bitt/ E. F. G. wolle sölches nit anders als aus vnterthenigen geneigten willen gescheen/ gnedichlich vermercken/ Nicht weiniger auch/ als dero Hochlöbliger Herr Vatter gethon/ mihr in Gnaden gewogen sein. Könte ich zu alter zeitt vnd gelegenheit E. F. G. hinwiederumb vnthertenige dienste/ worinne es sein wolle/ erzeigen/ solten dieselbigen meinen geneigten vnd getrewen willen in Werck gnedichligk zu erspüren haben/ die ich hiemit im gnedigen schutz des Allerhöchsten bei guter Seel vnd Leibes Gesundtheidt/ vnd angehender heilsamer Regierung (zu welcher Ew. F. G. ich auch hiemit von Gott dem Allmechtigen Glück vnd Segen wünsche) lange Zeitt zu erhalten getrewligk vnd vnterthenigk thu befhelen.

Datum Vraniburgk auff Huen/ den 22. Decembris, Anno 1592.
Ewer F. G.
Dienstwilliger
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Serene, High-born Prince and Lord, Lord Moritz, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda: my gracious Lord.

Most Serene, High-born, Gracious Prince and Lord: next after the tender of my humble, willing service, I should not humbly withhold from Your Princely Grace — as indeed Your Princely Grace will without doubt otherwise have gracious knowledge — in what manner the likewise Most Serene, High-born Prince and Lord, Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda, of most laudable memory, did at sundry times among other matters write to me very diligently, that I should procure for his Princely Grace's benefit certain elk [Ellende] out of Norway. Now although I let it be of the highest concern to me that I might show his Princely Grace my humble, sedulous goodwill in this matter in deed, and also brought several of those animals to pass, yet I could not have the good fortune that the same were delivered alive to his Princely Grace. But when he at length graciously dispatched to me the bearer of this [letter], with command that he should not come back before he brought those animals with him, I dispatched that same messenger from here onward into Norway, and applied all possible diligence that five such elk be procured for me — as was promised to me, and kept, by His Royal Majesty's Governor in Norway, my blood-kinsman Axel Gyldenstern. Of those same five, which he there obtained in good condition, he has delivered to me here two alive, a female and a male. In the meantime, however, the sorrowful tidings have rung out here of the aforesaid His Princely Grace's mortal departure out of this world. Whereat not only I (since he was always graciously well-disposed toward me), but also all godly hearts of high and low estate, have justly had a grieved compassion. And it were indeed greatly to be wished — had it so pleased the Most High — that his Princely Grace might have lived yet a long time, to the comfort and benefit of the Christian Church, and likewise of the whole fatherland of the German nation. But since His Divine Omnipotence in its wise judgment is not to be grasped, so must one in this too submit obediently to that same unalterable will. May the faithful God graciously turn away His rod, and well-deserved punishment, from us, and preserve the high heads and princes who yet remain over and living, so that His Church and her members may have through them — by gracious divine help — counsel, comfort, protection, and shelter. Since then, Gracious Prince and Lord, I justly hold it fitting that, in accordance with the aforesaid command of Your Princely Grace's laudable Lord Father, these animals which have remained over and alive be nonetheless now consigned to the appointed place, and, in the stead of his laudable Princely Grace, to Your Princely Grace, accordingly I herewith humbly send them to the same by Your own messenger; with humble request that Your Princely Grace would graciously take note that this is done from none other than a humble, well-affected will, [and] would no less than your most laudable Lord Father did be graciously well-disposed toward me. Could I at any time and occasion show Your Princely Grace humble service in return, in whatever it might be, the same should graciously find my well-affected and faithful will [made good] in deed; [and] I herewith faithfully and humbly commend Your Princely Grace [to God], to be preserved a long time in the gracious protection of the Most High, in good health of soul and body, and in a wholesome government now begun (to which I herewith likewise wish Your Princely Grace fortune and blessing from God the Almighty).

Given at Uraniborg on Hven, the 22nd of December, in the year 1592.
Your Princely Grace's
ready in service,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg, his observatory-house on Hven.
  • Recipient. Moritz, Landtgraff zu Hessen = Moritz ("the Learned"), Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632), who had just succeeded his father. The full titulature Graff zu Catzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain vnd Nidda (Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda) is the standing Hesse-Kassel style and is preserved by this chapter's convention; Katzenelnbogen is left in its period spelling Catzenelnbogen in the source.
  • Her WILHELM, … Hochloblicher gedachtnus = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), Moritz's father and Tycho's long-standing scientific correspondent. The phrase Hochloblicher gedachtnus ("of most laudable memory") marks him as recently deceased: Wilhelm died 25 August 1592 (O.S.), which the letter reports as the "sorrowful tidings" that arrived while the elk were being procured. Thus the gift, originally requested by the father, is here redirected to the son.
  • AXEL GVLDENSTERN = Axel Gyldenstierne (1542–1603), Royal Governor (Stadthalter / statholder) of Norway 1588–1601, here named as Tycho's Blutsverwanter ("blood-kinsman" — the Brahe and Gyldenstierne families were related by marriage and blood among the Danish high nobility). He supplied the elk from Norway.
  • Kuniglicher Majett. = "His Royal Majesty" — Christian IV of Denmark-Norway (a minor until 1596; Gyldenstierne governed Norway in the crown's name).
  • Subject term: Ellende / Ellende (sg./pl.). Early New High German Ell(en)d = the elk (European elk / moose, Alces alces; modern German Elch), a prized Scandinavian game animal sought by German princes for their menageries and parks. Rendered "elk" throughout; "those animals" (deroselbigen / dieselbigen Thiere) refers to the same. The letter records that of five elk obtained in Norway, two reached Hven alive — ein Weiblein vnd ein Menlein, "a female and a male" (i.e. a cow and a bull elk).
  • Negation check. The two load-bearing negatives are reproduced exactly: sol … ich vnterthenigklich nicht vorhalten ("I should not withhold" — a litotes of disclosure, i.e. "I make known"), and the courier's commission er solte nitt zu rücke kommen/ ehe er … brächte ("he was not to come back before he brought [them]"). No negation has been added or dropped. The litotes is kept literal ("should not withhold") rather than smoothed to "I hereby report."
  • Chancery register. The whole letter is in the deferential subject's register to a prince: Ew./E. F. G. = Euer/Eure Fürstliche Gnaden, "Your Princely Grace"; vntherthenig(lich) "humble / humbly"; gnedichlich "graciously." Spelling is Tycho's own Saxon-inflected chancery German as set by his Uraniborg/printed Epistolae: v/u for u/v (vnd, vntherthenig), -tz/-ck, doubled vowels (Hâupter, Zeittunge), mügen for mögen, dürch, sülcher. The circumflex in Hâupter is reproduced as printed.
  • Edition of record. Printed by Tycho himself in Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg/Wandsbek, 1596), the volume from which the edition's text descends; in Dreyer's Opera Omnia the Hesse-Kassel correspondence is in vol. VI. No manuscript witness is recorded in the edition.
  • Calendar. Dateline den 22. Decembris, Anno 1592 — given in the running civil style, no Roman-calendar idiom. Denmark used the Julian (Old Style) calendar in 1592 (and until 1700); the date is therefore O.S. Hesse-Kassel (Protestant) was likewise still Old Style at this date. No in-letter date discrepancy; the catalogue label ("1592. 22. december") agrees with the dateline.
  • Place. Vraniburgk auff Huen = Uraniborg on Hven (the island in the Øresund granted to Tycho by Frederik II in 1576).
