The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MAURITIUM LANDTGRAVIUM HASSIÆ.

CUM Illustrissimæ Celsitudinis Vestræ Legatus Magnificus, Dominus IOHANNES de Bopenhaussen, qui magni Regis Persarum Legationem huc, Celnis Tuæ iussu, deduxit, mihi (siquidem interea dum hîc fuit, nonnulla ex mutuo aliquoties congressu atque colloquio nobis intercessit familiaritas) indicâsset, se reditum in Hassiam ad Celnem Vm maturare, mei officij esse duxi Illmam Cm Vm hisce meis literis demisse salutare, et quæcunque ex mea tenuitate proficisci poterint obsequia, omni debitâ reverentiâ offerre. Auxit hanc scribendi confidentiam, et idipsum ut non omitterem, persuasit, non saltem ea, qua Laudatissimæ memoriæ Vræ Celnis Dominus Parens, quoad vixit, me Clementissime prosecutus est benevolentia, sed et insuper Vræ ipsius Celnis benignissimus animus et perseverans erga me favor, ut vel inde liquet, quod Celdo Vra etiam post obitum Parentis suis Clementissimis et cultissimis literis dignata sit. Quin et ex prædicto Celnis Vræ Legato gratissimâ mente nec sine voluptate percepi, Cm Vm etiamnum subinde per occasionem nonnunquam mei mentionem facere, quod constantis erga me Clementiæ et benignitatis indicium, quâ possum submissione ac gratiarum devota actione, lubens agnosco, inque eo mihi non parum congratulor.

De migratione autem e Dania, Patria mea, ante quadriennium factâ, quoniam Cm Vm non saltem ex proprijs meis literis, interea ad eam officiosissime datis, sed et aliorum proculdubio relatione per suas circumstantias iam satis edoctum esse, nihil addubito, nolo ulterius ea de re quippiam commemorare. Existimo etiam Celni Vræ iam antea innotuisse, quomodo a Sacra Cæsa Mate, Domino nostro Clementissimo, huc in Bohemiam ante biennium Clementissime vocatus et in ipsius Cæsæ Matis Clientelam benignissime receptus, quodque Mecœnatem eorum, quæ diu multumque tracto, studiorum Astronomicorum in ipsius Mate invenerim liberalissimum. Quocirca advectis, ex ipsius etiam Cæsæ Matis Clementissimo mandato, per Albim fluvium omnibus meis, quibus in Dania ad tam sublimis scientiæ exercitium et redintegrationem usus sum, Organis, ea nuper, ipsius quoque Mate sic Clementissime decernente, in domo quadam ampla et magnifice hîc iuxta Arcis hortum extructa disposui, quæ numero sunt 28, tam formæ et fabricæ quam magnitudinis et utilitatis varietate discreta, quemadmodum supra memoratus Celnis Vræ Dominus Legatus, qui ea singula coram intuitus est, ad Cm Vm plenius referre poterit. Etsi vero in libro Epistolarum, qui Illmi et nunquam satis laudati Patris vestri literas de re Astronomica mihi Clementissime scriptas, unâ cum mea responsione, complectitur (quem Illmæ Celni Vræ a me submisse dedicatum eam accepisse opinor) suo loco nonnulla horum Instrumentorum reperiatur descriptio, et licet insuper icones quasdam, pleraque ex hisce Instrumentis repræsentantes, postea miserim, de quibus, quod eas Celdo Vra obtinuerit, Opus illud Palatinum, mihi vicissim dono Clementissime remissum, omne exemit dubium: Tamen cum Organa mea Astronomica postmodum plenius delineari atque excidi, unâ cum uberiore Fabricæ et Usus explicatione, atque in peculiari libro inprimi curârim, quem Imperatoriæ Mati humilime dedicatum, unâ cum duobus alijs pari formâ et magnitudine, quorum unus de mille affixarum stellarum accuratissime a nobis facta Restitutione, alter de ☉lis et ☾næ verificatis motibus agit, cum primum huc advenissem, subiectissime obtulerim, Vræ Illmæ Celni eum, qui de Mechanica Astronomiæ parte tractat, cum hoc ipso suo Nobilissimo et eximio Legato demisse dono mittendum duxi, quem et Celdo Vra benignâ fronte accipere non dedignetur, quâ possum reverentia oro. Si reliquos duos Celdo Vra unâ Clementissime expetierit, præsertim eum, qui affixarum Rectificationem scrupulose exequitur, ut collatio cum ijs stellis, quas Vra Celdo ibi pariter observatas habet, institui possit, eos quoque per temporis oportunitatem quam obsequentissimo animo impertiar. Quod et nunc præstitissem, si exempla conficiendi (sunt enim ambo manu scripti) commoditas superfuisset. Quin etiam cum alia ex meis Operibus lucem publicam viderint, ea cum Celne Vra omni debita submissione communicare, eiusque solertissimo et gravissimo submittere iudicio non supersedebo: Et quacunque alias in re pro mearum virium modulo quippiam, quod Celni Vræ gratum sit, præstare potuero, habebit ea in me servitorem fidelem, cui id, quicquid erit, libere demandet, qui etiam pro virili Vræ Celnis Clementissimis desiderijs omni animi submissione obtemperaturus sit. Sed ne diutius, quam par est, Cm Vm, cui me meaque studia commendata esse submisse cupio, hac interpellatione, gravioribus negocijs intentam, remorer, eam quam prosperrime florere et quam diutissime saluberrimeque valere unâ cum Illma Coniuge et inclytis liberis, a Deo Opt. ter Maximo devoto pectore precor.

Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum Die 10 Novembris Anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO MORITZ, LANDGRAVE OF HESSE.

Since Your most illustrious Highness's magnificent Envoy, Lord Johann von Bopenhausen — who conducted hither, by Your Highness's command, the Legation of the great King of the Persians — had indicated to me (for while he was here some familiarity arose between us from our repeated meeting and conversation) that he was hastening his return into Hesse to Your Highness, I judged it part of my duty humbly to greet Your most illustrious Highness by this my letter, and with all due reverence to offer whatever services can proceed from my slenderness. What increased this confidence to write, and persuaded me not to omit it, was not only the benevolence with which Your Highness's Father, of most praised memory, while he lived, most clemently attended me, but also Your own Highness's most kindly disposition and persevering favour toward me — as is clear even from this, that Your Highness, even after Your Father's death, deigned to honour me with most clement and cultivated letters. And indeed I learned, from the said Envoy of Your Highness, with most grateful mind and not without delight, that Your Highness still now and then, as occasion offers, makes mention of me — which token of constant clemency and kindness toward me I gladly acknowledge, with what submission and devoted giving of thanks I can, and in it I congratulate myself not a little.

But of my migration from Denmark, my fatherland, made four years ago — since I in no way doubt that Your Highness has already been sufficiently instructed in its circumstances, not only from my own letters, dutifully sent meanwhile, but doubtless also from the report of others — I will recount nothing further about that matter. I think too that it has already become known to Your Highness how, by the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, I was two years ago most clemently called hither into Bohemia and most kindly received into His Imperial Majesty's clientele, and that I have found in His Majesty a most liberal Maecenas of those Astronomical studies which I have long and much pursued. Wherefore — all my Instruments, which in Denmark I used for the exercise and renewal of so sublime a science, having been transported by the river Elbe, by His Imperial Majesty's most clement mandate also — I have lately, His Majesty so most clemently decreeing, set them up in a certain ample house, magnificently built here beside the Castle garden; they are 28 in number, distinguished by variety as much of form and fabric as of magnitude and usefulness — as Your Highness's above-mentioned Lord Envoy, who beheld each of them in person, will be able to report more fully to Your Highness. And although in the Book of Epistles — which comprises the letters on Astronomical matters most clemently written to me by Your never-sufficiently-praised illustrious Father, together with my reply (which I think Your most illustrious Highness received, humbly dedicated to You by me) — some description of these Instruments is found in its place; and although I afterward sent besides certain engravings [icones] representing most of these Instruments (and that Your Highness obtained them, that Palatine Work [Opus Palatinum], most clemently sent back to me in turn as a gift, removed all doubt): yet, since I have since had my Astronomical Instruments more fully delineated and engraved, with a richer explanation of their Fabric and Use, and printed in a special book — which, humbly dedicated to the Imperial Majesty, together with two others of equal form and size (one of which treats of the Restitution of the thousand fixed stars, most accurately made by us, the other of the verified motions of the Sun [☉] and Moon [☾]), I most submissively offered as soon as I had come hither — I have judged that the one which treats of the Mechanical part of Astronomy should be humbly sent as a gift to Your most illustrious Highness, by this same most noble and excellent Envoy of Yours; which Your Highness may not disdain to accept with kindly countenance, I pray with what reverence I can. If Your Highness should clemently desire the remaining two as well — especially the one that scrupulously carries out the Rectification of the fixed stars, that a collation may be set up with those stars which Your Highness has likewise observed there — I will impart these too, in time's opportunity, with a most obedient mind. Which I would have done even now, had the convenience of making copies remained (for both are in manuscript). And indeed, when others of my Works shall have seen the public light, I will not forbear to communicate them to Your Highness with all due submission, and to submit them to Your most skilful and weighty judgement. And in whatever matter otherwise I shall be able, according to the measure of my powers, to do anything welcome to Your Highness, You shall have in me a faithful servant, to whom You may freely entrust whatever it be, and who will, to the utmost, obey Your Highness's most clement desires with all submission of mind. But lest I detain Your Highness — to whom I humbly wish myself and my studies commended — longer than is fitting with this interruption, [You being] intent on weightier affairs, I pray from a devoted heart to God the Best and Thrice-Greatest that You may flourish most prosperously and fare most long and healthily, together with Your illustrious Consort and renowned children.

Given at Prague of the Bohemians, the 10th day of November, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Moritz "the Learned," Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632) — son and successor of the astronomer-prince Wilhelm IV. The letter is formal court Latin, all in the deferential third-person Celsitudo Vestra ("Your Highness") register.
  • The late father. Illustrissimi et nunquam satis laudati Patris vestri = Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the observing prince of the Kassel observatory, Tycho's great correspondent — "of most praised memory."
  • The envoy & the Persian embassy. Johann von Bopenhausen, the Hessian envoy who "conducted hither the Legation of the great King of the Persians" — i.e. the Safavid embassy (Husain Ali Beg / the Sherley mission) that reached Prague in 1600; he is the bearer of this letter and of the gift book back to Kassel, having seen Tycho's instruments in person.
  • Significance — the Prague instrument-installation and the three-volume imperial program.
  • The 28 instruments at Prague. Tycho's instruments, shipped from Denmark up the Elbe by imperial mandate, are now set up in "an ample house, magnificently built beside the Castle garden" (the Curtius house; cf. brahe-t_16010415001, "the new Rudolphine Uraniborg") — "28 in number, varied in form, fabric, magnitude and usefulness." A precise, datable census of the working Prague apparatus.
  • The three Opera offered to Rudolf II. Tycho names the program he presented to the Emperor on arrival: (1) the book "on the Mechanical part of Astronomy" = the Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica (the gift here sent); (2) "the Restitution of the thousand fixed stars" = the Stellarum octavi orbis … restitutio / the 1000-star catalogue; (3) "the verified motions of the Sun and Moon" = the Progymnasmata. Crucially, (2) and (3) were still in manuscript in November 1600 ("for both are in manuscript … no convenience of making copies remained").
  • A documented Mechanica gift-distribution event. Tycho sends Landgrave Moritz a gift copy of the Mechanica via Bopenhausen (Prague → Kassel, Nov 1600), offering the star-catalogue volume too — explicitly for collation against the Kassel star observations ("those stars which Your Highness has likewise observed there"). Cross-ref the Mechanica production/distribution layer.
  • The earlier books referenced.
  • The "Book of Epistles" = Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum liber (Uraniborg 1596), which printed Wilhelm IV's astronomical letters with Tycho's replies, dedicated to Moritz — with some instrument-descriptions already in it; Tycho had also sent engravings (icones) of the instruments.
  • The Opus Palatinum = the Opus Palatinum de triangulis of Rheticus / Valentin Otho (Neustadt an der Haardt, 1596), which Moritz had sent Tycho as a return gift — proof that Moritz received the engravings.
  • Persons & works named. Moritz of Hesse-Kassel; Wilhelm IV (the late father); Johann von Bopenhausen (envoy); the King of Persia (the Safavid embassy); Rudolf II; the Epistolae astronomicae (1596); the Opus Palatinum (Rheticus/Otho); the Mechanica, the 1000-star Restitutio, the Progymnasmata (Sun/Moon motions). Places: Prague (the house by the castle garden), Denmark, the Elbe, Kassel (implied "there").
  • Calendar. Die 10 Novembris Anni 1600 at Prague (imperial reckoning) → 10 November 1600 [N.S.]; header and label agree.
