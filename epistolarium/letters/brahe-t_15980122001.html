The original

NOBILISSIMO ET PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO OLIGERO ROSÆKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, COGNATO ATQUE AFFINI SUO UNICE DILECTO.

OPTIME a te factum, Exoptatissime mi OLIGERE, Cognate et Affinis desideratissime, quod hisce indicaris et tuæ moræ caussas, ac quod CRAGIUS noster Lubecam confestim transiturus sit, non autem huc, uti sperabamus, venturus. Utrumque vestrum satis excusatum habeo, cum neuter proprij adhuc sit arbitrij. Tu ergastulo et compedibus istis aureis solutus, brevi (quod etiam tibi summopere exopto) tibi ipsi redderis, quo virtuti et studijs, non aulicis indulgeas affanijs. Tumque te promissi fidem liberaturum nullus addubito. Interea quod nonnihil Iutizâris, cum et Iutus sis et in Iutia verseris, nolo Scanico supercilio exactius rimari. Adsis nobis felici sidere, quam primum adfueris, quod etiam, ut mature fiat, et cupio et opto. Iam enim teneris, quia sponte promisisti; nec nippizare licet Iuto in Scanicas transituro Nuptias, gentemque Scanicam constantem et dicti factique memorem æmulaturo: transplantatione quadam, ut fieri solet, quam etiam tibi non ingratam aut infelicem futurum, auguror atque confido.

Si res meæ ullo modo id patientur, dabo operam, ut intra triduum vel quatriduum Lubecæ adsim. Et si communis ille noster, dum per diem unum vel alterum isthic commoror, supervenerit, erit mihi gratissimum, quod ipsius fruar alloquio; utcunque iam antea prævidebam, non licere illi hac transire, vel mea forte de caussa. Quod ex alto rideo. Habeo alias Lubecæ quod agam, satisque necessario. Nam res meæ atque Instrumenta nuper eo appulerunt, quorum quædam huc recepi, quædam isthic in ædibus RANZOVIJ conservantur. Et nemo adest, qui discrete ordinare possit, quæ et quomodo huc vehenda, ita ut nunc discohærentia attulerint aurigæ, non sine meo incommodo et temporis dispendio. Sunt et alia quædam, quæ isthic exequi constitui, quæ etiam volens reieci in tempus tui vel ipsius adventus, ne peregre abessem, aliquo vestrum huc interea superveniente, utque aliquem ex vobis, si non utrunque, eo deducerem, prout res vestræ ferrent, absolutis interea quibusdam ex meis, quæ præ manibus habeo, et indies urgeo. Vellem autem, ut tuum ad nos accessum ita disponeres, quo Lubeca redux hîc domi esse queam, ubi adveneris. Quod etiam ipsas, quas tibi isthic vestri Politici nectunt, moras, sponte effecturas prævideo. Promisit OTTO LINDENOW, se tibi certo traditurum centum illos Ioachimicos, quos mihi pro curru et tribus equis sibi venditis debet, quando me accederes. Rogo itaque, ut ante abitum eos ad te recipias et meo nomine persolutionis des chirographum, tecumque eosdem sumas. Cuperem etiam, mi amantissime Cognate atque Affinis, quod quingentos vel sexcentos Ioachimicos mihi uspiam, sub tuo tamen silenter nomine procurares, quos etiam tecum adferre queas. Restituet eos ad Pascha ESCHILLUS BILLEUS noster, quem satis integræ esse fidei, nosti, ex ijs, quos mihi debet, duobus millibus. Et si opus erit, curia mea Haffniensis interea pignoris loco erit, tam pro summa quam interesse; aut etiam ædes in orientali platea Haffniæ SCHYRMANNI, quas hypothecæ loco habeo pro 1000 Ioachimicis, qui illi ad festum Paschatis resignati sunt iam antea. Pendet vero domus quotannis 80 Ioachimicos, interesse pro millenis paulo maius solito, ut res satis tuta sit. Sin vero commode fieri nequeat, ut hac in parte nostris desiderijs morem geras, nolim te nimium sic occupari, aut ullas adventus tui remoras inde invenire. Caussa, cur id expetam (ut tibi secreto eam communicem, cui his maiora concredo) est, quod post paucas hebdomadas tres ex meis, quos intimos habeo, in diversa Germaniæ loca ablegare decreverim: inter quos etiam filius natu grandior erit, qui nunc apud Administratorem per mensem abest, ipso sic volente. Et quoniam emissarij mei præcipuos Germaniæ Magnates, adeoque ipsum Imperatorem quidam ex ijs meo nomine accessuri sunt, de rebus magni momenti, uti ipsemet facile coniectari possis, isthic acturi, et a me quædam exhibituri, volo ut nihil ijs ad viaticum desit, quo minus omnia decenter et honorifice peragant. Cumque summa hîc sit annonæ caritas, adeo ut triplo ferme pluris veneant, quam Rostochij, ubi 700 thaleros intra anni quadrantem insumpsi, facile æstimare poteris, quantam hîc, cum familia, quæ 24 personas attingit, per dimidium fere annum exposuerim pecuniam. Summa vero illa maior, quam mecum accepi, apud Duces Megapolitanos fœnori exposita est: uti nihil inde repetere liceat ante statutum tempus. Charissime OLIGERE, te in his facturum, quicquid per te præstari queat, omnino mihi persuadeo. Neque quid amplius peto aut desidero. Ego tibi vicissim in quovis officij genere exhibendo quam lubentissime morem geram.

Vale, resalutatus a meis omnibus quam officiosissime.
Dabantur raptim Wandesburgi die 22. Ianuarij, quo ipse Tabellarius tuas attulit, anni 98.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most excellent gentleman, Master Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, his kinsman and relative-by-marriage, uniquely beloved.

You did most kindly, my much-longed-for Holger, dearest of kinsmen and relatives, in informing me by these [lines] both of the reasons for your delay, and that our friend Cragius is to pass on directly to Lübeck and is not, as we had hoped, going to come here. I hold each of you sufficiently excused, since neither of you is as yet his own master. You, once released from that workhouse and those golden fetters, will shortly (which indeed I most earnestly wish for you) be restored to your own self, so that you may give yourself to virtue and to studies, not to courtly vexations. And I have no doubt that you will then redeem the pledge of your promise. Meanwhile, that you play the Jutlander somewhat — since you are both a Jute and dwelling in Jutland — I will not scrutinise too exactly with a Scanian's raised eyebrow. Be present with us under a happy star as soon as you can be present, which I both desire and wish to come about in good time. For you are now bound, since you promised of your own accord; nor is it permitted to a Jute, who is about to cross over into a Scanian marriage and to emulate the Scanian people — steadfast and mindful of word and deed — to nippizare [shilly-shally / haggle]: by a certain transplanting, as is wont to happen, which I forecast and trust will prove neither unwelcome nor unhappy for you.

If my affairs will in any way allow it, I shall take pains to be at Lübeck within three or four days. And if that common [friend] of ours should turn up while I tarry there a day or two, it will be most welcome to me, that I may enjoy his conversation; although I already foresaw beforehand that he is not permitted to pass this way, perhaps even on my account. At which I laugh from on high. I have, besides, business to do at Lübeck, and that necessarily enough. For my goods and instruments have lately put in there, some of which I have received here, while some are kept there in Rantzau's house. And there is no one at hand who can judiciously arrange what is to be carried here and in what manner, with the result that the carters have now brought [things] disjointedly, not without inconvenience to me and waste of time. There are also certain other matters which I have resolved to carry out there, which too I have willingly put off to the time of your coming, or his, lest I be away abroad while one of you turned up here in the meantime — and so that I might conduct one of you, if not both, thither, as your affairs should allow, certain of my own [tasks] being finished meanwhile, which I have in hand and press on with daily. But I should wish you to so arrange your coming to us that, on my return from Lübeck, I may be here at home when you arrive. Which I also foresee that the very delays your politicians are weaving for you there will of themselves bring about. Otto Lindenow has promised that he will certainly deliver to you those hundred Joachimsthaler which he owes me for a carriage and three horses sold to him, when you come to me. I ask, therefore, that before [your] departure you take them in [hand] and, in my name, give a written acknowledgment of payment, and bring the same with you. I could also wish, my most loving kinsman and relative, that you would procure for me somewhere five or six hundred Joachimsthaler — under your own name, though, and quietly — which you might likewise bring with you. Our [friend] Eskil Bille will repay them by Easter — a man, as you know, of fully sound credit — out of the two thousand which he owes me. And if there be need, my Copenhagen courtyard-house [curia] will meanwhile stand in the place of a pledge, both for the principal and for the interest; or else Schyrmann's house in the eastern street of Copenhagen, which I hold by way of mortgage for 1,000 Joachimsthaler, that were already before this assigned over to him at the feast of Easter. The house in fact pays 80 Joachimsthaler yearly, the interest on the thousand being a little more than the usual, so that the matter is secure enough. But if it cannot conveniently be done that you gratify our wishes in this part, I should not want you to be too much taken up in this way, or to find therein any delays to your coming. The reason why I seek it (that I may share it with you in secret, to whom I entrust greater things than these) is that, after a few weeks, I have resolved to dispatch three of my [men], whom I count my intimates, to different places in Germany — among whom my elder-born son too will be, who is now away for a month at the Administrator's [court], he himself so willing it. And since my emissaries are to approach the chief magnates of Germany, and indeed the Emperor himself — certain of them in my name — there to treat of matters of great moment, as you may yourself easily conjecture, and to present certain things from me, I want nothing of their travelling-money to be lacking, that they may carry everything out fittingly and honourably. And since the dearth of provisions here is so great that they sell for nearly three times more than at Rostock — where I spent 700 thalers within a quarter of a year — you will easily be able to reckon how much money I have laid out here, with a household that reaches 24 persons, over nearly half a year. But that larger sum which I brought with me has been put out at interest with the Dukes of Mecklenburg, so that nothing may be recalled from there before the appointed time. Dearest Holger, I am wholly persuaded that you will do in these matters whatever can be performed by you. Nor do I ask or desire anything further. I, in my turn, will most gladly gratify you in showing every kind of good office.

Farewell, greeted again in return by all my [household] most dutifully.
Given hastily at Wandsbek on the 22nd day of January — the very [day] on which the courier brought your [letter] — of the year [15]98.
Your TYCHO BRAHE,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from his self-imposed exile at Wandsbek (Wandesburgum), the castle near Hamburg belonging to Heinrich Rantzau, where Tycho settled in 1597 after quitting Denmark. This is the period of the search for a new patron that culminated in the move to the Imperial court of Rudolf II; the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (Wandsbek, 1598) was being readied for the press, its foreword to Rudolf II dated 31 December 1597 (Christianson, On Tycho's Island).
  • Recipient. Oligerus Rosaekrantzius de Rosenholm = Holger Rosenkrantz (1574–1642), "the Learned," Danish nobleman of Rosenholm, later a councillor of state; here Tycho's young kinsman, addressed throughout as cognatus atque affinis (kinsman by blood and by marriage). He was about to marry into a Scanian family (see the Jute/Scanian banter), and would assist Tycho at the Wandsbek lunar-eclipse observation later in 1598.
  • cognatus atque affinis — "kinsman and relative-by-marriage": cognatus = a blood relation, affinis = a connection by marriage; both kept distinct.
  • Cragius noster — "our Cragius": most probably Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, 1550–1602), the Danish humanist historian and diplomat; the identification is not in the letter itself and is flagged as editorial.
  • communis ille noster — "that common [friend] of ours," left unnamed in the source (Tycho is being discreet); rendered with the bracketed gloss, the referent not asserted.
  • ergastulo et compedibus istis aureis — "that workhouse and those golden fetters": a metaphor (cf. the 1571 Wolf letter's ergastulum) for Rosenkrantz's court service, the "golden fetters" being the gilded constraint of attendance at court. ergastulum = a slaves' workhouse/prison.
  • aulicis affanijs — "courtly vexations / fuss"; affania(e) is a late/rare word for trifling annoyances or idle talk.
  • Jute / Scanian banter. Iutizâris … Iutus … in Iutia … Scanico supercilio … Iuto … in Scanicas … Nuptias … gentemque Scanicam … nippizare — a sustained play on regional identity: Rosenkrantz is a Jute (from Jutland) about to cross over into a Scanian marriage (Scania, then Danish, was Tycho's own home region). Tycho teases that Rosenkrantz "plays the Jutlander" (Iutizaris, a coinage = behaves Jute-fashion, i.e. dawdles/temporises), which he, with a "Scanian's raised eyebrow" (Scanico supercilio), will overlook.
  • Crux: nippizare. "nor is it permitted to a Jute … to nippizare." nippizare is a rare/coined verb of uncertain sense — most plausibly "to be stingy / haggle / shilly-shally," continuing the regional jest (a Jute must not haggle or hang back when crossing into a Scanian marriage). The Source block keeps the printed form; the translation keeps nippizare italicised with a bracketed gloss [shilly-shally / haggle] rather than committing to one sense. Flagged, not smoothed.
  • in aedibus Ranzovij — "in Rantzau's house": Heinrich Rantzau (Henrik Rantzau, 1526–1598), the Holstein statesman and humanist, Tycho's host-patron at Wandsbek; his town establishment at Lübeck stored part of Tycho's instruments in transit.
  • Ioachimici — Joachimsthaler (the silver thaler struck at Joachimsthal/Jáchymov); rendered "Joachimsthaler." Distinguished in the same letter from plain thaleri ("thalers," used of the Rostock expenditure).
  • Otto Lindenow — Otte Lindenow, a Danish nobleman, Tycho's debtor for "a carriage and three horses."
  • chirographum — a hand-written bond / acknowledgment of payment (a receipt under one's own hand); kept as "written acknowledgment."
  • Eschillus Billeus noster — Eskil Bille, a Danish nobleman, Tycho's debtor "for two thousand [Joachimsthaler]," vouched for as a man of sound credit (integrae fidei).
  • curia mea Haffniensis — "my Copenhagen courtyard-house": curia here = a town manor/courtyard property (Hafnia = Copenhagen, this chapter's convention), offered as security (pignoris loco, "in the place of a pledge").
  • aedes … Schyrmanni … hypothecae loco … ad festum Paschatis resignati — Schyrmann's house in the "eastern street" of Copenhagen, held by Tycho by way of mortgage (hypotheca) for 1,000 Joachimsthaler, "assigned over" (resignati) at Easter. The financial mechanics (annual yield of 80 Joachimsthaler; interest "a little more than usual") are rendered as written; Schyrmann unidentified beyond the name.
  • filius natu grandior … apud Administratorem — Tycho's elder son (Tyge Brahe the Younger, b. 1581), away "at the Administrator's" — i.e. at the court of an Administrator (the holder of a secularised prince-bishopric or regency); the specific office is not named in the letter, so left as "the Administrator's [court]." Flagged as under-determined.
  • emissarij … praecipuos Germaniae Magnates, adeoque ipsum Imperatorem … accessuri … de rebus magni momenti — Tycho's confidential disclosure that he is about to send intimates (including his elder son) to the leading magnates of Germany and to the Emperor himself (Rudolf II) "on matters of great moment": the patronage embassy that preceded Tycho's move to the Imperial court. The phrase ut tibi secreto eam communicem ("that I may share it with you in secret") marks the confidence; preserved.
  • Duces Megapolitanos — the Dukes of Mecklenburg (Megapolis = Mecklenburg, a learned Graecism on megalē polis ≈ "great town", folk-etymology of Mecklenburg); with whom Tycho's reserve capital was "put out at interest" (foenori exposita).
  • Rostochium — Rostock; the comparison point for living costs ("700 thalers within a quarter of a year").
  • Epistolary formulae. Salutation in the dative-of-address (titulature), by this chapter's convention Vale = "Farewell." resalutatus a meis omnibus = "greeted again in return by all my [household]." manu propria = "with his own hand" (autograph subscription).
  • Dateline / calendar. Dabantur raptim Wandesburgi die 22. Ianuarij … anni 98 = "Given hastily at Wandsbek, 22 January … of the year [15]98" (O.S. — Denmark and the Lutheran north kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The edition label ("1598. 22. januar. Wandsburg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. The clause quo ipse Tabellarius tuas attulit ("the very day on which the courier brought your [letter]") dates Tycho's reply to the day of receipt; preserved.
