The original

IOHANNES ANTONIUS MAGINUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

VLTIMA Bacanalium die fuit ad me Dominus FRANCISCUS TENGNALIUS, nobilis iuvenis, omnique virtute ornatissimus, et talis qualis a te mihi descriptus fuit, qui deferendo ad me tuas literas unâ cum munere tui libelli de machinis Astronomicis et de restitutione stellarum fixarum adeo mentem meam gaudio affecit, ut nescirem quo me continerem, sed hæc mea lætitia aliquantum remissa fuit, quando intellexi, ipsum discessurum esse sequenti mane Florentiam versus, nec posse mihi hîc referre ea quæ ipsi secreto, ut mecum communicaret, imposuisti, qui tamen mihi promisit se hoc facturum post suum reditum Bononiæ circiter dimidium Quadragesimæ, sed frustra per totam Quadragesimam a me expectatus fuit, imo et totam æstatem, cum toto hoc tempore Florentiæ et Romæ commoratus sit. Quin et ego quoque die 15a Maij me Mantuam traduxi vocatus a Serenissimo Duce Mantuæ, ut Principes eius filios in Mathematicis erudirem; ubi permansi tota æstate, nec Bononiæ redire potui ante primum Novembris, tempore scilicet novi studij. In hoc autem meo reditu inveni literas Domini FRANCISCI tui, quibus me commonefecit se quam primum velle Bononiæ redire, ut mihi exponere posset tuum desiderium, quod post duos dies fecit, relinquens mihi simul quædam scripta pro instructione dicti negocij. Valde profecto mihi grata fuit hæc occasio tibi gratificandi, et plus quam credi possit, quod eventus rei ipsius Deo volente tibi clare declarabit, neque enim nunc verbis hoc tibi indicare studebo, sed effectibus ipsis me facturum reservo; habeo enim qui hanc provinciam libentissime suscipiet, qui mihi inprimis devotissimus est, et habeo etiam plures literatos amicos per totam Italiam, qui et mihi et huic omne auxilium præstabunt.

Plura ad te scribere deberem, nisi improvisus reditus Domini FRANCISCI et maturus discessus eius obstarent; præsertim cum multis curis et molestijs familiaribus nunc afficiar. Nolo tamen prætermittere, quin te subnixe et instantissime rogem, ut mihi gratissime communices Tabulas motuum luminarium a te editas et forsan publicatas unâ cum eius usu pro his temporibus; cupio enim primus omnium Ephemerides condere novas secundum tuas observationes et interim, dum alias tabulas absolves, potero ego supputare motus luminarium in eclipsibus et insuper figuras ingressus Solis in puncta cardinalia erigere et lunationes omnes perpendere, et ex his iudicia annua contexere; nam pro hoc negocio sufficiet calculus reliquorum planetarum iuxta usitatam rationem COPERNICI.

De meo statu certior fies a Domino FRANCISCO tuo, scilicet me esse perturbationibus onustum, qua de causa finem huic epistolæ facio, rogans te efficaciter ut hæc omnia in bonam feras partem, meque ut verum et sincerum amicum et devotissimum servum habeas. Deum optimum deprecor, ut tuis votis satisfaciat plene.

Vale.
Bononia 4 Novembris 1599.
Perillustri Dominationi tuæ
addictissimus
IO. ANTONIUS MAGINUS,
Patavinus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

GIOVANNI ANTONIO MAGINI TO TYCHO BRAHE.

On the last day of Carnival, Lord Franz Tengnagel came to me — a noble young man, most adorned with every virtue, and just such as you described him to me — who, by delivering to me your letter together with the gift of your little book on Astronomical machines and on the restitution of the fixed stars, so affected my mind with joy that I knew not how to contain myself. But this gladness of mine was somewhat lessened when I learned that he was to depart the next morning toward Florence, and could not relate to me here the things which you had secretly enjoined upon him to communicate with me; he promised me, however, that he would do this after his return to Bologna, about mid-Lent. But he was awaited by me in vain through the whole of Lent — indeed through the whole summer too, since during all this time he stayed at Florence and Rome. Moreover I too, on the 15th of May, removed to Mantua, summoned by the Most Serene Duke of Mantua to instruct his sons, the Princes, in Mathematics; where I remained the whole summer, nor could I return to Bologna before the first of November — the time, namely, of the new [academic] term. But on this return of mine I found a letter from your [man] Franz, by which he advised me that he wished to return to Bologna as soon as possible, so as to lay out for me your desire — which he did two days later, leaving with me at the same time certain writings for the instruction of the said business. Truly this occasion of obliging you was very welcome to me, and more than can be believed — as the outcome of the matter itself, God willing, will clearly declare to you; for I will not now strive to indicate this to you in words, but reserve myself to do it by the very effects. For I have one who will most gladly undertake this charge, who is especially devoted to me; and I have, besides, several learned friends throughout all Italy, who will render every help both to me and to him.

I ought to write more to you, did not the unexpected return of Lord Franz and his early departure stand in the way — especially since I am now beset with many cares and domestic troubles. Yet I will not omit earnestly and most insistently to ask you that you most kindly share with me the Tables of the motions of the luminaries composed by you, and perhaps published, together with their use for these present times; for I desire, first of all men, to compose new Ephemerides according to your observations; and meanwhile, while you complete the other tables, I shall be able to compute the motions of the luminaries in eclipses, and moreover to erect figures of the Sun's ingress into the cardinal points, and to weigh all the lunations, and from these to weave the annual [astrological] judgements — for, for this business, the calculation of the remaining planets by the usual method of Copernicus will suffice.

Of my own state you will be informed more fully by your [man] Franz — namely, that I am laden with disturbances; for which reason I make an end of this letter, asking you earnestly that you take all this in good part, and hold me as a true and sincere friend and most devoted servant. I pray God most good that he may fully satisfy your prayers.

Farewell.
Bologna, 4 November 1599.
Most devoted to Your Very Illustrious Lordship,
Giovanni Antonio Magini,
of Padua.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), professor of mathematics at Bologna, cartographer, ephemeris-maker and practising astrologer — Tycho's principal Italian correspondent (cf. brahe-t_15900911001). He subscribes Patavinus ("of Padua," his birthplace). The MS is in the Archivio Malvezzi de' Medici, Bologna; the letter was printed in Magini's Tabulae primi mobilis.
  • Tengnagel as courier (cross-links). Franciscus Tengnalius = Franz Tengnagel, Tycho's assistant (later son-in-law), travelling in Italy — the same figure whose Italian journey frames the Tycho→Galileo and Tycho→Concini letters of 4 May 1600 (brahe-t_16000504002, brahe-t_16000504001) and the Melissus correspondence. He reached Magini "on the last day of Carnival" (Shrovetide, early 1599), delivering Tycho's letter and the gift of two books — "the little book on Astronomical machines and on the restitution of the fixed stars," i.e. the _Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica_ (Wandsbek 1598) and the fixed-star catalogue (the same pairing Tycho presented to Duke Ulrich, brahe-t_15980914002). Tengnagel's movements (Florence, Rome; Magini's own absence at Mantua) delayed delivery of Tycho's confidential message until Magini's return on 1 November 1599.
  • The "secret business" (secretum negotium / dictum negotium). Tycho had charged Tengnagel with a confidential commission for Magini, supported by "certain writings for the instruction of the said business"; Magini promises to further it warmly through "one who will most gladly undertake this charge" and his learned friends across Italy, but pointedly will not name it in writing ("I will not indicate this in words, but reserve myself to do it by the very effects"). The referent is not stated; it plausibly concerns Tycho's Italian observational/placement plans (cf. the Siena-Academy-and-Egypt observer scheme of brahe-t_16000504001) — flagged as a hypothesis, not asserted.
  • Magini at the Gonzaga court. Serenissimus Dux Mantuae = the Duke of Mantua (Vincenzo I Gonzaga); Magini was summoned (from 15 May 1599) to teach the ducal princes mathematics, returning to Bologna only for the new academic term on 1 November.
  • The request — Tycho's solar/lunar tables for new ephemerides (astronomy +/). Magini asks for Tycho's _Tabulae motuum luminarium_ (tables of the Sun's and Moon's motions) so as to be "first of all men" to compute new Ephemerides on Tycho's observations: he will calculate the luminaries' motions in eclipses, erect figures of the Sun's ingress into the cardinal points (the equinox/solstice charts), weigh all the lunations, and from these "weave the annual judgements" (astrological prognostications) — for which the Copernican computation of the other planets "will suffice." A vivid statement of how Tychonic data was wanted to feed the ephemeris-and-prognostication industry.
  • Persons named. Magini; Franz Tengnagel; the Duke of Mantua; Copernicus.
  • Calendar. Dateline Bononia 4 Novembris 1599 — Catholic Italy was on the Gregorian (New Style) calendar; marked [N.S.]. (Hence too ultima Bacanalium die and dimidium Quadragesimae reckon the 1599 Roman-rite Carnival/Lent.)
