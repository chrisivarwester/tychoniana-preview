The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

EX itinere, quo peregre abieram, Vir Nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile Princeps, iam tandem reversus, relegi literas tuas 28 die Augusti ad me datas, et ex adiunctis exemplis deprehendi, me semel atque iterum in calculo lapsum esse, quo nomine gratias tibi ago eô maiores. Sed inprimis summe miror, atque adeo satis mirari nequeo, calculum tuum novissimum Eclipseos Solaris diei 10 Iulij huius anni ἀκριβέστατον tam praecise observationi coelesti eidemque exactae congruere, ita ut nec sexagesimâ quidem particulâ unius gradus a se invicem discrepare videantur.

Ἦ μέγα θαῦμα τόδ’ ὀφθαλμοῖσιν ὁρῶμαι.
Respondet numeris tua contemplatio coeli
Unius arctataque ad sexagessma partis.
Θαῦμα ἴδεσται.
Respondent coelo numeri, quibus attrahis astra,
Unius arctatique ad Sexage partis.
Θαῦμ’ ἀκόεσται.

Summo itaque desiderio flagro, ut quanto citius pollicitationem hac in re tuam adimpleas, totus enim iam calculum hunc tuum Solis et Lunae novissimum, posteaquam eundem plane absolveris, exspecto et opto.

Libros, quos petijsti, habeo, sed iam deest tabellarius. FRANCISCUS VIETÆUS Ao 89 Londini edidit librum in folio Regali, cui titulus FRANCISCI VIETÆI opera Mathematica, in quibus tractatur Canon Mathematicus, seu ad Triangula, item Canonion Triangulorum laterum rationalium, unâ cum universalium inspectionum ad Canonem Mathematicum libro singulari etc. In eoque libro multa tractat de resolutionibus triangulorum, quare plane conijcio ADRIANUM ROMANUM de hoc ipso libro locutum esse. Quod si eum desideres, eurabo omnino, ut habeas. ALBATEGNIUS latius habetur, sed is ipse, qui multis in locis incorrectus est, Norimbergae Ao 37 unâ cum ALFRAGANO editus, et nec is quidem facile invenitur, attamen πολλαπλασίωσις pretij effecit, ut ego quoque exemplar eiusdem mendosae impressionis ex Bibliotheca quadam redemerim. Quod si nihilominus eundem petas, mittam illico. Mihi sane, praeter nomina propria, non videtur adeo mendosus, si unica cautio adhibeatur; solet enim numerum binarium et quinarium confundere. Quod si hoc unicum caveatur, in calculo vix error oboriatur.

Iungo his literis Catalogum nundinarum autumnalium, quem ipsum quoque iam demum e peregrinatione domum reversus sum nactus; quod si aliquos libros ex ijs desideres, transmittentur. Affinis meus PAULUS ZEHENTNER ab ZEHENTGRUEB quaedam meo nomine tibi referet, atque hisce vale quam foelicissime.

Datae Monachij die 8 Nov. A. 1600.

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

Now at last returned from the journey on which I had gone abroad, most noble man and easily Prince of all Mathematicians, I have reread your letter sent to me on the 28th of August, and from the worked examples enclosed I have detected that I went astray, once and again, in calculation — for which I thank you all the more. But above all I greatly marvel, and indeed cannot marvel enough, that your latest calculation of the Solar Eclipse of the 10th of July this year — most exact [ἀκριβέστατον] — agrees so precisely with the exact celestial observation, that they do not seem to differ from each other even by a sixtieth part of one degree [i.e. one arc-minute].

Truly, a great wonder is this I behold with my eyes! [Greek]
Your contemplation of the heaven answers to the numbers,
drawn close [to within] a sixtieth of a single part.
A wonder to behold. [Greek]
The numbers by which you draw down the stars answer to the heaven,
[to within] a sixtieth of a single part.
A wonder to hear. [Greek]

I burn, therefore, with the highest longing that you fulfil your promise in this matter as soon as may be; for I now wholly await and desire this your latest calculation of the Sun and the Moon, once you have fully completed it.

The books you asked for, I have — but now a courier is lacking. François Viète published, in the year [15]89, at London, a book in royal folio, titled The Mathematical Works of François Viète, in which are treated the Mathematical Canon, or [the road] to Triangles; likewise the Canonion of Triangles of rational sides, together with a separate book of universal inspections to the Mathematical Canon, etc. And in that book he treats many things about the resolutions of triangles; wherefore I plainly conjecture that Adriaan van Roomen spoke of this very book. If you should want it, I will entirely see to it that you have it. Albategnius is more widely to be had, but that very [edition] — which is incorrect in many places — was printed at Nuremberg in the year [15]37 together with Alfraganus, and not even that is easily found; yet the multiplying [πολλαπλασίωσις] of the price has brought it about that I too redeemed a copy of that same faulty impression from a certain Library. But if you should ask for it nonetheless, I will send it at once. To me, in truth, apart from the proper names, it does not seem so faulty, provided one single caution be used; for it is wont to confuse the number two and five. If this one thing be guarded against, scarcely an error would arise in the calculation.

I join to this letter the Catalogue of the autumn fair, which I myself only just came by, now returned home from my travels; if you should want any books from them, they will be sent on. My kinsman Paul Zehentner von Zehentgrueb will report some matters to you in my name; and with these, fare most happily.

Given at Munich, the 8th of November, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sequel. An inbound scientific letter to Tycho, the sequel to brahe-t_16000504003 (Herwart, 4 May 1600). Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (1553–1622), Chancellor of Bavaria, writing again from Munich, now reacting with astonishment to Tycho's solar tables.
  • The eclipse-calculation match (the scientific point; this chapter's convention). Herwart marvels that Tycho's calculus novissimus of the solar eclipse of 10 July 1600 agrees with observation to within "not even a sixtieth part of one degree" — i.e. better than one arc-minute (sexagesima particula unius gradus = 1/60°). Tycho had enclosed adiuncta exempla (worked examples) that exposed Herwart's own computational slips. Herwart presses for Tycho's promised calculus Solis et Lunae novissimus — the renewed solar and lunar theory (the restitutio luminarium,).
  • The Greek-Latin verse (this chapter's convention, — Greek transcribed and translated; corruption flagged). Herwart frames his wonder in macaronic couplets alternating Greek tags with Latin elegiacs:
  • Ἦ μέγα θαῦμα τόδ' ὀφθαλμοῖσιν ὁρῶμαι — "Truly, a great wonder is this I behold with my eyes" (a Homeric-style hexameter; cf. the epic formula θαῦμα ἰδέσθαι, "a wonder to behold").
  • Θαῦμα ἰδέσθαι / Θαῦμ' ἀκοῦσθαι (so to be read; source prints ἴδεσται / ἀκόεσται) — "a wonder to behold" / "a wonder to hear."
  • The two Latin lines are transmitted corruptly in the working text: "Unius arctataque ad sexagesim[a]e partis" and the manifestly garbled doublet "Unius ar & atique ad Sexage partis." The sense is recoverable — your reckoning of the heavens answers [the observations], drawn close to within one-sixtieth of a single degree — and is so rendered; the corrupt forms are preserved verbatim in the source block by this chapter's convention, not silently emended.
  • The bibliographical postscript (instrument of the book-exchange).
  • Franciscus Vietaeus = François Viète (Vieta) (1540–1603); Herwart describes his Opera Mathematica — the Canon Mathematicus (on triangles / trigonometric tables) — though his "London 1589, royal folio" details are garbled (Viète's Canon mathematicus seu ad triangula was Paris, 1579). He conjectures this is the book Adriaan van Roomen (Adrianus Romanus) had mentioned to Tycho.
  • Albategnius = al-Battānī and Alfraganus = al-Farghānī, in the faulty Nuremberg 1537 edition (al-Battānī's De motu stellarum, ed. with al-Farghānī) — scarce and dear (πολλαπλασίωσις pretij, "the multiplying of the price"); Herwart notes its one systematic flaw, a tendency to confuse the numerals 2 and 5.
  • Catalogus nundinarum autumnalium = the Frankfurt autumn book-fair catalogue, enclosed.
  • Paulus Zehentner ab Zehentgrueb = Paul Zehentner von Zehentgrueb, Herwart's kinsman and a further messenger.
  • Greek vocabulary (transcribed + glossed,). ἀκριβέστατον = "most exact"; θαῦμα = "wonder"; πολλαπλασίωσις = "multiplication."
  • Place. Monachium = Munich.
  • Epistolary formulae. vale quam felicissime → "fare most happily"; Datae (epistolary) → "Given."
  • Calendar. Dateline die 8 Nov. A. 1600 at Munich; n.s. (Catholic Bavaria, Gregorian). Reproduced. The eclipse is dated 10 July 1600 on the same New-Style reckoning.
  • Correction. Corrected the Latin verse: "ar & atique" restored to Dreyer's single word "arctatique" (TBOO VIII p.386).

The English translation happens to render the intended sense correctly (it silently smooths over the corrupted Latin), so the reader is not misled, but the transcribed original itself departs from Dreyer.
