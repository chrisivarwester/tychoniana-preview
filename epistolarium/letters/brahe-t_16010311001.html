The original

NOBILISSIMO ET CLARISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHEN HÆREDITARIO IN KNUDTSTRUP INCLYTI REGNI DANIÆ, ASTRONOMO EXCELLENTISSIMO, IN S. CÆSAREÆ MAIESTATIS AULA PRAGENSI, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO VETERI, PLURIMUM COLENDO. PRAGAM.

S. P.

NOBILISSIME et clariss. vir, Domine et amice vetus plurimumque colende. Reddidit mihi literas tuas vir clarissimus D. IOH. GEORGIUS GODELMANNUS, amicus meus singularis, cuius consuetudine iucundissima per dies complures usi sumus familiariter. Huic confectis feliciter negocijs suis Dresdam redituro, ducente ac fortunante Deo, 600 illos thaleros a Quæstore Iuniorum Principum tuo nomine acceptos, et hactenus apud me servatos, numeravi, recepta apocha eius. Commutavi autem thaleros Belgicos, quorum maior copia fuit, in alios imperiales, præter paucos. Aurum, in quod eos commutarem, in hisce locis vix reperitur amplius, cum maioris sit valoris in inferiori et superiori Germania. Alios sexcentos ad instans Pascha tibi debitos Quæstor solvere nunc non potuit, licet compellatus eo nomine a D. D. BORDINGO Cancellario, iam etiam Lubecensium Consule. Excusat enim magnos sumptus et expensas superiori Octobri in funus Ducis SIGISMUNDI AUGUSTI p. m. factas Suerini, quos expendere oportuit iuniores Principes tanquam proximos hæredes. Petit igitur brevis adhuc temporis dilationem sibi concedi, et quamvis haberi pecunia ista potuisset, tamen intellexi grave futurum fuisse D. GODELMANNO eam secum deferre. De summa capitali, quam etiam te repetere scribis, quid responderit, nondum comperi. Inquiram vero diligentius, anne Lubecæ vel Hamburgi mercatores sint, qui Pragæ negotientur, ut per hos pecuniæ Pragam transscribi possint; idem tu inquiras inter Pragenses mercatores. Mea sane officia et studia tibi non deerunt.

Literas ad D. CRAGIUM in Daniam mittendas non vidi. Accepi vero alias tuis adiunctas et cuidam CASPARO AGRICOLÆ, Musico regio, inscriptas, quas soluta glacie et navigatione repetita, quæ asperitate hyemis diu intermissa fuit, Hafniam transmittam. M. BRASCHIO, qui minus recte aliquamdiu valuit, quod iussisti, indicavi. Amisimus iterum nuper virum et Iureconsultum præstantissimum D. HENRICUM CAMERARIUM, qui cum patriæ suæ Brunsuigæ in dissidio cum Principe eius componendo inservijsset, et in itinere non procul Soltquella Marchiæ oppido abesset, subita cordis compressione extinctus est die 11 Februarij. Funus huc advectum 27 eiusdem terræ mandatum est honorifice. Hemiplecticus etiam est D. MARCUS LUSCOVIUS Iuriscoss. vir excellens et nunc facultatis senior. Ita nos visitat Dominus, ut appareat magnas imminere in Academia nostra mutationes.

CAROLUS Sueciæ Dux magna celeritate et successu Livoniam occupat, et quamvis exiguam nuper cladem accepit, tamen ab altera parte Dörptam urbem in finibus Livoniæ et Moscoviæ oportuno loco sitam vi occupavit, ac pleraque iam loca munita tenet, præter Rigam urbem primariam, cuius tamen vicinas arces ita occupat, ut commeatu eos terra marique intercludere possit, nisi auxilia maturius a Rege Poloniæ submittantur. Et hæc omnia hyeme maxima ex parte egit. Habet confœderatum sibi Moscovum, et quod mirum est, Suecia interim tota quiescit, et rerum necessariarum copia abundat. Poloni dicuntur in proximis regni Comitijs tres hostes proclamasse, Suecum, Moscovum et Walachum, et in tres annos magnam pecuniæ summam ad bella hæc Regi promisisse. Eventum reget Deus pro suo beneplacito, non pro hominum arbitrio aut potentia. In his regionibus, Dei beneficio, tranquillitas est, et optimus Princeps noster, Dux ULRICUS, recte adhuc valet. Ego tibi tuisque omnibus valetudinem prosperam diuturnamque opto. Salutant te filij mei IOHANNES et LUCAS, qui Theologiam iam profitetur hîc publice, et sponsus est, reverenter. Ex D. GODELMANNO Pragam aliquando veniente copiosius de statu nostro audies.

Rostochij die 11 Martij Anno 1601.
Nobil. et Excell. T. studiosiss.
LUCAS BACMEISTERUS D.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 405; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
M. CHRISTIANUS LONGICAMPIANUS mathematicus paulo post reditum suum Praga in Daniam traiecit, ubi adhuc vivit, nescio tamen quo in loco. Amo hominem, et optassem nobiscum eum manere potuisse propter studia mathematica, quæ fere iam hîc iacent. Iterum vale.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most renowned man, Lord Tycho Brahe, hereditary [lord] in Knudstrup of the renowned Kingdom of Denmark, most excellent Astronomer, in the Prague court of the Sacred Imperial Majesty, his Lord and old friend, much to be cultivated. To Prague.

Heartiest greeting.

Most noble and most renowned man, Lord and old friend much to be cultivated. The most renowned man Lord Johann Georg Gödelmann, my especial friend — whose most pleasant company I enjoyed familiarly for several days — delivered your letter to me. To him, his business happily concluded, about to return to Dresden (God leading and prospering), I paid out those 600 thalers received in your name from the Treasurer of the Junior Princes and hitherto kept with me, taking his receipt. But I exchanged the Belgian thalers, of which there was the greater quantity, into imperial ones, except a few; for gold, into which I might change them, is scarcely to be found any more in these parts, being of greater value in Lower and Upper Germany. The other six hundred, owed you at the coming Easter, the Treasurer could not now pay — although urged on that account by Lord Doctor Bording, Chancellor, [and] now also Consul [Burgomaster] of the Lübeckers — for he excuses [it by] the great costs and expenses made last October at Schwerin for the funeral of Duke Sigismund August, of pious memory, which the junior Princes, as nearest heirs, had to bear; he asks, therefore, that a delay of yet a short time be granted him. And although that money could have been had, I understood it would have been burdensome for Lord Gödelmann to carry it with him. Concerning the principal sum, which you write you are also demanding back, what he answered I have not yet learned; but I will inquire more diligently whether there be at Lübeck or Hamburg merchants who do business at Prague, so that through these the moneys may be transferred by bill to Prague — let you inquire the same among the Prague merchants. My services and zeal will surely not be wanting to you.

The letters to be sent to Lord Cragius [Krag] in Denmark I did not see. But I received others, enclosed with yours and addressed to a certain Caspar Agricola, the royal Musician, which — the ice melted and navigation resumed (long interrupted by the harsh winter) — I will transmit to Copenhagen. To Master Brasch, who for a while was less than well, I conveyed what you ordered. We have again lately lost a most excellent man and Jurisconsult, Lord Henrik Camerarius, who — having served his fatherland Brunswick in composing its dispute with its Prince, and being on a journey not far from Salzwedel, a town of the [Brandenburg] Mark — was carried off by a sudden constriction of the heart on 11 February; the body, brought hither, was honourably committed to earth on the 27th of the same. Also Lord Marcus Luscovius, jurisconsult, an excellent man and now senior of the faculty, is struck with one-sided paralysis. So the Lord visits us, that great changes appear to be imminent in our University.

Charles, Duke of Sweden, occupies Livonia with great speed and success; and although he lately took a slight defeat, on the other side he seized by force the city of Dorpat [Tartu], conveniently set on the border of Livonia and Muscovy, and now holds most fortified places except Riga the chief city — whose neighbouring castles, however, he so occupies that he can cut them off from supply by land and sea, unless aid be sent in time by the King of Poland; and all this he did, for the most part, in winter. He has the Muscovite confederated with him; and — what is wonderful — Sweden meanwhile is wholly at rest, and abounds in necessaries. The Poles are said, in the last Diet of the kingdom, to have proclaimed three enemies — the Swede, the Muscovite, and the Wallachian — and to have promised the King a great sum for these wars for three years. God will govern the outcome by his good pleasure, not by men's will or power. In these regions, by God's grace, there is tranquillity, and our best Prince, Duke Ulrich, is still well. I wish you and all yours prosperous and lasting health. My sons John and Lucas — [the latter] who now publicly professes Theology here, and is betrothed — greet you reverently. From Lord Gödelmann, coming to Prague at some time, you will hear more fully of our state.

Rostock, 11 March, the year 1601.
Most devoted to Your Nobility and Excellence,
Lucas Backmeister, Doctor.

[Postscript:] Master Christian Longomontanus, the mathematician, a little after his return from Prague crossed over into Denmark, where he still lives — in what place, however, I do not know. I love the man, and I could have wished that he had been able to remain with us, for the sake of mathematical studies, which now lie here almost [abandoned]. Again, farewell.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Lucas Backmeister (Bacmeister, 1530–1608), Superintendent of Rostock and theologian (cf. brahe-t_16000916002, brahe-t_16001115001). Reply carried via Gödelmann.
  • The Mecklenburg debt (confirmation). Backmeister reports the mechanics of the first 600-thaler interest tranche: paid to Gödelmann (returning to Dresden) against receipt, with the Belgian thalers exchanged into imperial ones (gold being scarce locally). The second 600 (due Easter) the Treasurer of the junior Princes could not pay — though pressed by Bording (here styled "Chancellor, and now also Consul [Burgomaster] of Lübeck") — pleading the funeral expenses for Duke Sigismund August of Mecklenburg at Schwerin last October (which the junior princes, as heirs, bore; cf. the death reported in brahe-t_16000920002). On the principal, no answer yet; Backmeister proposes transfer by bill of exchange through Lübeck/Hamburg–Prague merchants.
  • Rostock news. Letters for Krag (Cragius) and Caspar Agricola, the royal [Danish] Musician to be forwarded to Copenhagen once the ice clears; Brasch "for a while unwell." Two academic losses: the jurist Henrik Camerarius (d. 11 Feb 1601 of a sudden "constriction of the heart" near Salzwedel, after mediating Brunswick's dispute with its Duke; buried 27 Feb), and Marcus Luscovius, jurist and faculty-senior, struck with hemiplegia — "great changes imminent in our University."
  • The Polish–Swedish war (across the correspondence). Charles of Sweden sweeping through Livonia (taking Dorpat/Tartu, threatening Riga), allied with Muscovy; the Poles at their Diet declaring three enemies (Swede, Muscovite, Wallachian) and voting three years' war-funds. Dovetails with the news of brahe-t_16010321001 (Henniochus), brahe-t_16000920002 (Brasch), and brahe-t_16010418001 (Rollenhagen). Duke Ulrich reported well.
  • Persons named. Lucas Backmeister (and his sons John and Lucas the Younger, a theology professor); Johann Georg Gödelmann; Jacob Bording (Chancellor & Lübeck Consul); the Mecklenburg Treasurer; Duke Sigismund August of Mecklenburg (d., funeral Oct 1600); Niels/Krag (Cragius); Caspar Agricola (Danish royal musician); Martin Brasch; Henrik Camerarius (jurist, d.); Marcus Luscovius (jurist); Charles of Sweden; Duke Ulrich III.
  • Calendar. Dateline Rostochij die 11 Martij Anno 1601; Lutheran Rostock kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar. Header agrees with the label.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (Longomontanus crossed Prague→Denmark, "adhuc vivit"), restored from Dreyer VIII p.405 (after "Lucas Bacmeisterus D."; dropped by the working transcription).
