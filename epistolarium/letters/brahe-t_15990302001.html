The original

CLARISSIMO ET EXCELLENTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO VITO WINSHEMIO, I. V. DOCTORI EXIMIO, ET DECANO HAMBURGENSI, AMICO SUO HONORANDO.
HAMBURGK.

S. P.

CLARISSIME et Præstantissime Vir, Amice honorande. Accepi binas a T. Ex. literas, postquam huc Wittebergam veni, quibus iam antea respondissem, si per negotia licuisset, et tabellarius oportune in promptu fuisset, quo nunc se offerente, non intermittendum duxi, quin aliqua saltem, utut et nunc occupatissimus, ad te darem. Ago autem T. Ex. gratias, quod amicitiæ nostræ memor, me suis invisere non sit degravata, et peto, ut tarditatem in rescribendo prorsus candore excusatam habere velit. Existimo Ex. Tuam ex filio meo TYCHONE, cum istic esset, adducturus huc Matrem cum sororibus, intellexisse, in quo statu res meæ essent, et qua de causa huc Wittebergam me recepissem, monstrato Magnifici Domini CORADUCIJ Procancellarij Imperialis rescripto, quod, dum Dresdæ essem, nactus sum. Præterita vero septimana mihi denuo allatæ sunt Amplissimi illius CORADUCIJ literæ, me ex clementissima Imperatoriæ Maiestatis Voluntate hinc Pragam benignissime evocantes, quarum exemplum addidi. Accingo itaque me nunc itineri, ut, quam primum fieri possit, me istic Cæsareæ Maiestati subiectissime sistam, relicturus interea hîc Gynæceum meum, donec, quomodo Cæsarea Maiestas erga me meaque studia animata sit, coram intellexero. Deus Opt. Max. faxit, ut omnia prosperos habeant successus. Cæterum Dominum OLIGERUM ROSENKRANTZIUM nostrum de hac Augusta (ut et CORADUCIUS eam in literis appellat) evocatione, certiorem per Tuam Ex. reddi, misso illi hoc exemplo, aut eius descripto averem, idque ut quam primum certus in illas oras datur nuncius expediat, peramanter rogo. Plura nunc non vacat. Te itaque protectioni Divinæ commendatum habeo.

Datæ Wittebergæ die 2. Martij Anni 99.
Tuæ Excell. studiosiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most excellent man, Master Veit Winsheim, distinguished Doctor of Both Laws and Dean at Hamburg, his friend to be honoured. Hamburg.

Hearty greeting.

Most distinguished and most excellent man, friend to be honoured. I received two letters from Your Excellency after I came here to Wittenberg, to which I would already have replied, had business allowed and a courier been opportunely at hand; one now offering itself, I judged I should not omit to send you at least something — however most occupied I am even now. And I give Your Excellency thanks that, mindful of our friendship, you did not find it burdensome to visit me with your [letters]; and I ask that you be willing to hold my tardiness in replying excused with all candour. I think Your Excellency understood, from my son Tycho — when he was there, [intending] to bring hither [his] Mother with [his] sisters — in what state my affairs were, and for what cause I had betaken myself here to Wittenberg, the rescript of the Magnificent Master Coraduz, Imperial Vice-Chancellor, having been shown [him], which I obtained while I was at Dresden. But this past week the letters of that most ample Coraduz were brought to me again, most kindly summoning me from here to Prague, by the most clement will of the Imperial Majesty — a copy of which I have added. I therefore now gird myself for the journey, that, as soon as possible, I may present myself most submissively there to the Imperial Majesty, leaving meanwhile here my women's household, until I have understood in person how the Imperial Majesty is disposed toward me and my studies. May God Best and Greatest grant that all [things] have prosperous outcomes. For the rest, I would be eager that our Master Holger Rosenkrantz be made more certain, through Your Excellency, about this august summons (as Coraduz too calls it in his letters) — this copy, or a transcript of it, being sent to him; and I most lovingly ask that you despatch it as soon as a sure messenger into those regions is given. There is no more leisure now. I therefore hold you commended to the Divine protection.

Given at Wittenberg, the 2nd of March, in the year [15]99.
Most zealous of Your Excellency,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Veit Winsheim (Vitus Winshemius), I[uris] V[triusque] Doctor (Doctor of Both Laws) and Dean at Hamburg — a jurist-correspondent of Tycho in his Hamburg orbit; the letter is addressed to Hamburg.
  • The imperial summons relayed. Tycho reports that, while at Dresden, he had a rescriptum of Vice-Chancellor Coraduz; and that "this past week" Coraduz's letters came again, "summoning me from here to Prague by the most clement will of the Imperial Majesty" — i.e. the formal summons of brahe-t_15990127001 (27 Jan 1599). Tycho is now girding for the journey, and asks Winsheim to forward a copy of "this august summons (Augusta evocatio, as Coraduz too calls it)" to Holger Rosenkrantz in Denmark.
  • Family. filius meus Tycho … adducturus huc Matrem cum sororibus — Tycho the Younger had been at Hamburg, to bring Tycho's wife (their mother) and the daughters (sisters) to Wittenberg; Gynaeceum meum ("my women's household / womenfolk") whom Tycho leaves at Wittenberg while he goes ahead to Prague to test the Emperor's disposition.
  • Calendar. Wittebergae die 2. Martij 99 = 2 March 1599; Wittenberg (Lutheran Saxony) = Old Style. Header agrees with the label.
