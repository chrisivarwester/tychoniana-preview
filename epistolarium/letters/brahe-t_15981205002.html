The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD NICOLAUM KRAGIUM.

S. P.

CUM Dresdæ adhuc essem, ante paucos dies literas tuas accepi, Præstantissime et Amantissime Domine Doctor NICOLAE KRAGIE, quæ Haffnia datæ erant Calendis Octobris huius anni. Indicas autem ab initio te meas non accepisse, quas HOLGERUS ROSENKRANTZIUS noster in tua absentia secum in Cimbriam recepit; miror itaque eum tanto tempore illas retinuisse. Existimaverat quidem ille, te esse invisurum sese Rosenholmij, uti ei forte pollicitus eras, at ego illi scripseram, quod frustra te exspectaret, æque enim rarum esse Iutum Iuto vera dicere et fidem facere, atque augurem auguri, neque sane in hoc augurio falsus sum meo ipsius incommodo, siquidem plæraque erant in illo, quem tibi inscripseram, fasciculo, quæ te mature scire avebam; spero tamen te id iam accepisse et eius contenta omnia percepisse, quare ut de ijs, ubi præsertim opus est, mihi mature respondeas, rogo; atque simul indices, quid de literis illis actum sit, quas IOHANNES STYGIUS in Daniam ad quendam mea de caussa se scripsisse rettulit, et quomodo intellectæ acceptæque. Quæ de Praefecto meo Huennensi in hisce ultimis attingis, parum moror, is non saltem erga me infidelis deprehenditur, atque iniquas reddere rationes suæ villicationis, sed insuper nihil ferme eorum exequitur, quæ aliquoties ei per literas mando, non sine rerum mearum gravi dispendio, adeo ut aliqua adhuc ex instrumentis meis et reliqua supellectile isthic in Insula, aut etiam Haffniæ residua sint, quæ necdum Lubecæ accipere potuerim, utut aliquoties hac de re scripserim et MULÆUS, cum præsens esset, illi singula oretenus exposuit; taceo quod nullos pisces ex tot meis piscinis tanti constantibus, sive salitos sive exsiccatos, uti volui, nancisci queam, sed is illis in suum et suorum commodum saltem utitur, vel potius abutitur. Verum quid de eo faciendum sit, ESCILLUS BILLEUS dispiciat et statuat, cui supremam inspectionem rerum mearum concredidi. Quod autem ais esse quosdam, qui Insulam meam a me petitam volunt, peiore mea futura conditione, equidem non intelligo, quid hæc verba sibi velint, nimis enim obscura sunt, et in varium sensum trahi possunt. Cum tota Dania mihi nimis coarctata censeatur, quî quæso insulula tam angusta tamque illucrifera me remorabitur, his pauciora et longe maiora affectantem sperantemque? Sed vertat se meus osor et invidus in quascunque volet partes, quicunque tandem is sit, non multum de me triumphabit, sed potius infringet dentem solido, propria delusus ab Arte. Atqui ego de Insula illa non sum magnopere sollicitus, neque etiam quodpiam emolumentum inde habeo, sed potius plus damni, neque tamen ullo iure eam mihi adimere possunt, salvis Regis FRIDERICI Literis, neque etiam sine Senatorum omnium iudicio et consensu id facere licet, si modo non quidvis licet citra ius et fas; oportet etiam me legitime citari. Sed restituant mihi solummodo meas impensas aut dimidiam saltem earum partem, quas in illam expendi ultra summam quam a Rege FRIDERICO et Senatoribus Regni accepi, aut decernant, om det er billigt att jeg skulle liide saadan Skade thill forgeffvis. Sed de his alias agemus, quando status mearum rerum fuerit in tutioribus et inexpugnabilibus terminis, quod brevi me assecuturum, favente summo Numine, confido. Erit forte aliquis potentior, qui mei patrocinium in se recipiet, atque a tot iniurijs et damnis, quot in Dania passus sum, vindicabit.

Quia vero de rebus meis certior fieri desideras, sic habe: Paulo post Michaëlis festum Wandesburgo Pragam versus ad Cæsarem iter suscepi, comitante me etiam familia mea muliebri Magdeburgum usque, deinde reversa. Substiti autem Magdeburgi per octiduum in gratiam ERICI LANGIJ nostri, qui mihi per quinque dies illic aderat, postea Dresdam profectus, ubi certo illic comperissem, Cæsarem relicta Praga ob morbos Epidemicos, in tutiorem locum septem miliaribus inde distantem se recepisse, nec quempiam admittere, nisi paucissimos quosdam quotidianos ministros, Aulam dissipatam esse, Cancellariam remotam, Consilia et Iudicia omnia silere, in summa Iustitium esse et actiones cunctas ob contagium populare conturbatas, consultius duxi scriptis Pragam ad THADDÆUM nostrum literis, an in tali rerum statu ulterius procedendum sit, expiscari, atque interea Dresdæ responsum præstolari. THADDÆUS acceptis meis in hanc sententiam literis, eas Domino Procancellario Imperij RUDOLPHO CORRADUCIO, qui Pragæ remanserat, actutum legendas exhibuit, is vero descriptum earum confestim Cæsari misit, petens a BARVICIO, ut de Cæsaris voluntate quamprimum fieri possit, quidpiam certi rescribat cumque hoc aliquamdiu protraheretur, uti sunt lenta, quæ apud Magnates expedire oporteat, præsertim eo tempore, quando Literæ Praga allatæ ob infectionis metum, non nisi post aliquot dierum intervallum legebantur. Hinc evenit, ut usque in quartam hebdomadam Dresdæ responsum exspectare necesse habuerim, THADDÆO interea moræ etiam plusquam ego ipsemet impatiente, semel atque iterum responsum urgente, et cum BARVICIO hac de re quasi expostulante, illique IACOBUM CURTIUM Prædecessorem, quod ille multo citius me concernentia præstitisset, exprobrante, et tandem etiam ad Dominum CORRADUCIUM, qui Pragæ adhuc erat, hac de re schedam scribente. Accidit autem, ut eo ipso die BARVICIJ Secretarij Literæ ad CORRADUCIUM pervenirent, quibus Cæsaris resolutio continebatur, quam CORRADUCIUS actutum Schedæ THADDÆI propria manu subscripsit, eamque sigillo suo munitam THADDÆO demisit, ut me de voluntate Cæsaris inde certiorem efficeret. Quæ autem erant eorum, de quibus dixi, formalia verba, ex adiuncta charta percipies. Ego itaque accepto hoc Clementissimo Cæsaris responso, me illi lubenter acquiescere rescripsi; nam et antea THADDÆO per literas significaram, me in superiori Saxonia potius quam Dresdæ mansurum, donec morbi illi populariter in Bohemia grassantes remiserint, et Cæsari commodius fuerit me eo vocare; neque enim periculo caruit cum meo iuvenili comitatu illas oras passim peste et dysenteria infectas tunc intrare. Recepi itaque me cum meis huc Wittebergam, ubi D. V. hyemare constitui, nisi prius a Cæsare, sopita istic lue Epidemica accersitus fuero, eritque mihi hîc oportunum in operibus meis necdum Hamburgi ob Typographorum imperitiam et paucitatem plane absoluta Typis complere. Habito hîc in ædibus, quæ olim erant optimæ recordationis viri PHILIPPI MELANCHTONIS, nunc CASPARIS PEUCERI, inquilino earum modo exciente Domino Doctore IOHANNE IESSENIO Philosopho et Medico eximio, qui me perlubenter, imo non sine gaudio ad se recepit, et potissimam ædium partem pro me meaque familia, quam diu lubuerit, inhabitandam benigne concessit. Adest commoditas pro observationibus continuandis, suntque mihi ad manus hîc quædam ex meis Instrumentis, quæ in Bohemiam mecum sumere constitui. Est etiam locus pro laboratorio Pyronomico, in quo nonnulla, interea dum hîc sum, parare animus est, præter ea, quæ prius Wandesburgi confecta in promtu habeo. Cæsar enim talibus impense delectatur, eaque proprijs manibus versat, non minus quam Mathematica, quæ probe intelligit et alijs præfert. Ideoque aiunt, ipsum magno mei desiderio teneri, adeo ut in locum, quo nunc secessit, Podebra dictum, me ad se accersere, ut quotidie istic illi adessem, voluerit, iam præparato pro me et meis amplo hospitio, sed increbrescente pestis vehementia in illa vicinia, ita ut aliquot bruta animantia inficerentur, de mutatione loci cogitavit, ita ut aut in Sileciam, aut Styriam aut etiam versus Insbrug se recipere cuperet, relicta Bohemia, modo id tutum foret, et ab indigena Nobilitate permissum. Atque hinc etiam factum, ut responsum de me aliquandiu retardaretur quodque eiuscemodi quale in charta vides acceperim. Adfuit mihi Dresdæ doctor IOHANNES GŒDELMANNUS Consiliarius Saxonicus, CHYTRÆI gener, ijs ipsis diebus, quando Dresda mihi abeundum fuit, is inter alia mihi dixit, se Pragæ, unde nuper advenerat, a fidedignis audivisse, Cæsarem in tantum meum expetivisse adventum, ut hæc verba pronunciarit: »Si solummodo illum virum hîc mecum haberem, cum quo de rebus Mathematicis et Astronomicis, tum quoque Pyronomicis iucunde conferre possim, atque eius opera perlustrare, non caperet me tantum temporis tædium, veluti nunc sit, dum Praga absum. Sed quid faciam, si illum confestim huc vocavero, et mihi ipsi sit alio discedendum, non bene consulo illi et eius comitatui, hoc præsertim periculoso tempore. Præstat itaque id in commodiorem et oportuniorem commoditatem differre, interim de illo convenienti loco constituendo dispiciam.« Atque alia eiuscemodi, quæ nunc prætereo, ut spem magnam conceperim, Cæsarem non ita aversurum me meaque studia, quemadmodum in Dania factum est. Curo autem per filium meum TYCHONEM, addito uno vel altero comite, familiam meam muliebrem huc Wandesburgo evocari, missis meis curribus, qui eam cum supellectile necessaria advehant; eo enim regredi, nisi forte pro disponendis residuis rebus, ijsque alio mittendis, non est animus. Sat Boreæ datum, utinam non nimium, sed tribuendum erat aliquid patriæ, quam diu id fieri potuit.

FRANCISCUS meus TEGNAGLIUS cum ego Dresda huc concederem, in Italiam abijt, illic aliquantulum mansurus linguæ discendæ causa; eum Principi Venetorum, Duci Parmensi, et magno Duci Hetruriæ atque Florentiæ per Literas commendavi, missis ad singulos eorum ijs, quos Regi nostro offerre volui, Libris, quod tamen an factum sit, ignoro. Itidem feci apud MAGINUM Mathematicum. Quin et Elector Coloniensis illum in mei gratiam, et a me rogatus, ijsdem Italiæ Principibus, excepto Veneto, Italicis Literis, quarum hîc exemplum habes, commendavit, ut sperem res eius bono loco istic futuras. Cum mihi scripseris, quod Rostochio ad D. CHRISTOPHORUM STURTIUM I. C. directis literis facile efficies, singulis duabus, uti scis, septimanis huc inde veniente auriga, de omnibus, quæ me meaque negotia concernere nosti, luculenter commonefacias velim, idque solito sæpius, cum nunc maior, interea dum hîc sum, concedatur oportunitas, nisi mare apud vos congelatur. Carmina in mei gratiam ab eximijs Poëtis ad Cæsarem congratulationis caussa concinnata, tum quoque ad me brevi videbis, otium ea describendi in hac festinatione non supererat, mitto tamen exemplum CHYTRÆI Rostochiensis, quod nuper Dresdæ accepi, siquidem illud in promptu erat, isthic ob POLICARPUM sic volentem ex autographo immutatum. Sed ille ob festinum meum inde discessum illud necdum accepit, est alias vir humanissimus, satisque Politicus, meque, dum ibi essem, honorifice excepit. Sum etiam benigne habitus a Duce Administratore, qui me ad se invitavit, posteaque ad iuniores Principes, donata deinde eleganti torque aurea cum sua Effigie; quin et alibi a Principibus Germaniæ clementer acceptus fui, qui, ut eos in reditu inviserem, rogarunt, spe etiam tacite facta, quod si Cæsar mihi benefacere velit, eos etiam suo officio non defuturos. Quomodo Coloniensis me meaque Cæsari commendavit, vel potius illi mea de caussa congratulatus sit, et quid in mei gratiam ad eundem Cæsarem Dux ULDARICUS Avus maternus Regis Daniæ scripserit, exempla earum literarum, quas ROSENKRANTZIO, aut etiam tibi, si recte memini, communicavi, edocebunt. Dedit etiam ad me Princeps MAURITIUS humanissimas literas, in quibus non pro autoritate Principum agit, sed me pro amico bono habet et sic nuncupat. Ex literis TEGNAGLIJ Amsterodamo de ijs, quæ in legatione eius evenerunt, antequam Wandesburgum ad me rediret, perscriptis, hæc et plura cognosces, ubi eas describere vacabit, tibique mittere. Sed desino plura, cum charta vix plura ferat.

Tu itaque, mi optime KRAGIE, cum coniuge a me salutata, liberisque vestris felicissime vale.
Dabantur Wittebergæ die 5. Decembris Anno 1598.

TYCHO BRAHE TO NIELS KRAG.

Heartiest greeting.

While I was still at Dresden, a few days ago I received your letter, most excellent and most loving Lord Doctor Niels Krag, which had been sent from Copenhagen on the Kalends of October [1 October] of this year. You indicate at the outset that you did not receive mine, which our Holger Rosenkrantz took with him into Jutland in your absence; I wonder, then, that he kept them so long. He had thought, indeed, that you would visit him at Rosenholm (as perhaps you had promised him); but I had written him that he would wait for you in vain, for it is as rare for a Jutlander to tell a Jutlander truth and keep faith as for augur to augur — nor, indeed, was I wrong in this augury, to my own inconvenience, since most things were in that bundle addressed to you which I longed for you to know in good time. Yet I hope you have now received it, and perceived all its contents; wherefore I ask that you answer me in good time about them, especially where there is need; and at the same time indicate what was done with those letters which Johannes Stygius reported he had written to someone in Denmark on my account, and how they were understood and received. What you touch on, in your last, about my Praefect of Hven [the island-governor], I care little for: he is found not only unfaithful toward me, and to render unfair accounts of his stewardship, but moreover executes almost none of the things I order him several times by letter — not without grave loss of my affairs — so that some of my instruments and remaining furniture are still on the Island, or even at Copenhagen, which I have not yet been able to receive at Lübeck, though I have written several times of this, and Mule, when present, explained each thing to him by mouth. I say nothing of the fact that I can get no fish from so many of my fishponds (costing so much), whether salted or dried, as I wished — but he uses them, or rather abuses them, only for his own and his people's benefit. But what is to be done about him, let Eske Bille look to and decide, to whom I have entrusted the supreme oversight of my affairs. As to what you say — that there are some who wish my Island taken from me, my condition to be worse — I do not understand what these words mean, for they are too obscure, and can be drawn into various senses. Since all Denmark is reckoned too cramped for me, how, pray, will so narrow and unprofitable a little island detain me, who aspire and hope for fewer and far greater things? But let my hater and enviar turn himself whatever way he will — whoever he be at last — he will not much triumph over me, but rather break his tooth on the solid, deluded by his own Art. And indeed I am not greatly solicitous about that Island, nor do I have any profit from it, but rather more loss; nor yet can they take it from me by any right, King Frederick's letters being valid, nor is it allowed to be done without the judgement and consent of all the Senators — unless, indeed, anything be allowed beyond right and divine law; I must also be lawfully cited. But let them only restore me my expenses, or at least half of them, which I spent on it beyond the sum I received from King Frederick and the Realm's Senators — or let them decree whether it is fair that I should suffer such loss in vain [Danish]. But of these we shall treat another time, when the state of my affairs is in safer and impregnable terms — which I trust I shall shortly attain, the supreme Godhead favouring. There will perhaps be some more powerful one, who will take my patronage upon himself, and vindicate me from so many injuries and losses as I have suffered in Denmark.

But since you desire to be more certainly informed of my affairs, take it thus: A little after Michaelmas [29 Sept] I undertook the journey from Wandsbek toward Prague, to the Emperor — my womenfolk accompanying me as far as Magdeburg, then returning. I halted at Magdeburg for eight days, for the sake of our Erik Lange, who was with me there for five days; afterward I set out for Dresden, where, when I had learned for certain that the Emperor, having left Prague on account of the epidemic diseases, had withdrawn into a safer place seven miles distant thence, and admitted no one but a very few daily servants; that the Court was dispersed, the Chancellery removed, all Councils and Courts silent — in sum, that there was a suspension of justice [iustitium], and all transactions thrown into confusion by the popular contagion — I judged it more advisable, by letters sent to Prague to our Thaddaeus [Hagecius], to fish out whether in such a state of affairs I should proceed further, and meanwhile to await the answer at Dresden. Thaddaeus, having received my letters to this effect, at once showed them to the Imperial Vice-Chancellor Rudolf Coraduz, who had remained at Prague, to read; he sent a copy of them straightway to the Emperor, asking Barvitius to write back something certain about the Emperor's will as soon as could be. And since this dragged on a while (as the things are slow that must be expedited with Magnates, especially at that time, when letters brought from Prague were not read, for fear of infection, until after an interval of several days), it came about that I had to await the answer at Dresden into the fourth week — Thaddaeus meanwhile even more impatient of the delay than I myself, urging the answer again and again, and as it were expostulating with Barvitius about it, and reproaching him with Jacob Curtius, his predecessor (who would have done the things concerning me much sooner), and at last writing a note about this to Lord Coraduz, who was still at Prague. It happened, however, that on that very day Secretary Barvitius's letter reached Coraduz, in which was contained the Emperor's resolution — which Coraduz straightway subscribed in his own hand to Thaddaeus's note, and, secured with his seal, sent down to Thaddaeus, to inform me thence of the Emperor's will. And what were the formal words of those [resolutions] of which I spoke, you will perceive from the adjoined paper.

I, then, having received this most clement response of the Emperor's, wrote back that I gladly acquiesce in it; for I had earlier signified to Thaddaeus by letter that I would stay in upper Saxony, rather than at Dresden, until those diseases raging popularly in Bohemia should abate, and it should be more convenient for the Emperor to call me thither; for it was not without danger then to enter, with my youthful retinue, those regions infected everywhere with plague and dysentery. So I withdrew with my [household] hither to Wittenberg, where I have resolved, God willing, to winter — unless I am first summoned by the Emperor, the epidemic plague there laid to rest; and it will be convenient for me here to complete in print my works not yet quite finished at Hamburg (on account of the unskillfulness and scarcity of the Printers). I dwell here in the house which was once that of the man of best remembrance, Philip Melanchthon — now Caspar Peucer's — its tenant, Lord Doctor Johannes Jessenius, an eminent Philosopher and Physician, having just vacated it; who received me most gladly, nay not without joy, and kindly granted the chief part of the house for me and my family to inhabit as long as I please. There is convenience here for continuing observations, and I have at hand here certain of my Instruments which I resolved to take with me into Bohemia. There is also a place for a Pyronomic [alchemical] laboratory, in which I intend to prepare some things while I am here, besides those previously made at Wandsbek which I have at hand. For the Emperor is greatly delighted by such things, and handles them with his own hands, no less than the Mathematical [arts], which he well understands and prefers to others. And so they say he is held by a great desire for me — so much that he wished to summon me to the place to which he has now retired (called Poděbrady), that I might be with him there daily, an ample lodging being already prepared for me and mine; but, the plague's vehemence increasing in that neighbourhood (so that some brute animals were infected), he thought of changing his place — so as to wish to withdraw either into Silesia, or Styria, or even toward Innsbruck, leaving Bohemia, provided it were safe, and permitted by the native Nobility. And hence too it came about that the answer about me was delayed a while, and that I received such a one as you see in the paper. There was with me at Dresden Doctor Johann Gödelmann, a Saxon councillor, Chytraeus's son-in-law, on those very days when I had to leave Dresden; he told me, among other things, that he had heard at Prague (whence he had lately come), from trustworthy [men], that the Emperor so desired my arrival that he pronounced these words: "If only I had that man here with me, with whom I could pleasantly confer about Mathematical and Astronomical matters, and Pyronomic ones too, and peruse his works, so great a tedium of time would not seize me, as it now does, while I am away from Prague. But what shall I do, if I summon him here at once, and I myself must depart elsewhere? I do not consult well for him and his retinue, especially at this dangerous time. It is better, therefore, to defer it to a more convenient and opportune occasion; meanwhile I will look into establishing a fit place for him." And other such things, which I now pass over — so that I have conceived a great hope that the Emperor will not so turn away me and my studies, as was done in Denmark. I am arranging, through my son Tycho (with one or two companions added), that my womenfolk be summoned hither from Wandsbek, my wagons being sent to bring them with the necessary furniture; for I have no mind to return thither, except perhaps to arrange the remaining things and send them elsewhere. Enough has been given to Boreas [the North] — would it were not too much; but something had to be granted to the fatherland, as long as it could be done.

My Franz Tengnagel, when I came hither from Dresden, went off into Italy, to stay there a while for the sake of learning the language; I commended him by letter to the Prince of Venice [the Doge], the Duke of Parma, and the Grand Duke of Tuscany and Florence — sending to each of them the Books which I wished to offer our King — though whether it has been done, I know not. Likewise I did with Magini the Mathematician. Nay, and the Elector of Cologne, for my sake and at my request, commended him to the same Princes of Italy (except the Venetian) by Italian letters — a copy of which you have here — so that I hope his affairs there will be in good place. Since you have written me that you can easily effect [delivery] by letters directed to Rostock, to Dr. Christoph Sturtz, jurist — a wagon coming hither and thither (as you know) every two weeks — I would have you inform me clearly about all the things you know to concern me and my affairs, and that more often than usual, since a greater opportunity is now granted while I am here, unless the sea freezes with you. The poems composed in my honour, by distinguished Poets, to the Emperor for congratulation's sake — and also to me — you will see shortly; the leisure for copying them out in this haste was not left over; yet I send a specimen of Chytraeus the Rostocker's, which I lately received at Dresden (since it was ready), there altered from the autograph at Polycarpus's wish. But he [Chytraeus] has not yet received it, on account of my hasty departure thence; he is otherwise a most humane man, and sufficiently Politic, and received me honourably while I was there. I was also kindly entertained by the Duke Administrator [of Saxony], who invited me to him, and afterward by the younger Princes, an elegant gold chain with his Effigy being then given me; nay, and elsewhere I was clemently received by the Princes of Germany, who asked that I visit them on my return, with a tacit hope too given that, if the Emperor should wish to benefit me, they too would not fail their office. How the Cologne [Elector] commended me and mine to the Emperor — or rather congratulated him on my account — and what Duke Ulrich, the maternal grandfather of the King of Denmark, wrote to the same Emperor for my sake, the copies of those letters (which I communicated to Rosenkrantz, or also to you, if I remember rightly) will teach. Prince Maurice [of Nassau] also gave me most humane letters, in which he does not act by the authority of Princes, but holds me for a good friend, and so names me. From Tengnagel's letters written from Amsterdam, about the things that happened in his legation before he returned to me at Wandsbek, you will learn these and more, when there is leisure to copy them and send them to you. But I cease [to add] more, since the paper scarcely bears more.

So fare you, my excellent Krag, most happily — with your wife, greeted by me, and your children.
Given at Wittenberg, the 5th of December, the year 1598.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius), the Danish royal historian — the counterpart to Krag's reply of 4 Dec 1599 (brahe-t_15991204001). Written from Wittenberg, where Tycho wintered 1598–99 before the move to Bohemia.
  • Significance — the fullest account of the 1598 journey, the imperial-summons mechanism, and the Wittenberg sojourn.
  • The journey narrative. A little after Michaelmas 1598, Tycho left Wandsbek for Prague, the women returning from Magdeburg (where he stayed 8 days for Erik Lange); then Dresden, where he learned the Emperor had fled the Bohemian plague (the Court dispersed, a iustitium). The summons mechanism is vividly documented: Hagecius → Coraduz → Barvitius → the Emperor, with the answer delayed four weeks (plague-quarantined letters), Hagecius "more impatient than Tycho," reproaching Barvitius with Jacob Curtius (his predecessor).
  • ★ The Wittenberg lodging. Tycho wintered in Wittenberg, in "the house once Philip Melanchthon's, now Caspar Peucer's," vacated by Johannes Jessenius — with room for observations and a Pyronomic (alchemical) laboratory. (Cf. the Vedel letter, brahe-t_15981230001, from the same Wittenberg winter.)
  • ★ Rudolf II's eagerness (the Gödelmann quote). Via Johann Gödelmann (Chytraeus's son-in-law), Tycho relays the Emperor's own reported words — "If only I had that man here, with whom I could confer on Mathematical, Astronomical, and Pyronomic matters …" — a vivid window on Rudolf's alchemical and astronomical eagerness and the reason for the delayed call (the Emperor at Poděbrady, contemplating flight to Silesia/Styria/Innsbruck).
  • The defiant Hven stance. Tycho dismisses rumours of the island being taken from him ("all Denmark is too cramped … how will so narrow an island detain me?"); insists it cannot be taken without King Frederick's letters, the Senators' consent, and a lawful citation — or else his expenses repaid (the Danish tag "whether it is fair that I should suffer such loss in vain"). The faithless Hven Praefect (withholding instruments and fish) is left to Eske Bille.
  • The patronage embassy. Tengnagel went to Italy with Tycho's gift books for the Doge of Venice, the Duke of Parma, the Grand Duke of Tuscany, and Magini — plus the Elector of Cologne's Italian letters of commendation. Tycho recalls the German princes' receptions (a gold chain from the Saxon Duke Administrator), the commendations of Cologne and Duke Ulrich of Mecklenburg (Christian IV's grandfather) to the Emperor, and Maurice of Nassau's friendly letters. (Cf. Tengnagel's own report, brahe-t_15980706001.)
  • The Stygius cross-reference. Tycho asks Krag "what was done with the letters Johannes Stygius reported writing to someone in Denmark on my account" — a direct reference to the anti-Tycho letter brahe-t_15980710001; Tycho was aware of the hostile letter-writing.
  • "Enough given to Boreas." A poignant line on his self-exile: "Sat Boreae datum, utinam non nimium; but something had to be given to the fatherland, as long as it could be done."
