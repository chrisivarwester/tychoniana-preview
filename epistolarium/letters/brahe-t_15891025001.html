The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD DR. IOANNEM CRAIGUM.
S.

CUM tres hac elapsa æstate a te receperim literas, Doctiss. CRAIGE, nulla ijs hactenus respondendi commodior est oblata occasio quam per hunc Magnificum et Nobiliss. virum WILHELMUM STUARTUM, qui etiam tibi priores meas tradi curavit; cum enim conterranei tui hic essent, qui magnificum et illustrem Dominum Legatum comitabantur, non concedebatur tibi rescribendi otium præ alijs curis politicis et œconomicis. Ab eo vero tempore si quando aliquid ab alijs occupationibus respirationis supererat, amanuensi meo responsionem Apologeticam ad has ipsas tuas literas, quibus et sententiam meam de æthereo Cometarum situ et Hypothesium cœlestium nuper a me excogitatam innovationem in dubium vocas, et quantum in te est, labefactare niteris, successivis horis dictavi, quam etiam nunc mitto. Si quid amanuensis incuria minus apposite et distincte scriptum est, tuum erit idipsum discernere, quam rem alias per temetipsum satis intelligis, neque enim nobis alijs et maioribus intentis otium supererat denuo hæc revidere et recognoscere. Si tibi prolixior in hoc responso visus fuero, id ob materiæ intricatæ molem, quæ non paucis satis dilucide explicatur, excusationem apud te habeat; volui enim vitare illud Horatianum: Brevis esse laboro, obscurus fio.

Pro singulari autem illa tua erga me meaque studia benevolentia et candido iudicio plurimas tibi habeo gratias, et vicissim te diligere atque in albo amicorum meorum singularium numerare non intermittam, nec non amicitiam semel per literas inchoatam postea continuare et crebra scriptione, quoties occasio datur (siquidem aliter non licet) conservare. Chimicorum aliquot medicamentorum præparationem tecum non invite communicarem, si scirem, quidnam in ijs maxime expeteres, et ubi in laboribus his pyronomicis dubia præcipue moveris, licet ut dicam id quod res est: hæc arcana neque tuto neque competenter literis consignantur. ROLANDI illius, cuius in ultimis literis mentionem facis, Centurias nondum vidi; ubi nactus fuero, dubitationem, quam circa quasdam inibi contentas præparationes moves, minimo negotio resolvam. Distillatis uti purgantibus nec adeo paratu facile est neque etiam, nisi plura adhibeantur et circumspecte omnia fiant, satis consultum; amittunt enim ea, quæ per se purgantia sunt, vim ferme omnem, si distillando exaltentur, neque extracta illorum adeo valida sunt, atque ipsa materia, per se licet impurior. Sed de his alias plenius tecum agere licebit, omnium vero optime coram id fieret, si nostram invisendi Uraniam unâque officinam Hermeticam tibi unquam occasio contingeret.

Pro communicatis quibusdam compendijs Triangulorum gratias habeo, licet ipsemet, ubi primus numerus est integer sinus, hæc satis antea perspecta habeam ; ubi plura inquirere atque enodare tibi otium fuerit, feceris mihi rem gratam, si omnia quotquot eiuscemodi colligere poteris compendia, peculiari libello comprehensa et exemplis illustrata, mihi transmiseris, utque inventum illud quod tibi Helix Geometrica appellatur, unâ cum canonis confectione plenius aperias velim. Ego laboribus et sumptibus non parcam, ut per operarios sufficientes res hæc executioni mandetur, modo ufufructus par sit labori, et si per me quidpiam rursus, quod tibi gratum sit, præstari poterit, invenies me perpetuo ad quodvis officij genus quam promptissimum. Scripseram tibi et alias literas per quendam Studiosum Germanum, qui aliquot mensibus mecum fuerat, quemque frater meus in quadam navi Illustrissimam Principem nostram Serenissimi Regis vestri sponsam in Scotiam, si venti secundi fuissent, deductura in suo comitatu habuit, sed cum re ob maris sævitiam et ventorum adversantium vehementiam infecta classis nostra in portum reversa sit, te eas non accepisse compertum habeo, neque existimo Studiosum illum hac hyeme, ut constituerat, Scotiam visurum. Quod ideo refero, ne ob moram hanc aliquantulam, quæ in rescribendo commissa est, me prorsus officij immemorem fuisse suspiceris. Verum si quid hactenus intermissum est, id scriptum nostrum Apologeticum sua copia recompensabit, quod ut per otium diligenter evolvas, etsi quid adhuc foveris scrupuli, ingenue significes, te etiam atque etiam rogatum habeo.

Vale.
Uraniburgi 25. Octobris Anno 89.

Tycho Brahe to Doctor John Craig.
Greeting.

Since I have received three letters from you this past summer, most learned Craig, no more convenient occasion for answering them has until now presented itself than through this Magnificent and most Noble gentleman William Stuart, who also saw to it that my earlier [letters] were delivered to you; for while your countrymen were here, who were accompanying the magnificent and illustrious Lord Ambassador, no leisure for writing back was allowed you, on account of other political and economic cares. But from that time onward, whenever any breathing-space was left over from my other occupations, I dictated to my amanuensis, in successive hours, an Apologetic reply to these very letters of yours — in which you both call into doubt my opinion concerning the ethereal situation of comets and the innovation in the celestial Hypotheses lately devised by me, and strive, so far as in you lies, to overthrow [them] — and this [reply] I now also send. If anything has been written less aptly and distinctly through my amanuensis's carelessness, it will be for you to make it out for yourself, a matter which in any case you understand well enough by your own [ability]; for, intent on other and greater things, I had no leisure left over to review and go over these [pages] again. If I shall have seemed to you rather long-winded in this reply, let it find its excuse with you in the bulk of the intricate subject-matter, which is not explained clearly enough in few [words]; for I wished to avoid that saying of Horace's: I labour to be brief, [and] I become obscure.

For that singular goodwill of yours toward me and my studies, and your candid judgement, I render you very many thanks; and in turn I shall not cease to esteem you and to number you in the roll of my particular friends, and likewise to continue afterward the friendship once begun through letters, and to preserve it by frequent writing, as often as occasion is given (since otherwise it is not permitted). I would not unwillingly share with you the preparation of certain chemical medicaments, if I knew what in them you most desire, and where in these pyronomic labours you are chiefly troubled by doubts — although, to tell the matter as it is, these secrets are committed to letters neither safely nor adequately. Of that Roland whom you mention in your last letter I have not yet seen the Centuries; once I have got hold of [it], I shall resolve with the least trouble the doubt which you raise concerning certain preparations contained therein. To use distilled [substances] as purgatives is neither so easy in the preparing, nor indeed, unless several are employed and everything is done circumspectly, sufficiently advisable; for those things which are purgative in themselves lose almost all their power if they are sublimated by distillation, nor are their extracts as potent as the material itself, impure though it be in itself. But it will be possible to treat of these matters with you more fully another time; and best of all it would be done face to face, if ever the occasion should fall to you of visiting our Urania, and at the same time our Hermetic workshop.

For certain abridgements of Triangles you have communicated I render thanks, although I myself, where the first number is the whole sine, have had these well enough perceived before; when you have leisure to inquire into and unravel more [of them], you will do me a welcome service if you transmit to me all such abridgements as you can gather, comprised in a separate little book and illustrated with examples; and I should wish you to disclose more fully that invention which is called by you the Geometric Helix, together with the construction of the canon [table]. I shall spare neither labours nor expenses, that this matter may be carried into execution by sufficient workmen, provided that the benefit be equal to the labour; and if anything in turn can be furnished by me that should be welcome to you, you will find me ever most ready for every kind of service. I had also written you other letters by a certain German student who had been with me for some months, and whom my brother had in his company on a certain ship that was to convey our most Illustrious Princess, the bride of your most Serene King, into Scotland, had the winds been favourable; but since, the matter unaccomplished on account of the savagery of the sea and the violence of the contrary winds, our fleet has returned into harbour, I have ascertained that you did not receive those [letters], nor do I suppose that that student will see Scotland this winter, as he had arranged. This I report for the reason that you should not suspect me, on account of this little delay which has been incurred in writing back, of having been wholly forgetful of my duty. But if anything has hitherto been left undone, our Apologetic writing will make up for it by its abundance; and I have asked you again and again that you peruse it diligently at your leisure, and that, if you still nurse any scruple, you frankly make it known.

Farewell.
At Uraniborg, 25 October, the year [15]89.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven. Ioannes Craigus = John Craig (d. 1620), the Scottish physician and mathematician, then practising at Frankfurt an der Oder / and in Scotland, later first physician to James VI. Between 1589 and 1594 Craig conducted a bitter epistolary dispute with Tycho, disputing both Tycho's supralunary (ethereal) placement of comets and his newly devised geo-heliocentric world-system (the Hypotheses coelestium); the literature consulted (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens; Tycho Brahe and Prague) documents the exchange and Tycho's habit of circulating copies of it to Brucaeus and Hagecius.
  • Salutation. S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting".
  • WILHELMUS STUARTUS — William Stuart, a Scottish nobleman in (or associated with) the embassy retinue then in Denmark; he carried the present letter and had forwarded Tycho's earlier letters to Craig. (Distinct from the "William Fuller" of Craig's circle named in the secondary literature.)
  • Dominum Legatum — "the Lord Ambassador": the head of the Scottish embassy in Denmark in connection with the James VI / Anne of Denmark marriage negotiations of 1589.
  • responsionem Apologeticam … scriptum nostrum Apologeticum — Tycho's Apologia, the polemical reply (dictated to his amanuensis) defending his cometary theory and world-system against Craig's objections; "Apologetic" is kept as Tycho's own characterisation of the piece.
  • sententiam meam de aethereo Cometarum situ — "my opinion concerning the ethereal situation of comets": Tycho's doctrine that comets move in the ethereal (supralunary) region, not in the sublunary air. aethereo rendered "ethereal," not modernised.
  • Hypothesium coelestium … innovationem — "the innovation in the celestial Hypotheses": Tycho's geo-heliocentric system. Hypotheses kept as the period term for a planetary model (cf. this chapter's convention orbis/sphaera), not flattened to "theory."
  • Horace. Brevis esse laboro, obscurus fio — Ars Poetica 25–26, "I labour to be brief, [and] I become obscure." Quoted verbatim as a sententia and kept as such; Tycho names it illud Horatianum.
  • laboribus … pyronomicis — "pyronomic labours," i.e. operations governed by fire = alchemical/chemical (Paracelsian pyronomia, the regulation of fire in the laboratory). Kept as the period term, glossed.
  • Chimicorum … medicamentorum — "chemical medicaments" (iatrochemical / Paracelsian remedies); arcana "secrets" kept (the alchemical sense of guarded preparations).
  • ROLANDI illius … Centurias — "that Roland … the Centuries": a chemical/medical author named by Craig whose work (organised in centuriae) Tycho has not yet seen. Most plausibly Roland = a contemporary writer of medical/chemical Centuriae (so styled because arranged in hundreds of observations); the precise identification is not fixed by the text. Crux flagged rather than resolved.
  • officinam Hermeticam — "Hermetic workshop": Tycho's alchemical laboratory in the basement of Uraniborg; Hermetica = alchemical (after Hermes Trismegistus). Uraniam "our Urania" = Uraniborg (the seat of the Muse of astronomy).
  • Mathematics. compendijs Triangulorum = "abridgements of Triangles," i.e. shortcut methods in trigonometry / the solution of triangles; ubi primus numerus est integer sinus = "where the first number is the whole sine," the convention of taking the radius (sinus totus) as the reference unit in the sine canon. Helix Geometrica = the Geometric Helix, Craig's own coinage for an invention of his (so named quod tibi … appellatur, "as it is called by you"); kept untranslated and italicised, with Craig's authorship preserved. canonis confectione = "the construction of the canon," i.e. the making of the trigonometric table. The phrase prosthaphaeresis does not occur here; this passage concerns Craig's computational shortcuts, which scholars connect to the prosthaphaeretic method later associated with Tycho's circle — but the term is not in the letter and is not introduced.
  • Illustrissimam Principem nostram Serenissimi Regis vestri sponsam in Scotiam … deductura — "our most Illustrious Princess, the bride of your most Serene King, [to be conveyed] into Scotland": Anne of Denmark (1574–1619), daughter of Frederik II, betrothed to James VI of Scotland ("your most Serene King"). The autumn 1589 bridal fleet was driven back by storms (here ob maris saevitiam et ventorum adversantium vehementiam … in portum reversa) — the celebrated storm-bound crossing that led James to sail to Norway himself. frater meus = Tycho's brother (Steen Brahe), in the escort fleet.
  • Studiosum Germanum — "a German student," an unnamed assistant who had lived some months at Uraniborg and was to have travelled to Scotland with the fleet; left unidentified by the text.
  • Dateline. Uraniburgi 25. Octobris Anno 89 = "At Uraniborg, 25 October [15]89" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1589, 25 October, Uraniborg); no date discrepancy. The MS witness (cod. Vindob. lat. 9737) is described in the edition as a Kladde (draft/concept copy).
  • No subscription formula (no Vale-plus-name beyond the bare Vale); the draft ends at the dateline.
