The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS, VIRTUTE ET DOCTRINA PRÆSTANTI AC MATHEMATUM ILLUSTRATORI EXIMIO D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, INSULAe HUENAe DANICI HELLESPONTI DOMINO, D. ET AMICO OBSERVANDO.
S.

NOBILISS. vir, domine et amice observande. Binas ab eo tempore, quo meus famulus abs te redijt, dedi ad te literas. Alteras Gallus quidam Mathematicus praestans, cuius nomen nunc non succurrit, reddere debuerat, alteras Rostochiensi tabellario tradideram. Cum neutrarum facias mentionem, utrasque intercidisse conicio. Tuae literae cum libro abs te edito sub finem mensis Iulij sunt mihi redditae. Et, profecto, fuerunt gratissimae, quibus perlectis ad librum avidissime accessi et percurri, initio celeriter, ut generalem totius operis cognitionem haurirem. Vehementer, ita me Deus amet, eo sum oblectatus. Doleo autem, quod tum ob aetatem iam ingravescentem, tum ob curas domesticas ac professionem Medicam, quae alimenta affert, illi studio amplius navare non possum; nihilominus aliorum in hoc studij genere laboribus mirifice delector.

De tuo libro quod dicam habeo nihil, ita accurate omnia sunt abs te excussa et examinata, ut mea lima non egeant. Novam illam orbium coelestium dispositionem nondum accuratius considerare potui. D. CURTIUS Caesaris Consiliarius et Procancellarius Imperij, Mathematicus excellens et iudicio acri, fuit quoque vehementer tuo scripto delectatus. Cum eius iudicium de tuo libro requirerem, respondit non temere de hypothesibus iudicandum esse. Rem attentius considerandam esse. Te officiose salvere iussit, cohortarique, ut coeptum cursum non interrumpas. Inter alia dixit quoque te nimis multam fecisse mentionem et praedicasse nimium VINCKLERUM illum hominem Astronomiae rudissimum. Ex mea Dialexi integras pagellas descripsit, et pessime suo Cometae Anni 77 accommodavit. In mea epistola ad MYLIUM impressa adiunxeram in chartula, quid insuper nugatus fuisset de Cometa Anni 80. Adscribebat eidem parallaxin maximam 15 fere grad. et tamen supra Lunam illum collocabat. Te hanc annotatiunculam meam non animadvertisse existimo, alioquin dedolasses eum asperiori dolabro et pectine acriori depexuisses, aut plane praeterijsses silentio, tanquam omnium indignissimum qui caeterorum, quorum abs te honorificentissima facta est mentio, latus clauderet. Sed valeat iste nugator et ardelio. Satis ipsemet propinavit se deridendum omnibus. Porro quod a me in plerisque dissentias, non fero moleste, mi TYCHO, neque tibi propterea succenseo, imo gratias ago maximas, quod tuo beneficio factus sim eruditior, et veritas abs te asserta est, iamque triumphat. Non mihi mea ita placent, nec meos partus ita diligo, ut in illis et errata et nevos aliquos non agnoscam, et cum possint abstergi, venustiorque forma induci, admittere nolim. SCULTETICUM ingenium mihi non est, qui quod aliquando candide meam de eius Cometa Anni 77 et de Calendarij sui editione sententiam tulerim, et quod nimium Papissare et cum Iesuitis colludere videretur, odium erga me concepit, et tum in literis ad amicos perscriptis, tum in suis istis nugacissimis Calendarijs suggillare non destitit, donec illi significari mandassem, desinat me lacessere, alioquin clausurum me ipsi os. Sic mea amica commonefactio in odium et simultatem erupit. Iam vero sibi temperat et sapit. Nuper enim per meum filium me salvere iussit. Ego vicissim dabo operam, ut et benevolum meum erga se animum, et redintegrandae cum ipso amicitiae cupidissimum esse liquido agnoscat. Haec ideo commemoro, ut meum animum plane cognoscas.

Meis lapsibus omnino patrocinari nec possum, nec debeo. Ac etsi contra tuas defensiones pro MÆSTLINO aliquid dici possit, tamen tuo iudicio repugnare nolo, tum etiam quod MÆSTLINUM ob insignem peritiam Mathematum mirifice diligo, tantumque tribuo, ut dicere ausim, sed absque aliorum invidia, scholas Germanicas, PRÆTORIO unico excepto, parem ipsi habere neminem. Hoc tantum adijcio, bona tua et MÆSTLINI pace, de ratione motuum Cometarum, quod Planetarum ideo certae hypotheses sunt constitutae, quia eorum motus integri poterant animadverti, quomodo item accederent ad Solem aut ab eo regrederentur. At Cometae non absolvunt suas periodos, neque certa lege accedunt ad Solem, et recedunt ab eodem. Itaque non videtur possibile motus eorum rationem explicare et veluti vincire certis legibus. Pulchram quidem et ingenuosam fuisse speculationem MÆSTLINI praedicas, et ego assentior. Sed pulchritudo et subtilitas veritati, virtuti in hac universitate omnium pulcherrimae, anteferenda non est, quam veritatem nec MÆSTLINUM nec quemquam alium in Theoria Cometarum plane, quomodo aliorum Planetarum, video esse assequutum. Tendimus quidem eo omnes, maxime vero tu, qui ex professo iam tractas hoc argumentum, et iam ex hisce, quae veluti fundamenta futuri aedificij posita esse video, multa profecto mihi de te polliceor, et Deum oro ut ad haec Dei opera mirabilia illustranda longum satis curriculum vitae concedere dignetur. Porro in calculo MÆSTLINI, quem tu sequeris, te commonefacere volui, ut inspicias, an ille in numeris alicubi lapsus sit, an vero nos fallamur, item an ea differentia notabilem in sequentibus errorem pariat. Inspexi meas chartas, in quibus olim MÆSTLINI calculum a me examinatum reperi. Primum Epocham Commutationis mediae ad diem 24 Novembris cum MÆSTLINO statuis 206 gr. 33'. Meus calculus habet tantum 206 gr. 18'. Aequationem Commutationis Cometae habet MÆSTLINUS 5, 48', tu vero 5, 1 2/3. Item Aequationem Eccentri habet MÆSTLINUS O, 5. Sic et ego; tu vero 1, 18 1/3. Haec fortassis levicula sunt. Videnda tamen, an in scopo ad quem tendimus, notabilem aliquam differentiam afferant. Mihi repetere et prosequi calculationem ob meas occupationes non licuit.

De nova Stella nullos alios scriptores, praeter illos quos in mea Dialexi vidisti adductos, cognovi.

APIANI Astronomicum aliquando habui, et diligenter inquisivi an ex ipsius Datis in parallaxium cognitionem pervenire potuissem, verum insufficientia fuerunt eius Data. Alicubi diserte scribit parallaxim Cometae inveniri non posse, nisi prius distantia eius a Terra cognita fuerit, quod falsissimum esse nemo est qui ignoret. Ab APIANO igitur nihil est quod expectes. Hunc eruditum pulverem, in quo nos versamur, ille ne somniavit quidem. Eius artem saepe RHETICUS, bonae memoriae, vocare solitus erat faden Dunst.

IOANNIS PENAe Galli scriptum de Cometa nunquam vidi. Librum illum, quem olim tibi Ratisbonae donaveram, ab eo tempore nunquam vidi, neque etiam authoris recordari possum. Lustrabam, Bibliothecas nostrates, invenire non potui. Eius loco mitto ad te olim expetitum librum VERNERI de Elementis Conicis, ubi etiam tractatulus de motu 8 Sphaerae et alia habentur. A FABRICIO Vienna illum accepi, te salvere iubet. His quod addam habeo nihil, nisi ut feliciter valeas. Datae Pragae die 1. Novemb. veteri stylo Anno 88.

Literas quas scripsisti 17 Augusti ex mercatu Francofordensi accepi, una cum alijs ad SCULTETUM, quas illi Gorlicium misi.

T. studiosiss.
THAD.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, outstanding in nobility of birth, in virtue and in learning, and an eminent illuminator of the mathematical sciences, lord of the island of Hven in the Danish Hellespont, my lord and friend to be esteemed — greeting.

Most noble sir, my lord and friend to be esteemed. Since the time when my servant returned from you I have sent you two letters. The one a certain distinguished French mathematician — whose name does not now come to me — was to have delivered; the other I had handed to the Rostock courier. Since you make mention of neither, I conjecture that both have gone astray. Your letter, together with the book published by you, was delivered to me toward the end of the month of July. And indeed they were most welcome; once they had been read through I turned most eagerly to the book and ran through it, at first quickly, so as to drink in a general acquaintance with the whole work. Vehemently, so help me God, was I delighted by it. Yet I grieve that, both on account of my now advancing age and on account of my domestic cares and my profession of medicine, which supplies my livelihood, I am no longer able to devote myself further to that study; nonetheless I take wonderful delight in the labours of others in this kind of study.

Concerning your book I have nothing to say, so accurately has everything been sifted and examined by you, that it does not need my file. That new arrangement of the celestial orbs I have not yet been able to consider more closely. Master Curtius, the Emperor's Councillor and Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, an excellent mathematician and of keen judgement, was likewise vehemently delighted by your writing. When I sought his judgement of your book, he replied that one must not judge rashly concerning hypotheses, [and] that the matter must be considered more attentively. He bade me greet you dutifully, and exhort you not to interrupt the course you have begun. Among other things he also said that you had made too much mention of, and praised too highly, that Vinckler, a man most ignorant of astronomy. From my Dialexis he copied out whole little pages, and most wretchedly fitted them to his comet of the year [15]77. In my letter to Mylius, [now] in print, I had added on a slip what he had moreover trifled about concerning the comet of the year [15]80. He assigned to it a parallax of nearly 15 degrees at the maximum, and yet placed it above the Moon. I think you did not notice this little note of mine; otherwise you would have hewn him with a rougher axe and combed him down with a sharper comb, or else passed him over altogether in silence, as the least worthy of all to close the file of the others, of whom most honourable mention has been made by you. But let that trifler and busybody fare [as he may]. He has himself given all men enough to mock at. Furthermore, that you dissent from me in very many points I do not take amiss, my Tycho, nor am I angry with you on that account; rather I give the greatest thanks, that by your kindness I have been made more learned, and the truth has been asserted by you, and now triumphs. I am not so pleased with my own things, nor do I so cherish my own offspring, that I do not acknowledge in them both errors and certain blemishes, and — when they can be wiped away and a more comely form put on — am not unwilling to admit it. I have not a Scultetus's temper, who, because I once frankly delivered my opinion of his comet of the year [15]77 and of the edition of his calendar, and because he seemed to play the papist too much and to collude with the Jesuits, conceived a hatred toward me, and ceased not to taunt me, both in letters written to friends and in those most trifling calendars of his, until I had ordered it to be signified to him that he should stop provoking me, or else I would shut his mouth for him. Thus my friendly admonition burst out into hatred and feud. But now indeed he restrains himself and is wise. For lately, through my son, he sent me his greeting. I in turn shall take pains that he may plainly recognise both my goodwill toward him and that I am most desirous of renewing my friendship with him. These things I therefore recall, that you may know my mind clearly.

My own lapses I can in no way defend, nor ought I. And although something might be said against your defences on Maestlin's behalf, yet I am unwilling to oppose your judgement — and also because I wonderfully cherish Maestlin for his remarkable skill in the mathematical sciences, and rate him so highly that I would venture to say (but without envy toward others) that the German schools, with the single exception of Praetorius, have no one equal to him. This much only I add — by your leave and Maestlin's — concerning the account of the motions of comets: that the hypotheses of the planets have been established as certain for this reason, that their motions could be observed entire, and likewise how they approached the Sun or receded from it. But comets do not complete their periods, nor do they approach the Sun and recede from it by a fixed law. And so it does not seem possible to explain the account of their motions and, as it were, to bind them with fixed laws. You proclaim that Maestlin's speculation was indeed beautiful and ingenious, and I agree. But beauty and subtlety are not to be set before truth — that virtue most beautiful of all in this universe — which truth I do not see that either Maestlin or anyone else has fully attained in the theory of comets, in the manner [achieved] for the other planets. We all do indeed strive toward that, but most of all you, who now treat this subject professedly; and already, from these things which I see laid down as it were the foundations of a future edifice, I truly promise myself much concerning you, and I pray God that he may deign to grant a course of life long enough for the illustrating of these wonderful works of God. Furthermore, in Maestlin's calculation, which you follow, I wished to put you on notice, that you may examine whether he has somewhere erred in the numbers, or whether instead we are deceived, and likewise whether that difference begets a notable error in what follows. I have looked over my papers, in which I once found Maestlin's calculation examined by me. First, the epoch of the mean commutation for the 24th day of November you set with Maestlin at 206 deg. 33′. My calculation has only 206 deg. 18′. The equation of the commutation of the comet Maestlin has at 5°, 48′, but you at 5°, 1 2/3′. Likewise the equation of the eccentric Maestlin has at 0°, 5′. So have I too; but you at 1°, 18 1/3′. These things perhaps are trifling. They must be looked into, nonetheless, [to see] whether in the goal toward which we strive they bring some notable difference. To repeat and pursue the calculation has not been permitted me, on account of my occupations.

Of the new star I have come to know no other writers besides those whom you saw adduced in my Dialexis.

Apian's Astronomicum I once had, and I diligently inquired whether from his own data I might have arrived at a knowledge of the parallaxes; but his data were insufficient. Somewhere he expressly writes that the parallax of a comet cannot be found unless its distance from the Earth has first been known — which there is no one who does not know to be utterly false. From Apian, therefore, there is nothing for you to expect. This learned dust in which we are engaged he did not so much as dream of. His art Rheticus, of good memory, used often to call faden Dunst [insipid vapour].

The writing of Jean Pena the Frenchman on the comet I have never seen. That book which I once gave you at Regensburg I have never seen since that time, nor can I even recall the author. I scoured our local libraries [but] could not find it. In its place I send you the book of Werner On the Conic Elements, long sought by you, in which there is also a little treatise on the motion of the eighth sphere and other matters. I received it from Fabricius at Vienna; he bids you greeting. To these I have nothing to add, save that you fare happily. Given at Prague, the 1st day of November, Old Style, the year [15]88.

The letter you wrote on 17 August I received from the Frankfurt fair, together with others addressed to Scultetus, which I sent on to him at Görlitz.

Most devoted to you,
Thad[daeus].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer and personal physician at the imperial court in Prague; a leading European authority on the nova of 1572 and the comet of 1577, and Tycho's chief correspondent and ally in Bohemia. He signs THAD. (abbreviated).
  • Recipient address. INSULAe HUENAe DANICI HELLESPONTI DOMINO — "lord of the island of Hven in the Danish Hellespont": the Hellespont = the Øresund (the Sound), in whose narrows Hven (Uraniborg) lies; an honorific conceit for Tycho's seat.
  • The book published by you. libro abs te edito … Novam illam orbium coelestium dispositionem — Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (Uraniborg, 1588), which presents the comet of 1577 and, in cap. VIII, the new arrangement of the celestial orbs (the geo-heliocentric / Tychonic world-system). Hagecius received it in late July 1588.
  • D. Curtius = Jacob Kurtz (Curtius) von Senftenau (d. 1594), Imperial Councillor and Vice-Chancellor (Procancellarius Imperii) under Rudolf II, a capable mathematician; his cautious verdict — non temere de hypothesibus iudicandum ("one must not judge rashly concerning hypotheses") — is preserved verbatim and unsmoothed.
  • Vincklerus — Nicolaus Winckler (Vincklerus, d. 1613), physician and astrological writer of Hall in Swabia; Hagecius (reporting Curtius) dismisses him as Astronomiae rudissimum ("most ignorant of astronomy") and a plagiarist of the Dialexis. The vituperative imagery — dedolasses … asperiori dolabro et pectine acriori depexuisses ("you would have hewn him with a rougher axe and combed him down with a sharper comb") and nugator et ardelio ("trifler and busybody") — is kept as metaphor.
  • Dialexis — Hagecius's Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt, 1574), his treatise on the 1572 nova; integras pagellas descripsit = "copied out whole little pages."
  • Mylius — Hagecius refers to his own printed epistola ad Mylium (the letter to Mylius), almost certainly addressed to the Görlitz physician-astronomer of that name; the precise identification is not fixed by the text and is left as given.
  • Scultetus — Bartholomäus Scultetus (Bartoloměj Scultetus, 1540–1614), mathematician, cartographer and calendar-maker of Görlitz (Gorlicium); the Cometa Anni 77 is his tract on the 1577 comet, the Calendaria his almanacs. Papissare (a coinage, "to play the papist") and cum Iesuitis colludere ("collude with the Jesuits") are confessional jibes, kept literal. The closing postscript routes Tycho's letters to Scultetus via Görlitz.
  • Maestlin = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), mathematician at Tübingen, Kepler's teacher; his De Cometa anni 1577 attempted to bind the comet's motion to a circular path about the Sun. Hagecius rejects this in principle: comets non absolvunt suas periodos ("do not complete their periods") and so cannot be reduced to certae hypotheses like the planets. PRAeTORIO unico excepto — Johannes Praetorius (1537–1616), mathematician at Altdorf, named as the only German mathematician Hagecius rates Maestlin's equal.
  • Cometary calculation (technical). The numerical comparison concerns Maestlin's reduction of the 1577 comet, which Tycho follows. Epocha Commutationis mediae = "epoch of the mean commutation" (the heliocentric–geocentric elongation angle) for 24 November: Tycho/Maestlin 206° 33′ vs. Hagecius 206° 18′. Aequatio Commutationis (the equation of commutation): Maestlin 5° 48′, Tycho 5° 1 2/3′. Aequatio Eccentri (equation of the eccentric): Maestlin and Hagecius 0° 5′, Tycho 1° 18 1/3′. The minutes-values are transcribed exactly as printed (e.g. 5, 1 2/3, 1, 18 1/3); fractional minutes are kept, not rounded. Aequatio in its astronomical sense is rendered equation per glossary. parallaxin maximam 15 fere grad. = "a parallax of nearly 15 degrees at the maximum" — maximam is the source's own word (not an editorial insertion); the gloss "[the] maximum" stands on Hagecius's text. The internal contradiction Hagecius flags in Winckler (a c. 15° parallax would place a body well below the Moon, yet Winckler set it above the Moon) is preserved, not corrected.
  • Apiani Astronomicum — Peter Apian's Astronomicum Caesareum (Ingolstadt, 1540); Hagecius judges its cometary data insufficient for parallax and singles out Apian's claim that a comet's parallax cannot be found without first knowing its distance — which he calls falsissimum ("utterly false"). Hunc eruditum pulverem ("this learned dust") is his self-deprecating figure for the technical drudgery of the discipline.
  • German interpolation. faden Dunst — Early New High German, "insipid/stale vapour" (fad "insipid" + Dunst "vapour, haze"); reported as Rheticus's habitual put-down of Apian's art. Transcribed verbatim in the source and glossed; Rheticus = Georg Joachim Rheticus (1514–1574), Copernicus's disciple, bonae memoriae ("of good memory," i.e. now deceased).
  • Ioannes Pena — Jean Pena (c. 1528–1558), French royal mathematician; his views on comets appeared in the preface to his edition of Euclid's Optica/Catoptrica (Paris, 1557). Verneri de Elementis Conicis — Johannes Werner (1468–1522) of Nuremberg, whose works include De motu octavae sphaerae (on the motion of the eighth sphere = precession/trepidation) and treatises on conics; sent in Pena's place. Fabricius (at Vienna) — most plausibly Paul Fabricius (c. 1519–1589), imperial mathematician and physician at Vienna; identification offered, not asserted, as the text gives only the surname.
  • Places. Pragae = Prague; Ratisbona = Regensburg; Vienna; Gorlicium = Görlitz; mercatus Francofordensis = the Frankfurt (book) fair.
  • Calendar. The dateline reads Datae Pragae die 1. Novemb. veteri stylo Anno 88 — "Given at Prague, the 1st day of November, Old Style, the year [15]88." Hagecius, writing from Catholic Prague (on the Gregorian/New Style calendar since 1584), here deliberately gives the date in Old Style for his Danish correspondent, who kept the Julian calendar; the explicit veteri stylo is the period's own gap-bridging convention. No conversion is supplied or implied beyond what the text states. The catalogue label ("1588. 1. november") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Correction. Two mistranslations corrected against Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p.146 (Latin unaltered, confirmed correct as stored): 'am unwilling to admit it' restored to 'am not unwilling to admit it' (the negative result clause 'non...ita...ut...nolim' had lost its outer negation, reversing Hagecius's stated openness to correction); and 'he bade me greet [him in turn]' corrected to 'he sent me his greeting' (the idiom 'me salvere iussit' had been reversed in direction, making Hagecius the greeter rather than the greeted).

Found two localized but genuine sense-reversing mistranslation (both negation/direction flips, not fabrications): (1) Hagecius's stated willingness to have his own errors corrected is rendered as an unwillingness; (2) the direction of a reconciliatory greeting with Scultetus (Scultetus greets Hagecius first) is reversed into Hagecius being told to greet Scultetus. Both are confined to single clauses and do not corrupt any name, date, or number — the translation is otherwise careful and largely faithful across a long, dense letter — but per the grading rule any sense-changing mistranslation caps the grade at C.

2 defects found:
- mistranslation (medium). The Latin is a negative result clause ("Non mihi mea ita placent... ut... non agnoscam, et... admittere nolim" = 'I am not so pleased with my own things that I fail to acknowledge errors, or refuse to let them be corrected'). Both verbs sit inside the same 'not so X that...' negation, so the sense is that Hagecius DOES acknowledge his errors and IS willing to have them corrected. The English drops the negation on the second verb and reads as a flat, un-negated assertion ('am unwilling to admit it'), inverting Hagecius's stated position from openness-to-correction to refusal — exactly backward, and it undercuts the humility topos that sets up his forbearance toward Tycho's disagreements in the very next sentence. At: “and — when they can be wiped away and a more comely form put on — am unwilling to admit it.”.
- mistranslation (medium). "[Scultetus] me salvere iussit" is the standard Latin epistolary greeting idiom (literally 'he bade me be well/greeted' = 'he sent his greeting to me') — Scultetus is the one extending the greeting to Hagecius, via the son as go-between. The English reverses the direction, making it read as Scultetus ordering Hagecius to send greetings to him — the opposite of who initiated the conciliatory gesture. This also breaks the logic of the immediately following sentence ('Ego vicissim dabo operam...' = 'I IN TURN will take pains...'), which presupposes Scultetus acted first. At: “For lately, through my son, he bade me greet [him in turn].”.

Repaired. Verified both mistranslation defects against the Dreyer page image (TBOO VII p.146, lines 5 and 14-15 respectively), read visually in the extract PDF. Both Latin phrases match the stored the transcribed original exactly, so only the English clauses were corrected — one restoring a dropped negation in a 'non...ita...ut' construction, the other restoring the correct direction of a standard epistolary greeting idiom. Nothing else in the letter was altered.
