The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD MELCHIOREM IŒSTELIUM.

EX ultimis meis literis, Eruditissime et amantissime Domine IÖSTELI, quas Domino Doctori FRANTZIO Iurisconsulto in eas oras proficiscenti commisi, facile intellexisti, quae superioribus tuis respondenda habui, et quaenam deinceps a te desiderarem, quod peracta promotione, quae ut fausta felixque sit precor, meliori restitutus otio efficias, te etiamnum per amicitiam nostram rogatum volo. Invenies facile aliquem, qui id, quicquid erit, meis sumptibus ad mundum describat. Promptissimum vero erit, si hoc per studiosum, quem mihi polliceris (quod et gratum est) huc ipsum miseris, modo is non prius, quod tamen mallem, accesserit.

Quid M. OSWALDUS et Typographus certo addicere velint, etiam mature resciscere cupio, atque ut omnia pro me isthic peragenda accelerentur, ne irrecuperabile tempus nimis diu incassum labatur. Sum iam occupatus in disponendis instrumentis meis superius iuxta arcem in domo quadam ampla, in quam Caesar ex horto suo, cui propinqua est, solitarie, quoties lubuerit, ingredi potest et ea perlustrare, quod et nuper clementissime nec sine voluptate fecit, licet saltem ea, quae Benatica advecta sunt, tunc in promptu fuerint, caetera omnia, de quibus etiam pridie inquisivit, ubi rite collocata fuerint, visurus, atque tum demum, quo in loco, ut ad usum perpetuentur, constituenda sint, decreturus. Neque enim vult, ut ego cum meis studijs tam longe, ut antea, Pragâ absim, quod etiam mihi ipsi non oportunum videtur. Verum de huius consilij eventu, quem spero utilem et commodum futurum, te postmodum certiorem reddam.

Advenit ante quatriduum per postam, uti vocant, Caesaris frater Archidux MATTHIAS ab ipsius Maiestate illico vocatus, statim ac magna illa et inopinata mutatio hîc in aula facta est, reiecto RUMPFIO et TRAUTSONIO, praecipuis Consilij secreti Capitibus, qui intra biduum discedere iussi sunt, quod et factum, caeteris eiusdem Consilij membris retentis quidem, sed necdum in priori gratia habitis. Qua de caussa haec alteratio peracta, nondum satis liquet. Fuit quoque dictus serenissimus Archidux heri in domo illa, ubi instrumenta mea partim erecta sunt, partim adhuc eriguntur, et singula, quae interim maiori copia advecta erant, diligenter perlustravit, siquidem et is artibus his iucunde et laudabiliter afficiatur.

Vale, et cum otium fuerit, plenius rescribe. Atque ubi sexcenti illi Ioachimici e ducatu Megapolitano advenerint, 50 inde pro laboribus typographicis tecum retine, et papyrum idoneam pro aliqua eorum parte tempestive coëmi effice. Plures mittam, ubi opus fuerit. Reliquos intermediante affine tuo Dresdense huc dirige. Iterumque vale, et D. D. IESSENIUM, licet mei immemorem, reliquosque amicos Witebergenses, inter quos etiam obmutescentem illum TAUBMANNUM, saluta.

Dabantur festinanter Praga die 8 Oct. stylo novo Anno 1600.

TYCHO BRAHE TO MELCHIOR JÖSTEL.

From my last letter, most learned and most loving Lord Jöstel — which I entrusted to Lord Doctor Franz, the jurisconsult, as he was setting out for those parts — you will easily have understood what I had to answer to your earlier ones, and what I should henceforth desire of you; which (the promotion being accomplished — that it be auspicious and happy, I pray — and you restored to better leisure) I wish you to bring about, asking you still, by our friendship. You will easily find someone who, at my expense, will copy out fair whatever it shall be. It will be readiest if you send it hither by the student you promise me (which is welcome too), provided he has not come earlier — which, however, I should prefer.

What Master Oswald and the Printer will definitely undertake [to deliver], I also wish to learn in good time, and that everything to be done for me there be hastened, lest irrecoverable time slip too long away in vain. I am now busy arranging my instruments up above, near the castle, in a certain ample house, into which the Emperor can come alone from his garden — to which it is near — as often as he pleases, and survey them; which he lately did, most graciously and not without pleasure, although only those brought from Benátky were then at hand. He is to see all the rest (about which he also inquired the day before) when they have been duly placed, and then at last to decide in what place they are to be set up, that they may be kept in permanent use. For he does not wish me, with my studies, to be as far from Prague as before — which to me too seems inconvenient. But of the outcome of this plan, which I hope will prove useful and convenient, I will inform you hereafter.

Four days ago there arrived, by post (as they call it), the Emperor's brother, Archduke Matthias, summoned at once by His Majesty — just as that great and unexpected change was made here at court, Rumpf and Trautson, the chief heads of the Privy Council, having been cast out, who were ordered to depart within two days, which was done; the other members of the same Council being indeed retained, but not yet held in their former favour. For what cause this alteration was carried through is not yet sufficiently clear. The said most serene Archduke was also, yesterday, in that house where my instruments are partly set up and partly still being set up, and diligently surveyed each one — those which in the meantime had been brought in greater number — since he too is pleasantly and laudably taken with these arts.

Farewell, and when you have leisure, write back more fully. And when those six hundred joachimsthalers from the duchy of Mecklenburg arrive, keep 50 of them with you for the printing labours, and see that suitable paper is bought in good time for some part of them. I will send more when need be. The rest, direct hither through your kinsman at Dresden. And again, farewell; and greet Doctor Jessenius — though forgetful of me — and the rest of my Wittenberg friends, among them also that silent Taubmann.

Given in haste at Prague, the 8th day of October, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Melchior Jöstel (1559–1611), Wittenberg professor of mathematics and medicine, Tycho's friend — at this moment "the chief candidate among six" for the medical doctorate at Wittenberg (peracta promotione). This is the personal companion to Tycho's same-week letter declining the doctoral ceremony, addressed to the Wittenberg Medical College (brahe-t_16001009001, 9 October 1600); the two share the instrument-installation report.
  • The instruments near Prague Castle (material history). Tycho is installing his instruments "up above, near the castle, in an ample house" so placed that Rudolf II can walk over alone from his garden to view them whenever he wishes — which he had lately done "not without pleasure," though only the Benátky-brought instruments were then in place; the rest (newly arrived in greater number) were still being erected. The Emperor wants Tycho no longer to reside far from Prague. This dovetails with brahe-t_16001009001 (transport from Benátky and Magdeburg via Leitmeritz/Litoměřice up the Elbe) and the Hamburg-transit letter (brahe-t_15990929001).
  • The court purge of autumn 1600. "That great and unexpected change at court": Wolfgang Rumpf (von Wielross, Rudolf II's chief steward) and Paul Sixt Trautson — praecipui Consilii secreti Capita, the chief heads of the Privy Council — were abruptly dismissed and ordered to leave within two days; the remaining councillors kept their seats but not their former favour. The Emperor summoned his brother Archduke Matthias by post; Matthias inspected Tycho's instruments the day before this letter, "taken with these arts." Tycho notes the cause of the upheaval "is not yet sufficiently clear." (Rumpf was Tycho's earlier court patron — cf. brahe-t_16000813001, where Tycho sends Rumpf his respects.)
  • The printing project & Mecklenburg money. Tycho funds Wittenberg printing from the Mecklenburg payments: when the 600 joachimsthalers from the duchy of Mecklenburg arrive, Jöstel is to keep 50 for the typographic labours and buy paper in advance; the rest to come via Jöstel's kinsman at Dresden. (Same debt-stream as the 10,000-thaler thread, here a tranche put to press-work.) M. Oswaldus = Master Oswald, evidently a Wittenberg agent coordinating with the Printer; not further identified (flagged).
  • Persons named.
  • Doctor Frantzius Iurisconsultus = Doctor Franz, a jurist, used as courier to "those parts" (Wittenberg/Saxony). Identity flag: in brahe-t_16000327002 the recipient "Wolfgang Franz" was identified as the Wittenberg theologian; this courier is styled a jurisconsultus. Whether they are the same man (then the earlier "theologian" needs correction) or two different Franzes is unresolved.
  • D. D. Jessenius = Johannes Jessenius (the Wittenberg anatomist), "forgetful of me" — echoing brahe-t_16000327002, where Jessenius had not visited; Taubmannus = Friedrich Taubmann, the Wittenberg poet (cf. brahe-t_16000305001), "that silent one" (no longer writing).
  • Archidux Matthias = Archduke Matthias (later Emperor); Caesar = Rudolf II.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 8 Oct. stylo novo — New Style, Prague/imperial. Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Corrected the Latin: "serenificus" restored to Dreyer's "serenissimus" (TBOO VIII p.381).

- text error (low). The transcribed original reads the nonsense word 'serenificus' where Dreyer's page clearly prints 'serenissimus' (most serene), the standard honorific for an archduke used elsewhere in the letter. The English translator supplied the correct sense regardless, so the published translation is unaffected, but the transcribed original itself departs from Dreyer. Dreyer prints: Fuit quoque dictus ferenißimus Archidux heri in domo illa (TBOO VIII p.381).

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.374-383; the relevant sentence is on p.381, line 35, clearly reading "fereniβimus Archidux" (serenissimus). Only the Latin word required correction; the published English translation already carried the correct sense and needed no change. No other defects found across the ten pages.
