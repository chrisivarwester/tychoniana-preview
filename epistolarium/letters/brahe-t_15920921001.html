The original

ILLVSTRISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO, DOMINO MAVRITIO, LANDTGRAVIO HASSIÆ, COMITI IN CATZENELLEBOGEN, DIETZ, ZIGENHAIN ET NIDDA: PRINCIPI AC DOMINO CLEMENTISSIMO. S. D.

ILLVSTRISSIME et Generosissime Princeps: Plurimâ equidem T. C. me læticiâ affecit, dum non modo Literis suis clementer nostris respondere: Sed et Carmen commendatitium in Opera, quæ molimur, Astronomica, scite et eleganter concinnare, proprijsque digitis circa frontispicia chartæ transmissæ consignare, non dedignata sit. Pro qua etiam clementi erga me, meaque studia affectione gratias T. C. demisse ago, quas possum, maximas. Exhibebunt utique versus illi clarissimum T. C. erga tam sublimes Artes et me etiam favoris et benevolentiæ Τεκμήριον, Posteritati in auspicijs meorum Operum Astronomicorum iugi memoria consecrandum: mihi vero devotæ erga T. C. observantiæ μνημοσὺνον nulla oblivione interiturum suppeditabit. Cur autem non antea T. C. hoc nomine rescripserim, et quare Tabellarium hunc tam diu hîc detinuerim, caussas in Literis ad Illustrissimum T. C. Dominum Parentem datis, luculenter exposui. Quin et antea idem per Præstantis. et Eruditis. Virum Doctorem NICOLAVM THEOPHILVM, nunc in Academia Hafniensi Iuris Professorem, olim T. C. Parenti in Cancelliæ negotijs operam nauantem, indicare animus erat: Quo etiam nomine ad ipsum perscripseram. Verum intellexi postea, ob aduersam valetudinem iter eius, quod in HASSIAM adornârat, præpeditum fuisse. Præter voluntatem igitur meam accidit, quod adeo tarde tam Illustrissimi Parentis, quam T. C. Literis nunc respondeatur. Quam moram ut C. T. clementi animo in potiorem partem accipiat, atque excusatam habere velit, etiam atque etiam submisse rogo. Nec ulterius T. C. impolitis hisce meis literulis, alijs forte intentam, gravabo. Verum C. T. Deo Opt. Maximo, quam plurimum commendatam cupio. Is C. T. vitam et actiones in vestigia Paternarum virtutum dirigat, et ad Reipub. emolumentum feliciter disponat.

Vale.

Datæ Vraniburgj, 21. Septemb. Anno 1592.

Celsit. T. Addictis.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Illustrious Prince and Lord, Lord Moritz, Landgrave of Hesse, Count in Catzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda: a Most Clement Prince and Lord. [He] sends greeting.

Most Illustrious and Most Noble Prince: Your Highness has indeed filled me with the greatest joy, in that you have not disdained not only graciously to reply to my letters, but also skilfully and elegantly to compose a commendatory poem upon the astronomical works which we are undertaking, and to sign it with your own hand about the title-page of the sheet sent [to me]. For this clement regard toward me and toward my studies I humbly render Your Highness the greatest thanks I can. Those verses will assuredly furnish, to posterity, a token [Τεκμήριον] — to be consecrated in lasting remembrance at the very outset of my astronomical works — of Your Highness's most illustrious favour and goodwill toward such lofty arts and toward me as well; and to me, in truth, they will supply a memorial [μνημοσὺνον] of my devoted respect toward Your Highness, one that shall perish by no forgetting. But why I did not write back to Your Highness on this account before now, and why I have kept this courier here so long, I have set forth clearly in the letter given to Your Highness's Most Illustrious Lord Father. Indeed, before this, it had been my mind to make the same known through the most excellent and most learned man, Doctor Nicolaus Theophilus — now Professor of Law in the University of Copenhagen, formerly busying himself in chancery affairs for Your Highness's Father — and on that account I had written to him as well. But I learned afterward that, owing to ill health, his journey, which he had arranged into Hesse, had been hindered. It has therefore happened against my will that a reply is being made only now, and so tardily, both to the Most Illustrious Father's and to Your Highness's letters. That Your Highness may take this delay, with a clement mind, in the better part, and be willing to hold it excused, I most submissively beg again and again. Nor shall I burden Your Highness any further with these unpolished little letters of mine, your attention being perhaps bent on other things. But I desire that Your Highness be most fully commended to God, Best and Greatest. May He direct Your Highness's life and deeds into the footsteps of your Father's virtues, and dispose them happily for the good of the commonwealth.

Farewell.

Given at Uraniborg, 21 September, the year 1592.

Devoted to Your Highness,
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven. The letter was later printed by Tycho himself in his Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus (Uraniborg, 1596), the volume that is the edition of record here; Dreyer reprints it in Opera Omnia VI.
  • Recipient. Mauritio, Landtgravio Hassiae = Moritz "the Learned," Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632), who had succeeded his father Wilhelm IV on the latter's death on 25 August 1592. The salutation's territorial style — Comiti in Catzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain et Nidda — is the standard Hessian comital titulature; place-names kept in their period form with modern spellings given (Katzenelnbogen, Diez, Ziegenhain, Nidda).
  • "Dominum Parentem" / "Paternarum virtutum." The letter twice invokes Moritz's father. This is the diplomatic crux of the dating (see Calendar / crux below): Tycho writes as though Wilhelm IV were still living — he says he had set out his reasons "in the letter given to Your Highness's Most Illustrious Lord Father," and closes praying that Moritz follow "the footsteps of your Father's virtues."
  • Carmen commendatitium … proprijsque digitis … consignare. Moritz had written a commendatory poem for Tycho's projected astronomical works and signed it in his own hand about the title-page (frontispicia) of the sheet sent to Tycho. Carmen commendatitium rendered "commendatory poem"; frontispicia = the title-page / frontispiece region of the sheet.
  • Opera … Astronomica, quae molimur. "the astronomical works which we are undertaking" — Tycho's planned publications (the instauratio astronomiae programme); Opera kept as "works," not narrowed to a single title.
  • Greek. Τεκμήριον (tekmḗrion) = "a sure token, proof, attestation"; rendered "a token" (of favour). μνημοσὺνον (printed thus; classical μνημόσυνον, mnēmósynon) = "a memorial, remembrance, keepsake"; rendered "a memorial." Both Greek nouns are transcribed in the source block as printed and glossed here by the edition's convention The accentuation μνημοσὺνον (grave on the antepenult-area) is the 1596 print's own; not normalised.
  • Nicolaus Theophilus, nunc in Academia Hafniensi Iuris Professorem, olim T. C. Parenti in Cancelliae negotijs operam nauantem. The Latinised Nicolaus Theophilus, then Professor of Law at the University of Copenhagen and formerly active in the chancery service of Moritz's father (Wilhelm IV). Tycho had meant to route his reply through him, but Theophilus's planned journey into Hesse was stopped by ill health. Crux (identity): the precise Danish/vernacular form of "Nicolaus Theophilus" is not securely established from the present text alone; the man is identified only by office and prior service and is so left, rather than asserting a vernacular name the source does not give. Cancelliae is the print's spelling for Cancellariae ("chancery"); operam nauantem = navantem, "devoting effort, busying himself."
  • Tabellarium hunc … detinuerim. Tabellarius = courier / letter-carrier; Tycho apologises for having detained Moritz's courier so long at Hven.
  • Negation / sense check. non modo … respondere, sed et … concinnare … non dedignata sit = "you have not disdained not only to reply … but also to compose …" — the two negatives belong to distinct clauses (non modo "not only" + non dedignata sit "did not disdain"); the net force is affirmative courtesy ("you graciously deigned to do both"), and is so rendered, with no negation added or dropped. nulla oblivione interiturum = "destined to perish by no forgetting," i.e. that will never be forgotten — the single litotes is preserved as the emphatic positive it is, not flattened to a bare "lasting."
  • Honorifics. S. D. = salutem dicit, "sends greeting". T. C. / C. T. = Tua Celsitudo / Celsitudo Tua, "Your Highness," used interchangeably through the letter; rendered uniformly "Your Highness." Celsit. T. Addictis. (subscription) = Celsitudini Tuae Addictissimus, "Most devoted to Your Highness." Deo Opt. Maximo = "to God, Best and Greatest."
  • Orthography (Neo-Latin print, 1596). laeticiâ for laetitiâ; caussas for causas; proprijsque, negotijs, alijs, auspicijs with consonantal -ij-; adornârat = adornaverat (contracted); Reipub. = Reipublicae; Vraniburgj / Datae for Uraniburgi / Datae. e-caudata printed as HASSIAe / LANDTGRAVIO HASSIAe retained.
  • Textual note (extraction artifact). The working transcription carries a stray capital "A" interpolated at numerous word-boundaries (e.g. LANDTGRAVIOA HASSIAe, DOMINOA CLEMENTISSIMO, ExhibebuntA … illiA, ΤεκμήριονA, μνημοσὺνονA, VirumA, nauantemA, nuncA, cupioA, BRAHE.A). These are not letters of the source: they fall at points carrying no Latin sense and recur mechanically. They are read as an digitised text layer/encoding artifact (most likely Dreyer's apparatus reference-marks or a footnote-anchor glyph mis-decoded as "A") and have been removed from the Source block to give the readable Latin; the decision is logged here by the edition's convention rather than reproducing the noise as printed. None affects the sense of any clause.
  • Calendar / crux. Dateline Datae Vraniburgj, 21. Septemb. Anno 1592 — 21 September 1592 (O.S.); Denmark and Hesse were both still on the Julian calendar. The catalogue label gives the same date, so no header/label conflict. The substantive crux is the addressee-vs-content tension: Wilhelm IV had died on 25 August 1592 (O.S.), four weeks before this dateline, yet Tycho addresses the new Landgrave Moritz while speaking of Wilhelm as a living "Lord Father" to whom he had written and whose footsteps he prays Moritz will follow. The likeliest reading is that news of Wilhelm's death had not yet reached Hven by 21 September — Tycho already knew Moritz as a correspondent (the commendatory poem) but not yet that he had succeeded — and that the salutation to "Landgrave Moritz" reflects Moritz's standing as the verse's author rather than knowledge of the succession. This is flagged, not resolved; the date is reproduced exactly as written and is not "corrected."
