The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD TAUBMANNUM.
Num, TAUBMANE, tibi vox rauca in faucibus haeret?
Piscibus et stupidis mutior esne feris?
Forte Poëtarum quia mos est fingere quidvis,
Nos etiam vanis ludis imaginibus.
At praestare fidem vanos quoque Iura Poëtas,
Et rata pollicitis debita inesse iubent.
Solve igitur, quae iure mihi tu Carmina debes,
Falsaque (cum nequeas vera referre) refer.

EN habes hîc antesignani vestri tuique plasmatoris MELISSI (qui tamen raucos et mutos Poëtas creavit nunquam) Carmina nuper ad me missa, ubi nonnunquam dissimilis a seipso videtur. Adest etiam Doctoris cuiusdam Ingolstadiensis Heroicum, quod per Cancellarium Bavaricum, Dominum IOHANNEM GEORGIUM HERWARTUM ab HOHENBURG (cum quo mihi creberrima, ob studia, quibus impense afficitur, Mathematica, intercedit per literas familiaritas) hesterna vespera Monachio accepi: Tu lege, dispice, cognosce, et loquela recuperata, alio genere supple; ni mature parueris, Iambos in te vibrabo, et ex muto sordoque caecum, imo truncum te reddam, aut in saxum convertam, tanquam alter PERSEUS capite Gorgonio te petens. Sed prius Caducaei usum tibi adimam, et per MELISSUM tuum degradari faciam, alias enim nefas foret, sacros vates castigare et merita poena afficere.

Tuque vale, et tenerum cum matre valere columbum
Effice, sic vobis pullulet alma domus,
TYCHONISque memor, vates qui suspicit, esto,
Sique voles, carmen non breve mitte brevi.
Deproperabam Pragâ die 20 Septembr. stylo reformato Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Possible

Tycho Brahe to Taubmann.

Is your voice, Taubmann, stuck hoarse in your throat?
Are you more mute than fishes and dull beasts?
Perhaps, because the poets' way is to feign whatever they please,
you make sport of us, too, with empty images.
But the Laws bid that even vain poets keep faith,
and that due, ratified obligations inhere in promises.
Pay up, then, the Poems which you owe me by right,
and render falsehoods (since you cannot render truths).

Behold, here you have the Poems — lately sent to me — of your party's standard-bearer and your own moulder, Melissus (who, however, never created hoarse and mute Poets), in which now and again he seems unlike himself. There is also at hand the Heroic [poem] of a certain Doctor of Ingolstadt, which I received yesterday evening from Munich, through the Bavarian Chancellor, Master Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (with whom I have a most frequent familiarity by letters, on account of the Mathematical studies to which he is keenly devoted). You — read, examine, take note; and, your speech recovered, make good [your debt] with another piece. Unless you obey in good time, I will brandish Iambics against you, and from mute and deaf will render you blind — nay, maimed — or turn you into stone, like a second Perseus attacking you with the Gorgon's head. But first I will take from you the use of the Caduceus, and have you degraded through your own Melissus; for otherwise it would be impious to chastise sacred bards and visit them with deserved punishment.

And fare you well; and make the tender dove fare well, with its mother;
so may a nurturing house teem [with young] for you;
and be mindful of Tycho, who looks up to the bards;
and, if you will, send before long a poem — not a short one.
I hurried [this] off from Prague, on the 20th day of September, reformed style, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Friedrich Taubmann (1565–1613), professor of poetry at Wittenberg, the celebrated humanist wit and Latin poet, protégé of Melissus. A playful verse-and-prose letter mock-scolding Taubmann for failing to send a promised poem, in the bantering register of the late-humanist poetae; the threats (Perseus, the Gorgon, the Caduceus, "Iambics") are jocular.
  • Persons. antesignanus vester … plasmator Melissus = Paul Schede Melissus (1539–1602), the doyen ("standard-bearer" and "moulder") of the German neo-Latin poets, whose own Carmina on Tycho's affairs Tycho encloses (cf. brahe-t_16001116003). Doctor Ingolstadiensis = an unnamed Ingolstadt doctor whose Heroicum (heroic-verse poem) Herwart had just forwarded. Cancellarius Bavaricus … Herwart ab Hohenburg = Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (the Munich correspondent of the whole 1600 series).
  • Mythological play. Iambos vibrabo — "I'll brandish iambics" (the invective metre of Archilochus). alter Perseus capite Gorgonio — Tycho jokingly threatens to turn Taubmann to stone with the Gorgon's (Medusa's) head. Caducei usum tibi adimam … per Melissum tuum degradari faciam — "I'll strip you of the Caduceus [Mercury's herald-staff, the poet's emblem] and have you degraded through your Melissus" — i.e. drummed out of the poets' guild; sacros vates ("sacred bards") is the conventional dignity of poets.
  • Closing verse. tenerum cum matre columbum — "the tender dove with its mother": an affectionate domestic figure (Taubmann's wife and child / household); vobis pullulet alma domus — a wish for a fruitful household. The whole sealed by the demand for "a poem — not a short one."
  • ⚠ Calendar / dateline. The printed dateline Pragâ die Septembr. stylo reformato Anno 1600 gives no day-number ("on the … day of September"); the day 20 is supplied from the edition (identifier …0920… / label "20. september n.s."). stylo reformato = "reformed style" = New Style (Prague, imperial). Header dates 20 September 1600 [N.S.], the day-number flagged as editorially supplied.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: the day "20" is not merely editorial — Dreyer (Opera Omnia VIII, p. 365; running-head 10/20 Sept.) prints die 20 Septembr. Restored to the Source dateline; the "supplied from the edition" qualification is withdrawn.

This looks like a one-letter corruption in the transcribed original ('sordo' for 'surdo'), silently corrected by the translator rather than flagged.
