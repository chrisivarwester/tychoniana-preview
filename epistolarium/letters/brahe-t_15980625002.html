The original

NOBILISSIMO NOBIS SINGULARITER DILECTO TYCHONI BRAHE, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP.

ERNESTUS electus et confirmatus archiepiscopus Coloniensis, sacri Romani imperij per Italiam archicancellarius et princeps elector, episcopus Leodiensis, palatinus Rheny, dux utriusque Bauarie. Clementi Nostra salutatione premissa. Nobilissime singulariter dilecte, quas mihi iuuenis ille notus a te literas dedit et quæ de rebus tuis addidit, singulari lætitia legi et audiui. Munus vero litterarium librorum tuorum, quos ut a te rerum istarum peritissimo profectos et de cœlestibus illis corporibus tractantes, quorum vel solus aspectus idiotarum oculos pascit, cognitio uero et meum et omnium peritorum animos honestiore uoluptate moratur, mihi supra modum fuit acceptum. Gratias pro ijs tibi ago et quod factum a me hactenus non indiligenter, pergam sedulo te tuaque cæs: mti: commendare. Declaraui hunc animi mei sensum latori literarum tuarum et harum relatori. Ex eo intelliges, quæ de omnibus, quæ scire cupiebas, illum feci certiorem. Vale et tibi persuade et tua et studiorum caussa me quiduis conaturum.

Leody xxv iunij 1598.
Ernestus, mpp.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble, singularly beloved by us, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup.

Ernst, elected and confirmed Archbishop of Cologne, Arch-Chancellor of the Holy Roman Empire throughout Italy and Prince-Elector, Bishop of Liège, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of both Bavarias. Our clement greeting premised. Most noble, singularly beloved: the letters which that known youth gave me from you, and the things he added about your affairs, I read and heard with singular gladness. But the literary gift of your books — which, as proceeding from you, most skilled in those matters, and as treating of those celestial bodies whose very sight alone feeds the eyes of the unlearned, but whose knowledge engages both my mind and the minds of all the skilled with a more honourable pleasure — was beyond measure acceptable to me. I give you thanks for them; and, as has been done by me hitherto not undiligently, I will continue diligently to commend you and your affairs to His Imperial Majesty. I have declared this sentiment of my mind to the bearer of your letters and the reporter of these [present ones]. From him you will understand the things whereof I have made him more certain, concerning all that you desired to know. Farewell; and persuade yourself that, both for your sake and for the sake of [your] studies, I will attempt anything.

Liège, the 25th of June, 1598.
Ernst, with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Ernst of Bavaria (1554–1612), Wittelsbach prince, Elector and Archbishop of Cologne (from 1583), also Prince-Bishop of Liège (episcopus Leodiensis; Leodium / Leody = Liège), Münster, Hildesheim and Freising — here writing from Liège. The full electoral titulature (Arch-Chancellor of the Empire throughout Italy; Count Palatine of the Rhine; Duke of both Bavarias) is reproduced.
  • Occasion. Tycho had sent Ernst a gift of his books (the literary gift, munus litterarium — his recently printed works, e.g. the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata / Mechanica / the Epistolae astronomicae) and a verbal report of his affairs through a young messenger. Ernst thanks him warmly and — crucially — promises to continue commending Tycho to His Imperial Majesty (Rudolf II): this is part of the network of princely intercessions (cf. the Cologne, Hesse, and Hamburg channels) by which Tycho secured his Bohemian/imperial position in 1598–99.
  • Sentiment. The fine antithesis quorum vel solus aspectus idiotarum oculos pascit, cognitio vero … honestiore voluptate moratur — "whose mere sight feeds the eyes of the unlearned [idiotae = laymen, non-specialists], but whose knowledge engages the learned with a more honourable pleasure" — distinguishes the vulgar gaze at the heavens from the philosopher's understanding. idiotae rendered "the unlearned," not "idiots."
  • mpp = manu propria (with his own hand).
  • Calendar. Leody xxv iunij 1598 = 25 June 1598; Cologne/Liège (Catholic, and an electoral chancery) used the Gregorian / New Style calendar (adopted in the Catholic Rhineland from 1583). Taken as N.S.; header so marked.
