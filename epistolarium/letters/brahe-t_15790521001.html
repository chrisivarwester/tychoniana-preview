The original

A. WELLEIO CONCIONATORI REGIS. SALUTEM IN SALVATORE.

ACCEPI literas tuas, dilecte M. ANDREA, et de equo minus bene vidente D. I. FRANCISCI (cæcum ne appellem, multis deierationibus absterremur) intellexi: qui [quoniam] est vir benevolus et nobis Amicus, eum non denegat sed sponte offert sive venalem, sive commutabilem; ego ubi artificium molæ nostræ, in quo eo uti vellem, paratum fuerit, cum eo de hoc agam, si proprijs equis uti non licuerit, eo quod eo dono destituantur, quo suum etiam carere mallet. Agas itaque ipsi interea meo nomine gratias. De istius vafri hominis perfidia, qui sponte oblatam reconciliationem callide aversatur, ut impune sibi per Legati adventum famam meam læsisse consultum sit, nihil dicam, donec vir ille opt. et æquus adveneri, cuius etiam arbitrio primum stabo, an æquum sit me ista immerito ab eo pati et per civitatem ab ijs, quibus nihil mali volui, sed ab his passus sum, defamari. Quid Hessus iste dicat et quam gratiam referat, nihil curo, cum non alia ab eo exspectârim unquam, neque ob id medicinam conficiam, quod vel gratiam vel lucrum inde sperem ullum, miror saltem eousque Christianismi institutionem pervenisse, ut Magistratus ipse gloriari audeat, a membris Satanæ opem, quam ferre nequeunt, allatam sibi esse, posthabitis Dei auxilio et ab ipso ordinatis medijs. O Doctores, o Discipuli. De Curia ista quæ ad nos pertinet in Angulo Civitatis Matri, ut consuluisti, scripsi, ab ea brevi responsum expecto, quod ubi nactus fuero, modo ex votis nostris contigerit, cum CHRISTIERNO URUP præsens, ubi Haffniam advenero, agam; effice interea apud ipsum, ne curiam suam prius alteri divendat. Controversiam, quæ inter Fratrem meum et BLESSIUM istum incidit, nondum a quoquam intellexi, felicior enim sum in hac Insula, quam quod plurimas mundanæ fabulæ nugas, hominumque Eccentricitates sæpius cum molestia exauditas doleam. Velim tamen ut me de isto negotio certiorem reddas, siquidem Fratris mei magnopere interesse ex te intelligo. Vale et significa quando Iohannes Patri redierit. Iterum vale et saluta Mag. LAURENTIUM Amicum communem meo nomine plurimum.

Datæ Huennæ 21. Maij Anno 79.
T.
TYCHO BRAHE.

Mitto tibi Catalogum librorum Francofurtensium et peto ut BALTHAZARO significari curare velis, ut eos libros quos
lineola in margine notavi, quamprimum licuerit, mihi procurare velit. Sunt
autem novem Latini, tres Germanici et unus Gallicus. Remitto tibi 8
Ioachimicos per hunc CHRISTIERNUM piscium mercatorem, pro quibus ago gratias.
Iterum vale.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To [Anders] Vedel, the King's preacher. Greeting in the Saviour.

I have received your letter, beloved Master Anders, and have learned about the not-well-seeing horse of Master J. Franciscus (we are deterred from calling it blind by his many oaths): since he is a kindly man and a friend to us, he does not refuse it but freely offers it, whether to be sold or to be exchanged. When the mechanism of our mill, in which I should wish to use it, is ready, I shall treat with him about this — if it shall not prove possible to use my own horses, on the ground that they are without that endowment which he too would prefer his own [horse] to lack. So in the meanwhile give him thanks in my name. Of the treachery of that crafty man, who cunningly turns away from a reconciliation freely offered him, so that he might have contrived to injure my good name with impunity through the coming of the Legate, I shall say nothing, until that excellent and fair-minded man shall arrive — to whose judgment, moreover, I shall first submit [the question] whether it be fair that I should undeservedly suffer these things from him, and be defamed throughout the city by those toward whom I willed no harm, yet at whose hands I have suffered it. What that Hessian says, and what thanks he returns, I care nothing — since I have never expected anything else from him; nor do I prepare medicine on the ground that I hope for any favour or gain from it. I marvel only that the practice of Christianity has come to such a pass that the Magistrate himself dares to boast that help, which they [the members of Satan] cannot give, has been brought to him by the members of Satan, while God's aid and the means ordained by Him are set aside. O [such] Doctors, O [such] Disciples! About that property which belongs to us in the Corner of the city, I have written to [my] Mother, as you advised; I expect a reply from her shortly, and when I have got it — provided it turn out according to our wishes — I shall deal with Christiern Urup in person, when I have come to Copenhagen. See to it meanwhile with him that he does not sell off his property to another beforehand. The dispute that has arisen between my Brother and that Blessius I have not yet learned of from anyone — for I am more fortunate on this Island than that I should have to grieve over the very many trifles of worldly tattle, and the Eccentricities of men, so often and so vexingly heard out. Yet I should like you to inform me about that matter, since I understand from you that it greatly concerns my Brother. Farewell, and let me know when Johannes shall have returned to [his] Father. Again, farewell, and greet warmly in my name Master Laurentius, our common friend.

Given at Hven, 21 May, the year [15]79.
T.
Tycho Brahe.

I send you the Catalogue of the Frankfurt books, and I ask that you see to it that Balthasar be informed, so that he may be willing to procure for me, as soon as it shall have been possible, those books which I have marked with a little line in the margin. There are nine in Latin, three in German, and one in French. I send back to you 8 Joachimsthalers through this Christiern, a fish-merchant, for which I give thanks. Farewell again.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, writing from his island of Hven (Huenna), where building works were under way. The addressee, A. Welleio / M. Andrea = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), the historian and royal chaplain, here styled Concionator Regis "the King's preacher" — Vedel was preacher at the royal chapel in Copenhagen Castle. A long-standing friend of Tycho's (cf. the 1571 Vedel letter in the pilot).
  • Salutation. SALUTEM IN SALVATORE — "Greeting in the Saviour," a devotional variant of the standard salutem, suited to writing to a clergyman.
  • Persons named.
  • D. I. FRANCISCI — "Master J. Franciscus," owner of the horse offered to Tycho; not further identified here. D. = Dominus, "Master".
  • Hessus iste — "that Hessian": rendered as an ethnic/by-name epithet for an adversary in the medical dispute (a man called or from Hesse(n)). Whether Hessus is a surname or "the Hessian" is not decidable from the text; kept literal and flagged (crux below).
  • CHRISTIERNO URUP — Christiern (Christen) Urup, a Danish nobleman, owner of an adjoining or desired property ("his property," curiam suam) that Tycho wishes to acquire.
  • BLESSIUM istum — "that Blessius," a party to a dispute with Tycho's brother; Latinised surname, not securely identified; kept as Blessius.
  • Fratrem meum / Fratris mei — "my Brother": one of Tycho's brothers (most likely Steen Brahe, the eldest surviving brother active in Danish affairs), not named in the letter; left unspecified as in the source.
  • Matri — "[my] Mother," Beate Bille, Tycho's mother (widow of Otte Brahe), whom Tycho must consult about the family property "in the Corner of the city."
  • Iohannes … Patri — "Johannes … to [his] Father": a young man (perhaps a student or servant) whose return is awaited; deliberately under-determined in the source ("Father" not further specified).
  • Mag. LAURENTIUM — "Master Laurentius," a common friend; Mag. = Magister (academic "Master,").
  • Legati adventum — "the coming of the Legate / envoy": an official whose arrival the "crafty man" hoped to exploit; not identified.
  • Editorial reading. qui [quoniam] est vir benevolus — the bracketed [quoniam] ("since") is an editorial supplement carried in the text of record (the relative qui is read as causal); kept in brackets in the Source block by this chapter's convention(2), and rendered "since" in the translation.
  • Technical / realia. artificium molae nostrae — "the mechanism (contrivance) of our mill": Tycho's mill on Hven (the water-/paper-mill complex he was constructing; the horse was wanted as draught/turning power for the mill works). Eccentricitates here is used figuratively, "[men's] eccentricities/oddities," not the astronomical eccentricitas of the glossary — the same Latin word in a colloquial sense; flagged so a consistency check does not mis-tag it.
  • Medicine / religious-polemic passage. Tycho defends his practice of preparing medicina (Paracelsian/spagyric remedies) against a churchman's charge that such cures come from "the members of Satan." The clause a membris Satanae opem, quam ferre nequeunt, allatam sibi esse, posthabitis Dei auxilio et ab ipso ordinatis medijs is reproduced with its full logic: the relative quam ferre nequeunt ("which they cannot give") is Tycho's sarcastic aside that the so-called "members of Satan" are precisely unable to furnish such help; posthabitis … medijs ("the … means being set aside," ablative absolute) marks what the Magistrate neglects. O Doctores, o Discipuli — an exclamatory lament ("O [what] Doctors, O [what] Disciples!"), kept as exclamation.
  • Negation check. Five operative negations in the body, all preserved one-for-one: caecum ne appellem ("lest I call it blind"); eum non denegat ("does not refuse it"); si proprijs equis uti non licuerit ("if it shall not prove possible to use my own horses"); quo suum etiam carere mallet — the verb carere ("to be without/lack") carries privative force, rendered "to lack / be without"; quibus nihil mali volui ("toward whom I willed no harm"); quam ferre nequeunt ("which they cannot give"); medicinam neque … conficiam … quod … sperem ("nor do I prepare medicine … on the ground that I hope …"); ne curiam suam prius alteri divendat ("that he does not sell off his property to another beforehand," negative purpose). None added or dropped.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Huennae 21. Maij Anno 79 = "Given at Hven, 21 May, [15]79" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The catalogue label agrees with the dateline (1579-05-21, Hven); no discrepancy. T. / TYCHO BRAHE — the subscription T. is Tycho's initial above his full name.

### Named cruxes
1. quo suum etiam carere mallet — the clause explaining why Tycho's own horses may not serve: literally "[the endowment] which he [Franciscus] would prefer his own [horse] too to be without." The referent of eo dono ("that endowment/gift") is unstated — most plausibly the quality of sight (i.e. Tycho's horses, unlike the half-blind offered horse, still have the sight that Franciscus would rather his own horse lacked, so that Franciscus is content to part with the defective animal), but the syntax admits other readings (a draught quality wanted for the mill). The text does not decide it; rendered close to the Latin and flagged rather than smoothed.
2. Hessus iste — surname vs. "the Hessian" undecided (see above).
3. donec vir ille opt. et aequus adveneri — the form adveneri is anomalous (for advenerit, future-perfect "shall have arrived"); read as such and so translated; the irregular spelling is left in the Source block.
- What this is. A business paragraph preceding the letter's close, showing Tycho using the twice-yearly Frankfurt book-fair catalogue as a live order form: he has marked, by hand, thirteen titles (nine Latin, three German, one French) he wants procured through an intermediary, and is settling an account of 8 Joachimsthalers (Joachimsthaler silver coins, the ancestor of the word "dollar") via a courier.
- Two named agents, unidentified in the works consulted. "Balthazaro" — presumably a bookseller or book-buying agent in Vedel's circle, tasked with placing the actual order at Frankfurt — and "Christiernum," described only as "piscium mercatorem" (a fish-merchant), who is here doubling as an informal courier of coin between Hven and Vedel. Neither is corroborated by name among the works consulted; both would repay a dedicated search in the Tychonis Brahe Opera Omnia correspondence apparatus or Danish merchant records.
- Context. Recipient Anders Sørensen Vedel was Tycho's long-time friend and the Danish royal historiographer; letters between them regularly mix scholarly, financial, and household business in one breath, as here.
- Coinage note. "Ioachimicos" = Joachimsthaler, the large silver coin minted from the Joachimsthal mines in Bohemia from 1520, whose name was later clipped to "thaler" and ultimately gave English "dollar" — retained untranslated as a proper coin-name.
