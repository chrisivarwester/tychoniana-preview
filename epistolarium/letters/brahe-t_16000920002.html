The original

ILLUSTRI ET MAGNIFICO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, ASTRONOMO FACILE NOSTRI TEMPORIS PRINCIPI, DOMINO ET PATRONO COLENDO.
PRAGAM IN BEHMEN.

ILLUSTRIS et magnifice Domine, patrone colende. Ut doleo priores meas tibi non redditas esse, ita laetor posteriores te accepisse: quibus quod prolixe et humanissime respondes, pergratum mihi est. De Elegia mea statues, utcunque libet, nihil me offendet, neque a me edetur, antequam tu volueris. De MELISSI carminibus plane tibi assentior. Alius quodammodo et quasi vulgaris videtur in istis carminibus: vel senectus hoc facit (ut ipse solet interdum queri) vel laboris fuga: certe isti odae omnis lima et intentio videtur deesse. Sed haec tibi in aurem dixerim, apud ipsum nolim proferri, qui me nuper etiam Elogio ornavit in carmine de obitu CHYTRÆI, quod mitto. Diligentissime egi cum CHYTRÆI filijs de Designatione ista tuae migrationis in Germaniam. Dixerunt se legisse et meminisse, quod patri reddita esset: sed quamvis accurate inquisita, reperta nondum est. Deinde etsi reperiatur, nobis tamen non liquet, quomodo ea possit in huius anni Chronicon regeri ad finem, ut vis, cum incurrat reipsa in praecedentem. Praeterea Chronicon illud iam editum est post mortem CHYTRÆI in id tempus, quousque ipse deduxit: nam ut quispiam Venerem imperfectam perficiat, huius anni extrema absolvat, fortasse nemo reperietur. Exemplum tibi mitto. Sed in tui gratiam facile recudi posset, nisi priora obstarent: et hoc quoque, quod iam haec Exemplaria in hominum manibus versentur. Nihil sane est, quod malim, quam tibi gratificari, sed tu ipse de his cogitabis et statues, et si quid in mentem venerit, nos tantum docebis. Locutus sum de hoc negocio etiam cum D. LUCA, qui suam sententiam sine dubio ipse perscribet.

De carmine ad Caesaream Maiestatem tali, quale innuis, quid fieri possit, dispiciam, quando otium nactus fuero. Modo versor in campo Eloquentiae et Intemperantiam ac sectatores eius vehementi oratione seu libro castigo. Erasmianum illud erit quiddam, ut spero. Sed haec ante dicens, in lente unguentum quaero.

Obijt nuper SIGISMUNDUS Megapolitaniae princeps, IOHANNIS ante defuncti (cuius duo filij supersunt) frater: corpus eius humabitur Suerini 10 Octobris stylo veteri. CAROLUS dux Sueciae cum magno exercitu et tota familia contulit se in Livoniae partes; quid moliturus et quo res evasura sit, videbimus. Transylvaniam periclitari audimus, et propter adventum Turcae et tyrannidem Walachi, de quibus ex illis locis nos incertiora audimus, vos, qui prope adestis, certa.

Irascor illi, qui effecit, ut suppressis literis meis VARADINUM nostrum nondum legeris. Habe vero iam etiam sero: quod tibi probatum iri omnino confido, magno equidem mihi labore constitit. Elegantius videbitur, si conferetur cum inepto quodam Silesij Carmine, qui idem argumentum susceperat ante, sed sine Minerva et Gratijs, adeoque nimis pueriliter. RHEDERUM audio id misisse ad Typographiam Lignicensem, ut recudatur.

M. CHRISTIANUS iam discessit in Daniam, cum ante mihi adfuisset. Tu ut diutissime valeas et in Germania nostra floreas, utque tibi diu etiam incolumis sit ille Caesar optimus et clementissimus: illud vero est, quod opto et voveo summopere.

Bene vale, vir magnifice.
Datae Rostochij 20 Septemb. Anno 1600.
T. Magnif.
reverenter colens
MARTINUS BRASCHIUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious and magnificent man, Lord Tycho Brahe, easily the prince of the astronomers of our time, Lord and patron to be revered. [To] Prague in Bohemia.

Illustrious and magnificent Lord, patron to be revered. As I grieve that my earlier letters were not delivered to you, so I rejoice that you received the later ones; and that you answer them at length and most courteously is most pleasing to me. Concerning my Elegy, you will decide however you please: nothing will offend me, nor will it be published by me before you wish it. Concerning Melissus's poems I plainly agree with you. He seems somehow different, and as it were commonplace, in those poems — either old age does this (as he himself is wont sometimes to complain) or the avoidance of labour; certainly that ode seems to lack all polish and concentration. But these things I would say in your ear; I should not wish them brought out to him, who lately even adorned me with a eulogy in his poem on the death of Chytraeus, which I send. I have dealt most diligently with Chytraeus's sons about that account of your migration into Germany. They said they had read it, and remembered that it had been delivered to their father; but, though carefully sought, it has not yet been found. And then, even if it be found, it is not clear to us how it could be inserted into this year's Chronicle at the end, as you wish, since it really falls into the preceding [year]. Besides, that Chronicle has already been published, after Chytraeus's death, up to the time to which he himself carried it down; for, that someone should complete an unfinished Venus, and finish off the last parts of this year — perhaps no one will be found. I send you a copy. But for your sake it could easily be reprinted, did not the earlier [matters] stand in the way — and this too, that these copies are already in men's hands. There is indeed nothing I would rather do than gratify you; but you yourself will think and decide about these things, and if anything come to mind, you have only to instruct us. I have spoken about this business also with Lord Lucas [Backmeister], who will doubtless write out his own opinion himself.

Concerning a poem to the Imperial Majesty, such as you hint at, I will consider what can be done, when I have got leisure. Just now I am engaged in the field of Eloquence, and chastise Intemperance and its followers with a vehement oration, or book; it will be something Erasmian, I hope. But, saying these things beforehand, I seek ointment among lentils [I lavish pains on a trifle].

Recently died Sigismund, prince of Mecklenburg, brother of the previously deceased Johann (whose two sons survive); his body will be buried at Schwerin on 10 October, Old Style. Charles, Duke of Sweden, with a great army and his whole household, has betaken himself into the parts of Livonia; what he intends, and how the matter will turn out, we shall see. We hear that Transylvania is in peril, both from the coming of the Turk and the tyranny of the Wallachian — about which we hear more uncertain [reports] from those places, [while] you, who are near, [hear] sure ones.

I am angry at the one who brought it about that — my letters being suppressed — you have not yet read our Varadinum. But have it now, even if late; which I am wholly confident will win your approval, for it cost me indeed great labour. It will seem the more elegant if compared with a certain inept Poem by a Silesian, who had taken up the same subject before, but without Minerva and the Graces, and so too childishly. I hear that Rhederus has sent it to the Liegnitz printing-house, to be reprinted.

Master Christen [Longomontanus] has now departed into Denmark, having before been with me. That you may fare most long, and flourish in our Germany, and that that best and most clement Emperor may long be safe for you — that, indeed, is what I wish and vow most greatly.

Fare well, magnificent man.
Given at Rostock, 20 September, the year 1600.
Reverently revering Your Magnificence,
Martin Brasch.

Apparatus

- Significance — Brasch's reply to Tycho's literary-critique letter. This is Martin Brasch's direct answer to Tycho's letter of 29 March 1600 (brahe-t_16000329004), responding point by point. Brasch (1565–1601), the Rostock humanist and Poeta laureatus, opens by noting that his earlier letters miscarried but the later reached Tycho, who answered "at length and most courteously."
- Point-by-point with letter 106.
1. The Elegy. Brasch accepts Tycho's verdict entirely: "you will decide however you please … nor will it be published by me before you wish" — yielding the suppress-pending-revision control Tycho had asked for.
2. Melissus's verses. Brasch "plainly agrees" with Tycho's low estimate of Paul Melissus's recent poems — "commonplace … either old age or the avoidance of labour … that ode lacks all polish and concentration" — but begs discretion (in aurem, "in your ear"), since Melissus had just honoured him with a eulogy in a poem on David Chytraeus's death (enclosed).
3. The Chytraeus migration-narrative. Brasch reports on the hunt for Tycho's Designatio of his migration into Germany (cf. brahe-t_16010415002, where Sturtz pursues the same): Chytraeus's sons recall it was delivered to their father but cannot find it; and even if found, it cannot be fitted into "this year's Chronicle" (it belongs to the previous year), which is already published to the point Chytraeus reached before dying — "for, that someone should complete an unfinished Venus [the topos of Apelles' Venus, left unfinished at his death and which none dared complete], perhaps no one will be found." He has consulted Lucas Backmeister (Superintendent of Rostock; cf. brahe-t_16001115002), who will write separately.
4. The imperial poem. Brasch will consider the carmen ad Caesaream Maiestatem Tycho had hinted at, when free; he is now writing an "Erasmian" oration against Intemperance.
- News (autumn 1600) — cross-links.
- Death of Sigismund of Mecklenburg, "brother of the previously deceased Johann (whose two sons survive)" — to be buried at Schwerin, 10 October O.S. This identifies the "younger princes of Mecklenburg" of Tycho's 10,000-thaler loan (brahe-t_16000331001, brahe-t_16001115002 etc.) as Duke Johann's two surviving sons.
- Charles of Sweden (later Charles IX) gone into Livonia with an army — the Polish-Swedish war (cf. Henniochus, brahe-t_16010321001).
- Transylvania imperilled by "the Turk and the tyranny of the Wallachian" [Michael the Brave] — the Long War (cf. Sturtz, brahe-t_16010415002); Brasch notes Tycho, being "near," has the surer reports.
- Brasch's own work. His poem Varadinum (on Várad / Oradea, the Transylvanian fortress), at last sent; he commends it against "a certain inept Silesian poem … without Minerva and the Graces" on the same subject, which Rhederus has sent to the Liegnitz (Legnica) press to be reprinted.
- Persons named. Martin Brasch (sender); Paul Melissus; David Chytraeus (and his sons); Lucas Backmeister; Sigismund and Johann of Mecklenburg; Charles, Duke of Sweden; the Wallachian (Michael the Brave); Rhederus; M. Christianus = Christen Sørensen Longomontanus, who "has now departed into Denmark" (cf. brahe-t_15981231001, where Tycho urged him to come — he had been with Brasch en route); Rudolf II ("that best and most clement Emperor").
- Allusions / proverbs (kept and glossed). Venerem imperfectam perficiat — the unfinished Venus of Apelles (none dared complete it); in lente unguentum quaero — "ointment among lentils," the classical figure for misplaced/lavished effort; sine Minerva et Gratijs — "without Minerva and the Graces," i.e. without skill or charm.
- Calendar. Dateline Rostochij 20 Septemb. Anno 1600; Lutheran Rostock kept the Julian (Old Style) calendar — confirmed by Brasch's own "10 Octobris stylo veteri" for the burial. Header marked [O.S.].
- Correction. Latin address heading corrected from 'PRAGAMIN BEHMEN.' to 'PRAGAM IN BEHMEN.' per Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.379; no change to the English, which already read correctly.
