English translation machine translation, Probable

To the most renowned and most learned man, Master Christoph Rothmann, distinguished mathematician of the Most Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, etc., his much-beloved friend — heartiest greeting.

I marvel, my most learned and beloved Rothmann, that since you departed from here you have sent me no letter at all, now that nearly a year and a half has slipped by. For I do not think that you were either so inhumanely received or dismissed by me that it would not have been fair, by way of remembrance, to share in turn a few words by writing, or at least to greet me through others and to keep me informed about your affairs. Your Most Illustrious Prince has written to me once and again, and has wondered where you were tarrying so long. But since I did not know in what places you were dwelling, I wrote about this matter to your brother Johannes Rothmann, who at length signified that you were stuck in your homeland in poor health. Yet since I suppose that you have meanwhile returned to your Prince, I was unwilling to refrain from addressing you with these [lines]. I ask, then, that you share with me — as to the whole course of the observation — the last eclipse of the Moon, if only it was accurately observed there. With us at that time the sky was not clear; we did at least diligently observe the Moon for two days, both before and after, when it was about the 90th degree of the ecliptic from the horizon, for that alone was granted [us]. And if it shall be possible to gather any other eclipses, whether [observed] at Kassel, or also carefully recorded by Maestlin or others, see to it that I obtain at least the bare observations of them written out, for sounding out and reconciling on this side and that the differences of meridians. I seek also the acronychal observations of Saturn around the years [15]76, [15]77, and [15]78, and of Mars [in] [15]76 and [15]78 — considerations which you will doubtless obtain from Maestlin by letter, provided he has attended to such things. About those 18 minutes (which, when you were here, I showed to be everywhere deficient in the meridian altitudes of the comet of the year 1580 observed there) I desire to be more certainly informed by you, as indeed you promised, [namely] by what occasion this very thing came about. I should like, furthermore, that you set out for me that method of most easily constructing canons of sines which is said to have been devised by your Justus Byrgius. For I do not sufficiently grasp whether it holds in reserve what it promises at first sight; and I ask that, both about these matters and about others, and about the state of your affairs, you write back to me fully by this very man, our bookseller, returning to us from the Frankfurt Fair.

Farewell.
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven, to Christoph Rothmann (Christophorus Rothmannus, c. 1550–after 1600), mathematician to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel. Rothmann had visited Hven in 1590; the letter opens by reproaching him for a year and a half of silence since that departure. The letter is one of the set Tycho himself printed in the 1596 Epistolae astronomicae (Liber I), which is the edition of record.
  • Persons named. IOHANNI ROTHMANNO = Johannes Rothmann, Christoph's brother. MESTLINO = Michael Maestlin (Maestlin, 1550–1631), then professor at Tübingen, Kepler's teacher; Tycho seeks his planetary and cometary observations through Rothmann. IVSTO BYRGIO vestro = Jost (Justus) Bürgi (Byrgius, 1552–1632), the Landgrave's clockmaker and instrument-maker at Kassel; "your Justus Byrgius" marks him as a member of the Kassel circle.
  • Place. Cassellis = at Kassel (the Hessen court observatory); Nundinae Francofurdianae = the Frankfurt Fair (book fair), via which the carrier bookseller travels.
  • Astronomy — technical. Eclipsin Lunae = the (last) lunar eclipse; Eclipses = eclipses; Meridianorum diuersitates … conciliandae = the differences of meridians (longitude differences between observing sites) to be reconciled — the rationale for collecting others' eclipse timings. circa 90. ab Horizonte Eclipticae gradum — "about the 90th degree of the ecliptic from the horizon," i.e. the point of the ecliptic culminating / highest above the horizon; rendered literally and not smoothed, as the phrasing is compressed. Acronychas … considerationes = acronychal observations (a body observed at acronychal rising, i.e. rising at sunset, in opposition to the Sun) of Saturn and Mars for the stated years. Altitudines meridianae Cometae Anni 1580 = the meridian altitudes of the comet of 1580.
  • The "18 minutes" crux of substance (not of reading). De 18. illis Minutis … vbique deficere — Tycho reports that the meridian altitudes of the 1580 comet observed "there" (at Kassel) were found to be deficient everywhere by 18 arc-minutes, a systematic error he had demonstrated to Rothmann in person, and about whose cause (quâ occasione) he now asks. The number, the systematic sense ("everywhere deficient"), and the open question of cause are preserved exactly; Tycho's own request to be "more certainly informed" is a genuine hedge and is kept.
  • Canones Sinuum / Bürgi's method. rationem … contexendi Canones Sinuum, quae a IVSTO BYRGIO … dicitur excogitata = "the method of constructing canons of sines said to have been devised by Justus Byrgius." This is the Kassel computational method (Bürgi's Kunstweg / sine-table technique), connected to the additive–subtractive prosthaphaeresis technique then current at Kassel; "canons of sines" is kept literal per the editorial glossary, and prosthaphaeresis is not introduced into the translation because Tycho does not use the word here. Tycho's skepticism is hedged — Neque enim satis assequor, an in recessu habeat id quod primâ fronte pollicetur ("I do not sufficiently grasp whether it holds in reserve what it promises at first sight") — and the hedge is preserved, not sharpened into a verdict.
  • Editorial sigla. The superscript A markers scattered through the text (sesquialtero<sup>A</sup>, locis<sup>A</sup>, existimem<sup>A</sup>, illîc<sup>A</sup>, sit<sup>A</sup>, solum<sup>A</sup>, 78.<sup>A</sup>, illic<sup>A</sup>, ipsum<sup>A</sup>, and after the signature) are footnote/variant-reading anchors carried over from the printed edition's apparatus, not part of the letter's own text. They are reproduced in the Source block as printed and ignored in the translation. The underlying readings adopted are the unmarked text as printed; no variant affects the sense of the translation.
  • Orthography. Edition spelling retained in the Source block: consonantal u for v and j for final i (Landtgrauij, alijs, literarum); vbi, vt, vltimam with v for initial u; hîc / illîc / quâ / primâ with the edition's circumflexes; AMIROR is the edition's printed form of admiror ("I marvel"), so read and translated.
  • Salutation / subscription. S. D. = salutem dicit, "(sends) heartiest greeting". Vale = "Farewell."
  • Calendar. The letter bears no dateline in the printed text supplied; the catalogue assigns 20 February 1592, Uraniborg, which (Denmark being on the Julian calendar until 1700) is Old Style. The header follows the catalogued date because the source text carries no internal dateline to lead with; this is flagged here rather than asserted as in-text.
