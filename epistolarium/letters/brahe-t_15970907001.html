The original

MAGNIFICO ET AMPLISSIMO VIRO, NOBILITATE GENERIS ET ERUDITIONIS ADMODUM CONSPICUO D:NO ERICO SPARRE, SER:MI SVECIÆ REGIS CANCELLARIO AC CONSILIARIO PRUDENTISSIMO, LIBERO BARONI IN N., COGNATO ATQVE AMICO SUO INPRIMIS HONORANDO.
S. P. D.

Literæ tuæ eruditissimæ pariter ac humanissimæ, cognate optime, amoris eximij erga me testes, ijs ipsis diebus, quibus Haffnia relicta in Germaniam migrandum erat mihi, redditæ sunt. Verum enimvero, tunc rebus meis ad abitum adornandis ita distrahebar, tum quoque amicis ob abitum invisentibus præpediebar, ut, licet nihil magis mihi in votis fuerit, attamen temporis iniquitas omnem scribendi occasionem præriperet. Postquam vero huc Rostochium appuli, atque hic certis de caussis, quemadmodum et antea Haffniæ per integrum anni quadrantem substiti (præsertim, quo nostratibus, si pænitere ipsos admissi erroris posset, deliberatius rem expendendi tempus concederem, neve nimis præpropere ardua, quæ tracto, studia alio transtulisse viderer), ne hic quidem aptior scribendi facultas, contra quam opinabar, oblata fuit, cum nullum iter ad confinia utriusque regni parantem nancisci liceret. Accessit et hoc, quod nescio quis rumor, utrum te inde in Sueciam interiorem vel Poloniam ad s:m vestrum regem te recepisses, incertum me redderet. Cumque eo nomine celeberrimum virum d. Davidem Chytræum, huius academiæ antesignanum, honorifica, prout virtus merebatur, nominis tui mentione compellassem, siquidem eum crebro de rebus regnorum illorum a multis edoceri scirem, et inter te ac ipsum alias commutari literas inaudivissem, attamen dubium, quod animo conceperam, eximere non potuit. Jtaque scribendi officium adhuc paulo diutius differendum censui, donec, ubinam locorum morareris, certo mihi innotesceret. Cum vero ne sic quidem profecerim, et longiores in hac urbe moras nectere, nec mihi commodum, nec etiam ob ingravescentem indies pestem consultum sit, propediem ad magnificum dominum Henricum Ranzovium, affinem et amicum meum singularem, perbenigne ab eodem invitatus, me conferre statui, isthic moraturus, donec rebus meis ulterius ex divino provisu prospexero. Quocirca aliquid ante meum hinc abitum literarum ad te omnino dandum ratus sum, ea spe fretus posse id ad communem utrique necessarium Gabrielem Sparre directum, ubicunque tandem locorum hæreres, commodum tibi transmitti.

Desiderium tui cognoscendi et compellandi, non saltem ob sanguinis coniunctionem, sed etiam ob suspiciendam virtutem ac eruditionem tuam pari rerum usu auctam me quoque iampridem incesserat, ut hoc votum explendi copiam sive in Dania sive alibi fata hactenus invidisse admodum moleste feram. Nunc vero cum magis magisque disiungamur et mihi patrium solum vertere ac alias in exteris regionibus commoditates quærere necessum fuerit, omnem spem alloquij tui mihi ereptam video et quam maxime doleo. Caussæ vero, quæ me huc adegerunt, tot tantæque fuerunt, ut calamo eas exprimere vix valeam, neque etiamnum tutum arbitrer. Qui magni viri haberi cupiunt, licet graviter nonnunquam impingant, tamen nec errores agnoscere volunt, nec in ijs redargui. Satis equidem mature hanc mutationem prævidi, nihilque prius habui, quam ut eam quovis pacto præcaverem et averterem, literis idcirco ad s:mi regis nostri cancellarium datis (quarum hic exemplum adiunxi), sed successu prorsus irrito. Nihil enim impetrare licuit, imo inter alia responsum, regi nostro quidquam sumptuum in jnstrumentorum meorum astronomiæ conservationem impendere integrum non esse. An vero ex regis ipsius voluntate vel alterius privato affectu idque eo prætextu promanaverit, non dixerim. Nec verbis eventus defuit. Nam non tantum feudo illo Norvagico, cuius in literis fit mentio, confestim post coronationem regiam peractam adempto contenti non fuerunt ij, qui fatis reipublicæ in Dania nunc præsunt; sed et reliqua omnia ab optimo rege Frederico l. m. in artis vere regiæ sustentationem deputata subtraxerunt, et simul senatorum regni literas, artis eius liberalem conservationem perpetuamque spondentes non respexerunt; licet quidam adeoque præcipuus eorum has una subsignarit et subscripserit; quin etiam canonicatum Roskyldensem (quem solum habui residuum) dictus cancellarius sibi acquirere ac in proprium commodum vertere non dubitavit. Tantum abest, ut artis adeo præclaræ et patriæ nostræ forte non inhonorificæ continuationem et perpetuationem pro virili (uti decuit) promovere voluerit. Scripsit autem non ita pridem illustrissimus dux Uldaricus, s:mi regis nostri avus, ad ipsum regem atque artis huius in integrum istinc restitutionem diligenter suasit. Quibus literis et meas addideram, etiamnum operam meam s:mo regi nostro et patriæ in perficienda isthic astronomiæ instauratione, quam tam diu tantisque conatibus molitus sum, per eas submisse offerens, modo id commodum exequi liceat et indemnitati meæ consulatur. Sin vero nihil his effectum fuerit, quod priora ac inconsulta quorundam consilia emendet (uti sperare vix licet, idque non tam ob ipsius s:mi regis nostri animum per se satis generosum et integrum, quam eorum, qui nunc soli quidvis pro arbitrio moderari volunt, aversam voluntatem), mox ad jmperatoriam ma:tem aliosque viciniores reges ac præcipuos Germaniæ principes scribere decrevi, et quid clementis eorum circa artis tam sublimis ulteriorem sustentationem voluntatis ac arbitrij sit, experiri. Vtrum videlicet quidpiam sumptuum ad hoc subministrare velint, quo ea in publicum reipublicæ literariæ emolumentum perfectior quam unquam antea, prodeat, atque orbi communicetur. Quo etiam nomine potentissimum vestrum regem Sigismundum submisse sollicitare proposui, si (uti spem concepi) ipsius m:tas, ut est erga ingenuas artes, cum et his ipsamet imbuta sit, benificentissima, hanc etiam curam et laudem cæteris addendam, suaque regia munificentia dignam censeat. Posset forte aliqua ex jnsulis Prussiæ vicinis commoditatem huic negotio præbere, vel id quocunque alio modo fieri, prout ipsius m:ti visum foret. Qua in re tuum prudens cum auxilio consilium, mi amicissime cognate, obnixe peto et expecto, an videlicet hoc studium astronomicum ipsius ma:ti offerendum, et an idem cum magno quoque cancellario communicandum putes; et utrum specifice jnsula Dalan prope Rigam, vel Bornhovium, aut jnsula Werden prope Dantiscum, similesve loci nominandi nec ne, vel quavis alia ratione quidpiam, quod utile foret, tentandum. Jdque eo audentius a te peto, quod in literis tuis, singulari benevolentia refertis, tale quid sponte offerre videris, dum officia mihi et studia tua, hoc præsertim tempore perbenigne ac amice addicis. Ego, si nihil aliud de me polliceri possum, hoc certe sponsum volo, me ex eorum numero, qui virtutes tuas et eruditionem maximi faciunt et colunt, si non primas tenentem, saltem nemini concedentem fore, et ipso ad minimum animo tua erga me officia pro virili compensaturum.

Vale mi charissime cognate et me (quod te facturum nihil ambigo) redamare perge.
Datæ Rostochij pridie eius diei, qua inde discedere constitui, 7:a nimirum septembris stylo veteri anni etc. 1597.
Tui studiosiss.
Tycho Brahe
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent and most worthy man, by nobility of birth and of learning altogether distinguished, Master Erik Sparre, Chancellor and most prudent Councillor to the Most Serene King of Sweden, free baron in N., his kinsman and friend especially to be honoured. [Tycho Brahe] sends heartiest greeting.

Your letter, most learned and likewise most kind, best of kinsmen, a witness of your singular love towards me, was delivered to me in those very days on which, Copenhagen left behind, I was to remove into Germany. But in truth I was then so distracted with setting my affairs in order for departure, and likewise so hindered by friends calling on me on account of that departure, that — although nothing was more in my prayers [than to write] — the unkindness of the time nevertheless snatched away every opportunity of writing. And after I had put in here at Rostock, and for certain reasons stopped here (as also earlier at Copenhagen) for a whole quarter of a year — chiefly that I might grant my countrymen a more deliberate time for weighing the matter, in case they could come to repent of the wrong committed, and that I might not seem to have transferred elsewhere too headlong the arduous studies I am engaged in — not even here was a fitter chance of writing offered to me, contrary to my expectation, since I could find no one preparing a journey to the borders of either kingdom. To this was added that some rumour or other left me uncertain whether you had withdrawn from there into the interior of Sweden or into Poland to your Most Serene King. And although on that account I had addressed that most celebrated man Master David Chytraeus, the foremost figure of this university, with honourable mention of your name (as your worth deserved) — since I knew him to be often informed by many about the affairs of those kingdoms, and had heard that letters were on other occasions exchanged between you and him — yet he could not remove the doubt I had conceived in my mind. And so I judged that the duty of writing must be put off a little longer still, until it should become certainly known to me in what place you were staying. But since I have made no progress even so, and since to weave longer delays in this city is neither convenient for me nor, on account of the plague growing heavier by the day, advisable, I have resolved very shortly to betake myself to the magnificent Master Henrik Rantzau, my kinsman by marriage and my especial friend, most kindly invited by him, intending to stay there until, by divine providence, I shall have provided further for my affairs. On which account I have thought I must by all means give you some letter before my departure hence, relying on this hope, that it may conveniently be transmitted to you, directed to Gabriel Sparre, the kinsman necessary to us both, wherever in the end you may be lodged.

A longing to know you and to address you had long since come upon me too — not merely on account of the bond of blood, but also on account of your virtue, to be looked up to, and your learning, augmented by a matching experience of affairs — so that I bear it most grievously that the fates have hitherto begrudged me the chance of fulfilling this wish, whether in Denmark or elsewhere. But now, when we are parted more and more, and when it has been necessary for me to turn from my native soil and seek other conveniences in foreign regions, I see every hope of conversing with you snatched away from me, and I grieve at it as much as may be. The causes, indeed, that have driven me hither were so many and so great that I can scarcely express them with the pen, nor do I even now judge it safe. Those who desire to be held great men, though they sometimes strike grievously against [the truth], yet are unwilling either to acknowledge their errors or to be refuted in them. Early enough, to be sure, did I foresee this change, and I held nothing more important than to guard against and avert it by any means whatsoever — letters being therefore sent to the Chancellor of our Most Serene King (of which I have appended a copy here) — but with an outcome entirely vain. For nothing could be obtained; nay, among other things [came] the reply that it was not open to our King to expend any costs upon the maintenance of my instruments of astronomy. But whether this issued from the King's own will, or from another's private feeling and under that pretext, I would not say. Nor was the deed wanting to the words. For not only were those who now preside over the destinies of the commonwealth in Denmark not content with that Norwegian fief — of which mention is made in the letters — being taken away immediately after the royal coronation had been carried through; but they also withdrew all the rest that had been assigned by that best of kings, Frederick of blessed memory, for the support of the truly royal art, and at the same time gave no regard to the letters of the senators of the realm, pledging the generous and perpetual maintenance of that art — although a certain one, and indeed the chief of them, had countersigned and subscribed these together [with the others]; nay more, the said Chancellor did not hesitate to acquire for himself the canonry of Roskilde (the only thing I had left remaining) and to turn it to his own profit. So far was he from being willing to promote with his whole strength (as was fitting) the continuation and perpetuation of an art so illustrious and perhaps not without honour to our fatherland. Not long ago, however, the Most Illustrious Duke Ulrich, grandfather of our Most Serene King, wrote to the King himself and diligently urged the full restoration of this art there. To which letter I had also added my own, even now through them submissively offering my service to our Most Serene King and to the fatherland in completing there the restoration of astronomy, which I have laboured at so long and with such great efforts — provided only it may be allowed to carry it out conveniently and that my own indemnity be cared for. But if nothing shall be effected by these [letters] to mend the earlier and ill-considered counsels of certain persons (as one may scarcely hope, and that not so much on account of the disposition of our Most Serene King himself, of itself sufficiently generous and upright, as on account of the averse will of those who now alone wish to manage everything at their own pleasure), I have resolved presently to write to His Imperial Majesty and to other nearer kings and to the chief princes of Germany, and to learn what their gracious will and pleasure may be concerning the further support of so sublime an art — namely, whether they are willing to furnish any costs towards this, that it may come forth more perfect than ever before for the public benefit of the republic of letters, and be communicated to the world. On which account, too, I have proposed humbly to solicit your most powerful King Sigismund — if (as I have conceived hope) His Majesty, being most beneficent towards the liberal arts, since he is himself imbued with them too, should judge that this care and praise also is to be added to the rest, and worthy of his royal munificence. Perhaps some one of the islands neighbouring Prussia might afford a convenient place for this business, or it might be done in whatever other way, as should seem good to His Majesty. In which matter I earnestly seek and await your prudent counsel together with your help, my most friendly kinsman — namely, whether you think this astronomical pursuit is to be offered to His Majesty, and whether the same is to be communicated also with the Grand Chancellor; and whether specifically the island of Dalan near Riga, or Bornholm [Bornhovium], or the island of Werden near Danzig, or similar places are to be named or not, or whether by some other plan something should be attempted that would be useful. And this I ask of you the more boldly because in your letter, filled with singular goodwill, you seem to offer such a thing of your own accord, when you most kindly and amicably pledge your services and devotion to me, especially at this time. As for me, if I can promise nothing else of myself, this at least I would have pledged: that of the number of those who esteem and cherish your virtues and learning most highly I shall be, if not holding the first place, at any rate yielding to none; and that, in mind at the very least, I shall requite with all my strength your services towards me.

Farewell, my dearest kinsman, and continue to love me in return (which I do not at all doubt you will do).
Given at Rostock, on the day before that on which I have determined to depart hence — namely on the 7th of September, Old Style, in the year, etc., 1597.
Most devoted to you,
Tycho Brahe
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Rostock during his self-imposed exile, three months after leaving Hven (he had departed Copenhagen for Rostock in mid-1597 and would move to Henrik Rantzau's castle at Wandsbek that October). The letter is a candid appeal to a Swedish kinsman for help in finding a new royal patron after the loss of his Danish support.
  • Recipient. Ericus Sparre = Erik Sparre (1550–1600), the learned Swedish High Chancellor and royalist statesman, partisan of King Sigismund III against the regent Duke Karl (later Charles IX). He had passed through Denmark in March 1597 fleeing Duke Karl, heading for Sigismund's court; he was executed in the Linköping Bloodbath of 1600. liber baro in N. — "free baron in N." — the place-name is abbreviated to a bare initial in the address (left as "N." in both source and translation).
  • Address / salutation. S. P. D. = salutem plurimam dicit, "sends heartiest greeting"; the sender's name is implicit in the formula and supplied in brackets.
  • cognatus — "kinsman." Tycho repeatedly addresses Sparre as cognate (the Brahe and Sparre families were related); kept consistently as "kinsman."
  • David Chytraeus — Davidem Chytraeum, huius academiae antesignanum = David Chytraeus (1530–1600), the eminent Lutheran theologian and historian, leading figure ("standard-bearer," antesignanus) of the University of Rostock, where Tycho was then residing.
  • Henrik Rantzau — Henricum Ranzovium, affinem et amicum meum singularem = Henrik Rantzau (Heinrich Rantzau, 1526–1598), Statthalter of the Danish king in Holstein, humanist and astrologer, Tycho's friend and kinsman by marriage (affinis), who invited Tycho to his Wandsbek castle near Hamburg.
  • Gabriel Sparre — a kinsman common to both correspondents (communem utrique necessarium), proposed as the intermediary for forwarding the letter; the Latin necessarium here in the legal/familial sense "close relation/connection," rendered "kinsman."
  • The "letters to the Chancellor of our King" and the appended copy. literis … ad s:mi regis nostri cancellarium datis (quarum hic exemplum adiunxi) — Tycho had petitioned Christian IV's chancellor and enclosed a copy with this letter. s:mi regis nostri (Most Serene King "of ours") = the Danish king, Christian IV, throughout; s:m vestrum regem / vestrum regem Sigismundum (King "of yours") = Sigismund III of Sweden and Poland. The possessives carefully distinguish Tycho's sovereign from Sparre's.
  • cancellarius / dictus cancellarius — the Danish chancellor is Christian Friis (of Borreby, 1556–1616), royal chancellor under Christian IV; by an executive order of June 1597 Tycho's Roskilde canonry was transferred from Tycho to Friis. canonicatum Roskyldensem (quem solum habui residuum) = "the canonry of Roskilde (the only thing I had left remaining)" — the prebend of the Three Holy Kings (the Magi) in Roskilde Cathedral, which Tycho had held until 1597.
  • feudo illo Norvagico … confestim post coronationem regiam peractam adempto — "that Norwegian fief … taken away immediately after the royal coronation had been carried through": Tycho's Norwegian fief of Nordfjord, withdrawn after Christian IV's coronation (29 August 1596). cuius in literis fit mentio — "of which mention is made in the [appended] letters."
  • optimo rege Frederico l. m. — "that best of kings, Frederick, of blessed memory": Frederik II (r. 1559–88), Tycho's original patron; l. m. = laudatae (or piae) memoriae, "of blessed/praiseworthy memory."
  • senatorum regni literas … licet quidam adeoque praecipuus eorum has una subsignarit — the council of the realm (Rigsråd) had pledged perpetual maintenance of Tycho's work in a signed letter; Tycho notes pointedly that one of the signatories — "and indeed the chief of them" (praecipuus eorum, i.e. the chancellor himself) — was among those now reneging. The barbed clause is kept intact.
  • Duke Ulrich — illustrissimus dux Uldaricus, s:mi regis nostri avus = Ulrich III, Duke of Mecklenburg-Güstrow (1527–1603), maternal grandfather (avus) of Christian IV (through Queen Sophie of Mecklenburg). He had written to the Danish king urging the full restoration (in integrum … restitutio) of Tycho's astronomical establishment.
  • Astronomy / Tycho's programme. astronomiae instaurationem … perficienda = "completing … the restoration of astronomy" (instauratio = Tycho's astronomia instaurata programme, the edition's convention); artis vere regiae / artis tam sublimis = "the truly royal art" / "so sublime an art" — astronomy. jnstrumentorum meorum astronomiae conservatio = "the maintenance of my instruments of astronomy" (his Uraniborg instruments, the edition's convention).
  • Proposed sites for a new observatory. jnsula Dalan prope Rigam — an island "Dalan" near Riga (probably Dalen / Dole (Dalholm), an island in the Daugava near Riga; identification not certain — see crux); Bornhovium — most plausibly Bornholm (the Danish/Baltic island), though the form is unusual; jnsula Werden prope Dantiscum — the island of Werder near Danzig (Gdańsk). All are weighed as possible locations under Sigismund's Baltic dominions. ex jnsulis Prussiae vicinis = "some one of the islands neighbouring Prussia."
  • imperatoriam ma:tem — "His Imperial Majesty": the Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II, to whom Tycho would in the event turn, becoming Imperial Mathematician in 1599.
  • magno … cancellario — "the Grand Chancellor": the Polish-Lithuanian Grand Chancellor (under Sigismund as King of Poland), distinct from the Swedish chancellor Sparre himself.
  • Orthography. The transcription preserves the edition's j for consonantal/final i (ijs, eximij, amicissimo → ijs, jnstrumentorum, jnsula, Jtaque, Jdque), the v/u conventions, and the abbreviations s:mi / ser:mi (serenissimi), ma:tem / m:tas / m:ti (maiestatem etc.), d:no (domino), l. m. (laudatae memoriae); these are expanded in the apparatus, not in the source block.
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Rostochij … 7:a nimirum septembris stylo veteri anni etc. 1597 = "Given at Rostock … on the 7th of September, Old Style, in the year, etc., 1597." Tycho explicitly flags the style (stylo veteri, O.S.) himself — characteristic of a Lutheran writing to a correspondent in the orbit of Catholic (New-Style) Poland. The as-written date (7 September 1597 O.S.) agrees with the catalogue label; no discrepancy. The N.S. equivalent would be 17 September 1597, but by the edition's convention it is not silently substituted. Tycho dates the letter "the day before that on which I have determined to depart" (pridie eius diei, qua inde discedere constitui) — i.e. he planned to leave Rostock on 8 September O.S.
