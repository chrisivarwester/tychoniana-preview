The original

COPIE AFF ITT BREFF, SOM BLEFF SCHREFFUEN THILL TYGE BRAHE.

VORES ganske venlig helßen nu oc allthid forsent med Gud. Gunstige welbiurdige Tyge Brahe, nest wores wenlig tacksigelße for all beuiste ære oc gode, giffuer wi eder thillkiende, att oß nu nyligen er forekommen en woris tienere, Lauge vdj Klare mølle, met klagemaall po en aff eders thienere ved naffn Peder, i Hellig 3 kongers mølle, huilched hand schrifftlig vdj dette medfølgendis breff forfatted oc oß offuerantuordet haffuer. So wilde wi, førend wi wores bonde met retten forsuare, lade eder, som wores universitetz gode wen oc fordrer, saadant wide, begierendis ganske gierne her om eders raad oc gode suar, om i selff wille straffe eders thienere, som denne fattige mand i so maade haffuer forwretted, eller wi hannem for saadant skall lade thilltale, huilcked wi dog icke gierne giorde, der som wi wiste, attj derudoffuer skulle lade eder i nogen mode fortørne, men megit heller giør allthid gierne, huer y sit sted, hues eder thill ære, willie oc thieniste kand werre. Dett kiende Gud, huilchen wi will her med haffue eder befaled.

Datum Hafniæ 11. novembris anni 1594.
Rector och mænige professores
academiae Hafniensis.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Copy of a letter that was written to Tyge Brahe.

Our wholly friendly greeting, now and always, sent in advance with God. Gracious, well-born Tyge Brahe: next after our friendly thanks for all the honour and kindness shown [us], we make known to you that there has lately come before us one of our servants, Lauge at Klare Mill, with a complaint against one of your servants, by name Peder, at the Mill of the Holy Three Kings, which [complaint] he has set down in writing in this accompanying letter and handed over to us. So we wished — before we defend our peasant at law — to let you, as our University's good friend and patron, know of such a matter, asking very gladly herein for your counsel and good answer: whether you yourself will punish your servant, who has in this manner wronged this poor man, or [whether] we shall have him prosecuted for such [conduct] — which, however, we would not gladly do, if we knew that thereby you should in any way be displeased; but [we] much rather do always gladly, each in his own place, whatever may be to your honour, goodwill, and service. That God knows, to whom we will herewith have commended you.

Given at Copenhagen, 11 November in the year 1594.
The Rector and the general body of professors
of the University of Copenhagen.

Apparatus

  • Senders. The Rector och maenige professores academiae Hafniensis = the Rector and the assembled professors of the University of Copenhagen (the Consistorium, the university's governing senate). Maenige (= menige) here is the chancery sense "common / general (body)," i.e. the full collegium, not "ordinary." The letter survives as the consistory's own register copy (hence the Danish rubric COPIE AFF ITT BREFF heading it).
  • Recipient. Tyge Brahe = Tycho Brahe, addressed as the university's gode wen oc fordrer ("good friend and patron / promoter"). Fordrer (= forfremmer / befordrer) carries the sense of one who advances or furthers the institution's interests — rendered "patron."
  • The dispute. A jurisdictional courtesy over a quarrel between two millers' households: Lauge vdj Klare mølle — Lauge (Laurids) at Klare Mill, a tenant ("servant"/bonde) of the university — has lodged a written complaint against Peder, i Hellig 3 kongers mølle — Peder at the Mill of the Holy Three Kings (Hellig 3 kongers mølle, the mill named for the Magi), one of Tycho's servants. The two mills lay near Copenhagen; the university defers to Tycho on whether he will discipline his own man before it takes the matter to law on its tenant's behalf.
  • Legal sense — handled with care. The operative offer is a strict either/or framed as a request for Tycho's raad oc gode suar: om i selff wille straffe eders thienere … eller wi hannem … skall lade thilltale = "whether you yourself will punish your servant … or [whether] we shall have him prosecuted." The single negation in the courtesy clause is reproduced exactly: huilcked wi dog icke gierne giorde, der som wi wiste, attj derudoffuer skulle lade eder … fortørne = "which, however, we would not gladly do, if we knew that thereby you should be displeased." The clause is a conditional courtesy (the university would rather Tycho act himself than risk offending him by prosecuting his servant); its mood is the past subjunctive/conditional of polite deference (wilde … giorde … wiste … skulle), rendered as such without sharpening into an assertion or a threat.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1594; German-influenced spelling). helßen = hilsen ("greeting"); forsent = forsendt ("sent ahead/forward"); welbiurdige = velbyrdige ("well-born," the standard address to nobility); nest = næst ("next [after]"); tacksigelße = taksigelse ("thanks"); beuiste = beviste ("shown, demonstrated"); thillkiende … giffuer = giver til kende ("make known"); forekommen = forekommet ("come before [us]"); klagemaall = klagemål ("complaint, plaint"); po = på ("against / upon"); huilched = hvilket ("which"); medfølgendis = medfølgende ("accompanying, enclosed"); forfatted = forfattet ("composed, set down"); offuerantuordet = overantvordet ("handed over, delivered"); forsuare = forsvare ("defend [at law]"); fordrer = forfremmer / befordrer ("patron, furtherer"); begierendis = begærende ("asking, desiring"); straffe = "punish"; forwretted = forurettet ("wronged"); lade thilltale = lade tiltale ("have prosecuted / proceeded against at law"); attj = at I ("that you," polite plural); derudoffuer = derudover ("thereby, on that account"); i nogen mode = i nogen måde ("in any way"); fortørne = fortørne ("anger, displease, offend"); hues = hvad/hvis ("whatever"); werre = være ("be"); kiende = kende ("know"); befaled = befalet ("commended, entrusted").
  • Calendar / place. Datum Hafniae 11. novembris anni 1594 — the dateline is in Latin: "Given at Copenhagen (Hafnia), 11 November in the year 1594" (O.S.; Denmark held to the Julian calendar until 1700). The Latin signature rubric Rector och maenige professores academiae Hafniensis code-switches Danish och maenige between the Latin Rector and academiae Hafniensis — the mixed register preserved by the edition's convention Dateline and catalogue label agree (11 November 1594); no date discrepancy.
