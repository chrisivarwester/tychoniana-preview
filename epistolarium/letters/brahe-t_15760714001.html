The original

GENEROSO ET VERE NOBILI VIRO TYCHONI BRAHE A KNUDSTORP, AMICO SUO SEMPER OBSERVANDO.

GENEROSE et clarissime vir, amice perpetuo colende, cum hîc Holbecæ apud Dominum PACKIUM essem, redditæ mihi sunt tuæ literæ. Tristia obmittam, cum corrigi non possint: lapis promissus paratus est, inscriptio addita, ut voluisti, cum me vocabis, mature adero, gratissimumque mihi feceris, si plures invitaveris! Ego idem quoque facturus sum, ne vulgaris hæc sit dedicatio. Utinam ille PRATENSIS viveret et nobis unâ adesse posset. Ego D. IOHANNI FRANCISCO nisi adfuerit, ominatus sum aliquid mali. Ille igitur volens (ut spero) vel metu aderit. Literas tuas hîc expecto, ubi iam iucundissime et suavissime vivo. Vale, Vir præstantissime, et DANZEUM tuum, ut incepisti, ama, credeque illum tibi esse deditissimum.

Vale et vale felicissime.
Holbecæ 14. Iulij A. 1576.
Tibi deditissimus Carolus Danzeus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the high-born and truly noble man Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, his ever-to-be-honoured friend.

High-born and most illustrious man, friend forever to be cherished: while I was here at Holbæk with Master Pack, your letter was delivered to me. The sad matters I shall pass over, since they cannot be put right. The promised stone is ready, the inscription added as you wished; when you summon me, I shall be there in good time, and you will do me a most welcome favour if you invite more people! I too shall do the same, so that this dedication may not be a common one. Would that the late Pratensis were living and could be present with us together. As for Master Iohannes Franciscus, unless he is present, I have foreboded some ill. He therefore — willingly (as I hope), or else out of fear — will attend. I await your letter here, where I am now living most agreeably and most pleasantly. Farewell, most excellent man, and love your Dançay, as you have begun [to do], and believe him to be most devoted to you.

Farewell, and most happily farewell.
At Holbæk, 14 July, the year 1576.
Most devoted to you,
Charles de Dançay.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Carolus Danzeus = Charles de Dançay (c. 1510–1589), the long-serving French ambassador (resident) at the Danish court of Frederik II. A humanist and a steady patron-friend of Tycho's from the early 1570s; he was among the small circle invited to the ceremonies attending the founding of Uraniborg. The address-form Tychoni Brahe a Knudstorp ("Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup," the family seat in Scania) is the standard noble style; Danzeum tuum ("your Dançay") is the writer's affectionate self-reference, rendered in the third person as in the Latin.
  • Place / occasion. Holbecae apud Dominum Packium — at Holbæk (the Zealand market town), staying apud Dominum Packium, "with Master Pack" — i.e. as the guest of a host surnamed Pack/Packe (the Latin genitive Packii gives a nominative Packius; the man is otherwise unidentified here and is left as named). The lapis … inscriptio addita … dedicatio ("the stone … the inscription added … this dedication") almost certainly concerns the foundation/dedication stone of Uraniborg, whose cornerstone was laid on Hven on 8 August 1576 (O.S.); Dançay reports the inscribed stone ready and pledges to attend the ceremony and to bring guests so that it "not be a common dedication." This reading is consistent with the date (mid-July 1576, weeks before the laying) but rests on the historical context rather than on any word in the letter; flagged accordingly. (Tycho's own commemorative verses for the Uraniborg cornerstone survive; whether the inscriptio here is that text or a separate dedicatory inscription the letter does not say — left open.)
  • Persons named.
  • Pratensis = Johannes Pratensis (Hans Philipsen, 1543–1576), professor of medicine at Copenhagen and Tycho's close physician-friend. Utinam ille Pratensis viveret ("would that the late Pratensis were living") shows him already dead at the time of writing — Pratensis died in 1576 — so the wish is for a departed friend; ille + the past-counterfactual subjunctive carry the sense "the late," made explicit in brackets-free idiom here ("the late Pratensis") and noted.
  • D. Iohannes Franciscus = "Master Iohannes Franciscus" — an associate (Italian first-name form Giovanni Francesco) expected at the same gathering; not further identified in the letter and left as named. The clause nisi adfuerit, ominatus sum aliquid mali … vel metu aderit is playful: "unless he attends I have foreboded some ill [for him]; so he will attend, willingly or out of fear." The threat is jocular, not literal.
  • Negations (checked). Two negative constructions: (1) cum corrigi non possint — "since they cannot be put right" (one non); (2) ne vulgaris haec sit dedicatio — the purpose-negative ne, "so that this dedication may not be a common one"; and the conditional nisi adfuerit — "unless he is present." Each is reproduced once; none added or dropped.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading GENEROSO ET VERE NOBILI VIRO … AMICO SUO SEMPER OBSERVANDO — the honorific superscription, rendered in full. Closing Vale et vale felicissime ("Farewell, and most happily farewell") and Tibi deditissimus ("Most devoted to you," parallel to Wolf's E. Tuae studiosiss.) by this chapter's convention
  • Calendar. Holbecae 14. Iulij A. 1576 = "At Holbæk, 14 July, the year 1576" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the edition label (1576. 14. juli. Holbaek); no discrepancy. (The edition's note that MS witness B is also the witness for the 1571 Wolf letter, cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66, reflects shared-codex foliation, not a dating issue.)
