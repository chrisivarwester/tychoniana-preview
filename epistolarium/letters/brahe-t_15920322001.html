The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD THADDÆUM HAGECIUM.

QUAMVIS non ita dudum sit, quod tibi scripserim, optime et amantissime mi THADDÆE, tamen et has superaddere non gravabor; amicorum enim est sæpius se invicem, si coram non licet, saltem literis invisere. Ut vero aliquatenus cognoscas, id quod superioribus indicavi, me hortatu et suasu magnifici Domini CURTII potissimum impulsum, Astronomiæ instaurationem, quam in opus integrum et peculiare reservare constitueram, nunc Progymnasmatis quibusdam Prodromi loco in publicum emittere, en habes operum meorum, quæ typis brevi, Deo volente, absolventur, quaternionem primum, eorum contenta sua inscriptione indicantem, ex qua cætera facile ratiocinabere; hincque colliges me idipsum, quod tunc literis pollicitus sum, nunc moliri. Addidi autem eiusdem quaternionis sena exempla, ut suppeterent, quæ hincinde ad Bibliopolas tum vestrates, tum etiam in Austria, Hungaria atque Polonia degentes mitteres, si forte aliqui illic essent, qui exemplaria nonnulla horum tomorum iusto pretio obtinere sategerint. Erunt autem quatuor, et quilibet eorum iustæ magnitudinis, ad semiquinque videlicet Alphabeta in hac Regali sic dicta (uti vides) papyro excrescens, suntque plurimis schematibus et tabulis hincinde referti. Ubi a te certior factus fuero, num aliquot exemplaria cupiant, deque numero et pretio, mittam hac instante æstate, favente Numine, quotquot expetierint, præsertim quantum duos priores tomos attinet. Expecto indies Privilegium regis Hispaniarum, Cæsareano subiungendum, quod ubi nactus fuero, publicationem non diutius differam.

Carmina, quæ hîc vides, in secunda pagina, non semper id spacij replebunt, sed ubi a serenissimo Scotorum Rege et illustrissimo Principe Landgravio iuniore MAURITIO, qui ambo eximia pollent vena poëtica, quædam in operum horum commendationem concinnata, prout confido, accepero, epigrammata illa hanc paginam honoris caussa occupabunt. Secundo autem tomo genere et autoritate nobilium virorum carmina pariter præfigam. Ubi scio quæ prædictus magnificus CURTIUS in gratiam communium studiorum concinnare non degravetur (nam ut Oratorem agit eloquentem et eximium, sic a Poësi quoque illum non abhorrere existimârim) ea Producis Holsatiæ Domini HENRICI RANZOVIJ versibus iam antea obtentis (quod et is hisce studijs non parce afficiatur) præponam, reliquis clarorum et doctorum virorum lusibus metricis in frontispicia duorum postremorum tomorum reservatis. Scripsi autem ipsemet proximis diebus serenissimo Scotorum Regi IACOBO Sexto et ipsius Cancellario IOANNI METELLO hac de re carmine elegiaco, tum quoque Epigramma quoddam literis ad illustrissimum Principem MAURITIUM Landgravium iuniorem hoc nomine datis subiunxi, quorum carminum descriptum hîc addidi, si forte talibus delectaris, et si plures nosti Principes, poëseos gnaros et horum studiorum amantes, indica eorum nomina unâ cum titulis, nam et ad eos pariter carminibus scribam, carminaque in Astronomiæ commendationem, uti spero, ab ipsis impetrabo. Conducibile enim et decorum sentio, hæc studia a viris Principibus atque in fastigio humanarum dignitatum collocatis commendari atque ornari, ut eo facilius alacriusque plures ad hæc per se ob sublimitatem difficultatemque, quodque minus lucrosa sint, alias abhorrentes, ad eorum culturam invitentur. Scis enim tritum illud: Principibus placuisse viris non ultima laus est, licet ego Philosophice et penitius rem introspiciendo aliter censuerim. Nolo te nunc pluribus obtundere, nec enim dubito, quin tam his quam alijs nostris desiderijs, quantum in te est, sis sponte profuturus. Ego ad tibi pariter gratificandum offero omnem operam et studium, adeoque me ipsum totum. Vale, et magnisicentissimum Dominum CURTIUM meis verbis honorifice saluta.

Iamque iterum bene vale.
Dat. 22 Martij Anno 92.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, pp. 333–334; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Intellexi ex Domini Doctoris SAGERI Lubecensis literis, nuper ad me datis, RHETICI Bibliothecam tibi fuisse in testamento deputatam. Quod si sic est, rogo, ut quicquid ad rem Astronomicam facit, sive id a præceptore suo COPERNICO, sive suapte industria habuerit, meis limitibus descriptum mature communicare velis. Et si eclipsin Lunae ultimo in fine præcedentis anni contingentem, caelitus illic denotastis, vel ullas alias, sive a te ipso sive aliunde conquisitas, in promptu habes vel nancisci poteris observationes, me quoque earum compotem per primam occasionem effice, quo Meridianorum discrepantiam hinc certius quam alias, adhibitis nostris vel observationibus vel numeris, rimari concedatur.

Tycho Brahe to Thaddaeus Hagecius.

Although it is not so very long since I wrote to you, my excellent and most loving Thaddaeus, yet I shall not begrudge adding these lines too; for it is the way of friends to visit one another the more often — if not in person, then at least by letter. But that you may to some degree learn what I intimated in earlier [letters] — that I, impelled chiefly by the exhortation and persuasion of the magnificent Master Curtius, am now bringing out before the public, in the guise of a Forerunner, in certain Progymnasmata, the restoration of astronomy which I had resolved to reserve for a complete and separate work — here you have the first quaternion of my works (which, God willing, will shortly be finished at the press), indicating its contents by its own title, from which you will easily reckon the rest; and from this you will gather that I am now setting in motion the very thing which I then promised you by letter. I have added, moreover, six copies of the same quaternion, so that there might be a supply for you to send out here and there to the booksellers — both those among you and those residing in Austria, Hungary, and Poland — if perchance there should be any there who would take pains to obtain some copies of these volumes at a fair price. They will be four [in number], and each of them of a just size, growing namely to four and a half [sic] alphabets [of sheets] in this so-called Royal paper (as you see); and they are filled throughout with very many diagrams and tables. As soon as I am made more certain by you whether they desire any copies, and about the number and the price, I shall send — this coming summer, the Deity favouring — as many as they shall have requested, especially as concerns the two earlier volumes. I am awaiting daily the Privilege of the King of the Spains, to be joined to the Imperial one; and as soon as I have obtained it, I shall no longer defer the publication.

The verses which you see here, on the second page, will not always fill that space; but as soon as I have received — from the most serene King of the Scots and from the most illustrious Prince, the younger Landgrave Maurice (both of whom are strong in an exceptional poetic vein) — certain pieces composed in commendation of these works, as I trust [I shall], those epigrams will occupy this page for honour's sake. To the second volume, likewise, I shall prefix verses [matching it] in kind and in the authority of noble men. When I know what the aforesaid magnificent Curtius will not think it beneath him to compose for the sake of our common studies (for as he plays the eloquent and excellent Orator, so I should judge that he does not shrink from Poetry either), these I shall set before the verses of the Governor of Holstein, Master Henrik Rantzau, already obtained before now (since he too is not sparingly affected by these studies) — the remaining metrical sports of famous and learned men being reserved for the frontispieces of the two last volumes. I myself, moreover, have in these last days written to the most serene King of the Scots, James the Sixth, and to his Chancellor John Maitland [Metellus], on this matter in an elegiac poem; and then too I appended a certain Epigram to a letter given to the most illustrious Prince Maurice, the younger Landgrave, in this name [i.e. on this same account]; of which poems I have added a transcript here, in case you happen to take delight in such things. And if you know more Princes, skilled in poetry and lovers of these studies, signify their names together with their titles, for I shall write to them likewise in verse, and I shall obtain from them, as I hope, poems in commendation of astronomy. For I judge it both profitable and fitting that these studies should be commended and adorned by men who are Princes and set on the summit of human dignities, so that the more easily and the more eagerly many — who otherwise shrink from these things on account of their loftiness and difficulty, and because they are less lucrative — may be invited to their cultivation. For you know that well-worn line: "To have pleased leading men is not the least praise" — although I, looking into the matter philosophically and more deeply, should judge otherwise. I do not wish to weary you now with more, for I do not doubt that, in these as in our other desires, so far as in you lies, you will of your own accord be of service. I for my part, to do you a like kindness, offer all my labour and zeal, and indeed my whole self. Farewell, and greet the most magnificent Master Curtius honourably in my words.

And now once more, fare you well.
Given 22 March, the year [15]92.

[Postscript:] I have understood, from the letter of Doctor Sager of Lübeck lately sent to me, that Rheticus's library was bequeathed to you by [his] testament. If that is so, I ask that you be willing in good time to communicate to me, described within my bounds, whatever bears upon astronomical matters — whether he had it from his teacher Copernicus or by his own industry. And if you noted from the heavens there the eclipse of the Moon occurring at the very end of last year, or have to hand, or can procure, any other observations — whether obtained by yourself or from elsewhere — make me too a sharer of them at the first opportunity, so that the discrepancy of meridians may from this be more surely investigated than otherwise, with our observations or numbers applied.

Apparatus

  • Sender / place. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven (not stated in the dateline, which gives only day, month, and year; the place is supplied from the correspondence context and is bracketed in the header accordingly). The witness is the draft (Danish Kladde) in Tycho's own papers, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.57.
  • Recipient. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), the Prague physician-astronomer, imperial court physician, and Tycho's principal Bohemian correspondent and broker at the Imperial court; here serving as Tycho's distribution agent for the Progymnasmata among Central-European booksellers and as a conduit to potential princely patrons.
  • magnificus Dominus Curtius — Jakob Kurz von Senftenau (Iacobus Curtius a Senftenau, d. 1594), Imperial Reichsvizekanzler (vice-chancellor). He had urged Tycho to issue the work; Tycho both names him as the prompter of the early publication and hopes for commendatory verses from him ("as he plays the eloquent Orator, so [he] does not shrink from Poetry"). Tycho closes by asking Hagecius to greet him.
  • Astronomiae instaurationem … Progymnasmatis … Prodromi loco — the restoration of astronomy (Tycho's astronomia instaurata programme, per glossary) which he had meant to reserve for a single complete work, but now issues piecemeal as Progymnasmata (preliminary "exercises") "in the place of a Forerunner (Prodromus)." The work meant is the Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata, eventually completed posthumously (Prague, 1602). Progymnasmata and Prodromus are kept as period titles/terms.
  • quaternionem … sena exempla — a quaternion is a gathering of four sheets (eight leaves); Tycho sends the first printed gathering as a specimen, plus sena exempla = six copies of it, for Hagecius to circulate to booksellers in Austria, Hungary, and Poland.
  • semiquinque … Alphabeta … in hac Regali … papyro — a bibliographical measure: each of the four volumes will run to "four and a half alphabets" (semiquinque Alphabeta) of sheets, on "Royal" paper (papyrus Regalis, a large sheet-format). An "alphabet" = the signature-run A–Z (commonly 23 sheets), so "four and a half alphabets" indicates the projected bulk. Lexical note: semiquinque is an unusual coinage; rendered literally "four and a half" (= quinque less a half), and flagged with [sic] rather than smoothed.
  • Privilegium regis Hispaniarum, Caesareano subiungendum — Tycho awaits a printing privilege from the King of Spain (rex Hispaniarum, Philip II), to be added to the Imperial privilege already in hand, before publishing. regis Hispaniarum (genitive plural "of the Spains") reflects the standard early-modern style of the Spanish crown's composite realms; rendered "King of the Spains."
  • serenissimus Scotorum Rex … IACOBUS Sextus — James VI of Scotland (later James I of England), who had visited Hven in March 1590 and was a practising Latin poet ("strong in an exceptional poetic vein"). Tycho hopes for, and has solicited by an elegiac poem, a commendatory epigram from him.
  • Cancellarius IOANNES METELLUS — John Maitland of Thirlestane (c. 1543–1595), Lord Chancellor of Scotland, who accompanied James VI on the Hven visit; Metellus is the latinised by-name. Identification confirmed against Norlind, Tycho Brahe (1970), who notes the king came "in the company of his chancellor Maitland (Metellus)."
  • illustrissimus Princeps Landgravius iunior MAURITIUS — Moritz "the Learned," the younger Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632), son of Wilhelm IV; himself a poet. (To him Tycho would dedicate the Epistolae astronomicae of 1596.)
  • Produx Holsatiae Dominus HENRICUS RANZOVIUS — Henrik Rantzau (Heinrich Rantzau, 1526–1598), royal Statthalter (governor; here Latin Produx, lit. "Produce/Governor") of the Danish duchy of Holstein, humanist patron and astrology enthusiast, whose commendatory verses Tycho already holds. Produx is an unusual title-form; rendered "Governor."
  • lusibus metricis — "metrical sports / playful pieces," the humanist term for occasional verse; rendered "metrical sports" to preserve the deprecatory register Tycho intends.
  • Allusion (proverb). "Principibus placuisse viris non ultima laus est" = Horace, Epistles I.17.35 — "To have pleased leading men is not the least praise." Tycho quotes it as a tritum illud ("that well-worn line") and then immediately distances himself from it: "although I, looking into the matter philosophically and more deeply, should judge otherwise." The hedge is preserved, not flattened — Tycho endorses princely patronage as expedient for promoting astronomy while reserving a higher philosophical valuation.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading without an explicit S. salutation (a working draft); closes Vale … Iamque iterum bene vale = "Farewell … And now once more, fare you well" (glossary). meis verbis … saluta = "greet [him] in my words / on my behalf."
  • Calendar. Dat. 22 Martij Anno 92 = "Given 22 March, the year [15]92" — Old Style (Denmark on the Julian calendar). The edition label ("1592. 22. marts.") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. No day-name or place in the dateline; the year is given as "92" and expanded to 1592 in brackets-by-context (the century is unambiguous from the correspondence).
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript, restored from Dreyer VII, pp. 333–334 (dropped by the working transcription); the report (via Sager of Lübeck) that Rheticus's library was bequeathed to Hájek, with Tycho's request for any Copernicus/Rheticus astronomical material.
