The original

BRIEFF AN DEN HERRENN RENTMEISTERN ANDRES MEIERN.

JCH Tycho Brahe, erbgesessen zu Knudtstrup vnd kön: maytt: zu Dennemarck ambtman auff Vraniburgk, bekenne mit diesem meinem offenen brieff, daß ich mit dem ehrnhafftenn vnnd achtbarn Andreas Meyernn, furstlichenn meckelburgischen rentmeistern, reden lassen vnndt bey jhm freundlichen ahngelanget, daß ehr auß der rentenn von sechs hundert thallernn, so mir vber ein jahr alda zustehenn werden, ein hundert itzo zuuor außgebenn wolle, daß man dafur im lanndt zue Meckelnburgk etzliche victualien vndt andere sachenn kauffenn köntte, welche hundert thaller sollenn widerumb quitirt werdenn inn den sechß hundertt thallernn, so mir verfallenn, wann die zeit verlauffenn, daß jch oder andere von meinentwegen alßdann nur funffhundert sollenn zu fordern haben, vnndt soll dieser mein brieff ahn stadt dero hundert thaller bleibenn vnndt vorsicherung genueg seinn. Dessenn zu vrkundt hab ich dieß mit eigener hanndt vnterschriebenn vnndt mit meinem angebornenn petzschafft besiegelt.

Datum Wandeßburgk denn 31t. martij anno 1598
Tycho Brahe
eigenh. (l.s.)

[inscriptum est:]

Hierauf Tycho Brahen zugeschickett:3 faß bier, jeder faß von 3 tonnen,

6 sch ℔ rogken brott,

1 tonne butter,

30 seitten speck, haben gewogen 1½ schippunt 5½ liß ℔,

6 reichsthaler dem altten meister Peter an bahrem gelde.

    Peter Zacherow.

English translation machine translation, Secure

A letter to the lord treasurer Andreas Meier.

I, Tycho Brahe, hereditary lord at Knudstrup and bailiff [ambtman] of His Royal Majesty of Denmark at Uraniborg, acknowledge by this my open letter that I have had words conveyed to the honourable and worshipful Andreas Meier, ducal Mecklenburg treasurer, and have made a friendly request of him: that out of the revenue of six hundred thalers which will be owing to me there in over a year's time, he be willing to pay out one hundred now in advance, so that for it one might buy in the land of Mecklenburg certain victuals and other things. Which hundred thalers shall in turn be receipted [discharged] against the six hundred thalers owed to me, [so that] when the time has elapsed, I or others on my behalf shall then have only five hundred to claim; and this my letter shall stand in place of those hundred thalers and be security enough. In witness whereof I have subscribed this with my own hand and sealed it with my inborn seal.

Given at Wandsbek, the 31st of March, in the year 1598.
Tycho Brahe,
in his own hand (place of the seal).

[It is inscribed:]

Sent hereupon to Tycho Brahe: 3 casks of beer, each cask of 3 tuns;

6 sch[?] pounds of rye bread;

1 tun of butter;

30 sides of bacon, which weighed 1½ ship-pounds, 5½ lispounds;

6 imperial thalers to old master Peter, in ready money.

Peter Zacherow.

Apparatus

  • Sender / context. Tycho Brahe, writing during his self-imposed exile from Denmark. Having left Hven after losing Frederik II's patronage, he resided 1597–1599 at Heinrich Rantzau's castle of Wandsbek (Wandeßburgk; now part of Hamburg), where in 1598 he printed the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica. He still styles himself with his Danish titles — erbgesessen zu Knudtstrup ("hereditary lord at Knudstrup," his ancestral Scanian seat) and kön. maytt. zu Dennemarck ambtman auff Uraniburgk ("His Royal Majesty of Denmark's bailiff at Uraniborg") — though by this date he no longer held Hven.
  • Recipient. Andreas Meier (Andres Meiern / Andreas Meyern) = Andreas Meier, the fürstlicher mecklenburgischer Rentmeister — the treasurer/receiver of revenues of the Duke of Mecklenburg. The MS survives among the Renterei-Akten (treasury files) of the Landeshauptarchiv Schwerin, consistent with a financial bond addressed to that office.
  • Document type. An offener Brief (open letter) functioning as a promissory note / bond (Verschreibung): Tycho authorises a 100-thaler advance against 600 thalers owed to him, the advance to be set off against the principal. The financial arithmetic, stated once and not contradicted in the source: 600 thalers will fall due to Tycho in over a year; 100 are to be advanced now; those 100 are then deducted (quitirt, receipted/discharged) from the 600, leaving only 500 (nur funffhundert) claimable when the term expires. The single restrictive nur ("only") is the sole limiting particle in the operative clause; there is no negation, and the conditional wann die zeit verlauffen is temporal ("when the time has elapsed"), not contrary-to-fact.
  • zustehenn / verfallenn — zustehen "be owing/due to" and verfallen "fall due, become payable"; both here describe the 600-thaler sum maturing to Tycho. quitirt = quittiert, "receipted, acquitted, discharged" (the advance is to be booked against the principal, not repaid separately).
  • victualien — Latin-derived Viktualien, "victuals, provisions"; the stated purpose of the advance is to buy foodstuffs and other supplies within Mecklenburg.
  • Subscription. Dessen zu Urkund … mit eigener Hand unterschrieben und mit meinem angebornen Petschaft besiegelt — "In witness whereof … subscribed with my own hand and sealed with my inborn [hereditary, i.e. family-armorial] seal" (angeborn Petschaft = the seal of his birth-right, his family arms). The editorial note eigenh. (l.s.) records that the original bore Tycho's autograph signature and (l.s.) = loco sigilli, "in place of the seal."
  • Orthography (chancery German, c. 1598). Doubled final consonants and -nn (thallernn, brieff, sachenn), v/u for u/v (vnnd, vber, auß), th- for t- (thaller), and Latinate forms (martij, anno, victualien) are standard Frühneuhochdeutsch chancery usage; itzo = jetzt ("now"); zuuor = zuvor ("in advance, beforehand"); itzo zuuor together = "now in advance."
  • Calendar / place. Datum Wandeßburgk denn 31t. martij anno 1598 = "Given at Wandsbek, the 31st of March, in the year 1598" (Old Style; Denmark and the Lutheran north German territories were still on the Julian calendar in 1598). The dateline date and the catalogued label date ("1598. 31. marts. Wandsburg") agree.
  • Language. This segment is in Early New High German (with Low German admixture typical of the Wandsbek/Mecklenburg area), not Latin, unlike the letters around it. It is a separately inscribed itemized account ("[inscriptum est:]") attached to the Latin/German bond that the published tail preserves — the physical receipt for the 100-thaler advance the main letter discusses, showing exactly what was delivered against it.
  • Crux — "6 sch ℔ rogken brott." The abbreviation "sch" before the pound-sign "℔" is not confidently resolved: it may stand for Schiffpfund (ship-pound, matching the unit used two lines later for the bacon) or for a measure of rye itself rather than the bread's finished weight. Left untranslated/unexpanded and flagged rather than guessed.
  • Weights and money, expanded for clarity. Schippunt = Schiffpfund ("ship-pound," a large Baltic/North-German weight unit); liß = Liespfund ("lispound," a smaller unit, roughly a sixteenth of a ship-pound in most regional systems); ℔ = the pound sign (libra). Reichsthaler = imperial thaler; "an bahrem gelde" = "in ready/cash money."
  • The people named. Peter Zacherow, who signs the account, appears elsewhere in the Mecklenburg treasury records (Landeshauptarchiv Schwerin, Renterei-Akten) handling a lump-sum cash payment to/from Tycho's affairs in this same period (April 1598) [Dreyer, Opera Omnia vol. XIV] — he is evidently a Mecklenburg ducal financial agent or factor, not a member of Tycho's household. "Old master Peter," paid 6 reichsthaler in cash, is a separate, unidentified third party (a craftsman or purveyor, to judge by the honorific "meister").
  • The recipient, Andreas Meier, and the larger transaction. The register of Tycho Brahe's correspondence confirms that this very letter — 31 March 1598, Wandsbek, Tycho Brahe to Andreas Meier — is preserved at the Landeshauptarchiv Schwerin among the Renterei-Akten (treasury records), consistent with the main letter's subject: repayment terms on a debt (part of the 10,000-thaler loan Tycho had extended to the guardians of the young Dukes of Mecklenburg, attested elsewhere in the Opera Omnia for April 1598). This attached account is the concrete paper trail of one such advance being spent on provisions.
  • Correction. Fabricated numeral '9' removed from the itemised bacon-weight entry ('5½ liss 9 [pound-mark]' to '5½ liss [pound-mark]') and from the parallel English clause, per Dreyer, TBOO XIV p.136, which shows no numeral there. Latin heading 'BRIEFE' corrected to the singular 'BRIEFF' per Dreyer, TBOO XIV p.135.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). Source_text's title reads plural 'BRIEFE' (letters) where Dreyer prints singular 'BRIEFF' (letter) — a corrupted word. The English happens to use the correct singular 'A letter' regardless, so no mistranslation resulted. Dreyer prints: BRIEFF AN DEN HERRENN RENTMEISTERN.
- text error (medium). Source_text and the English translation both report an extra weight component of 'nine pounds' in this itemized delivery account that has no counterpart in Dreyer's print — a fabricated numeral introduced in the transcription and carried straight into the translation. Dreyer prints: 30 feitten fpeck, haben gewogen 1½ fchippunt 5½ liß ℔ (no numeral before the pound-mark).

Repaired. Verified visually against Dreyer page images, TBOO XIV pp.134-137 (full extract). Both defects are confirmed as described and fixed; the fabricated '9' before the pound-mark is absent from the printed page, and the heading is singular in Dreyer. No other divergence found on these pages.
