The original

PETRO IUNIO TYCHO BRAHE. S.

SED quorsum, nobilissime et amplissime vir, silentium hoc plus quam Pythagoreum? Itane crebrioribus meis ad te datis literis nihil plane responderis? Quod sive veteris tecum initæ amicitiæ oblivione sive ultroneâ scribendi intermissione acciderit: a nostra id equidem pristina, quantulacunque hæc fuerit, conversatione, meaque in te fide et expectatione alienissimum censeo. Tempus itaque est, ut vel nunc tandem per nostrates ad vos Ablegatos, tam commoda præsertim oblata occasione, quidpiam mecum literarum commutes. Scripsi autem ad te, ut et alias, superiore anno paulo ante ingruentem brumam, unâque Elegiacam quandam epistolam serenissimo Regi vestro, tua (uti desiderabam) opera meo nomine submisse offerendam, adiunxi. Quæ utrum ad te sarta tecta pervenerint, an tabellarij forte incuria interciderint, nihil interea temporis rescire potui. Mitto itaque iam aliud eiusdem carminis exemplar, maiorem in modum rogans, ut si prius illud ad ipsius Maiestatis manus non pervenerit, hoc alterum ipsius Maiestati legendum demisse exhibere, tuaque qua plurimum vales auctoritate et consilio apud ipsius Maiestatem intercedere non degravere: quo promptius hac in parte meis desiderijs benigne satisfiat. Quod si igitur regale et antiquissimum hoc studium regio suo condecorare carmine, favorabili sua erga me et sublimes has artes voluntate permota, non dedignetur: operam sane apud candidos et cordatos æstimatores inprimis laudabilem præstiterit et eximij erga hæc sacra Uranica favoris monumentum quoddam perenne iugi posteritatis memoriæ consecrandum reliquerit. Id quod serenissimam suam Maiestatem tuo præsertim sensu et consilio clementer concessuram de eadem mihi recipio et polliceor. Constitui autem Regium hoc epigramma statim in frontispicio meorum instaurandæ Astronomiæ Progymnasmatum unâ cum Landtgraviano, quod iamdudum obtinuerim, in pleniorem huius prælustris scientiæ commendationem apponere: prout harum exhibitor tibi ulterius indicare poterit. Proinde te iterum atque iterum obnixe rogatum habeo, ut quantum possis (potes autem, nec immerito, plurimum) tua opera et intercessione, quo id clementissimi beneficij mihi concedatur, et per hunc ipsum voti compos fiam, sedulo elaborare non degravere. Quin et hunc ipsum D. NICOLAUM CRAGIUM, Iuris Doctorem, et Legationis ad vos in Scotiam munus unâ cum alijs subeuntem, virum alioquin in literis præclare versatum, et tuo genio suopte (si recte iudico) non displiciturum ingenio, vel meo nomine tibi commendatum habeas.

Vale.
Datæ Uraniburgi 27 April. A. 1593.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Peter Young, Tycho Brahe — greeting.

But to what end, most noble and worthy sir, this more-than-Pythagorean silence? Have you really made no answer at all to the rather frequent letters I have sent you? Whether this has come about through forgetfulness of the old friendship struck up with you, or through a voluntary breaking-off of correspondence, I for my part judge it utterly foreign to our former intercourse — slight though that may have been — and to my own trust in you and expectation of you. It is time, therefore, that even now at last, through our own envoys [sent] to you, and especially since so convenient an occasion has presented itself, you should exchange some letters with me. Now I wrote to you, as on other occasions, last year a little before the onset of winter, and at the same time added a certain elegiac epistle to be offered humbly in my name to your most serene King, through your good offices (as I desired). Whether these reached you safe and sound, or perchance went astray through the carrier's negligence, I have in the meantime been unable to learn. I therefore now send another copy of the same poem, asking in the strongest terms that, if the former one did not reach His Majesty's own hands, you should not think it a burden humbly to present this second one to His Majesty to read, and to intercede with His Majesty by that authority and counsel in which you are so very powerful — so that in this matter my wishes may the more readily be graciously satisfied. If, therefore, [His Majesty], moved by his favourable disposition toward me and toward these sublime arts, should not disdain to grace this royal and most ancient study with a royal poem of his own, he will assuredly have rendered a service especially praiseworthy in the eyes of fair-minded and judicious appraisers, and will have left behind a certain perennial monument of his exceptional favour toward these sacred Uranian things [haec sacra Uranica], to be consecrated to the unceasing memory of posterity. And this I take upon myself and promise concerning His most serene Majesty — that he will graciously grant it, especially through your good sense and counsel. I have moreover determined to place this royal epigram at once on the frontispiece of my Progymnasmata for the restoring of astronomy [instaurandae Astronomiae], together with the Landgrave's [poem], which I obtained long since, for the fuller commendation of this most illustrious science — as the bearer of this [letter] will be able to indicate to you further. Accordingly I hold you asked, again and again and earnestly, that you should not think it a burden diligently to labour, so far as you can (and you can — and deservedly so — very much), by your effort and intercession, that this most gracious benefit be granted to me, and that through this very man I may attain my wish. Indeed I would have this very man, Master Niels Krag [D. Nicolaum Cragium], Doctor of Law, who is undertaking the office of the embassy to you in Scotland together with the others — a man besides excellently versed in letters, and one who (if I judge rightly) will of his own accord not be displeasing to your disposition by his talent — commended to you also in my name.

Farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 27 April, the year 1593.
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Petrus Iunius = Peter Young (Sir Peter Young of Seaton, 1544–1628), Scottish humanist and royal tutor; with George Buchanan he was preceptor to James VI of Scotland (later James I of England), and remained a trusted royal almoner and diplomat. James VI's interest in Tycho is attested in the period (he visited Hven in 1590); Young is the courtier through whom Tycho seeks to reach the king (cf. A. Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, on James VI in Tycho's orbit). The Latinization Iunius renders the surname Young.
  • Sender / salutation. TYCHO BRAHE. S. — salutem [dicit], "[sends] greeting". The reversed name-order of the address (recipient first, then sender) follows the manuscript.
  • silentium hoc plus quam Pythagoreum — "this more-than-Pythagorean silence," an allusion to the proverbial five-year silence imposed on Pythagoras's pupils; kept as the learned figure it is.
  • "His most serene King" / serenissimo Regi vestro — James VI of Scotland (r. 1567–1625). Tycho never names him in the body, addressing him only as vester Rex / ipsius Maiestas; the rendering keeps that indirection.
  • Elegiacam quandam epistolam / eiusdem carminis exemplar / Regium hoc epigramma — the verse Tycho had sent (an elegiac poem) soliciting from James VI a commendatory poem of his own; the king's hoped-for regium carmen ("royal poem") would, with the Landgrave's, head the Progymnasmata. James VI did in fact furnish such verses, printed in the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata.
  • Landtgraviano [carmine] — the commendatory poem already obtained from Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, Tycho's astronomical correspondent; "which I obtained long since" (iamdudum obtinuerim).
  • meorum instaurandae Astronomiae Progymnasmatum — "my Progymnasmata for the restoring of astronomy," i.e. the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (the first volume of Tycho's restored-astronomy programme, the work on the new star of 1572 and the solar/lunar theory; printed largely at Uraniborg, completed and published 1602/1610). instauratae / instaurandae = "restored / to be restored," Tycho's programmatic instauratio astronomiae. The genitive plural Progymnasmatum is treated as the title.
  • haec sacra Uranica — "these sacred Uranian things," i.e. astronomy as the cult of Urania, muse of the heavens; cf. the sacris Uraniae sign-off flourish. Kept as Tycho's devotional metaphor for his science.
  • Carrier / envoy. harum exhibitor ("the bearer of this [letter]") and per hunc ipsum ("through this very man") = Niels Krag, who carries the letter and the poem.
  • D. Nicolaum Cragium, Iuris Doctorem — Niels Krag (Nicolaus Cragius, c. 1550–1602), Danish humanist, jurist (Doctor of Law) and later royal historiographer; here a member of the Danish embassy to Scotland of 1593. D. = Dominus, "Master".
  • Legationis ad vos in Scotiam munus … subeuntem — "undertaking the office of the embassy to you in Scotland": the Danish diplomatic mission to the Scottish court (in the aftermath of the 1589/90 marriage of James VI to Anne of Denmark). nostrates … Ablegatos ("our own envoys") in the opening refers to the same embassy, the "convenient occasion" Tycho exploits to renew contact.
  • Epistolary hedges preserved. quantulacunque haec fuerit ("slight though that [intercourse] may have been"); si recte iudico ("if I judge rightly"); forte … incuria ("perchance … through negligence"); uti desiderabam ("as I desired"). The thrice-repeated litotes non degravere ("do not think it a burden") is kept each time as a courtesy formula rather than smoothed to "please."
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uraniburgi 27 April. A. 1593 = "Given at Uraniborg (on Hven), 27 April 1593" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700, the edition's convention). The dateline agrees with the edition label (1593. 27. april. Uraniborg); no discrepancy.
  • Text-of-record. Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII (Epistolae); sole MS witness cited is a later copy (afskrift), Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Smith 77 — consistent with transmission through Young's / Scottish-humanist papers.
