The original

CLARISSIMO INPRIMISQUE ERUDITO VIRO D. THOMAe SAVELLO, ANGLO, AMICO SUO DILECTO. ENGELANDTT. S.

LITERAS tuas circa initia Septembris ad me Hamburgo perscriptas, vir praestantissime eruditissimeque, misit huc clarissimus vir D. Doctor WINSHEMIUS, easque dum peregre in Seelandia absum, die 22. Novembris recepi, quae mihi admodum gratae erant. Nec enim insolitum quid accidit, si viri eruditi in varijs Europae locis, utut de facie mihi ignoti, ad me subinde dent literas. Quod et ego vicissim per occasionem facere non supersedeo. Non tamen ita mihi ipsi placeo, nec tanto me dignor honore, quo tu candore et benevolentia singulari erga me potius allectus, quam quod eiuscemodi quid agnoscam, me afficis. Si praesens praesentem aliquando allocutus fuisses, ex quo sic aliquoties animum induxeras, equidem tuus huc accessus mihi percarus evenisset. Solent praeter alias nationes diversas tui quoque Conterranei Angli me quotannis, aestatis praesertim tempore, crebro invisere, quos et, uti par est, in pretio habeo, et prout cuiusque fert ingenium atque desiderium, libenter cum illis de re qualibet proposita confero, quod et tecum non gravatim facerem, cum ex literis tuis perspiciam, tanquam ex ungue leonem, te ingenij acumine iudicio et eloquentia praepollere, modo Aeolus atque Neptunus tam averso animo mihi tuum huc adventum non invidissent. Reddentur forte alias placatiores, nam et eos, qui ventis atque undis praesunt, instar eorum mutabiles esse condecet. Nec fera tempestas toto perdurat in anno. Dabitur forsan aliquando occasio, qua, his faventibus et benignius aspirantibus, id quod hactenus non successit, commodius expedies. Eris mihi, ubi id evenerit, hospes apprime acceptus. Interim quod licet et decet, quodque a me tam obnixe expetis, praestare non intermittam. Mitto siquidem bina Exemplaria libri nostri secundi de recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis. Licet enim non libenter haec a caeteris tomis nondum typis integre absolutis, avello, tamen tuae sincerae et amicae petitioni morem gerere volui. Habebis et reliquos, sine quibus hic pene mutilus est, quando typis nostris ad Colophonem fuerint deducti. Interea temporis per otium hunc, quo nunc te dono, evolve, disquire, dijudica. Et si quid in hoc dubij, aut minus apodictice prolatum adinveneris, ingenue admone, tuamque et aliorum praestantium in Anglia Philosophorum de singulis eruditam censuram, sive pro sive contra nos faciat, conquire, audacter profer, meque per literas, cum libera restituetur navigatio, de his certiorem redde. Vale et magnificum spectatissimumque virum Dominum DANIELEM ROGERSIUM, Regineae Maiestatis Consiliarium atque Secretarium dignissimum (qui etiam Legationis huc in Daniam munere functus, me cum suo comitatu aliquando invisere non est gravatus) meo nomine saluta, unâque illum de mihi promisso serenissimae Reginae Privilegio librario, pro quo iam aliquoties frustra ad ipsum scripsi, admone ut mature stet promissis, quo Diploma illud primo vere, si antea ob hyemis asperitatem fieri nequeat, certo obtineam, cum caeteris quibusdam, ab Imperatore atque alijs Regibus impetratis, Operibus nostris praefigendum. Aut si Privilegium illud non est missurus, indicet saltem, quod et cur id non concedatur, sicque fidem datam apud me sufficienter redemerit, eritque excusatus. Saluta quoque meo nomine officiose Nobilissimum et excellentissimum Dominum IOHANNEM DEE, quem in patriam feliciter reversum audivi, ipsique hoc nomine congratulor, omniaque prospera opto. Nobilissimum quoque et pariter eruditissimum Mathematicum THOMAM DIGGESSÆUM ex meo ore non insalutatum relinque, cui etiam ex animo faveo et bene cupio. Utrique autem horum librum nostrum transmissum ostende, ut et illi in eum disquirant atque suam de eius contentis sententiam libere pronuntient. Quod si literis ad me datis praestiterint, ego et respondere et amicitiam in posterum cum illis colere atque de rebus Philosophicis et Mathematicis iucunde conferre nequaquam detrectabo.

Iterum atque iterum diu et bene vale.
Dabantur Uraniburgi, Calendis Decembris, Anno 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE
manu propria.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 285; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Addidi quaterna Exemplaria meae Effigiei nuper Amsterodami cupro insculptae. Si qui sunt apud vos excellentes poëtae, quales in fecunda et facunda vestra et terra et venâ plurimos inveniri non dubito, cuperem, ut argutum aliquod Epigramma in Iconam hanc atque operum meorum commendationem delectationis ergo luderent. Quâ in re eruditißimus Dominus DANIEL ROGERSIUS suam quoque erga me probare poßet benevolentiam, modo a Reipublicae serioribus negotijs tantillulum vacaret.

To the most renowned and especially learned gentleman, Master Thomas Savile, Englishman, his beloved friend. England. Greeting.

Your letter, written out to me from Hamburg about the beginning of September, most excellent and most learned Sir, the renowned gentleman Doctor Winsheim sent on hither, and I received it on 22 November while I was away from home in Zealand — and it was most welcome to me. For it is no unusual thing that learned men in various parts of Europe, however unknown to me by face, send me letters from time to time; which I too, in turn, do not refrain from doing when occasion offers. Yet I am not so well-pleased with myself, nor do I deem myself worthy of so great an honour, as that with which you regard me — drawn rather by your singular candour and goodwill toward me than because I recognize anything of the kind [in myself]. Had you at some time addressed me in person, face to face — since you had so often turned your mind that way — your coming here would assuredly have proved most dear to me. Besides other various nations, your own countrymen the English are wont to visit me often every year, especially in the summer season; and these, as is fitting, I hold in esteem, and, according as each one's bent and wish carries him, I gladly confer with them on whatever matter is put forward — which I would do with you too, and not grudgingly, since from your letter I perceive, as the lion from its claw, that you excel in keenness of intellect, in judgement, and in eloquence: if only Aeolus and Neptune had not, with so adverse a temper, begrudged me your coming hither. They will perhaps be rendered more placable another time, for it befits even those who preside over the winds and the waves to be, like them, changeable. Nor does a fierce tempest endure throughout the whole year. Perhaps occasion will some day be given whereby — with these favouring and breathing more kindly — you will more conveniently accomplish what has not hitherto succeeded. When that comes to pass, you will be to me a guest most welcome. Meanwhile what is permitted and seemly, and what you so earnestly seek of me, I shall not fail to perform. For I am sending two copies of our second book, On the More Recent Phenomena of the Heavens. Although indeed it is not willingly that I tear these away from the other volumes, not yet wholly finished at the press, nevertheless I wished to comply with your sincere and friendly request. You shall have the rest also — without which this one is well-nigh mutilated — when they shall have been brought to the colophon by our press. In the meantime, at your leisure unroll, examine, and judge this [book] with which I now present you. And if you find anything in it doubtful, or set forth less than apodictically, frankly admonish me; and seek out your own learned verdict, and that of other eminent philosophers in England, on the several points — whether it make for us or against us — boldly bring it forward, and by letter inform me of these things, when free navigation shall be restored. Farewell; and greet in my name the magnificent and most distinguished gentleman Master Daniel Rogers, Her Majesty the Queen's Councillor and most worthy Secretary (who, when discharging here in Denmark the office of an embassy, did not grudge to visit me at one time with his retinue), and at the same time admonish him concerning the printing-privilege promised me by the most serene Queen — for which I have already several times written to him in vain — that he stand maturely by his promises, so that I may certainly obtain that diploma in the first spring, if it cannot be done before on account of the harshness of winter, to be prefixed to our works together with certain others obtained from the Emperor and from other Kings. Or, if he does not intend to send that privilege, let him at least indicate that, and why, it is not granted, and so he will have sufficiently redeemed in my eyes the pledge he gave, and will stand excused. Greet also dutifully in my name the most noble and most excellent gentleman Master John Dee, whom I have heard has happily returned to his homeland, and on this account I congratulate him and wish him all prosperity. The most noble and equally most learned mathematician Thomas Digges, too, do not leave ungreeted from my lips, to whom likewise I am heartily favourable and wish well. To both of these show our book, sent on [to them], that they too may examine into it and freely pronounce their opinion on its contents. And if they perform this by letters sent to me, I shall by no means decline both to reply and henceforth to cultivate friendship with them, and to confer pleasantly on philosophical and mathematical matters.

Again and again, long and well, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, on the Kalends of December, the year 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE
in his own hand.

[Postscript:] I have added four copies of my likeness, lately engraved on copper at Amsterdam. If there are among you any excellent poets — such as I do not doubt are to be found in great number in your fertile and eloquent land and vein — I should wish that, for delight's sake, they might play [i.e. compose] some witty Epigram upon this Image and in commendation of my works. In which matter the most learned Daniel Rogers could also prove his goodwill toward me, if only he had a little leisure from the graver affairs of the Commonwealth.

Apparatus

  • Sender / place. Tycho Brahe writing from Uraniborg on the island of Hven, at the height of his Hven period.
  • Recipient. Thomas Savellus, Anglus = Thomas Savile (c. 1561–1593), Oxford scholar, younger brother of Sir Henry Savile (Warden of Merton, Provost of Eton). He had corresponded with Tycho from Hamburg in September 1590. He died in 1593; the De recentioribus phaenomenis copy Tycho here despatches survives in the Bodleian Library, Oxford (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, App. I, citing TBOO VII, 284.15–16, 285.1–3; Norlind 1970, 123). The address "Savello" is the Latinised form; the modern surname is Savile, not (as sometimes conflated) the elder brother Sir Henry.
  • Address-line ENGELANDTT. A vernacular geographic tag — German/Danish Engeland ("England") — appended after the Latin salutation, marking the letter's destination. It is a chancery/postal direction, not Latin; kept verbatim in the source block and rendered "England." in the translation. ANCLO in the address is a copyist's form of ANGLO ("Englishman"); printed C for G is retained in the source block.
  • Intermediary. D. Doctor Winshemius — "Doctor Winsheim," the learned go-between at Hamburg who forwarded Savile's September letter to Hven. The exact identity (the Latin toponymic surname Winshemius = "of Windsheim") is not firmly fixed by the present sources; identified only as the Hamburg forwarder named in the letter. Flagged, not asserted.
  • Persons greeted.
  • Daniel Rogersius = Daniel Rogers (c. 1538–1591), the English diplomat and humanist, "Her Majesty's Councillor and Secretary," who had served on an embassy to Denmark and had visited Tycho on Hven. Tycho presses him about the English printing-privilege promised by Queen Elizabeth I (serenissima Regina).
  • Iohannes Dee = John Dee (1527–1608/9), the English mathematician and magus, whom Tycho has heard "has happily returned to his homeland" (Dee returned to England from the Continent in late 1589). Tycho's request that Dee see a copy is the documentary basis for the long-discussed Dee↔Tycho contact.
  • Thomas Diggessaeus = Thomas Digges (c. 1546–1595), Dee's pupil, the English Copernican mathematician.
  • Work sent. liber noster secundus de recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis = Tycho's second book, De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (Uraniborg [C. Weida], 1588), treating the comet of 1577. The two copies are to be shown to Dee and Digges; Tycho explicitly invites criticism ("whether it make for us or against us"). reliqui … tomi … ad Colophonem — the remaining volumes of the projected set (the Progymnasmata etc.) "not yet wholly finished at the press," to follow when carried "to the colophon."
  • Classical figures / sententiae.
  • tanquam ex ungue leonem — "as the lion from its claw," the proverb (ex ungue leonem): to judge the whole from a small part; here, Savile's quality inferred from his letter. Kept as the proverb.
  • Aeolus atque Neptunus — Aeolus (god of the winds) and Neptune (god of the sea): a periphrasis for the storms that prevented Savile's intended crossing to Hven. The conceit is sustained ("those who preside over the winds and the waves … changeable"; "a fierce tempest [does not] endure throughout the whole year").
  • cum libera restituetur navigatio — "when free navigation shall be restored": the same maritime obstacle that frustrated the visit also delays the post.
  • Technical / institutional terms. Privilegium librarium / Diploma — a printing-privilege (book-privilege), the legal monopoly Tycho sought to prefix to his works, "together with certain others obtained from the Emperor and from other Kings"; rendered "printing-privilege / diploma," not modernised to "copyright." apodictice — "apodictically," i.e. demonstratively/with certainty (the Aristotelian sense); retained as a technical adverb.
  • Negations (verified count). The letter's force turns on several negations, each reproduced one-for-one: (1) Non tamen ita mihi ipsi placeo, nec … dignor — a double negative of modesty ("I am not so well-pleased … nor do I deem myself worthy"), Tycho deflecting Savile's praise; (2) non gravatim facerem — "I would [confer] … and not grudgingly"; (3) modo … non invidissent — counterfactual, "if only … had not begrudged"; (4) non est gravatus — Rogers "did not grudge to visit me"; (5) Aut si Privilegium illud non est missurus … quod et cur id non concedatur — "Or if he does not intend to send … why it is not granted"; (6) nequaquam detrectabo — "I shall by no means decline." None added or dropped.
  • Calendar. Calendis Decembris, Anno 1590 = "on the Kalends of December (1 December), 1590," Roman-calendar idiom glossed to the civil date by the edition's convention (Tycho, on Hven, used Old Style). The reception date die 22. Novembris (22 November) is likewise O.S. The edition label ("1590. 1. december. Uraniborg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Subscription. manu propria — "in his own hand," the autograph attestation.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript, restored from Dreyer VII, p. 285 (dropped by the working transcription); the four Amsterdam-engraved portrait copies and the epigram request naming Daniel Rogers (parallels the 7 Dec 1590 Rantzau portrait passage).
  • Correction. Latin address heading 'ANCLO' corrected to 'ANGLO' and 'praeponere' corrected to 'praepollere' (Dreyer TBOO VII pp.283-284); the English translation already read correctly in both places and required no change.
