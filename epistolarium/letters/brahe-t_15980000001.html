The original

IOHANNES ISACIUS PONTANUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

.....SCALIGER observationes utrasque (quæ munificentiæ tuæ sunt) vernalem aio et autumnalem, suis inseruit, et videbis propediem. De secessu vero in Germaniam itidem intellexerat, et dolere se fatetur, si eiusmodi rerum conturbaretur commoditas, quarum iam fructus redundaret ad Europam universam. Placet quidem ei Germania; sed nescit, an mores undiquaque gentis ad illam facilitatem humanitatemque apti, quæ debebatur, apti tibi. Optaret, si Dania omnino deserendi staret animus, Bataviam potius inviseres, amicam studijs, benignam....; et denique te tuasque virtutes et honorabit, et fere dixerat, adorabit. Esset certe in omnem eventum hæc ora propinquior Daniæ, et ille hîc Ordinum sensus, ut omnes gratias non modo retinendas, verum etiam acquirendas sciant.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johannes Isaksen Pontanus to Tycho Brahe.

..... Scaliger has inserted both observations (which are [a token] of your generosity) — the vernal, I say, and the autumnal — among his own, and you will see [them] very soon. About your withdrawal into Germany he had likewise understood, and he confesses that he grieves [to think] that an opportunity for affairs of this kind should be disturbed, the fruit of which would now overflow to the whole of Europe. Germany pleases him, to be sure; but he does not know whether the manners of a nation [that are] everywhere suited to that easy graciousness and humanity which was owed [to you] are suited to you. He would wish — if your mind were set on abandoning Denmark altogether — that you would rather visit Batavia [Holland], friendly to studies, kindly....; and, in fine, he both will honour you and your virtues and, he had almost said, will adore [them]. This shore would certainly, in any event, be nearer to Denmark; and such here is the disposition of the States, that they know all favours are not only to be retained but even to be gained [for you].

Apparatus

  • Sender. Iohannes Isacius Pontanus = Johannes Isaksen Pontanus (1571–1639), Danish-Dutch humanist, physician and historian (later royal historiographer of Denmark and professor at Harderwijk); in the later 1590s he was in the Netherlands and moved in the Leiden learned circle, which placed him to relay Scaliger's words to Tycho. The letter survives only as a later copy (Afskrift) in the Langebek collection (Det Kongelige Bibliotek, Langebek 178), which accounts for the truncated openings (the leading and internal ellipses are in the transmitted text).
  • Recipient / occasion. Addressed to Tycho Brahe in 1598, the year he left Denmark for good; the letter speaks of his contemplated secessus ("withdrawal") and of "abandoning Denmark altogether," and relays counsel on where to settle — squarely the 1597–98 self-exile context.
  • Scaliger — Joseph Justus Scaliger (1540–1609), the foremost philologist of the age, then professor at the University of Leiden (from 1593). Pontanus reports Scaliger's reaction at second hand; the epistemic frame is Pontanus's ("he confesses," "he would wish," "he had understood"), and is preserved as reported speech rather than asserted as Tycho-fact.
  • observationes utrasque … vernalem … et autumnalem — "both observations … the vernal and the autumnal": evidently a pair of Tycho's observations (most naturally of an equinox — the vernal and the autumnal — though the noun aequinoctium is not in the text and is therefore not supplied in the translation). Scaliger, whose chronological work De emendatione temporum (1583; 2nd ed. 1598) turned on exactly such equinox data, has "inserted [them] among his own." Rendered "observations" per glossary; the unstated object (equinoxes vs. other phenomena) is flagged, not decided.
  • quae munificentiae tuae sunt — literally "which are of your munificence," i.e. which are [a gift / token] of your generosity (Tycho had supplied the observations); rendered with a bracketed [a token] since the predicate noun is elided in the Latin.
  • aio — "I say" (Pontanus's own parenthetical emphatic, not Scaliger's); kept as the writer's interjection.
  • secessu … in Germaniam — "withdrawal into Germany": Tycho's mooted plan to settle in the Empire. Secessus here = a retiring/withdrawal (to live elsewhere), not a literal secession.
  • Bataviam … amicam studijs, benignam — "Batavia, friendly to studies, kindly": Batavia = Holland / the (northern) Netherlands, the humanist name for the Dutch provinces; Scaliger (himself settled at Leiden) urges Tycho toward the Dutch Republic instead of Germany. Rendered "Batavia [Holland]" on first occurrence.
  • Ordinum sensus — "the disposition of the States": Ordines = the States (the States-General of the United Provinces, or the States of Holland), the governing assembly; sensus their inclination/mind. The sentence reports the States' favourable attitude toward attracting Tycho.
  • Textual cruxes. (1) an mores undiquaque gentis … apti tibi — the clause is grammatically strained in the transmitted copy: apti ("suited") stands twice (once before the relative clause, once after debebatur), one of which is a copyist's dittography in this Afskrift. The sense is "[he does not know] whether the manners of the nation everywhere are suited to you [as fitted to] that graciousness and humanity which was owed [to you]." The doubled apti is preserved verbatim in the Source block and the redundancy flagged here rather than silently emended. (2) The internal "....." after benignam marks a lacuna/omission in the transmitted text, not an editorial cut here.
  • Date / calendar. The source carries no dateline; the edition catalogues it as "1598. empty" (i.e. year only, no day/month). The header therefore gives the year as catalogued and flags that the source text substantiates no finer date. Denmark was on the Julian calendar (O.S.) in 1598; absent a dateline, no style is asserted by the source itself, and no conversion is made.
