The original

MAGNIFICO NOBILISSIMO ET AMPLISSIMO VIRO D. TYCHONI BRAHE, MATHEMATICORUM SECULI NOSTRI ET PRÆSERTIM APUD SACRATISSIMAM CÆSAREAM MAIESTATEM PRINCIPI, DOMINO ET AMICO SUMME OBSERVANDO. BENNATICAe BOHEMORUM.

MAGNIFICE Domine TYCHO, Vir praestantissime: Non vulgari laetitia fui affectus, quando mihi redditae sunt literae tuae XVI Maij scriptae, e quibus libentissime cognovi, meas, quas mense Martio scripseram, rectâ pervenisse in manus tuas. Eram enim de ijs nonnihil sollicitus, postquam missae fuerant ad illum, quem Pragae non invenerant.

Ad typographum nostrum quod attinet, facile poteram cogitare hoc ipsum, quod contigit: nimirum te pretio magis aequo posse alicubi locorum curare tua inprimenda, quam apud nos, ubi non sunt omnia sic expedita et in promptu. Boni tamen consules meum studium qualecunque gratificandi tuis postulatis et desiderijs: opto ex animo, ut in re alia possim tibi probare meum animum tibi deditissimum.

Ad Doctorem BUCRETIUM quod attinet: is te amanter, diligenter et officiose rogat, et pro missis literis humaniss. magnas agit gratias. Bene te iubeo sperare et de Wittichianis reliquijs et de chartis alterius illius, qui quondam apud nos vixit, Astronomi. Utroque loco hanc rem tanta et fide et diligentia et tam accurate et sedulo egit et adhuc agit, ut non dubitem nos habituros, quod optamus. Non voluit nunc respondere literis tuis, quod confidat se propediem reipsa votis tuis satisfacturum: modo parvam moram aequo feras animo, magnopere te rogatum volo.

Domino Doctori FISCHERO, Viro certe opt. et doctissimo, gratissimae fuerunt literae tuae, daturo modis omnibus operam, ut suam tibi fidem et diligentiam omni, quod a se praestare poterit, officio probet. Nec te spero unquam poenitebit tuae in ipsum benevolentiae.

De Bibliotheca Duditiana, quae mihi est notissima, velim mihi credas. Eam nunc velut inter coelum et terram pendere, postquam ab haeredibus DUDITIJ non possidetur, sed alteri cuidam pro certa pecuniae summa non tam vendita est quam oppignorata. Iacet nunc misera apud nos in domo quadam, et non aliter iacet (vidi enim aliquoties) quam solent apud nobiles iacere acervi frumentorum: neque in illam plenum ius habet Emtor neque etiam venditor. Res plane intricata est, et metuo, ne libri isti magnum aliquod et irreparabile damnum accipiant.

Dictum mihi est nuper ab amico quodam, instrumenta tua Mathematica non levi iactura fuisse per vim ventorum et tempestatum in arce istac tua affecta. Si ita est, admodum tibi condoleo. Dubito tamen, an sit, cum tu nullam eius rei mentionem facias.

Magnificus huius urbis et Ducatus Capitaneus, Dn. ABRAHAMUS IENGTIUS, olim summi nostri WITICHIJ, dum viveret, patronus, tui amantiss. et studiosissimus, magnopere te salutat. Cogitat paulo post ad thermas Boiemicas proficisci, et plane constituit in illo itinere te convenire, ut te videat coram, quem dudum est unice miratus. Dixit ei nuper Doctor IESSENIUS de quodam libro tuo recens edito, quem obtuleris Caesari. Eum ipse unice videre gestit, et iam semel et iterum an viderim ex me quaesivit. Epistolarum tuarum volumen, quod ex dono liberalitatis tuae habeo, apud me primus vidit, et per aliquot dies secum habuit.

Si quid ad me scribere aut mittere voles, scias rectissime ad me perventurum, quicquid Pragam per certos homines miseris ad Dn. IOANNEM MYLLNERUM, Sacrae Caesae Matis Consiliarium et Regni Boem. Secretarium supremum, Compatrem meum cariss. et omni fide observandum. Apud illum enim vivit IOANNES PALUDIUS, Principis mei Consiliarius Pragae habitans et negotia Cels. suae in aula illa curans. Is et diligenter et libenter curabit, quicquid ad me pertinens acceperit. Bene et feliciter vale, Vir praestantissime, et me, quem semel amare coepisti, perpetuo amare perge: sic te vicissim curet ametque Deus.

Vratisl. XIIIJ Iunij 1600.
T. Μ. P. observantiss. et amantiss.
IAC. MONAW.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the magnificent, most noble, and most ample man, Lord Tycho Brahe, Prince of the Mathematicians of our age — and especially with the most sacred Imperial Majesty — his Lord and friend most highly to be respected. [To] Benátky of the Bohemians.

Magnificent Lord Tycho, most excellent Man: I was filled with no common joy when your letter, written 16 May, was delivered to me, from which I most gladly learned that mine, which I had written in March, came straight into your hands; for I was somewhat anxious about them, after they had been sent to him whom they had not found at Prague.

As concerns our printer, I could easily imagine this very thing which happened — namely, that you can have your works printed somewhere at a fairer price than with us, where not everything is so expedited and ready at hand. Yet you will take in good part my zeal, such as it is, to gratify your requests and desires; I wish from my heart that in some other matter I may prove to you my mind most devoted to you.

As concerns Doctor Bucretius: he greets you lovingly, diligently, and dutifully, and gives great thanks, most courteously, for the letters sent. I bid you hope well both of the Wittich remains and of the papers of that other Astronomer who once lived among us. In both cases he has dealt, and still deals, with the matter with such faith and diligence, and so accurately and assiduously, that I do not doubt we shall have what we wish. He did not wish now to answer your letters, because he trusts he will soon satisfy your wishes in deed; only bear a small delay with an even mind — which I greatly ask you to do.

To Lord Doctor Fischer, a man certainly most good and most learned, your letter was most welcome; he will give effort in every way to prove to you his faith and diligence by every service he can render. Nor, I hope, will you ever regret your benevolence toward him.

Concerning the Library of Dudith, which is very well known to me, I would have you believe me: it now hangs, as it were, between heaven and earth — since it is not possessed by Dudith's heirs, but has been, to a certain other party for a fixed sum of money, not so much sold as pawned. It now lies wretched among us in a certain house, and lies no otherwise (for I have seen it several times) than heaps of grain are wont to lie in the houses of the nobles: neither has the buyer full right in it, nor the seller. The matter is plainly entangled, and I fear lest those books take some great and irreparable harm.

It was lately told me by a certain friend that your Mathematical instruments had been damaged, with no slight loss, by the force of winds and storms in that castle of yours. If it is so, I greatly condole with you; yet I doubt whether it is, since you make no mention of the matter.

The Magnificent Captain of this city and Duchy, Lord Abraham Jengtius — formerly, while he lived, the patron of our great Wittich, most loving and devoted to you — greets you greatly. He thinks of setting out a little later to the Bohemian baths, and has fully resolved on that journey to meet you, that he may see in person you whom he has long uniquely admired. Doctor Jessenius lately told him of a certain book of yours recently published, which you had offered to the Emperor; he longs uniquely to see it, and has now once and again asked me whether I have seen it. The volume of your Letters, which I have from the gift of your liberality, he first saw at my house, and had with him for several days.

If you wish to write or send anything to me, know that whatever you send to Prague by sure men, to Lord Johann Myllner, Councillor of the Sacred Imperial Majesty and Supreme Secretary of the Kingdom of Bohemia — my dearest gossip, and to be trusted in all faith — will come most directly to me. For with him lives Johann Paludius, Councillor of my Prince, dwelling at Prague and managing his Highness's affairs at that court; he will both diligently and gladly take care of whatever pertaining to me he receives. Fare well and happily, most excellent Man, and continue to love perpetually me whom you once began to love; so may God in turn care for you and love you.

Wrocław, 14 June 1600.
Most respectful and most loving of Your Magnificence,
Jacob Monau.

Apparatus

  • Sender & occasion. Jacob Monau (Monavius) of Breslau (Wrocław) — this is his reply to Tycho's letter of 16 May 1600 (brahe-t_16000516001), answering it point by point (and confirming that Monau's own March letter, brahe-t_16000110001's antecedent, had reached Tycho after going astray "to him whom it had not found at Prague").
  • Significance — a major book-provenance letter (the fate of two libraries).
  • The Dudith Library (vivid and important). Monau, "to whom it is very well known," reports that Andreas Dudith's library now "hangs between heaven and earth": no longer the heirs', but pawned (not sold) to a third party for a fixed sum, so that neither buyer nor seller has full right. It "lies wretched in a certain house … as heaps of grain lie in the houses of the nobles" (Monau has seen it several times), and he fears "great and irreparable harm" to the books — a poignant eyewitness account of the dispersal/decay of a famous humanist collection (the purchase-bid of brahe-t_16000516001).
  • The Wittich remains. Good hope, via Doctor Bucretius, both for the Wittich remains (Wittichianae reliquiae — the dispersed library of Paul Wittich, incl. the annotated Copernicus copies) and for the papers of "that other Astronomer who once lived among us" (the unnamed dead Breslau astronomer of brahe-t_16000110001 / brahe-t_16000516001); Bucretius "is dealing with both with such diligence … that I do not doubt we shall have what we wish."
  • Abraham Jengtius — Wittich's former patron. The Captain (Landeshauptmann) of Breslau and the Duchy, Abraham Jengtius, "formerly the patron of our great Wittich while he lived," sends warm greetings and plans to meet Tycho on a journey to the Bohemian baths (Karlsbad/Teplice); he longs to see the book Tycho lately presented to the Emperor (of which Jessenius had told him). A valuable provenance link (Wittich's patron) for the Wittich-library question.
  • The storm-damage rumour. Monau passes on a report that Tycho's instruments were storm-damaged at his castle [Benátky], condoling — but doubts it, "since you make no mention of it." (Tycho's silence suggests the rumour was false or exaggerated.)
  • The relay channel. For letters to Monau: send to Prague to Johann Myllner (imperial councillor and Supreme Secretary of the Kingdom of Bohemia, Monau's compater), with whom lives Johann Paludius, the Silesian Duke's councillor resident at Prague — who will forward to Breslau. A concrete glimpse of the Prague–Breslau correspondence infrastructure.
  • Persons named. Jacob Monau (sender); Daniel Bucretius; Tobias Fischer (Doctor Fischer); Andreas Dudith (and his heirs); Paul Wittich; Abraham Jengtius (Breslau Captain, Wittich's patron); Johannes Jessenius; Johann Myllner (Bohemian Supreme Secretary); Johann Paludius (Silesian councillor at Prague); the printer (unnamed); Rudolf II.
  • Calendar. Dateline Vratisl. XIIIJ Iunij 1600; Silesia (Breslau) had adopted the Gregorian (New Style) calendar by 1600; marked [N.S.].
  • Correction. Latin corrected: removed the interpolated clause 'quod ut facias' (Dreyer VIII p.325), which is absent from the printed text; this does not affect the published English, which already matched the Dreyer sense.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). Source_text inserts the clause 'quod ut facias', which has no counterpart in Dreyer's printed text; the translation follows the padded source and renders the interpolated clause as an extra dash-clause. Dreyer prints: modo parvam moram aequo feras animo, magnopere te rogatum volo.
- text error (low). Source_text reads 'devotissimum' where Dreyer prints 'deditissimum'; both mean approximately 'most devoted' so the sense is unaffected, but it is a literal word-level departure from Dreyer as read from the page image. Dreyer prints: meum animum tibi deditissimum.

Repaired. The the page reference extract (18 pages, TBOO VIII pp.310-327) required reading past several intervening letters (Tycho-Galileo, Tycho-Herwart, Tycho-Corraducius, the Kepler diploma, Tycho-Monau of 16 May, etc.) before locating Monau's 14 June 1600 letter on p.325. That page confirms Dreyer reads 'modo parvam moram aequo feras animo, magnopere te rogatum volo' (no 'quod ut facias'), which is fixed. On the second defect, the transcribed original was found already to read 'meum animum tibi deditissimum', matching Dreyer exactly - the defect's own cited 'source' excerpt ('devotissimum') was not actually present in the transcribed original, so no change was made there.
