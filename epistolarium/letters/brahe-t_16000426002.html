The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

PERVENERUNT ad manus meas, Vir nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile princeps, libelli duo, quos vir doctissimus et in Geometricis plane rarus et insignis, FRANCISCUS VIETA, nuper in lucem prodire voluit. Hos cum non dubitem te perlibenter visurum, neque forte aliunde absque difficultate exemplaria adepturum, his iunctos ad te mitto.

Cogito his diebus, si modo negotia publica et politica, quibus admodum distrahor, id permittunt, Augustam me conferre, atque ibi, si ulla ratione fieri poterit, manuscripta opera posthuma CYPRIANI LEOVITIJ indagare et nancisci, ut ea quoque ad te transmittere possim.

Atque hisce tibi fælicissima quæque ex animo precor et opto.
Datæ Monachij die 26 Apr. A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

There have come into my hands, most noble man and easily prince of all mathematicians, two little books which that most learned man — plainly rare and distinguished in geometrical matters — François Viète lately wished to bring into the light. Since I do not doubt that you will see these most gladly, nor perhaps obtain copies elsewhere without difficulty, I send them to you joined herewith.

I am thinking, these days, if only the public and political affairs by which I am much distracted permit it, of betaking myself to Augsburg, and there, if it can be done by any means, of tracking down and obtaining the posthumous manuscript works of Cyprian Leovitius, that I may transmit those too to you.

And with these I pray and wish you from my heart every happiness.
Given at Munich, the 26th day of April, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (see the Munich series), continuing as Tycho's procurer of new mathematical literature.
  • Franciscus Vieta = François Viète (1540–1603), the founder of symbolic algebra; the "two little books lately brought into the light" are among his recent Paris tracts (the Apollonius Gallus, the Responsum / Relatio on the calendar, or the analytic opuscula of c. 1593–1600 — not individually named here). Herwart, himself in correspondence with Viète, judged Tycho would want them.
  • Cyprianus Leovitius = Cyprian Leovitius (Leowitz) (1524–1574), Bohemian astronomer-astrologer; Herwart hopes to recover his posthumous manuscripts at Augsburg (where Leovitius had worked) for Tycho. Augusta = Augsburg (per glossary).
  • Calendar. Munich (Catholic Bavaria) = New Style; die 26 Apr. 1600. Header agrees with the label.
