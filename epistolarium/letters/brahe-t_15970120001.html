The original

NOBILISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHEN, HÆREDI IN KNUDSTRUP, AMICO SUO CHARISSIMO.

S. Quæ apud serenissimum regem, dominum nostrum clementissimum, nobilissime Tycho, amice charissime, tibi a me duplici in negocio effecta dari cupis, ea ex literis tuis, quæ mihi una cum exemplo literarum magnificorum regni senatorum redditæ sunt, intellexi, utque tibi pro amicitia nostra per me in utroque liberaliter satisfieret, nihil diligentiæ prætermisi.

Verum te cælare non possum, regiam majestatem, cui postulata tua summa fide exposui, ad primum illud de instrumentis astronomicis negocium ita respondisse: ex regio ærario aliquid sumptuum in istorum instrumentorum conservationem impendere, sibi non esse integrum.

Ad alteram vero quod attinet petitionem, quam de beneficii istius Norvagici clementissima vel saltem ad exiguum illud in literis tuis dictum tempus proroganda concessione instituis, serenissima ipsius majestas eam admittere plane recusavit, persuasissimum habens, præfecturam Bergensem reditibus istius absque insigni incommodo carere minime posse.

Quæ cum ita, uti dixi, se habeant, te eventum exspectationi tuæ non respondisse, pro tua prudentia æque laturum, studium vero ac voluntatem meam, quam tibi hac in re libentissime (quamvis incassum) impendi, in aliis vero, quibus potero, tibi testatam facere maximopere cupio, pro animi tui candore, synceraque æquitate, boni consulturum, plane confido, teque quam diutissime vivere et valere ex amino precor.

Dabantur Coldingi xx. januarii 1597.
Christianus Friis,
manu pp:a
.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe, lord of Knudstrup, his most dear friend.

Greeting. What you desire to have effected for you by me, in a twofold matter, with the most serene king, our most clement lord — most noble Tycho, most dear friend — that I have understood from your letter, which was delivered to me together with a copy of the letter of the magnificent senators of the realm; and that you might, in keeping with our friendship, be liberally satisfied through me in both [matters], I have omitted no diligence.

But I cannot conceal from you that the king's majesty, to whom I set forth your requests in utmost good faith, replied thus to that first matter concerning the astronomical instruments: that it is not open to him to expend any costs from the royal treasury on the upkeep of those instruments.

As for the other petition — which you frame concerning a most gracious extension of the grant of that Norwegian benefice, or at least [its extension] to that brief term named in your letter — his most serene majesty flatly refused to admit it, holding it as most firmly settled that the prefecture of Bergen can by no means do without the revenues of that [benefice] without notable detriment.

Since these things stand thus, as I have said, I am fully confident that you, in keeping with your prudence, will bear with equanimity that the outcome did not answer to your expectation, and that — by reason of the candour of your spirit and your sincere fairness — you will take in good part my zeal and goodwill, which I have most willingly (though in vain) expended for you in this matter, and which I greatly desire to make manifest to you in other things, in whatever I shall be able; and from my heart I pray that you may live and prosper as long as may be.

Given at Kolding, 20 January 1597.
Christian Friis,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Christian Friis of Borreby (1556–1616), appointed royal Chancellor (kongelig kansler) by the newly-crowned Christian IV in 1596. Friis was personally well-disposed toward Uraniborg but now served an assertive young monarch; this is the cold, formal reply that announced the collapse of Tycho's standing. The letter survives only as a later copy (afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium, Rigsarkivet, Copenhagen.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, here styled haeres in Knudstrup ("lord/heir of Knudstrup"), his Scanian family seat (this chapter's convention, Knudstrup kept as is).
  • Occasion and the two negocia. The "twofold matter" is Friis's point-by-point answer to Tycho's petition of 31 December 1596. (1) The primum negocium de instrumentis astronomicis — Tycho had sought a royal subvention from the treasury to maintain his instruments — is refused. (2) The altera petitio concerns the Norwegian fief of Nordfjord (whose revenues attached to a benefice/prebend Tycho had held): on 21 September 1596 it had been transferred from Tycho's jurisdiction to the Lord Lieutenant (prefect) of Bergen. Tycho had asked either for renewal of the grant or, failing that, to retain it only to the end of the fiscal year (1 May 1597) — "that brief term named in your letter." Both requests are denied. (Background: Dreyer, Tycho Brahe; On Tycho's Island; Lord of Uraniborg, all consulted.)
  • "beneficium Norvagicum" / "praefectura Bergensis." beneficium is the ecclesiastical/feudal benefice (the Nordfjord prebend and its revenues); praefectura Bergensis = the prefecture (lensmandskab) of Bergen, i.e. the jurisdiction of the Lord Lieutenant of Bergen, to which Nordfjord's revenues had been reassigned. Place-name Bergensem → "of Bergen."
  • Negation / legal force (verified count). The two operative refusals are rendered without adding or dropping a negation. (1) ex regio aerario aliquid sumptuum … impendere, sibi non esse integrum — one negation: "it is not open to him [= he is not free] to expend any costs from the treasury" (integrum esse alicui = "to be at one's free disposal/option"). (2) praefecturam Bergensem reditibus istius absque insigni incommodo carere minime posse — accusative-and-infinitive with two negating elements (absque "without"; minime "by no means") governing carere + ablative (reditibus istius "the revenues of that [benefice]"): "that the prefecture of Bergen can by no means do without the revenues of that [benefice] without notable detriment" — i.e. it cannot spare them. The doubled negation is the Latin idiom, not a logical double-negative reversing the sense; the king's refusal stands. plane recusavit ("flatly refused") is the third refusal and is a negating verb, not a particle.
  • Idiom / register. sibi non esse integrum glossed above. boni consulturum (sc. te) = "that you will take [it] in good part." quamvis incassum = "though in vain" — Friis's own hedge that his effort failed; preserved, not softened. manu pp:a = manu propria ("in his own hand," the chancery subscription); since the witness is a copy, this records the original's autograph subscription.
  • Salutation formula. Opening S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting".
  • Calendar / place. Dabantur Coldingi xx. januarii 1597 = "Given at Kolding, 20 January 1597." Denmark was on the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1597 (kept until 1700); the date is O.S. The dateline date (20 January) agrees with the edition label (1597. 20. januar. Kolding); no discrepancy.
