The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD AXILLUM GYLDENSTIERNE]

MIN gandske wenlig hilsen nu och altiid forsend met Vor Herre. Kierre Axell Gyldenstiern och kierre modersøster søn, Gud allmechtigste beuare dig naadelligen och well fraa allt ont, och tacker ieg dig gandske gierne for megit beuist gode, huilket igien att forskylde ieg stedtze vill findis aff formuen villig y huiß mode ieg kan giørre nogit, som dig till ære och gode kan werre. Kierre Axel, bekom ieg din skriffuelße met min søn Tyge, som kom hid hiem till mig vdigien nogit epther nøtaarßtag, och skalt du haffue tack for din gode wille, du derudj giffuer tillkiende, att mine wilkor och thillstand y denne landß eyen dig er angenem att høre. Allthingest er endnu, Gud ske loff, vdj samme wesen, som ieg dig tillskreff, och du ødermere aff Eske Bilde vden tuill haffuer forfaret, ieg habis, att all det her epther enda kan bliffue bedre, Gud giffue dertill lycke och benedidelse. Her war offuermøget farlig søgdom y forgangen aar, som bort tog en vtallelligen hoff folck, och keysern drog derudoffuer fra Prag till en fast bø ved naffn Pilsen, 10 mille derfra liggendis, hor hanß may. endnu er och icke wentendis till Prag vdigien førend epther paaske. Hanss may. haffde aff sig selff en naadigst omhu for mig och mine y samme farlige thiid och haffde nogle gange bod hid att lade forfare, horledis det gick oß, lod och naadigst tilbøde mig, att dersom ieg wilde drage nogen andenstedz hen paa itt andit aff hans may. slott, som icke so farligt war, da skulde det stande mig offuen for, och dersom ieg wilde drage hen till Wien y Osterich, da motte ieg det giørre, hor hanß may. wille ocsaa vdj thiide lade forskaffe mig god plaß och leylighed; menß ieg gaff vnderdanigst for suar igien, att ieg helst wille bliffue her och ware min bestilling, befallendis Gud allthing, huilken gode Gud och naadelligen haffuer bewarit mig och mine y samme skrøffuelige thiid.

Kierre Axell, som dit breff formelder om die penge, oss er vdimellom, att du icke kunde komme dennom adsted thill forgangen omslag y Lantheholsten, och est derfor begerendis, att die motte bliffue stoendis till dette nu tillkommendis omslag vdigien, saa haffde ieg wisselligen forlat mig till, att die forgangen omslag skulle haffue werrit tillrede epter din egen skriffuelßis lødelße, menß eptherdj det icke kunde ske, for ieg att haffue gedult forseendiß mig till, att die vist bliffuer vdlagt till dette nestkommendis omslag, eptersom du vdj dette dit andit breff wist loffuer och thilsier, thi mig er der macht paa ligendis, epter som Eske Bilde ved beskening om. Jeg lader opsige die penge, ieg haffuer staendis y Lanthemeckelburg, thi ieg wille gierne haffue dennom hid till mig, for ieg achter nest Gudtz hielp att kiøbe jore godz for dennom her vdj landit, som er for temmelig got kiøb att bekomme och god profit derhos. Och naar ieg formedelst keyserns autoritet och bistand bliffue[r] met rederskaffuens och adellenß samtøcke intagen for dette rigens indwonner och lim, huilket er letteligen att ske till nestkommendis herredag her vdj landit, saa maa ieg siden kiøbe och erbligen besede allt hues gotz, ieg kan for penge bekomme, ligesom ieg war indefød her vdj landit, och nøde alle die preueleger, som riderskaffuit her haffuer, huilke icke ringe er. Oc skulde dette allerede y denne nest forgangene herredag verre sked, haffde ieg vdj thiide kundit bekommit mine penge dertill. Forseer mig derfor till dig, kierre Axell, att du mig icke lenger opholder met die oss er emelom, paa det ieg kan søye mit gaffn och fordeel dermed vdj andre made. Och kanst du lade Eske Bilde bekome denom, om du icke selff vedst andre medel dertill, allthingest dermet bestille, thi hand ved vidre besken om min handell bode vdj Lantheholsten och Lanthemeckelburg.

Kiere Axel, jeg ved inted sønderligt nøt att skriffue dig till andet end die siger, at tørcken røster sig sterck, och woriß keyser giører och derimod hueß hannom mueligt er. Die tødske førster vill ingen bistand giøre for spanierenß skyld, som anfecter Tydskeland. Die tarter giorde y Nehr Vngern och Mehrland en stor skade y forgangen sommer paa en stackid thiid siiden, och førde mange folck bort. Horlediß keysern bekom Sibenburgen igien, haffuer du well hørt, Gud giffue, att det mote bliffue bestendigt [att han kunde beholde det met rolighed]. Kierre Axell, will ieg nu icke lenger opholde dig met denne skriffuelse, menß nu oc altiid haffue dig met alt, hueß du vel vilst, Gud allmechtigste befalit.

Daterit paa det keyserlige slot Benatky den 21 dag martij stylo nouo anni 1600.

[TYCHO BRAHE TO AXEL GYLDENSTIERNE]

My wholly friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with [the blessing of] Our Lord. Dear Axel Gyldenstierne, and dear cousin [mother's-sister's-son], may God Almighty preserve you graciously and well from all evil; and I thank you quite gladly for the much kindness shown, which to repay I shall always be found willing according to my means, in whatever way I can do anything to your honour and good. Dear Axel, I received your letter with my son Tyge, who came home to me again somewhat after New Year's Day; and you shall have thanks for the good will you declare therein, that my circumstances and condition in this region are agreeable to you to hear. Everything is still, God be praised, in the same state as I wrote you, and as you have doubtless further learned from Eske Bille; I hope all this may yet grow better hereafter — may God give fortune and blessing thereto. There was an exceedingly dangerous sickness here last year, which carried off an innumerable host of people, and the Emperor on that account moved from Prague to a strong town named Pilsen, lying 10 miles thence, where His Majesty still is, and not expected at Prague again before Easter. His Majesty had, of himself, a most gracious care for me and mine in that dangerous time, and several times sent a messenger to learn how it went with us, and graciously offered me that, if I would move to another of His Majesty's castles, not so dangerous, it should be at my choice; and if I would move to Vienna in Austria, I might do it, where His Majesty would in time provide me good place and accommodation. But I gave for answer, most humbly, that I would rather stay here and attend to my business, commending everything to God — which good God has graciously preserved me and mine in that dreadful time.

Dear Axel, as your letter mentions about the money [that is] between us, that you could not get it ready by the past Umschlag in the land of Holstein, and are therefore desirous that it remain standing until this now-coming Umschlag: I had certainly relied on its being ready by the past Umschlag, according to the tenor of your own letter; but since it could not be, I will have patience, trusting it will surely be paid out by this next Umschlag, as you in this your second letter surely promise and assure — for it is of importance to me, as Eske Bille knows the account of. I am giving notice on the money I have standing in the land of Mecklenburg, for I would gladly have it [come] here to me; for I intend, with God's help, to buy landed estates with it here in the country [Bohemia], which are to be had for a fairly good price, and with good profit besides. And when I, by the Emperor's authority and assistance, am received — with the consent of the [Council of the] Realm and the nobility — as an inhabitant and member of this realm (which is easily to be done at the next Diet here in the country), then I may afterward buy, and hold by inheritance, all the estates I can obtain for money, just as if I were native-born here, and enjoy all the privileges the nobility here has, which are not slight. And this should already have happened at this last past Diet, had I been able to get my money for it in time. I trust therefore, dear Axel, that you will not hold me up longer with the [money] that is between us, so that I may seek my benefit and advantage with it in other ways. And you can let Eske Bille receive it, if you do not yourself know other means thereto, and arrange everything therewith — for he knows further account of my dealings both in the land of Holstein and the land of Mecklenburg.

Dear Axel, I know nothing special as news to write you, except that they say the Turk stirs himself strongly, and our Emperor does against him what he can. The German princes will give no assistance, on account of the Spaniards, who trouble Germany. The Tartars did great damage in Lower Hungary and Moravia last summer, a short time since, and carried off many people. How the Emperor got Transylvania back, you have well heard; God grant it may be lasting, [that he may keep it in peace]. Dear Axel, I will not now detain you longer with this letter, but, now and always, commend you, with all you wish well, to God Almighty.

Dated at the imperial castle Benátky, the 21st day of March, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient & kinship. Axel Gyldenstierne (1542–1603), Statholder of Norway — Tycho's maternal first cousin (modersøstersøn, "mother's-sister's-son"; their mothers were Bille sisters). This is Tycho's reply to Axel's letter of 3 Dec 1599 (brahe-t_15991203001). One of the cluster of same-day Danish family letters (21 March 1600 N.S.; cf. brahe-t_16000321004 to his mother, brahe-t_16000321005 to his brother Steen).
  • The plague & the Emperor's care. The same account as the other family letters: the 1599 plague ("carried off an innumerable host"), Rudolf II's withdrawal to Pilsen, and his repeated solicitude and offer to relocate Tycho — even to Vienna — which Tycho declined to "stay here and attend to my business."
  • Significance — Tycho's plan to naturalize in Bohemia and buy estates (a key biographical document). Tycho explains why he is calling in the Mecklenburg money (Axel's deferred debt likewise pressed): he means to buy landed estates in Bohemia ("to be had for a fairly good price, with good profit"). And he states his naturalization plan: "when I, by the Emperor's authority and the consent of the [Council of the] Realm and the nobility, am received as an inhabitant and member of this realm — easily done at the next Diet — then I may buy and hold estates by inheritance, as if native-born, and enjoy all the [Bohemian] noble privileges." He regrets it could not be done at the last Diet for lack of ready money. This is the clearest statement of Tycho's late ambition to root himself permanently in Bohemia as a naturalized noble landowner (cf. the estate-purchase mentioned to Duke Ulrich, brahe-t_16010410001) — a plan cut short by his death in October 1601.
  • The debt. Axel could not pay "the money between us" by the past Kiel Umschlag; Tycho grants delay to the next, with Eske Bille (who "knows the account of my dealings in Holstein and Mecklenburg") empowered to receive it.
  • News. The Turk stirring; the German princes refusing aid "on account of the Spaniards"; the Tartar raids into Lower Hungary and Moravia; Transylvania recovered by the Emperor (cf. brahe-t_16000328002, brahe-t_16000329001).
  • Persons named. Axel Gyldenstierne; Eske Bille; Tyge (Tycho the Younger); Rudolf II; the Turk, the German princes, the Tartars.
  • Calendar. Dateline Benatky den 21 dag Martij stylo novo anni 1600; New Style (Prague). Header agrees with the label.
