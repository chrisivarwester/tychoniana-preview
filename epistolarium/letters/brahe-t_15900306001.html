The original

TYGE BRAHE FICK BREFF ETT AFF HANS BØRNN ANRØRENNDIS VT SEQUITUR

Sophia etc.

WOR gunst thilfornn. Wider, att ßom j wnderdannigst lader giffue thilkiennde, att ßom j epther thennd sthormegthigste hogbornne første och herris her Fr[e]drich thend annden, Danmarckis, Norgis etc. konning, Wor kierre sallig affganngnne here och gemhals begierre, epther thennd naade och begaffuede forstanndtt, ßom j mett synderlig flid och sthuderinng, jcke wden stor omkostning, haffuer forhuerffuitt wdj thennd lofflige astronomiæ konnst, haffuer paa thennd jnßel Huen anretthitt och fulddreffuitt enn ahnßehelig bygning, och ther wdinnden ßynnderlig ny och fornemme jnstrumennther thill ßamme lofflig astro[no]miæ konnstis jnstauration och forbedrinng haffuer forhuerffuitt och ther thill forordnnitt, saa att hogbemelte Wor kierre ßalig herre haffuer haffdt tthill ßlig ethers flitthig forneme och lofflig konnst en synderlig gefallenn och welbehag, och epther att ßamme lofflige konnst er aff ether ther paa lannditt anretthitt och fuldkommitt, haffuer j althiid [ønsket], att epther ethers dødtt och affganng enn aff ethers egnne wdj synderlighed, ßom wdj samme lofflig konnst kunnde findis schickelig och dueligh, motthe ther thill forordnnis, och ßamme jnnßell mett hues ther paa anretthett, stiffthett och fuldkommitt er, niude och beholde, och therforre haffuer haffd wdj achtt och forßett, hoes hogbemelte Wor kierre sallige herre att wille paa thett wnnderdannigst forhuerffuitt Hanns Kierlighetz thilladellße, att therßom nogen aff ethers egne børnn kunde kiendis ther thill duelig och skickeligh, thennd tha for andre ßamme jnßell Huenn mett hues ther anretthitt er att schulle werre nest att bekomme, och therfor er aff Oß wnnderdannigst begierrendis, att Wij naadigst wille bekiennde och ßige hueß Wij haffue hørtt aff hogbemelte Wor kierre sallige herre om ßamme ethers forßett och menning. Tha wille Wij paa ßlige ethers wnnderdannigste begierre naadigst jcke forholde, att Wij weed Oß naadigst att erindre och er aldellis wittherlig, att Wij haffue hørtt aff hogbemelte Wor kierre sallige herre, nogenn thiid for Hans Kierlighedtz dødelig affganng, attj Hannß Kierlighet thett schall haffue forgiffuitt att wille jnstituere enn aff ethers egnne børnn, ßom schulle findis schichelig och duelig wdj ßamme lofflig astronomie konnst, huilke Wij well jnndnu kannd thilßinde føre och erachthe, och haffue hørtt aff hogbemelte Wor kierre sallige herre, och wille ßligtt paa ethers wnnderdannigst anlangende ether naadigst jcke forennttholde, och althiid ellers mett ald gunst och naade ether werre beuogenn.

Befallendis etc.
Actum Haffniæ thend 6. martij anno 1590.

Tyge Brahe received a letter, one concerning his children, as follows:

Sophie, etc.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, as you most humbly cause it to be made known — that, in accordance with the desire of the most mighty, high-born prince and lord, lord Frederik the Second, King of Denmark, Norway, etc., our dear departed lord and consort of blessed memory; and in accordance with the grace and the gifted understanding that you, with singular diligence and study, [and] not without great cost, have acquired in the laudable art of astronomy — you have set up and carried through, upon that island Hven, a considerable building, and have therein acquired and appointed singular new and excellent instruments for the renewal [instauration] and improvement of that same laudable art of astronomy, so that our aforementioned dear lord of blessed memory had toward such diligent, excellent, and laudable art of yours a singular liking and good pleasure; and inasmuch as that same laudable art has been set up and brought to completion by you there on the land [i.e. on Hven], you have always [wished] that, after your death and decease, one of your own [children] in particular — such as might be found fit and able in that same laudable art — might be appointed thereto, and might enjoy and keep that same island, together with whatever has been established, founded, and completed upon it; and you have therefore had it in mind and intent to wish to obtain, in the most humble manner, from our aforementioned dear lord of blessed memory His Love's permission, that if any of your own children might be found able and fit thereto, that one should then, before [i.e. in preference to] others, be the next to obtain that same island Hven with whatever has been set up [there]; and it is therefore most humbly desired of us that we would graciously acknowledge and declare what we have heard from our aforementioned dear lord of blessed memory concerning that same intent and meaning of yours. Then upon such most humble desire of yours we would graciously not withhold [the reply] that we graciously call to mind, and it is entirely known to us, that we have heard from our aforementioned dear lord of blessed memory, some while before His Love's mortal decease, that His Love is said to have declared that he would institute one of your own children, such as might be found fit and able in that same laudable art of astronomy — which [matter] we can indeed still recall to mind and bear in remembrance, and have heard from our aforementioned dear lord of blessed memory; and we would, upon your most humble petition, graciously not withhold the same from you, and would in all else always be inclined toward you with all favour and grace.

Commending [you to God], etc.
Done at Copenhagen, the 6th of March, the year 1590.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Sophie of Mecklenburg (1557–1631), queen dowager of Denmark, widow of Frederik II (d. 4 April 1588). The letter is a chancery-register entry from her own register (Dronning Sophies tegnelser), written during the minority of her son Christian IV (b. 1577; crowned 1596), when she was active in royal business. She had a personal interest in astronomy and astrology and had visited Tycho on Hven.
  • Wor kierre sallig affganngnne here och gemhals — "our dear departed lord and consort of blessed memory," i.e. Frederik II, who in 1576 had granted Tycho the island of Hven (Huen / jnßel Huen, Latin Hvenna / Venusia). The whole letter is Sophie's response to a petition (supplication) from Tycho, asking her to confirm, from her own memory, a verbal undertaking of the late king.
  • Document type & rubric. A chancery-register copy; the Latin rubric "…HANS BØRNN ANRØRENNDIS ut sequitur" ("concerning his children, as follows") heads the Danish body — a code-switch preserved by the edition's convention, exactly as in the Frederik II compass-letter of 1584. ANRØRENNDIS = anrørendes ("concerning, touching"). Sophia etc. abbreviates the full royal style.
  • Substance. The petition concerns the hereditary succession to Hven and Uraniborg: Tycho asks the dowager to attest that Frederik II had verbally promised that, after Tycho's death, one of Tycho's own children — if found schickelig och dueligh ("fit and able") in astronomy — should have first claim to the island and its establishment (the buildings and instruments). Sophie confirms she distinctly remembers hearing the late king say so, "some while before his mortal decease." The undertaking was never honoured: after Tycho left Denmark (1597) Hven reverted to the crown.
  • astronomiae konnst … jnstauration och forbedrinng — "the art of astronomy … its renewal [instauration] and improvement." jnstauration is a direct Latin loan (instauratio) and is Tycho's own programmatic term (the edition's convention: instauratio astronomiae = "the renewal of astronomy," astronomia instaurata); kept as renewal [instauration]. konnst here = "art / science / skill," the period sense, not modern "art."
  • jnstrumennther — "instruments," the Uraniborg astronomical instruments. The Danish uses the Latin loan directly.
  • Hanns Kierlighet / Hans Kierlighedtz — "His Love / His Love's": the chancery honorific Hans Kærlighed (German Seine Liebden, Latin Dilectio Sua), a formal third-person style used between and of high princes. Rendered literally as His Love and flagged here; it denotes the late king, not affection in the ordinary sense.
  • begaffuede forstanndtt — "gifted understanding" (begavet forstand), i.e. natural talent / endowed intelligence.
  • Honorifics & register. Throughout, the queen writes of herself in the royal plural (Wij "We," Oß "us") and addresses Tycho with the deferential second-person plural (j / ether / ethers "you / you / your"), while Tycho's own act is repeatedly marked wnderdannigst / wnnderdannigste ("most humbly / most humble"), the subject-to-sovereign register; preserved. WOR gunst thilfornn = Vor gunst tilforn, "Our favour beforehand" (the royal address-formula, the edition's convention).
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1590). Heavy German-influenced spelling (sch-, w-, -tt, ß, doubled n/l) that can fool automatic language detection — reading-verified as Danish, not German. Selected forms: Wider = Vider ("Know ye"); j = I ("you," pl.); epther = efter ("according to / after"); sthormegthigste = stormægtigste ("most mighty"); hogbornne = højbårne ("high-born"); gemhals = gemal(s) ("consort"); forhuerffuitt = forhvervet ("acquired / obtained"); anretthitt = anrettet ("set up / established"); fulddreffuitt / fuldkommitt = fuldrevet / fuldkommet ("carried through / completed"); ahnßehelig = anselig ("considerable, imposing"); gefallenn och welbehag = gefallen og velbehag (German-loan doublet, "liking and good pleasure"); schickelig och dueligh = skikkelig og dygtig ("fit and able"); forordnnis = forordnes ("be appointed"); niude och beholde = nyde og beholde ("enjoy and keep"); forßett och menning = forsæt og mening ("intent and meaning"); erachthe = agte / erakte ("bear in mind, deem"); forennttholde / forholde = forholde ("withhold"); beuogenn = bevågen ("favourably inclined").
  • [ønsket] — at haffuer j althiid [ønsket] the source/edition supplies the verb "[wished]" in brackets, the construction otherwise lacking its main verb; the bracketed editorial supplement is carried into the translation as [wished].
  • Calendar / place. Actum Haffniae thend 6. martij anno 1590 = "Done at Copenhagen (Hafnia), the 6th of March, the year 1590" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar; the edition's convention). Actum "Done/Given" kept by the edition's convention Befallendis etc. (= befalendes, "commending [you to God / to God's protection]") is the closing-formula head, abbreviated in the register.
