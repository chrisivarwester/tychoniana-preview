The original

ET NATALIUM SPLENDORE ET INGENIJ PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO DN. TYCHONI BRAHÆO OTTONIDI, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO COLENDO.
S.

REDIERUNT e Dania vestra, Nobilissime Domine TYCHO, superiore anno GEMPERLINUS, nuper Aurifaber, quorum neuter me accessit, uterque tamen per alios salutem mihi tuo nomine dixit. Etsi autem speraveram te pro necessitudine nostra ne absentis quidem mei memoriam abiecturum, tamen cum astra non quidem exitium (id enim a religione nostra et saniore Philosophia esset alienum) sed vitæ saltem periculum superiore anno, te autore, mihi portenderint, minus miror, me utcunque viventem in mortuis abs te numerari, si forte prædictio illa apud te mollior fuit, quam mihi tunc indicare voluisti, ideoque supervacaneis litteris supersedisse. Memineris tamen, Ascendens meum necdum Saturni Anaretæ oppositum attigisse, quod ex tua quoque supputatione circa annum ætatis meæ 65 demum eveniet, quem etiam ob id ex Astrologicis ratiocinijs fatalem mihi esse insinuasti, a quo, ut conscius sis, si forte mea ætas et nostra colloquia tibi memoria exciderunt, adhuc triennio absum. Aiebas etiam fieri posse, ut directionem Horoscopi ad Trinum suum proprium circa annum 68 incidentem assequerer, plerumque id concedi ijs, qui Aquarium ascendentem obtinent, mihi revelasti, quando de his et similibus familiariter et iucunde sermones miscuimus, atque e thesauris tuis Astrologicis, quos alias in abscondito habes, mihi quædam impartiri voluisti, quorum etiamnum magna animi voluptate recordor. Quare cum adhuc et vivam et qualitercunque valeam, quod te non ignorare arbitror, qui tam probe meam Genesin et hinc pendentia fata nosti, quatenus humanæ subiacent industriæ, nullatenus eam tibi inhumanitatem tribuo, ut me, quamvis pauperem et genere ignobilem, sed a Musis ditatum et nobilitatum asperneris, vel superbiam, ut secundis rebus elatus, neminem tenuioris fortunæ hominem amicitia tua dignum existimes, vel calliditatem, ut benevolentiam pro tempore ex loco simules. Sed facessant hæc omnia, indigna generosa natura, qualem esse tuam e diutina nostra conversatione expertus sum, pleneque persuasum habeo. De me autem sic existimes velim, me tibi et viventem et mortuum fore amicum.

Vale.
Augustæ Vindelicorum. 9. Maij Anno 1578.
Hieronymus Wolfius.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To a man most pre-eminent both in the splendour of his birth and in the excellence of his genius, Master Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, his lord and friend to be honoured.
Greeting.

There returned from your Denmark last year Gemperlinus, lately a goldsmith — neither of whom came to me, yet each, through others, conveyed a greeting to me in your name. And although I had hoped that, given the bond between us, you would not cast off the memory even of me in my absence, yet since the stars portended to me last year — on your authority — not indeed destruction (for that would be foreign to our religion and to a sounder philosophy), but at least a danger to life, I wonder the less that I, living however I may, am reckoned among the dead by you, if perhaps that prediction was milder with you than you then chose to disclose to me, and that you therefore forbore from a superfluous letter. Yet you should recall that my ascendant has not yet reached the opposition of Saturn the Anareta, which by your reckoning too will come about only around my sixty-fifth year of age — which year, for that very reason, you intimated from astrological reasonings to be fatal to me; and from that year, so that you may be aware (in case perhaps my age and our conversations have slipped your memory), I am still three years distant. You also said it could come to pass that I should attain the direction of the horoscope to its own proper trine, falling around the sixty-eighth year; you revealed to me that this is for the most part granted to those who have Aquarius as the ascendant — when we mingled discourse about these and like matters in a familiar and pleasant way, and you were willing to impart to me certain things out of your astrological treasures, which otherwise you keep hidden, things which even now I recall with great delight of mind. Wherefore, since I both still live and am, however that may be, in health — which I judge you do not fail to know, you who so thoroughly know my geniture and the fates that hang from it, in so far as they lie subject to human industry — I in no way ascribe to you that inhumanity, that you should spurn me, poor though I am and of ignoble birth but enriched and ennobled by the Muses; nor that arrogance, that, exalted by good fortune, you should judge no man of slenderer means worthy of your friendship; nor that craftiness, that you should feign goodwill for the time, as the place requires. But away with all these things, unworthy of a generous nature such as I have found yours to be from our long converse, and of which I am fully persuaded. As for me, I would have you so judge: that I shall be your friend both living and dead.

Farewell.
At Augsburg, 9 May, the year 1578.
Hieronymus Wolf.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Hieronymus Wolfius = Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and Greek scholar (the "father of Byzantine studies"), then directing the city's library and school; an early mentor-friend of Tycho's from his Augsburg years (1569–70). The same correspondent as the 13 Nov. 1571 letter (brahe-t_15711113001), and continuing its themes — Wolf's own horoscope as cast by Tycho, and his astrologically-feared old age.
  • Ottonides — "son of Otte," a Greek-style patronymic for Otte Brahe, Tycho's father (kept, as in the 1571 letter).
  • Gemperlinus, nuper Aurifaber — "Gemperlinus, lately a goldsmith": personal crux, flagged. The Latin reads as one or two travellers returned from Denmark. Gemperlinus is most plausibly the Augsburg painter Abdon (or Tobias) Gemperle / Gämperlin, of the Augsburg artist family; nuper Aurifaber ("lately/recently a goldsmith") is left as a literal apposition rather than resolved into a named second man — the syntax (quorum neuter … uterque, "neither of whom … each") requires two persons, but the text names only the one and characterises the other only by trade. The pairing is recorded, not invented; do not assert a single identity for "Aurifaber."
  • necessitudine nostra — "the bond/tie between us" (a close personal connection), rendered "the bond between us."
  • Astrology. The letter is built on the natal figure Tycho had cast for Wolf (cf. the 1571 letter):
  • Ascendens meum necdum Saturni Anaretae oppositum attigisse — "my ascendant has not yet reached the opposition of Saturn the Anareta." Anareta (ἀναιρέτης, "the destroyer/killer") is the technical astrological term for the planet that, by direction, marks the native's death; here Saturn is identified as Wolf's anaretic planet. The term is kept (Latinised Anareta) and glossed; oppositum rendered "opposition" by this chapter's convention
  • directionem Horoscopi ad Trinum suum proprium — "the direction of the horoscope to its own proper trine" (a predictive direction,; Trinum = the trine aspect, aspectus; Horoscopi here = the ascendant/natal degree). Kept as a directional technique.
  • Aquarium ascendentem — "Aquarius as the ascendant"; zodiac sign named, no glyph in source.
  • Genesin … et hinc pendentia fata — "my geniture and the fates that hang from it" (genesis → geniture,).
  • e thesauris tuis Astrologicis, quos alias in abscondito habes — "out of your astrological treasures, which otherwise you keep hidden": Wolf alludes to Tycho's reticence in sharing his astrological methods.
  • Theological hedge — preserved. astra non quidem exitium (id enim a religione nostra et saniore Philosophia esset alienum) sed vitae saltem periculum … portenderint — Wolf is careful to deny that the stars portend outright destruction (which would offend "our religion and a sounder philosophy"), only a danger to life. The qualifier is rendered intact, not sharpened into a flat prediction of death.
  • Age arithmetic (consistent, recorded not corrected). Wolf (b. 1516) writes in 1578 at age 62; he says the anaretic year is his 65th and that he is "still three years distant" (adhuc triennio absum) from it — 62 + 3 = 65. Internally consistent. The further directio to the trine he places "around the sixty-eighth year." No date discrepancy.
  • Sub-text. The letter's occasion is Wolf's gentle reproach that Tycho had fallen silent — Wolf surmising that Tycho, believing his own death-prediction, had written him off "among the dead" (in mortuis … numerari) and so spared a "superfluous letter." The closing protestation (me tibi et viventem et mortuum fore amicum, "I shall be your friend both living and dead") answers that surmise.
  • Dateline. Augustae Vindelicorum. 9. Maij Anno 1578 = "at Augsburg, 9 May 1578" (O.S.). The edition label ("1578. 9. maj. Augsburg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
