The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD JACOBUM BORDINGUM].
Magnifice et excellentissime vir, amice inprimis honorande.

ACCEPI tandem diu desideratum responsum Magnif: Tuæ ad petitionem meam bis vel ter reiteratam nimirum de 600 joachimicis, qui loco interesse a quæstore juniorum principum Megapolensium isthic mihi debentur, et simul caussas, cur expectatione mea tardius M: T: mihi de hoc ipso negotio rescripserit, ex ijs quas 19. septembris Gustrouio ad me dedit, intellexi. Quibus etiam acquiesco. Sexcentos vero illos joachimicos ad clarissimum virum d. d. Lucam Backmeisterum, affinem tuum et communem nostrum amicum, missos esse, lubenter ex ijsdem percepi. Scribit is ad me, se pecuniam illam secum reseruaturum, donec illustriss: Saxoniæ electoratus administratoris magnificus legatus d:ns d. Gödelmannus illuc aduenerit. Quod etiam mihi non est ingratum, siquidem jam antea ipsum ob penuriam occasionum aliarum tuto mittendi, vt ita faceret, admonuerim. At cum prædictus d:ns Gödellmannus, tardius quam sperassemus, hinc discedat, adeo vt vix ante initia anni Juliani vestri Rostochium sit perventurus, nimis diu pecunia ista careo, quod tamen, siquidem aliter fieri non potuit, ferendum. Quia vero postea circiter quadrans anni solummodo restabit ad completionem ejus temporis, quo reliqui 600 loco hujus anni interesse numerandi sunt, Mag: T: enixe et peramanter rogatam habeo, vt efficere velit, totam summam a quæstore juniorum principum exsolui, et prædicto d:no Gödellmanno, dum isthic est, committi, ita vt 1200 joachimici, qui mihi ad instantis anni calendas aprilis pro duobus transactis annis debentur, pariter cedant, neque enim id iniquum censeri poterit, licet pauculi menses restent ad terminum præfinitum, quo sic aliquatenus compensetur ea, quam modo passus sum, plus quam dimidij anni frustratio. Quod autem id per eundem fieri quam maxime desiderem, duæ me impellunt graues caussæ, quarum vnam ex ipso d:no Godelmanno aut saltem ab affine tuo cognosces. Altera est, quod tam certam et tutam rationem 600 illos alios circa initia aprilis instantis anni huc adferendi non invenire potero quam hæc ipsa est per sæpedictum d:nm d. Gödellmannum, amicum meum singularem, huc expeditis Rostochij suis rebus, quam citissime fieri poterit, reuertentem, qui etiam suam mihi hac in re operam beneuole addixit. Quin et hoc Magnificentiam Tuam celare non possum, si id ipsi prius forte non innotuit, me certis de caussis et deliberato consilio totam summam decem illorum millium joachimicorum, quam illustrissimis junioribus principibus mutuo dedi, ad præfinitum terminum juxta tenorem contractus nostri reposcere, quod et antea d:no quæstori indicari jussi. Et jam denuo ipsum de eodem per literas admonui, neque enim ex re mea est, vt pecunia illa in loco hinc adeo remoto, a quo interesse statuto tempore huc non sine periculo et difficultate peruenire possit, diutius hær[eat], cum etiam alias plus commodi et usufructus intermediante eadem pecunia hic nancisci queam, veluti ipse quoque d:ns d. Gödellmannus plenius meum hac de re propositum aperiet. Quibus igitur possum precibus M: T: rogatam habeo, vt votis meis in hisce per omnia adesse non degrauetur. Ego, quæcumque a me vicissim proficisci poterint officia, eidem quam paratissimo animo defero, atque M: Tuam cum conjuge et familia quam diutissime et optime valere a Deo ter optimo maximo opto.

Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum die 15to nouembris a:o 1600.

[TYCHO BRAHE TO JACOB BORDING].
Magnificent and most excellent man, friend especially to be honoured.

I have at last received Your Magnificence's long-desired reply to my petition, twice or thrice repeated — namely concerning the 600 joachimsthalers which are owed me there, in lieu of interest, by the treasurer of the younger princes of Mecklenburg — and at the same time I have understood, from those [letters] you sent me from Güstrow on 19 September, the reasons why Your Magnificence wrote back to me on this business later than I expected. With which I also acquiesce. That those six hundred joachimsthalers were sent to the most renowned man Lord Doctor Lucas Backmeister, your kinsman and our common friend, I gladly gathered from the same. He writes to me that he will keep that money with him until the magnificent legate of the most illustrious Administrator of the Saxon Electorate, Lord Doctor Gödelmann, shall arrive there — which is not unwelcome to me, since I had already advised him to do so, for the scarcity of other ways of sending it safely. But since the said Lord Gödelmann departs hence later than we had hoped — so that he will scarcely reach Rostock before the beginning of your Julian year — I am too long without that money; which, however, since it could not be otherwise, must be borne. And since afterward only about a quarter-year will remain to the completion of the time at which the other 600, in lieu of this year's interest, are to be paid, I earnestly and lovingly ask Your Magnificence to bring it about that the whole sum be paid out by the treasurer of the younger princes, and entrusted to the said Lord Gödelmann while he is there, so that the 1,200 joachimsthalers owed me by the Kalends of April of the coming year, for the two elapsed years, may come to me together — nor can this be judged unfair, though a few months remain to the appointed term, that thereby the frustration of more than half a year, which I have just suffered, may be somewhat compensated. That I most greatly desire this be done through the same [Gödelmann], two weighty reasons impel me: one you will learn from Lord Gödelmann himself, or at least from your kinsman; the other is that I shall find no way so sure and safe of bringing those other 600 hither, about the beginning of April of the coming year, as this very one — through the oft-mentioned Lord Gödelmann, my especial friend, returning hither as quickly as may be once his affairs at Rostock are dispatched, who has also kindly promised me his help in this matter. Moreover I cannot conceal from Your Magnificence, if it has not perhaps become known to you before, that I — for certain reasons and by deliberate counsel — demand back the whole sum of those ten thousand joachimsthalers which I lent to the most illustrious younger princes, at the appointed term according to the tenor of our contract, which I also previously ordered to be indicated to the treasurer; and now again I have admonished him of the same by letter. For it is not to my advantage that that money remain longer in a place so remote from here, whence the interest cannot reach hither at the set time without danger and difficulty, since I can otherwise obtain more advantage and usufruct with the same money here — as Lord Gödelmann too will more fully disclose my purpose. With what prayers I can, therefore, I ask Your Magnificence not to find it burdensome to assist my wishes in these things in every way. I, in turn, offer you with most ready mind whatever services can proceed from me, and wish from God thrice best and greatest that Your Magnificence may fare most long and well, with your wife and household.

Given at Prague of the Bohemians, the 15th day of November, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient & companion letter. Jacob Bording of Lübeck (cf. brahe-t_16000331001, brahe-t_16010412001). This is the Latin companion to Tycho's same-day German letter to the rent-master Andreas Meier (brahe-t_16001115002, Prague, 15 Nov 1600): the two coordinate the same collection.
  • Position in the Mecklenburg debt-thread. Acknowledging Bording's reply (from Güstrow, 19 Sept 1600), Tycho confirms the first 600-thaler interest tranche was sent to Lucas Backmeister (Superintendent of Rostock, Bording's kinsman), who holds it until Gödelmann — here styled "legate of the Administrator of the Saxon Electorate" — reaches Rostock (scarcely "before the beginning of your Julian year," i.e. O.S. New Year). Tycho asks that the whole 1,200 thalers (two years' interest, due by 1 April 1601) be paid out together to Gödelmann now, to compensate the half-year's delay; and re-announces that he is calling in the entire 10,000-thaler principal at the contract term, "since I can obtain more advantage with the money here" in Bohemia.
  • Persons named. Jacob Bording; Lucas Backmeister (Bacmeister, Superintendent of Rostock); Johann Georg Gödelmann (now legate of the Saxon Administrator); the treasurer of the younger Mecklenburg princes; the younger princes (Duke Johann's sons, cf. brahe-t_16000920002).
  • Calendar note. Tycho's phrase initia anni Juliani vestri — "the beginning of your Julian year" — neatly marks the O.S./N.S. divide: Bording's Lübeck and Rostock keep Julian, Tycho's Prague keeps Gregorian. Dateline Pragae … die 15to Novembris a:o 1600, New Style; header agrees with the label.
  • Abbreviations. Chancery contractions (M: T: = Magnificentia Tua, d:ns d. = dominus doctor, a:o) silently expanded; the source block keeps them.
