The original

GENEROSO ET VIRTUTE AC LITERIS HUMANIORIBUS EXCELLENTI VIRO D. OLIGERO ROSENKRANTZIO, DOMINO DE ROSENHOLM, COGNATO AFFINI AC AMICO SUO INTEGERRIMO ET DESIDERATISSIMO.
S. P.

TUAS Rosenholmio in initio Martij ad me dedisti, Generose OLIGERE, Consangvinee et affinis exoptatissime, circa 24tum eiusdem recte accepi, mihique fuere longe gratissimae, ad quarum contenta, ubi opus fuerit, nunc brevibus respondebo, postea alia quaedam subiuncturus. ERICUS SPARRE (quem tu per festinationem BRAHE vocas) hac non transivit, sed alia via Rostochium declinavit, ubi cum Doctore STURTZIO locutus grandia pollicitus est, quae apud Regem suum, compositis potissimum Svecicis rebus, meo nomine efficere vellet. Sed ego talibus Politicis et politis verbis tantum fidei adhibeo, quantum et tu. De Coldingensi conventu nondum certi quid nobis constat. Quidam Hamburgensibus, quidam Suecis irrequietandis bellum destinatum fuisse autumant. Exitus acta probat. RANZOVIUS noster victor quidem in sua caussa Haderslebiae fuit, idque iure merito. At sententiam in adversarium unâ cum exsequatione obtinere nequivit, utut Rex admodum illi infensus fuerit. Quam primum enim is, qui se periclitari animadvertit, [Cancellarium accessit, iniurias, non saltem RANZOVIO], sed et [ipsi Regi illatas superavit manum forte non vacuam porrigens]. De [PEPLERO] quod pudore quosdam affeceris, si modo quem habent, recte quidem a te factum, sed nihil tam iniquum est, quin si mihi ullo modo adversetur, aequum sit. Ego vero nihil inde moveor, saltem hominum quorundam effrenem malitiam et conscientiam nimis latam demiror, quibus sibi ipsi plus nocent quam mihi. Iudex generalis [Bergensis], qui cum suis assessoribus sententiam protulit, eam facile tuebitur, si ius locum invenerit, sin minus, transeant ista cum caeteris. Ego me ijs, nisi admodum fuerit necessarium, non miscebo. Interim tamen, quae a me accepisti, [Laumanno] communica, ut, quaecunque ex ijs probationis contrariae loco expetierit, habeat, aut etiam [Praefectus meus] prius ab [ESCILLO BILLE et te] instinctus illa proponet. Addam adhuc plura, si quae invenero huc facientia. Sententiam conscriptam iudex ipsemet adferet, nec difficulter obiectionibus singulis obviabit. Scripsi ad illum nonnulla hac de re, sed nihil adhuc responsi accepi. Citationem unâ misi, quam is tamen forte prius obtinuit. Mihi nihil de ea, [nisi ex te] constat. An [Uraniburgi] lecta sit, aut adhuc legenda, ignoro. Sed quidquid sit, perinde est, nec teneor secundum Leges, si modo pondus haberent, citationi illegitimae et non annuae auscultare, etiamsi res maioris esset momenti. Dette er lige saa gott som allt dett andit; naar de erre trette aff dette, saa kand de finde paa nogen ny Huffroe. Ego interim alia cogito. Quod tam fuse cum [Cancellarij uxore] de me rebusque meis locutus sis, recte et cordate a te factum. Vellem adhuc particulariora scire, atque ut ea ipsa per occasionem (dummodo induciae inter vos sunt) denuo inculcares, additis commentarijs et amplificationibus. Existimant [Machiavellistae isti] suas machinationes ita latere, ut nemo in [Dania] praeter authores eas intelligat. Interim exterae nationes de his loquuntur et scribunt; uti vel ex hac epistola mihi a tuo discessu missa, quam prope rem ipsam in Anglia attigerint, cognosces. Ipsum originale addidi, mihi, ubi descripseris, remittendum, ut eo certius probetur, me nihil in eo mutasse. Doctor IANUS MULIUS, qui hîc nuper apud me fuit, Doctorem illum, qui ea scripsit, in Italia, antequam promotus est, novit, et ex alijs literis ad se datis eius, uti mihi retulit, chirographum cognovit. Is tibi secreto sciscitanti plura referet. Nam et illi exemplum, ut sibi describeret, permisi, quo eo facilius [Machiavellistis] innotesceret, atque ut hinc perciperent, quam caute et prudenter sibi ipsis consuluerint. Quid eruditi passim in Germania atque alibi de mea isthinc emigratione sentiant, longum foret referre, neque nostratibus forte satis honorificum. Habes hîc unâ solius MONAVIJ Wratislaviensis literas ad me nuperas, quas cupio mihi remitti, quibus in quantum hunc casum exaggeret, percipies, adeo ut quodam in loco nimis hyperbolice loquatur. Det var noch hand var en Jyde. Ego semper Regem nostrum Serenissimum, aliosque in patria cordatos viros excusare soleo. Caeteri satis sunt [famosi] tam intra quam extra Regnum, ut me notatore non indigeant. Exemi quoque et Regem et quosvis integros viros ab ijs, quae mihi evenerunt, in prioribus meis ad MONAVIUM. Hinc est, quod ait, me recte facere, quod discernam actorem a Poëta fabulae. Quod vero de administratore nihil apud [praedictam] attuleris, consulte factum est. De ULFELDIO quod scribis, eum ementita omnia, quae tacite tibi significaram, asseverasse, equidem facile ita esse credo, cum nunquam pro veris ea habuerim, cumque multa alia ijs graviora efficta sciam.

GERHARDO RANZOVIO de equis scripsi, prout a te indicatum est, nec tamen me eos expetere ante festum Pentecostes. Victualia iam antea intellexi in Dania et cara esse, et ne eveherentur prohibitum. Nescio tamen, quo iure viro e familia Nobili isthic oriundo, qui in oeconomiae propriae subsidium ijs uti vellet, nec lucrum aut mercaturam circa ea exercere, interdictio obstare possit, praesertim si per alium virum nobilem, non autem mercatores negotium hoc procuretur. Sed cum maioribus in Patria carere possim, cur non et istis? Curabo aliunde e vicinis locis minoribus (uti spero) difficultatibus id effectum dari. Interim tua propensa voluntas mihi apprime grata est. Baronem illum, cuius inter nos mentio facta, in Austriam abijsse intellexi, nec scitur, an reversus sit. De maledico isto scripto, cuius caussa ad ipsum literas dare volui, non sum sollicitus, nam author eius tanta impudentia verecundiae et veritatis limites in eo superavit, ut nulla alia redargutione, quam propria ipsius temeritate et scurrilitate opus habeat, prout Eruditi et cordati omnes fatentur, uti colliges ex postscriptis quibusdam ad THADDÆUM, quid hac de re mihi Helmstadio significatum sit; ubi etiam appositum est, quid Mathematicus Wittebergensis Rostochium ad CHRISTIERNUM SEVERINI, qui mecum per aliquot annos fuit, perscripserit. Habes quoque exemplum literarum ROLLENHAGIJ, quas his ipsis diebus accepi, qui fusius de his agit et librum istum scurrilem unâ misit, quem ERICO LANGIO destinavi, siquidem author eum semel atque iterum isthic pro testimonio in re falsissima alleget. Verum iste calumniator alia ratione per oportunitatem castigandus est; ubi superfluum habuero exemplar, tibi mittam; aut obtinebis forte aliunde. Nam in Daniam famosum illum librum iam antea venisse intellexi. Habebunt in hoc mei osores, quibus se titillent; multaque proprijs commentis non dissimilia ibidem reperient; licet non ita κατ’ ἐξοχήν quidvis fingere ausi sint. Sed valeant ista.

Venit heri in manus meas liber Ephemeridum cuiusdam MARTINI EVERHARTI (ut et hoc obiter intermisceam) quas ex novis suis tabulis Belgicis se supputasse in frontispicio asserit, atque in epistola dedicatoria [ad Regem nostrum] multa de ijsdem pollicetur. Et recte quidem ad apparentias Coelestes appellat, modo pro ipso sententiam ferre vellent, cuius tamen contrarium deprehenditur, adeo ut ne fixarum quidem loca nedum Erraticarum hinc inquirenda ipsi Coelo consona protulerit. In fixis enim stellis retinuit ubique easdem longitudines et latitudines, quae ex calculo Prutenico derivantur. Quae si rite se habuissent, quantis laboribus et quot millibus Ioachimicorum parcere potuissem? In Planetis motus eorum ex utroque calculo nimirum Alphonsino et Coperniceo simul mixto, et huic quam illi pro arbitrio propius accedendo, quodlibetice, quo imperitis fucum faceret, consarcinavit, maiori sane compendio, quam si ex ipso Coelo (prout ait) omnia deduxisset, an vero pari certitudine, non dixerim. Arbiter ille, quem pro se allegavit, constans est, nec in ullius gratiam, aut ex affectu vel inscitia quidpiam facit: adeo ut difficilem teneat tali sub iudice caussam. Si ad terrestres rem detulisset, minori id foret periculo; tunc enim similia similibus congruerent atque placerent. Cupio ex te scire, quid remunerationis loco [a Rege nostro] acceperit, et an fides illi habita, eum in recessu praestare, quod primo accessu pollicetur. Non crediderim, eius oblationem liberalius exceptam, quam Iureconsultorum et aliorum. Verum hoc extra propositum, nec de istis mentionem ullam fecissem, nisi ex te etiam scire animus foret, quomodo nostrates ex ijs, qui mei odio laborant, quosque satis nosti, de utroque scripto pronuncient. Neque enim dubito ursinum et ferinum istud ijs multum solatij additurum, quo malevolentiam propriam aliena probent, mendacia mendacijs, iniurias iniurijs. Atqui haec demonstratio talibus sufficiat, qui veriora nec amant, nec cupiunt, nec tolerant. Ab Everhartianis Ephemeridibus solaque earum inscriptione et praefatione inducere possunt ea, quae ego tot annis molitus sum, et necdum absolvi, ab alio quodam iam peracta esse et Regi dedicata; ut tanto apparatu Astronomico, tantisque impensis, prout ego feci, non fuerit opus; Tabulas iam ab alio esse confectas, quae diu frustra a me expectabantur; minori negotio iam omnia peracta esse, ita ut totus meus frustretur labor. Haec et sexcenta alia eiuscemodi pro iudicij libertate seu potius levitate, procul dubio comminiscentur, et facilis est assensus. Quis enim Coelo inconsulto haec dijudicarit? Coelum vero consulere quotusquisque rite novit? Sed suo tempore patebit, quam parum aut potius nihil ab EVERHARTO isto praestitum sit, motusque siderum potius confusione quadam utriusque calculi sic intricatos quam extricatos esse, adeo ut nullo certo, sed saltem opinabili et pro authoris arbitrio utcunque assumpto nitantur fundamento; quod potius est Astronomiam depravare quam instaurare, destruere quam aedificare. Tuum erit nostrorum hominum, praesertim eorum, quos nosti, acre (si Dijs placet) iudicium de utroque libro, quatenus clam expiscari licuit, mihi tacite innuere.

Exemplum literarum tuarum, quod requiris, mitto, originale autem reservo. Quidquid in hoc mutaveris, idem fiet in altero, nec quidpiam tuorum edam, nisi consenseris. Quod [a GERHARDO RANZOVIO pecuniam nanciscij nequiveris, facile credo. Servant enim [Holsati ea elocanda] stata tempora. A mercatore illo Hamburgense [nihil impetrare licuit] uti [† nerue] nec Flensburgense, unde incommoditatibus quibusdam aliquandiu obnoxius fui. Nemini potius quam tibi multis de causis aedes illas meas Haffniae extructas concederem. Et de pretio facile inter nos conveniret, si prius nimium tibi videretur. Da adhuc operam, ut eas prae alijs possideas. Invenies (uti existimo) isthic pro aliqua saltem pecuniae parte conquirenda rationes non admodum difficiles aut tibi (quod nollem) damnosas. Si minus, effice, ut alium emptorem habeam, quem censueris non incompetentem. De libris tuis apud D. BŒKELIUM residuis aliquoties diligenter egi, sed is multa quaerit subterfugia. Minatus sum, me rem ad iudices delaturum, et ne sic quidem profeci. Neque per [WINSHEMIUM] tuum alias, uti haberi vult, Amicum quidpiam efficere potui, uti ex schedis quibusdam, quas conquisitas adiungam, ipsemet colliges. Solam VESALIJ Anatomiam unâ cum PERERIJ in Genesin Tomo primo nactus sum, quorum hic libellus est in 8vo. Utrumque mitto. Nam licet VESALIUM, ut mihi reservarem, liberum concesseris, nolui tamen id facere, nisi aequivalens quid ex mea reciperes bibliotheca. Sed nescio, quidnam in ea sit, quod tibi arrideat. Praeterieram fere ea, quae de commentarijs tibi notis ais, te per oportunitatem in ijs laborare, ut propediem obtineam unâ cum responsione ad [Germaniam,] in quorum utroque rem acceptam praestiteris, tibique res meas curae esse, eo plus probaveris. Mitto autem nunc carmen illud Germaniae ad Daniam. Si quid in hoc critizare volueris, tibi liberum erit. Responde luculenter, prout commodum fuerit, et replica nonnulla in Germaniam acute. Patriam quoque et Regem excusa aliosque integros et cordatos viros, praeteritis ijs vel potius tacite notatis, qui culpa non vacant; et in fine me Germaniae Dania commendet, siquidem ita evenit, ut me carere debuerit. Sed nihil hîc tibi praescribo. Per te ipsum facile invenies responsionem. Materia est locuples et tibi non ignota. Praestiterit autem mea Sententia, carmen esse Elegiacum, et rationes addere ab initio, quare non Heroico et pomposo versu rescribas. Sed audi quidpiam aliud, sive fortuitum, sive quod sic fieri debuit; cum [Rex Haderslebiae RANZOVIJ] hospitium ingressus, forte fortuna in eius conclave venisset, illo absente, in mensa calendarium [Ranzovianum] reperiens, cui Elegia nostra ad Daniam inserta erat, quam [RANZOVIO] miseram, conspecto [Rex] titulo et mei nominis subscriptione (uti existimo) avide Elegiam illam perlegit, adeo ut circiter horam in ea perspicienda absumpserit, statimque seposito Libro tacitus discessit. Quod ubi [RANZOVIUS] a ministris percepit, valde succensuit pueris, quod librum illum non amovissent, illi autem per ignorantiam se excusarunt. Sed [Rex] nullum verbum de hoc ad [RANZOVIUM] retulit, neque mei ullam fecit mentionem toto illo tempore dum Haderslebiae esset, uti neque Bredenbergae cum in vicinia a RANZOVIO splendide exciperetur; quod consulto factum esse existimamus. Ego vero ubi rescivi, [Regem] Elegiam illam tam studiose evolvisse, mihi volupe erat ita factum. Utinam saltem omnia in ea ad unguem intellexisset. Et optassem, ut carmen SOMMERI, qui hîc nobiscum aderat, unâ adiunctum fuisset, quod is domum reversus mihi misit, in quo manes regis FRIDERICI Danos alloquentes deque meo abitu cum ijs quasi expostulantes introducit, cuius etiam hîc exemplum adiungo, et tuae censurae subijcio. Si et illi sive per te sive alium quempiam respondere placuerit, liberum erit. Videtur is, nescio quo furore Poëtico percitus, veritatem in multis libere ebuccinare. Et pro tali authore Carmen non est inelegans. Subiunxi insuper denuo nostram Elegiam ad Patriam, ut habeas correctius quam antea exemplum. Est enim distichon quoddam additum, quod prius aliqua incuria omissum erat, cum a [Rege] tam diligenter perlustratum praeter morem in talibus, et sic eiusdem silentio quasi authenticum redditum sit. Astronomia nostra Mechanica iam typis absoluta est, cuius exemplar dono mitto secreto habendum, et nulli communicandum, nisi forte SOPHIAe nostrae utrique, si placuerit, tacite ostendendum. Addita sunt paucula quaedam ad tuum carmen, ut compleretur pagina, sententia tamen manente ut prius. Videbis in fine RUDOLPHI illius, qui me hîc invisere solet, odam Horatianam, quae, nisi animus me fallit, tibi non displicebit. Aemulatur Poëtam illum Wittebergensem F. TAUBMANNUM, qui etiam quaedam de meo abitu nunc concinnare dicitur, unâ cum plaerisque alijs hinc inde Poëtis, quae ubi nactus fuero, tibi impertiar. Habebis etiam cum hoc eodem nuncio Stellarum octavae sphaerae restitutionem, etiam Caesari dedicatam, sed saltem in membrana manuscriptam. Quin et tertium quendam eiusdem quantitatis et formae librum ad Caesarem adorno, qui ☉ et ☾ motus velut in Ephemeride ad aliquot sequentes annos, quotquot mature absolvere licuerit, continebit, apposita ad singulos dies differentia utriusque calculi, Alphonsini et Copernicei, quae subinde maior est quam putatur, etiam additis quibusdam alijs, huc proprie et utiliter facientibus, quo habeat Imperator Specimen quoddam in ambobus luminaribus nostrae instaurationis Astronomicae, donec ea, quae pariter in quinque Planetis molior, penitus absolvantur, qua in parte nunc strenue laboro; et compendia quaedam adinveni, quibus id commode exequi liceat, etiam antequam Tabulae nostrae huc facientes prorsus consummatae fuerint. Nam ea, quae principaliter huc conducunt, in promptu habeo. Ablegavi autem hisce diebus FRANCISCUM TEGNAGLIUM nostrum ad Archiepiscopum Coloniensem cum exemplaribus duobus librorum iam paratorum. Deditque unâ RANZOVIUS ad eum meo nomine diligenter literas, meis quas hîc vidisti adiunctas. Intentio est, ut impetrem a Coloniensi literas ad Caesaream Matem, quibus me meaque studia ei serio commendet, illisque adiutus eo confidentius Caesari postea mea offeram, atque ea, quae desidero, proponam. Interim Pragam ad THADDÆUM singularem meum amicum scripsi, et eum de proposito meo secreto certiorem reddidi, ut omnia tacite exquirat, quid in his consultum fuerit, meque de eo admoneat; spero me propediem responsum obtenturum. De negotio apud Illustrissimum Administratorem, qui nunc est Septemvir Imperij, sic habe: cum filius meus TYCHO Berlini diu substitisset, quod ob plurima irrepentia in ea Electoratus mutatione obstacula essent in Aula alias occupatissimi, post nonam tandem hebdomadem reversus unâ cum IOHANNE MÜLLERO Mathematico Principis et liberorum eius instructore, literas quidem tam ab Electore quam Electorissa attulerunt, et Regi nostro Sermo et Reginae inscriptas, tum quoque aulae Magistro et Cancellario, ac ad me etiam seorsim dederunt literas tam is quam illa unâque eorum Cancellarius. At ego cum omnes perlustrassem, et ex copijs earum, quae obsignatae erant, contenta perspexissem, vidi nimis aulico more compositas esse, parumque ad rem facere. Fundantur praecipue literis illis quas Bramstedij Principi praesens obtuli, quae obiter in ea festinatione (siquidem summo mane Princeps discedere voluit) exaratae erant. At Instructio, quam postea MÜLLERO tradidi, alia quaedam et particulariora continebat, quorum minima pars in literis Electoris attingitur. Incertus itaque sum, an literas hasce non magni ponderis ad Regem et Reginam mittere velim, cum mihi usui isthic futuras vix putem. Si Megapolitanae tam diligenter scriptae nihil profuerunt, adeo ut ne responsionem quidem promereri potuerint, multo minus hae. Sed mitto tibi earundem Exemplaria, ut tu ea secreto perlegas, et nulli nisi ESCILLO nostro aperiens deliberare et mihi significare possis, an in Comitijs proximis exhibendae sint eae literae vel secus? Tum quoque an Regi duo illi libri de Astronomia Mechanica et Stellis cum meis ad ipsum literis offerendi, et an quidpiam ad Senatum Regni scribere conducat, de restituendis mihi impensis, siquidem ea, quae molitus sum, ex Regis FRIDERICI et eorum consensu ac comprobatione iuxta Senatusconsulti tenorem et pollicitationem conservari nequeant; et de instrumentis meis isthic residuis in Germaniam transvehendis, cum in Dania otiosa sint: ac quibusdam alijs ad res nostras peculiariter facientibus. Quod si tale quid tentandum censueris, de ijs, quae principaliter attingenda, captato etiam cum alijs, quos fideles nosti, praesertim ESCILLO nostro, consilio, me mature admone; et simul commentaria in utranque Epistolam (uti nosti) mitte. In responsione ad Germaniam non opus est festinatione. Fiat id per oportunitatem et otium. [Genealogias et Geneses Aulae Magistri et Cancellarij,] tum quoque res gestas, prout inter nos facta est mentio, tacite inquire, mecumque communica. Caetera habes in scheda memoriali tibi tradita, nec quidpiam haesito, quin operam daturus sis, ut nihil in ijs praetereatur. Plura itaque nunc non addam, cum forte in his nimius fuerim, teque plus satis (quod amicitiae nostrae tribuas) molestarim. Unum saltem addam, quod te tuamque terrigenam SOPHIAM concernit. Scripsi mox a tuo hinc discessu AXILLO fratri, eique hortator fui, ut permitteret te ex arbitrio Nuptias tuas disponere atque celebrare, et rationes addidi, cur id suaderem. At si vera sunt, quae nuper hîc a cive quodam Malmogiensi inaudivi, parum profeci hoc meo consilio. Aiebat enim ille, quod AXILLUS frater trium filiarum e suis Nuptias uno eodemque tempore Malmogij circa medium Augusti celebrare decreverit, quare et tuae inter eas erunt. Si id tibi non satis placuerit, aequa mente ferendum est, quod aliter fieri nequit. Scis Damasellas hîc fata regere, neque ullis rationibus moveri posse. Verum ego hoc saltem ex indicatione illius civis scribo nihil certi alias habens. Tu vero, quid in his futurum nosti, indica, atque per circumstantias explica, Deique providentiae (uti facis) omnia committe. Is tibi et tuae omnia fausta ex animi sententia largiatur. Vale et resaluta ex me Matrem tuam officiosissime, tum quoque fratrem AXILLUM unâ cum coniuge et liberis, non praeterita, quam nosti, SOPHIA, in quo nomine acquiescere lubet. Dabantur subito Wandesburgo die 8vo Maij Anni 98.

Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

Feci superius mentionem libri de Mechanica Astronomia, quod eum tibi mitterem, verum postea consultius duxi exemplar illud, quod Regi nostro destinavi, unâ cum altero libro fixarum restitutionem continente, ad te dirigere, ut utrunque in hisce Comitijs (modo tibi visum fuerit) ipsi meo nomine submisse offeras, et siquidem talibus non delectetur, uti a te et alijs cognovi, tibi facile expetenti ambos concedet. Da itaque operam, ut obtineas, et ne veniat in manus ac potestatem aliorum, mihi forte minus faventium. Quod si Rex te ijs non donarit, habebis a me postea alia exemplaria. Monstra haec saltem sorori SOPHIAe et KRAGIO nostro et fratribus meis, si qui ex ijs talia curant. LITERAS alias, quas scis me ad varias Principes Germaniae scripsisse, non duxi consultum ijs mittere, antequam de Caesaris voluntate, postquam intercessorias Coloniensis et meas ipsi cum libris destinatas acceperit, quidpiam certi compertum habeam, ne in pluribus locis negotium hoc tentatum difficilius forte fiat, et pluribus anfractibus involutum reddatur iuxta tritum illud: qui Lepores binos etc. Interea tamen iussi, ut FRANCISCUS noster cum Comite MAURITIO in transitu per illa loca meo nomine secreto loquatur, ut intelligere queam, quid spei sit apud Batavos, si Caesar et Germania oblata non acceptarint, dedique hac de re ad MAURITIUM literas, ita tamen ut nihil absolute statuerem, sed FRANCISCO omnia hypothetice saltem proponenda et expiscanda concredidi, obtuli etiam MAURITIO duo librorum exemplaria, hac tamen cautela, ut eas tacite sibi soli reservaret, donec Caesari, cui dedicantur, exhibiti essent. Habet etiam alias FRANCISCUS secum ex operibus meis singula quaedam Exemplaria, quae illustribus et praecipuis viris, prout oportunum fuerit, monstrare poterit, quo videant rem serio agi. Iamque iterum vale, et Doctorem NICOLAUM KRAGIUM nostrum, ad vos procul dubio reversum, de ijs quae hisce literis scripsi, certiorem redde, quatenus eum scire conveniat, et carmina atque literas, ut singula per otium perlegat, cum ipso clam communica, et illius quoque de ijs, quae perscripsi, consilium exquire. De ipso enim nihil addubito, quin rebus nostris ex animo bene cupiat.

Non fuit mihi otium literas hasce relegendi, sed tu pro tua industria sententiam facile percipies, si qua in parte inter scribendum aberratum est, aut forte praetermissum.

T. T. B.

To the noble man, excellent in virtue and in humane letters, Lord Holger Rosenkrantz, Lord of Rosenholm, his kinsman, kinsman-by-marriage, and most upright and most longed-for friend.
Heartiest greeting.

The letter you sent me from Rosenholm at the beginning of March, noble Holger, most longed-for kinsman and kinsman-by-marriage, I duly received about the 24th of the same, and it was by far most welcome to me; to its contents, where needful, I shall now reply briefly, meaning afterwards to add certain other things. Erik Sparre (whom you, in haste, call "Brahe") did not pass this way, but turned aside by another route to Rostock, where, having spoken with Doctor Sturtz, he made grand promises of what he would do for me with his King in my name, once the Swedish affairs were chiefly settled. But to such political and polished words I lend just as much credit as you do. Of the Kolding assembly nothing certain is yet established for us; some suppose a war was intended against the Hamburgers, some against the Swedes. The outcome proves the deeds. Our Rantzau was indeed victor in his case at Haderslev, and that deservedly by right; but he could not obtain sentence against his adversary together with execution, however hostile the King was to him. For as soon as the man, perceiving himself in peril, [approached the Chancellor, he overcame the injuries done not only to Rantzau] but also [to the King himself, by holding out a hand perhaps not empty]. As for [Pepler], that you have put some to shame — if indeed they have any — was rightly done by you; but nothing is so unjust that, if it opposes me in any way, it is not [reckoned] just. I, however, am not moved by it; I only marvel at certain men's unbridled malice and too-broad conscience, by which they harm themselves more than me. The General Judge of Bergen, who with his assessors delivered the sentence, will easily defend it, if law finds a place; if not, let these things pass with the rest. I will not involve myself in them, unless it be quite necessary. Meanwhile, what you have received from me, share with [Laumann], so that he may have whatever he shall seek from them by way of contrary proof; or else [my Praefect], first prompted by [Eske Bille and you], will put them forward. I shall add more still, if I find anything serving this purpose. The judge will himself bring the written sentence, and will not with difficulty meet the several objections. I wrote some things to him about this matter, but have as yet received no reply. I sent a citation along, which, however, he perhaps obtained earlier. Nothing about it is known to me [except from you]. Whether it was read [at Uraniborg], or is still to be read, I do not know. But whatever it be, it is all one; nor am I bound, according to the Laws (if only they had weight), to heed an illegitimate and non-annual citation, even were the matter of greater moment. This is just as good as all the rest; when they are tired of this, they can think up some new device. I, meanwhile, think of other things. That you spoke so fully with [the Chancellor's wife] about me and my affairs, was rightly and heartily done by you. I should wish to know yet more particulars, and that you would, as occasion offers (provided there is a truce among you), inculcate those very things again, with commentaries and amplifications added. Those [Machiavellists] suppose their machinations so lie hidden that no one in [Denmark] but the authors understands them. Meanwhile foreign nations talk and write of these things — as you will learn even from this letter sent me after your departure, how nearly they touched the matter itself in England. I have added the original itself, to be sent back to me when you have copied it, that it may the more surely be proved that I have changed nothing in it. Doctor Janus Mule, who was lately here with me, knew in Italy — before he was promoted — the Doctor who wrote those things, and from other letters of his sent to him recognized (as he told me) his handwriting. He will tell you more if you inquire secretly. For I allowed him too to copy it for himself, so that it might the more easily become known to the [Machiavellists], and that they might thence perceive how cautiously and prudently they have provided for themselves. What the learned everywhere in Germany and elsewhere think of my emigration hence, it would be long to relate, nor perhaps honourable enough to our countrymen. You have here, along [with the rest], a recent letter to me from Monau of Breslau alone, which I desire returned to me, by which you will perceive how greatly he exaggerates this case — so much so that in one place he speaks too hyperbolically. It was enough that he was a Jute. I always make a habit of excusing our most Serene King, and the other sensible men in the fatherland. The rest are infamous enough, both within and without the Kingdom, that they need no marker from me. I exempted both the King and all upright men from the things that befell me, in my earlier [letters] to Monau. Hence it is that he says I do rightly to distinguish the actor from the Poet of the play. That you brought nothing before [the aforesaid lady] about the administrator, was advisedly done. As to what you write of Ulfeldt — that he asserted as lies everything I had tacitly signified to you — I indeed easily believe it to be so, since I never held them for truths, and since I know many other things graver than these to have been fabricated.

To Gerhard Rantzau I wrote about the horses, as was indicated by you — yet that I do not want them before the feast of Pentecost. I had already understood that victuals are both dear in Denmark, and that their export is forbidden. Yet I do not know by what right an interdiction can stand in the way of a man sprung from a Noble family there, who would use them as a support for his own household economy, and not exercise gain or trade about them — especially if this business be procured through another nobleman, and not through merchants. But since I can do without greater things in the fatherland, why not these too? I shall see to it that it be effected from elsewhere, from neighbouring places, with smaller (as I hope) difficulties. Meanwhile your forward goodwill is most welcome to me. That Baron of whom mention was made between us, I have understood went off into Austria, nor is it known whether he has returned. Concerning that slanderous writing, on whose account I wished to send him a letter, I am not anxious; for its author so surpassed the bounds of modesty and truth in it, by such impudence, that it needs no other refutation than its own rashness and scurrility, as all the learned and sensible admit — as you will gather from certain postscripts to Thaddaeus, [namely] what was signified to me about this from Helmstedt; where it is also appended what the Wittenberg Mathematician wrote to Rostock, to Christiern Severin, who was with me for some years. You also have a copy of the letter of Rollenhagen, which I received these very days, who treats of these things more fully and sent that scurrilous book along — which I destined for Erik Lange, since the author alleges him, once and again, as a witness in a most false matter. But that calumniator must be chastised in another way, as opportunity serves; when I have a spare copy, I shall send it to you, or you will perhaps obtain it elsewhere — for I have understood that the famous book has already come to Denmark. In it my haters will have wherewith to tickle themselves, and will find there much not unlike their own fabrications, though they have not dared to invent anything so par excellence. But let these things fare well.

There came yesterday into my hands a book of Ephemerides by one Martin Everart (that I may interweave this too in passing), which he asserts on the frontispiece to have computed from his new Belgian tables, and in the dedicatory epistle [to our King] promises much of them. And he rightly appeals to the celestial appearances — provided they would give sentence for him; whereof, however, the contrary is found, so much so that he has not even produced the places of the fixed [stars], let alone of the wandering [planets], to be sought from this [work], consonant with heaven itself. For in the fixed stars he everywhere retained the same longitudes and latitudes that are derived from the Prutenic calculation. Had these been right, what labours, and how many thousands of joachims, could I have spared! In the Planets, he patched together their motions from both calculations mixed — namely the Alphonsine and the Copernican — approaching now this, now that, at his pleasure, "quodlibetically," that he might cast a mist before the unskilled, with far greater brevity indeed than if he had derived everything (as he says) from heaven itself; but whether with equal certainty, I would not say. That arbiter [heaven], which he alleged for himself, is constant, and does nothing for anyone's favour, or out of partiality or ignorance — so that he holds a difficult case under such a judge. Had he referred the matter to earthly [judges], it would have been at less peril; for then like would have agreed with, and pleased, like. I desire to know from you what he received by way of remuneration [from our King], and whether he is trusted to perform in the upshot what he promises at the first approach. I should not believe his offering was received more liberally than [those] of the jurists and others. But this is beside the point, nor would I have made any mention of these things, were it not that I have a mind to know from you too how our countrymen — those who labour under hatred of me, whom you know well enough — pronounce on both writings. For I do not doubt that that bear-like and brutish thing will add them much solace, whereby they may prove their own malevolence by another's, lies by lies, injuries by injuries. But let this demonstration suffice for such men, who neither love, nor desire, nor tolerate truer things. From Everart's Ephemerides, and their very title and preface alone, they can infer that what I have laboured at for so many years, and not yet finished, has already been accomplished by some other and dedicated to the King; that there was no need of so great an astronomical apparatus, and such great expenses, as I have made; that the Tables, long awaited from me in vain, have already been made by another; that everything has now been accomplished with less ado, so that my whole labour is frustrated. These, and six hundred other such things, they will doubtless invent, in the freedom — or rather levity — of their judgment; and assent comes easily. For who would judge these things without consulting heaven? And how few rightly know how to consult heaven! But in its own time it will appear how little, or rather nothing, has been performed by that Everart, and that the motions of the stars have rather been entangled than disentangled by a certain confusion of both calculations, so that they rest on no certain foundation, but only on an opinable one, assumed however the author pleased — which is rather to corrupt astronomy than to restore it, to destroy than to build. It will be for you secretly to hint to me the sharp (if it please the gods) judgment of our men — especially those you know — on both books, so far as it has been possible to fish it out in private.

A copy of your letter, which you ask for, I send; the original, however, I keep. Whatever you change in this, the same shall be done in the other; nor will I publish anything of yours, unless you consent. That you could not obtain money [from Gerhard Rantzau], I easily believe; for the [Holsteiners keep] fixed times [for letting those things]. From that Hamburg merchant [nothing could be obtained], as [†] nor [from] the Flensburg one, whence for a while I was subject to certain inconveniences. To no one rather than to you would I, for many reasons, grant those houses of mine built at Copenhagen. And about the price we should easily agree, if it seemed too much to you beforehand. Take pains still, that you may possess them before others. You will find there (as I think) ways, not too difficult or (which I should not wish) damaging to you, for raising at least some part of the money. If not, arrange that I have another buyer, whom you shall judge not incompetent. About your books remaining with Dr. Boekel, I have dealt diligently several times, but he seeks many subterfuges. I threatened that I would bring the matter before the judges, and not even thus did I get anywhere. Nor through your Winsheim — otherwise, as he wishes to be held, a Friend — could I accomplish anything, as you will yourself gather from certain notes, which, when collected, I shall add. I have got only Vesalius's Anatomy, together with Pererius on Genesis, the first volume, of which this is a little book in octavo. I send both. For although you freely granted that I keep the Vesalius for myself, I was unwilling to do it, unless you received something equivalent from my library. But I do not know what there might be in it that would please you. I had almost passed over what you say about the commentaries known to you — that you are working on them as occasion offers, so that I may shortly obtain them, together with the response to [Germany] — in both of which you will have done a welcome thing, and the more shown that my affairs are your care. I now send that poem of Germany to Denmark. If you wish to criticize anything in it, you shall be free. Reply clearly, as shall be convenient, and rejoin a few things to Germany acutely. Excuse also the fatherland and the King, and the other upright and sensible men, passing over — or rather tacitly marking — those who are not free of fault; and at the end let Denmark commend me to Germany, since it has so fallen out that it had to lose me. But I prescribe nothing to you here. By yourself you will easily find the response. The material is rich and not unknown to you. It will be better, in my opinion, that the poem be Elegiac, and that you add reasons at the beginning why you do not reply in Heroic and pompous verse. But hear something else, whether by chance, or as it had to happen so: when [the King, at Haderslev,] having entered Rantzau's lodging, came by chance into his chamber, the latter being absent, and finding on the table [Rantzau's] calendar, into which our Elegy to Denmark had been inserted — which I had sent [to Rantzau] — [the King], having caught sight of the title and the subscription of my name (as I suppose), read that Elegy avidly, so much so that he spent about an hour in perusing it, and at once, laying the book aside, departed in silence. When [Rantzau] learned this from his servants, he was very angry with the boys for not having removed that book; but they excused themselves by ignorance. But [the King] said not a word of this to [Rantzau], nor made any mention of me the whole time he was at Haderslev — nor at Bredenberg, when he was splendidly received nearby by Rantzau — which we suppose was done of set purpose. But I, when I learned that [the King] had so studiously turned over that Elegy, was pleased that it so happened. Would that he had at least understood everything in it to the fingernail. And I could have wished that the poem of Sommer — who was here with us — had been attached along [with it], which he, having returned home, sent me, in which he introduces the shade of King Frederik addressing the Danes and, as it were, expostulating with them about my departure; a copy of which I also add here, and submit to your judgment. If it please [you] to reply to it too, whether through yourself or some other, you shall be free. He seems, struck by some poetic fury, to trumpet the truth freely in many things; and for such an author the poem is not inelegant. I have moreover subjoined once more our Elegy to the Fatherland, that you may have a more correct copy than before; for a certain distich has been added, which earlier had by some carelessness been omitted — since it was so diligently perused by [the King], beyond custom in such matters, and thus, by his silence, rendered as it were authentic. Our Mechanical Astronomy is now finished in print, of which I send a copy as a gift, to be kept secret, and communicated to no one — unless perhaps to be shown tacitly to both our Sophies, if it please. A few little things have been added to your poem, so that the page might be filled out, the sense, however, remaining as before. You will see at the end the Horatian ode of that Rudolf who is wont to visit me here, which, unless my mind deceives me, will not displease you. He emulates that Wittenberg poet F. Taubmann, who too is now said to be composing something about my departure, together with very many other poets here and there, which, when I have got them, I shall share with you. You will also have, with this same messenger, the Restitution of the Stars of the eighth sphere, also dedicated to the Emperor, but only written by hand on parchment. Nay, I am also preparing a certain third book, of the same size and form, for the Emperor, which will contain the motions of the Sun and Moon as in an Ephemeris for some following years — as many as I shall be able to finish in good time — with the difference of both calculations, the Alphonsine and the Copernican, appended for each day (which is sometimes greater than is thought), and certain other things added, properly and usefully serving this purpose; so that the Emperor may have a certain specimen, in both luminaries, of our astronomical restoration, until those things which I am likewise labouring at in the five Planets be wholly finished — in which part I now strenuously labour; and I have found certain shortcuts by which it may conveniently be carried out, even before our Tables serving this purpose are entirely completed. For the things that principally conduce to this, I have ready to hand. I have, moreover, these days sent off our Frans Tengnagel to the Archbishop of Cologne with two copies of the books now ready. And Rantzau gave him, along [with them], a diligent letter to him in my name, with mine, which you saw here, attached. The intention is that I obtain from the Cologne [Elector] letters to His Imperial Majesty, by which he may seriously commend me and my studies to him; and, aided by these, that I may afterwards the more confidently offer my [work] to the Emperor, and propose what I desire. Meanwhile I have written to Prague, to Thaddaeus, my singular friend, and informed him secretly of my purpose, that he may quietly inquire what is advisable in these matters, and advise me of it; I hope shortly to obtain a reply. Concerning the business with the most illustrious Administrator, who is now an Elector [Septemvir] of the Empire, take it thus: when my son Tycho had stayed long at Berlin — because, on account of the many obstacles creeping in at that change of the Electorate, [those] in the court were otherwise most busy — returning at last after the ninth week, together with Johann Müller, the Prince's Mathematician and instructor of his children, they brought letters both from the Elector and the Electress, addressed to our most Serene King and Queen, and also to the Court Master and Chancellor; and to me too they separately gave letters, both he and she, and their Chancellor along [with them]. But I, when I had perused them all, and had seen the contents from copies of those which were sealed, saw that they were composed too much in courtly fashion, and made little to the point. They are founded chiefly on those letters which I presented to the Prince in person at Bramstedt, which were written hastily in that haste (since the Prince wished to depart at early dawn). But the Instruction which I afterwards handed to Müller contained certain other and more particular things, the least part of which is touched in the Elector's letters. I am therefore uncertain whether I should wish to send these letters of no great weight to the King and Queen, since I scarcely think they will be of use there. If the Mecklenburg ones, so diligently written, did no good — so much so that they could not even merit a reply — much less will these. But I send you copies of them, that you may read them secretly, and, opening them to no one but our Eske, may deliberate and signify to me whether those letters are to be presented at the next assembly, or otherwise; and likewise whether those two books, on the Mechanics of Astronomy and on the Stars, are to be offered to the King with my letters to him; and whether it conduces [to anything] to write something to the Senate of the Realm about restoring my expenses to me — since the things I have laboured at cannot be preserved according to the tenor and promise of the Senate-decree, by King Frederik's and their consent and approbation; and about transporting my instruments remaining there into Germany, since in Denmark they are idle; and certain other things particularly serving our affairs. But if you judge that any such thing should be attempted, advise me in good time about the things principally to be touched — taking counsel too with others whom you know faithful, especially our Eske — and at the same time send commentaries on each Letter (as you know how). For the response to Germany there is no need of haste; let it be done at leisure and as occasion offers. The [Genealogies and nativities of the Court Master and Chancellor], and likewise their deeds, as mention was made between us, inquire into quietly, and share with me. The rest you have in the memorial schedule handed to you; nor do I doubt at all that you will take pains that nothing in them be passed over. So I shall add no more now, since I have perhaps been excessive in these things, and have troubled you more than enough (which you will attribute to our friendship). One thing only I shall add, which concerns you and your earth-born Sophia. I wrote, right after your departure hence, to my brother Axel, and exhorted him to permit you to arrange and celebrate your nuptials at your own discretion, and I added reasons why I advised it. But if what I lately heard here from a certain citizen of Malmö is true, I have profited little by this counsel of mine. For he said that my brother Axel had resolved to celebrate the nuptials of three of his daughters at one and the same time at Malmö, about the middle of August, wherefore yours too will be among them. If this does not please you well enough, it must be borne with an even mind, since it cannot be done otherwise. You know that the Damsels here rule the fates, and cannot be moved by any reasons. But I write this only from that citizen's indication, having nothing certain otherwise. Do you, however, indicate what you know will be in these matters, and explain it through the circumstances, and commit everything (as you do) to God's providence. May He bestow on you and yours all happy things according to your heart's wish. Farewell, and greet again on my behalf your Mother most dutifully, and likewise your brother Axel together with his wife and children, not omitting — as you know — Sophia, in which name it pleases [me] to rest. Given hastily at Wandsbek, the 8th [of May], the year [15]98.

Your Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

POSTSCRIPTS.

The other letters, which you know I wrote to various Princes of Germany, I have not thought it advisable to send to them before I have learned something certain of the Emperor's will — after he has received the intercessory [letter] of the Cologne [Elector] and mine destined for him with the books — lest this business, attempted in several places, perhaps become more difficult, and be rendered entangled in many windings, according to that well-worn [saying]: he who [chases] two hares etc. Meanwhile, however, I have ordered that our Frans speak secretly, in my name, with Count Maurice, in passing through those places, that I may understand what hope there is with the Dutch, if the Emperor and Germany do not accept what is offered; and I gave letters on this matter to Maurice — yet so that I determined nothing absolutely, but entrusted everything to Frans, to be proposed and fished out only hypothetically. I also offered Maurice two copies of the books, with this caution, however, that he keep them secretly for himself alone, until they had been presented to the Emperor, to whom they are dedicated. Frans has also with him otherwise certain single copies of my works, which he can show to illustrious and chief men, as shall be opportune, that they may see the matter is being seriously pursued. And now again farewell; and inform our Doctor Niels Krag — doubtless returned to you — of the things I have written in this letter, so far as it is fitting for him to know; and share with him secretly the poems and letters, that he may peruse each at leisure; and seek his counsel too on the things I have written. For of him I doubt not at all that he heartily wishes well to our affairs.

After these things had been written, I received your Cramer's letters, and two written to you, one of which has been read by me, as he allowed in the inscription. You have both here; yet I should wish those Apologetic ones returned to me, but first let them be shown to Doctor Niels, that he may see how candidly Cramer professes the truth against that Scot. I have added also those he wrote to me — although he forbade it — that you may see from his very own words what he wishes me to do with you in his name, which I leave to your judgment, who know best what is fitting in this matter, or otherwise.

I had no leisure to reread this letter; but you, by your industry, will easily perceive the sense, if in any part there has been a slip in the writing, or perhaps something passed over.

Yours wholly, T. B.

I should surely have laboured otherwise on their behalf, had those things befallen any of them which have befallen me. [In Danish:] "It has now come so far into the world that it does much good to one who does no evil. Would that I could but enjoy what comes of it." But whither do I descend? whither does just grief draw me? — and yet I do not grieve, since this age so bears it; I only marvel that my Brahes retain almost nothing of upright and ancient manners, who through a long course of time earned the name of constancy and mutual faith. So are all things changed and transplanted. Again and often, and long and much, fare you well with yours. Hastily, at Wandsbek, the 28th day of May, the year [15]98. Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Edition of record. Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia vol. VIII (Epistolae); orig. in Tychonis Brahei et ad eum doctorum virorum epistolae ab anno 1588. MS witness: A = Copenhagen, Rigsarkivet, Langebeks Diplomatarium (a copy). Square brackets in the source are the editor's conjectural readings; a † marks an editorially-flagged corrupt word (nerue).
  • Date / place. Dateline "die 8vo Anni 98" — the month is omitted in the text; the catalogue supplies May ("8. maj"). Written from Wandsbek "hastily" (subito); Old Style (Tycho). This is the earliest of the Wandsbek-period cluster in this translation set (cf. 10 Aug 1598 to Holger = 15980810001; 20 Aug to Krag = 15980820001), written while the Mechanica was just off the press and the imperial campaign was being launched.
  • Recipient & the marriage. Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm; the closing greetings to "your earth-born Sophia" and "brother Axel" with "Sophia, in which name it pleases to rest" again work the Sophia = Wisdom/bride pun (cf. 15980810001) and confirm that Holger's bride is Sophie Axelsdatter Brahe, daughter of Tycho's brother Axel Brahe — Tycho's niece (as 15990909001 states outright). Tycho reports interceding with Axel to let Holger arrange his own wedding, but hears Axel means to marry off three daughters at once at Malmö about mid-August 1598 ("you know the Damsels here rule the fates").
  • The patronage campaign (launch phase). This letter documents the opening of the imperial bid: Frans Tengnagel dispatched to the Elector-Archbishop of Cologne (Ernst of Bavaria) with two finished books and a Rantzau-backed letter, to obtain a commendation to Rudolf II; Thaddaeus (Tadeáš Hájek) at Prague secretly sounding out the ground; and — as a hedge — Frans to speak "hypothetically" with Count Maurice of Nassau about the Dutch option if Emperor and Germany decline (Tycho citing "he who chases two hares…" against pressing too many courts at once). The Brandenburg letters (from the new Elector Joachim Friedrich — "the Administrator, now a Septemvir of the Empire" — and Electress, brought back by son Tycho with the Brandenburg court mathematician Johann Müller after nine weeks at Berlin) are judged "too courtly, little to the point," and Tycho is unsure whether to present them to Christian IV.
  • The books. The Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica is "now finished in print" (a firm dating datum: the Wandsbek Mechanica, 1598); also the manuscript "Restitution of the Stars of the eighth sphere" (the 1000-star catalogue, on parchment, dedicated to the Emperor) and a planned third book — a specimen Ephemeris of Sun (☉) and Moon (☾) motions with the daily Alphonsine-vs-Copernican difference — pending completion of the five-planet theory. In the postscript Tycho redirects the King's presentation copies (Mechanica + Stars) to Holger, to offer at the Diet and, failing royal interest, to keep.
  • The Ursus libel & the priority witnesses. The "slanderous/bear-like writing" is again Ursus's De astronomicis hypothesibus (cf. 15980820001, 15990918001, 15991209001): needing "no refutation but its own scurrility"; sent to Erik Lange (whom Ursus falsely cites as witness); Rollenhagen's letter and material from Helmstedt (to Thaddaeus) and from the Wittenberg mathematician (to Christiern Severin at Rostock) enclosed. Christiern Severin = Christen Sørensen Longomontanus, Tycho's chief assistant ("who was with me for some years"). The closing postscript adds Daniel Cramer's apologetic letters "against that Scot" = Duncan Liddel — the same Liddel/Cramer plagiarism dossier as 15991209001 / 15990926001 / 15991204001 / 16000324002.
  • Martin Everart's rival Ephemerides (a notable episode). A whole movement attacks the Ephemerides of Martin Everart (Martinus Everhartus), computed from "new Belgian tables" and dedicated to Christian IV — Tycho's fear being that the Danish faction will use its mere existence to argue that "what Tycho laboured at for years and never finished, another has already done," making Tycho's apparatus and expense pointless. Tycho's technical demolition: Everart kept the fixed-star places of the Prutenic (Reinhold's Copernican) tables unchanged, and "patched" the planetary motions by mixing Alphonsine and Copernican calculation "quodlibetice" to deceive the unskilled — "corrupting astronomy rather than restoring it." He asks Holger to report what reward Everart got from the King, and how the anti-Tycho Danes judge "both books" (Everart + Ursus).
  • The poetic war. Three poems travel with the letter: (1) the "Germania ad Daniam" verse (Germany reproaching Denmark for losing Tycho), which Holger is asked to answer in Elegiac (not Heroic) verse, excusing the King and the upright while tacitly marking the guilty; (2) Sommer's poem, introducing the shade of King Frederik II expostulating with the Danes over Tycho's departure; (3) Tycho's own Elegy to the Fatherland (now with a restored distich) — with the vivid anecdote that Christian IV, at Haderslev, chanced on the Elegy in Rantzau's calendar and read it avidly for about an hour, then left in silence, never mentioning Tycho — Rantzau's servants having failed to remove the book. Also a Horatian ode by "that Rudolf who visits me here" (a young poet at Wandsbek, emulating the Wittenberg poet Friedrich Taubmann), appended to the printed Mechanica.
  • Persons named. Erik Sparre (Swedish statesman); Dr. Sturtz (Rostock); Henrik Rantzau (Ranzovius; the Haderslev case) and his son Gerhard Rantzau (the horses); the bribed Danish Chancellor; Pepler; the General Judge of Bergen; Laumann and Tycho's Praefect (Hven/estate officials); Eske Bille; Doctor Janus Mule (recognised the handwriting of the English-letter's author); Jacob Monau (Monavius) of Breslau; Ulfeldt; the unnamed Baron (gone to Austria); Thaddaeus (Tadeáš Hájek); Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (Christiern Severin); Rollenhagen; Erik Lange; Martin Everart; King Christian IV and Queen (Anne Catherine); King Frederik II (in Sommer's poem); Sommer; Rudolf (the Wandsbek poet) and Friedrich Taubmann; Vesalius (the Fabrica/Anatomy) and Benedictus Pererius (In Genesim); Dr. Boekel and Winsheim (holders of Holger's books); the Administrator/Elector Joachim Friedrich of Brandenburg, the Electress, their Chancellor, and the court mathematician Johann Müller; Count Maurice of Nassau; the Elector-Archbishop of Cologne (Ernst of Bavaria); Axel Brahe (Axillus, Tycho's brother) and his daughter Sophie (Holger's bride); Niels Krag; Daniel Cramer (vs. "that Scot" = Duncan Liddel); Sophie Brahe (Tycho's sister). The "Court Master and Chancellor" whose genealogies/nativities Holger is to investigate are the Danish officials Tycho blames for his exile.
  • Latin, Greek & Danish tags. "Exitus acta probat" — Ovid, Heroides 2.85. "ad unguem" — to perfection (Horace). "κατ' ἐξοχήν" (Greek) — "par excellence." "qui Lepores binos [sectatur, neutrum capit]" — the proverb "who chases two hares catches neither." "quodlibetice" — "in disputation-at-will fashion / arbitrarily." Danish asides (kept and translated): "Dette er lige saa godt som alt det andet; naar de er trætte af dette, så kan de finde på nogen ny [device]" (the word Huffroe is obscure — rendered "device/pretext," flagged); "Det var nok han var en Jyde" ("It was enough that he was a Jute"); "Saa skal I see hor høfflig ieg haver ganget om der med, for Sandhed lader sig icke siige" ("Then you shall see how courteously I have dealt with it, for the truth cannot be hidden").

  • Correction. Six defects corrected per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII pp.60-68: the bracketed supplement '[uxore]' restored to '[Cancellarij uxore]' (the Chancellor's wife, not Rosenkrantz's own wife) in both Latin and English; a Danish proverbial sentence on p.68, wrongly presented as corrupt in the digitised text, restored to Dreyer's clearly legible reading in both languages ('it does much good to one who does no evil'); the reversed English sense of 'lader sig icke siige' corrected from 'cannot be hidden' to 'cannot be told'; the Jesuit commentator's name restored from 'PERIJ' to 'PERERIJ' (Pererius); and the closing date restored from 'die 8vo Anni 98' to 'die 8vo Maij Anni 98'. The last two needed no English change, as the translator had already rendered the intended sense despite the corrupted Latin.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VIII pp. 58–68; defects the audit noted: text error (4), mistranslation (2) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Collated visually against the Dreyer page images for TBOO VIII pp.58-69 (the letter proper runs pp.59-68a, bracketed by the tail of the preceding Thaddaeus letter on p.58-59 and the following short Thaddaeus/Cramerus letters on p.68b-69, correctly excluded). Checked the opening and closing lines and every proper name, date, place-name and sum in the body. Found the transcribed original itself departs from Dreyer in four places (dropped "Cancellarij" before "uxore"; "PERIJ" for "PERERIJ"; dropped "Maij" in the 8 May date; a substantially garbled Danish restored-postscript sentence mislabelled as corrupt in the digitised text when Dreyer's page is clear) and found one further mistranslation independent of the source defects (the Danish idiom "lader sig icke siige", "cannot be told", rendered as "cannot be hidden"). The most consequential defect is translating the (already-corrupted) "[uxore]" as "[your wife]": Dreyer's actual text names the Chancellor's wife, not Rosenkrantz's own, and Rosenkrantz has no wife yet at this point in the letter.

6 defects found:
- text error (medium). The transcribed original silently drops "Cancellarij" (the Chancellor's) from the bracketed supplement, leaving bare "[uxore]". Dreyer prints: Quod tam fufe cum [Cancellarij uxore] de me rebufque meis locutus fis (p.60).
- mistranslation (high). Even granting the corrupted source, "uxore" alone carries no possessive; the translator supplied "your", which is wrong on Dreyer's actual text and misleading in context since Rosenkrantz is unmarried in this same letter (his own nuptials are still pending, discussed later) while a "Cancellarius" (Chancellor) recurs elsewhere in the letter as a distinct figure. A scholar would wrongly conclude Rosenkrantz already had a wife. At: “[your wife]”.
- text error (low). The transcribed original drops a syllable from the Jesuit commentator's name, "PERIJ" for Dreyer's "PERERIJ" (Pererius). The English translator happened to render the correct full name "Pererius" anyway, so no consequential mistranslation resulted, but the source itself is corrupted at this proper name. Dreyer prints: unâ cum PERERIJ in Genefin Tomo primo (p.63).
- text error (low). The transcribed original drops "Maij" from the closing date formula. The translator correctly supplied "[of May]" as a bracketed editorial restoration, so no consequential error reached the English, but the source text itself departs from Dreyer. Dreyer prints: Dabantur subito Wandefburgo die 8vo Maij Anni 98. (p.66).
- text error (medium). Dreyer's page is clearly printed and legible, not corrupted; our transcribed original's Danish differs substantially in wording (e.g. "er nu" vs "[?] nil", the whole middle clause restructured) and is mislabelled as corrupt in the digitised text. Because the translator honestly hedged ("rendered only tentatively") rather than asserting false confidence, the resulting English is a defensible paraphrase of a garbled source rather than a fabrication, but it fails to give the actual ascertainable sense (roughly: "it has now come so far into the world that it does much good to one who does no evil"). Dreyer prints: Mens dett er nu faa viit kommen y verden, att den giør en møgitt gott, som giør en intedt ont. Gid jeg kunde kun nyde dett derpaa. (p.68).
- mistranslation (medium). "lader sig icke siige" means "cannot be told/said", not "cannot be hidden"; the translation reverses the sense of the Danish clause (unspeakable vs. unconcealable). At: “Then you shall see how courteously I have dealt with it — for the truth cannot be hidden:”.

Repaired. All six defects verified directly against Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.60, 63, 66, 67, and 68 page images (not the digitised text layer), reading the full 12-page extract in three passes. Each defect's claimed Dreyer reading was confirmed exactly as stated in the audit, including the Danish passage on p.68 which reads clearly and is not corrupt in the digitised text as the transcribed original's editorial label claimed. No other part of this long letter was touched; every changed word is accounted for in outcomes above.
