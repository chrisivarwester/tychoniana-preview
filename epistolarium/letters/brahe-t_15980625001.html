The original

COPIA LITERARUM SERENISS: ELECTORIS COLONIENSIS AD DOMINUM HENRICUM RANZOUIUM.

ERNESTUS electus et confirmatus archiep[i]scopus Coloniensis, sacri Romani imperij per Italiam archicancellarius et princeps elector, episcopus Leodiensis, palatinus Rheny, utriusque Bauariæ dux. Clementi salutatione Nostra premissa. Nobilissime singulariter dilecte mihi et illis studijs, in quibus tam preclaram Braheus operam posuit, nihil est nunc gratius et literis a te missis nihil acceptius, et tua etiam commendatione nihil grauius, et ipso Braheo, ut mores et doctrina viri merentur, nihil fuit neque erit commendatius. Vidi, quæ misit sublimibus de illis specimina, mire placuerunt et reliquorum magnam sitim in animo meo excitauerunt. Perge, Ranzoui præstantissime, affinem illum, te dignum, Daniæ totique Germaniæ gloriosum, fouere. Ego, quod potero, et ipsius causa, et quia uideo tibi, quem, ut æquum est, facio maximi, rem curæ esse, diligenter eum, ut feci, cæs: ma:ti commendare pergam, et non committam, ut meam in se beneuolentiam ulla ex parte sentiat claudier. Ex eodem Francisco Tengnaglio hæc eadem copiosius intelliges.

Vale.
Leody xxv junij 1598.
Ernestus mpp.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Copy of the letter of the Most Serene Elector of Cologne to Master Heinrich Rantzau.

Ernst, elected and confirmed Archbishop of Cologne, Arch-Chancellor of the Holy Roman Empire throughout Italy and Prince-Elector, Bishop of Liège, Count Palatine of the Rhine, Duke of both Bavarias. Our clement greeting premised. Most noble, singularly beloved: to me, and to those studies in which Brahe has placed such distinguished effort, nothing is now more pleasing, and than the letters sent by you nothing more acceptable, and than your recommendation also nothing weightier; and than Brahe himself — as the man's character and learning deserve — nothing was, nor will be, more commended [to me]. I have seen the specimens which he sent concerning those lofty matters; they pleased me marvellously, and excited in my mind a great thirst for the rest. Continue, most excellent Rantzau, to cherish that kinsman of yours — worthy of you, and glorious to Denmark and to all Germany. I, for what I shall be able, both for his sake and because I see that the matter is a care to you (whom, as is just, I esteem most highly), will continue diligently — as I have done — to commend him to His Imperial Majesty, and will not allow him to feel my benevolence toward him to be in any part curtailed. From the same Frans Tengnagel you will understand these same things more copiously.

Farewell.
Liège, the 25th of June, 1598.
Ernst, with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • The companion to brahe-t_15980625002. This is the copy of Elector Ernst's covering letter to Heinrich Rantzau, written the same day as (and enclosed with / paralleling) his direct letter to Tycho. Where the letter to Tycho thanks him for his books, this one to Rantzau answers Rantzau's recommendation and confirms the Elector's continued intercession for Tycho with the Emperor.
  • Sender. Ernst of Bavaria, Elector-Archbishop of Cologne and Bishop of Liège (see brahe-t_15980625002 for the full titulature note).
  • Recipient. Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), Holstein humanist and Danish Statholder, Tycho's patron and affinis (kinsman by marriage — affinem illum); the broker of this princely intercession. (Cf. brahe-t_15860301001, where Rantzau is also the conduit.)
  • Braheus … specimina sublimibus de illis — the "specimens concerning those lofty matters" = the works/observations Tycho had sent (his printed books), which whetted the Elector's appetite for more. Franciscus Tengnaglius = Frans Tengnagel, the bearer who would relate the rest.
  • claudier — archaic/poetic passive infinitive of claudo ("to be closed/curtailed"); rendered "to be … curtailed."
  • Calendar. Leody xxv junij 1598 = 25 June 1598, Liège (Catholic) = New Style (as in the parallel letter).
