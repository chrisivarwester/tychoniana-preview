The original

DEM VESTEN/ VNSERM LIEBEN BESONDERN/ TYCHONI BRAHE, HERRN ZU KNUDSTRUP.
WILHELM VON GOTTES GNADEN/ LANDTGRAUE ZU HESSEN/ GRAUE ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ ETC.

VNSERN gnedigen Gruß zuvor/ Vester lieber Besonder. Ihr wisset euch zweiffels frey zu erinnern/ welcher gestalt jr vns hiebevor etlicher Elende halber gute vertröstung gethan/ Darauff wir auch nicht vnderlassen/ vnderm dato, den 2. Aprilis, einen eigenen Botten mit einem Schreiben an euch haltendt/ abzufertigen/ daß/ wo solche Elende bey euch ankommen weren/ er vns dieselbige anhero bringen solte. Wir haben aber sider dem das geringste nicht vernommen/ ob solcher Botte bey euch ankommen/ oder aber ob er lebendig oder todt sey/ oder wie es sonst vmb jnen stehe/ daß vns biß noch von euch keine Antwort zukommên ist. Wie aber deme/ weil vns die Elende/ so vorm Jar auß Schweden bekommen/ alle gestorben/ vnd wir nur einen Elendtochsen/ so vns vor zweyen Jaren auß Schweden zukommen/ haben/ derselbe dan sehr schön vnd fein zahm ist/ vnd diß Jar albereit ein fein Gehörn von anderthalben Schuhe hoch geschoben hat/ vnd jm niergendt woran mangelt/ alß daß er kein Weibichen hat/ damit er sich paren möchte. Alß begeren wir gnediglich/ woferne euch solche vertröstete Elendsthier/ vnd sonderlich ein Weibichen zukommen were/ jr wollet vns dieselbige auff vnsern kosten so baldt vberschicken. Daran thut jr vns zu angenemen gefallen. Vnd seyndt wir es vmb euch hinwider in allen Gnaden/ damit wir euch ohne das gewogen/ zu erkennen geneigt.

Datum in vnser Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel/ am 30. Iulij, Anno 1592.
WILHELM LANDTGRAFF ZU HESSEN.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the steadfast, our trusty and well-beloved, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup.
Wilhelm, by the grace of God, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, etc.

Our gracious greeting beforehand, our trusty and well-beloved. You doubtless know to call to mind in what manner you gave us, some time ago, a good promise touching certain elk; whereupon we also did not fail, under the date of 2 April, to despatch a courier of our own to you, carrying a letter, [to the effect] that, if such elk had come into your hands, he was to bring the same to us here. But since then we have heard not the least thing — whether that courier reached you, or indeed whether he be alive or dead, or how it otherwise stands with him — so that up to now no answer has come to us from you. But however that may be: since the elk we obtained a year ago out of Sweden have all died, and we have only one elk-bull, which came to us out of Sweden two years ago — the same being indeed very handsome and finely tame, and having already this year put forth a fine antler a foot and a half high, and which lacks nothing anywhere, save that it has no female with which to mate — therefore we graciously desire that, in case such promised elk-beasts, and especially a female, should have come into your hands, you would send the same over to us at our cost as soon as may be. Therein you do us an acceptable pleasure. And we are inclined, for our part, to acknowledge it to you again in all graciousness, in which we are besides well-disposed toward you.

Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on 30 July, Anno 1592.
WILHELM, LANDGRAVE OF HESSE.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Wilhelm von Gottes Gnaden, Landtgraue zu Hessen = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the "astronomer-prince," Tycho's foremost princely correspondent and the patron of the Kassel observatory (with Christoph Rothmann and the instrument-maker Jost Bürgi). This is one of his last letters: he died on 25 August 1592, less than a month after this dateline.
  • Recipient titulature. Herrn zu Knudstrup = "Lord of Knudstrup," Tycho's ancestral seat (Knutstorp) in Scania; the full princely address-formula and titulature are preserved by the edition's convention
  • Honorifics / formulae. Dem Vesten = "to the steadfast" (an honorific epithet, vest/fest "firm, steadfast," for a nobleman below comital rank); Vnsern gnedigen Gruß zuvor = "Our gracious greeting beforehand"; Vester lieber Besonder = "our trusty and well-beloved" (the standard princely address to a favoured subordinate, the edition's convention). The princely first-person plural (Wir / euch) is kept throughout.
  • Catzenelnbogen = the County of Katzenelnbogen, a hereditary Hessian comital title; kept in the period spelling.
  • Subject: Elend / Elende / Elendtochsen / Elendsthier. The pivotal term. Elend(t) here is elk (European elk / moose, Alces alces) — early-modern German Elen(d)tier, not the modern homograph Elend "misery." Rendered "elk" throughout. Specifically: Elende (plural) = "elk"; Elendtochsen = "elk-bull" (Ochse "ox/bull," here the male animal); Elendsthier = "elk-beast(s)"; Weibichen (dimin. of Weib) = "female (cow)." The exchange concerns living elk shipped from Sweden via Tycho (well-placed to procure Scandinavian game) to stock the Landgrave's menagerie at Kassel — Wilhelm's one surviving bull lacks a mate.
  • Schuhe (in anderthalben Schuhe hoch) = "feet," the linear measure (singular Schuh ≈ 0.28–0.29 m in Hessian usage); anderthalben = "one and a half." The antler is described as "a foot and a half high"; the measure-word is kept as "foot/feet," not converted.
  • Negation count. The letter carries the legally/factually load-bearing negations: nicht vnderlassen ("did not fail" — i.e. did despatch the courier, a double-negative affirmation, preserved as such); das geringste nicht vernommen ("heard not the least thing"); keine Antwort ("no answer"); niergendt woran mangelt, alß daß ("lacks nothing anywhere, save that"). Each is reproduced one-for-one; none added or dropped.
  • vertröstung … vertröstete = "promise / promised" (Vertröstung "assurance, undertaking"); rendered as a promise/undertaking, not a modern "consolation."
  • Orthography (chancery German, 1592). jr/jm/jnen = ihr/ihm/ihnen; vns/vnserm/vnser = uns/unserm/unser; sider dem = seitdem ("since then"); vorm Jar = vor dem Jahr / "the year before"; albereit = bereit(s) ("already"); paren = paaren ("to pair, mate"); woferne = "in case, if"; vberschicken = "send over"; zukommên prints with a tilde for the nasal (zukommen). The virgule (/) is the printed sentence-comma of the 1596 edition and is left in the source block.
  • Calendar / place. Datum … am 30. Iulij, Anno 1592 = "Given … on 30 July, 1592," Old Style (Hesse-Kassel, a Lutheran state, kept the Julian calendar in 1592). Datum "Given" rendered by the edition's convention The in-letter back-reference den 2. Aprilis (the April courier) is likewise O.S. The edition label ("1592. 30. juli. Kassel") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
  • Edition. Printed by Tycho himself in the Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596), Liber I — the volume gathering the Hesse-Kassel correspondence; reprinted in Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI. No manuscript witness is recorded in the edition.
  • Correction. Restored the distinction between the letter's two elk shipments: 'vorm Jar' now reads 'a year ago' (not 'the year before last', which duplicated the letter's separate later phrase 'vor zweyen Jaren', 'two years ago').

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). 'vorm Jar' (a year ago / last year) is rendered as 'the year before last', which collapses onto the letter's separate, distinctly-phrased 'vor zweyen Jaren' (two years ago) used one clause later for the surviving elk-bull; the original draws a clear one-year-ago vs. two-years-ago distinction between two different elk shipments that the translation blurs. At: “since the elk we obtained the year before last out of Sweden have all died”.

Repaired. No Dreyer page images were available for this letter (no page reference), so the correction rests entirely on the German text already given in the transcribed original, which is internally consistent and unambiguous on this point (two distinct time-phrases naming two distinct elk deliveries, one 'vorm Jar' and one 'vor zweyen Jaren'). This cannot be verified against the edition of record and is flagged accordingly; the fix is limited to what is demonstrable from the letter's own internal evidence, by the audit's rule for a defect with no supplied extract.
