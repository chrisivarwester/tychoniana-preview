The original

NOBILISSIMO ET CLARISSIMO VIRO D. TYCHONI BRAHE HVENÆ INSULÆ DOMINO, MATHEMATICO LONGE PRÆSTANTISSIMO.

CLARISSIME D. TYCHO, in altero antilogiæ nostræ exemplarium, quæ ab eodem amanuensi descripta sunt, quorum primum ad te missum fuit, hæc errata reperiuntur: εδειξαι pro δεῖξαι. Ubi SCULTETI fit mentio, exprobas pro exprobras. Et cum artificium vestrum examinatur, ex his pro in his, ut in Cœlestibus: quæ si in tuo occurrant, vel similia, peto corrigas. Pro reliquis responsurus sum, nec discipulos tuos veriturus, imo ne præceptores tuos. Veritas et honestas mihi sufficient ad defensionem contra quoscunque. Et tamen, ut percurri obiter primum Tomum tuum, quamvis in multis a te dissentiam, cœpi potius tuam industriam et eximias virtutes admirari, quam illarum iniuriarum, quæ nec te nec me digna fuere, recordari. Clementissimus Deus cœptis tuis benigne faveat et ad optatum exitum perducat.

Datæ 5 Augusti Edinburgi.
IOAN. CRAIGIUS
Doctor Medicus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most renowned man, Master Tycho Brahe, lord of the island of Hven, by far the most outstanding mathematician.

Most renowned Master Tycho, in the second of the copies of our controversy [antilogia] — which were written out by the same amanuensis, the first of which was sent to you — the following errata are found: εδειξαι [edeixai] for δεῖξαι [deîxai]. Where mention is made of SCULTETUS, exprobas [you reproach] for exprobras [you reproach]. And where your method is examined, ex his [from these] for in his [in these], as in [the passage on] the Heavens: should these, or the like, occur in your [copy], I ask that you correct them. As for the rest, I shall make my reply, and I shall not stand in awe of your disciples — nay, not even of your teachers. Truth and honesty will suffice me for a defence against any whomsoever. And yet, having run through your first Tome cursorily, although I dissent from you in many things, I have begun rather to admire your industry and your exceptional merits than to call to mind those injuries which were worthy neither of you nor of me. May God most merciful graciously favour your undertakings and bring them to the wished-for end.

Given 5 August, at Edinburgh.
JOHN CRAIG,
Doctor of Medicine.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Ioan. Craigius, Doctor Medicus = John Craig (d. 1620), the Scots Aristotelian physician then practising in Edinburgh; earlier professor of mathematics and logic at Frankfurt an der Oder, later royal physician to James VI, fellow of the London Royal College of Physicians (1604) and incorporated M.D. at Oxford (1605). Despite his court contacts, he was not yet a royal physician at the time of this exchange, and he never visited Denmark (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens; Tycho Brahe and Prague, "The Dynamic of a Dispute," p. 75).
  • Recipient titulature. Hvenae insulae domino — "lord of the island of Hven," Tycho's fief (granted by Frederik II in 1576); the seat of Uraniborg. Mathematico longe praestantissimo — the high astronomical compliment, kept formal.
  • antilogia (Greek ἀντιλογία, "contradiction, controversy, debate-in-utramque-partem") — here the controversy / point-by-point exchange between Craig and Tycho. The substance was their epistolary dispute (1589–94) over Tycho's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (1588) on the comet of 1577: Craig, on Aristotelian grounds, rejected Tycho's parallax demonstration that the comet was a supralunary (aethereal) rather than a meteorological body, and objected to Tycho's fluid-heaven world-system that dispensed with solid celestial spheres. The term is left as controversy in the running text with the Greek-derived word flagged.
  • amanuensis — a copyist/scribe; two fair copies of the controversy were made by the same hand, the first sent to Tycho, the second (the one Craig is now collating) retained by Craig, who lists the scribal errata by which the two diverge so that Tycho may bring his copy into agreement.
  • The errata list (a copy-collation, not a substantive argument). Three scribal slips are corrected:
  • Greek: εδειξαι for δεῖξαι. The transmitted source prints the erroneous form εδειξαι (no accents) and the correct form δεῖξαι with circumflex; reproduced as printed by the edition's convention δεῖξαι is the aorist active infinitive of δείκνυμι, "to show / to demonstrate / to prove" — the technical sense here (to demonstrate the comet's parallax). The faulty εδειξαι would be a malformed augmented form; the point is purely orthographic/scribal, so both forms are transliterated and glossed rather than emended into one.
  • exprobas for exprobras — at the mention of Scultetus. The intended word is exprobras, "you reproach / you cast in [his] teeth" (2nd person sing. of exprobrare); exprobas is the copyist's haplography (dropped -r-). Both forms carry the same sense ("you reproach"), so the English cannot show the difference; the Latin pair is kept to preserve the correction Craig is actually making.
  • ex his for in his — "from these" mistakenly written where "in these" is meant, "where your method is examined... as in [the passage on] the Heavens" (ut in Coelestibus). Again a one-preposition scribal slip; the Latin pair is retained.
  • Sculteti (gen.) = Bartholomäus Scultetus (Bartholomew Schultz, 1540–1614), the Görlitz astronomer-mathematician and Tycho correspondent whose observations of the 1577 comet figured in Tycho's analysis in De mundi — hence a passage in the controversy where Tycho is said to "reproach" Scultetus.
  • primum Tomum tuum — "your first Tome." The referent is the printed comet-volume Tycho had circulated (the 1588 De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis, the second part of Tycho's larger Progymnasmata programme). Craig's "first Tome" is taken at face value as the volume he had received; whether it designates that volume's ordinal place in Tycho's scheme is not decided by the letter and is not silently reconciled here.
  • nec discipulos tuos veriturus, imo ne praeceptores tuos — "I shall not stand in awe of your disciples — nay, not even of your teachers." The combative register (Craig promising a substantive responsio and disdaining intimidation) is preserved; imo ne... quidem force ("nay, not even") rendered without softening.
  • Negation check. The letter contains two negations in the defiance sentence — nec... veriturus and ne praeceptores — and one in the concessive quamvis in multis a te dissentiam ("although I dissent from you in many things"); all three are reproduced, none added or dropped. The closing quae nec te nec me digna fuere carries a double nec ("worthy neither of you nor of me"), both retained.
  • Source-text artifact. The working transcription (harvested CSV) carries a stray > immediately before quam illarum iniuriarum; this is a markup/extraction artifact, not a feature of the Dreyer text or the MS, and is omitted from the Source block. It does not affect the reading: admirari... quam... recordari ("admire... rather than... call to mind") is a straightforward potius... quam comparison.
  • Calendar / place. Datae 5 Augusti Edinburgi = "Given 5 August, at Edinburgh." No year is given in the dateline; the year 1592 comes from the edition label and the Vienna MS context, not from the letter's own subscription — so it is not asserted in the header's date-as-written portion beyond what the document attests. Scotland under James VI used the Julian (Old Style) calendar, so the date is O.S. (catalogued "1592. 5. august. Edinburgh").
