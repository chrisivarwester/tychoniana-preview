The original

ERVDITIONE EXCELLENTI ET INGENIOSA PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO D. CHRISTOPHORO ROTHMANNO, ILLVSTRISSIMI PRINCIPIS WILHELMI LANDTGRAVII HASSIÆ, ETC. MATHEMATICO EXIMIO, AMICO SUO PLURIMUM DILECTO. S. D.

REVERSVS ad nos is per quem instantibus vltimo Francoforti vernalibus Nundinis Illustriss. Principi tuo et tibi de rebus Astronomicis copiose perscripseram, vestræque expectationi, vti spero, pro virili satisfeceram, præter omnem meam opinionem nihil prorsus literarum retulit. Cuius causam admiranti sciscitantique mihi, Illustrissimum Principem LANDTGRAVIVM eo tempore, quo transactis Nundinis Cassellas redierat, nuptijs quibusdam occupatum fuisse, quo minus tunc responsioni vacare ipsius Celsitudini commodum foret, et te etiam eâdem de causa varijs auocationibus implicitum fuisse referebat, subiungebatque ipsum Illustrissimum Principem per te sibi significasse, quod breui misso proprio Tabellario literas, quæ meis responderent, essem recepturus. Huic Relationi hactenus lubens acquieui, et indies ab Illustrissimo Principe, tum etiam a te responsionem cupide expectaui, quam cum vltra speratum tempus differri videam, non potui non oblata per hunc Bibliopolam HENRICVM, (eidem per quem priores miseram inseruientem) hac opportuna scribendi occasione, ad te aliquid literarum dare, et rescriptionem hactenus intermissam in memoriam reuocare. Rogo itaque peramanter, vt velis apud Illustrissimum Principem tuum de responsione mihi remittenda, meo nomine obnixe et reuerenter sollicitare, vtque tu etiam ijs, quas ad te seorsim dedi literis, omnimode rescribendo satisfacere non diutius supersedeas. Si per proprium Principis Tabellarium negotium hoc commode expediri nequeat, idipsum per harum exhibitorem satis opportune efficietur, modo literas, quamprimum is e Nundinis Francofordianis Cassellas reuersus fuerit, in promptu paratas inuenerit, nam ne comitum et vehiculi dispendium faciat, moram istic aliquam nectere vix illi commodum fuerit. Retulit idem, qui meas vobis attulit, te impetrata a Principe tuo venia, me coram inuisere decreuisse, quod vt prima quaque opportunitate exequi non intermittas, te etiam atque etiam rogatum habeo. Erit tuus accessus mihi pergratus, et de multis præsentes oretenus plenius et commodius, quam absentes per literas, iucunde, nec, vti spero, inutiliter conferemus, Vnaque Instrumentorum nostrorum varia exquisitaque structura, obseruandique per hæc in fallibilis apprimeque concinna ratio oculari inspectione omnium optime tibi patebit. Omninoque tibi persuadeas, quod te eius itineris et laboris non pæniteat vnquam. Eclipsin Lunarem proxime instantem, non dubito, quin per serenitatis opportunitatem diligenter sis cælitus animaduersurus. Eius itaque apparentias et temporum momenta, vel potius ipsa Obseruationum Data mecum communicare non graueris, Ego etiam eandem hîc meis Organis adamussim perscrutari (volente Deo) non intermittam, teque de meis inuentis postmodum certiorem reddam. Vale et nostris desiderijs, inprimis vero ijs quæ in literis ad Illustrissimum Principem tuum, et te vltimo datis continebantur, quæque earum exhibitor priuatim meo nomine significauit, vt omni ex parte mature ante ingruentem hyemem satisfiat, omnem moue lapidem. Iterum feliciter vale, et absque intermissione Sacris Vraniæ lita.

Datæ Vraniburgi 12. Augusti, Anno 1587.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the most excellent man, foremost in fine and ingenious learning, Master Christoph Rothmann, distinguished mathematician of the Most Illustrious Prince Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, etc., his much-beloved friend — sends greeting.

The man through whom, at the last spring fair at Frankfurt now past, I had written at length on astronomical matters to your Most Illustrious Prince and to yourself — and had, as I hope, satisfied your expectation to the best of my power — has returned to us, and, contrary to all my expectation, brought back no letters whatsoever. When I wondered at this and asked the reason, he reported that the Most Illustrious Prince the Landgrave, at the time when he had returned to Kassel after the fair, had been taken up with a certain wedding, so that it was not then convenient for His Highness to be free for a reply, and that you too, for the same reason, had been caught up in various distractions; and he added that the Most Illustrious Prince had signified to him, through you, that by a courier of his own, to be sent shortly, I should receive letters answering mine. With this report I have hitherto gladly rested content, and day by day eagerly awaited a reply from the Most Illustrious Prince, and from you as well. But seeing it put off beyond the time I had hoped, I could not but — this convenient occasion for writing being offered through the bookseller Heinrich (who serves the same man by whom I had sent the earlier letters) — send you a few lines, and recall to mind the reply so far left unmade. And so I ask you most affectionately to be willing, in my name, earnestly and reverently to press your Most Illustrious Prince for a reply to be sent back to me; and that you too put off no longer satisfying, by a full reply, the letters I sent to you separately. If this business cannot conveniently be dispatched by the Prince's own courier, the same will be managed suitably enough through the bearer of this letter — provided only that, as soon as he has returned from the Frankfurt fair to Kassel, he find the letters ready to hand; for, that he may not incur the expense of attendants and a carriage, it will scarcely suit him to contrive any delay there. The same man who brought you my letters reported that you, having obtained leave from your Prince, had resolved to visit me in person; and I beg you again and again not to fail to carry this out at the very first opportunity. Your coming will be most welcome to me, and on many matters we shall confer face to face, by word of mouth, more fully and conveniently — and pleasantly, and, as I hope, not unprofitably — than we can, apart, by letter. And at the same time the varied and exquisite construction of our instruments, and the unerring and singularly elegant method of observing by means of them, will be laid open to you, best of all, by inspection with your own eyes. And be wholly assured that you will never regret that journey and labour. The lunar eclipse now next at hand — I do not doubt that, with the chance of a clear sky, you will diligently observe it in the heavens. So do not find it a burden to share with me its appearances and the precise moments of time — or rather the very data of your observations; I too, for my part, shall not fail to scrutinize the same here, to the line, with my instruments (God willing), and will afterward inform you of my findings. Farewell; and, that our wishes — above all those contained in the letters last sent to your Most Illustrious Prince and to you, and those that their bearer privately signified in my name — may be met in every part in good time, before winter comes on, move every stone [leave no stone unturned]. Again, fare you well and happily, and without ceasing make your offerings at the rites of Urania [devote yourself unceasingly to astronomy].

Given at Uraniborg, 12 August, in the year 1587.
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann (d. c. 1600), mathematician–astronomer to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, at the Kassel observatory. The Tycho–Rothmann correspondence (begun 1586) is the technical heart of the printed Epistolae astronomicae.
  • Context. This is a logistical letter within that exchange — chasing an overdue reply routed through the Frankfurt book fair (Nundinae Francofordianae, the twice-yearly Frankfurt fair, the hub of the learned book and letter trade) and a bookseller named Heinrich — but its substance is the standing invitation to Kassel↔Hven scholarly exchange and a concrete request: joint observation of the coming lunar eclipse.
  • Instrumentorum … structura … observandi ratio infallibilis — "the construction of our instruments … the unerring method of observing": Tycho's recurring boast of the Uraniborg instruments and their precision, here as a lure for Rothmann to come and see them.
  • meis Organis adamussim perscrutari — "to scrutinize with my instruments, to the line": adamussim (literally "to the mason's/carpenter's line") = with exact precision; Organa = instruments.
  • apparentias et temporum momenta … ipsa Observationum Data — "the appearances and the precise moments of time … the very data of the observations": the technical request — phases/aspects and exact timings of the eclipse.
  • omnem move lapidem — "move every stone," the proverb (Greek pánta kínei pétron); rendered with the gloss "leave no stone unturned."
  • Sacris Uraniae lita — "make offering at the rites of Urania," i.e. devote yourself to astronomy (Urania = the Muse of astronomy); a characteristic Tycho sign-off, kept as sacral metaphor.
  • Calendar. O.S.; Uraniborg/Hven kept Julian.
