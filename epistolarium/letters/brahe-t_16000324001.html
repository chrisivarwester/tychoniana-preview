JOHANNES JESSENIUS A JESSEN TO TYCHO BRAHE.

Heartiest greeting.

Three days ago, most noble Lord Tycho — having set out from Wittenberg eight days before — I came to Pilsen, to plead my old father's long and difficult case, which I had hoped to carry through both more happily and more quickly because the Most Illustrious Prince, the Administrator of Saxony, my most clement Lord, had diligently interceded for me by letters with the Imperial Majesty. But as all things are wont to be accomplished at a slow pace, so too, from these preludes, I suspect I shall conclude my business somewhat more slowly — especially as the scattered court throws up very many inconveniences and delays; nevertheless I press, I insist, even to the weariness and annoyance of those with whom I must solicit the business. Yet I take some pleasure even from these delays, since by several of the Emperor's Councillors I am not only kindly admitted, but even sent for of their own accord. To whom the friendship that is between you and me prepares so easy an access for me — for as soon as I make mention of you, I feel their countenance at once changed into a certain singular graciousness; and you certainly have more men here who venerate you in your absence than others have to praise them to their face — or rather, to flatter them. And, among other things, take at least this one, for brevity's sake.

Lately, when I addressed Lord Gifanius — the most celebrated jurisconsult and Aulic Councillor of the Empire, known to me also beforehand — among our other conversations he too made honourable mention of you (whom, however, he has never seen face to face), reciting to me what had been told him, not long before, by the Magnificent Lord Coraduz, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire: namely, that the Imperial Majesty had asked Lord Coraduz, returned from his Italian legation, whether he had heard anything at Rome, and elsewhere there, about Tycho Brahe's coming into these parts; and when he had answered that he had indeed learned of it both at Rome and elsewhere, the Emperor went on [to ask] what he and others thought of the matter. Then Coraduz freely answered: Tycho Brahe is a man to whom, in the astronomical disciplines, neither this age nor former ages have seen an equal, and scarcely will the ages to come have his like. Whence he congratulated — not so much himself, who by the Imperial Majesty's own mandate had summoned him hither, but far rather His Sacred Imperial Majesty — on the winning of so great a Man, by whose industry and labours, surpassing the common, the Imperial Majesty himself would augment the glory and fame of his Name among all posterity, with indelible memory. At which words the Emperor himself assented, and added that this man pleased him exceedingly and was dear to him. These things that most renowned Gifanius then recited to me with the greatest applause, and confirmed with his own added approval, and bade you very heartily greeted in his name, eagerly awaiting your conversation when you shall meet at Prague. But more — both of these matters and of others — when we shall meet, which I hope will be soon, since it is constantly affirmed that the Imperial Majesty intends [to come] to Prague soon after Easter is past.

Farewell; and that I have not visited you earlier, impute to my haste — since I perceived my parent's case to be in difficulty — and hold me excused.
Given at Pilsen, 24 March, the year 1600.
Devoted to Your Generosity,
Johannes Jessenius a Jessen,
Physician to the Dukes of Saxony and Professor at Wittenberg. [Attestation appended to the copy:] I, Doctor Jessenius, acknowledge that this agrees with my original, in my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johannes Jessenius (Jan Jessenský z Jessenu) (1566–1621), anatomist and physician — here "Physician to the Dukes of Saxony and Professor at Wittenberg." The same friend who recurs across the corpus: Tycho lodged with him at Wittenberg (brahe-t_15981231001), called him "the Planet" who had not yet visited (brahe-t_16000327002), and sent him greetings via Jöstel (brahe-t_16001008001). The MS is a later Langebek afskrift.
  • The errand at Pilsen. Jessenius has come to Pilsen (Plzeň) — where Rudolf II's court had withdrawn during the 1599 plague — to plead his aged father's "long and difficult case," backed by a letter of intercession from his patron, the Administrator of (Electoral) Saxony (the regent for the under-age Elector Christian II). This explains, in his own words, why he had not yet visited Tycho (cf. brahe-t_16000327002, where Tycho noted Jessenius's non-appearance and "his father's case in jeopardy"). The aula distracta — the "scattered/distracted court" — is the plague-displaced court at Pilsen.
  • The Coraduz testimonial (the heart of the letter). Jessenius relays, via Gifanius, a court testimonial to Tycho. The Emperor, asking Coraduz — newly back from his Italian legation — what was said of Tycho's coming "at Rome and elsewhere," drew from him the verdict: "Tycho Brahe is a man to whom, in astronomy, neither this nor former ages have seen an equal, and scarcely will the ages to come have his like"; Coraduz congratulated the Emperor on the acquisition, foretelling it would add "indelible" lustre to Rudolf's name among posterity — to which the Emperor assented, adding that Tycho "pleased him exceedingly and was dear to him." A first-rate document of Tycho's standing at the imperial court on the eve of his Prague establishment.
  • Persons named.
  • Gyphanius I. C. = Hubert van Giffen (Obertus Gifanius, 1534–1604), the noted jurisconsult (Iurisconsultus) and Imperial Aulic Councillor.
  • Magnificus Dn. Corraducius, Imperii Procancellarius = Rudolf Coraduz (Coraduz), Vice-Chancellor of the Empire — one of Tycho's principal court patrons (the Coraduz/Barvitius axis; cf. brahe-t_16000813001, brahe-t_16000106001).
  • Illustrissimus Princeps, Saxoniae Administrator = the Administrator (regent) of Electoral Saxony, Jessenius's clement lord.
  • Caesar / Caesarea Maiestas = Rudolf II.
  • Calendar. Dateline Pilsene 24. Martij Anno 1600; New Style (imperial context; the edition marks n.s.). The note that the Emperor would come to Prague "after Easter" dates the court's planned return.
  • A notarial-style collation note. Recovers a short attestation appended after Jessenius's signature and title, in which Jessenius personally certifies — in his own hand ("manu propria") — that this copy agrees with his own retained original. This is a standard collation-endorsement formula, of the kind added when a transmitted copy of a letter is checked against the author's autograph; it is evidence for how this particular letter survives (an authenticated copy, not the unverified original).
  • Identifying Jessenius. Corpus biographical sources confirm Johannes Jessenius a Jessen (Ján Jesenský), professor of medicine at Wittenberg from 1596, in whose house (the former Melanchthon/Peucer house) Tycho and his family lodged from December 1598 to June 1599 [Christianson, On Tycho's Island (Cambridge, 2000); Christianson, Tycho Brahe and the Measure of the Heavens (2020); Norlind, Tycho Brahe (1970); Studnička, Prager Tychoniana (1901)]. Jessenius later delivered Tycho's funeral oration in Prague (1601), became the celebrated anatomist who performed Prague's first public human dissection (1600), and was among the Bohemian nobles executed in 1621 after the Battle of White Mountain — the same figure the audit's rule flags as "the anatomist... in Prague in Tycho's circle."
