The original

M. CUNRADO ASLACIO BERGENSI.

... Doctissimi illius B. PITISCI de Triangulis acutum et compendiosum libellum lubens accepi, rogoque ut illi ex me gratias agas. Optarem plures eiuscemodi concionatores reperiri, qui Geometrica gnaviter callerent, forte plus esset in ijs circumspecti et solidi iudicij, rixarum inanium et logomachiarum minus. Si is mihi aliquando scripserit et de hisce studijs contulerit, inveniet responsorem non invitum. Idem apud alios eruditos viros, quos scis emunctioris nasi, effice.

19. Febr. 1596 Uraniburgi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Master Cunradus Aslacius of Bergen.

... I gladly received that most learned B. Pitiscus's keen and compendious little book on triangles, and I ask you to give him thanks on my behalf. I could wish that more preachers of this kind were to be found, men who had a diligent command of geometry: perhaps there would be in them more of circumspect and solid judgement, and less of empty wrangling and logomachy [word-battling]. If he should at some time write to me and confer about these studies, he will find me a not-unwilling correspondent. Bring this about likewise among the other learned men whom you know to be of a more discerning nose.

19 February 1596, [at] Uraniborg.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. M. Cunrado Aslacio Bergensi — Master Cort Aslaksen (Conradus Aslacus / Cunradus Aslacius Bergensis, 1564–1624), born in Bergen, Norway; one of Tycho's pupils and assistants (famuli) on Hven in the early 1590s, later professor at the University of Copenhagen (matriculated there 22 Oct. 1607; d. 10 Feb. 1624). This is an excerpt from Tycho's letter to him, printed in Aslaksen's own Sphaericorum libri tres (the edition of record; the source's edition field gives the genitive Sphaericorum librorum trium). The leading ... shows the editor's excision of the letter's opening — only this passage and the dateline are transmitted here.
  • B. Pitisci de Triangulis libellum — Bartholomäus Pitiscus (1561–1613), Calvinist court chaplain (hence Tycho's calling him and his like concionatores, "preachers") and mathematician, whose Trigonometria sive de dimensione triangulorum (first appended to A. Scultetus's Sphaericorum libri tres, Heidelberg 1595) introduced the very word "trigonometry." de Triangulis … libellum = "the little book on triangles," i.e. this Trigonometria; acutum et compendiosum = "keen and compendious." The abbreviation B. before the name is a courtesy initial (Bartholomaeus).
  • concionatores — "preachers" (contionatores), here in the literal clerical sense: Tycho wishes more clergymen had Pitiscus's command of geometry. Rendered "preachers," not metaphorically.
  • qui Geometrica gnaviter callerent — "who had a diligent command of geometry" (Geometrica callere = to be skilled in geometrical matters). The subjunctive callerent / esset keeps Tycho's wish-and-conjecture mood ("I could wish … perhaps there would be"); the hedge forte ("perhaps") is preserved, not sharpened.
  • logomachiarum — logomachia (from Greek λογομαχία, "word-battle"), "logomachy": disputes over mere words. Tycho's Greek-derived term is kept and glossed in brackets, by the edition's convention Paired with rixae inanes ("empty wrangling").
  • responsorem non invitum — "a not-unwilling correspondent" (literally "a not-unwilling answerer/respondent"); the litotes is reproduced rather than flattened to "willing."
  • quos scis emunctioris nasi — "whom you know to be of a more discerning nose," a Horatian idiom (emunctae naris, "of a wiped/keen nose" = of sharp critical judgement; cf. Horace, Sat. 1.4.8); comparative emunctioris, "of a keener nose." Kept as the period idiom and glossed; effice ("bring [it] about, see to it") governs idem ("the same," i.e. convey my like greeting/openness).
  • Calendar / place. 19. Febr. 1596 Uraniburgi = "19 February 1596, at Uraniborg" (on Hven) — Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar. The edition label ("1596. 19. februar. Uraniborg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
