To the Most Illustrious Prince and Lord, Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, Count in Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda, his most clement Prince and Lord — heartiest greeting.

Although I have received no reply from Your Highness to my more recent letters [sent] about the time of the autumn fair, I am nonetheless well enough persuaded that it is by no means unwelcome that I should write to Your Highness from time to time and confer on the business of astronomy; for amid so great an administration of political affairs many things may intervene that call Your Highness away from replying, even should you most greatly wish [to reply]. For Your Highness's Mathematician too — a man exceedingly diligent in these arts — signified to me by letter that Your Highness was at that time occupied in the giving-in-marriage of your illustrious Daughter and the magnificent preparation of the nuptials, so that it was not timely to attend to these matters; and he reports that it was at the same time enjoined upon him to reply to my [letters] in Your Highness's name, which he has indeed done in brief, since he did not judge it necessary to write back on every point. Above all he touches on the slight discrepancy that stands between us in the verification of the Fixed [stars], and reckons that it has by no means come about through a fault of Refractions that in the year [15]87, with the planet Venus mediating between [the star and] the Sun, he found the Longitudes of the Fixed [stars] earlier than by our determination by some five Scruples [arc-minutes] still very nearly — but in this he will scarcely persuade me. For I too at the same time did not cease to explore the limits [positions] of the Fixed [stars] by a similar method, and on several occasions, applying diligent examination, I found that, if the impediments of Parallaxes and Refractions are not warily enough guarded against, they fall back five or six Minutes further than they ought. But in December of the year just past, when Venus was again seen by day as a morning star [Eôa], I explored the whole matter the other way round, and found in the same manner that there too the places of the Fixed [stars] fell earlier than is right by just as much. Nor do I doubt that, if Your Highness's Mathematician should also, at the end of the past year and the beginning of this passing one — when Venus was for a long time seen in the daytime — weigh these matters again at the very morning [appearance], he would have discovered the same as I, and, his opinion now changed, would have come into agreement with our Longitudes. Now around both occasions the Declinations of the Sun and of Venus, and the latter's distances from the Earth, were not greatly unequal — which contributes much to the more deeply investigating the certainty of the matter under inquiry, especially if comparable Altitudes are used, as one ought. But so that Your Highness may have some example from our Observations ready to hand, I shall append this single one. In the year [15]87, then, as I said, on 24 January, at 0 hours 15 minutes P.M., when the Sun had an Altitude of 17½ degrees, there was observed here, by means of a four-cubit Trigonal Sextant, the distance of Venus [♀] from the centre of the Sun [☉] as 37 degrees 5¾ minutes, its Declination being found at the same time as 0 degrees 25 minutes North — and this by means of an Armilla ten feet in Diameter, resting upon an Axis pointed to the Poles of the World and revolvable together with it. Hence, and from the given Declination and Right Ascension of the Sun, known through its place in 14 degrees 17⅙ minutes of ♒ Aquarius, there is elicited, by the aid of Triangles, a difference of Right Ascension between the Sun and Venus of 33 degrees 26 minutes; which, added to the Right Ascension of the Sun [☉], yields the Right Ascension of Venus [♀] as 350 degrees 12⅙ minutes. Then, after the Sun had gone below the Horizon, the hour being 5¼, there was found the distance of Venus [♀] and of the Brighter [star] in the head of ♈ Aries [i.e. the Lucida of Aries] as 40 degrees 46 minutes, and Venus's Declination as 0 degrees 29½ minutes North. Therefore, since the Declination of the Lucida of ♈ Aries is known to be 21 degrees 29 minutes, the difference of the two on the Equator comes out as 35 degrees 50⅔ minutes; and consequently, account being taken of the advance Venus was making in the meantime, the Right Ascension of the Lucida of ♈ Aries at this time results as 26 degrees 7⅙ minutes; to which, the just-said Declination being applied, there comes out the Longitude of the Star at 1 degree 59 minutes of ♉ Taurus — which differs as little as can be from your determination, reduced to this [date]. Yet it exceeds the truth by very nearly five Scruples, on account of the neglected involvements of Refractions and Parallaxes in the Sun and in Venus. But again, in the year just past, 1588, on 26 October, at 11 hours 48 minutes A.M., when the Sun was raised nearly 18 degrees, it was distant from Venus [♀] as an eastern [morning] star by 37 degrees 1¾ minutes, and Venus's Declination was at the same time found to be 3 degrees 36¼ minutes South. And since the place of the Sun is given as 13 degrees 9⅓ minutes of ♏ Scorpio, from its Declination, with the Declination of Venus and the distance known, there is given an Ascensional difference of 35 degrees 33 minutes. Thence, from the known Right Ascension of the Sun, the Right Ascension of Venus will be established as 185 degrees 8⅙ minutes. And on the very next day, at 6 hours 38 minutes, there was taken the distance of Venus and of the Heart of ♌ Leo [i.e. Regulus, cor Leonis] as 42 degrees 24¾ minutes, Venus being then placed below the Equator by 3 degrees 34½ minutes. Hence, and by the given Declination of the Heart of ♌ Leo, 13 degrees 57⅙ minutes, there is found an Ascensional difference of 38 degrees 56½ minutes; which, taken away from the Right Ascension of Venus (which is known from the observation made beforehand to the Sun, the motion occurring in the meanwhile being nonetheless applied), discloses the Right Ascension of the Heart of ♌ Leo as 146 degrees 28⅚ minutes. And therefore the Right Ascension of the Lucida of ♈ Aries comes out as 25 degrees 56½ minutes; for the Ascensional difference between Regulus and this Star in ♈ Aries, gathered from other Observers, is 120 degrees 32⅓ minutes. Now from this Right Ascension of the Lucida of ♈ Aries, the Declination being applied — which was then 21 degrees 29½ minutes — there comes out its Longitude at 1 degree 50 minutes of ♉ Taurus; which falls short of [literally: anticipates, runs ahead of] the true Mark by just as much, namely by five Scruples, as it [the former] exceeded before, in that the Parallaxes and Refractions, here too not applied, drive the whole matter into the preceding [degrees], and so one departs from your annotation by twice as much again. But if the observation of the year [15]87 be reduced to this [date] by the motion that meanwhile inheres in the Fixed Stars, and the mean of the two places be taken — for thus the obstacles of Refractions and Parallaxes on both sides, which are equal, are excluded — the Longitude of the Star will be shown at 1 degree 55 minutes of ♉ Taurus, with a Latitude of 9 degrees 57½ minutes; and that these two stand correctly to the very Scruple I have no doubt: although your position, at 2 degrees 1 minute of ♉ Taurus, places that same Star [as it was] at the end of the year [15]88, so that an excess of 5½ minutes is committed as to Longitude — for as to Latitude we agree within half a Scruple. By a like method I have made trial of this investigation not only for these two years in Venus, as much in its western [evening] as in its eastern [morning] [appearance], but also in certain others — most notably, that is, by a comparison made of the Observations of the year [15]82 with [those of] [15]85, and of [15]86 too with the past [15]88 — and, many and varied observations being diligently weighed in each case and duly compared with one another, I have everywhere found that the limits of Longitude of the Fixed Stars are to be fixed in plainly the same way as they had earlier been established by me. Nay more, lest in the Solar track [the Sun's path] there should lurk any tiny grain of doubt which might hinder this Practice [Pragmatia] from being carried through to exact precision, I have recalled to the anvil of Demonstrations and numbers the Observations of the entire immediately preceding five-year period concerning [the Sun] itself, [made] with four or five skilfully-wrought Instruments, and of such magnitude that they assured us of each [datum] to within at least the sixth or fourth part of a Scruple. And although in the solar Apogee I have found that some little is to be taken away from our prior determination, and that the Eccentricity too is to be set somewhat smaller than before, yet these differences are so slight that they scarcely come to be reckoned of any moment: for they make the Apse [aux] of the Sun earlier by scarcely half a Degree, and the Eccentricity tighter [smaller] by only a third part of one Minute, [reckoned] in [units of] whole sixtieths of the portions of the Semidiameter of the Eccentric. But in the Sun's Longitude itself these same [corrections] introduce far less discrepancy, so much so that only [a difference] of one Minute creeps in around the Winter Solstice [Bruma], where the difference from the prior [value] is greatest — which is, especially in that place, altogether imperceptible. Nor do I doubt that this latter refinement [limitatio] in the Solar course, instituted from the more diligent examination of several years, has shown us its career so [closely] Analogous to the heavens, with such precision, that no Instruments nor the senses of the eyes could reach to a greater subtlety. Nevertheless, by applying this — of which I speak — once-more-rectified correction of the solar motion, I perceived that the places of the Inerrant [Fixed] Stars stand no otherwise than had earlier been ascertained from the prior emendation of the Sun's course, so that I do not hesitate in the least but that not even a single Scruple is either deficient or in excess in our rectification of the Fixed [stars] — [I have done this] also by instituting the practice of this investigation by other methods, and by variously corroborating the matter itself this way and that. Rothmann moreover indicates that I shall shortly see the procedure of your Observations, and the reasons why you dissent from me in those five Scruples — which I indeed desire with great eagerness. Nay, and my own reasonings, why I judge that nothing else is to be established in these matters than was long since indicated by me, Your Highness too will, God's favouring aid assisting, shortly perceive from the second chapter of the prior Volume of our On the More Recent Phenomena of the Aethereal World [De recentioribus Aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis], which I now have under the presses. For after I had, in the first chapter, deduced an exquisite standard of the solar motion from certain least-doubtful Observations of past years, through competent Geometrical Demonstrations, into a precise reckoning suited to these ages [present centuries], in the second I undertake, as I said, the restoration of the Fixed Stars from the very Foundations, and establish it as ratified and least-doubtful by several selected Observations through the Sun and the planet Venus, in both situations, the morning as well as the evening, together with certain other experiments concerning the same. Nor do I distrust but that, when Your Highness shall have surveyed my reasonings there too, and compared them with your own, you will grant that there is no fault underlying my investigation, and that I have striven toward the inner kernel of truth with such diligence and subtlety that scarcely anyone could apply greater. These things, however, I therefore recall the more at length, so that Your Highness may know that I do not in the least doubt of the Opinion formerly transmitted, as regards the disposition of the Fixed [stars], but that it is most exactly consonant with the Heaven itself; and because I should most greatly wish that at some time a uniform agreement might be entered into between us in the Longitudes of the Stars no less than in the Declinations and distances. But since a little before I happened upon mention of the Solar path once again restored, I am sending to Your Highness the Ephemerides of two years — namely of this passing [year] and of the one next at hand — computed from this very newfangled [neoteric] calculation, so that these places too may be explored by Your Highness's Instruments and compared with those formerly transmitted, however very slight be the discrepancy that intervenes. It is to be known, however, that these Solar Ephemerides are directed to the Meridian of our place, which I presuppose to be removed from the [point of] sunset [i.e. west] by 36 degrees 45 minutes; whence your Meridian, such as is at Kassel, adds nearly one Minute everywhere in the Sun's motion. For we here are, as it were, more easterly than you by a quincunx of an hour [five-twelfths of an hour] (as I reckon). I have also added a Table of Refractions, which sets forth, in the Sun, the Refraction occurring at each single Degree of Altitude, by which it appears higher than it really is — a thing hitherto wanting; so that Your Highness may examine the place not only in the Northern signs, by applying to the observed Altitude the Parallax (the reckoning of which too, as is in use with us, I have subjoined in a second Table for the threefold distance of the Sun from the Earth), but also in the more Southern [signs], although these are subject to evident Refraction, may guard against this same [refraction] by means of the said Table; and thus, throughout the whole Year — especially when the Sun lingers in those Signs which, within a day's space, admit some little variation of Declination — it will be possible to compare and corroborate the goal [terminus] of its [the Sun's] career, from Meridian Observations, with our calculation. For although Refractions cannot be predetermined so precisely for any given Altitudes but that sometimes a discrepancy of half a Scruple, or at most a whole one, may arise in them — owing to the air not being of the same purity everywhere, on this side or beyond — yet this [quantity] which I have defined, their quantity assembled from very many observations carefully made over several past years, I do not doubt will usefully and well enough answer to those Refractions which occur in a middling way and for the most part; of which too a trial can be instituted by you, with several observations compared one with another. But lest I detain Your Highness too long — now perhaps intent upon other business — I shall put an end to the Letter. And I pray God that He may govern Your Highness, unharmed and prosperous, for the longest time.
And now may Your Highness fare as well as may be.
Given at Uraniborg, 21 February, in the year 1589.
Devoted to Your Highness,
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent / recipient. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from Uraniborg on Hven, to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince whose Kassel observatory was Tycho's chief rival and partner in fixed-star astronomy. The full titulature — Comiti in Catzenellebogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain et Nidda — names the Hessian comital domains: Katzenelnbogen, Dietz (Diez), Ziegenhain, and Nidda. Salutation S. P. = salutem plurimam (dicit), "heartiest greeting".
  • "Your Highness's Mathematician." Christoph Rothmann (named explicitly as ROTHMANNVS later in the letter), the Kassel court mathematician, who had replied to Tycho in the Landgrave's name. The reply was occasioned by Wilhelm's preoccupation with the marriage of his daughter — most probably Christine of Hesse-Kassel, whose wedding fell in this period; the letter does not name her, so she is left unnamed here.
  • Place. Vraniburgum → Uraniborg (Tycho's observatory on Hven); Cassellae → Kassel.
  • Subject of the letter. A point of dispute in the verification (verificatio) of the fixed stars' longitudes: the Kassel and Hven star-catalogues differ by about five arc-minutes ("five Scruples") in longitude. Tycho's method derives the longitude of his fundamental reference star — the Lucida Arietis (see below) — by observing the planet Venus as an intermediary between the Sun (whose place is computed) and the star, at both an evening and a morning appearance, then taking the mean of the two to cancel the equal-but-opposite errors of refraction and parallax.
  • Technical terms (editorial glossary,–5.2): verificatio Fixarum → verification of the Fixed [stars]; Longitudo / Latitudo → (celestial) longitude / latitude; Declinatio → declination; Ascensio recta → right ascension; differentia Ascensionalis → ascensional difference; Refractio / Parallaxis → refraction / parallax; Apogaeum solare → solar apogee, with Absis Solis → the apse (aux) of the Sun; Eccentricitas → eccentricity; Eccentricus → eccentric; Altitudo → altitude; Meridianus → meridian. Sextans Trigonicus quadricubitalis → four-cubit Trigonal Sextant; Armilla → armilla / armillary (the equatorial armillary, "ten feet in Diameter, resting upon an Axis pointed to the Poles of the World"); Organa → Instruments.
  • Scrupulus. Rendered Scruple throughout (with [arc-minute] glossed at first occurrence). In this letter Scrupulus and Minutum are both used for the arc-minute; Minutum is rendered Minute, Scrupulus Scruple, preserving Tycho's own two words. Sub-minute fractions printed as ⅙, ¾, ⅓, ⅚, ½ are reproduced as in the source.
  • Symbols / glyphs (named in English, glyph kept in the Source block,): ☉ = Sun, ♀ = Venus; zodiac signs ♒ = Aquarius, ♈ = Aries, ♉ = Taurus, ♏ = Scorpio, ♌ = Leo.
  • Named stars. Lucidior in capite ♈ / Lucida ♈ = the Lucida of Aries (the bright star α Arietis, Hamal), Tycho's principal fundamental star, fixed at ≈ 1°55′ Taurus, latitude 9°57½′ N. Cor ♌ / cor Leonis = the Heart of Leo = Regulus (Regulus, named explicitly as Reguli). The whole numerical argument chains Venus → Sun → Regulus → Lucida Arietis.
  • Eôa / Orientalis vs. occidua / Vespertina. Eôa (Greek-derived, "of the dawn") and Orientalis both denote Venus as morning star; occidua / vespertina denote Venus as evening star. Translated descriptively as morning / evening star with the period sense preserved.
  • "earlier" / "anticipates the Mark." anteriores … cadere, anteuertit verum Scopum — the disputed positions fall at lower (earlier) longitude than the truth. Scopus = the "mark / target," i.e. the true value. The two uncorrected determinations err equally in opposite senses (one in excess, one running ahead into the preceding degrees), so their mean cancels the error — Tycho's central methodological point, kept literal.
  • Pragmatia — a Graecism for a "practical procedure / practice"; kept as Practice [Pragmatia].
  • De recentioribus Aetherei Mundi Phaenomenis — Tycho's Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata / the work "On the More Recent Phenomena of the Aethereal World" (the comet/nova volume), here said to be under the presses in 1589; the "prior Volume" (prior Tomus) with its first chapter on solar motion and second on the restoration of the fixed stars. Title kept in Latin with translation.
  • Solar parameters. Tycho reports a small downward revision: the solar apogee earlier by scarcely ½°, and the eccentricity smaller by 1/3 of a minute "of whole sixtieths of the portions of the Semidiameter of the Eccentric" (de sexagesimis integris Semidiametri Eccentrici portionibus) — i.e. the eccentricity expressed in sixtieths of the eccentric's semidiameter (radius = 60 parts). The effect on solar longitude is at most ≈ 1′ near the winter solstice (Bruma), called "imperceptible." Hedges preserved: modicum quid, aliquantulo minorem, vix dimidio Gradu, insensibile.
  • Longitude / meridian datum. Tycho gives Uraniborg's meridian as 36°45′ removed from sunset [west] and states Kassel is ≈ 1′ (in solar motion) west of Hven, the two being separated "by a quincunx of an hour." Horae quincunx = the Roman fractional quincunx = 5/12, hence five-twelfths of an hour (≈ 25 minutes of time) of easterly difference — kept literal with the fraction glossed. (This time-difference is internally large relative to the c. 1′ figure; Tycho's own "as I reckon" vt existimo hedge is preserved and not reconciled.)
  • Refraction & parallax tables. Tycho encloses a solar refraction table (refraction at each degree of altitude — "a thing hitherto wanting") and a solar parallax table "for the threefold distance of the Sun from the Earth" (ad triplicem Solis a Terra remotionem — a triple set of values, one per assumed solar distance, not "three times the distance"); both rendered with the ambiguity left as Tycho phrased it. He concedes refraction cannot be fixed perfectly: a residual of ½′ "or at most a whole one" from variable air purity — hedge kept.
  • The "A" markers. Throughout the printed source a superscript/inline "A" recurs (e.g. ETA NIDDA, PRINCIPI ACA DOMINO, anterioresA, M. 17⅙.A ♒, HorâA, cognitaA, cognoscatA, incidiA, redintegratae mentionem incidiA, the closing BRAHE.A). These are edition/transcription apparatus sigla (marginal note or variant markers carried over from the source transcription), not part of the Latin text; they are reproduced verbatim in the Source block by the edition's convention(2) and are disregarded in the translation. ETA NIDDA / ACA DOMINO read et Nidda / ac Domino with a stray "A"; Part. 3.A M. 36¼ reads Part. 3. M. 36¼. No semantic weight is given to them. (Flagged crux — see Confidence.)
  • Calendar. Dateline Datae Vraniburgi 21. Februarij, Anno 1589 — 21 February 1589, Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar; the edition's convention). The internal observation dates (24 January 1587; 26 and 27 October 1588; December 1588) are likewise O.S. The catalogue label agrees with the dateline (1589.21. februar. Uraniborg); no date discrepancy. Hours are given in the astronomical/civil reckoning of the source (P. M., A. M.) and left as written.
  • Closing formulae. Iamque T. Celsit. quam optime valeat → "And now may Your Highness fare as well as may be"; T. C. addict. = Tuae Celsitudini addictissimus/addictus, "Devoted to Your Highness".
