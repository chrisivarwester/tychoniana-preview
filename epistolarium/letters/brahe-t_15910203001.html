English translation machine translation, Probable

To the worthy, our trusty and well-beloved, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup. Wilhelm, by the grace of God Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, etc.

Our gracious greeting beforehand. Our trusty and well-beloved: this long while now we have been unable to learn of any letter, nor anything of your mathematical observations [Observationibus Mathematicis], much less how it fares with you and what tidings have occurred in those parts — whereas it would always have been to our particular pleasure had any such thing reached us from you. Since, then, we bear a special longing for [news of] this, it is our gracious desire of you that you would not only write to us of your own [affairs] and of the state of Denmark [Daniae statum], but would also communicate [communiciren] to us something out of your mathematical observations, and in particular your observations of the fixed stars [Observationes Stellarum Fixarum], so that we may compare [conferiren] them with our own. Furthermore we will not conceal from you that we have been told of an animal which is to be found in Norway, and of which the King is said to have several at Copenhagen, likewise at Frederiksborg in the deer-park; and the animals are said to be somewhat taller than a stag, but to have very pliant antlers, and to be called a Rix. Now although we have searched in all our authorities that write of animals, yet we have found neither description nor name of this same [beast]. We therefore graciously desire of you that you would have an image [Iconem] of these same animals made for us, and would carefully send us that [image] together with the natural history of the said animal — as also the aforementioned observations of the fixed stars — and would further give order that the enclosed letters be carefully forwarded to our mathematician and dear trusty [servant] Christoph Rothmann. Herein you do us particular pleasure, and we are in grace toward you, well disposed therewith to requite you in our favour and goodwill. Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on 3 February, in the year 1591. WILHELM, LANDGRAVE OF HESSE.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Wilhelm von Gottes Gnaden, Landtgraue zu Hessen = Wilhelm IV ("the Wise"), Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the observing prince of Kassel and Tycho's chief princely correspondent on astronomy. The titulature is abridged in this letter (Catzenelnbogen, etc.) relative to his fuller style elsewhere (…Dietz, Ziegenhain und Nidda).
  • Recipient style. Dem Vesten … Herrn zu Knudstrup — "to the worthy … Lord of Knudstrup" (Tycho's Scanian seat). Vester lieber besonder = "Our trusty and well-beloved" (the princely address-formula, the edition's convention); Unsern günstigen Gruß zuvor rendered "Our gracious greeting beforehand" (cf. Unsern gnedigen Gruß zuvor; the source here reads günstigen, "gracious/favourable," kept).
  • Latin code-switches (preserved by the edition's convention; the chancery German embeds Latin technical nouns and a verb): Observationibus Mathematicis = "mathematical observations"; Daniae statum = "the state of Denmark"; Mathematicis Observationibus; Observationes Stellarum Fixarum = "observations of the fixed stars" (Tycho's stellar-position catalogue work); communiciren (Latinate German verb) = "communicate, share"; conferiren = "compare, collate" (§2 lists conferiren as a characteristic Hesse-Kassel code-switch); Iconem (acc. of icon) = "image, figure/illustration"; Mathematico = "mathematician"; Februarij (Latin genitive in the dateline). All are italicised on first use and glossed.
  • Christoph Rothmann. vnserm Mathematico vnd lieben Getrewen Christoff Rothmann = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician at Kassel and himself a major Tycho correspondent. The Landgrave routes enclosed letters to Rothmann through Tycho — by this date (Feb. 1591) Rothmann had in fact left Kassel for Hven (he visited Tycho in 1590 and did not return); the request to forward letters "to our mathematician" reflects the court's expectation of his continued service.
  • Places. Koppenhagen = Copenhagen; Friderichsburg = Frederiksborg (the royal castle and deer-park at Hillerød, Zealand); Cassel = Kassel; Norwegen = Norway; Knudstrup (Tycho's estate). der König = the King of Denmark (Christian IV, then a minor; or the crown/royal household generally).
  • The animal — Rix (crux). The Landgrave, a keen natural-historian, describes an animal found in Norway, kept by the Danish king at Copenhagen and in the Frederiksborg deer-park, "somewhat taller than a stag, but with very pliant/slender antlers" (gar schwancke Hörner), "called a Rix," for which he could find neither Description nor Name in his zoological authorities, and of which he asks Tycho to procure a drawn Iconem with the beast's Historien (natural history). The name Rix is not standard German and is left untranslated and flagged: the description (Scandinavian habitat, larger than a red deer, distinctive antlers, kept in royal parks) fits the reindeer (Norwegian rein/reinsdyr) — the likeliest referent, Rix perhaps a Latinised/garbled form of rein- — though the elk/moose (Elch, Norwegian elg) also answers "taller than a stag." The text neither names the species in modern terms nor decides between them; "called a Rix" is kept and the identification recorded rather than resolved. Hörner is rendered "antlers" (the German word covers both horns and antlers).
  • "A" markers. The repeated stray capital A in the source (e.g. KNUDSTRUP.A, zuvorA, wenigerA, dennA, mügen.A, angezeigteA, BrieffeA, wirA, gewogenA, geneigtA, FebruarijA) are footnote/sidenote anchor artifacts carried over from the working transcription of the 1596 printed edition, not part of the letter's text; they are reproduced in the Source block by the edition's convention(2) (verbatim transcription) but are disregarded in the translation. No word of the German is affected by them.
  • Negation check. Three negations in the body, all reproduced: kein Schreiben … vernemen mügen ("unable to learn of any letter"); nicht allein … Sondern auch ("not only … but also"); Wollen euch … nicht verhalten ("we will not conceal from you"); plus the litotes weder Description noch Namen … nicht erfunden ("found neither description nor name") — the source's pleonastic double negative (weder … noch … nicht) is normal Frühneuhochdeutsch emphasis, rendered as a single English negation without adding or dropping negative force.
  • Closing formula. wir seynds jegen euch in Gnaden, damit wir euch gewogen, zu verschulden wol geneigt — the elliptical chancery sign-off of princely favour: "we are in grace toward you, well disposed to requite you therewith in our favour and goodwill." Rendered to preserve the register without supplying a finite verb the source omits.
  • Calendar / place. Datum in vnser Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel, am 3. Februarij, Anno 1591 = "Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on 3 February 1591." Old Style (Hesse-Kassel, a Lutheran territory, retained the Julian calendar at this date). The edition label ("1591. 3. februar. Kassel") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. Header date follows the dateline.
