The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD IACOBUM BORDINGUM.

S. P.

CLARISSIME et Excellentissime Domine Doctor BORDINGE, Cancellarie dignissime et Amice honorande. Cum hic Mechanicus senex M. PETRUS IACHENOW denuo isthuc a me rediret, non omittendum duxi, quin ad Illustrissimum Principem ULDARICUM, Dominum meum Clementissimum, aliquid literarum submisse darem, siquidem eius Celnis Clementissimam erga me voluntatem indies magis magisque experiar. Rogoque ut ipsius Celni easdem meo nomine submisse offeras atque in meliorem partem interpreteris, si quid liberius, re sic urgente, indicavi, idque in silentio secreto prematur, cum ex eâ, quam erga Illustrissimum illum et optimum Principem, tum quoque te, gero fiduciâ, sinceroque animo proficiscantur. Poterunt hæc forte nimis audacter prolata censeri, siquidem veritas plærumque odiosa sit; attamen si rei molem et eorum, quæ mihi immerito contigerunt, cumulum spectemus, vix supremis labris attigisse, quæ dicenda forent, videbor. Verum ego hæc omnia Deo committo. Veritas temporis filia aliquando forte prodet, quid æque aut inique factum sit. Illmo Duci necdum quidpiam responsum esse, etiamnum intelligo. Quod et præsagiebam. Quale responsum ad meas receperim, ex ijs utcunque intellexisti, quas Lubecâ tibi perscripsi, cum inde Instrumenta mea et supellectilem librariam huc transferri curarem. Quare

Sat Patriæ nostrisque datum, nunc Teuthona tellus
Excipit, et Patriam largitur maximus Orbis.

Curo quædam ex meis Operibus hîc labore Typographico compleri, quæ, ubi absoluta fuerint, vobiscum communicabo. In re pecuniariâ me isthic concernente satis actum est. Recepi literas fideiussorias et priores restitui.

Novarum rerum nihil peculiare est, nisi quod res composita dicatur inter Pontificem et Ferrariæ Ducem. Batavos se absentare et tractationem de pace aversari, neque enim hostibus satis fidunt tam sæpe circumventi. Novam Reginam in Anglia electam frementibus Scotis narrant. Nihil tamen adhuc certi constat. Vobis proculdubio isthic his plura et certiora innotuerunt. Rex noster Sermus fuit apud Dominum RANZOVIUM Bredenbergæ præcedente hebdomadâ ab eo isthic magnifice et splendide exceptus et mox inde Haderslebiam recepit. Plura nunc non vacat. Sum enim occupatissimus. Tu vero mi amantissime Domine BORDINGE quam felicissime vale, meque solitâ benevolentiâ (quemadmodum et ego tui amorem non deponam unquam) constanter prosequere.

Raptim Wandesburgo die 29. Martij Anni 1598.

TYCHO BRAHE TO JAKOB BORDING.

Heartiest greeting.

Most renowned and most excellent Master Doctor Bording, most worthy Chancellor and honoured Friend. Since the old craftsman here, Master Petrus Jachenow, was returning thither from me once again, I thought it not to be neglected that I should submissively address some lines to the Most Illustrious Prince Ulrich, my Most Clement Lord — seeing that day by day I experience more and more His Highness's most clement goodwill toward me. And I ask that you submissively present them to His Highness in my name, and interpret them in the better sense, if I have indicated anything rather freely, the matter being so pressing; and that it be kept in close silence — since they proceed from the trust which I bear toward that Most Illustrious and best Prince, and toward you as well, and from a sincere heart. These things may perhaps be reckoned to have been uttered too boldly, since the truth is for the most part odious; yet if we consider the weight of the matter and the heap of the things that have undeservedly befallen me, I shall be seen scarcely to have touched, with the tips of my lips, what would need to be said. But I commit all this to God. Truth, the daughter of time, will perhaps one day bring forth what has been done justly or unjustly. That nothing has yet been answered to the Most Illustrious Duke, I understand even now. Which I also foresaw. What sort of answer I received to my own [letters], you have learned in some fashion from those [letters] which I wrote out to you from Lübeck, when I was arranging for my instruments and my library furnishings to be transferred hither from there. Wherefore —

Enough has been given to my homeland and to my own; now the German land
Receives me, and the widest world bestows a homeland.

I am having certain of my works completed here by the labour of the press, which, when they have been finished, I shall share with you. In the money matter concerning me thither, enough has been done. I have received the letters of guaranty and have returned the earlier ones.

Of new things there is nothing particular, except that the affair is said to be settled between the Pope and the Duke of Ferrara. [They report] that the Dutch are absenting themselves and turning away from the negotiation for peace, for, so often circumvented, they do not sufficiently trust their enemies. They tell of a new Queen elected in England, with the Scots seething. Yet nothing certain is as yet established. Doubtless more, and more certain, things have come to your knowledge there. Our Most Serene King was with Master Rantzau at Bredenberg in the preceding week, magnificently and splendidly received by him there, and soon thereafter withdrew to Hadersleben. There is no leisure now for more, for I am most busy. But do you, my most beloved Master Bording, fare most happily, and continue to follow me steadfastly with your accustomed benevolence (just as I, too, shall never lay aside my love of you).

In haste, from Wandsbek, on the 29th day of March, the year 1598.

Apparatus

  • Sender / context. Tycho Brahe, writing from Wandsbek (Wandesburgo), the castle of Heinrich Rantzau near Hamburg where Tycho lodged in 1597–98 after leaving Denmark; the Astronomiae instauratae mechanica was completed here, and this letter ("I am having certain of my works completed here by the labour of the press") belongs to that printing campaign. The letter is a covering note: it accompanies a now-lost submissive letter to Duke Ulrich that Tycho asks Bording to deliver and gloss favourably.
  • Recipient. Iacobus Bordingus = Jakob Bording (the younger), at this date chancellor of (the Duke of) Mecklenburg (so Dreyer, Tycho Brahe, p. 246); a learned correspondent and Tycho's intermediary at the Güstrow/Mecklenburg court. Salutation Cancellarie dignissime ("most worthy Chancellor") confirms the office.
  • Illustrissimus Princeps Ulldaricus / Illmo Duci = Ulrich, Duke of Mecklenburg(-Güstrow) (1527–1603), to whom Tycho was appealing in his exile; Dominus meus Clementissimus ("my Most Clement Lord") is the courtesy register, with eius Celnis / ipsius Celni = eius Celsitudinis / ipsius Celsitudini ("His Highness," abbreviated), rendered "His Highness" by the edition's convention
  • M. Petrus Iachenow — "the old craftsman" (Mechanicus senex) Petrus Jachenow, a workman/messenger shuttling between Tycho and the Mecklenburg court; M. = Magister, "Master."
  • Dominus Ranzovius … Bredenbergae = Heinrich Rantzau (Henrik Rantzau, 1526–1598), royal governor (Statthalter) of the Danish duchies and Tycho's Wandsbek host, at his seat Bredenberg (Breitenburg, Holstein). Rex noster Sermus = Rex noster Serenissimus, "our Most Serene King" — Christian IV of Denmark.
  • Haderslebia = Haderslev (Hadersleben), in the duchy of Schleswig.
  • Lubeca = Lübeck, Tycho's prior staging-point; Instrumenta mea et supellectilem librariam = "my instruments and my library furnishings" — the documented Lübeck→Wandsbek transfer of Tycho's apparatus and books. Instrumenta rendered "instruments" by the edition's convention
  • Verse couplet. Sat Patriae nostrisque datum … is kept as verse by the edition's convention It is a Tychonic adaptation of the Virgilian/Lucanic patriae … datum topos (cf. Aeneid / the sat patriae tag); the sense: having given enough to Denmark, Tycho now takes Germany — and the wide world — as his homeland. maximus Orbis rendered "the widest world" (superlative kept; "world" not "orb," since here it is the terrestrial world, not an astronomical orbis/sphaera).
  • Veritas temporis filia — "Truth, the daughter of time" (veritas filia temporis), the proverbial sententia; kept as the maxim it is.
  • literas fideiussorias — "letters of guaranty / suretyship" (bonds of guarantee); priores restitui = "I returned the earlier ones." A financial-settlement detail (In re pecuniariâ … satis actum est), left in its own legal-commercial register.
  • News items (all hedged by Tycho — preserved, not sharpened). (1) res composita … inter Pontificem et Ferrariae Ducem — the settlement between the Pope (Clement VIII) and the Duke of Ferrara, i.e. the Devolution of Ferrara to the Papal States (1597–98); Tycho reports it as dicatur, "is said to be." (2) Batavos … tractationem de pace aversari — the Dutch (States) shunning peace negotiations (the run-up to the Franco-Spanish Peace of Vervins, May 1598); given with the explanatory hedge neque enim hostibus satis fidunt ("for they do not sufficiently trust their enemies"). (3) Novam Reginam in Anglia electam frementibus Scotis — a report (narrant) of "a new Queen elected in England, with the Scots seething." This is unfounded rumour: Elizabeth I reigned until 1603. Tycho immediately flags it — Nihil tamen adhuc certi constat ("yet nothing certain is as yet established") — and the translation preserves the rumour-frame ("They tell of …") and the explicit hedge rather than resolving or "correcting" it.
  • Greek / symbols. None in this letter.
  • MS / textual note. The sole witness is the Vienna draft (Kladde), cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.24; readings follow Dreyer's edited text. Abbreviations in the source expanded silently only where unambiguous (Sermus = Serenissimus, Illmo = Illustrissimo, Celnis/Celni = Celsitudinis/Celsitudini); flagged here rather than altered in the Source block.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 29. Martij Anni 1598, from Wandsbek (Holstein). Denmark and the German Protestant territories were still on the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1598; the date is O.S. and is not converted. The catalogue label ("1598. 29. marts. Wandsburg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
