The original

NOBILISSIMO DOCTRINAQUE ET VIRTUTIBUS PRÆSTANTISSIMO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE OTTONIDI DANO, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP, DOMINO SUO OBSERVANTER COLENDO.

POST tuum Augusta discessum, generose Domine TYCHO, ad te semel litteras dedi, in quibus T. D. pro scripto de stella anno 1572 visa edito, mihique dono misso gratias egi. Deinde aliquid de voluntate SCHISLERI et Globi conficiendi ratione cuiuscunque magnitudinis subiunxi. Spero autem easdem litteras tibi esse redditas. De voluntate vero tua cum nihil adhuc certi cognoverim, pauca eadem de re tum cum affine meo PETRO tum cum N. GEMPERLIN pictore egi coram, ut de mea erga te benevolentia tibi quid certi constaret. Meretur enim virtus tua, ut tibi omnibus modis gratificetur. Quare cum singulari Dei beneficio ad excolendas artes Mathematicas, quas ipse impense amo, excitatus esse videaris, Deum Opt. Max. precor, ut tibi vitam largiatur diuturnam, quo laudatissimis artibus iam quidem nutantibus brevique casuris tuo ingenio quam felicissime succurrat. Quadrans, quem hîc in horto Domino Consuli magna industria extruxisti, adhuc constat et usui observationum cœlestium inservit. Machina certe ingens, vixque ullibi nostro ævo similis reperitur, quam etiam magnus ille PETRUS RAMUS summopere, cum hîc et tecum esset, admiratus est. D. WOLFIUS tibi plurimam salutem ex me dicere voluit: ipse scripsisset, nisi publica negotia illi impedimento fuissent et affinis meus non cum summa festinatione abitum parasset.

Vale feliciter.
Augustæ Rheticæ pridie Matthæi Apostoli 1577.
T. D. deditiss.
M. Iohan. Maior.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and, in learning and in virtues, most excellent Master Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup, his lord to be honoured with respect.

After your departure from Augsburg, noble Master Tycho, I once sent you a letter, in which I gave thanks to Your Lordship for the writing on the star seen in the year 1572, published and sent to me as a gift. Then I added something concerning Schisler's intention and the method of constructing a globe of any size whatever. I hope, moreover, that that same letter has been delivered to you. But since I have as yet learned nothing certain about your wishes, I have dealt face to face a little on the same matter, both with my kinsman Peter and with N. Gemperlin the painter, so that something certain about my goodwill toward you might be established for you. For your virtue deserves that one should oblige you in every way. Therefore, since by God's singular favour you seem to have been roused to the cultivation of the mathematical arts, which I myself ardently love, I pray to God, Best and Greatest, that He grant you a long life, whereby He may most happily succour, through your genius, those most praiseworthy arts that are now indeed faltering and soon to fall. The quadrant which you built here in the garden for the Lord Consul, with great industry, still stands and serves the use of celestial observations. A mighty machine, to be sure, and scarcely anywhere is its like to be found in our age — which that great Peter Ramus, too, when he was here and with you, exceedingly admired. Master Wolf wished me to convey his heartiest greeting to you: he would have written himself, had public affairs not been a hindrance to him, and had my kinsman not prepared his departure in the greatest haste.

Fare you happily well.
At Augsburg in Rhaetia, the day before [the feast of] Matthew the Apostle, 1577.
Most devoted to Your Lordship,
Master Johann Major.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. M. Iohan. Maior = Johann Major (Magister), a mathematics-minded Augsburg correspondent within Tycho's circle from his 1569–70 Augsburg sojourn. The M. is the academic Magister ("Master,"); T. D. = Tua Dominatio ("Your Lordship") and T. D. deditiss. = Tuae Dominationi deditissimus ("most devoted to Your Lordship"), expanded in the translation.
  • Recipient address. TYCHONI BRAHE OTTONIDI DANO, DOMINO DE KNUDSTRUP — "Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, the Dane, Lord of Knudstrup." Ottonides is the Greek-style patronymic for Otte Brahe, Tycho's father (cf. brahe-t_15711113001); Knudstrup (Knutstorp, Scania) is the Brahe family seat.
  • scriptum de stella anno 1572 visa — "the writing on the star seen in the year 1572": Tycho's De nova stella (Copenhagen, 1573), his account of the 1572 supernova in Cassiopeia, which he had sent Major as a gift.
  • Schisler. Christoph Schissler (Schissler the Elder, c. 1531–1608), the celebrated Augsburg maker of mathematical and astronomical instruments; the letter relays his "intention" (voluntas) and a scheme for constructing a globe (globus,) of arbitrary size. The reading of the proper name follows the source; identification is editorial.
  • affinis meus Petrus — "my kinsman Peter": affinis denotes a relative by marriage (an in-law); the man is named only as Peter and is not further identified here.
  • N. Gemperlin pictor — "N. Gemperlin the painter": Georg Gemperle(in) (or a relative), an Augsburg painter; the initial N. stands for an unsupplied forename (Latin Nomen/nescio quis convention), left as "N." rather than expanded.
  • Quadrans … in horto Domino Consuli … extruxisti — "the quadrant which you built in the garden for the Lord Consul": the great quadrant Tycho erected at Augsburg in 1569–70 in the garden of Paul Hainzel (the Consul, i.e. a burgomaster of Augsburg), the c. 19-foot (5–6 m) instrument described in Tycho's Mechanica. Consul is the Latin term for the city's chief magistrate (Bürgermeister); rendered "Consul" with the office flagged here. Quadrans → quadrant.
  • Machina … ingens — "a mighty machine": machina here = the large instrument (the quadrant), in the period sense of a great engineered structure; rendered "machine" with the sense noted.
  • Petrus Ramus — Petrus Ramus (Pierre de la Ramée, 1515–1572), the French humanist and logician, who visited Augsburg and met Tycho there (c. 1570) and admired the Hainzel quadrant; he was killed in the St Bartholomew's Day massacre, August 1572, hence "that great Peter Ramus" is spoken of as deceased.
  • D. Wolfius — Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and Greek scholar (cf. brahe-t_15711113001), here sending greetings via Major.
  • Astronomy / instruments. artes Mathematicae → "the mathematical arts"; observationum coelestium → "celestial observations" (cf. motus, altitudo,); quadrans and globus by the edition's convention
  • laudatissimis artibus iam quidem nutantibus brevique casuris … succurrat — the hope that Tycho's genius may "succour those most praiseworthy arts, now faltering and soon to fall": the author's own hedged, elegiac framing of the state of the mathematical sciences is preserved, not sharpened.
  • Calendar / place. Augustae Rheticae pridie Matthaei Apostoli 1577 = "at Augsburg in Rhaetia, the day before [the feast of] Matthew the Apostle, 1577." The feast of St Matthew the Apostle is 21 September; pridie ("the day before") therefore = 20 September (O.S.; Denmark and the Empire alike were still on the Julian calendar in 1577). The Roman-style ecclesiastical dateline is kept and glossed by the edition's convention; the as-written date (20 September) agrees with the edition label ("1577. 20. september. Augsburg"). Augusta Rhaetica is a variant of Augusta Vindelicorum = Augsburg.
  • MS / edition. MS witness as given: Vienna, ÖNB, cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66 (the same Vienna codex of Tycho's incoming correspondence as the Wolf letter). Edition of record per the source's edition field: Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia VII (Epistolae).
