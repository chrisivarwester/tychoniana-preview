The original

ILLUSTRI, GENEROSO ET MAGNIFICO VIRO, MAIORUM INSIGNIBUS, VERA VIRTUTE ET DOCTRINA MULTIPLICI PRÆLUSTRI DOMINO TYCHONI A BRAHE IN BENATIA ET KNUTHSTORFFP EQUITI DANICO, SERENISSIMÆ MAIESTATIS IMPERATORIÆ ET CONSILIARIO ET DOMINO SUO PERPETUA OBSERVANTIA COLENDO.
AD MANUS GÆ MÆ IPSIUS PROPRIAS.

ILLUSTRIS, Magnifice et Generose Domine TYCHO, omni cum observantia perpetuo mihi colende Domine: Literæ Gæ Mæ Væ scriptæ Pragæ 6a Augusti stylo novo, redditæ mihi sunt 10a Augusti stylo veteri, cum reverterer illo ipso tempore ex Comitijs Iuridicis, habitis apud Dithmarsos, ubi per septimanas tres hæseram, evocatus eo ab Inclyto Rege nostro Daniæ. Ago autem primo omnium serio pectore gratias Deo opt. Max. quod Gen. Mm Vm cum omni familia hactenus sospitem et salvam clementer tutatus fuerit, eundemque precor, ut quam diutissime illam florentem servet.

Cœterum Illustrissimo Principi OTTHONI, Duci Brunsvicensium et Lunæburgensium, ea quæ mihi commisit Ga Μ Va, suo nomine debita cum submissione retuli: is quid ad eam rescribi per me voluerit, ex Cels. eius autographo ad me misso, hisce nostris inserto, intelliget.

De congressu Embdæ indicto subitoque iterum discessu Legatorum Danicorum et Anglicorum, quæ scripseram, hæc ita vere se habent. Verum ex eo ad me relatum est, Anglos in Daniam usque sequutos Regem nostrum, ibique negotium omne probe compositum et sopitum esse. Rex ipse noster hoc tempore in Iutiam cum Regina Coniuge excurrisse fertur ad venationes, cuius quoque adventus momentaneus forte Crempæ in vicinia expectatur. De emissis 50 navibus nihil hîc accepimus, certo quod communicari Gæ Mæ Væ possit.

FREDERICO ROSENCRANZIO fœliciorem iam auram aspirare profecto lætor, et ubi occasionem scribendi nactus fuero, OLIGERUM de præsenti hominis fortuna hilariori certiorem reddam. Domino RUMPIO et Domino CORADUCIO, Viris honoratissimis, G. M. Va officia mea ut submissa, data occasione, deferat, reverenter oro. Gm Mm Vm Dominus VINCENTIUS MÖLLERUS Consul quoque officiose resalutat. Valeat quam optime Ga Ma Va omni cum familia, cui me totum dedo.

Datæ raptim Hamburgæ 13a Augusti Anno 1600.
Gm Mm Vm omni cum observantia
qui colit
VITUS WINSHEMIUS,
m. p.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the illustrious, generous and magnificent man, pre-eminent in ancestral arms, in true virtue and in manifold learning, Lord Tycho a Brahe at Benátky and Knudstrup, Danish knight, councillor of His Most Serene Imperial Majesty, his lord to be revered with perpetual observance.
Into His Generous Magnificence's own hands.

Illustrious, Magnificent and Generous Lord Tycho, my lord forever to be revered with all observance: Your Generous Magnificence's letter, written at Prague on the 6th of August New Style, was delivered to me on the 10th of August Old Style, just as I was at that very time returning from the juridical assembly held among the Dithmarschers, where I had been detained three weeks, having been summoned thither by our Illustrious King of Denmark. First of all I give serious and heartfelt thanks to God, best and greatest, that he has thus far mercifully kept Your Generous Magnificence safe and sound with all your household; and I pray him to keep it flourishing as long as may be.

For the rest, to the Most Illustrious Prince Otto, Duke of Brunswick and Lüneburg, I reported in your name, with due submission, the things which Your Generous Magnificence committed to me; what he wished to be written back to it through me, Your Magnificence will learn from his own autograph sent to me and enclosed in this our letter.

Concerning the congress appointed at Emden and the sudden departure once more of the Danish and English legates — what I had written holds thus, truly. But it has since been reported to me that the English followed our King all the way into Denmark, and that there the whole business was duly composed and laid to rest. Our King himself is said at this time to have gone off into Jutland with the Queen his consort for the hunt; his arrival, momentary [and brief], is perhaps expected in the neighbourhood of Crempe. Of the 50 ships sent out we have heard nothing here certain enough to be communicated to Your Generous Magnificence.

That a happier breeze now blows upon Frederik Rosenkrantz I am truly glad; and when I get the occasion to write, I will inform Holger of the man's present, more cheerful fortune. To Lord Rumpf and Lord Coraduz, most honoured men, I reverently beg Your Generous Magnificence to convey my submissive services, as occasion offers. Lord Vincent Möller, the Burgomaster, also dutifully returns Your Generous Magnificence's greeting. May Your Generous Magnificence fare as well as possible with all your household, to whom I give myself wholly.

Given in haste at Hamburg, the 13th of August, in the year 1600.
Your Generous Magnificence's, with all observance,
who reveres [you],
Vitus Winshemius,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound letter to Tycho. Veit Ortel von Windsheim (Vitus Winshemius) (1542–1608), Wittenberg physician, writing from Hamburg — replying to Tycho's letter (Prague, 6 Aug) and continuing the relationship attested in Tycho's own letter to him of 20 April 1600 (brahe-t_16000420001).
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, titled eques Danicus (Danish knight) and imperial councillor, "at Benátky and Knudstrup." The address-line ad manus Generosae Magnificentiae ipsius proprias = "into his Generous Magnificence's own hands."
  • Persons named.
  • Otto, Dux Brunsvicensium et Lunaeburgensium = Otto, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, to whom Winshemius delivered a commission of Tycho's, enclosing the Duke's autograph reply.
  • Fredericus Rosencranzius = Frederik Rosenkrantz (1571–1602), Danish nobleman (disgraced and exiled from the Danish court after the 1599 scandal with Rigborg Brockenhuus, then in military service abroad), whose fortunes are now improving — distinct from Oligerus = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (cf. brahe-t_16000420001).
  • Dominus Rumpius = Wolfgang Rumpf von Wielross, Rudolf II's chief steward / privy councillor; Dominus Coraducius = Rudolf Coraduz (Coraduz), imperial vice-chancellor — two of Tycho's principal court contacts at Prague (the Coraduz/Barvitius axis).
  • Vincentius Möllerus, Consul = Vincent Möller, Burgomaster (Consul) of Hamburg, who adds his greeting.
  • Inclytus Rex noster Daniae / Rex … cum Regina coniuge = Christian IV of Denmark and his consort.
  • News & places. Comitia Iuridica apud Dithmarsos = a juridical assembly in Dithmarschen (Holstein) where Winshemius had served three weeks at the Danish king's summons; congressus Embdae = the abortive Anglo-Danish conference at Emden; the English envoys followed Christian IV into Denmark, where the dispute was settled; the King is hunting in Jutland; Crempa = Krempe in Holstein; 50 naves = an unspecified fleet rumour. (The Anglo-Danish friction of 1600 turned on Sound tolls, fishing, and the Spitsbergen/Greenland question.)
  • Epistolary formulae & abbreviations. Heavily abbreviated honorifics: G[enerosa] M[agnificentia] V[estra] = "Your Generous Magnificence" (declined throughout — Gae Mae Vae, Gm Mm Vm); m. p. = manu propria ("with his own hand"); Datae raptim → "given in haste"; closing qui colit → "who reveres [you]."
  • Calendar (this chapter's convention — explicit double date). The letter itself records the Julian/Gregorian gap: Tycho's letter was "written at Prague 6 August stylo novo [New Style], delivered … 10 August stylo veteri" [Old Style] — the 10-day difference between Imperial (Catholic, Gregorian) Prague and Lutheran north Germany. Winshemius's own dateline, 13a Augusti 1600 at Hamburg, is Old Style. Reproduced as written; not converted.
