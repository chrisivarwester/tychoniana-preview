The original

TAM DOCTRINA EXIMIA QUAM PROSAPIA CLARA VIRO INSIGNITER DECORATO DOMINO OLIGERO ROSENKRANTZIO DE ROSENHOLM, CONSANGVINEO, AFFINI ET AMICO INTIMO.
S. P.

QUAe nuper tibi destinavi, exoptatissime mi OLIGERE consangvinee et affinis carissime, per CLAUDIUM MULÆUM, quem ante Comitia istuc misi, te omnia nunc accepisse non dubito. Succurrit post MULÆI abitum in libro de Astronomia Mechanica pagina quae est G. 3. in qua Domini IACOBI CURTIJ, circa quadrantis cuiusdam subdivisionem, inventio exhibetur, in ipso titulo ante hoc nomen: CURTIO omissam esse literam I. vel potius IAC. unde praenomen eius indicaretur, et postea in infima eiusdem tituli linea impressum est: GENEROSE ADINVENTA pro INGENIOSE ADINVENTA; quod nescio an in eo, quod misi, exemplari emendatum sit; sin minus, cuperem id adhuc abs te calamo, quod facile fit in tam densa papyro, corrigi, ne Zoili et hîc habeant quo abutantur. Quid de literis Berlinensibus actum sit, ex te cognoscere aveo. Si quid in Comitijs illis, quod mea interest scire, tibi innotuit, hoc quidquid est, mature etiam, antequam MULÆUS (modo fieri potest) reversus fuerit, significa. Fuit ante triduum hîc apud me IACOBUS ROSENKRANTZIUS noster, in Belgium hinc cogitans; is graves et difficiles in hisce Comitijs controversias motum iri referebat; et inter alia PEPLERI istius caussam recognoscendam decidendamque existimavit. At quo iure id fieri queat, ignoro, cum citationis forma mihi etiam non sit praesentata, nec quidpiam a Praefecto meo de ea innotuerit, ut existimem, eam ne quidem Uraniburgi lectam esse, aut si id factum, non debito tempore aut modo; sed sit hoc quidquid velit; si ius et fas locum inveniunt, nihil periculi, sin secus, nihil novi. Existimo, imo expertus sum, quod si ego in Dania quidpiam proponerem, quod aeque verum sit atque ea, quae Geometrice demonstrantur, posse nihilominus ea ipsa apud quosdam, qui iustitiae praeesse volunt, falsitatis insimulari. Quid igitur faciendum, ubi ius et veritas ob privatos affectus atque personae odium locum non merentur? Aiebat insuper dictus noster Consangvineus, se exemplum literarum, quas Sermo Regi nostro, cum Rostochij essem, scripsi, unâ cum responso ad easdem, cum Fioniam transiret, apud Nobiles quosdam reperisse, et si recte memini, CANUTUM RUD hîc nominavit, quem a CAROLO BRUSCHIO talia accepisse opinabatur. Quod si sic est, optarem, eos, qui horum facti sunt participes, ea, quae mihi rescripta sunt, rectius quam litera ipsa praetendit, explanata habere, quae incautis et inscijs plausibilis forte videri queat, cum tamen longe aliter res se habeat, et mihi foret quam facilimum, uti ipsemet scis, omnia isthic obiecta replicare et contrario modo demonstrare, sed obstant ea, quae prius dixi, et authoritas eorum, qui haec vafre consarcinarunt, veritati nunc praevalet. Opus igitur est patientia, et tandem bona caussa triumphat, si non in Dania, saltem alibi: O utinam in nos illud Virgilianum non propediem quadraret. Excessere omnes adytis arisque relictis Dij, quibus imperium hoc steterat. Item illud paulo antea: Fuimus Troes, fuit Ilium et ingens gloria Danorum. Solet enim regnum divinitus puniendum sanis destitui consilijs, et in partes quorundam vafritia discindi, atque sub veri bonique praetextu contraria tecte agere. Solent etiam omnia praecedere, qualia non pauca isthic audiuntur, si modo, quae narrantur, veritati congruunt. Cum quaesivissem a praenominato ROSENKRANTZIO de halece characterizato in Norvegia reperto, nihil quidem certi de eo referre potuit, attamen aliud piscium genus, quod latini aculeatum, nostrates Hornfisk vocant, adhuc longe mirabilioribus notatum characteribus nuper prope insulam Tossingam, seu potius parvulam illi adiacentem, repertum esse, certissime affirmavit; quarum figurarum, si tibi hac de re quid constat, efformationem mecum communicari vellem. Scripsit mihi postea CRAMERUS tuus, et carmen quoddam literis inclusit. Misit etiam BRASCHIUS Poëta Rostochiensis Heroicum in mei gratiam concinnatum, sed neutrum satis placet; habes tamen hîc utrumque, Cramerianum ipsius manu scriptum, unâ cum literis, prout accepi; neque enim describendi otium suppetit; quod tamen, cum exemplum eius non habeam, mihi remitti velim. Habeo praeterea alia eiusmodi, inter quae est quoddam ex orientali Frisia admodum prolixum; et cuduntur hinc inde adhuc plura in memoriam rei apud nos bene gestae. Expectat autem Germania avide tuum pro vicina sua responsum. Expecto et ego commentaria illa, quae scis, quibus mea per oportunitatem intertexam; ubi vero tua absoluta fuerint, ea cum KRAGIO nostro tacite communica, ut et is ex rei transactae historia, quam pro maiori parte novit, suam quoque interponat sententiam. Exemplum earum, quas THADDÆUS HAGGÆCIUS ante aliquot hebdomadas Praga mihi scripsit, Domino Doctori NICOLAO KRAGIO iam antea misi, qui procul dubio te eius reddidit participem. Sed et hisce diebus breves quasdam ab eodem THADDÆO denuo accepi, quarum hîc habes descriptum; paucioribus rem expedit, siquidem dubitavit an hîc essem, an vero iter Pragam versus suscepissem; vides eos serio meum adventum expetere et expectare, atque ipsi Caesari me concernentia iam innotuisse. Tu et KRAGIUS consulite in medium, et aliorum quoque, quibus res meae serio et sincere cordi sunt, sententiam tacite expiscemini. Ego nihil certi concludere possum, antequam TEGNAGLIUS et MULÆUS ad me reversi fuerint. Fuit hesterna die apud me Dominus Doctor VITUS WINSHEMIUS a comitibus Oldenburgensibus, ubi dissidij cuiusdam inter eos componendi caussa adfuerat, iam primum reversus; is inter alia de Doctore BŒKELIO rettulit, ipsum urgere pecuniam sibi pro libris, quos accepisti, promissam; a te vero sibi perscriptum aiebat, ne pecuniam illam eidem BŒKELIO traderet, antequam omnes libros residuos depromeret, quod ipsum praestare aut nolle aut non posse intelligo, et praetendere tres vel quatuor saltem restare, eosque non magni momenti, quique in ipsius non sint potestate. Quaesivit igitur WINSHEMIUS a me, quid illi hac in re faciendum existimarem; suasi, ut tuae voluntati morem gereret et secum pecuniam reservaret, donec alter per omnia tuis desiderijs et suae pollicitationi satisfecisset. At ille excipiebat, sibi brevi Berlinum et in Misniam eundum, BŒKELIUM vero tam diu pecunia carere nolle, imo stomachabundum dixisse, quod mallet libros suos recipere, quam diutius pecuniam istam expectare. Statuimus itaque, ut Dominus Doctor VITUS in sua absentia pecuniam a te missam alteri fidedigno viro ex amicis suis Hamburgi traderet, et si interea responsum a te reciperet (nam se tibi hac de re scripsisse innuebat) filius ipsius, qui isthic remanet, literas tuas aperiret, atque secundum earum contenta negotium hoc exequeretur. Tuum itaque est mature, quid in his fieri vis, significare; si tuo loco essem, aut restituerem illi libros, siquidem sic vult, aut concederem illi pecuniam iam antea addictam, utut pauculos illos libros procurare nolit aut forte nequeat, quo possis ab ipso liberari; est enim paulo morosior, et insuper (quod tamen cum multis commune habet) γυναικοκρατούμενος, Imperatrice eius nimis importuna existente. Cuius rei MULÆUS documentum dabit; ego nihilominus, quidquid hac in re fieri velis, quantum in me est, effectum dabo. Currum, quem promisisti ante Pascha, Pentecostes etiam festi, imo et IOHANNIS BAPTISTAe tempora, frustratus est, et licet Cimber sis, privilegioque gentis gaudeas, tamen te praeter caeterorum morem quidpiam Schanicae constantiae habere, praesertim cum per coniugium in Schanum quodammodo transplanteris, existimarem potius, ideoque per incuriam quandam eorum, quibus hoc negotium mandasti, neglectum esse. Sed sit quidquid velit, nolim tibi hac in parte uti etiam nulla alia gravis esse; saltem scire desidero, quid expectandum sit vel secus, ne frustra alium confieri procurem. De aedibus etiam meis Haffniensibus quid fieri possit vel non, a te certior reddi aveo. Iamque cum utraque tua SOPHIA, addita insuper tertia illa quae et URANIA appellatur, quam felicissime vale; saluta fratrem AXILLUM et alios, si quos mei isthic tangat memoria. Deus vos omnes tueatur a malo.

Dabantur Wandesburgo die 23. Iunij Anni 98.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE.

To the man signally adorned as much by eminent learning as by illustrious lineage, Lord Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm, my kinsman, relation, and intimate friend.
Heartiest greeting.

What I lately destined to you, my most longed-for Holger, kinsman and dearest relation, through Claus Mule — whom I sent thither before the Diet — I do not doubt you have now received all. It came to me, after Mule's departure, that in the book on Mechanical Astronomy [the Mechanica], on the page which is G.3, where the invention of Lord Jacob Curtius about the subdivision of a certain quadrant is shown — in the title itself, before this name "Curtio," the letter "I" (or rather "IAC.") is omitted, by which his praenomen would be indicated; and later, in the lowest line of the same title, "GENEROSE ADINVENTA" was printed for "INGENIOSE ADINVENTA." Whether it is corrected in the copy I sent, I do not know; if not, I should wish it yet to be corrected by you with the pen (which is easy in such dense paper), lest the Zoiluses have here too something to abuse. What was done about the Berlin letters, I long to learn from you. If anything in that Diet which concerns me to know has become known to you, signify it — whatever it be — in good time, even before Mule returns (if it can be done).

Three days ago Jacob Rosenkrantz, our [kinsman], was here with me, thinking [to go] hence into the Low Countries; he reported that grave and difficult controversies would be stirred in this Diet, and among other things judged that the case of that "Pepler" would be reviewed and decided. But by what right this can be done, I do not know, since the form of citation was not even presented to me, nor has anything of it become known from my Praefect [the Hven governor] — so that I think it was not even read at Uraniborg, or, if it was, not at the due time or in the due manner. But be this what it will: if right and divine law find place, no danger; if otherwise, nothing new. I think — nay, I have experienced — that if I should propose anything in Denmark as true as the things that are demonstrated Geometrically, those very things could nonetheless be accused of falsehood [falsitatis insimulari] by certain men who wish to preside over justice. What, then, is to be done, when right and truth, on account of private passions and hatred of a person, do not merit a place?

Our said kinsman said besides that, passing through Funen, he found among certain Nobles a copy of the letters I wrote to our Most Serene King [Christian IV] while I was at Rostock, together with the answer to them; and, if I remember rightly, he named here Knud Rud, who he thought had received such things from Carl Brusch. If it is so, I should wish that those who are made partakers of these have the things written back to me explained more rightly than the letter itself pretends — which might perhaps seem plausible to the incautious and ignorant, though the matter stands far otherwise; and it would be most easy for me (as you yourself know) to rebut all the objections there and demonstrate the contrary — but the things I said before obstruct [me], and the authority of those who craftily patched these together now prevails over truth. Patience, then, is needed; and at last the good cause triumphs — if not in Denmark, at least elsewhere. O would that the Virgilian [line] did not soon fit us: "All the gods, by whom this empire had stood, have departed, the sanctuaries and altars left"; likewise that, a little before: "We have been Trojans; Ilium has been, and the vast glory of the Danes." For a kingdom that is to be punished by Heaven is wont to be deprived of sound counsels, and torn into factions by certain men's craft, and, under the pretext of truth and good, to do contrary things covertly. And all such things are wont to precede [a fall] — not a few of which are heard there, if only what is narrated agrees with the truth.

When I had asked the aforesaid Rosenkrantz about the "characterized" herring found in Norway [a herring marked with figures], he could indeed report nothing certain of it; yet he most certainly affirmed that another kind of fish — which the Latins call aculeatum, our people Hornfisk [garfish] — marked with far more wonderful characters, was lately found near the island of Tåsinge (or rather a little [island] adjacent to it); the formation of which figures, if anything is established to you on this matter, I should wish communicated to me. Your Cramer afterward wrote me, and enclosed a certain poem in the letter; Brasch too, the Rostock Poet, sent a Heroic [poem] composed in my honour — but neither pleases enough. You have both here, however: Cramer's in his own hand, with the letter, as I received it (for I have no leisure to copy it) — which, however, since I have no copy of it, I should wish returned to me. I have besides others of the kind, among which is a very prolix one from East Frisia; and more are being struck, here and there, in memory of the matter well done among us. Germany eagerly awaits your response on behalf of its neighbour [Denmark]. I too await those commentaries you know of, in which I may by occasion interweave my own; and when yours are finished, share them quietly with our Krag [Niels Krag], that he too may interpose his opinion, from the history of the transacted matter which he for the greater part knows. A copy of those which Thaddaeus Hagecius wrote me from Prague some weeks ago I sent already to Lord Doctor Nicolaus Krag, who doubtless made you partaker of it. But these days too I have again received some brief [letters] from the same Thaddaeus, of which you have here the copy; he expedites the matter in fewer words, since he doubted whether I was here, or had set out on the journey toward Prague. You see they seriously seek and await my arrival, and that things concerning me have already become known to the Emperor himself. Do you and Krag consult together, and quietly fish out the opinion of others too, to whom my affairs are seriously and sincerely at heart. I can conclude nothing certain before Tengnagel and Mule have returned to me.

Yesterday Lord Doctor Veit Winsheim was with me, just returned from the Counts of Oldenburg (where he had been to settle a certain dissension among them); he reported, among other things, about Doctor Bökel — that he presses for the money promised him for the books you received; but that you had written him not to give Bökel that money before he produced all the remaining books, which I understand he either will not or cannot do, alleging that only three or four remain, of no great moment, and not in his power. Winsheim therefore asked me what I thought he should do in this; I advised that he comply with your will and keep the money by him, until [Bökel] had in all things satisfied your wishes and his own promise. But he objected that he must soon go to Berlin and into Meissen, and that Bökel would not lack the money so long — nay, said stomachfully that he would rather take back his books than wait longer for that money. So we resolved that, in his absence, Lord Doctor Vitus would give the money sent by you to another trustworthy man of his friends at Hamburg; and if meanwhile an answer came from you (for he hinted he had written you of this), his son who remains there would open your letter and execute this business according to its contents. It is for you, then, to signify in good time what you wish done in these. If I were in your place, I would either restore him the books (if he so wishes), or grant him the money already assigned — even though he will not, or perhaps cannot, procure those few books — that you may be freed from him; for he is a bit morose, and besides (which yet he has in common with many) ruled by his wife [γυναικοκρατούμενος], his Empress being too importunate — of which Mule will give proof. I nonetheless will give effect, so far as in me lies, to whatever you wish done in this.

The wagon which you promised before Easter, then Whitsun, nay and the time of St. John the Baptist [24 June], has disappointed [me]; and though you are a Cimbrian [Jutlander], and enjoy your nation's privilege [of slowness], I should rather think you have something of Scanian constancy beyond the custom of the rest — especially since by marriage you are somewhat transplanted into a Scanian — and that therefore it was neglected through some carelessness of those to whom you entrusted this business. But be it what it will: I should not wish to be burdensome to you in this — as in no other thing; I only desire to know what is to be expected, or not, lest I procure another to be made in vain. About my Copenhagen house, too, what can be done or not, I long to be made more certain by you. And now, with both your Sophias — adding besides that third one which is also called Urania — fare most happily. Greet brother Axel, and others, if my memory touch any there. May God guard you all from evil.

Given at Wandsbek, the 23rd day of June, the year [15]98.
Yours, Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Holger Rosenkrantz of Rosenholm — Tycho's kinsman. The earliest of the Wandsbek-to-Holger letters (cf. brahe-t_15980909001, brahe-t_15980925001), on St. John's Eve 1598.
  • Significance — a Mechanica erratum (Jacob Curtius!), Tycho's prophecy of Denmark's fall, prodigies, and the developing Prague prospect.
  • The Mechanica printer's-error (and the Curtius connection). Tycho asks Holger to pen-correct an erratum on page G.3 of the Mechanica, where Jacob Curtius's invention for subdividing a quadrant is credited: the praenomen abbreviation "IAC." was dropped before "Curtio," and "GENEROSE ADINVENTA" was misprinted for "INGENIOSE ADINVENTA." A precise bibliographical datum — and the same Jacob Curtius (Kurz) whose Prague house Tycho would later receive as "the new Uraniborg" (cf. brahe-t_16010414001): here Curtius is honoured in the Mechanica for a quadrant-subdivision device.
  • Danish legal troubles & the prophecy. Tycho's bitterness at Danish justice — the "Pepler" case to be heard at the Diet without due citation; his maxim that in Denmark even geometric truth can be "falsely accused" by enemies. He fears leaked copies of his Rostock letters to Christian IV (via Knud Rud / Carl Brusch) are spun against him. Climax: a dark Virgilian prophecy of Denmark's fall, quoting Aeneid 2 — "All the gods … have departed" and "We have been Trojans … and the vast glory of the Danes" — a kingdom "to be punished by Heaven" losing sound counsel.
  • Prodigies — the "characterized fish." A vivid wonder-report: a herring marked with mysterious figures found in Norway, and a garfish (Hornfisk) "marked with far more wonderful characters" found near Tåsinge — Tycho asks Holger for the shapes of the marks. (A datum for the prodigy/portent interest of the age.)
  • The Prague prospect (June 1598). Already developing: Hagecius writes from Prague (forwarded via Niels Krag); "they seriously seek and await my arrival, and things concerning me are already known to the Emperor." Tycho awaits Tengnagel and Mule before deciding.
  • The literary monument & the Bökel business. Poems from Cramer, Brasch (the Rostock poet), and a "very prolix" East-Frisian one; Tycho awaits Holger's "commentaries" (a defence of Denmark's side) to share with Krag. The Bökel book-debt (cf. brahe-t_15980925001, brahe-t_15980909001) drags on — Bökel "morose and ruled by his wife [γυναικοκρατούμενος]." Plus the perennially-late wagon (Holger's "Jutish privilege" of slowness) and Tycho's Copenhagen house.
  • Cross-references. The Jacob Curtius thread (Mechanica credit ↔ the Prague house, brahe-t_16010414001); the Wandsbek-Holger cluster; the Bökel thread; the Prague-prospect/Hagecius thread; and a prodigy/portent note.
  • Persons & places named. Holger Rosenkrantz; Claus Mule; Jacob Curtius (Kurz); Jacob Rosenkrantz; "Pepler" (an unidentified Danish legal case — flagged); Christian IV; Knud Rud, Carl Brusch; Daniel Cramer; Martin Brasch; Niels Krag; Thaddaeus Hagecius; Veit Winsheim; Johann Bökel; Franz Tengnagel; brother Axel; Holger's wife Sophie + "Urania." Places: Wandsbek, Uraniborg/Hven, Funen, Tåsinge, Norway, Rostock, Prague, Berlin, Hamburg, Copenhagen, East Frisia.
  • Greek/Latin notes. γυναικοκρατούμενος ("ruled by his wife"); falcatis insimulari ("falsely accused," lit. "with sickles" — a textual oddity rendered by sense). The Virgil tags adapt Aen. 2.351–352 and 2.325 (with "ingens gloria Danorum" substituted for "Teucrorum").
  • Calendar. die 23. Iunij Anni 98 at Wandsbek (Holstein O.S.; "St. John the Baptist's time" = 24 June confirms the season) → 23 June 1598 [Old Style] (no stylo marker; label gives no N.S.).
  • Correction. Collated against Dreyer, TBOO VIII p.74: 'falcatis' is corrected to 'falsitatis' ('falfitatis infimulari' in Dreyer's long-s type, l.34), restoring 'accused of falsehood'; the English translation's sense was already correct ('falsely accused'), with only its bracketed citation of the Latin word updated to match.

- text error (low). Dreyer prints: poffe nihilominus ea ipfa apud quofdam, qui iuftitiae praeeffe volunt, falfitatis infimulari (TBOO VIII p.74, l.34).

Repaired. The sole text error was verified directly against the Dreyer page image for TBOO VIII p.74 (within the letter's pp.73-77 extract, pages 1-4). The translation's sense required no change; only the bracketed inline Latin citation was brought into sync with the corrected word. No crux remains.
