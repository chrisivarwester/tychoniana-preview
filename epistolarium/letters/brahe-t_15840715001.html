The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD IOHANNEM MAIOREM.

QUAS ad me ultimo dedisti literas, eruditissime Domine IOHANNES MAIOR, recte accepi et de libris procuratis gratias habeo. WELLERIANOS hac vice non cupio. De Calendario Gregoriano quod tanta fuerit apud vos animorum concertatio, ut ferme ad arma res devenerit, miror, et meo sane iudicio non decuisset eos, qui Theologiam apud vos profitentur, occasionem sic dare his turbis et populum contra Magistratum legitimum commovere, in re, uti opinor, adiaphora et religioni, quam illi tuentur, nihil efficiente. Sed hac tamen salva, quatenus divino verbo fundatur, manente, nonne prioris et diu usitati Calendarij Nicenum Concilium secuti Romani Pontifices autores et conservatores fuêre, et aliorum dierum festorum, quibus ij, qui se Euangelium nuncupant, etiamnum utuntur? Cur igitur Pontifici Romano non liceret, praesertim approbante id Imperatoria Maiestate, idem Calendarium, quatenus mancum et insufficiens longo aevi tractu redditum est, emendare et in melius, quoad fieri potuit, restituere? Certe videntur hi, qui tam contentiose illi obstrepunt, non zelo secundum scientiam id agere, sed potius affectibus indulgere et philautia laborare, atque odio Pontificis saltem id facere. Si ante LUTHERI tempora tali modo fuisset renovatum Calendarium, quando per excellentem Germanorum Mathematicum, IOHANNEM REGIOMONTANUM, Pontifex, qui tum Romae praefuit, id fieri satagebat, certe LUTHERUS huic emendationi non invite acquievisset, cum nihil contineret ipsius doctrinae contrarium. Et cur non successores eius eo, quod nunc redintegratum est, contenti sunt? Videant, ne sic dent occasionem ijs, qui veterem doctrinam se profiteri aiunt, obijciendi, quod nimio contradictionis studio occaecati quidvis impugnent, sive id speciem aliquam dubitationis mereatur, sive non, atque ideo eos in caeteris quoque minus considerate agere praesumatur. Et siquidem de hoc Calendario renovato meam exquiris sententiam, dicam tibi id, quod et alijs eadem sciscitantibus respondi.

Calendarium hoc Gregorianum noviter reformatum praevalet veteri et prius usitato, et recte factum est a Pontifice opera LILII Mathematici, ut 10 dies in eo omissi sint. Totidem enim defectus anni coelestis et aequinoctialis a Iuliano, qui inde a Concilio Niceno direxit, quam proxime requirit. Et alia etiam quaedam non inconvenienter in nupero Calendario emendata sunt. Licet vero aliqua in his adhuc castigatius in eo proponi possint, si coelitus deducti Luminarium motus in consilium adhiberentur, tamen cum hoc non facile detur in tanta exactissimarum observationum penuria, et Ecclesiae ac Politiae usus summam in his praecisionem quam ipsi etiam Astronomi vix assequuntur, non exigat, nec etiam magnopere opus habeat, acquiescendum utique erit huic Pontificis Romani, cuius etiamnum maxima est in terris autoritas, consultae et laudabili provisioni in Calendarij diu frustraque desiderata reformatione, nec temere quid in contrarium movendum cum periculo pacis publicae.

Habes breviter, quid ego sentiam, et forte mecum viri cordati non pauci. Quid vero penitius in hac renovatione praestari possit, dicere non audeo, antequam de exacto motu ☉ et ☾ novis illis et maximis, quae adorno, organis certius quid expertus fuero. Nam coelo inconsulto de ijs, quae inde profluunt, censuram proferre velle temerarium et insulsum esse iudico. Laudo itaque Ampliss. vestrae Reipub. decisionem consultam, quod Calendarium hoc Gregorianum pacis publicae causa et ob vicinarum regionum consuetudinem atque cum ijs commercia susceperit, utut reclamitante incerto vulgo, quod semper studia in contraria scinditur. Atque de his hoc tempore satis, de tuis per occationem, si res ita postulârit, alias plura in medium proferam.

Vale.
Dabantur ex aede nostra Knudstrupiana 15. die Iulij stylo veteri, nobis adhuc usitato, Anno 1584.
T.
TYCHO DE BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Johannes Major.

The letter you last sent me, most learned Master Johannes Major, I have duly received, and I give thanks for the books procured. The Wellerian ones I do not want this time. That there should have been among you so great a clash of feelings over the Gregorian calendar that the matter nearly came to arms, I marvel at; and in my own judgement it ill became those who profess Theology among you to give occasion for these tumults in this way, and to stir up the people against the lawful magistrate, in a matter that, as I think, brings about nothing with respect to matters of religious indifference [adiaphora] and to the religion which they uphold. Yet, this remaining safe so far as it is founded upon the divine word, were not the Roman Pontiffs, following the Council of Nicaea, the authors and preservers of the earlier and long-used calendar, and of the other feast-days that those who call themselves of the Gospel still use even now? Why, then, should it not be permitted to the Roman Pontiff — especially with His Imperial Majesty approving it — to amend that same calendar, in so far as it has been rendered defective and insufficient through the long course of time, and to restore it for the better, as far as could be done? Surely those who so contentiously cry out against him seem to do this not from a zeal that is according to knowledge, but rather to indulge their passions and to labour under self-love [philautia], and to do it at least out of hatred of the Pontiff. Had the calendar been renewed in this manner before Luther's time — when, through the excellent German mathematician Johannes Regiomontanus, the Pontiff who then presided at Rome was busying himself to have it done — surely Luther would not unwillingly have acquiesced in this emendation, since it contained nothing contrary to his own doctrine. And why are his successors not content with what has now been set right again? Let them take care lest they thus give occasion to those who say they profess the old doctrine to object that, blinded by an excessive zeal for contradiction, they assail anything whatever — whether or not it deserves any appearance of doubt — and that therefore they may be presumed to act less considerately in other matters too. And since indeed you ask my opinion about this renewed calendar, I shall tell you what I have answered to others asking the same.

This Gregorian calendar, newly reformed, prevails over the old and formerly used one, and it was rightly done by the Pontiff, through the work of Lilius the mathematician, that 10 days were omitted in it. For just so many does the shortfall of the celestial and equinoctial year from the Julian — which had governed [the reckoning] ever since the Council of Nicaea — most nearly require. And certain other things too have been not unsuitably amended in the recent calendar. But although some things in these matters could still be set forth in it more correctly, if the motions of the Luminaries derived from the heavens themselves were brought into the deliberation, nevertheless, since this is not easily granted amid so great a scarcity of the most exact observations, and since the use of the Church and the State does not demand — nor even greatly need — that highest precision in these matters which even the Astronomers themselves scarcely attain, one must by all means acquiesce in this prudent and praiseworthy provision of the Roman Pontiff — whose authority is still the greatest on earth — in the long and vainly desired reformation of the calendar, and nothing must rashly be set in motion to the contrary, at the peril of the public peace.

You have in brief what I think, and perhaps not a few sensible men think with me. But what might more deeply be accomplished in this renewal, I do not dare to say, before I shall have ascertained something more certain about the exact motion of the Sun [☉] and the Moon [☽] by those new and very great instruments which I am preparing. For to wish to pronounce a verdict, without consulting the heavens, upon the things that flow therefrom, I judge to be rash and foolish. I therefore praise the prudent decision of your most ample commonwealth, in that it has taken up this Gregorian calendar for the sake of the public peace and on account of the custom of neighbouring regions and of commerce with them, however much the unsettled common crowd cries out against it — that crowd which is always split into opposing partialities. And on these matters enough for this time; about your affairs, as occasion offers, if the matter so require, I shall bring forward more another time.

Farewell.
Given from our seat at Knudstrup, the 15th day of July, by the old style, still used by us, in the Year 1584.
T[ycho].
Tycho de Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender / place. Tycho writes from Knudstrup (Knudstrup / Knutstorp), the Brahe family seat in Scania, his birthplace — distinct from Uraniborg on Hven; aede nostra Knudstrupiana = "our seat at Knudstrup."
  • Recipient. Iohannes Maior = Johann Major / Johannes Maior (1533–1600), the Wittenberg humanist, Lutheran theologian and Neo-Latin poet (poeta laureatus), professor at Wittenberg; a learned correspondent who had procured books for Tycho. (To be distinguished from the medieval Scotist Johannes Maior/John Mair, and from other contemporaries of the name.) Identification confidence MEDIUM: the period name Iohannes Maior is shared by several figures; the Wittenberg poet-theologian fits the German Protestant setting of the calendar dispute described as occurring apud vos ("among you"), but the letter itself gives no decisive disambiguator. Flagged rather than asserted.
  • Wellerianos — "the Wellerian ones [books]," i.e. works by or associated with Hieronymus Weller (1499–1572) of Freiberg, the Lutheran theologian and close associate of Luther (or possibly another of the Weller family of theologians); the substantivised adjective refers back to libris ("books"). Rendered "the Wellerian ones." Identification of the precise referent is MEDIUM.
  • The calendar dispute (apud vos … ferme ad arma). The Gregorian reform was promulgated by the bull Inter gravissimas (Feb. 1582); the Protestant Imperial estates refused it, and its attempted introduction provoked violent resistance in several German cities — most famously the Augsburg "calendar riot" (Kalenderstreit) of 1583–84, where confessional conflict over the new calendar brought a city near to armed disorder. Tycho's ut ferme ad arma res devenerit ("that the matter nearly came to arms") reflects this; the specific city is not named in the letter and is not supplied here.
  • anaphora — a technical term of the chronological/computistical reckoning (the Easter cycle / the carrying-forward of the calendrical correction); Tycho's point is that the dispute affects nothing in the substance of religion. Kept and glossed [Easter-]reckoning with the Latin flagged; the precise sense is a minor crux (see Confidence).
  • philautia (φιλαυτία) — "self-love," the vice of inordinate self-regard; a learned Graeco-Latin term, kept and glossed.
  • Persons named. Cyprianus / — none here beyond: Johannes Regiomontanus (Johann Müller of Königsberg, 1436–1476), the German mathematician-astronomer whom Pope Sixtus IV had summoned to Rome c. 1475 in connection with calendar reform (he died there in 1476); Luther (Martin Luther); Aloysius Lilius (Luigi Lilio / Giglio, d. c. 1576), the Calabrian physician-mathematician whose computational scheme underlies the Gregorian reform — opera LILII Mathematici = "through the work of Lilius the mathematician" (confirmed against the reference literature, which names Lilius "the principal architect of the Gregorian calendar"). Imperatoria Maiestate = the Holy Roman Emperor (Rudolf II), whose approval Tycho cites.
  • The ten omitted days. ut 1O dies in eo omissi sint — the source prints "1O" (numeral 1 + capital O) for 10; the ten days dropped in October 1582 to realign the equinox. The Julian year's small excess over the tropical year had, since Nicaea (325), accumulated to about ten days; Tycho's defectus anni coelestis et aequinoctialis a Iuliano … quam proxime requirit states that this accumulated discrepancy "most nearly requires" just that number — i.e. the correction is approximately, not exactly, right (an epistemic hedge preserved: quam proxime, "most nearly," not "exactly").
  • Tycho's reservation. Note the carefully hedged scientific stance: the reform is endorsed for civil and ecclesiastical use and for the public peace, while Tycho reserves judgement on whether it is astronomically optimal until he has determined the exact solar and lunar motions with novis illis et maximis … organis ("those new and very great instruments," the edition's convention) — the Uraniborg programme. coelo inconsulto = "without consulting the heavens." The hedges (non audeo "I dare not"; certius quid expertus fuero "when I shall have ascertained something more certain") are preserved, not sharpened.
  • Symbols. ☉ = Sun, ☽ = Moon (named in the translation, glyphs kept in the Source block, the edition's convention). motu ☉ et ☾ = "the motion of the Sun and the Moon."
  • Ampliss. vestrae Reipub. = Amplissimae vestrae Reipublicae, "your most ample commonwealth/republic" — the civic authority (city council / academic body) that had adopted the new calendar; the Magistratum legitimum ("lawful magistrate") of the first paragraph.
  • restituere / redintegratum / reformatione / renovatione — Tycho's vocabulary of restoration / renewal, here of the calendar; cf. his own programme of instauratio astronomiae. Rendered consistently with "restore / set right again / reformation / renewal."
  • Calendar / dateline. Dabantur … 15. die Iulij stylo veteri, nobis adhuc usitato, Anno 1584 = "Given … the 15th day of July, by the old style, still used by us, in the Year 1584" — Tycho expressly flags his date as Old Style (Julian), the running theme of the letter; 15 July 1584 O.S. The edition label ("1584. 15. juli. Knutstorp") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. Per the edition's convention the date is not converted; the N.S. equivalent would be 25 July 1584, but the source asserts O.S. and so does the header.
  • Subscription. T. / TYCHO DE BRAHE — the abbreviated monogram T. followed by the full signature; both kept.
  • Correction. Latin restored 'apud vos' (Theologiam apud vos profitentur) and corrected 'anaphora' to 'adiaphora', the Lutheran technical term for matters of religious indifference at stake in the Gregorian calendar dispute (Dreyer VII p.86); English correspondingly restored 'among you' and replaced the invented gloss 'Easter-reckoning' with 'matters of religious indifference [adiaphora]'. Latin numeral '1O' corrected to '10' (Dreyer VII p.87).

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VII pp. 86–87; defects the audit noted: text error (4), mistranslation (1) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Collated in full against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.86-88 (all 3 pages read as page images). The letter occupies the lower half of p.86 through p.87, preceded by the tail of Henricus Brucaeus's letter to Tycho and followed by a second Brucaeus letter, both correctly excluded. Full Latin text collated: the Wellerian-books remark, the Gregorian calendar controversy, Regiomontanus and Luther, Lilius's 10-day correction, the sun/moon-instrument caveat, and the closing (Knudstrup, 15 July 1584 O.S.) all check against Dreyer. Two text error found in the transcribed original (a dropped 'apud vos'; 'adiaphora' corrupted to 'anaphora'), the second of which the translation compounds into an invented, unsupported gloss ('Easter-reckoning') that misrepresents the specific theological term central to the passage - this is what drives the serious verdict despite the letter being published at grade Secure.

4 defects found:
- text error (low). Source_text silently drops 'apud vos' (among you) from Dreyer's text; the English translation follows the truncated source and likewise omits 'among you'. Dreyer prints: non decuisset eos, qui Theologiam apud vos profitentur.
- text error (medium). Source_text corrupts Dreyer's 'adiaphora' (the Lutheran theological/legal term for matters of religious indifference - precisely the concept at stake in the Gregorian-calendar adiaphora controversy) into 'anaphora', a different word (rhetorical repetition / Eucharistic prayer). Dreyer prints: in re, uti opinor, adiaphora et religioni, quam illi tuentur, nihil efficiente.
- mistranslation (high). Working from the corrupted 'anaphora', the translator invents an unsupported interpretive gloss ('Easter-reckoning') matching neither Dreyer's actual word 'adiaphora' (matters of religious indifference) nor any plausible sense of 'anaphora'. A scholar would come away with a false idea of the specific theological concept Tycho is invoking in a letter about the Gregorian calendar dispute. At: “the [Easter-]reckoning [anaphora]”.
- text error (low). Source_text renders the numeral as digit-plus-letter '1O' rather than '10'; likely a process rendering artifact, but a literal departure from Dreyer's printed numeral. Translation correctly reads '10 days'. Dreyer prints: ut 10 dies in [eo] omissi sint.

Repaired. Collated all four defects against the Dreyer page images (TBOO VII pp.86-87). Page 86 confirms 'Theologiam apud vos profitentur' and 'in re, uti opinor, adiaphora et religioni'; page 87 confirms the numeral '10'. All four defects fixed with high confidence; no crux remains.
