The original

DAVID CHYTRÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

NOBILIS, generose et magnifice Domine. Gratulor non tam Magnificentiæ vestræ, quam Academiæ et urbi Rosarum, de incolumi in hanc urbem adventu vestro, quam vere Generosæ Nobilitatis vestræ sapientia, virtus, doctrina et in Rempublicam literariam merita et Nominis vestri claritas, splendor ac gloria, excellenti virtute ac bene meritis parta, deinceps illustriorem et clariorem efficiet. Quod igitur CICERO alicubi in Orationibus exclamat: O terram illam beatam, quæ hunc virum exceperit! patriam ingratam, si eiecerit, miseram, si amiserit! idem ego ijsdem verbis nunc usurpo, et Deum æternum, suæ sapientiæ et virtutis radios, ingenij et virtutis Heroicæ dona in mentes præstantium virorum effundentem, precor, ut Μ. V. incolumem et florentem perpetuo servet, et migrationem in hanc urbem clementer gubernet, ut paulo post eos, qui talem virum dimiserunt, erroris sui pœniteat, et quem retinere omnibus viribus non studuerunt, retrahere tandem ad se omni contentione enitantur. His coram Μ. V. ad nos advenienti gratulaturus eram, si accurrere ad M.V. per Arthritidem, quæ me totum possedit, licuisset. Nunc, quæ proficisci a mea tenuitate possunt officiola, reverenter defero, et pro eruditissimis ad me literis et volumine epistolarum, mecum superioribus mensibus communicato, reverenter gratias ago.

Bene et feliciter Μ. V. in æternum valeat.
Postridie Viti, Anno 1597.
Μ. V. reverenter colens
DAVID CHYTRÆUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

DAVID CHYTRAEUS TO TYCHO BRAHE.

Noble, well-born and magnificent Master. I offer my congratulations not so much to Your Magnificence as to the University and the city of the Roses [Rostock], upon your safe arrival in this city — [an arrival] which the wisdom, virtue, and learning of your truly well-born Nobility, and your deserts toward the republic of letters, and the renown, splendour, and glory of your name, won by [your] excellent virtue and good deserts, will hereafter render the more illustrious and the more famous. That, therefore, which CICERO somewhere cries out in his Orations — "O blessed [is] that land which shall have received this man! [O] ungrateful the homeland, if it cast him out; wretched, if it lose him!" — I now take up in the very same words; and I pray the eternal God, who pours out the rays of His wisdom and virtue, the gifts of genius and of heroic virtue, into the minds of outstanding men, that He may keep Your Magnificence safe and flourishing for ever, and graciously govern your removal into this city, so that a little later those who let such a man go may repent of their error, and may at last strive with all their effort to draw back to themselves the man whom they did not endeavour to keep with all their might. I would have offered Your Magnificence these [congratulations] in person, as you arrived among us, had it been permitted me to hasten to Your Magnificence — [hindered] by the arthritis [gout] which has wholly possessed me. Now I reverently tender such small services as can proceed from my slenderness [of means], and I reverently give thanks for your most learned letter to me and for the volume of letters shared with me in the past months.

May Your Magnificence fare well and happily for ever.
The day after [the feast of] St. Vitus, the year 1597.
Reverently honouring Your Magnificence,
DAVID CHYTRAEUS.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. David Chytraeus = David Chytraeus (Kochhafe, 1530–1600), the eminent Lutheran theologian and historian, professor at the University of Rostock and one of its dominant figures; a Melanchthonian, author of the Chronicon Saxoniae. He had been in correspondence with Tycho and is recorded by Dreyer as having written this friendly note on 16 June 1597 regretting that his health prevented him from paying his respects in person.
  • Occasion. The letter congratulates Tycho on his arrival at Rostock in June 1597, at the outset of his self-imposed exile from Denmark. The barbed passage about "those who let such a man go" / refused "to keep [him] with all their might" alludes, by transparent implication, to the Danish authorities whose withdrawal of support drove Tycho abroad; Chytraeus predicts they will come to regret it. The hedge is the author's own — he speaks of those who let such a man go without naming them — and is preserved rather than sharpened into a direct accusation against the Danish crown.
  • urbs Rosarum — "the city of the Roses," a learned epithet for Rostock (playing on Rostochium / the rose in the city arms); rendered "the city of the Roses [Rostock]." Academia here = the University of Rostock.
  • Μ. V. — the abbreviation throughout (printed with a Greek capital mu, Μ, for Latin M) = Magnificentia Vestra, "Your Magnificence" — the standard honorific for a person of Tycho's rank; the Greek-letter mu is a compositor's substitution for the Latin majuscule and carries no separate sense. Rendered consistently "Your Magnificence."
  • Cicero allusion. O terram illam beatam … miseram, si amiserit! — Chytraeus quotes (acknowledging it as Cicero, in Orationibus) the apostrophe from Cicero, De lege agraria (Second Oration) II.xxxv.95, on Capua, here redeployed to Tycho and Denmark. The quotation is kept as a quotation and set off accordingly.
  • Arthritis — period term for gout / arthritic joint-disease ("which has wholly possessed me"); glossed "the arthritis [gout]." Not silently modernised.
  • officiola — diminutive of officia, "small [dutiful] services / little kindnesses"; the self-deprecating diminutive (paired with mea tenuitate, "my slenderness [of means/ability]") is preserved.
  • volumine epistolarum … superioribus mensibus communicato — "the volume of letters shared with me in the past months." Almost certainly Tycho's Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I, Uraniborg 1596), which he was then circulating to learned correspondents; identification likely but not stated in the letter, so left as the text gives it.
  • Negations. Two negations occur and are reproduced as exactly two: (1) non tam … quam — the idiomatic "not so much … as" correlative (no true denial, a comparative emphasis), and (2) quem retinere omnibus viribus non studuerunt — "the man whom they did not endeavour to keep with all their might." Neither is dropped or added.
  • Dateline. Postridie Viti, Anno 1597 = "the day after [the feast of] St. Vitus." St. Vitus's day is 15 June, so postridie Viti = 16 June 1597 (O.S.; Denmark and Lutheran Germany were still on the Julian calendar). This agrees with the edition label ("1597. 16. juni") and with Dreyer's dating; no discrepancy. The place is not named in the dateline but is Rostock, established by the body (in hanc urbem … urbi Rosarum) and by Dreyer — hence supplied in brackets in the header.
