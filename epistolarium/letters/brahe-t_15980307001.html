English translation machine translation, Secure

Jakob Monau to Tycho Brahe. Greeting.

In good faith indeed, but a little too late, was that packet delivered to me and came into my hands which you sent me toward the end of last year, most noble and most excellent Master Tycho; and I owe great thanks to that best of men and most upright friend Master Vincent Moller, in that, in seeing to its delivery, he was willing to do a kindness both to you and to me. I can scarcely express in words how welcome your letter was to me, written in so friendly a way, and how acceptable were those things which you wished to reach me together with your letter. As for my sending so late a reply, it comes about not only for the reason I have mentioned, but also because of my ill-health, which for nearly three whole months I felt to be wholly[A] troublesome, and which made[A] me, as it were, unfit for transacting any business. I am indeed not yet wholly[A] freed of it. Yet nevertheless, by the grace of God, I have begun to be better, and I feel the powers both of mind and of body not a little stronger than they were. God, Best and Greatest, if he will, can increase this strength; and I desire it to come about only for the glory of his holy name, and for the salvation of my soul, which is my chief care in these lands. As for the report of my death carried to you there — whence you were given occasion to make mention of my name in your letters — this error could easily be committed at the Emperor's court, where I had an only brother, an Imperial Physician, a man whom I would rather have praised by others than by me, who[A] died at Prague ten years ago. But because this error drew[A] from you such honourable and friendly words about me, that from them one may estimate how greatly you would have honoured the memory of my name after my death: I acknowledge that I owe very much to your love and goodwill toward me, which I earnestly beg that[A] you keep for me as long as I shall live; and I in turn, so long as there is blood in this body, shall not cease to love, esteem, and honour you in turn … to proclaim and to praise you. That dearest brother of mine, of whom I have just made mention, was both a most devoted and a most understanding student of mathematical matters, and on that account uniquely respectful of you too and devoted to you; and [I know][A] that he often did this, that he might gain your friendship — from which care he would not have refrained, had God granted him a longer span of life. Now he philosophizes in another School, beholding God face to face, and contemplating the heavens, the work of his hands …… [out of] love toward my brother, [it is] if I have wished in this place to commend his memory to you. For the rest, as touching your departure from your homeland, and especially from Uraniborg: I was first made more certain of it by a letter from our common friend Master David …… for afterward I was more fully informed of all those circumstances by Christian Sørensen Longomontanus, who came to us toward the end of last year ……, and whom we kept with us for some days and held a most welcome guest; and would that your letter had come to me then, while he was still tarrying with us. For he was very anxious about you, and chiefly on this account. As for me, what indeed should I say of these things? When I was once at Magdeburg, I saw inscribed in golden letters on a stone, on a certain house, which was above the door, this verse — silly in its sense, but in fact only too true: It is, it will be, and it has been — Ingratitude at the end of labours [Vndanck in fine laborum]. This little verse comes to my mind again and again, whenever I have thought about you and your affairs. And this too occurred to me: what if this world is not worthy of those Atlantean goods and gifts of our Tycho? This too I thought: what if this Chance [Τύχη], which has befallen Master Tycho, announces that the end of the world is at hand? However it be, I am still very much in doubt whether I ought to grieve more for your lot [or rather for your homeland's][A] — nay, for that of the whole Christian Commonwealth. I have diligently read what you sent me, and I give you great thanks for sharing them; nor is it as you might think — that they will come into the hands of others. I marvelled that the King was willing to confer nothing upon these studies, most worthy of kings and of their subsidies and [help][A], who yet lately made[A] such great expenditures at the festival of his coronation. Other was the judgment of Alfonso; nay, other too was that of his own father Frederick. I will say no more of these matters; on which, however, I add this also, that I do the same as you do — that is, to distinguish the Actor from the Poet of the play[A]. You too do this admirably, in that with so great a spirit (worthy indeed of heaven and the stars) you bear these things that have befallen you, nor on that account do you think to leave off anything in that laudable course [begun] by you …… and once instituted and kept for so many years; but you think [to pursue] it right to the goal …… God grant here that you complete what you have begun. Lazarus ……, a great-spirited man, was wont frequently to have on his lips that verse: If you hold to willing, you hold something; and you will hold to being able [Esse aliquid si velle tenes, et posse tenebis]. But to you it is given both to will and to be able. The glory of that most distinguished man Master Heinrich Rantzau will now be the greater, and his illustrious name will become more renowned, now that he founds[A] a lodging for your Muses. For the neighbouring city of Hamburg will be opportune enough for you, for completing those things whose publication was begun on Hven. About your designs, which you deposit with my …… [friend], I have thought diligently, and I hope they can be so managed that they will [not][A] altogether lack success — but there will be need of time: since now all things that are transacted at the Imperial court [look] only to[wards] …… Mars, in this most grave peril of the Christian Commonwealth, which this year, not without cause (God avert all this!), is feared. Meanwhile I shall do what can now be done through friends, so that a way may, as it were, be paved for those things which are next …… to be handled by care and effort; I shall also make you more certain about all matters which make an argument toward those things which are truly [matters] of your own spirit, counsel, and purpose ……[A]. If Master Jacobus Curtius were still living, how greatly he would show that your affairs and the public business were dear and a care to him! But still I hope that friends at court will not be lacking in other ways too, about whom, at another time, if God will, I shall advise you once the matters have been more fully explored. For the book of Letters sent to me, which is from you a most ample gift, I give great thanks, and I greatly ask that, when your remaining works, which are now begun, have been brought to an end, you not refuse to adorn with them my …… [name] also. Pains will be taken that you may not, by right and deservedly, repent of this …… and kindness. Your letters to Johann Rothmann, whom I have learned now leads his life in the Republic of Erfurt, I will[A] see to through trusty men; if[A] you ask anything else of me to be done, my faithful service will always be ready for you to the best of my powers. From your Christian I also gladly learned this — namely, that you were born in the same year and the same month in which I was brought into the light; I was also greatly delighted, among …… the things you have now shared with me, by the reading of your poems to …… which I did not read without various perturbations of mind. For I thought[A] much about many things, both yours and those that are like them. I will gratify[A] you in what you ask, and I will see to it that no one unworthy of you …… about your affairs which are worthy of you. You in turn, if it is not troublesome, embellish my Symbolum with some Epigram. What my Symbolum is, you will learn from our Vincent Moller, who, if you wish, will give you to read a certain little book of ours, which concerning that same Symbolum I had …… published. I had indeed sent you a copy, which I grieve has perished. If you should ask for another, I will not unwillingly share it. Meanwhile, keep for yourself this inscription …… of mine. Our most excellent old man Doctor Hermann greets you reverently, lovingly, and dutifully, to whom you would scarcely believe how welcome was the greeting spoken by you, and in the name of that most excellent old man[A] …… If it is not troublesome, I ask that in my words too you reverently and dutifully greet the Illustrious Master Heinrich Rantzau, who has declared his goodwill toward me once and again. You, if it please, will increase and confirm it. And since mention has been made of your[A] Hemmingius, and you can now write more freely about him, I ask you, by our friendship, tell me in my own ears whether this enclosed little page deserves credence, which some years ago was circulated. God knows that I ask this neither out of ill-will, nor toward bad faith …… for I love and revere that best of men, who has so well deserved — an old man whom one ought to love and revere when one can; but only so that the history of the matter, of which I and others always [had] a free judgment by right, might begin to be clear to me. Fare well and happily, most excellent and most noble man, and keep for me your …… goodwill, whose first foundations — and those then very slight …… — were [laid] many years ago …… when, returned from your travels, you were living for some weeks in the house and at the table of my then host, Master Lauritz Dan. I shall never cease to love and esteem you, and to [pledge] my faith to you by every kind of means I can ……

At Wrocław,[A] 7 March, at the time of an eclipse of the Sun, 1598.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iacobus Monavius = Jakob Monau (1546–1603), the Wrocław (Breslau) humanist, poet, and Calvinist patron of letters, celebrated for his personal device (symbolum) "Pietas contenta lucratur," around which he gathered a famous album of contributed epigrams from European scholars — hence his request below that Tycho contribute one. Letter from Tycho's exile period: after losing Hven and royal support, Tycho had left Denmark (mid-1597) and was in 1598 a guest of Heinrich Rantzau in Holstein, working toward Hamburg-area publication.
  • Dateline / calendar. Vratislaviae VII. Martij … 1598 = "At Wrocław (Breslau, Vratislavia), 7 March 1598." The edition labels this n.s. (New Style); Monau, in Habsburg Silesia, used the Gregorian calendar. Header carries the as-written date with [N.S.]. "tempore eclipseos Solis" ("at the time of a solar eclipse") is an astronomical timestamp, not a separate date: a partial solar eclipse fell on 7 March 1598 N.S. — the dateline is internally consistent and is not converted here, by the edition's convention
  • MS witness / state of the text. The sole witness is a later transcript (Afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium (Copenhagen, Rigsarkivet). The copy is defective: Dreyer's printed text is riddled with lacunae (each..... marks lost or illegible words) and with superscript A markers keying his textual notes — chiefly editorial restorations of damaged words. Bracketed words in the source ([ante], [scio], [non], [an patriae potius tuae], [adiumento]) are Dreyer's conjectural restorations, reproduced as such; the superscript A's and the..... lacunae are preserved verbatim in the Source block and carried as Secure-marks and ellipses in the translation rather than silently smoothed. The translation's own bracketed words supply English syntax across the lacunae and are flagged as editorial.
  • Persons named.
  • Vincentio Mollero = Vincent(ius) Moller of Hamburg, the intermediary forwarding the packet (corroborated by Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, citing TBOO VIII 30.20–22).
  • frater … unicus Medicus Imperatorius … obijt Pragae — Monau's brother Peter Monau (Petrus Monavius, 1551–1588), personal physician to Emperor Rudolf II, who died at Prague; "ten years ago" fits a 1598 letter. The false rumour of Jakob's death (which Tycho had repeated in his published Epistolae) arose by confusion with the brother's real death.
  • David — "common friend Master David," unidentified here (name stands without further specification in the defective copy); not over-identified.
  • Christiano Severino Longomontano / Christiano tuo = Christian Sørensen Longomontanus (1562–1647), Tycho's chief assistant, who had visited Monau late in 1597.
  • Henrici Ranzovij / Henricum Ranzovium = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), royal Statthalter (viceroy) in Holstein, who gave Tycho hospitality and a base near Hamburg in his exile (corroborated by On Tycho's Island).
  • Iacobus Curtius = Jacob Kurz von Senftenau (Jacobus Curtius), Imperial vice-chancellor and a patron of the sciences (d. 1594); Monau wishes him still alive to advance Tycho's cause at court.
  • Iohannem Rothmannum = Johann Rothmann, then resident in Erfurt (the Republica Erfurdensis); recipient of a letter Monau will forward. (Distinct from Tycho's earlier correspondent Christoph Rothmann of Kassel.)
  • Hermannus noster — the "old Doctor Hermann," a Wrocław associate of Monau's, not securely identifiable from the damaged text; left unidentified.
  • Hemmingij vestri — almost certainly Niels Hemmingsen (Nicolaus Hemmingius, 1513–1600), the Danish theologian ("your Hemmingius" — i.e. the Dane); the "enclosed little page … circulated some years ago" concerns a contested report about him that Monau asks Tycho to authenticate. Identification is probable but rests partly on the damaged context; flagged.
  • M. Laurentij Dani = Master Lauritz (Laurentius) Dan, Monau's former host with whom the young Tycho lodged for some weeks on returning from his travels.
  • The King / Alfonso / Frederick. Regem … nihil voluisse conferre … qui tamen nuper tantos sumptus fecit in coronationis festo — Christian IV of Denmark (crowned 1596, hence "coronation festival") withdrew support; iudicium Alfonsi invokes Alfonso X "the Wise" of Castile, the royal patron of the Alfonsine Tables, as the model of a king who funded astronomy; ipsius patris Friderici = Frederik II, Tycho's late patron. The contrast (lavish coronation vs. nothing for science) is Monau's, kept unsoftened.
  • Greek. Τύχη — "Chance / Fortune"; a pun on Tycho's name, exactly as in the Wolf letter's εὐτυχέστατε Τυχών. "haec Τύχη, quae Domino TYCHONI contigit" = "this Chance [Τύχη] which has befallen Master Tycho" — wordplay on Τύχη / Tycho preserved (transcribed + glossed), not flattened.
  • The Magdeburg verse (German tag). "Est erit atque fuit — Vndanck in fine laborum": a macaronic Latin-German house-inscription, "It is, it will be, and it has been — ingratitude at the end of labours." Vndanck (German Undank, "ingratitude / thanklessness") is kept in the Source-block spelling and glossed; the line is kept as the verse Monau quotes, applied as bitter comment on the world's treatment of Tycho.
  • The two maxims, kept as verse.
  • "Esse aliquid si velle tenes, et posse tenebis" — "If you hold to willing, you hold something; and you will hold to being able," attributed to "Lazarus … a great-spirited man." The name is immediately followed by a lacuna; probably Lazarus von Schwendi (1522–1583, Imperial general and statesman), but the loss prevents certainty — left as "Lazarus …" and flagged, not asserted.
  • The Atlas image ("istis Atlanticis bonis et donis", "those Atlantean goods and gifts") casts Tycho as an Atlas bearing the heavens; kept as metaphor.
  • Technical / programmatic terms. Symbolum — Monau's personal device/emblem (the kernel of his epigram album); kept untranslated and italicised on first use, per the keep-rule for fixed humanist terms. libro Epistolarum = Tycho's printed Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596), the "book of Letters" Tycho had gifted. "ea, quorum editio fuit coepta in Huenna" = the works whose printing had begun on Hven (Uraniborg) and which Tycho hoped to finish near Hamburg. "discernere Actorem a Poëta fabulae" — the Stoic-theatrical commonplace of distinguishing the Actor (who merely plays the assigned part) from the Poet/Author of the play (who assigns it): God writes the drama, man acts his role; kept literal.
  • Epistolary formulae. Opening S. = salutem [dicit], "greeting". coram Deum cernens of the dead brother — "beholding God face to face." Closing Bene et feliciter vale = "Fare well and happily".
  • Negation check. The two operative negations are reproduced exactly: (1) the King "nihil voluisse conferre" — "was willing to confer nothing," and (2) Dreyer's restored "ut [non] plane careat successu" — "that they will not altogether lack success" (i.e. that the designs may yet partly succeed). The [non] is editorial (bracketed in both Source and translation); without it the clause would assert the opposite, so the bracket is load-bearing and is flagged as Dreyer's restoration, not silently absorbed.
