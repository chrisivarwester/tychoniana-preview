The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM.

S.

NOBILISSIME et Magnifice vir, Domine et amice honorande: Accepi hac ipsâ horâ literas a meo domestico DANIELE FELS, qui isthic apud vos in Aulâ moratur, quibus iussu tuae Magae scribit, Caesaream Matem Clementissime cupere, ut per primam oportunitatem iudicium meum de hoc Anno, praesertim quoad morbos Epidemicos, brevibus et succincte comprehensum eius Mati per primam oportunitatem transmittam. Etsi vero Astrologis praedictionibus indulgere non soleam, quod eam certitudinem non spondeant, quam requiro, et quam Astronomia, quae motiones siderum solummodo scrutatur, admittit (quam etiam ob id potius excolo) et praeter haec generales illae Mundi influentiae non saltem a superioribus Astris, sed et inferioribus caussis atque Elementari Naturâ dependeant (quod et antea Tuae Magae me innuisse memini) adeo ut qui has citra omnem hallucinationem rite praeviderint, vel nulli vel admodum pauci hactenus extiterint, qui vero particulares et individua concernentes rectius vaticinati sint, plures inventi: Attamen quia Sa Caesarea Maiestas, Dominus noster Clementissimus, sic a me fieri vult, cuius mandatis lubens et subiectissime (uti decet) obtempero, quam primum fieri poterit, meas hac de re coniecturas ex Astrologiâ qualicunque petitas paucis consignabo, atque ipsius Mati ad tuam Magam meo nomine humillime exhibendas dirigam, utut libris meis tam manuscriptis quam alijs huc conducentibus, quod Magdaeburgi apud reliquam supellectilem detineantur, etiamnum destituar. Alias iamdudum perfecissem id, quod ex mandato Caesae Matis Tua Maga eo ipso die, quo Clementissime audientiam obtinueram, expetijt; quod quale sit, ipsemet novit.

Porro Magam tuam caelare non possum (quod et alias ad ipsam hodie perscribere in animum induxeram) filium meum natu maiorem, quem cum alio quodam ex familiaribus meis ante sedecim hebdomadas in Daniam pro auferendis residuis isthic Instrumentis ablegâram, nunc primum huc reversum esse, re omni bene expeditâ. Instrumenta enim quatuor, quae ob magnitudinem discedens ibi reliqui, unâ cum quibusdam alijs, primum Lubecam, deinde ad Albis ostia Hamburgum, ubi nunc conservantur, sarcta tecta transferri fecit, contradicente isthic in Patriâ nemine: licet eo ipso tempore, quo Rex Comitia extraordinarie cum toto Regni Senatu et potissimâ Nobilitate celebravit, Haffniam appulerit: sed omnibus potius cordatis ac ingenuis unâ cum consanguineis et Amicis nostris de Caesae Matis Clementissimo erga me favore atque horum studiorum promotione mihi affatim congratulantibus, paucis tantum invidis et malevolis dolorem dissimulantibus ac atque obmutescentibus. Quae pariter indicanda duxi, quo per oportunitatem ipsius Mati a tuâ Magâ Instrumenta mea omnia, tam ea, quae prius evehi curâram, quam quae in Daniâ reliqui, nunc esse in fundo Germanico, atque instante vere, favente Deo, huc per Albim non incommode advehi posse, referri queat.

Coeterum ut dictum DANIELEM sollicitatorem isthic meum, qui iam sex ferme hebdomadibus abest, quamprimum per graviores occupationes licuerit, expedire et huc remittere non degravetur, officiose rogo.

Valeat Maga tua quam felicissime et diutissime.
Raptim Benachiâ die 20 Ianuarij 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

TYCHO BRAHE TO AN ANONYMOUS [PERSON].

Greeting.

Most noble and magnificent Man, Lord and friend to be honoured: I have received this very hour a letter from my servant Daniel Fels, who stays there with you at the court, in which, by Your Magnificence's order, he writes that the Imperial Majesty most graciously desires that, at the first opportunity, I transmit to His Majesty my judgement about this Year — especially as regards epidemic diseases — comprised briefly and succinctly. Now, although I am not accustomed to indulge in astrological predictions — because they do not promise that certainty which I require, and which Astronomy, that examines only the motions of the stars, does allow (which for that reason I rather cultivate); and because, besides, those general influences of the World depend not only on the higher Stars, but also on lower causes and on Elementary Nature (which I remember having hinted to Your Magnificence before); so that of those who have rightly foreseen these [general influences] without any delusion there have hitherto been either none, or very few, while of those who have prophesied more rightly concerning particulars and individual matters, more have been found — nevertheless, because the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, wishes it so done by me, whose commands I willingly and most submissively (as is fitting) obey, as soon as it can be done I shall set down in a few words my conjectures on this matter, drawn from astrology of whatever sort, and shall direct them to be most humbly laid before His Majesty, in my name, through Your Magnificence — even though I am still deprived of my books, both manuscript and others bearing on this, since they are detained at Magdeburg with the rest of my furniture. Otherwise I should long ago have completed that which, by the Imperial Majesty's mandate, Your Magnificence requested on the very day on which I had most graciously obtained an audience; what it is, you yourself know.

Furthermore, I cannot conceal from Your Magnificence (which I had also resolved to write to you fully today, on another matter) that my elder son — whom, with another of my household, I had sent into Denmark sixteen weeks ago to bring away the instruments remaining there — has now at last returned hither, every matter well dispatched. For the four instruments which, on account of their size, I had left there on departing, together with certain others, he had transferred safe and sound first to Lübeck, then to Hamburg at the mouths of the Elbe, where they are now kept, with no one in the fatherland gainsaying — although he arrived at Copenhagen at the very time when the King was holding an extraordinary Diet with the whole Senate of the Realm and the chief Nobility; rather, all the wise and well-born, together with our kinsmen and friends, congratulated me abundantly on the Imperial Majesty's most clement favour toward me and the promotion of these studies, only a few envious and malevolent ones dissembling their chagrin and falling silent. These things I thought should likewise be indicated, so that, at an opportunity, it may be reported to His Majesty by Your Magnificence that all my instruments — both those I had earlier had carried out, and those I left in Denmark — are now on German soil, and that, with spring approaching, God favouring, they can be conveyed hither up the Elbe without difficulty.

For the rest, I dutifully ask that you would not find it burdensome — as soon as more serious occupations permit — to dispatch and send back hither the said Daniel, my agent there, who has now been away nearly six weeks.

May Your Magnificence fare most happily and most long.
In haste, at Benátky, the 20th of January 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient (unnamed; flagged). Headed Ad Anonymum — an unnamed senior court official (Magnificentia Tua) at the imperial court, under whom Tycho's agent Daniel Fels operated and through whom Tycho's communications reached the Emperor. On the evidence (Fels writes "by your order"; the official conveys Rudolf II's wishes and will lay Tycho's prognostication before him) the most likely identification is Johann Anton Barvitius, the imperial secretary who handled exactly this traffic (cf. brahe-t_16000106001), though Coraduz (the Vice-Chancellor) is also possible. Not asserted.
  • The imperial request — a prognostication for 1600. Via Fels, the official relays that Rudolf II wants Tycho's astrological judgement on the year 1600, especially as to epidemic diseases (the plague of 1599 was barely past). The request itself is a window onto the Emperor's appetite for astrology.
  • Tycho on astrology vs astronomy (the doctrinally important passage). Tycho states his reservations plainly: he does not normally indulge astrological prediction, because it lacks "that certainty which I require, and which Astronomy — examining only the motions of the stars — does allow" (hence his preference for astronomy); the general "influences of the world" depend "not only on the higher Stars but also on lower causes and Elementary Nature"; and so general forecasts have almost never been got right "without delusion," whereas forecasts of particulars have more often hit the mark. Yet he will obey the Emperor and supply conjectures "drawn from astrology of whatever sort" — hampered, he notes, by his books being detained at Magdeburg with his furniture. A characteristic, qualified Tychonic statement on the limits of judicial astrology.
  • The return of the four great instruments from Denmark. Tycho reports that his elder son (Tycho the Younger; cf. brahe-t_16000126001/002), sent to Denmark sixteen weeks earlier, has returned, having moved the four large instruments left behind (with other goods) safe first to Lübeck, then to Hamburg at the Elbe mouth, "with no one in the fatherland gainsaying" — even though he reached Copenhagen during an extraordinary Diet (the King with the whole Rigsraad and chief nobility). He stresses the warm reception (kinsmen and the "wise and well-born" congratulating him on the imperial favour), "only a few envious and malevolent ones … falling silent." The official is to report to the Emperor that all Tycho's instruments are now on German soil and can come up the Elbe in spring. (Dovetails with the Hamburg-transit letter, brahe-t_15990929001, and the Jöstel/Wittenberg-college letters, brahe-t_16001008001/16001009001.)
  • Persons named. Daniel Fels (Tycho's court agent/sollicitator); the elder son (Tycho the Younger); the King (Christian IV) and the Danish Diet; Rudolf II.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 20 Ianuarij 1600 at Benátky; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Latin: 'ipidemicos' corrected to 'Epidemicos', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p.240 (translation unaffected — already correctly rendered 'epidemic diseases').
