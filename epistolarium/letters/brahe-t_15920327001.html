The original

CLARISS. ET NOBILISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, WENÆ INSULÆ DOMINO REGIS DANIÆ CONSILIARIO ET MATHEMATICO DOCTISSIMO, AMICO SUO.

APOLOGIAM tuam Clariss. D. TYCHO, ut accepi, obiter percurri, nam prolixitas tua et meæ occupationes accuratius legere non permiserunt, et primo eius argumento, quod aliquo modo munieras, statim respondi hoc Naturæ motu, quo Sublunaria maximi circuli tramitem tenere possunt, exposito, idque veritatis caussa, ne pluribus hac de re opus esset; in reliquis tum nec nostra quicquam infirmare, nec tua magis confirmare visus es: responsum D. FULLERO nostro datum est, qui in una navium erat, quæ ad Regem nostrum tum in Dania profectæ sunt; sed illis, audito Regis inde discessu ex medio itinere redeuntibus, redditum est, nec remittendum putavi, nisi Mathematicis tuis, quæ pervertere dicebamur, similiter excussis. Verum tot nos ab eo tempore obruerunt negocia et profectiones, ut vestrates hîc vix semel atque iterum convenire licuerit, nec ante hyemem tua relegere potuerim, nonnihil otij tum nactus, quod breve fuit, et controversia de veneficarum nugis sæpe interruptum, hæc Elenctica meditatus sum, et scripsi non in te solum, quem amicitiæ causa lenius tractavi, sed illos quoque, qui meo tempore hunc errorem in Germania publicarunt, contra quos theses quasdam, tredecim aut pluribus abhinc annis scriptas, inter chartas reperi; deerant tantum ipsorum observata, quæ ex Germania expectaveram, ex quibus plenius redarguendi erant: Interea literæ tuæ cum observatione cuiusdam obscurioris Phænomeni sub superioris veris finem ad me perlatæ sunt, ex quibus intellexi responsum ad tua et expeti et expectari abs te, itaque ne te diutius remorarer, nostra in hanc antilogicam ad te formam statim redegi et eo die absolvi, quo Eclipsin ☉lis superiori anno conspexeram, et paucis post diebus hoc exemplum rescribi curavi, ut ad te mitti potuerat cum legato vestro, qui tum hîc erat; sed me ad biduum tantum absente prius discesserat, quam speraveram, et redeunti mihi nulla alia opportunitas ad te scribendi vel mittendi restabat, ante autumnalem navigationem: Interea dum in hac vicinia cum ægris versor, unde ad quamcunque oblatam occasionem redire facile poteram, præter opinionem in Angliam vocor longissimo itinere, nec prius domum redire concessum fuit, nec inde iterum discedere ante summam hyemem licuerat. Expectanda enim erat gravissimi circa hepar Apostematis suppuratio et expurgatio in amicissimo quodam nobili: post Martini festum et prope circa solstitium hybernum reversus, hoc scriptum nostrum in Museo reperi ac ad te missum non fuisse ægerrime tuli: atque hinc bona fide factum est, quod superiori anno nostra non acceperis: sat cito nunc, si, quod opto, illam miram opinionem tibi poterit excutere: quod ni fecerit, omni ratione destitutam relinquet, scio: at absque ratione nec sentiendum nec loquendum Philosopho; cede igitur et veritatem amplectere, nec conare amplius nova sydera cœlo addere: cur enim tranquillum Æthera turbares? Si consenseris, tua iuncta opera hanc veritatem plene decidi posse mihi persuadeo: sunt enim mihi perfectæ demonstrationes, quibus remotiones Cometarum, quocunque ferantur modo, certo definiri possunt, nec ulla opinor observata tibi desunt: σὺν δύ' ἐρχομένω: conferamus operas: nihil apud me erit, quod tibi non lubenter communicabitur: tu vicissim id agito: Valde cupio illius ROTHMANNI videre, quibus volatili admodum sagacitate ad ♄num usque has fumositates insequitur: si communicaveris, tuo committetur iudicio, quam vere ille tam alte sapiat, alioqui paradoxon a vobis ex proposito defendi videri poterit. Observata quæ hactenus videre contigit, pro principijs demonstrationis remotionum haberi nequeunt: tantum ad apparentem in longitudinem et latitudinem motum faciunt: talia expeto ubi azimutha non variantur et quæ diversis regionibus accepta sunt. Canon mirificus a generoso quodam consangvineo nostro hîc construitur, cui otij et ingenij ad tantum opus satis est: cum perfectus fuerit, tibi communicabitur: Sed de tuis nihil adhuc resciscere potuimus: fac, quæ in chymicis singularia habeas, intellegamus. Scire nostrum nihil est, nisi alij sciant nos scire: De ☉ et ☾ in ☿ reductione aut physicorum tinctura, si quid experientia constat, rescribe, non oberit fortasse nostra collatio: Si quid contra nos amplius cogitas, paucis agito et maiori calami modestia, et si ita libet, publico iudicio nostra subijciamus, ne etiam antilogijs nostris amicitia et consuetudo scribendi intercidat. Si quid hîc expetis, mihi significa: non deero ut potero. Vale quam felicissime D. TYCHO et hoc cerotum ad restinguendam illam in cœlo phlogosin abs te accensam ab amico antagonista medico missum benigne accipito et usurpato.

Datæ Edinburgi 27 Martij 92.
Tuus IOAN. CRAIGIUS Scotus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe, lord of the island of Hven, councillor to the King of Denmark and most learned mathematician, his friend.

Your Apologia, most distinguished Master Tycho, I have run through cursorily, as I received it — for your prolixity and my own occupations did not allow me to read it more carefully — and to its first argument, which you had in some manner fortified, I at once replied, having set out this motion of Nature whereby things sublunary can keep to the track of a great circle; and that for truth's sake, lest there should be need of more words on this matter. In the rest you did not then seem either to weaken our position at all, or to confirm your own the more. My answer was given to our Master Fuller, who was on one of the ships that set out at that time for our King, [then] in Denmark; but when those [ships], hearing of the King's departure from there, turned back from the middle of their journey, it was returned [to me]; nor did I think it should be sent back unless your mathematical [arguments], which we were said to be subverting, were likewise sifted through in the same way. But from that time so many affairs and journeys have overwhelmed me that I was scarcely able to meet your countrymen here once or twice, nor was I able to re-read your [piece] before winter; having then got some little leisure — which was short, and often interrupted by the controversy over the trifles of the witches — I meditated these refutations [Elenctica], and wrote not against you alone, whom for friendship's sake I have handled more gently, but also against those who in my time published this error in Germany, against whom I found among my papers certain theses, written thirteen or more years ago; only their observations were lacking, which I had awaited from Germany, [and] from which they were to have been refuted more fully. Meanwhile your letter, with the observation of some rather obscure phenomenon, was brought to me toward the end of last spring, from which I understood that a reply to your [arguments] was both sought and awaited by you; and so, that I might not delay you longer, I at once reduced our [piece] into this disputational form for you and finished it on that very day on which the previous year I had observed an eclipse of the Sun [☉]; and a few days afterward I had this copy written out afresh, so that it could have been sent to you with your envoy, who was then here. But while I was absent only two days, he had departed sooner than I had hoped; and on my return no other opportunity remained for me to write or to send to you before the autumn sailing. Meanwhile, while I was busy in this neighbourhood with the sick — whence I could easily return at whatever occasion should offer — I was, beyond expectation, summoned into England on a very long journey, nor was I permitted to return home sooner, nor had I been allowed to depart thence again before deep winter. For there was to be awaited the suppuration and clearing-out of a most grievous abscess about the liver in a certain very dear nobleman. Having returned after the feast of [Saint] Martin and nearly about the winter solstice, I found this writing of ours in my study, and bore it most grievously that it had not been sent to you; and from this it came about in good faith that you did not receive our [piece] last year. [It comes] soon enough now, if — as I hope — it can shake that strange opinion out of you; and if it does not do so, it will leave it [the opinion] bereft of all reason, I know. But the philosopher must neither think nor speak without reason; yield therefore and embrace the truth, and try no longer to add new stars to the heaven — for why would you disturb the tranquil Aether? If you agree, I am persuaded that, with your effort joined [to mine], this truth can be settled in full; for I have perfect demonstrations by which the distances of comets, in whatever way they may be carried, can be determined with certainty, nor, I think, do you lack any observations: two going together [σὺν δύ' ἐρχομένω]. Let us pool our labours: there will be nothing of mine that will not gladly be shared with you; do you in turn do the same. I very much desire to see those [observations] of that Rothmann, by which with a quite volatile shrewdness he pursues these vapours right up to Saturn [♄]: if you communicate them, it will be left to your judgement how truly he is so deeply wise, otherwise it may seem that a paradox is being deliberately defended by you [pl.]. The observations that it has so far befallen me to see cannot be taken as principles for a demonstration of distances: they bear only on the apparent motion in longitude and latitude. Such [observations] I seek where the azimuths are not varied and which are taken in different regions. A marvellous table [Canon] is being constructed here by a certain noble kinsman of ours, who has leisure and talent enough for so great a work: when it is finished, it will be communicated to you. But of yours we have so far been able to learn nothing: see that we understand what singular things you have in chemical matters. Our knowing is nothing, unless others know that we know. Concerning the reduction of the Sun [☉] and Moon [☾] into Mercury [☿], or the tincture of the natural philosophers [physici], if anything is established by experience, write back; our comparing [of notes] will perhaps do no harm. If you think anything further against us, do it in few words and with greater restraint of the pen; and, if it so please [you], let us submit our [pieces] to public judgement, lest by our disputations our friendship and our habit of writing should also lapse. If you seek anything here, signify it to me: I shall not fail [you], as far as I can. Fare you most happily, Master Tycho, and receive kindly and make use of this cerate [cerotum], sent by a friend who is your antagonist [and] a physician, to quench that flame [phlogosis] in the heaven kindled by you.

Given at Edinburgh, 27 March [15]92.
Yours, John Craig, Scot.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Ioannes Craigius Scotus = John Craig (d. 1620), Scottish physician and mathematician, educated at Frankfurt an der Oder (where he taught logic and mathematics) and later first physician to James VI of Scotland. An Aristotelian, he held that comets are sublunary and disputed Tycho's celestial cometology; this letter belongs to the Craig–Brahe comet controversy. Tycho replied with his forty-page Apologia (Apologetica Responsio) … ad Craigum Scotum de Cometis (preserved in Opera Omnia IV).
  • Recipient address. Wenae insulae domino … regis Daniae consiliario et mathematico = "lord of the island of Hven, councillor to the King of Denmark, and mathematician." Wena = Hven (the island granted to Tycho in 1576).
  • Apologiam tuam — "your Apologia": Tycho's defence of his cometary doctrine, sent earlier to Craig and here being answered. Craig confesses to having read it only obiter ("cursorily"), pleading Tycho's prolixitas.
  • Naturae motu, quo Sublunaria maximi circuli tramitem tenere possunt — "the motion of Nature whereby things sublunary can keep to the track of a great circle": Craig's physical mechanism for making comets (held to be sublunary, i.e. below the Moon) appear to follow a great-circle path — the crux of his anti-Tychonic argument.
  • Master Fuller. D. Fullero nostro — a "Fuller" attached to a Scottish embassy/fleet to Denmark; not securely identified here. Rendered "Master Fuller" (D. = Dominus,); identity flagged.
  • controversia de veneficarum nugis — "the controversy over the trifles of the witches": the North Berwick witch-trials (1590–91), in which Craig (a royal physician at James VI's court) was caught up; nugis ("trifles") marks his dismissive view. Kept literal.
  • Elenctica — "refutations / things refutative" (from Greek elenktikós, "of refutation"); Craig's title-word for his polemical tract against Tycho. Retained and glossed.
  • Eclipsin ☉lis — "an eclipse of the Sun." The symbol ☉ with the Latin genitive ending -lis (= Solis) is kept in the Source block and named "Sun" in translation. The solar eclipse Craig dates his completion by is that observed "the previous year."
  • nova sydera coelo addere … tranquillum Aethera turbares — "to add new stars to the heaven … disturb the tranquil Aether": Craig's reproach that Tycho's celestial comets (and his 1572 nova) violate the immutability of the heavens. sydera = sidera. Kept as the author's rhetoric, not endorsed.
  • Greek. σὺν δύ' ἐρχομένω — "two going together," a quotation of Homer, Iliad 10.224 (σύν τε δύ' ἐρχομένω καί τε πρὸ ὃ τοῦ ἐνόησεν, "when two go together, one perceives before the other"), proverbial for collaboration; transliterated syn dy' erchoménō and glossed. Craig's appeal to pool labours with Tycho.
  • remotiones Cometarum — "the distances of comets" (their remoteness); remotio in this technical sense = the comet's distance/parallax determination. azimutha non variantur = "the azimuths are not varied," i.e. simultaneous observations from differing regions for a parallax baseline.
  • Rothmann. illius Rothmanni = Christoph Rothmann, the Hesse-Kassel mathematician; ad ♄num usque … fumositates insequitur = "pursues these vapours right up to Saturn [♄]," mocking Rothmann's (and Tycho's) placing of cometary "vapours" as high as the sphere of Saturn. The glyph ♄ (with Latin accusative -num = Saturnum) is kept in the Source block and named "Saturn".
  • Canon mirificus … a generoso quodam consangvineo — "a marvellous table being constructed by a certain noble kinsman": Canon in the mathematical sense of a computational table. The "kinsman" is very probably John Napier of Merchiston (Craig's known associate, to whom Craig is traditionally said to have carried news of Tycho's prosthaphaeretic methods); "marvellous table" anticipates the language of Napier's later Mirifici logarithmorum canonis descriptio (1614). The identification is inferential and is flagged here, not asserted in the translation. consangvineus = consanguineus.
  • Alchemy. De ☉ et ☾ in ☿ reductione aut physicorum tinctura = "concerning the reduction of the Sun [☉=gold] and Moon [☾=silver] into Mercury [☿], or the tincture of the natural philosophers." The metal/planet symbols are kept in the Source block and named in translation; in this alchemical register ☉/☾/☿ also denote gold/silver/quicksilver — the double sense is intrinsic and is noted rather than resolved. physici = the natural philosophers (alchemical adepts), rendered "natural philosophers" not "physicians."
  • Scire nostrum nihil est, nisi alij sciant nos scire — "Our knowing is nothing, unless others know that we know": a sententia (a well-worn tag, ultimately after Persius, Sat. 1.27, scire tuum nihil est nisi te scire hoc sciat alter); kept as an epigram.
  • cerotum … ad restinguendam illam in coelo phlogosin — "this cerate [a medicinal wax salve], to quench that flame [φλόγωσις, phlogosis, "inflammation/conflagration"] in the heaven kindled by you": Craig's closing conceit, the physician-antagonist sending a salve to "treat" the celestial fire (the comet/nova) that Tycho had "kindled" in the sky. cerotum and the Graecism phlogosin are kept and glossed; the joke (medical cure for an astronomical "inflammation") is preserved, not flattened.
  • Calendar & place (dateline crux). Datae Edinburgi 27 Martij 92 = "Given at Edinburgh, 27 March [15]92" (O.S.; Scotland kept the Julian calendar and, in 1592, still began the civil year on 25 March — but here "92" plainly = 1592, the controversy's known date). The edition label field reads "Uraniborg," which the letter's own dateline contradicts: Craig writes from Edinburgh. The header therefore leads with the as-written place/date and notes the catalogued value; "Uraniborg" most likely records the addressee's location (place of receipt) rather than the place of writing, and is not adopted. No date is silently converted.
