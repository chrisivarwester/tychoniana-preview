The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

LITERAS tuas ex arce Benaticâ 8 die Aprilis ad me datas, Vir nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile Princeps, singulari cum lætitia accepi; ex ijs enim perlibenter intellexi, nullas earum, quas ad me misisti, id quod unice verebar, vel interijsse vel interceptas esse. Nec est quod moram in respondendo ulla ratione excuses; facilius enim plurima et innumerabilia quoque proponuntur, quam vel ad unicum respondeatur. Mihi potius adeo frequens ista reiteratio literarum vitio vertenda foret, siquidem alia ratione libros, quos videre petieras, pluresve eorum simul unâ ad te transmittere potuissem.

Inprimis vero lætor, mihique congratulor, me totam illam hypothesin Lunæ, posteaquam ea ad apparentias cœlestes penitius a te transformata fuerit, a benignitate et benevolentia tua sperare et expectare debere. Atque adeo interea temporis sigillatim aliqua te rescripturum constituisse, ex quibus sententia tua de Lunæ hypothesi magis elucescat. Verissimum enim esse reor, me præcisius iusto calculum de Luna instituisse. Cæterum id a me propterea deditâ operâ et summo studio ita factum est, ut absque calculi errore mentem tuam de cursu Lunæ plane lubrico et vario assequi possem, tametsi verear me nihilominus subinde errasse. Facies itaque mihi rem gratissimam, si, dato et concesso tibi otio, viam mihi demonstraveris, qua intimius sententiam hac in re tuam assequi valeam. Abunde enim mihi nunc temporis satisfactum erit, si ea, quæ tu ex accuratissimis observationibus tuis hactenus constituisti, calculo venari seu imitari queam.

Libros illos, quos ex Bibliotheca Sermi Principis mei ad te transmisi, curabo, ut Ser. Clementiæ suæ Agens, cum is in aulam Imperatoriam redierit, sub indice et designatione eorum recipiat et mihi remittat. Manuscripta illa, quæ CYPRIANUS LEOVITIUS ἀνέκδοτα reliquit, per intermedias personas petieram et quidem instantissime efflagitaveram, sed frustra et incassum laboravi, oleumque et operam perdidi, ideoque captata occasione me ipse Augustam contuli, et præsens coram ea ab Generoso Domino PHILIPPO EDUARDO FUGGERO Barone etc. petij atque impetravi; ea itaque tibi mittam, quam primum securam aliquam rationem mittendi perquirendo investigare potero. Carmen quoque tibi mittam brevi, quod, uti spero, non displicebit.

Atque hisce me tibi officiosissime commendo.
Datæ Monachij 4 die Maij A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

Your letter, dispatched to me from the castle of Benátky on the 8th of April, most noble man and easily the Prince of all Mathematicians, I received with singular joy; for from it I learned, most gladly, that none of those [letters] you have sent me — the one thing I feared — had either perished or been intercepted. Nor is there any reason for you to excuse the delay in answering on any account; for very many, and innumerable, things are more easily proposed than a single one is answered. Rather, this so frequent reiteration of letters ought to be charged to my fault, since by some other means I might have transmitted to you the books you had asked to see, or several of them together at once.

But above all I rejoice, and congratulate myself, that I must hope and look for that whole hypothesis of the Moon — after it has been more deeply transformed by you to fit the celestial appearances — from your kindness and benevolence; and, further, that you have resolved meanwhile to write back some matters point by point, from which your view on the Moon's hypothesis may shine out more clearly. For I think it most true that I have set up my calculation of the Moon more precisely than is warranted. Yet I did this of set purpose and with the utmost zeal, in order that, without error of calculation, I might reach your mind regarding the Moon's course — plainly slippery and variable as it is — even though I fear I have nonetheless gone astray here and there. You will therefore do me a most welcome thing if, when leisure is given and granted you, you will show me the way by which I may more intimately attain your opinion in this matter. For it will abundantly satisfy me, at this time, if I can hunt out, or imitate, by calculation, the things which you have hitherto established from your most accurate observations.

As for those books which I sent you from the Library of my Most Serene Prince, I will see to it that His Serene Clemency's Agent, when he returns to the Imperial court, receives them under an inventory and designation, and sends them back to me. Those manuscripts which Cyprian Leovitius left unpublished, I had sought through intermediary persons, and indeed demanded most insistently, but I laboured in vain and to no purpose, and wasted oil and effort; and so, seizing the occasion, I betook myself to Augsburg, and there, present in person, sought and obtained them from the Generous Lord Philipp Eduard Fugger, Baron etc.; these, therefore, I will send you as soon as I can, by inquiry, find out some safe means of sending. A poem, too, I will send you shortly, which, I hope, will not displease.

And with these [words] I commend myself to you most dutifully.
Given at Munich, the 4th day of May, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Direction & sender. An inbound scientific letter to Tycho. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (1553–1622), Chancellor of Bavaria, jurist and polymath, a major correspondent of both Tycho and Kepler (Kepler's epoch-making letters on the Mars orbit were addressed in part to Herwart). He writes from Munich, replying to Tycho's letter of 8 April from Benátky.
  • Recipient. Tycho Brahe, hailed Mathematicorum omnium facile Princeps — "easily the Prince of all Mathematicians."
  • The lunar-theory matter (the scientific core; the edition's convention). Herwart awaits Tycho's hypothesis Lunae — Tycho's lunar theory, with its novel inequalities (the variation and the annual equation), the most intricate part of his work — "after it has been more deeply transformed to fit the celestial appearances (ad apparentias coelestes)," i.e. fitted to observation. ad apparentias [salvandas] is the period idiom salvare apparentias, "to save the appearances", kept in force here. Herwart confesses he has computed the Moon "more precisely than is warranted" (praecisius iusto) in trying to reverse-engineer Tycho's reasoning from the data, the Moon's course being "plainly slippery and variable" (plane lubricus et varius) — a fair characterisation of the lunar inequalities. He asks Tycho to show him the via (method) to imitate by calculation what Tycho established from observation — the recurring early-modern tension between calculus (theory/computation) and observationes.
  • Persons named.
  • Serenissimus Princeps meus = Herwart's sovereign, the Duke of Bavaria (Maximilian I, in whose service Herwart was chancellor; the ducal library is the Bibliotheca Serenissimi Principis), with Serenae Clementiae suae Agens = the Duke's agent at the Imperial court as the return-channel for the loaned books.
  • Cyprianus Leovitius = Cyprian Leovitius (Cyprián Leovic / Karásek z Lvovic) (1524–1574), the Bohemian astronomer-astrologer, whose unpublished manuscripts (ἀνέκδοτα, Greek anékdota, "unpublished things") Herwart pursued for Tycho.
  • Philippus Eduardus Fugger, Baro = Philipp Eduard Fugger (1546–1618), of the Augsburg banking dynasty, bibliophile and collector, from whom Herwart obtained the Leovitius manuscripts in person at Augsburg.
  • Allusions. oleum et operam perdidi — the proverbial "I have wasted oil and labour" (after Plautus, the lamp-oil of fruitless night-work); the Greek ἀνέκδοτα is transcribed and glossed by the edition's convention
  • Places. Monachium = Munich; Augusta = Augsburg; arx Benatica = Benátky.
  • Epistolary formulae. officiosissime me commendo → "I commend myself most dutifully"; Datae (epistolary) → "Given."
  • Calendar. Dateline 4 die Maij A. 1600 at Munich; the edition marks n.s. — correct, Catholic Bavaria being on the Gregorian calendar. Reproduced, not converted.
