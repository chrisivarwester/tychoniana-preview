The original

ILLVSTRISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO, DOMINO WILHELMO, LANDTGRAVIO HASSIÆ, COMITI IN CATZENELLEBOGEN, DIETZ, ZIGENHAIN ET NIDDA: PRINCIPI AC DOMINO CLEMENTISSIMO. S. D.

LITERAS Celsit. tuæ, Illustrissime Princeps, circa Aprilis auspicia per proprium Tabellarium ad me destinatas iuxta proxime sequentis Mensis initia recte accepi. In quibus Alces quasdam Noruegicas C. T. antea a me promissas, ad se e vestigio transmitti desiderat. Accidit autem, vt ijsdem pene diebus Consanguinei mei Nobilissimi viri AXILLI GVLDENSTERN Regij in Noruegia Vicarij literæ vna ad me perlatæ sint, quibus conquerebatur se Nauclerum, qui bestias istas incolumes et illæsas huc transferre in se recipere vellet, offendere illic neminem. Idcirco in animo se habere scribebat, possentne terrestri huc itinere perduci, periculum fieri curare. Scripsi nihilominus eidem AXILLO semel atque iterum, iamque antea mihi factas pollicitationes illi in memoriam reuocaui. Verum is neque terrenis locis, conuallibus vndique inuijs, et confragosis rupibus armentis illis transitum difficiliorem reddentibus, tuto et certo adduci posse respondebat. Et licet id fieri concederetur: neutiquam tamen negotium hoc ob æstiuum Solis ardorem, et antequam Caniculares præterijssent feruores (quos id genus animantium in peregrinis a natiuo solo terris, primo ingressu haud facile sustinet) optatos habiturum successus sperabat. Qua de re se quamprimum profectione in Daniam tum temporis institutâ plenius hîc mecum communicaturum recepit. Atque hinc contigit, vt dum indies vel ipsius Proregis ad nos aduentum auide expectarem, vel animalium istorum copiam nobis interea fieri posse confiderem, C. T. Tabellarium diutius hîc apud me detinere necesse habuerim, donec certi aliquid hâc de re mihi constaret. Nec enim sine Alcibus illis ad T. C. remittendum duxi, præsertim cum is indicârit, sibi iniunctum fuisse, vt non absque his rediret. Sed quamuis prædictus hic AXILLVS expectatione nostra serius, vtpote circa Mensis huius ingressum, huc aduentârit; Peropportune tamen accidit, vt ad idem fere tempus, quo alter T. C. nuntius, posteriores literas de eadem re me commonefacientes asportârit, AXILLVS e Noruegia huc primum appelleret, meque in hac Insula mox inuiseret. Confestim itaque cum ipso de Alcibus dudum promissis, sedulo egi. Verum is difficultates non paucas, quo minus terrâ mariue integra huc valetudine perduci queant, obijciebat, curque hactenus eas mittere non licuerit, causas edidit. At ego instabam, recentiorem illum C. T. nuncium, qui iampridem accedebat, quemadmodum Feræ eiusmodi e longinquis adduci debeant, satis callere: siquidem prius e Suecia aliquot tales T. C. indemnes attulerit. Protinus itaque duobus Nobilibus, quibus Arcem et Prouinciam, cui in Noruegia præest, se absente commiserat, per literas mandauit, vt quinque Alces, quas hinc inde in mei gratiam conquisierat, atque in varijs locis alendas (donec inde commode huc transferri possent) disposuerat, eidem nuncio vna cum alimentis cæterisque rebus necessarijs diligenter et sine mora procurarent. Is proinde acceptis hisce literis, vna cum libero a me commeatu in Noruegiam iter accelerat, vt obtentis ibi quinis hisce Alcibus, ijsdemque in nauem impositis, recta Bremam traijciat, atque hinc terrestri passu desiderata iam diu animalia ad T. C. qua poterit, cura et sedulitate perducat. Quod si singula sana et viuida istic sistere concessum fuerit, non dubito, quin T. C. petitioni, adeoque diutinæ expectationi, iam tandem, quantum in nobis erat, satisfecerimus. Retulit autem Consanguineus ille meus, cum hîc præsto esset, vnam inter istas quas habet Alces satis grandæuam, eandemque masculam esse: reliquas vero quatuor iuniores partim masculas, partim fæmellas. At quotquot et qualescunque fuerint, vt C. T. has boni consulere, atque moram hanc, qua tota pene æstate responsio hæc dilata sit, ob antedictas causas excusatam habere dignetur, demisse etiam atque etiam rogo: nec Tabellario primum amandato, quod tam diu hîc hæserit, succensere velit. Si qua alia in re T. C. in hisce oris mea qualicunque opella, quod eidem gratum acceptumque fuerit, quippiam præstari queat, inueniet me vbique et in omnibus quam promptissimum. De Astronomicis nostris exercitijs nunc nihil dicam. Quid in his moliar T. C. alias data occasione intelliget. Id saltem addam, me non libenter ex hoc vltimo T. C. Grammatophoro percepisse, ROTHMANNVM necdum redijsse, Tuæque Celsitud. Instrumenta Astronomica interea a Cœlestium considerationibus prorsus feriari. Ideoque quam maxime optarem, vt C. T. alium quendam harum rerum peritum, in ipsius locum substitui curet, qui inchoatam telam rite pertexat, et Obseruationibus sedulo continuandis deinceps inuigilet. Sed meum non est T. C. quippiam præscribere, præsertim cum naturali quadam propensitate et singulari fauore ad hæc studia amplianda et promouenda, non sine præclara laude, feratur. DEVS T. C. ad omne bonum opus exequendum, quam diutissime conseruet incolumem.

Iamque T. C. quam felicissime valeat.

Datæ Vraniburgi, 20. Septemb. Anno 1592.

Celsit. T. Addictiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Illustrious Prince and Lord, Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, Count in Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda: a most gracious Prince and Lord. Greeting.

Your Highness's letter, Most Illustrious Prince, dispatched to me by your own courier about the beginning of April, I duly received toward the beginning of the next month. In it Your Highness desires that certain Norwegian elk [Alces], earlier promised to you by me, be conveyed to you forthwith. But it happened that on almost those same days a letter from my most noble kinsman, Master Axel Gyldenstierne, the King's Governor in Norway, was brought to me together with it, in which he complained that he could find no skipper there who would undertake to bring those beasts here unharmed and unhurt. He therefore wrote that he had it in mind to make trial of whether they could be brought here by an overland route. None the less I wrote to that same Axel once and again, and recalled to his memory the promises that had earlier been made to me. But he replied that they could not safely and surely be brought, since the land places too — valleys impassable on every side, and rugged crags — made the passage harder for those herds. And even were it granted that this be done, he still did not hope that the business would have the wished-for success on account of the summer heat of the Sun and before the dog-day fervours had passed (which a creature of that kind, in lands foreign to its native soil, does not easily endure at its first arrival). On this matter he undertook that, on the journey into Denmark which he had then arranged for the time, he would communicate with me here more fully. And hence it came about that, while day by day I was eagerly awaiting either the Viceroy's own arrival to us, or trusting that in the meantime a supply of those animals might be procured for us, I was obliged to detain Your Highness's courier here with me longer, until something certain about this matter should be settled for me. For I did not think it right to send him back to Your Highness without those elk, especially since he indicated that it had been enjoined upon him that he should not return without them. But although the aforesaid Axel arrived here later than we expected — namely about the beginning of this month — yet it fell out very opportunely that at almost the same time at which Your Highness's other messenger brought a further letter reminding me of the same matter, Axel first put in here from Norway and soon visited me on this Island. Forthwith, therefore, I dealt diligently with him about the elk long since promised. But he raised not a few difficulties as to their being brought here by land or sea in sound condition, and set forth the reasons why hitherto it had not been possible to send them. Yet I pressed him, [arguing] that that more recent messenger of Your Highness, who had now for some time been arriving, was well enough skilled in how wild beasts of that kind ought to be brought from afar — seeing that he had earlier brought some such [beasts] from Sweden to Your Highness unharmed. So at once he gave order by letter to the two noblemen to whom, in his absence, he had committed the castle and the province over which he presides in Norway, that they should diligently and without delay furnish to that same messenger, together with fodder and the other necessary things, five elk which he had sought out here and there for my sake and had distributed in various places to be fed (until they could conveniently be brought hither from there). He accordingly, having received these letters, together with a free safe-conduct from me, hastens his journey into Norway, so that, once these five elk have been obtained there and put aboard ship, he may cross straight to Bremen, and thence by land bring the animals so long desired to Your Highness with what care and diligence he can. And if it be granted that he set them down there each one sound and lively, I do not doubt that we shall now at last, so far as in us lay, have satisfied Your Highness's request, and indeed your long expectation. That kinsman of mine, moreover, reported, when he was present here, that one among those elk which he has is of fair age and is a male; but the other four, being younger, are partly males, partly females. But however many and of whatever kind they prove to be, I most humbly beg, again and again, that Your Highness may deign to take these in good part, and to hold this delay — by which this reply has been put off almost the whole summer — excused for the aforesaid reasons; nor be angry at the courier first sent off, that he has lingered here so long. If in any other matter, on these shores, anything can be rendered to Your Highness by my poor service of whatever sort, that may be welcome and acceptable to you, you will find me everywhere and in all things most ready. Of our astronomical exercises I shall now say nothing. What I am setting in hand in these matters Your Highness shall learn on another occasion as opportunity is given. This much only I shall add: that I have learned, not gladly, from this last letter-bearer of Your Highness, that Rothmann has not yet returned, and that Your Highness's astronomical instruments meanwhile lie wholly idle from the observation of the heavens. And therefore I could most greatly wish that Your Highness would see to it that some other man skilled in these matters be put in his place, who may duly finish the web once begun, and may henceforth keep watch over the observations to be diligently continued. But it is not for me to prescribe anything to Your Highness, especially since by a certain natural inclination and singular favour you are borne toward the enlarging and advancing of these studies, not without distinguished praise. May GOD preserve Your Highness unharmed, for the carrying out of every good work, as long as may be.

And now may Your Highness fare most happily.

Given at Uraniborg, 20 September, in the year 1592.

Most devoted to Your Highness,
TYCHO BRAHE.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Addressed to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince of Kassel, Tycho's chief princely scientific correspondent. The full titulature — Comiti in Catzenellebogen, Dietz, Zigenhain et Nidda = Count in Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain, and Nidda — is the standard Hessian style, preserved by this chapter's convention Wilhelm died on 25 August 1592 (N.S.), shortly before this letter was written (20 September O.S.); Tycho on Hven would not yet have known of his death.
  • Sender / place. Tycho Brahe at Uraniborg on Hven (Vraniburgi, this chapter's convention).
  • Axel Gyldenstierne (Axillus / Axillo Guldenstern, here in the genitive/dative; d. 1603) — Danish nobleman, royal governor (statholder) of Norway 1588–1601. Tycho calls him Consanguineus meus ("my kinsman") — the Brahe and Gyldenstierne families were connected by marriage. The terms Regius in Noruegia Vicarius, Prorex ("Viceroy"), and the phrase Arcem et Prouinciam, cui in Noruegia praeest ("the castle and province over which he presides in Norway") all denote this governorship (his seat was Akershus, Oslo).
  • Alces — elk (European elk / moose, Alces alces). The letter is the principal document of Wilhelm IV's standing request, fulfilled through Tycho, for live Norwegian elk; the famous (later) story of one of Tycho's own elk dying after drinking beer and falling down stairs at Landskrona belongs to a separate, later episode and is not in this letter — not imported here.
  • Caniculares … feruores — "the dog-day fervours," the dies caniculares, the hottest part of summer associated with the heliacal rising of the Dog Star (Sirius/Canicula); rendered idiomatically with the dog-day sense kept.
  • Christophorus Rothmannus (ROTHMANNVM) — Christoph Rothmann, Wilhelm IV's mathematician at Kassel, who had left Kassel in 1590 (travelling to visit Tycho on Hven) and had not resumed his post. Tycho's remark that the Kassel Instrumenta Astronomica … a Coelestium considerationibus prorsus feriari ("the astronomical instruments lie wholly idle from the observation of the heavens") urges the Landgrave to appoint a successor observer — a request whose timing is poignant, the Landgrave being already dead.
  • inchoatam telam rite pertexat — "duly finish the web once begun": a weaving metaphor (Penelope's tela) for completing the unfinished programme of Kassel observations; kept as metaphor.
  • Astronomy. Astronomica nostra exercitia = "our astronomical exercises"; Instrumenta Astronomica = "astronomical instruments"; Coelestium considerationibus = "the observation of the heavens." No technical terms beyond these general ones occur — this is a logistical letter about livestock, not an observational report.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. D. = salutem dicit, "sends greeting"; Celsit. tuae / C. T. / T. C. = Celsitudo tua, "Your Highness"; valeat … quam felicissime = "may [Your Highness] fare most happily"; Celsit. T. Addictiss. = Celsitudini Tuae addictissimus, "Most devoted to Your Highness."
  • Tabellarius / nuntius / Grammatophorus — three words for the messenger/letter-carrier: Tabellarius ("courier"), nuntius ("messenger"), and the Greek-derived Grammatophorus (γραμματοφόρος, "letter-bearer"); the variation is the author's, preserved by varying the English.
  • libero … commeatu — "a free safe-conduct" (a commeatus, pass/permit for travel), here issued by Tycho to the messenger; rendered "safe-conduct."
  • Calendar. Dateline Datae Vraniburgi, 20. Septemb. Anno 1592 = "Given at Uraniborg, 20 September 1592" — Old Style (Denmark used the Julian calendar until 1700; this chapter's convention). The catalogue label ("1592. 20. september. Uraniborg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. Note that Wilhelm IV's death (25 August 1592) is reckoned New Style in Imperial/Hessian usage; the two styles are not reconciled here, only flagged.
