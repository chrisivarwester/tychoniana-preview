The original

ERLIG OCH WELBIURDIGH MAND, THIGE BRAHE THILL KNUDTZTORP, MIN KIERE BRODER, GANDSKE BRODERLIGEN THILSCHREFFUIDT.

MYNN gandsche wennligh hilßenn nu och alltiid forßennd met Gud Kere Tyge och kere broder, nest mynn wennligh tacßielße for allt beuist gode, huilchit ieg aff formuenn steze wyll findis goduylig at forschyle. Kere broder, ieg haffuer fornumit wdaff dyn schriffuelße, att dynn leylighed er nu bleffuenn god, saa att du haffuer nu bekomit hoss keysseren gode wylkar, huilchit mig wdj sandhed møgitt kert er att høre; och som du wdj dinn schriffuelße est begerendis, att der som dig bliffuer giortt nogenn forhynndring paa dine jnstrumenter, att ieg daa will hielpe til, saa møgitt mugligtt war, at dig jngenn forhyndringh der paa matte sche, saa forsser ieg mig, att dig well jngen forhindringh der paa scher; men der som dett schede, will ieg giøre saa møgit derwdj som mig mugligtt ehr; och wdj huess made ieg wid digh att tienne, schall du altiid fynnde mig wiligh. Och wyll nu och altiid haffue digh Gud alme[ct]iste beffalendis met allt huiss digh kertt er.

DatumKøbenhaffnn denn 25 October 99.
Axel Brahe
egen hann.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Honourable and well-born man, Tyge Brahe to Knudstrup, my dear brother, written to in all brotherliness.

My quite friendly greeting, now and always foresent with God, dear Tyge and dear brother; next, my friendly thanks for all the good [you have] shown, which I, of my ability, shall ever be found well-willing to requite. Dear brother, I have understood from your writing that your situation has now become good, so that you have now obtained good terms with the Emperor — which in truth is very dear to me to hear. And as, in your writing, you are requesting that, should any hindrance be done to you upon your instruments, I would then help, as much as may be possible, that no hindrance befall you thereon — so I trust that no hindrance does befall you thereon; but should it so happen, I will do as much therein as is possible to me; and in whatever way I know to serve you, you shall always find me willing. And [I] will, now and always, have you commended to God Almighty, with all that is dear to you.

Given at Copenhagen, the 25th of October, [15]99.
Axel Brahe,
[his] own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Axel Brahe (1550–1616), Tycho's younger brother, a member of the Danish Council of the Realm (Rigsråd); writing warmly from Copenhagen. An original letter, signed egen hånd ("own hand").
  • Content. Axel rejoices to learn that Tycho's affairs have turned out well — that he has secured good terms with the Emperor (Rudolf II) — and answers Tycho's request: should anyone obstruct him over his instruments (those still in Denmark, or their removal abroad), Axel will help as far as he can, though he trusts no such hindrance will arise. A letter of fraternal reassurance during Tycho's transition into imperial service.
  • Orthography. Late-16th-c. Danish chancery spelling: erlig (ærlig, honourable), welbiurdigh (velbyrdig, well-born), thilschreffuidt (tilskrevet), hilßenn (hilsen), tacßielße (taksigelse, thanks), leylighed (situation/circumstance), wylkar (vilkår, terms), forhynndring (forhindring, hindrance), jnstrumenter (instruments), beffalendis (befalende, commending). Reading-verified as Danish. The bracketed alme[ct]iste (almægtigste) is an editorial restoration of a damaged spot; kept as printed.
  • Wor gunst register: opens with the courteous min ganske venlige hilsen … forsendt med Gud ("my quite friendly greeting … sent with God"), the standard Danish noble epistolary salutation (cf. glossary).
  • Calendar. København denn 25 October 99 = 25 October 1599; Denmark kept the Julian / Old Style calendar. Header agrees with the label.
