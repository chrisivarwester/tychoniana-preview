The original

SPECTATISSIME DOMINE DECANE AC SENIOR CÆTERIQUE CLARISSIMI DOCTORES COLLEGIJ MEDICI.

QUOD tam honorifice et benevole ad promotionem Doctoratus in facultate medica circa medium Octobris stylo veteri apud vos solenniter celebrandam (memores adhuc illius convictus, qui inter nos fuit, dum superiore anno aliquantisper Witebergæ subsisterem) vestris humanissimis literis me invitatis, est cur vobis omnibus et singulis officiose gratias agam. Satisfacerem vero haud illubenter vestræ petitioni, si per me id staret, et temporis locorumque ratio, quibus inserviendum, id ferret. Sum enim ex Sacræ Cæsæ Matis, Domini nostri clementissimi, mandato iam occupatus in disponendis instrumentis meis Astronomicis, quæ partim ex Arce Benatica, partim Magdeburgo Leutmeritzium, quousque Albis est navigabilis, deinde huc Pragam, eiusdem Maiestatis iussu, nuperrime advecta sunt, ut in ampla quadam et magnifice superius prope arcem exstructa domo ijs collocatis, ipsius Maiestati (sic enim ei clementissime placuit) omnia propius in conspectu sint, donec quorsum ulterius transferenda, et ubi commode ad usum stabilienda veniant, benignissime ordinaverit. Quæ cum non ita cito expediri queant (res enim non parvæ est molis) ut vobis ad constitutum tempus gratificer, spero tam apud vos, quam Eruditissimum Magistrum IÖSTELIUM, Candidatum inter sex illos primarium, amicum mihi in primis dilectum, excusationi locum futurum. Quod superest, ut actus ille auspicatus et salutaris sit, precor, vosque omnes Deo Opt. Max. commendatos habeo.

Dabantur Praga die 9 Octobris stylo reformato Anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Most esteemed Lord Dean and Senior, and the rest of you most distinguished Doctors of the Medical College.

That you so honourably and benevolently invite me, by your most kind letter — still mindful of that fellowship which was between us, while last year I stayed for a while at Wittenberg — to the promotion of the Doctorate in the medical faculty, to be solemnly celebrated among you about the middle of October, old style: there is reason for me to give dutiful thanks to you, all and each. I would indeed not unwillingly satisfy your request, if it stood with me, and if the reckoning of time and of places, which must be served, allowed it. For I am now occupied, by the mandate of His Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord, in arranging my astronomical instruments — which, partly from the castle of Benátky, partly from Magdeburg, have very recently been conveyed to Leitmeritz [Litoměřice] (as far as the Elbe is navigable), and then hither to Prague, by order of the same Majesty — so that, these being placed in a certain ample house, magnificently built up above [the city], near the castle, everything may be more closely in His Majesty's sight (for so it has most clemently pleased him), until he has most kindly ordained whither they are to be transferred further, and where they may conveniently be established for use. Since these things cannot be despatched so quickly — for the matter is of no small bulk — that I may gratify you at the appointed time, I hope there will be place for [my] excuse, both with you and with the most learned Master Jöstel, the chief candidate among those six, a friend especially dear to me. As for what remains, I pray that that act [ceremony] may be auspicious and salutary, and I hold you all commended to God Best and Greatest.

Given at Prague, the 9th of October, reformed style, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipients. The Dean, Senior, and Doctors of the Medical Faculty (College) at Wittenberg, who had invited Tycho — himself a master of Paracelsian medicine and a Wittenberg guest the previous winter — to a doctoral promotion. Tycho declines, with thanks.
  • The instruments at Prague — a key logistical datum. Tycho explains he is "by the Emperor's mandate" busy setting up his astronomical instruments, "partly from the castle of Benátky, partly from Magdeburg," conveyed up the Elbe to Leitmeritz (Litoměřice) "as far as the Elbe is navigable," and thence to Prague — to be installed "in a certain ample house, magnificently built up above [the city], near the [Prague] castle," so that everything be "more closely in the Emperor's sight." This dates the arrival and Prague installation of the instrumentarium to autumn 1600, and is the downstream end of the Elbe transport organised by Rollenhagen at Magdeburg (brahe-t_16000619001). (The "house near the castle" is the curia/Belvedere quarter by Prague Castle, Tycho's interim observatory before the move to the Curtius house / Benátky question was settled.)
  • Magister Iöstelius = Melchior Jöstel (1559–1611), Wittenberg professor of mathematics and medicine, "the chief candidate among those six [doctoral candidates]," and Tycho's friend (cf. brahe-t_16000806001).
  • Calendar. The letter is dated Praga die 9 Octobris stylo reformato 1600 = 9 October 1600, reformed [New] style (Prague, imperial); the Wittenberg ceremony it declines is set "about the middle of October, old style" — a neat in-text illustration of the O.S./N.S. gap between Lutheran Saxony and imperial Bohemia.
