The original

DEM DURCHLEUCHTIGSTEN/ HOCHGEBORNEN FÜRSTEN VND HERRN/ HERRN WILHELM, LANDTGRAUEN ZU HESSEN / GRAUEN ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ DIETZ/ ZIEGENHAIN VND NIDDA: MEINEM GNEDIGEN HERRN.

DURCHLEUCHTIGSTER/ Hochgeborner Fürst. Ewer F. G. sind meine fleißwillige Dienste nach allem vermügen jeder zeit zuvoran bereit. Gnedigster Herr/ Ew. F.G. an mich vnder dato den 22. Maij nechsthin/ auß jhrer Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel außgangenes Schreiben/ ist mir nu erst den 7. huius allhie auff Vraniburg zu handen Commen. Wo es so lange 3½. Monat/ vnder Wegen sey auffgehalten/ kan ich nicht wissen/ sonst hette ichs zeitlicher zu beantworten nicht vnderlassen.

Was erstlich meines fürhabenden Operis primum Tomum de Recentioribus cœli Phænomenis belangt/ in welchem Capite 2. Stellarum fixarum Restitutio fundamentaliter tractirt wirdt/ dessen Ew. F. G. in jhrem Schreiben gedenckt/ Darauff kan Ew. F. G. ich dienstlich nicht verhalten/ daß dasselb Opus noch nicht vollends durch den Druck verfertigt ist/ Denn ich diesen Sommer anderer nothwendiger auffliegender Gescheffte halben nicht weyl gehabt/ solchem auffzuwarten. Wenn aber Gott Gnat vnd gelegenheit verleihen wirdt/ daß es diesen Winter möchte fertig werden/ alßdenn wil ichs Ew. F. G. jhrem begeren nach/ mittheilen.

Daß Ew. F. G. Mathematicus, ROTHMANNVS, noch nicht zu Ew. F. G. wider ankommen gewesen/ solchs thut mich nicht wenig verwundern/ denn er hat sich bey mir nicht anders mercken lassen/ denn daß er von stunden wider zu rück gen Cassel sich begeben wolte/ welches nu eben ein Jahr verlitten ist. Wofern der Morbus (desse nEw. F. G. meldung thut) in causa were/ daß er so lange außbleibt/ so hette er je mitler zeit sich können curiren lassen. Er ließ sich solchs mangels allhie gar nicht vernemen/ sonst were vielleicht hie so gut Paracelsisch Remedium dagegen zu finden/ alß anderßwo/ welches jhme auch ohn zweiffel wolbewust ist. Ich gedencke aber/ so er noch nicht widerumb zu Ew. F. G. ankommen ist/ er werde sich mit dem ersten wider einstellen. Es were gut/ daß er das Opus Stellarum Fixarum mit erster gelegenheit verfertigte/ auff daß die langweylung vnd groß angewandte mühe vnd fleiß nicht hinder bliebe/ sondern andern / vnd Posteritati zu nutz kommen möchte.

So viel das Riensthier betrifft/ da Ew. F. G. von schreibt/ hab ich wol auch in dem Deutschen Gesnero, welchs Buch mir auch bey der handt ist/ sein Iconem vnd Descriptionem gefunden/ der es ein Roßhirsch nennet/ Aber bißhero hab ich noch nicht erfahren können/ daß ein solches allhie soll verhanden seyn. Ich bin selber zu etlichen malen bey Königlicher Maiestat/ Hochlöblicher gedechtnuß/ im Thiergarten gewesen/ hab aber kein solchs allda jemals vernommen. Deßgleichen auch nicht zu Koppenhagen. So ich aber noch einige wissenschafft darumb kriegen kan/ wil Ew. F. G. ich davon Bericht zustellen.

Der Rhenböcke oder Thier/ seyndt wol allhie ein oder zwey auffm Holm zu Koppenhagen gegangen/ wie ich im worigen Schreiben angezogen hab/ Aber sie können allhie den Sommer vber nicht leben/ vnd die Hitze vertragen/ viel weniger im Landt zu Hessen/ oder andern orten/ die mehr gegen Süden liegen/ Denn sie haben jhre stelle in dem gar Septentrionali parte Noruegiæ vnd Sueciæ, vnd können nicht wol an andern orten vberbleiben. Aber die Elendsthier/ wiewol sie auch auß Norwegen vnd Schweden jren vrsprung haben/ so können sie doch allhie in Dennemarck etliche Jar vber/ wenn sie wol vnderhalten werden/ verharren. Versehe mich darumb/ man werde sie auch in Deutschlandt ziehen vnd halten können/ Denn es bißweylen hie im Sommer wol so hitzig ist alß dort. Ich hab letzst mal/ da Ew. F. G. ich zuschrieb/ eines hie gegen vber in Schonen/ auff meinen Gütern gehabt/ vnd wie ichs hab wollen herüber auff diese Insul holen lassen/ ist es an die Stadt/ mit Namen Landskronen/ die hie gegen vber ligt/ ankommen/ vnd allda etliche Tage auffm Schlosse/ bey einem Edelmann/ der meiner Schwester Tochter hat/ von luft wegen auffgehalten/ da es denn/ alß man vber Tisch gesessen/ ein mal die Treppe hinauff gegangen/ vnd sich in der Stuben von starckem Bier so voll getruncken/ daß es im wider herab gehen/ die Stiege hinab gefallen/ vnd das eine Bein zerbrochen/ welchs man mit keinem fleiß wider heylen kunde lassen. Vnd ist also darüber gestorben. So baldt ich aber ein anders oder ein par widerumb bekommen kan/ die zam seyn/ wil Ew. F. G. ich die gutwillig vnd mit allem fleiß zuschicken.

Ferner/ da Ew. F. G. in jrem Schreiben mention thut vom Papiermacher/ daß Ew. F. G. nach einem oder zween zu Franckfurt wolte vmbhören lassen/ kan Ew. F. G. ich nicht bergen/ daß ich selber schon einen bekommen hab/ der deß Handtwercks gnugsam erfaren/ vnd nu mit einem Gesellen im Wercke ist/ vmb Druckpapier zu machen. Thu mich gleichwol gegen Ew. F. G. dienstlichst vnd zum höchsten bedancken/ daß dieselbige gnedigen willens gewesen/ vnd sich nicht beschweret/ solch vngemach meinet halben auff sich zu nemen. Wo Ew. F. G. ich hinwiderumb nach meinem geringen eussersten vermügen zu dienst vnd gefallen seyn kan/ darzu erbiete ich mich gantz willig/ vnd soll Ew. F. G. mir ohn das zu gebieten haben.

Wil Ew. F. G. in Schirm deß Allerhöchsten zu langwiriger glücklicher Gesundtheit vnd Landsregierung hiemit dienstfleissig befolen haben.
Datum Vraniburg, den 8. Septemb. Anno 1591.
Ewer F. G.
Dienstwilliger
TYCHO BRAHE.

POSTSCRIPTA. AVFF daß aber diß mein Schreiben nicht alleine von den Irdischen Thieren, sondern etwas de Caelestibus (quæ nostra est voluptas) inhalte, wolte ich nicht vnderlassen, Ew. F. G. zu significiren, daß Martis Stella, der sonsten mehr alß die andern Planeten den Calculum Astronomicum eludirt, ist im letzsten Acronycho situ, welches geschehen ist im anfange deß 9. Tags Iunij, circa [Perigæum] solare, mit dem Calculo Coperniceo gar nahe vbereingestimmet, also daß kaum ½. Gratis gefehlet hat, so doch den Numeris Alphonsinis nach bey 4½. Grad zu viel gewesen, welchs sonsten in andern Oppositionibus Solis & Martis, deren ich [aliquot] obseruat, nicht geschicht. Vnd hab Martem zuvor, wenn er ist pernox gewesen, circa Arietis & Libræ initia, in medio quasi loco inter Apogæum & Perigæum solare, considerirt, daß er alßdan von deß COPERNICI Calculo bey 2. oder 3. Grad abgewichen ist, vnd das in partes contrarias, prout ferebat Eccentricitatis solaris ratio. Es hat auch die Alphonsina Supputatio allda nicht bestehen können. Auß diesem ist leichtlich [ab ijs qui rem penitius introspicientes] abzumercken, daß da noch ein ander [inæqualitas] fürhanden sey, e solari Eccentricitate proueniens, die sich in den apparentem motum Planetarum insinuirt. Vnd wird in Marte, quoad tres superiores, am meisten gespürt, dieweyl er seinen Circuitum ad Solem nicht so groß hat alß die andern, daß darumb Eccentricitas Solis respectu ambitus, quem is designat, ein sensibilem proportionem hat, die doch in Ioue geringer, vnd in Saturno gar wenig gespürt wirdt. Hierauß ist zu schliessen, daß vnsere Inuention in Hypothesibus caelestibus, welche ich lib. 2. de æthereis Phaenomenis cap. 8. generaliter & lato modo indicirt hab, alleine mit den Apparentes vbereinzustimmen vermag. Vnd daß weder die alte Ptolemeische, noch die newe Coperniceische hierin nicht bestehen, denn sie können diese inæqualitatem nicht gebürlich excusiren. Jch hab auch von diesem in geheim mit Ew. F. G. Mathematico, alß er hic war, geredt, der sich denn nicht wenig darob verwundert hat, vnd bekandt, daß er zuvor darhin nicht hab gedencken können, sonst hette er sein Iudicium de meis Hypothesibus nicht also præcipitirt. Vnd ist viel anders hierneben zu mercken, welches zu weitleufftig wer allhie zu erkleren. Diß hab ich nu allein zum Beschluß, vnd vmb einige Astronomische Collation halben, Ew. F. G. anzeigen wollen. Bittendt, Ew. F. G. wolle diesem, nach jrem hohen in hac scientia Verstande, nachdencken, vnd mir jhre Meynung davon gnedigst zukommen lassen. Datum vt supra.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Most Illustrious, High-born Prince and Lord, Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain and Nidda: my gracious Lord.

Most Illustrious, High-born Prince. To Your Princely Grace my diligent and willing services are at all times ready beforehand, to the utmost of my power. Most gracious Lord, Your Princely Grace's letter to me, under the date of the 22nd of May last, issued from your city and fortress of Kassel, has only now, on the 7th of this month [huius], come to my hands here at Uraniborg. Where it was delayed so long — three and a half months — on the way, I cannot tell; otherwise I should not have failed to answer it more promptly.

As regards, first, the first volume of my projected work On the more recent phenomena of the heavens [Operis … primum Tomum de Recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis], in the 2nd chapter of which the restoration of the fixed stars [Stellarum fixarum Restitutio] is treated from the foundations, which Your Princely Grace mentions in your letter — concerning this I cannot, in duty, conceal from Your Princely Grace that the same work is not yet fully completed through the press; for this summer, on account of other necessary business pressing upon me, I have not had leisure to attend to it. But when God grants grace and opportunity for it to be finished this winter, then I will, according to Your Princely Grace's desire, communicate it [to you].

That Your Princely Grace's mathematician, Rothmann [ROTHMANNVS], had not yet returned to Your Princely Grace — this causes me no little astonishment; for he gave me to understand nothing other than that he meant to betake himself back to Kassel forthwith — which is now just a year gone by. If the illness [Morbus] (of which Your Princely Grace makes mention) were the cause that he stays away so long, then he could surely in the meantime have had himself cured. He let no want of that sort be perceived here at all; otherwise there would perhaps be as good a Paracelsian remedy [Paracelsisch Remedium] to be found here against it as anywhere else — which is also without doubt well known to him. But I think that, if he has not yet returned to Your Princely Grace, he will present himself again at the first [opportunity]. It would be well that he complete the work on the fixed stars [Opus Stellarum Fixarum] at the first opportunity, so that the long tedium and the great pains and diligence expended should not be left behind [unfinished], but might come to the benefit of others, and of posterity [Posteritati].

As far as concerns the reindeer [Riensthier] of which Your Princely Grace writes: I have indeed found its picture [Iconem] and description [Descriptionem] in the German Gesner [Gesnero] too — which book is also at hand for me — who calls it a "horse-stag" [Roßhirsch]; but hitherto I have not been able to learn that such a one is to be had here. I have myself been several times in the deer-park [Thiergarten] of His Royal Majesty, of most laudable memory; but I have never heard of any such there. Nor likewise at Copenhagen. But if I can yet get any knowledge of one, I will furnish Your Princely Grace with a report of it.

Of the reindeer-bucks, or [reindeer] beasts [Rhenböcke oder Thier], there have indeed gone one or two here on the Holm at Copenhagen, as I noted in my previous letter; but here they cannot live through the summer and bear the heat — far less in the land of Hesse, or other places that lie more toward the south — for they have their place in the wholly northern part of Norway and Sweden [Septentrionali parte Noruegiae vnd Sueciae], and cannot well survive in other places. But the elk [Elendsthier], although they too have their origin out of Norway and Sweden, can nonetheless endure here in Denmark for several years, if they are well kept. I expect, therefore, that one will be able to rear and keep them in Germany too, for here it is at times in summer indeed as hot as there. The last time, when I wrote to Your Princely Grace, I had one over here across the way in Skåne [Schonen], on my estates; and when I meant to have it fetched over here to this island, it came to the town named Landskrona, which lies here across the way, and there it was kept some days at the castle, for the air's sake, with a nobleman who has my sister's daughter [to wife]; whereupon — as they sat at table — it once went up the staircase, and in the chamber drank itself so full of strong beer that, on going down again, it fell down the stair and broke its one leg, which with no diligence could be got to heal again. And so it died of it. But as soon as I can get another, or a pair, that are tame, I will willingly and with all diligence send them to Your Princely Grace.

Further, since Your Princely Grace in your letter makes mention of the papermaker — that Your Princely Grace would have inquiry made after one or two at Frankfurt — I cannot hide from Your Princely Grace that I have myself already obtained one, well enough experienced in the craft, who is now at work with a journeyman to make printing-paper [Druckpapier]. I render, nonetheless, my most dutiful and highest thanks to Your Princely Grace that you were of gracious will, and did not think it a burden, to take such trouble upon yourself for my sake. Wherein I can in turn be of service and pleasure to Your Princely Grace, according to my slight and utmost power, thereto I offer myself wholly willingly; and Your Princely Grace shall in any case have me at your command.

Herewith I would commend Your Princely Grace, in the protection of the Most High, to long-lasting, happy health and government of your land, in dutiful diligence.
Given at Uraniborg, the 8th of September, in the year 1591.
Your Princely Grace's
dutiful and willing servant,
TYCHO BRAHE.

[Postscript:] Postscript. But that this my letter may treat not only of the earthly animals but also somewhat of the celestial [bodies] (which is Our delight), I would not refrain from signifying to Your Princely Grace that the star Mars — which more than the other planets otherwise eludes the astronomical computation — in its last acronychal position, which occurred at the beginning of the 9th day of June, near the solar [perigee], agreed very nearly with the Copernican computation, so that it fell short by scarcely ½ a degree, whereas by the Alphonsine Numbers it was in excess by about 4½ degrees; which otherwise does not happen in other oppositions of the Sun and Mars, of which I have observed several. And I had earlier, when Mars was all-night visible, near the beginnings of Aries and Libra, in a place as it were midway between the solar apogee and perigee, considered that it then departed from Copernicus's computation by about 2 or 3 degrees, and that in contrary directions, as the reckoning of the solar eccentricity bore. There too the Alphonsine reckoning could not hold. From this it is easily to be noted, by those who look into the matter deeply, that there is yet another inequality present, arising from the solar eccentricity, which insinuates itself into the apparent motion of the planets. And it is most sensed in Mars, as regards the three superior [planets], inasmuch as its circuit about the Sun is not so great as the others', so that for that reason the eccentricity of the Sun, in respect of the circuit which it describes, bears a sensible proportion — which, however, is smaller in Jupiter, and very little sensed in Saturn. From this it is to be concluded that our discovery in the celestial hypotheses, which I have indicated generally and in a broad manner in Book 2 of the De aethereis Phaenomenis, chapter 8, is alone able to agree with the Appearances; and that neither the old Ptolemaic nor the new Copernican [hypotheses] hold here, for they cannot duly excuse this inequality. I have also spoken of this in confidence with Your Princely Grace's mathematician, when he was here, who thereupon marvelled not a little, and confessed that he had not been able to think of it before, else he would not have so precipitated his judgement of my hypotheses. And there is much else to be noted alongside this, which would be too lengthy to explain here. This I have wished to indicate to Your Princely Grace only by way of conclusion, and for the sake of some astronomical collation; begging that Your Princely Grace will, according to your high understanding in this science, reflect upon it, and graciously let me have your opinion thereof. Given as above.

Apparatus

  • Sender / recipient. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven, to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the astronomer-prince whose Kassel observatory and correspondence (largely conducted through his mathematician Rothmann) were Tycho's chief scholarly counterpart in the 1580s. The full chancery titulature (Graf zu Katzenelnbogen, Dietz, Ziegenhain und Nidda) is preserved by the edition's convention
  • Edition of record. Printed by Tycho himself in the Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Liber I, Uraniborg 1596), the volume from which the edition's edition field is taken; the text is also in Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI. No MS witness is recorded in the source header.
  • Language. Verified by reading as Frühneuhochdeutsch chancery German, not Danish; the heuristic tag de is correct. The register is the formal Ew. F. G. (Eu[e]r Fürstlich[e] Gnaden = "Your Princely Grace") address maintained throughout, with constant code-switching into Latin for technical and bibliographic nouns. Latin embeddings retained and italicised on first use: Operis primum Tomum de Recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis, Capite 2., Stellarum fixarum Restitutio, fundamentaliter tractirt, Mathematicus, Morbus, in causa, curiren, Paracelsisch Remedium, Opus Stellarum Fixarum, Posteritati, Iconem, Descriptionem, mention, Septentrionali parte Noruegiae … Sueciae.
  • The work in progress. primum Tomum de Recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis … Capite 2. Stellarum fixarum Restitutio = the first volume of the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata (the "Introduction to the renewed astronomy"), whose long-delayed printing occupied Tycho's Uraniborg press; Stellarum fixarum Restitutio = "the restoration of the fixed stars" (the fixed-star catalogue), rendered "restoration" per the editorial glossary (, restitutio/instauratio).
  • Rothmann. ROTHMANNVS = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician, who had visited Tycho on Hven in 1590 and — as this letter records — never returned to Kassel. The Morbus ("illness") is the reason the Landgrave had offered; Tycho is sceptical, noting Rothmann showed no sign of it on Hven and could have sought a Paracelsisch Remedium ("Paracelsian remedy") there. Paracelsisch Remedium kept as a period term (cf. remedium magneticum — not modernised).
  • Zoological matter. The bulk of the letter answers the Landgrave's request for northern animals. Riensthier = reindeer (Rangifer); Tycho cross-refers to the German Gesner (Gesnero = the German-language edition of Conrad Gesner's Historia animalium / Thierbuch), where the beast is figured under the name Roßhirsch ("horse-stag"). Rhenböcke oder Thier = "reindeer-bucks, or [reindeer-]beasts"; auffm Holm zu Koppenhagen = on the Holmen (the naval-yard island) at Copenhagen. Königliche Maiestat, Hochlöblicher gedechtnuß = the late King Frederik II (d. 1588), "of most laudable memory." Elendsthier = elk / moose (Alces; German Elen[d]tier) — the comic centrepiece anecdote. Septentrionali parte Noruegiae und Sueciae = "the northern part of Norway and Sweden."
  • The elk anecdote. Schonen = Skåne (then Danish), where Tycho held estates across the Øresund; Landskronen = Landskrona, the town opposite Hven. The nobleman "who has my sister's daughter [to wife]" is not named in the text; left unidentified rather than guessed. The elk drinks itself drunk on strong beer at table, falls down the castle stair, breaks a leg, and dies — reported deadpan. No interpretive softening applied.
  • Papermaker. Papiermacher … Druckpapier — the Landgrave had offered to find a papermaker at Frankfurt for Tycho's press; Tycho reports he has already engaged one, working with a Geselle (journeyman) to make printing-paper for Uraniborg.
  • Calendar. The dateline den 8. Septemb. Anno 1591 matches the catalogue label exactly (no discrepancy). Denmark and Hesse-Kassel both kept the Julian calendar, so the date is O.S. den 7. huius = "the 7th of this [month]," i.e. 7 September 1591 (O.S.), the day before writing. The incoming letter's date den 22. Maij (22 May) is likewise O.S.; Tycho's "three and a half months" in transit is his own reckoning of the 22 May → 7 September interval and is reproduced, not recomputed.
  • Formulae. Opening Ewer F. G. sind meine fleißwillige Dienste … zuvoran bereit and the closing in Schirm deß Allerhöchsten … befolen haben / … Dienstwilliger are the standard subordinate-to-prince courtesy frame (servant writing to lord, the inverse of the Landgrave's princely Wir/euch register); translated, not dropped.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. German closing postscript "POSTSCRIPTA … de Caelestibus", restored from Dreyer VI, pp. 236–237 (dropped by the working transcription); the Mars opposition (early 9 June, near solar perigee), the Copernican-vs-Alphonsine error, and the self-citation "lib. 2 de aethereis Phaenomenis cap. 8" confirmed against the facing Latin (VI, p. 239); bracketed words supply garbled tokens per the Latin.
