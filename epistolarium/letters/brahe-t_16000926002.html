The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD VITUM WINSHEMIUM.

MAGNIFICE et excellentissime Domine D. VITE WINSHEMI, amice inprimis honorande: Ex literis Excell. Væ ijsdemque adiunctis, quæ Illustrissimus Princeps OTTHO dux Brunsvicensis et Luneburgensis Clementissime a me requirat, intellexi; utque ipsius Celsitudo sciat, me ea in parte officiosissime illi pro meo modulo morem gesturum, dedi ad ipsius Celsitudinem literas meum responsum brevibus complectentes, quas ut ex me debita cum submissione ad ipsius Celsitudinem dirigere E. V. velit, peramanter rogo.

Meæ res adhuc in eodem sunt statu quo antea. Versor etiamnum Pragæ cum tota familia, huc a Sacra Cæs. Maiestate, paulo postquam Pilsena redierat, Clementissime evocatus. Instrumenta mea Astronomica, quotquot in arce Benachiæ habui, huc iussu ipsius Maiestatis allata sunt, atque in domo illa, quæ iuxta hortum Cæsaris prope arcem situm magnifice Italico more extructa est, et cupro superius tecta, quam E. V. proculdubio novit, disposita sunt, ut ipsius Maiestas ea promptius, cum lubuerit, perlustrare possit, quod et ante paucos dies, quamprimum ibi erecta erant, me præsente factum est, quæsivitque tunc ipsius Maiestas de cæteris, quæ cum Leutmeritzium advenisse percepit, etiam in eandem domum transferri atque aliquantisper ibi collocari, donec de loco oportuno, ubi rectius ad usum constitui atque perpetuo conservari possint, providerit, benignissime mandavit. Quid hac in re fiet, postmodum V. E. ex me cognoscet; procedunt hîc omnia (ut scit) ob rerum gerendarum copiam et molem lento gradu, quod patienter ferendum est. Interim in domo quadam ampla, non longe ab arce remota, satis commode cum meis habito, et consuetis studijs, quantum interpellationes et conversationes cum Illustribus Viris in tantæ aulæ præsentia ferunt, vaco.

Gaudeo rem esse compositam inter nostrum Serenissimum Regem et Anglam; Deus author Pacis avertat in posterum discordiarum semen et occasiones.

FREDERICUS ROSENKRANZIUS iam est in Ungaria in exercitu equitatus Cæsaris nostri, sed vereor, ne illi plus periculi immineat ex Asthmate et pulmonum vitijs, quibus graviter obnoxius est, præsertim si vinis istis fortioribus nimium indulserit, quam vel ab hoste propinquo, vel aliunde. Fuit hîc ante biduum quidam ex eius ministris, qui in Daniam properavit illi forte pecunias allaturus, cum quo OLIGERO nostro scribere voluissem, nisi tantopere festinasset, et eram alias uti nunc quoque admodum occupatus. Filia nihilominus mea natu maior ad illum quasdam exaravit, commonefacientes de equo quodam promisso, quem sponsione quadam, dum Wandesbeci essemus, amisit. Et quia nunc commoda datur occasio, equum illum cum patruelis sui ministro, qui eum proculdubio accedet, mittendi (is enim ibi resolutus in eo huc equitare poterit) voluit illa, me sic iubente, OLIGERO id insinuare. At minister ille iter accelerans hinc abierat, antequam literæ dictæ obsignatæ essent. Quare eas nunc Hamburgum ad SIMONEM de PETKUM mitto. Si Væ Exæ in promptu est commoditas eas ad OLIGERUM dirigendi, rogo id facere non degravetur. Sin minus, poterint GERHARDO RANZOVIO unâ cum alijs ipsi inscriptis transmitti, ita ut OLIGERUS eas recipiat, antequam FRIDERICI minister inde se huc et deinceps in Ungariam receperit. Sed sive id fiat, sive non, parum interest. Quare de tam levi re plura non addam, cum hæc ipsa per se nimia sint.

Turcam Canisium obsidere voluisse, sed ob aquarum copiam, cum udus et paludosus sit locus, inde discessisse ferunt. Quorsum, necdum satis constat; habet exercitum quadruplo maiorem nostro, sed ultra mensem ob instantem hyemem ibi perdurare nequit. Dicunt eum pacem expetere, an vero id serio, et æquis conditionibus, dubium est. Cæsar noster adhuc præsenti est animo, nec tam potentem hostem extimescit, utut Germaniæ Principum subsidijs maiori ex parte destitutus. Deus omnia vertat ad felicem exitum, et Christianismum protegat, cui æviterno numini te unâ cum tuis commendatum habeo.

Vale.
Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum Die 26 Sept. Anno 1600.

TYCHO BRAHE TO VITUS WINSHEMIUS.

Magnificent and most excellent Lord Doctor Vitus Winshemius, friend especially to be honoured: from Your Excellency's letter, and its enclosures, I have understood what the Most Illustrious Prince Otto, Duke of Brunswick and Lüneburg, most clemently requires of me; and that His Highness may know I will most dutifully comply with him in that part, according to my measure, I have given to His Highness a letter comprising my answer briefly — which I lovingly ask that Your Excellency would direct to His Highness from me, with due submission.

My affairs are still in the same state as before. I still dwell at Prague with my whole household, summoned hither most clemently by the Sacred Imperial Majesty, a little after he had returned from Pilsen. My astronomical instruments, as many as I had in the castle of Benátky, have been brought hither by His Majesty's order, and arranged in that house which is magnificently built in the Italian manner beside the Emperor's garden, near the castle, and roofed above with copper — which Your Excellency doubtless knows — so that His Majesty may the more readily survey them when he pleases; which, a few days ago, as soon as they were erected there, was done in my presence. His Majesty then asked about the rest, which when he learned had reached Leitmeritz [Litoměřice], he most kindly ordered to be transferred also into the same house and placed there for a while, until he should provide a convenient place where they could be more rightly set up for use and perpetually preserved. What will be done in this matter, Your Excellency will afterward learn from me; here all things proceed (as you know) at a slow pace, on account of the abundance and mass of business, which must be patiently borne. Meanwhile I dwell, conveniently enough, with my household in a certain ample house not far from the castle, and am free for my accustomed studies — as far as the interruptions and conversations with Illustrious Men, in the presence of so great a court, allow.

I rejoice that the matter has been settled between our Most Serene King and the Englishwoman [Queen Elizabeth]; may God, the author of Peace, avert in future the seed and occasions of discords.

Frederik Rosenkrantz is now in Hungary, in the cavalry army of our Emperor; but I fear more danger threatens him from the Asthma and lung-defects to which he is gravely liable — especially if he indulges too much in those stronger wines — than either from the near enemy or from elsewhere. There was here, two days ago, one of his servants, who hastened into Denmark, perhaps to bring him money, with whom I would have wished to write to our Holger [Rosenkrantz], had he not hastened so; and I was otherwise (as now too) quite occupied. Nevertheless my elder daughter wrote some lines to him [Holger], reminding him of a certain promised horse, which he lost by a wager, while we were at Wandsbek; and because a convenient occasion is now given of sending that horse with his cousin's servant — who will doubtless come to him (for he, being at leisure there, will be able to ride hither on it) — she wished, at my bidding, to intimate this to Holger. But that servant, hastening his journey, had departed before the said letter was sealed. Wherefore I now send it to Hamburg, to Simon von Petkum. If Your Excellency has at hand a means of directing it to Holger, I ask you not to find it burdensome to do so; if not, it can be sent to Gerhard Rantzau, together with others addressed to him, so that Holger may receive it before Frederik's servant betakes himself thence hither, and then into Hungary. But whether it be done or not, it matters little; wherefore I will add no more about so trifling a matter, since these very [words] are in themselves too much.

They report that the Turk wished to besiege Kanizsa, but, on account of the abundance of waters (the place being wet and marshy), departed thence — whither, it is not yet sufficiently certain. He has an army four times greater than ours, but cannot endure there beyond a month, on account of the approaching winter. They say he seeks peace, but whether seriously, and on fair terms, is doubtful. Our Emperor is still of resolute mind, and does not greatly dread so powerful an enemy, although for the most part destitute of the German Princes' subsidies. May God turn all things to a happy outcome, and protect Christendom — to which everlasting Power I hold you, with yours, commended.

Farewell.
Given at Prague of the Bohemians, the 26th day of September, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Vitus Winshemius (the younger), Wittenberg professor of medicine and Tycho's go-between (cf. brahe-t_15980328001, the Bökel affair). The MS is in Basel.
  • Duke Otto's request. Tycho answers (via Winshemius) a requirit from Otto, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg, enclosing a brief reply to be forwarded to the Duke "with due submission" — the matter itself not specified.
  • Significance — the instrument installation near Prague Castle (material history). A precise account of where the instruments first went on reaching Prague: the Benátky instruments were set up in "that house … magnificently built in the Italian manner beside the Emperor's [pleasure-]garden, near the castle, and roofed with copper," where Rudolf II viewed them in Tycho's presence "a few days ago"; the rest, arrived at Leitmeritz (Litoměřice) up the Elbe, the Emperor ordered brought to the same house "until a convenient place" be found (the move to the Curtius house would follow). Meanwhile Tycho lodges "in a certain ample house not far from the castle." Complements brahe-t_16001008001, brahe-t_16001009001 (the Benátky/Magdeburg → Leitmeritz → Prague transport).
  • The Anglo-Danish peace. Tycho rejoices the dispute "between our Most Serene King and the Englishwoman" (Christian IV and Elizabeth I) is settled — the Emden conference outcome (cf. brahe-t_16000516001, where he had feared it might "breed war").
  • Frederik Rosenkrantz and the horse (family/personal detail). Frederik Rosenkrantz — of the disgrace-and-rehabilitation thread — is now serving in the imperial cavalry in Hungary; Tycho fears his asthma and weak lungs (worsened by "stronger wines") more than the enemy. A charming domestic vignette: Tycho's elder daughter wrote to Holger Rosenkrantz about a horse Holger had lost to her in a wager at Wandsbek, now to be sent via a servant; the letter, missing the courier, is routed to Simon von Petkum at Hamburg, thence (via Winshemius or Gerhard Rantzau) to Holger.
  • The Turkish war. The Turk abandoned the siege of Kanizsa (Nagykanizsa) because of flooding/marsh; his army "four times greater than ours" but unable to winter there; reportedly seeking peace (doubtfully). Rudolf II "of resolute mind," though "for the most part destitute of the German Princes' subsidies." (Cf. the Long-War news of brahe-t_16000916002, brahe-t_16010415002.)
  • Persons named. Vitus Winshemius; Otto, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg; Rudolf II; Christian IV and Elizabeth I; Frederik Rosenkrantz; Holger Rosenkrantz (Oligerus); Tycho's elder daughter (unnamed — Magdalene or Elisabeth Brahe); Simon von Petkum (Hamburg factor); Gerhard Rantzau.
  • Calendar. Dateline Pragae Bohemorum Die 26 Sept. Anno 1600; New Style (imperial). Header agrees with the label.
