The original

THYGE BRAHE ETC.

WOR gunst tillforn. Wiid att som thu Oß haffuer fortrøstit om en almanach paa thette thillstandendis 88 Aar att wille giøre och Oß forskaffe nogit betiidliger end thu pleyer then Oß att lade tillkomme, thilligeste ochsaa haffuer giort Oß tilsagn om thend almanach, som thu therhoes pleyer Oß att giøre, att wille Oß forskaffe. Tha bede Wij thig och begere, attu samme almanacher, thersom the ere forferdigett, mett thenne breffuißer wor dreng wille Oß tilsende. Eller och,thersom the icke endnu ere beende,tha mett thet første muligt er wille them forferdige, och Oß tilbiude, nar Wij them mue were foruenthendis. Thet scheer Oß till wilge.

Befallendis
Actum Holm, 24. septemb. a:o 87.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tyge Brahe, etc.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, whereas you have given us your assurance regarding an almanac for this coming 88th year [1588] — that you would make and procure it for us somewhat more in good time than you are wont to let it reach us — and have likewise also given us your promise concerning the [other] almanac which, along with it, you are wont to make for us, that you would procure it for us: we therefore ask and desire you that you would send us those same almanacs, if they are finished, by this bearer, our servant-lad. Or else, if they are not yet ready, that you would finish them at the very first that is possible, and notify us when we may expect them. This [you do] to our pleasure.

Commending [you to God],
Done at Holm, 24 September, in the year [15]87.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), Tycho's royal patron, who in 1576 granted him Hven. As in the 1584 compass letter, the king treats Tycho as a personal supplier — here of the annual almanacs/calendrical predictions, a recurring obligation Tycho discharged from Uraniborg.
  • Recipient. Tyge Brahe (Tycho), addressed in the Danish vernacular form Thyge.
  • Document type. A chancery Kladde (draft / unentered concept), among the Danske Kancellis uindførte koncepter til skånske missiver — i.e. a draft missive of the Danish Chancery for the Scanian (skånske) correspondence. There is no Latin register rubric here (unlike the 1584 letter); the opening THYGE BRAHE ETC. is the chancery's own address-line shorthand.
  • almanach / almanacher — "almanac(s)." The king refers to two items: the almanac for the coming year 1588, and a second almanac that Tycho customarily made "along with it" (therhoes). These are the prognosticatory wall-/calendar-almanacs (with weather and astrological notes) that the Danish court expected annually from its astronomer; rendered "almanac" and the duo preserved (note the plural almanacher "those same almanacs").
  • In-letter date reference (NOT a dateline discrepancy). The body asks for the almanac paa thette thillstandendis 88 Aar — "for this coming 88th year," i.e. 1588 — while the letter itself is dated 1587 (a:o 87). This is a forward reference (an almanac for next year, requested this year), not a conflict; flagged by the edition's convention rather than reconciled. The catalogue label ("Holm") names the place, not a date, and agrees with the dateline.
  • Negation. The body contains a single negation — thersom the icke endnu ere beende "if they are not yet ready" — reproduced once, with no other negation added or dropped. The two thersom ("if") clauses set up the alternative the king offers: if finished → send them now; or else, if not yet ready → finish them as soon as possible and report when.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1587). WOR gunst tillforn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula,); Wiid = Vid (imperative "know"); som here introduces the recital ("whereas / inasmuch as"); thu = du; Oß = os; haffuer = haver/har; fortrøstit = fortrøstet ("assured, given confident promise"); thillstandendis = tilstundende ("coming, approaching"); betiidliger = betideligere ("more in good time, earlier"); pleyer = plejer ("are wont to"); tillkomme = tilkomme ("reach, come to"); thilligeste = tillige(st) ("likewise"); tilsagn ("promise, undertaking"); therhoes = derhos ("therewith, along with it"); attu = at du; thenne breffuißer = denne brevviser ("this bearer-of-the-letter"); wor dreng = vor dreng ("our servant-lad / boy"); forferdigett / forferdige = forfærdiget / forfærdige ("finished / to finish, complete"); beende = b-ende, i.e. berede(de)/beredte ("ready, prepared"); tilbiude here = tilbyde in the sense "give notice to, advise" (let us know); foruenthendis = forventendes ("to be expected, awaiting"); Thet scheer Oß till wilge = Det sker os til vilje ("this is done to our pleasure / will"); Befallendis = Befalendes ("commending [you, sc. to God]," the chancery valediction). The text's German-influenced spelling (w-, sch-, -ck, Oß) is the chancery hand, not the German language — verified Danish by reading.
  • Calendar / place. Actum Holm, 24. septemb. a:o 87 = "Done at Holm, 24 September [15]87" (O.S.). Holm = Bremerholm / Holmen, the royal naval dockyard and adjoining royal precinct in Copenhagen, a frequent place of issue for Frederik II's chancery. Actum "Done/Given" kept by the edition's convention
