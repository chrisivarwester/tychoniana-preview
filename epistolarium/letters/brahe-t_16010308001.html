The original

GENEROSO ET MAGNIFICO VIRO, DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHEO, MATHEMATICORUM FACILE PRINCIPI, CÆSAREÆ MAIESTATIS ASTRONOMO, PATRONO ET DOMINO COLENDO.
PRAG IN BEHEMEN.
MAGNIFICE ET NOBILISSIME DOMINE, PATRONE COLENDE.

TANDEM sub BACCHI sacra ad nos diu expectatus advenit Magnificus Dominus D. GODELMANNUS. Is mihi de te renunciavit, quod accepisses carmen, quod de Mathematicis exorsus sum, et ad te sub natalem CHRISTI misi. Gratum id mihi fuit. Addidit, si saltem in cursu progrederer, te laborem et artem meam Cæsareæ Maiestati commendaturum. Id quoque gratum. Verum interim mirabar, cum ego permulta mihi a te suppeditari petierim, cur non aut omnia illa, aut quædam, quæ tibi nota sunt, simul per D. GODELMANNUM unâ cum literis tuis ad me miseris. Hæc enim potissima causa erat, cur istud fragmentum vel potius bonam partem ad te miserim, ac significavi simul, me ante progredi non posse, quam responsum a te accepissem. Rogo igitur adhuc etiam atque etiam, vir magnifice et generose, ut certa data occasione literas tuas ad me mittas unâ cum illis, quæ petij, quantum in te est. Tandiu enim adhærescere cogor. Scio, quantum me ames; igitur etiam hîc tibi curæ sim. Plura iam non scribo propter imbecillem plane valetudinem.

Bene et feliciter T. M. valeat.
Datæ Rostochij 8 Martij stylo veteri Anno 1601.
T. Magnif.
reverenter colens
MARTINUS BRASCHIUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the generous and magnificent man, Master Tycho Brahe, easily prince of mathematicians, Astronomer of His Imperial Majesty, patron and lord to be honoured. At Prague in Bohemia. Magnificent and most noble Lord, patron to be honoured.

At length, about the rites of Bacchus [Shrovetide], the long-awaited Magnificent Master Dr. Gödelmann arrived among us. He reported to me, concerning you, that you had received the poem which I began about the Mathematicians and sent to you about the Nativity of Christ. That was pleasing to me. He added that, if only I would proceed in [my] course, you would commend my labour and art to His Imperial Majesty. That too [was] pleasing. But meanwhile I wondered why — since I had asked that very many things be supplied me by you — you did not send me, at the same time, through Master Gödelmann, together with your letter, either all those things, or some of them, which are known to you. For this was the chief reason why I sent you that fragment, or rather a good part [of the poem], and signified at the same time that I could not proceed before I had received an answer from you. I ask therefore, again and yet again, magnificent and generous man, that, when a sure occasion is given, you send me your letter, together with those things which I asked, as far as is in you. For until then I am compelled to stick fast. I know how much you love me; therefore may I be a care to you in this too. I write no more now, on account of my plainly feeble health.

May Your Magnificence fare well and happily.
Given at Rostock, 8 March, old style, in the year 1601.
Reverently honouring Your Magnificence,
Martin Brasch.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Martin Brasch (Braschius, 1565–1601), professor of philosophy and poetry at Rostock; here pressing Tycho for source-material he had promised, needed to complete a didactic poem "about the Mathematicians" (carmen de Mathematicis) which Brasch had begun and sent Tycho, in part, "about Christmas." (Brasch died later in 1601; his complaint of imbecillis valetudo, "feeble health," is poignant.)
  • Tie to brahe-t_16001116003. This is the follow-up to Tycho's reply of 16 Nov 1600: Gödelmann (the go-between) had relayed that Tycho received the poem and would commend Brasch to the Emperor "if he proceeded"; Brasch answers that he cannot proceed until Tycho sends the promised materials.
  • D. Godelmannus = Johann Georg Gödelmann (see brahe-t_15981112001), now Saxon-Electoral Legate, the courier between Prague and Rostock.
  • sub Bacchi sacra — "about the rites of Bacchus": the festival of Bacchus, here the Shrovetide/Carnival season (late winter), consistent with an 8 March letter reporting Gödelmann's recent arrival; rendered with the gloss. Caesareae Maiestatis Astronomo — the address already styles Tycho "Astronomer of His Imperial Majesty."
  • Calendar. Rostochij 8 Martij stylo veteri … 1601 = 8 March 1601, Old Style (stylo veteri explicit; Lutheran Rostock kept the Julian calendar). Header agrees with the label.
