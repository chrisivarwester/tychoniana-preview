The original

TYCHONI BRAHEO.

SALUTEM et officia. Quam semper dedito animo in id incubuerim unice, Generose TYCHO, quod necessitudinem eam, quam generis et sangvinis inter nos intemeratam esse voluit communio, ipse, quantum in me sit, non † tu tenes modo, sed et confirmarem, licet ex voto sat evidenter prodere huc usque nequiverim, ipse tamen, quin ex proprio candore te aemulantem reputes, nullus dubito. Quin et eis me sorte et censu a te sincere ascribi, quos eruditionem tuam ac sapientiam illustrem suspicientes, ipse redamare non nequis, indubius confido, ut adeoque operosam animi in te mei depraedicationem erga te accersere, nec operae nec curae precium esse censeam. Nil restat, nisi ut ad amicissimas tuas litteras, quam debeo et in officio imposito requiris, declaratam fidem reponam. Quam si in alijs operam meam ipsius concernentibus alias precij alicuius dehinc putaris, iube, impera, meque addictissimum tibi ex asse habe, macte. De reliquo est, quod submoneam, litteras tuas parenti insignitas ipsi redditas tum demum, ubi ex Senatu redijsset, qui ex Generosi STENONIS fratris tui relatione causam se cognovisse retulit, atque pro re est, quod rogem, Generosus meus amicus.

[Oligerus Rosaecrantz.]

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Tycho Brahe.

Greeting and my services. With what devoted spirit I have always applied myself to this one thing — namely, that the kinship which the bond of family and blood has willed to be inviolate between us I myself should, so far as in me lies, not [merely] † as you hold it, but should also confirm — although until now I have not been able to make it sufficiently evident in proportion to my wish: yet of this I have no doubt at all, that you, of your own candour, account me one who emulates you. Indeed I am confident beyond doubt that I am sincerely numbered by you, in lot and in standing, among those whom — looking up to your learning and your illustrious wisdom — you yourself cannot but love in return; and so I should judge it worth neither the labour nor the trouble to elicit toward you the laboured proclaiming of my regard for you. Nothing remains except that I render back to your most friendly letter the assured fidelity that I owe and that you require in the duty laid upon me. And if hereafter you shall reckon my service of any [further] worth in other matters concerning yourself, bid me, command me, and hold me most utterly devoted to you — good speed to you! For the rest, there is this of which I would remind you: that your letter addressed to [my] parent was delivered to him only then, once he had returned from the Senate [Council], who reported that he had learned the matter from the account of your brother, the Honourable Steno; and it is to the purpose that I should ask [it of you], my Honourable friend.

[Holger Rosenkrantz.]

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Oligerus Rosaecrantz = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (Holger Rosenkrantz den lærde, 1574–1642), the Danish nobleman, theologian and statesman. His kinship with Tycho — invoked in the opening (necessitudinem … generis et sangvinis … communio) — ran through the Brahe–Rosenkrantz connection; the Rosenkrantz and Brahe families were repeatedly intermarried, and Tycho's circle of noble correspondents included several Rosenkrantz kinsmen. The signature is supplied editorially (bracketed in the source), the body itself unsigned in the transcript.
  • Generose / Generosus — the standard address of a nobleman: "Honourable / Wellborn." Rendered "Honourable"; Generose TYCHO = "Honourable Tycho," Generosi STENONIS = "the Honourable Steno," Generosus meus amicus = "my Honourable friend."
  • STENONIS fratris tui — Steen (Steno) Brahe (1547–1620), Tycho's elder brother, the Danish statesman and Rigsråd member; here named as the source of the information that reached "the parent" (see crux below). The Latinised genitive Stenonis = "of Steno/Steen."
  • ex Senatu redijsset — "had returned from the Senate": Senatus renders the Danish Rigsråd (Council of the Realm), the body on which Steen Brahe sat; "Senate [Council]" given to preserve the Latin term and its referent without silently modernising.
  • litteras tuas parenti insignitas — "your letter addressed to [your] parent." insignitas (lit. "marked, inscribed [to]") is taken in the epistolary sense "addressed/directed to"; parenti ("to the parent") is taken in the epistolary sense "addressed to." Resolved: the paired letter (Tycho→Rosenkrantz, 22 Jan 1596, Opera Omnia VII p. 389) addresses utrumque tuum laudatissimum parentem ('both your parents'); the parens here is therefore Holger Rosenkrantz's own parent (his father Jørgen Rosenkrantz, d. 1596, or his mother), to whom Tycho's letter had been forwarded — not Tycho's own (Otte Brahe had died in 1571). The elder learned the matter via Steen Brahe's report.
  • Dateline / place. The transcript carries no dateline and no place of writing; the edition label is "1596. empty" — i.e. assigned to the year 1596 with no day or month, and the place unknown. The header therefore gives "[no place], 1596" rather than asserting any day; the year is the catalogue assignment, not a date written in the source. No calendar (O.S./N.S.) can be affixed to a yearless, placeless transcript.
  • Negation count. The source carries the negations precisely as reproduced: non (in the crux clause), nequiverim ("I have not been able"), quin … nullus dubito ("I have no doubt that …"), non nequis (double negative = "you cannot but," i.e. "you must"), indubius ("beyond doubt"), nec operae nec curae ("neither the labour nor the trouble"). The double negative non nequis is rendered affirmatively ("cannot but love in return") as its grammar requires; the count is preserved without adding or dropping a "not."

### Cruxes

  • The dagger †. The source prints a crux (obelus) in the clause "non † tu tenes modo, sed et confirmarem" — the edition flags a corruption/lacuna at this point and offers no clean reading. The construction does not resolve cleanly: non … modo … sed et ("not only … but also") frames an antithesis, but the verb governing the first limb is lost or garbled at the dagger, so the intended contrast ("not only [hold / acknowledge] as you hold it, but also confirm it") is reconstructed only by sense. The translation reproduces the dagger and renders the surrounding clause as conjecturally as the Latin permits ("not [merely] † as you hold it, but should also confirm"), without smoothing the gap into fluent English. Dreyer's apparatus (Opera Omnia IX p. 319) records tu tenes] tutarer conj. Friis — Friis conjectured non tutarer modo, sed et confirmarem ('I should not only safeguard [the bond], but also confirm it'); the dagger marks Dreyer's retention of the transmitted tu tenes over this conjecture.
  • Convoluted period. The whole letter is a single chain of heavily subordinated, mannerist humanist periods (e.g. operosam animi in te mei depraedicationem erga te accersere) whose syntax strains sense in several places; the rendering follows the most defensible construal clause-by-clause and does not impose a confidence the Latin does not support.
  • Correction. Corrected 'your letter addressed to [your] parent' to '[my] parent': the unmarked 'parenti' denotes the writer Holger Rosenkrantz's own father, Jørgen Rosenkrantz (a Rigsraad member), not Tycho's father Otte Brahe, who had died in 1571 — 25 years before this letter.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (high). 'parenti' carries no possessive in the Latin and, in context, refers to the writer's own father - Jorgen (Georgius) Rosenkrantz, a Rigsraad/Senate member (cf. the companion letter's heading 'Oligero Rosencrantzio, Georgii f. de Rosenholm' and 'ubi ex Senatu redijsset' immediately following) - not Tycho's father, who had been dead 25 years by 1596. The bracketed '[your]' asserts the opposite parent and produces a claim (Tycho's father returning from the Senate in 1596) that is historically impossible. At: “your letter addressed to [your] parent”.

Repaired. Collated against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.374-376 (in full); the clause is confirmed verbatim on p.375. High-severity defect fixed with full confidence; the correction also resolves what would otherwise be a historical impossibility (Tycho's father, dead since 1571, cannot have been the one 'returning from the Senate' in 1596).
