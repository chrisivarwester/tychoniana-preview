The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD VITUM WINSHEMIUM.

S. P.

EXCELLENTISSIME et Eruditissime Domine Doctor VITE WINSHEMI, Amice honorande: Literae Excellentiae Tuae humanissimae et candidi erga me amoris refertissimae bis vel ter, postquam in Bohemiam concessi, mihi redditae sunt; pro quo constanti et sincero erga me affectu gratias habeo plurimas. Memini etiam me Tuae Excae aliquoties rescripsisse, eamque meas pariter accepisse spero. Quantum vero ad ultimas, quas Exa Tua per Civem illum Hamburgensem, quibus Rollenhagianae adiunctae erant, dederat, commodum accidit, quod cum Pragae essem (ablegaturus TENGNAGLIUM nostrum, cum quodam ex meis domesticis, Pilsnam in Aulam Caesaris) eae mihi redderentur. Nam per emissarios meos negotium illud Civis, cuius caussam mihi commendâsti, Domino BARVITIO, summo Secretario, exponi iussi, eumque, ut petitioni eius non deesse velit, diligenter ex me rogavi: quod et factum est. Nam alter ille hisce diebus huc reversus (TENGNAGLIUS enim in suam Patriam inde profectus est, post aliquot septimanas huc, Deo volente, rediturus) retulit Dominum BARVITIUM in se recepisse negotij illius promotionem, quamprimum Civis iste Pilsnam veniret et caussam illam sibi proponeret; quod tamen necdum factum erat, antequam domesticus ille meus inde discederet, qui per integrum mensem ibi substitit. Miror itaque, ubi interea Civis ille fuit, et an Pragae remanserit, Caesaris isthuc reditum expectaturus, ignoro. Semel enim tantummodo mecum Pragae locutus est; postea hominem non vidi, quamvis volebam, ut denuo me accederet, quo plenius eius causae circumstantias cognoscere possem; quas etiam ut FRANCISCO indicaret, iussi. Si in alijs longe maioribus Tuae Exae in his oris gratificari unquam potero, habebit me semper quam promptissimum et nostrae amicitiae perpetuo memorem.

Quae ad OLIGERUM ROSÆKRANTZIUM communem Amicum proferre volui, ignoro an acceperit, multo enim iam tempore nihil ad me literarum dedit. Faciat igitur T. E. me certiorem, quomodo vivat et valeat, utque subinde mihi scribat, ipsum admoneat.

Vale diu et feliciter.
Dabantur ex Arce Caesarea Benaticâ Die 20 Aprilis stylo recognito Anni 1600.

Tycho Brahe to Vitus Winshemius.

Heartiest greeting.

Most excellent and most learned Lord Doctor Veit Winsheim, honoured friend: Your Excellency's most courteous letters, brimming with candid love toward me, have been delivered to me two or three times since I withdrew into Bohemia; for which constant and sincere affection toward me I give you many thanks. I recall, too, that I wrote back to Your Excellency several times, and I hope you likewise received mine. As for the last, which Your Excellency had sent through that Hamburg citizen — to which the Rollenhagen letters were attached — it fell out conveniently that they were delivered to me while I was at Prague (about to dispatch our Tengnagel, together with one of my servants, to Pilsen to the Emperor's court). For through my emissaries I had that citizen's business — whose cause you commended to me — laid before Lord Barvitius, the supreme Secretary, and diligently asked him on my behalf to be willing not to fail his petition; and this was done. For that other man [my servant], having returned hither these last days (Tengnagel, that is, set off from there for his homeland, to return hither, God willing, after some weeks), reported that Lord Barvitius had taken upon himself the furtherance of that business as soon as the citizen should come to Pilsen and put his case to him — which, however, had not yet been done before that servant of mine departed thence, who tarried there a whole month. I wonder, therefore, where that citizen was in the meantime; and whether he stayed on at Prague to await the Emperor's return thither, I do not know. For he spoke with me only once, at Prague; afterward I did not see the man, although I wished him to come to me again, so that I might more fully learn the circumstances of his case — which I also ordered him to set out to Franciscus. If ever I shall be able to oblige Your Excellency in other, far greater matters in these regions, you shall ever find me most ready and perpetually mindful of our friendship.

What I wished to convey to our common friend Holger Rosenkrantz, I do not know whether he received, for he has now sent me no letter for a long time. Let Your Excellency therefore inform me how he lives and fares, and remind him to write to me from time to time.

Farewell, long and happily.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 20th day of April, by the corrected style, of the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe at Benátky nad Jizerou.
  • Recipient. Vitus Winshemius = Veit Ortel von Windsheim (Vitus Winshemius the Younger) (1542–1608), physician and professor at Wittenberg — Tycho's correspondent and intermediary in the Saxon/Wittenberg academic world. Addressed throughout as Excellentia Tua, "Your Excellency".
  • Persons named.
  • Rollenhagiana[e literae] = letters of Georg Rollenhagen (1542–1609), rector at Magdeburg, enclosed with Winshemius's packet; Rollenhagen's own letter to Tycho of 15 May 1601 survives as brahe-t_16010515001.
  • Tengnaglius / Franciscus = Franz Gansneb Tengnagel von Camp, Tycho's noble assistant (and future son-in-law), here being sent to the imperial court at Pilsen and then travelling on to his Westphalian homeland.
  • Dominus Barvitius, summus Secretarius = Johann Anton Barvitius (Barwitz), imperial secretary to Rudolf II — Tycho's channel for promoting the petition of an unnamed Civis (citizen) whose cause Winshemius had commended.
  • Oligerus Rosaekrantzius = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), Danish nobleman and theologian, the "common friend" whose silence Tycho remarks.
  • Civis ille Hamburgensis / Civis = the unnamed Hamburg citizen who carried the post, and the (same or another) petitioner whose suit at court Tycho was advancing.
  • Places & events. Praga = Prague; Pilsna = Plzeň, where Rudolf II's court had withdrawn (cf. brahe-t_16000508001); the petitioner was to follow the Emperor there.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting"); Vale diu et feliciter → "Farewell, long and happily"; Dabantur (epistolary) → "Given."
  • Calendar (this chapter's convention — idiom of note). Dateline Die 20 Aprilis stylo recognito Anni 1600 — "by the corrected / reformed style," i.e. New Style (Gregorian). Stylo recognito is one of Tycho's variant labels for the Gregorian reckoning (alongside stylo novo) now standard in his Imperial-service correspondence; rendered literally and glossed, not silently converted.
  • Correction. Latin: 'cum is quodam' corrected to 'cum quodam', and 'reddererentur' corrected to 'redderentur', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 303.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). Our published the transcribed original inserts an extra pronoun "is" between "cum" and "quodam" that Dreyer's print does not have; it is also grammatically anomalous (nominative "is" cannot follow "cum" here). The translation's meaning is unaffected either way. Dreyer prints: ablegaturus TENGNAGLIUM nostrum, cum quodam ex meis domesticis, Pilsnam in Aulam Cæsaris (p.303, line 14-15; no "is").
- text error (low). Our published the transcribed original has a duplicated syllable ("reddererentur") not present in Dreyer's printed "redderentur" (imperfect subjunctive passive of reddo). A dittography in our transcription; the translation itself renders the correct sense regardless. Dreyer prints: eae mihi redderentur (p.303, line 15-16, split across the line break as "red-" / "derentur").

Repaired. Verified both defects by direct visual inspection of the Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.302-304 page images. Both corrections are unambiguous on p.303. Neither affects the English translation, which was already faithful to the correct sense. No other divergence from Dreyer was found in this letter.
