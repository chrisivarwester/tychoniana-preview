The original

SERENISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO D. FERDINANDO DE MEDICES, MAGNO DUCI HETRURIÆ, FLORENTIÆ, SENARUM, PISARUM, DOMINO MEO CLEMENTISSIMO.

SERENISSIME et Illustrissime Princeps, Domine Clementissime. Cum hîc Adolescens genere Nobilis et liberalibus disciplinis præclare imbutus, qui mihi per aliquot Annos fuit domesticus, in Italiam nunc cogitet, sitque Tuæ Serenitati ab Amplissimo et Nobilissimo Domino HENRICO RANZOVIO Produce Cimbrico Affini meo honorando, diligenter commendatus, non intermittendum duxi, quin et ego, licet Serenitati Tuæ ignotus, idem submisse præstarem, præsertim ex quo eius mores, integritas et docile ingenium mihi ex diutina consuetudine quam optime perspecta sint. Quocirca Serenitatem Tuam submisse rogatam habeo, ut eum vel meo etiam nomine, sibi de meliori (ut aiunt) nota commendatum habeat. Tradidi illi duos libros, simul ut commodius veherentur colligatos, Serenitati Tuæ ex me submisse offerendos: Quorum contenta Tituli et Præfationes expedient: Quos ut clementissimo vultu suscipere, meque suo favore et benevolentia complecti non dedignetur, qua possum submissione oro. Plura de me meisque studijs, atque ad Cæsaream Maiestatem e Dania Patria mea migratione ab eodem harum exhibitore (si cognoscere non degravetur) clementissime percipiet. Quocirca Serenitatem Tuam gravissimis Reipub. gubernandæ negocijs occupatam ulterius remorari desino. Deus Optimus maximus eam quam diutissime conservet florentem et incolumem.

Dabantur Dresdæ Misniæ die 8 Novembris Anni 1598.
Serenitati Tuæ
Submisse addictiss.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the Most Serene Prince and Lord, Lord Ferdinando de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany, of Florence, of Siena, of Pisa, my most clement Lord.

Most Serene and Most Illustrious Prince, most clement Lord. Since here a youth — noble by birth and excellently imbued with the liberal disciplines, who was my domestic for some years — now thinks of [going] into Italy, and has been diligently commended to Your Serenity by the most ample and most noble Master Heinrich Rantzau, Cimbrian Viceroy, my honoured kinsman, I judged it ought not to be omitted that I too — though unknown to Your Serenity — should humbly do the same, especially since his character, integrity, and docile talent have been most thoroughly known to me from long acquaintance. Wherefore I have humbly asked Your Serenity to hold him commended, in my name also, "with the better mark" (as they say). I have handed him two books — bound together, that they might be the more conveniently carried — to be humbly offered to Your Serenity from me, the contents of which the titles and prefaces will make plain; which [books] I beg, with what submission I can, that you not disdain to receive with most clement countenance, and to embrace me with your favour and benevolence. More about me and my studies, and about my migration from Denmark, my homeland, to the Imperial Majesty, you will most clemently learn from the same bearer of this letter (if you do not find it burdensome to learn it). Wherefore I cease to delay Your Serenity, occupied with the most weighty affairs of governing the commonwealth, any further. May God Best and Greatest preserve Your Serenity, flourishing and unharmed, as long as possible.

Given at Dresden in Meissen, the 8th day of November, in the year 1598.
Most humbly devoted to Your Serenity,
Tycho Brahe,
I wrote with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Ferdinando I de' Medici (1549–1609), Grand Duke of Tuscany (Florence, Siena, Pisa). A letter of recommendation and book-gift. The witness's provenance is notable: the autograph descended into the Galileo / Accademia del Cimento papers at the Biblioteca Nazionale in Florence (Gal. 286).
  • The young man recommended. "A youth, noble by birth … my domestic for some years," now going to Italy, already commended by Heinrich Rantzau (the Holstein humanist and Danish Statholder, here Produx Cimbricus = "Cimbrian Viceroy/Pro-duke," i.e. royal governor of the Cimbrian/Jutland–Holstein lands; Tycho's affinis). Identification flagged: most plausibly Frans Tengnagel (who travelled to Italy in this period and returned by early 1600 — cf. brahe-t_16000308002/…0309002), though Tycho does not name him.
  • The book-gift. duos libros … colligatos = two of Tycho's printed works, bound together for carriage, offered to the Grand Duke (their contents to be known from the titles and prefaces) — almost certainly the recently issued Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (1598) and/or Epistolae astronomicae (1596) / Progymnasmata. Identification flagged (the two are not titled).
  • de meliori nota — "with the better mark," i.e. in the most favourable terms (the same idiom as in brahe-t_15980530001).
  • Calendar. Dresdae Misniae die 8 Novembris 1598 = 8 November 1598, Dresden in Meissen (Lutheran Saxony) = Old Style. Header agrees with the label. (Tycho was wintering in Saxony — Dresden/Wittenberg — before his move to Bohemia.)
