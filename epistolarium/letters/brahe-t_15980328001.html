The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD VITUM VINSHEMIUM.

EXCELLENTISSIME Domine Doctor VINSHEMI, Amice honorande. Accepi nudius tertius ab OLIGERO nostro literas, in quibus facit circa calcem mentionem librorum, qui adhuc apud Dominum D. BŒKELIUM residui sunt, quos omnino obtinere desiderat, et æquum sane est, ut, ex quo pecuniam totam is receperit, libros omnes tradat, aut se ita apud OLIGERUM purget, ut sit contentus. Ego consanguineo meo et affini amicoque intimo deesse non possum, quin procurem id, quod a me requirit. Si sponte BŒKELIUS hoc præstare noluerit, cogor iure rem agere, utut id libenter non facerem. Rogo itaque peramanter tam OLIGERI quam meo nomine, velis adhuc semel apud ipsum urgere, ut stet promissis. Excusationes, quas ultimo adduxit, vix accipiet OLIGERUS, nam ille aliter ad me rettulit ea de re, imo VESALIJ Anatomiam mihi dono dedit, licet ego id recusarim, nisi alium ex meis libris æquivalentem eius loco habere vellet. Nec valor eius est maior sex vel septem Ioachimicorum, etiamsi de novo ematur. Fac itaque, mi Domine Doctor VINSHEMI, ut OLIGERI expectationi satisfiat, et schedas, quas hac de re ultimo accepisti, remitte. Scripturus enim sum hisce diebus OLIGERO et negotium hoc expositurus.

Vale, et si quæ nova scis, indica.
Raptim Wandesburgi die 28. Martij Anni 98.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Vitus Winshemius.

Most excellent Master Doctor Winshemius, my honoured friend. Two days ago I received a letter from our friend Oligerus [Holger], in which, towards the close, he makes mention of the books that still remain with Master Doctor Bökel — which he very much desires to obtain; and it is indeed only fair that, seeing he [Bökel] has received the whole sum of money, he should hand over all the books, or else so clear himself with Oligerus that he be satisfied. I cannot fail my kinsman and relative and most intimate friend, but must see to what he asks of me. If Bökel should be unwilling to do this of his own accord, I am compelled to pursue the matter at law, however little I should do so willingly. I therefore ask you most affectionately, in Oligerus's name as in my own, that you would be willing to press him once more to stand by his promises. The excuses he last brought forward Oligerus will scarcely accept, for he reported the matter to me otherwise — indeed he made me a gift of Vesalius's Anatomy, though I declined it, unless he would take in its place another, equivalent book of mine. Nor is its value greater than six or seven joachimsthalers, even if it be bought new. See to it, then, my Master Doctor Winshemius, that Oligerus's expectation be satisfied, and send back the papers which you last received on this matter. For I am about to write to Oligerus in these next days and set this business out [to him].

Farewell, and if you know any news, let me know.
In haste, at Wandsbek, on the 28th day of March, the year [15]98.

Apparatus

  • Sender / place. Tycho Brahe, writing in his self-imposed exile after leaving Denmark; Wandesburgi = Wandsbek (now part of Hamburg), where he stayed at the castle of Heinrich Rantzau from 1597 into 1598. Raptim ("in haste") is the standard hurried-subscription flourish.
  • Recipient. Vitus Vinshemius = Vitus Winshemius (Veit Oertel von Windsheim) — here the younger of that name (the elder humanist Veit Oertel, 1501–1570, was long dead), professor of medicine at Wittenberg, acting as Holger Rosenkrantz's intermediary in the dispute with Bökel.
  • Oligerus noster = Holger Rosenkrantz (1574–1642), "the Learned," the Danish nobleman and scholar, then a young man on his peregrinatio. Tycho calls him consanguineus meus et affinis amicusque intimus — "my kinsman and relative and most intimate friend"; the Brahe and Rosenkrantz families were indeed connected by blood and marriage. The edition's neighbouring letters confirm the identification (a 7 March 1599 letter is headed Oligerus Rosenkrantz ad Tychonem Brahe).
  • D. Boekelius = Johann Bökel (Johannes Bocatius / Boccatius, 1535–1605), the prominent Hamburg town physician and medical writer. The œ-ligature spelling BOeKELIUM / BOeKELIUS of the copy is preserved in the Source block.
  • Vesalij Anatomia — Andreas Vesalius's De humani corporis fabrica (or an Epitome of it); referred to generically as "Vesalius's Anatomy." Bökel had pressed the volume on Tycho as a gift, which Tycho declined except as an even exchange.
  • Ioachimicorum — joachimsthalers (Joachimsthaler, the silver coin struck at Joachimsthal, ancestor of the "thaler/dollar"); "six or seven joachimsthalers" gives the book's market value. Kept as the period coin-name and glossed.
  • schedas — "papers / slips," the documents (notes, accounts, or correspondence) bearing on the dispute that Winshemius had been sent and is asked to return; rendered "papers."
  • cogor iure rem agere — "I am compelled to pursue the matter at law": iure agere is the legal idiom for bringing a formal/judicial action. Tycho's hedge utut id libenter non facerem ("however little I should do so willingly") is preserved, not sharpened into a bare threat.
  • nudius tertius — the classical idiom "two days ago" (literally "now the third day [counting inclusively]"); rendered "two days ago."
  • Calendar. No Roman-calendar idiom in the dateline: die 28. Martij Anni 98 = 28 March 1598. Denmark and Tycho used the Julian (Old Style) calendar; marked [O.S.]. The header date matches the source dateline; the edition label ("1598. 28. marts. Wandsburg") agrees — no discrepancy.
  • Witness. Survives only in a later transcript (Afskrift) in Langebek's Diplomatarium, Rigsarkivet, Copenhagen; spellings (the œ-ligatures, the all-capitals proper names) follow that copy as printed by Dreyer.
