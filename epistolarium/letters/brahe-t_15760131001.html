The original

IOHANNES PRATENSIS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.
SALUTEM ET OMNIA FAUSTA.

RECEPI literas tuas, TYCHO optime, et pyxidem obsignatam cum pecunia, quam postera statim die Rostochium misi; qui tulit, chirographum quitantiarium referet: cui quoque negotium dedi de vase tuo huc perferendo. Ubi venerit, diligenter curabo. Quam vellem te apud nos hîc esse: desiderio enim tui tangimur non parvo. Avet animus cognoscere ex te, quid rei in mundo agatur, quem nuper aliqua ex parte perlustrasti. Sumus hîc extra cœli Solisque vias, scenam mundanæ fabulæ vix audimus quidem, tantum abest ut spectatores simus. Sed fuerit hoc fatum Aquiloni debitum. Tu nuper reversus a fœcundo illo Austro, postquam nobis cœli aperuisses penetralia, prius inaccessa ignotaque, moxque nobis inscijs et inopinantibus in Austrum dilapsum sub mediam brumam, totam deinde perlustrasti Teutoniam, atque Italiæ vicinas oras, cumque viris doctis atque ingentibus ingentes et doctos miscuisti sermones: quæ tua semper fuit voluptas. Sed vereor, ne hi una cum iucundo placidoque Austro te istuc alliciant, ut tacite eo mediteris iter. Sic enim Genius mihi in somnis dictavit, susurrans, te Basilæam præ alijs Germaniæ urbibus exoptare vicinam Italis atque Gallis, ubi magnus ille ERASMUS olim floruit, et multi etiamnum eximij viri clarent. At mallem te, modo amplus ille tuus sic restringi posset Genius, nobiscum in hac manere patria, et rigidos Boreæ flatus una persentiscere, ac quantum in te est emollire, fi non prorsus avertere: aut saltem ut huius intemperiei socium patientem in te habeamus. Sed si omnino nos relinquere stat sententia, uti etiam forte meretur hæc tuo Genio atque ingenio minus apta intemperataque aura, et importuna conversatio: da saltem adhuc insigne aliquod tuarum doctrinarum specimen: quale id fuit, cum te ultimo a nobis inexpectantibus subducens reliquisti. Fecisti autem hoc (quod laudo, bonique omnes prædicant) in Regis, qui sic expetebat, gratiam, quod nemo ex illustri nobilitate Danica ante te præstitit unquam, nec, uti existimo, quispiam in posterum facturus, adeo ut tute primus et postremus sis, qui hanc laudis adoream tibi vendicaris, quibus istæ, quas tui ordinis homines affectant, gloriolæ comparari nequeunt. Si itaque hoc meritum priori adjunxeris, ut antequam te carituri simus (vereor enim ne id fiat, licet tu propositum nos celes) nobis plura adhuc explanare non degravatus fueris, nos beatiores quam antea reddideris, tibique adhuc plus devinxeris. Et si id commodum tibi non fuerit, saltem de Hypothesibus PTOLEMÆI et COPERNICI nos summatim instrue, quænam harum verior sit, et utrum ulli earum acquiescendum, an vero potius Ptolemaica assumptio sit castiganda, prout a te factum est, et Copernicea ad stabilitatem Terræ convertenda, uti etiam insinuasti tuoque sic nobis præluxisti ingenio: aut si quid aliud in his secretioris cognitionis, quod nos fugiat, animo volvis, impertiaris quæso. Quin et hoc ex te scire aveo, idque ego solus, utpote qui in prælectione publica II libri PLINIJ de mundo circa hunc nodum resolvendum non parum hæserim: quomodo videlicet intelligendum sit, quod dictus autor in fine XVI cap. ea, quæ in Stationibus et Regressibus Planetarum videmus per inhibitionem et repercussionem radiorum Solarium fieri, ubi epicyclos omnes tollere videtur, ac si veteribus in usu non fuissent, mirabili sane invento, a se, uti tandem insinuare videtur, primum comperto, si modo res hæc ipsi cœlo congrua sit, et suis numeris constare possit, quod tibi per otium dispiciendum relinquo. Locus, quem indicavi, te de pluribus admonebit. Haberem et plura eius generis, de quibus avide tecum conferre cuperem, et nisi hyems et invalitudo ob continuas in pulmonem defluxiones mihi obstaret, ad vos in Scaniam transirem, atque ibi tecum, quæ nostra est voluptas, aliquandiu suaviter philosopharer. Sed, o spes hominum fluxæ et irritæ. Cogimur sæpe esse ubi nolumus, et rapimur eo, quo non ire deliberavimus. Verum optime mi TYCHO, amplius detinere te nolo. Tu fac nos hîc Haffniæ brevi tua præsentia exhilares, atque ingeniorum nostrorum radios, iam lumen remittentes, et in crepusculum tendentes vespertinum, illustres ac vegetiores reddas, animisque nostris sacer, uti soles, illabaris.

Iamque vale, et fratrem tuum, aliosque qui tecum sunt, sanguine iunctos ex me officiose saluta.
Dabantur Haffniæ ult. die Ianuarij Anno 1576.
T.
IOH. PRATENSIS.

Johannes Pratensis to Tycho Brahe. Greeting, and all good fortune.

I have received your letter, excellent Tycho, and the sealed box with the money, which I sent on to Rostock the very next day; the man who carried it will bring back a receipt for the quittance — to him I also gave the charge of conveying your vessel here. When it arrives, I shall see to it carefully. How I wish you were here with us: for we are touched by no small longing for you. My mind is eager to learn from you what is going on in the world [in mundo], which you have lately surveyed in some part. We are here outside the paths of the heaven and the Sun; we scarcely so much as hear the scene of the world's play — so far are we from being its spectators. But let this be the fate owed to the North. You, lately returned from that fruitful South, after you had opened to us the inner recesses of the heaven, before inaccessible and unknown — and then, while we were unaware and unsuspecting, having slipped away into the South in mid-winter — went on to traverse all Germany [Teutonia] and the neighbouring shores of Italy, and with men learned and great mingled great and learned discourse: which was ever your delight. But I fear lest these, together with the pleasant and mild South, lure you thither, so that you secretly plan a journey there. For thus a Genius dictated to me in my dreams, whispering that you long, before other cities of Germany, for Basel — close to the Italians and the Gauls — where that great Erasmus once flourished, and where many distinguished men are still illustrious today. Yet I would rather — if only that ample Genius of yours could be so reined in — that you remain with us in this homeland, and feel together with us the harsh blasts of Boreas, and, so far as in you lies, soften them, if not turn them away entirely; or at least that we may have in you a patient companion in this inclemency. But if it is your fixed resolve to leave us altogether — as perhaps this air, ill-suited and intemperate for your Genius and disposition, and this troublesome society, deserves — then give us at least some further notable specimen of your learning, such as that was when, withdrawing yourself from us last time when we did not expect it, you took your leave. This you did (a thing I praise, and which all good men proclaim) for the sake of the King, who so desired it — a thing no one of the illustrious Danish nobility ever rendered before you, nor, as I judge, will any do hereafter, so that you are at once the first and the last to claim this glory of praise for yourself, with which those little glories that men of your order pursue cannot be compared. If, therefore, you will add this service to the former — that, before we must do without you (for I fear that this will come to pass, though you conceal your intention from us), you will not think it a burden to expound still more to us — you will render us happier than before, and bind us yet more to yourself. And if that be not convenient to you, then at least instruct us in summary on the Hypotheses of Ptolemy and of Copernicus: which of these is the truer, and whether one ought to rest in either of them, or whether rather the Ptolemaic assumption is to be corrected — as has been done by you — and the Copernican turned back to the stability of the Earth, as you have also intimated and have by your genius thus lighted the way for us; or, if you turn over in your mind anything else of more secret knowledge in these matters that escapes us, share it, I beg. Moreover, this too I am eager to learn from you — and I alone — inasmuch as in my public lecture on the second Book of Pliny on the world I have stuck not a little over untying this knot: how, namely, it is to be understood that the said author, at the end of chapter XVI, [says] that those things which we see in the Stations and Retrogradations of the planets come about through the inhibition and repercussion of the Sun's rays — where he seems to do away with all epicycles, as though they had not been in use among the ancients — by a truly marvellous discovery first found out by himself, as he at length seems to intimate (if only this matter be congruous with the heaven itself, and can be made to agree with its own numbers): which I leave to you to examine at your leisure. The passage I have indicated will admonish you of further matters. I should have many more things of this kind too, about which I would eagerly desire to confer with you; and were not the winter and my ill-health — owing to continual catarrhs flowing down upon the lung — in my way, I would cross over to you into Scania, and there with you, which is our delight, philosophize agreeably for a while. But — O the fleeting and idle hopes of men! We are often compelled to be where we would not, and are dragged off whither we had resolved not to go. But, my excellent Tycho, I do not wish to detain you longer. Do you make us here at Copenhagen glad before long by your presence, and the rays of our wits — now sending back their light and tending toward the evening twilight — render bright and more vigorous, and glide, hallowed, into our spirits, as you are wont to do.

And now farewell, and dutifully greet from me your brother and the others who are with you, joined to you by blood.
Given at Copenhagen, the last day of January, the year 1576.
Yours,
Joh. Pratensis.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iohannes Pratensis = Johannes Pratensis (Hans Philipsen, 1543–1576), professor of medicine at Copenhagen and one of Tycho's closest friends; a Paracelsian physician in the circle of Petrus Severinus. He would die suddenly later in 1576. The letter's anxiety that Tycho may leave Denmark is poignant against that backdrop.
  • Recipient/dating context. Written 31 January 1576, shortly before Frederik II's grant of Hven (gift made 18 February 1576 N.S. / O.S. discussion aside) — Pratensis still fears Tycho will emigrate; the worry is real, not formulaic.
  • pyxidem obsignatam … vase tuo — "the sealed box … your vessel": Pratensis is forwarding money to Rostock on Tycho's behalf and arranging carriage of a vas (an alchemical/chemical vessel, given Tycho's and Pratensis's shared Paracelsian chemistry). chirographum quitantiarium = a written quittance/receipt; kept literal.
  • Geography of the metaphor. Aquilo / Boreas = the North (Denmark) and its cold winds; Auster = the South (Germany/Italy), figured as "fruitful" and "mild." Teutonia = Germany; Scania = Skåne/Scania (where Tycho's family seat Knudstrup lay). The whole passage casts Denmark as offstage, "outside the paths of the heaven and the Sun" — the rhetorical conceit of provincial darkness.
  • Genius — rendered "Genius" (the tutelary/inner spirit, twice: the dream-Genius that whispers, and Tycho's own "ample Genius"); kept as a period term, not flattened to "genius" in the modern sense.
  • Basilaeam … magnus ille ERASMUS — Basel (where Erasmus of Rotterdam had lived and where his press flourished); Pratensis imagines Tycho drawn there for its nearness to Italy and France and its humanist lustre.
  • Astronomy — the Ptolemy/Copernicus request. de Hypothesibus PTOLEMAeI et COPERNICI … Ptolemaica assumptio sit castiganda … et Copernicea ad stabilitatem Terrae convertenda — Pratensis asks Tycho to adjudicate the two hypotheses (geometric world-models): whether to correct ("castigate") the Ptolemaic assumptio and to turn the Copernican system back to the stability of the Earth. This is an early external witness — pre-dating the Hven instruments and the De mundi (1588) — that Tycho was already intimating a reformed, geo-stable model: "as has been done by you … as you have also intimated." Read with care: Pratensis reports Tycho's insinuation, not a finished system; the hedges (insinuasti, praeluxisti) are preserved.
  • The Pliny crux. in praelectione publica II libri PLINIJ de mundo … in fine XVI cap. … Stationibus et Regressibus Planetarum … per inhibitionem et repercussionem radiorum Solarium … ubi epicyclos omnes tollere videtur — Pratensis, lecturing publicly on Pliny, Natural History Book II (the cosmological book, "de mundo"), is stuck on a passage he places "at the end of chapter XVI," where Pliny explains the planets' stations and retrogradations as caused by the inhibition and repercussion of the Sun's rays, seeming thereby to dispense with epicycles entirely and to claim the explanation as his own discovery. (The relevant Plinian matter is in the chapters on planetary motion, NH II §§69–77 in modern numbering; Pratensis's "chapter XVI" follows the chapter-division of his 16th-century edition and is not silently renumbered here — see calendar/citation note.) nodum resolvendum = "untying this knot," the interpretive difficulty; suis numeris constare = "agree with its own numbers" (be quantitatively consistent). Rendered with Pliny's own causal language ("inhibition and repercussion of the Sun's rays") kept intact.
  • radios … in crepusculum tendentes vespertinum — "the rays [of our wits] tending toward the evening twilight": the closing conceit that without Tycho the Copenhagen scholars' intellects are guttering toward dusk; crepusculum = twilight (glossary). animis nostris sacer … illabaris — "glide, hallowed, into our spirits," a near-sacral compliment.
  • Orthography. Humanist Latin of c. 1576: coeli, foecundo, Ptolemaeī with the ae-ligature; ij for ii (alijs, eximij, inscijs, PLINIJ); fi non prorsus avertere — the printed fi is a long-s misreading for si ("if not"), kept as printed in the Source block and read si in the translation (it parses only as si, parallel to the later si … non fuerit). No negation is added or dropped by this reading.
  • Dateline. Dabantur Haffniae ult. die Ianuarij Anno 1576 = "Given at Copenhagen, the last day of January, the year 1576" — i.e. 31 January 1576 (O.S.; Denmark was on the Julian calendar). The catalogue label ("1576. 31. januar. København") agrees with the dateline. Haffnia = Copenhagen (glossary). The subscription T. before the name is the writer's flourish (cf. exemplar usage), left as "Yours,".
