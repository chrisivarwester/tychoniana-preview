The original

HENRICUS BRUCÆUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.
S. P.

PRODIJT catalogus librorum Francfurti ad Mœnum venalium, inter ceteros hunc invenio: Observatio et demonstratio Cometæ ætherei, qui anno 1577 et 1578 constitutus in sphæra ♀ apparuit, autore M. MICHAËLE MÆSTLINO, Tubingæ apud GEORGIUM GROPENBACHIUM. Hunc nondum videre contigit, quod si ad manus meas pervenerit, curabo, ut ad te mittatur, vel eo potissimum nomine, ut si forte eam methodum, easque rationes et observandi et demonstrandi sequeretur, quas tu approbas, ut tibi constet, in quibus, et quantum eis tribuendum iudices et in quibus dissentias. Porro cum multi in eodem genere laborârint, ut hinc inde edita scripta testantur, sumeret sibi forte quis laborem aliorum sententiam refellendi, id quod mihi minime probatur, cum rei propositæ vera demonstratione ex Geometricis fundamentis eruta, et observationum certitudinis confirmatione, abunde secus sentientes confutentur. Tu interim in tuis observationibus elabora et profer in lucem, quod pluteum cædat et demorsos sapiat ungues.

Nostras observationes non mitto. Cum enim eas ad Geometriam, tanquam ad Lydium lapidem revocarem, et animadverterem, aliquid in ijs desiderari, abieci aliquo, ut ipse nesciam ubi lateant. Hoc tamen satis recordor me nullam sensibilem parallaxin hinc colligere potuisse, sed calculum potius in contrarium abijsse, id, quod miratus sum, reluctante nostro ARISTOTELE.

Tu interim bene vale, et me mutuo ama.
Rostochij IV. Decem.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Heinrich Brucaeus to Tycho Brahe.
Heartiest greeting.

A catalogue of the books for sale at Frankfurt am Main has come out, and among the rest I find this one: Observation and Demonstration of the Aetherial Comet, which, established in the sphere of ♀ Venus, appeared in the year 1577 and 1578, by the author Master Michael Maestlin, at Tübingen at the press of Georg Gruppenbach. It has not yet fallen to me to see it; but if it comes into my hands, I will see to it that it is sent on to you — chiefly for this reason, that if perhaps he were to follow that method, and those principles both of observing and of demonstrating, which you approve, it may be clear to you in which points, and how much you judge is to be allowed to them, and in which you disagree. Furthermore, since many have laboured in the same kind, as the writings published here and there bear witness, someone might perhaps take upon himself the labour of refuting the opinion of others — a thing of which I by no means approve, since, once the true demonstration of the matter proposed has been drawn out from geometrical foundations, and confirmed by the certainty of observations, those who think otherwise are abundantly confuted [thereby]. You, meanwhile, work away at your own observations, and bring forth into the light something that may be carved on the bookcase and savour of nails bitten [to the quick].

Our own observations I do not send. For when I called them back to Geometry, as to a touchstone, and noticed that something was wanting in them, I threw them aside somewhere — so that I myself do not know where they lie hidden. Yet this much I well recall: that I was unable to gather any perceptible parallax from them, but rather the calculation went off to the contrary — at which I marvelled, our Aristotle resisting [it].

You, meanwhile, fare you well, and love me in return.
At Rostock, 4 December.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (also Brucäus; 1531–1593), Flemish-born professor of mathematics and medicine at the University of Rostock and Tycho's former teacher there; a regular correspondent. The Mosley biography (Bearing the Heavens) places this letter precisely: "towards the end of 1578 he had written that, having noticed Michael Maestlin's recent cometary [treatise in the fair-catalogue, he would forward it]."
  • Salutation. S. P. = salutem plurimam (dicit), "(sends) heartiest greeting".
  • The book. Observatio et demonstratio Cometae aetherei … autore M. Michaële Maestlino, Tubingae apud Georgium Gropenbachium = Michael Maestlin's Observatio et demonstratio cometae aetherei, qui anno 1577 et 1578 … apparuit (Tübingen: Georg Gruppenbach [Latin Gropenbachius], 1578). M. before the name = Magister ("Master," academic; this chapter's convention). The title is given as a quotation of the fair-catalogue entry; rendered in English with the Latin work-title-sense preserved. Maestlin = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), the Tübingen astronomer (later Kepler's teacher), who like Tycho treated the 1577 comet as supralunary, but did so within a Copernican frame, associating the comet with the sphere of Venus — exactly the point of the in sphaera ♀ phrase.
  • Symbol. in sphaera ♀ — the planetary glyph for Venus; named in the translation, glyph kept in the source block.
  • catalogus librorum Francfurti ad Moenum venalium — the printed catalogue of books offered for sale at the Frankfurt am Main book fair (the Messkatalog), Brucaeus's source for the new title.
  • Astronomy / technical. parallaxin = parallax: Brucaeus reports that his own reduction of his comet observations yielded no perceptible parallax — i.e. pointed to a supralunary (aetherial), not a sublunary, comet. ad Geometriam, tanquam ad Lydium lapidem revocarem — "I called them back to Geometry, as to a touchstone" (the Lydius lapis, the Lydian stone used to assay gold; a stock metaphor for a decisive test); kept and glossed. vera demonstratione ex Geometricis fundamentis eruta — "the true demonstration drawn out from geometrical foundations," Brucaeus's (and Tycho's) methodological creed: a sound geometrical-observational demonstration confutes opponents without polemic.
  • reluctante nostro Aristotele — "our Aristotle resisting [it]": the no-parallax result contradicted Aristotelian physics, in which comets are sublunary meteorological phenomena (and would therefore show parallax). "Our Aristotle" marks Brucaeus's own Aristotelian allegiance; he registers surprise (miratus sum) that his calculation ran against the doctrine. The participle is left as an ablative absolute ("Aristotle resisting") rather than smoothed.
  • Allusion. profer in lucem, quod pluteum caedat et demorsos sapiat ungues — a learned Horatian allusion (cf. Satires I.10.70–71, on painstaking composition: the poet who "scratches his head and bites his nails to the quick"; pluteus = the writing-desk/bookrest, also the board scored in revision). The sense: produce something so finely worked that it bears the marks of laborious, polished craftsmanship. Rendered close to the Latin ("that may be carved on the bookcase and savour of nails bitten [to the quick]") with the figure preserved rather than paraphrased away; [to the quick] bracketed as an interpretive completion of demorsos.
  • Subscription. Tu interim bene vale, et me mutuo ama = "You, meanwhile, fare you well [bene vale, this chapter's convention], and love me in return" — a standard humanist epistolary close.
  • Place. Rostochij = Rostock, the seat of the university and of Brucaeus.
  • Calendar. IV. Decem. = 4 December, given without year in the dateline; the edition label supplies 1578, consistent with the Frankfurt-fair / Maestlin-1578 content. Denmark and the Empire were on the Julian calendar (Old Style) in 1578 (the Gregorian reform came in 1582); dated O.S. accordingly. No year is asserted in the header beyond what the catalogue date and internal content (the 1578 Maestlin imprint) substantiate.
  • MS witness — flag. The edition records witness A as cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66 — the same shelfmark/folio carried for the 1571 Wolf letter in this corpus. This is very likely a edition propagation artifact rather than a true codicological fact (two distinct letters cannot occupy the identical leaf). The shelfmark is reported here exactly as given, but should be verified against Dreyer's apparatus before citation; not silently corrected.
