The original

DEM VESTEN/ VNSERM LIEBEN BESONDERN / TYCHONI BRAHE, HERRN ZU KNUDSTRUP.
WILHELM VON GOTTES GNADEN/ LANDTGRAUE ZU HESSEN/ GRAUE ZU CATZENELNBOGEN/ ETC.

VNSERN gnedigen Gruß zuvor/ Vester lieber Besonder. Wir haben ewer Schreiben de dato Vraniburg, den 20. Februarij, wol empfangen vnd gelesen. So viel nu erstlich die von vns begerete Elendsthier belangt/ thun wir vns der angewendten mühe vnd bestellung/ so ihr deßhalben gethan/ gegen euch in Gnaden bedancken/ Vnd mögen euch hiermit nicht verhalten/ daß wir vor dreyen Monaten vier Elende auß Schweden bekommen/ deroselben vns aber albereit drey gestorben/ daß wir also von denselben nur eine junge Kuhe/ vnd dan sonst noch einen Elendsochsen/ so wir vor zweyen Jaren auß Schweden bekommen/ haben. Die Vrsach aber/ daß sie also weg sterben/ halten wirs dafür/ daß es dahero komme/ weyl der liebe Gott vorm Jar so ein statliche Mast bescheret/ daß auch die Eycheln sehr dicke auff einander gelegen/ vnd faul worden seyn/ daß sie derselben etwan zu viel gessen haben mögen/ daß inen endtlich die feulung im Leibe gemacht/ vnd daran sterben müssen/ Inmassen vns dan ein gute anzahl Reher/ deßgleichen 19. Gembse/ so wir vorm Jar aussem Landt zu Beyern bringen/ vnd gleicher gestalt in vnserm Thiergarten zur Zapffenburg thun lassen/ auch kurtz auff einander gestorben seyn. Woferne jr vns nu zu dem noch vbrigen Elendsochsen/ ein par starcker Elendskuhe von zweyen oder dreyen Jaren bey gegenwertigem herausser schicken kundtet/ geschehe vns daran zu gantz angenemen gefallen/ Wollen es auch vmb euch in Gnaden widerumb zubeschulden vnuergessen seyn/ dan sonderlich der eine Elendsochse/ den wir nu baldt zwey Jar gehabt/ sich gerne paren wolte/ vnd sich in der brunst gantz vnfletig erzeigen thut.

Was dan die jüngst im verschienen Decembri geschehene Eclipsin Lunæ vnd deroselben Obseruation betrifft/ ist es an dem/ daß wir dieselbige in abwesen vnsers Mathematici ROTHMANNI (der sich dan seiner noch jmmerwehrenden Schwachheit halben bey vns nicht wider eingestellt) nicht obseruiret haben/ Sondern/ inmassen wie jr dan in beyliegendt zu sehen/ wie sie von vnserm Mathematico, den wir newlicher Tage auff vnsere Vniuersitet Marpurg bestellet haben/ auffgerissen worden.

Sonst mögen wir euch nicht verhalten/ daß die Römische Keyserliche Maiestet vor etlichen Tagen an vns allergnedigst geschrieben/ vnd begeret/ daß wir jrer Keyserlichen Maiestet vnsern Vhrmacher Iosten Byrgi mit dem Globo, den er hiebevor gemacht/ vnd darauff die Motus Planetarum eingebracht hat/ zuschicken wolten. Welches wir dan jrer Keyserlichen Maiestet nicht verwegern mögen/ vnd seyndt demnach gemeynt/ gedachten vnsern Vhrmachern erstes Tages zu jrer Keyserlichen Maiestet abzufertigen/ doch daß er sich förderlich widerumb bey vns einstellen solle/ wie wir vns dan versehen/ er auff künfftigen Michaelis widerumb bey vns seyn werde.

Vnd ob er euch wol den begerten Goldtschmidts gesellen/ so mit den Automatis vnd Horologijs etlicher massen vmbzugehen wisse/ gerne zuweisen wolte/ so ist doch deroselbigen jetziger zeit keiner allhier anzutreffen/ Es soll sich aber er vnser Vhrmacher auff seiner reyse nach einem solchen Goldtschmidts gesellen vmhoren/ vnd da er denselbigen antreffen wirdt/ alßdan mit jm handlen vnd euch zuweisen.

Was dan vnser Sohn Landtgraff MORITZ an euch schreiben thut/ vnd in Commendationem ewers Operis Astronomici gemacht/ daß habt jr hierneben zu empfangen/ vnd begeren gnediglich/ wan solch Opus gar versertigt/ jr wollet vns desselbigen ein Exemplar zuschicken.

Wolten wir euch hinwider gnediglich diß nicht verhalten. Vnd seyndt euch mit gnedigem Willen wol geneigt.

Datumin vnser Stadt vnd VestungCassel/ am 2. Aprilis, Anno 1592.
WILHELM, LANDGRAFF ZU HESSEN.

English translation machine translation, Probable

To the Steadfast, Our trusty and well-beloved, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup.
Wilhelm, by the grace of God, Landgrave of Hesse, Count of Katzenelnbogen, etc.

Our gracious greeting beforehand. Trusty and well-beloved. We have duly received and read your letter dated Uraniborg, the 20th of February. Now, so far as first concerns the elk that we requested of you, we thank you graciously for the pains and procurement that you have undertaken on that account; and we may not conceal from you herewith that three months ago we obtained four elk out of Sweden, of which, however, three have already died on us, so that of those we have only one young cow, and then otherwise one elk-ox besides, which we obtained out of Sweden two years ago. As for the cause that they die off thus, we hold it to be this: that, because the dear God last year sent so plentiful a mast that the acorns lay very thickly upon one another and turned rotten, they may perhaps have eaten too much of them, which finally bred the rot in their bodies, and they must die of it — even as a good number of roe-deer of ours, likewise 19 chamois, which we brought last year out of the land of Bavaria and had placed in the same manner in our game-park at Zapfenburg, have also died off in quick succession. If now, alongside the elk-ox still remaining to us, you could send us out a pair of strong elk-cows of two or three years' age by the present [bearer], it would be done us as a quite welcome pleasure; and we will be unforgetful to requite it graciously toward you in turn — for in particular the one elk-ox, which we have now had for nearly two years, would gladly mate, and in the rut shows itself altogether unruly.

Now as for the eclipse of the Moon that lately occurred this past December, and the observation of it: the case is that we did not observe it ourselves, in the absence of our mathematician Rothmann (who, on account of his still-continuing infirmity, has not yet presented himself again with us), but, just as you may see in the enclosure, [it has been recorded] as it was sketched out by our mathematician whom we have these recent days appointed to our University of Marburg.

Furthermore, we may not conceal from you that the Roman Imperial Majesty some days ago wrote to us most graciously and desired that we should send to His Imperial Majesty our clockmaker Jost Bürgi, together with the globe that he made some while ago and upon which he has brought in the motions of the planets [motus planetarum]. This we may not refuse His Imperial Majesty, and we are accordingly minded to dispatch our said clockmaker to His Imperial Majesty at the first day [i.e. without delay], yet on condition that he is to present himself again with us promptly, as we expect that he will be back with us again by next Michaelmas.

And although he [Bürgi] would gladly have assigned to you the goldsmith's journeyman you requested, one who knows in some measure how to handle automata and clocks [automata et horologia], yet none such is at present to be found here; but our clockmaker shall, on his journey, make inquiry after such a goldsmith's journeyman, and, when he meets with one, shall then treat with him and assign him to you.

Then, as to what our son, Landgrave Moritz, writes to you, and has composed in commendation of your astronomical work [in commendationem operis astronomici], that you have to receive herewith; and we graciously desire that, when such work is quite completed, you will send us a copy of it.

This we would graciously not conceal from you in return. And we are well disposed toward you with gracious goodwill.

Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on the 2nd of April, in the year 1592.
Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Wilhelm von Gottes Gnaden, Landgraue zu Hessen = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), "Wilhelm the Wise," the astronomer-prince of Kassel and Tycho's chief correspondent among the German rulers; this is among his last letters to Tycho (he died in August 1592). The titulature is given in full and the princely Wir/euch register preserved.
  • Recipient address. Dem Vesten, vnserm lieben Besondern, Tychoni Brahe, Herrn zu Knudstrup — "To the Steadfast, Our trusty and well-beloved, Tycho Brahe, Lord of Knudstrup." Vest(er) ("steadfast, firm") is the standard chancery honorific for a nobleman; vnser lieber Besonder(er) is the fixed princely address-formula rendered by this chapter's convention ("Our trusty and well-beloved"). Herr zu Knudstrup names Tycho's Scanian family seat.
  • Tycho's incoming letter. ewer Schreiben de dato Vraniburg, den 20. Februarij — Tycho's letter "dated Uraniborg, the 20th of February" [1592, O.S.], here acknowledged. de dato is a Latin chancery tag left in place.
  • The elk (Elende / Elendsthier / Elendsochse / Elendskuhe). Early New High German Elen(d) = the elk / European moose (Alces alces); Elendsthier "elk-beast," Elendsochse "elk-ox (bull)," Elendskuhe "elk-cow." The two princes traded live elk for their game-parks; Wilhelm here reports a die-off and asks Tycho to procure more from Sweden via the bearer. Mast = the autumn fall of acorns/beech-mast on which the animals fed; the Landgrave attributes the deaths to rotten acorns. Reher = roe-deer; Gembse = chamois; Beyern = Bavaria. Zapffenburg = the Landgrave's game-park at Zapfenburg (near Spangenberg, Hesse). brunst = the rut; vnfletig here = "unruly, uncleanly/violent" (in rutting behaviour). The animal-husbandry register is kept plain, not modernised.
  • The lunar eclipse. die jüngst im verschienen Decembri geschehene Eclipsin Lunae = "the eclipse of the Moon that lately occurred this past December" — the lunar eclipse of 9/19 December 1591. Eclipsin Lunae, Obseruation, Mathematici, obseruiret, auffgerissen ("sketched/drawn out," of the observation diagram) — the technical nouns code-switch into Latin within the German, by this chapter's convention. beyliegendt = the enclosure (the observation sent with the letter).
  • Christoph Rothmann. vnsers Mathematici Rothmanni = Christoph Rothmann, the Kassel court mathematician and Tycho's correspondent, here reported absent through his jmmerwehrende Schwachheit ("still-continuing infirmity"); he had left Kassel and did not return to court service. His unnamed successor is vnser Mathematicus, den wir … auff vnsere Vniuersitet Marpurg bestellet haben — newly appointed to the University of Marburg (the Hessian university).
  • The Emperor and Jost Bürgi. die Römische Keyserliche Maiestet = Emperor Rudolf II (Holy Roman Emperor, r. 1576–1612), styled "the Roman Imperial Majesty." vnser Vhrmacher Iosten Byrgi = Jost Bürgi (1552–1632), the Kassel court clockmaker and instrument-maker (and independent inventor of logarithms/prosthaphaeresis-style computation). The Globus … darauff die Motus Planetarum eingebracht is Bürgi's celebrated mechanized planetary globe (a clockwork-driven globe showing the motus planetarum, "motions of the planets"). Michaelis = Michaelmas (29 September), the term by which Bürgi is expected back. — erstes Tages rendered "at the first day," i.e. without delay.
  • Goldsmith's journeyman; automata and clocks. Goldtschmidts gesellen … so mit den Automatis vnd Horologijs etlicher massen vmbzugehen wisse — a journeyman goldsmith able "in some measure to handle automata and clocks" (automata et horologia), whom Tycho had requested for his own Uraniborg workshop; the Latin technical nouns are kept and glossed.
  • Landgrave Moritz's commendatory poem. vnser Sohn Landtgraff Moritz … in Commendationem ewers Operis Astronomici gemacht = Moritz, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1572–1632, "Moritz the Learned"), Wilhelm's son and successor, who has written verses in commendationem operis astronomici ("in commendation of [Tycho's] astronomical work"). The Opus Astronomicum whose completed copy Wilhelm asks for is most plausibly the volume Tycho was then assembling — the Epistolae astronomicae / Progymnasmata programme — but the letter does not name the title; the referent is left as written, not specified. The clause daß habt jr hierneben zu empfangen indicates the poem was enclosed.
  • Calendar / place. Datum in vnser Stadt vnd Vestung Cassel, am 2. Aprilis, Anno 1592 = "Given in our city and fortress of Kassel, on the 2nd of April, the year 1592" (O.S.; Hesse-Kassel, a Lutheran territory, kept the Julian calendar). The header date (2 April 1592) is the dateline as written and agrees with the catalogue label. Datum (the chancery dating-verb) rendered "Given."
  • Edition note. This letter is printed in Tycho's own Epistolarum astronomicarum libri (Uraniborg, 1596; reprinted in Dreyer, Opera Omnia VI). No manuscript witness is recorded in the edition (witnesses:[]).
  • Source typography. The printed run-together forms Datumin, VestungCassel (missing word-spaces at line breaks in the source) are reproduced verbatim in the Source block and silently word-spaced in the translation.
