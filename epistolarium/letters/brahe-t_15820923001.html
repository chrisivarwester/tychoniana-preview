The original

TYCHO BRAHE THADDÆO AB HAYCK S.

REDDIDIT mihi ante aliquot septimanas Gœrlicius quidam Mercator literas tuas, clarissime Domine D. THADDÆE, amice carissime, ex quibus præter singularem tuam et constantem erga nos benevolentiam intellexi etiam priores meas ad tuas manus secus ac suspicabar pervenisse. Unde WITTICHIUM satis ea in re nunc excusatum habeo. Saltem id admiror, quod nihil literarum a discessu suo elapso tempore ad me dederit. Sed habet is forte sua impedimenta, neque occasio facile in promptu est. Quare et hac in parte nihil ipsi imputo. Cum vero is qui tuas mihi attulerat, nunc ad me reversus sit, ut responsum, prout ipsi etiam iniunxeram, tibi reportaret, ego quamvis eo tempore id evenerit, quo maxime ad scribendum ob negociorum multitudinem impeditus sim, et a Serenissimo Rege nostro insuper avocer, non potui tamen intermittere, quin aliquid literarum tibi perferendum ipsi traderem, ne tui oblitus tibique rescribere data opera neglexisse apud te excusari possem. Quæ itaque tuis literis pro hac brevi occasione temporis respondenda habui, sic brevibus accipe.

Quod adventum tuum ad nos prolixe excusas, tibi ea in re non plurimum contrarior, neque ego aliam ob causam nomine Regis te huc invitavi, quam quod a WITTICHIO intelligerem officio in Aula Imperatoris destitutum cogitare de alia eligendæ vitæ et sustinendæ familiæ ineunda ratione, adeo ut iam quoque a Rege Sueciæ te vocatum esse dixerit. Cum vero ex proprijs tuis literis intelligam, res tuas non esse eo loco, ut patriam mutare alienoque Domino servire velis, nihilque tibi ad commodam vivendi rationem deesse in patria, tibi plurimum congratulor, et meam voluntatem, quod tibi, si opus fuisset, non deesse voluerim, ut boni consulas oro.

Quod de Apogeo ☉ scribis restitutionem nostram accedere propius Alphonsinorum rationibus, quam COPERNICI, ego sane ea in parte nullum habui respectum neque ALPHONSI neque COPERNICI; sed ex plurimis exquisitis observationibus Geometrice ad invicem collatis iuxta quartam partem ♋ hærere Apogeum (quam interdum una parte minus, interdum una plus) reperi, ut quamvis ex minimis hîc inquirantur maxima. Eccentricitatem vero multoties in eodem, quo assignavi, scrupulo reperi, novem minutis Coperniana maiorem. Et licet eccentricitas maxima ex parte prosthaphæreses variet, præsertim iuxta medias elongationes, tamen circa Apogeum et Perigeum intolerabilis est duarum vel trium partium loci, sub quo hæret Apogeum, error.

Quod lapsum in Cometa anni 77 et quosdam alios in Dialexi emendaveris, libenter audio. Id vero quod de culminatione Cometæ cum Aquila in excusationem adducis, mihi non satis facit. Impossibilia enim non erant ad probandum tentanda neque excusationem ullam merentur; sed emendanda potius et revocanda, partim propter imperitos, partim propter invidos et malevolos. Scribis te editurum copiosius scriptum de Nova Stella et Cometis præteritis, ubi intelligo, te etiam mea quædam velle adiungere, a quo tamen, si impressum non est, quantum ad mea attinet, ut supersedeas, te rogatum velim. Ipse enim decrevi, quam primum licuerit, quædam enucleatius et de Nova Stella (ubi pleraque festinanter scripta restitui, multa copiosius in demonstrationum certitudinem adduxi) et de subsequentibus præcipuis duobus Cometis, tum etiam de tertio illo non adeo illustri, qui hoc anno mense Maio in Septentrione sub Heniochi sidere per IO dies nobis apparuit, in lucem emittere observationefque certas in medium adducere et infallibilibus demonstrationibus rem omnem tum in motu et situ eorum, tum in parallaxibus comprobare. Nam quod quasdam observationes hîc habitas in Cometa anni 80 a WITTICHIO acceperis, et inde parallaxes indagare conatus fueris, oleum et operam perdidisti. Tempora enim ex diversis horologijs et ijsdem nondum satis correctis annotata erant, nec illam discretionem adhibueras, quænam certiora alijs essent, et quæ sibi invicem in quolibet horologio corresponderent, multo minus ut per transitus stellarum sive per Meridianum sive per certum Azimuth, prout necessarium erat, et a nobis postea factitatum est, ea corrigeres, adeo ut ob id pluribus minutis ubique fere aberratum sit, ut mirum esse non debeat, quod tantam in parallaxibus constituendis inconvenientiam, ob temporum incertitudinem inveneris. Ego vero etsi tempora omnia aliter correcta habeam, quam prima fronte præbebant ipsa horologia, tamen in parallaxibus indagandis longe certiori nitor ratione, quam ubi temporis opus sit intervallo. Ibi enim ex minimo peneque insensibili errore maximus et intolerabilis augmentatur. Ideoque alijs quibusdam rationibus, ubi exquisita temporis cognitio non requiritur, inquisivi, tam hunc quam antecedentem Cometam (in tertio enim nondum feci periculum, nec adeo facile erit possibile) pene insensibilem habuisse parallaxin, quemadmodum hæc et alia quædam sufficienter ex certis observationibus demonstrari intelliges, ubi libellus noster de tota hac materia in lucem prodierit, quem spero me per hybernum otium, Deo conatus nostros adiuvante, absoluturum. Velim itaque nihil mearum observationum in publicum edas, priusquam hinc certior factus fueris, quænam sit mea de his sententia, a qua te plurimum abesse et temporibus incertis minimeque correctis inniti video, et mirari satis non possum WITTICHIUM tibi tempora observationum transposita et per transitus stellarum minime verificata obtulisse, cum tamen ipsimet præsenti dixerim horologijs non esse fidendum, sed longe accuratiore emendatione opus esse. Quantum ad distantias fixarum indagandas attinet, ego non amplius fidem adhibeo Radio multas ob causas, quas longa foret mora hîc recensere. Sed post discessum WITTICHIJ duo exquisita instrumenta in hunc usum adinveni, quæ semper eandem distantiam in scrupulo præbent, adeo ut nec in defectu nec excessu aberrare possint. Oportet autem in utroque instrumento duos esse, qui observent. Et quamvis utrumque instrumentum ipsa fabricatione longe differat, tamen in quolibet eadem et sibi correspondens exquisite fit observatio. Sic nos per hæc invenimus distantiam polaris ab undecima Cassiopeæ p. 26 m. 25 exquisite (nisi quod aliquando dimidio scrupulo minor apparuit), quam tu 9 minutis maiorem collocas. Sic etiam in alijs discrimen inveni, de quo alio tempore tecum latius conferam. In declinatione Spicæ nullam differentiam inter tuas et meas observationes reperio. Nam et ego multoties hîc eius altitudinem maximam p. 25 m. 10¼ præcise observavi, ubi Æquator attollitur p. 34 m. 7, adeoque declinatio p. 8 m. 57 plane ut tua, quod errorem quartæ partis in æquinoctij præcessione Copernico hucusque irrepsisse convincit, posita eadem cum ipso latitudine, quam etiam exquisite duarum esse partium per alia instrumenta deprehendi, ut alibi hæc et plæraque alia copiose demonstrabo. Sed interim dum scribo, tempus me alio vocat, prohibetque, quantum velim, tecum colloqui. Dabo tamen operam, ut alia occasione oblata, copiosius de his atque alijs tecum agam. Vale, et mihi, cum licuerit, rescribere non intermitte. Verumtamen addam hoc unum, quod pæne neglexeram. Hisce diebus Comes quidam Anglus primarius Reginæ ad Regem nostrum Legatus, ut ipsum in ordinem Equitum Divi GEORGIJ honorifice consuetis ceremonijs reciperet, cum perfunctus esset sua apud Regiam Maiestatem legatione, me hic invisit, unâque inter cæteros Comites Medicum Doctorem THOMAM MUFETUM secum habuit. Is cum tui inter nos honorifica incidisset mentio, petijt ut tibi per occasionem datam suo nomine plurimam salutem dicerem tuumque filium, qui in Anglia est, recte habere indicarem.

Iterum feliciter vale.
Datæ Uranienburgi in Insula Huæna 23. Septemb. 1582.

Tycho Brahe to Thaddaeus ab Hayck — greeting.

A few weeks ago a certain merchant of Görlitz delivered to me your letter, most distinguished Master Thaddaeus, dearest friend, from which — besides your singular and constant goodwill toward us — I also understood that my earlier letter had reached your hands otherwise than I had supposed. So now in that matter I hold Wittich sufficiently excused. The only thing I wonder at is that he has sent me no letter at all in the time elapsed since his departure. But perhaps he has his own impediments, and an opportunity is not readily at hand. So in this respect too I impute nothing to him. And since the man who had brought me your letter has now returned to me, in order to carry back a reply to you, as I had also enjoined upon him — although it has fallen out at a time when I am most of all hindered from writing by a multitude of affairs, and am moreover called away by our most Serene King — I could not, even so, refrain from handing him at least some few lines to convey to you, lest I might be excused in your eyes as having forgotten you and deliberately neglected to write back. And so what I have had to answer to your letter, given this brief occasion of time, receive thus, in brief.

As to your excusing your coming to us at such length, in that matter I do not greatly contend against you; nor did I invite you here in the King's name for any other reason than that I understood from Wittich that, deprived of your office at the Emperor's court, you were thinking of entering upon some other plan of choosing a way of life and supporting your family — so much so that he said you had by now even been summoned by the King of Sweden. But since from your own letter I understand that your affairs are not in such a state that you would wish to change your homeland and serve another lord, and that nothing is wanting to you in your homeland for a comfortable manner of living, I congratulate you most heartily; and I beg you to take in good part my goodwill, in that I wished not to fail you, had there been need.

As to your writing that our restoration of the apogee of the Sun [☉] comes nearer to the reckonings of the Alphonsines than to those of Copernicus: I, for my part, in that matter had no regard either for Alphonso or for Copernicus; rather, from very many exquisite observations geometrically collated with one another, I found the apogee to lodge at about the quarter-point of Cancer [♋] (sometimes one degree less, sometimes one more) — so that here, indeed, the greatest matters are inquired into from the least. As for the eccentricity, I many times found it, at the very minute I had assigned it, greater than the Copernican by nine minutes. And although the eccentricity varies the prosthaphaereses in great part, especially about the mean elongations, yet around the apogee and perigee an error of two or three degrees in the place at which the apogee lodges is intolerable.

That you have corrected the slip concerning the comet of [15]77, and certain others, in the Dialexis, I gladly hear. But what you bring forward in excuse, concerning the culmination of the comet together with [the constellation] Aquila, does not satisfy me. For impossibilities ought not to have been attempted as if to be proved, nor do they merit any excuse; rather they are to be corrected and recanted — partly for the sake of the unskilled, partly for the sake of the envious and ill-disposed. You write that you are going to publish a fuller work on the New Star and on the comets past, where I understand that you also wish to append certain things of mine; from which, however — if it is not yet printed — so far as my own [matter] is concerned, I should wish you asked to forbear. For I myself have resolved, as soon as it shall be permitted, to bring into the light certain things more fully worked out, both concerning the New Star (where I have restored most of what was written in haste, and have carried much, more copiously, into the certainty of demonstrations) and concerning the two chief comets that followed, and then also concerning that third, not so conspicuous one, which this year in the month of May appeared to us in the North under the constellation of Auriga [Heniochus] for 10 days — and to bring forward sure observations into the open, and to prove the whole matter by infallible demonstrations, both as to their motion and place, and as to their parallaxes. For as to your having received from Wittich certain observations made here on the comet of [15]80, and having tried thereby to investigate the parallaxes — you have wasted oil and labour. For the times had been noted down from various clocks, and those not yet sufficiently corrected; nor had you applied that discrimination as to which were more reliable than others, and which corresponded with one another in any given clock — much less so as to correct them by the transits of stars, whether through the meridian or through a fixed azimuth, as was necessary, and as has since been done by us — so that on this account there has been error of several minutes almost everywhere; so that it ought to be no wonder that you found so great an inconsistency in establishing the parallaxes, on account of the uncertainty of the times. But I, although I have all the times corrected otherwise than the clocks themselves presented them at first appearance, nevertheless in investigating parallaxes rely upon a far more reliable method than where there is need of an interval of time. For there, from the least and almost imperceptible error, the greatest and intolerable one is magnified. And therefore by certain other methods, where exquisite knowledge of the time is not required, I have found that both this comet and the preceding one (for on the third I have not yet made the test, nor will it easily be possible) had an almost imperceptible parallax — just as you will understand these and certain other things to be sufficiently demonstrated from sure observations, once our little book on this whole matter shall have come into the light, which I hope, God assisting our endeavours, to complete during the winter's leisure. I should wish, therefore, that you publish nothing of my observations before you have been made more certain from here what my own opinion on these matters is — from which I see you are very far removed, and that you rely on times that are uncertain and by no means corrected; and I cannot wonder enough that Wittich offered you observation-times that were transposed and by no means verified by the transits of stars, since I had nevertheless told him myself, in person, that the clocks were not to be trusted, but that there was need of a far more accurate correction. As regards investigating the distances of the fixed stars, I no longer place faith in the cross-staff [Radius], for many reasons that it would be a long delay to recount here. But after Wittich's departure I devised two exquisite instruments for this use, which always yield the same distance to the [arc-]minute, so that they cannot err either in deficiency or in excess. It is necessary, however, that on each instrument there be two persons observing. And although each instrument differs greatly in its very construction, yet on either one the same and mutually corresponding observation is exquisitely made. Thus by these we have found the distance of the pole star from the eleventh star of Cassiopeia to be exquisitely 26 degrees 25 minutes (except that sometimes it appeared less by half a minute), which you place greater by 9 minutes. So too in other cases I have found a discrepancy, of which I shall confer with you more fully at another time. In the declination of Spica I find no difference between your observations and mine. For I too have many times here precisely observed its greatest altitude to be 25 degrees 10¼ minutes, where the equator is raised 34 degrees 7 minutes, and so the declination [is] 8 degrees 57 minutes, plainly as yours — which proves that an error of a quarter[-degree] in the precession of the equinox has hitherto crept in upon Copernicus, the same latitude being posited with him as I have also exquisitely ascertained, by other instruments, to be of two degrees — as I shall demonstrate copiously, these and most other things, elsewhere. But meanwhile, while I write, the time calls me elsewhere, and forbids me to converse with you as much as I would wish. I shall nonetheless take pains, when another occasion is offered, to treat with you more fully of these and other matters. Farewell, and do not, when it shall be allowed, neglect to write back to me. Yet I will add this one thing, which I had almost overlooked. In these recent days a certain English Count, the Queen's chief ambassador to our King — sent to receive him honourably, with the customary ceremonies, into the Order of the Knights of Saint George — when he had discharged his embassy before His Royal Majesty, visited me here, and had with him, among the other companions, the physician Doctor Thomas Muffet. When honourable mention of you fell among us, he asked that, given the occasion, I should convey to you in his name his very warm greeting, and indicate that your son, who is in England, is doing well.

Again, fare you happily well.
Given at Uraniborg, on the island of Hven, 23 September 1582.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus ab Hayck = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), Bohemian court physician and astronomer at Prague, the leading astronomical figure of Rudolf II's circle and Tycho's chief Bohemian correspondent. His Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt, 1574) treated the 1572 nova, and he also published a Descriptio of the comet of 1577. (No structured person-profile is yet held for him; identification from the literature consulted — Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, and Dreyer, Tycho Brahe.)
  • Sender's address-formula. S. = salutem (dicit), "(sends) greeting".
  • Goerlicius … Mercator — "a merchant of Görlitz" (the Lusatian town), the unnamed carrier who both delivered Hagecius' letter and was to bring back this reply.
  • Wittichius = Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586), the Wrocław/Breslau mathematician who had stayed several months on Hven in 1580 and who connected Hagecius and Tycho; Tycho here repeatedly faults the observation-times Wittich passed to Hagecius (see the parallax discussion). Tycho's irritation that the comet-of-1580 timings were "transposed and by no means verified by the transits of stars" is part of the larger Wittich-priority friction.
  • Serenissimo Rege nostro — Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), Tycho's patron, in whose name Tycho had (per this letter) invited Hagecius to Denmark; a Rege Sueciae … vocatum refers to a reported competing summons from the King of Sweden (John III).
  • Aula Imperatoris — the court of Emperor Rudolf II in Prague, the office from which Hagecius had reportedly been "deprived" (destitutum).
  • Astronomy — solar apogee. Apogeo ☉ … iuxta quartam partem ♋ — the apogee of the Sun lodging "at about the quarter-point of Cancer" (i.e. near Cancer 6¼°, quarta pars = the fourth part/quarter of the 30° sign). The glyph ☉ is named "Sun" and ♋ "Cancer" in the translation, the glyph kept in the source. Alphonsinorum rationibus … Copernici — the Alphonsine tables vs. Copernicus; Tycho insists he followed neither authority but his own observations. quamvis ex minimis hîc inquirantur maxima — rendered literally ("the greatest matters are inquired into from the least"), a sententious turn kept; maxima/minima not interpreted away.
  • Astronomy — eccentricity & prosthaphaeresis. eccentricitatem … novem minutis Coperniana maiorem = "the eccentricity … greater than the Copernican by nine minutes." prosthaphaereses kept untranslated (Tycho's additive-subtractive correction; this chapter's convention); medias elongationes = "the mean elongations"; circa Apogeum et Perigeum = "around the apogee and perigee." partium and minutis/scrupulo: in this technical register pars/partes = degree(s) and scrupulum/minutum = (arc-)minute — rendered accordingly and noted here.
  • Comets. Cometa anni 77 = the great comet of 1577; duobus … praecipuis Cometis = the two chief comets (1577 and 1580); tertio illo … qui hoc anno mense Maio … sub Heniochi sidere per IO dies = a fainter third comet of May 1582 seen "under the constellation of Auriga" for 10 days. Heniochi sidere: Heniochus (Greek Ἡνίοχος, "the Charioteer") = the constellation Auriga; named in English, the Latinised Greek noted. IO dies: the printed/transcribed form IO is the Roman numeral 10 (= decem); reproduced as printed in the Source block and read "10 days." culminatione Cometae cum Aquila — Hagecius' claim about the 1577 comet culminating together with Aquila (the Eagle), which Tycho rejects as an impossibility to be recanted, not excused.
  • Parallax method. oleum et operam perdidisti = "you have wasted oil and labour" — the proverbial oleum et operam perdere (to waste lamp-oil and toil), kept as the idiom it is. per transitus stellarum sive per Meridianum sive per certum Azimuth = correcting clock-times "by the transits of stars, whether through the meridian or through a fixed azimuth"; Azimuth kept. pene insensibilem … parallaxin = "an almost imperceptible parallax" — Tycho's supralunary conclusion for the comets; the hedge pene ("almost") preserved.
  • Instruments. Radio = the cross-staff (radius astronomicus), which Tycho says he no longer trusts for stellar distances. duo exquisita instrumenta … duos esse, qui observent — two newly devised instruments each requiring two observers; rendered descriptively (Tycho does not name them here).
  • Stellar measurements. distantiam polaris ab undecima Cassiopeae p. 26 m. 25 = "the distance of the pole star [polaris] from the eleventh star of Cassiopeia, 26°25′"; quam tu 9 minutis maiorem collocas = "which you place greater by 9 minutes." declinatione Spicae … p. 8 m. 57 = the declination of Spica (α Virginis), 8°57′; altitudinem maximam p. 25 m. 10¼ … Aequator attollitur p. 34 m. 7 = greatest altitude 25°10¼′, equator-altitude (co-latitude complement) 34°7′ at Hven. errorem quartae partis in aequinoctij praecessione Copernico … irrepsisse = Tycho's charge that "an error of a quarter[-degree] in the precession of the equinox has crept in upon Copernicus." latitudine … duarum … partium = the (ecliptic) latitude of Spica "of two degrees."
  • The English embassy. Comes quidam Anglus … in ordinem Equitum Divi Georgij — the English embassy to install Frederik II in the Order of the Garter (the Order "of the Knights of Saint George"); the chief ambassador was Peregrine Bertie, Lord Willoughby de Eresby (the 1582 Garter mission). Thomam Mufetum = Thomas Muffet (Moffet/Moufet, 1553–1604), the English physician and entomologist, who accompanied the mission and visited Hven. tuum filium, qui in Anglia est — Hagecius' son then in England, whom Muffet reports to be well.
  • Calendar / place. Datae Uranienburgi in Insula Huaena 23. Septemb. 1582 = "Given at Uraniborg, on the island of Hven, 23 September 1582" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar; this chapter's convention). The dateline date agrees with the edition label (1582-09-23); no discrepancy. The header carries the as-written date.
  • Orthography. THADDAeO / THADDAeE / Uranienburgi: the source prints the ae-ligature form (Ae), reproduced as in the transcription. observationefque: the transcription's long-s reading of observationesque ("and … observations"), kept as printed in the Source block and read -sque. negociorum, ijsdem, ijs, alijs, horologijs, parallaxin are period spellings, normalised in sense only.
