The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTOPHORUM STURCIUM.

QUOD mihi per Magnificum Dn. D. GÖDELMANNUM scripseris, Nobilis et eruditissime Domine D. STURTZI, utut ego ob eius festinationem et meas tum temporis occupationes nihil ad te literarum dederim, est cur tibi plurimas habeam gratias. Agnosco hinc tuum erga me candidum et constantem amorem, cui par pari referre non desistam. De statu Patriae tuae mirabili etiam multis annis miserabili hîc satis superque inaudivimus; Deus tandem pacem largiatur. Walachus inter alios, uti scribis, declaratus hostis Regni Polonici, Caesaris nostri clementissimi Vasallus nuper factus est, et belli dux contra SIGISMUNDUM BATORUM (qui Cancellarij Polonici instinctu per Tartaros Transylvaniam invasit, et aliquam eius partem Caesari ademit) ut inde rursus eum expellat, postea pro Canisio recuperando laboraturus. Accepit hac de causa a Caesare magnam vim pecuniarum. Bellum Hungaricum tanto conamine nunc reparatur quanto vix unquam antea, sic enim opus est.

Caeterum quod narratiunculam meae e Dania huc in Bohemiam ad S. Caes. Matem migrationis, qualem optimae memoriae Dn. D. DAVIDI CHYTRÆO sic expetenti misi, tuis historicis meditationibus inserere eamque expolire et ornare constitueris, rem mihi apprime gratam et quovis officio vicissim compensandam praestiteris. Existimassem complementum historiolae anni millesimi sexcentesimi ultra priorem mediam partem, quam Dn. CHYTRÆUS ante obitum absolvit, a te continuatum, ut hoc anno prostaret, atque in illa, quae de me, dum in vivis esset, inserere cupiebat, adiuncta forent, sed forte alia sunt vestra consilia, quibus lubens acquiesco. Cuperem nihilominus id, quod proposuisti, mature fieri, et si oportunitas sic feret, conceptionem primam, antequam excutiatur, mecum communicari.

Habito nunc Pragae in aedibus olim a laudatae memoriae Vicecancellario Dn. IACOBO CURTIO splendide et magnifice exstructis, unâ cum horto amplo et amoenissimo circumiacente, quas Sacra Caes. Matas in meos usus emit a vidua inhabitante pro 10000 Ioachimicorum, cum tamen duplo maiori summa vix exstructae sint. Est locus maxime in civitate editus, versus Zephyrum, nec longe ab arce situs, ut Caesarea Matas ultra alias domos et hortos interpositos secreto et minimo negotio huc, quando ipsi sic clementissime placuerit, venire queat. Disponuntur hîc modo Instrumenta mea omnia, quae e Dania huc transtuli numero 28; thesaurus incomparabilis, quem Patria mea non aestimans sibiipsi invidit. Adornatur Typographia, ut opera mea hactenus interrupta, quanto ocius fieri poterit, compleantur. Exstruitur etiam officina pyronomica pro exercitijs spagyricis, quae nostra est etiam voluptas. Deus Opt. Max. conservet et prosperet Augustissimum hunc Caesarem RUDOLPHUM II, qui nobis haec ocia fecit. Quod superest, aeviterno et omnipotenti Numini te et omnia tua commendo.

Datae ad Pragam ex novo Uraniburgo Caesareo, cuius inscriptionem mox subiungam, die 15 Aprilis stylo novo Anno 1601.

TYCHO BRAHE TO CHRISTOPH STURTZ.

That you wrote to me through the magnificent Lord Doctor Gödelmann, noble and most learned Lord Doctor Sturtz — although I, on account of his haste and my occupations at that time, sent you no letter [in return] — there is reason for me to give you very many thanks. I recognise from it your candid and constant love toward me, to which I shall not cease to render like for like. Of the state of your fatherland — wondrous, and for many years also wretched — we have heard here more than enough; may God grant peace at last. The Wallachian, among others (as you write) declared an enemy of the Polish kingdom, has lately been made a vassal of our most clement Emperor, and war-leader against Sigismund Báthory — who, at the instigation of the Polish Chancellor, invaded Transylvania with the Tartars and took some part of it from the Emperor — that he may drive him out thence again, and afterward labour for the recovery of Kanizsa. For this cause he has received from the Emperor a great sum of money. The Hungarian war is now being mounted afresh with such effort as scarcely ever before — for so there is need.

Moreover, that you have resolved to insert into your historical reflections the little narrative of my migration from Denmark hither into Bohemia, to the Sacred Imperial Majesty — such as I sent to the late Lord Doctor David Chytraeus, of best memory, at his so requesting — and to polish and adorn it, you will have done me a thing exceedingly welcome, and to be repaid in turn by any service. I should have thought that the completion of the little history of the year 1600, beyond the former half which Lord Chytraeus finished before his death, had been continued by you, so as to be on sale this year, and that the things he wished to insert about me while he lived would have been added to it; but perhaps your counsels are otherwise, in which I gladly acquiesce. I should wish nonetheless that what you proposed be done in good time, and, if opportunity so bring it, that the first draft, before it is struck off, be shared with me.

I now dwell at Prague in the house once splendidly and magnificently built by the late Vice-Chancellor Lord Jacob Curtius, of praised memory, together with an ample and most pleasant garden lying round about — which the Sacred Imperial Majesty bought for my use from the widow dwelling there for 10,000 joachimsthalers, though they were scarcely built for double that sum. It is a place set very high in the city, toward the west, and situated not far from the castle, so that the Imperial Majesty can come hither — past the other houses and gardens lying between — privately and with very little trouble, whenever it shall so most clemently please him. Here are now being arranged all my instruments, which I brought hither from Denmark, 28 in number — an incomparable treasure, which my fatherland, not valuing it, begrudged to itself. A printing-house is being fitted out, so that my works, hitherto interrupted, may be completed as quickly as may be. An alchemical laboratory is also being built for spagyric exercises, which is likewise our delight. May God Best and Greatest preserve and prosper this most august Emperor Rudolf II, who has made these leisures for us. For the rest, I commend you and all yours to the everlasting and omnipotent Deity.

Given at Prague from the new imperial Uraniborg — whose inscription I shall soon append — the 15th day of April, New Style, the year 1601.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Christoph Sturtz (Christophorus Sturcius), a learned correspondent at Rostock whose own letter to Tycho of 10 March 1601 survives (brahe-t_16010310001); here Tycho replies. He is evidently of Transylvanian/Hungarian connection — Tycho speaks of "the state of your fatherland … for many years wretched" and then gives the Transylvania/Hungary war news Sturtz had sent. He is composing a historical work into which he wishes to set Tycho's migration narrative (below). (Precise biography to firm up.)
  • Courier. Magnificus Dn. D. Gödelmann = Johann Georg Gödelmann (1559–1611), the Mecklenburg jurist-diplomat (cf. brahe-t_16010412001), who had carried Sturtz's letter.
  • The Long-War news (spring 1601; this chapter's convention context).
  • Walachus = the Voivode of Wallachia — Michael the Brave (Mihai Viteazul) — lately made an imperial vassal and war-leader against Sigismund Báthory of Transylvania.
  • Sigismundus Batorus = Sigismund Báthory, who (Tycho relays) had "at the instigation of the Polish Chancellor [Jan Zamoyski] invaded Transylvania with the Tartars" and seized part of it from the Emperor.
  • Canisium = Kanizsa (Nagykanizsa), the Hungarian fortress lost to the Ottomans in 1600, now to be recovered; the Hungarian war (the Ottoman–Habsburg "Long War") "mounted afresh with such effort as scarcely ever before."
  • Tycho's migration-narrative and David Chytraeus. Tycho had sent the late David Chytraeus (1530–1600, the Rostock historian; cf. brahe-t_16000305001) an autobiographical narratiuncula of his migration from Denmark to Bohemia, at Chytraeus's request, for Chytraeus's contemporary history of the year 1600. Chytraeus died with the work half-finished; Sturtz now proposes to continue it and incorporate Tycho's account. Tycho welcomes it and asks to see the first draft before printing — a characteristic concern for control over how his story is told.
  • The new imperial Uraniborg (the famed description of the Prague house). Tycho describes his Prague residence: the house of the late imperial Vice-Chancellor Jacob Curtius (Jakob Kurz von Senftenau; cf. brahe-t_15900125001, brahe-t_16010412001), with its ample garden, bought for Tycho by Rudolf II from Curtius's widow for 10,000 joachimsthalers ("though scarcely built for double that sum"); set high in the city, toward the west, near the castle, so the Emperor can come privately. Within it Tycho is installing all 28 of his instruments brought from Denmark — "an incomparable treasure, which my fatherland, not valuing it, begrudged to itself" — plus a printing-house (to finish his interrupted works) and an alchemical laboratory (officina pyronomica) "for spagyric exercises." He calls the whole the novum Uraniburgum Caesareum, "the new imperial Uraniborg," and promises to append its inscription. (Cf. the same self-styling in brahe-t_16010412001, "the new Rudolphine Uraniborg.")
  • Persons named. Christoph Sturtz; Johann Georg Gödelmann; the Wallachian Voivode (Michael the Brave); Sigismund Báthory; Jan Zamoyski (the Polish Chancellor); David Chytraeus; Jacob Curtius (and his widow); Rudolf II.
  • Calendar / dateline. Dateline die Aprilis stylo novo Anno 1601 — New Style, Prague/imperial; the day-number is missing/illegible in the source dateline (die [—] Aprilis) and is supplied as 15 from the edition label and identifier (16010415002), flagged here rather than silently inserted.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. The day is not merely label-supplied: Dreyer (Opera Omnia VIII p. 416; running-head 5/15 Apr.) prints die 15 Aprilis. Restored to Source + translation; the bracket withdrawn.
  • Correction. Latin: 'desinam' corrected to 'desistam', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p.415 (translation unaffected — 'shall not cease' is compatible with either verb).

1 defect found:
- text error (low). Our published Latin reads 'desinam' (from desino, 'cease') where Dreyer p.415 reads 'desistam' (from desisto, 'desist/stop') — a different verb, though a near-synonym in this context. The English ('shall not cease') is compatible with either verb, so the corruption does not visibly affect the translation. Dreyer prints: cui par pari referre non defiftam..
