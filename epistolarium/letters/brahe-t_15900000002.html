The original

QUÆDAM EX LITERIS CHRISTOPHORI ROTHMANNI, IVSSV SVI PRINCIPIS, AD DN. HENRICVM RANZOVIVM.

ABSOLVTIS pene literis, incidit hoc, quod adhuc subijciendum venit: Superiori anno Mense Septembri misimus ad Nobilissimum Dominum TYCHONEM BRAHE literas, Illustrissimus quidem Princeps noster vnas, ego vero binas, plenas variarum de rebus Astronomicis disputationum, ad quas Mathematicus ille Mathematicorum omnium longe præstantissimus nondum respondit. Cum itaque mihi mandarit Illustrissimus Princeps noster, vt eum de responsione admonerem, peto a tua Excell vt oblata occasione, clemens hoc Illustrissimi Principis nostri desiderium ipsi velis significare, ne responsionem diutius suspendat: qua quidem re T. Excell. officium longe gratissimum præstabit Illustrissimo Principi nostro. Coram, Illustrissimus Princeps, et ego in eius comitatu, inuisuri eramus Dn. TYCHONEM, eiusque Instrumenta Astronomica coram visuri, nisi immatura Serenissimi et optimi Regis mors, iter nostrum auertisset, proque tanto et tam læto visendi desiderio, luctum et mœstitiam Illustrissimo Principi nostro substituisset. Sed sunt omnia in manu præpotentis DEI.

Iterum vale.

English translation machine translation, Probable

Certain matters from a letter of Christoph Rothmann, by command of his Prince, to Master Heinrich Rantzau.

The letter being all but finished, this came up, which must yet be added below: Last year, in the month of September, we sent letters to the most noble Master Tycho Brahe — our Most Illustrious Prince one [letter], and I two — full of various disputations on astronomical matters, to which that mathematician, by far the most outstanding of all mathematicians, has not yet replied. Since therefore our Most Illustrious Prince has charged me to remind him about a reply, I ask of Your Excellency that, when an occasion offers, you would be willing to make known to him this gracious wish of our Most Illustrious Prince, that he should not put off his reply any longer: by which thing indeed Your Excellency will render a most welcome service to our Most Illustrious Prince. Face to face, our Most Illustrious Prince — and I in his retinue — were [intending] to visit Master Tycho, and to view his astronomical instruments in person, had not the untimely death of the Most Serene and most excellent King turned aside our journey, and, in place of so great and so joyful a longing to visit [him], substituted grief and sorrow for our Most Illustrious Prince. But all things are in the hand of God most powerful.

Again, farewell.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann (c. 1555 – c. 1600), mathematician and astronomer to Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel, and Tycho's principal scientific correspondent in the late 1580s. He writes here iussu sui Principis ("by command of his Prince"), i.e. at the Landgrave's direction.
  • Recipient. Dn. Henricum Ranzovium = Heinrich Rantzau (Henrik Rantzau, 1526–1598), Holstein statesman, humanist, and bibliophile, royal governor (Statthalter) of the Danish kings in the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein; a central node in the learned correspondence networks of the north, here asked to act as intermediary in prompting Tycho.
  • Illustrissimus Princeps noster — "our Most Illustrious Prince": Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), Rothmann's employer and himself an active observational astronomer.
  • Mathematicus ille … longe praestantissimus — "that mathematician, by far the most outstanding of all": Tycho Brahe, named just above; the encomium is kept as written.
  • Serenissimi et optimi Regis mors — "the death of the Most Serene and most excellent King": Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), who died 4 April 1588. His death is the event that diverted the Landgrave's intended visit to Tycho's observatory; immatura ("untimely / premature") is preserved as Rothmann's own characterisation.
  • Instrumenta Astronomica → astronomical instruments (the apparatus at Uraniborg/Stjerneborg); rendered per glossary.
  • Genre / structure. The heading QUAEDAM EX LITERIS … = "Certain matters from a letter of …" marks this as a printed excerpt — a postscript section (absolutis pene literis "the letter being all but finished") lifted from a longer Rothmann letter to Rantzau and inserted by Tycho into his Epistolae astronomicae (Uraniborg, 1596). It is not a self-standing letter; the surrounding letter-body is not reproduced.
  • Epistolary formulae. peto a tua Excell. … T. Excell. = "I ask of Your Excellency … Your Excellency"; Iterum vale = "Again, farewell". The honorific Excell. is the abbreviation for Excellentia Tua throughout, addressed to Rantzau.
  • Dating crux (named). The source carries no dateline and no place. The catalogue label is "1590 (?)" (itself queried). The header therefore asserts no date the text does not substantiate. The only internal time-anchor is relative: Superiori anno Mense Septembri misimus … literas ("last year, in the month of September, we sent letters"). Rothmann's and the Landgrave's letters to Tycho of September are those of 1589; combined with Frederik II's death (April 1588) already lying in the past, the excerpt's composition falls plausibly in 1590 — consistent with, but not proven by, the catalogue's queried "1590 (?)". The "September of last year" is not silently converted to a fixed civil date; it is left as the relative reference the text gives. No calendar style (O.S./N.S.) is stated in the source.
  • Negation check. Two negations in the operative clauses, both preserved: nondum respondit ("has not yet replied") and ne responsionem diutius suspendat ("that he should not put off his reply any longer"). The conditional nisi … mors … auertisset … substituisset ("had not the death … turned aside … [and] substituted") is a single contrary-to-fact condition (one nisi governing two pluperfect-subjunctive apodosis verbs) and is rendered as such; no additional negation introduced.
