The original

PRISCÆ AC VERÆ NOBILITATIS, INSIGNISQUE ERUDITIONIS VIRO, D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, MATHEMATUM ARTIFICI SUMMO, INSULÆ HUENÆ DOMINO ET AMICO SUO OBSERVANDO.
IN DENNEMARCK.
S.

PRIDIE eius diei, quam hinc discederet vestras ille ad Imp. nuncius, adij illum et de suo hinc discessu percontatus sum. Ille vero se eo ipso die habiturum audientiam apud Imp. aiebat. Quod vero sequenti mane discedere vellet, de eo ne verbum ullum. Dixeram me ipsi daturum literas ad te, quas paratas habebam; expectabam solum eo ipso die Bibliopolas nostrates e mercatu Francoffordensi, a quibus cognoscere cupiebam, num essent hoc tempore allaturi librum illum de astris, recordor fuisse cuiusdam IO. FRANCI, Parisijs impressum, et Reginæ Galliæ dedicatum, quem tibi olim Ratisponæ donaveram, et tu eundem superioribus mensibus a me petebas, ut hac commoditate eum ad te transmitterem. Cum autem non afferrent, mitto nudas literas in hospitium ad vestratem illum. Ecce vero illum iam die præterito hinc discessisse, mihi nunciant. Quam me id male habuerit, verbis exprimere non possum. Habes causam et iustam meam excusationem, cur vestras ille vacuus meis literis redierit ad vos, quam ut accipias beneque interpreteris, te oro.

B. V.
Pragæ die 20. Maij novo stylo Anno 1589.
T. studiosiss.
Thad.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man of ancient and true nobility and of distinguished learning, Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, supreme craftsman of the mathematical sciences, lord of the island of Hven, and his friend to be honoured.
IN DENMARK.
Greeting.

On the day before that on which your courier to the Emperor was to leave here, I went to him and inquired about his departure from here. He, however, said that on that very day he was to have an audience with the Emperor; but that he meant to leave the next morning — of this, not a single word. I had said that I would give him a letter for you, which I had ready by me; only, on that very day, I was waiting for our booksellers [to return] from the Frankfurt fair, from whom I wished to learn whether they were going to bring at this time that book on the stars — I recall it was by a certain Io. Francus, printed at Paris and dedicated to the Queen of France — which I had once given you at Regensburg, and which you had been asking of me these last months, so that I might by this convenient opportunity send it on to you. But since they did not bring it, I send a bare letter to that man of yours at his lodging. But lo, they now tell me that he had already departed from here the day before. How vexed this made me, I cannot put into words. You have the reason and my just excuse why that courier of yours has returned to you empty of my letter; and I beg you to accept it and put a kind construction upon it.

Fare you well.
At Prague, the 20th day of May, New Style, in the year 1589.
Most devoted to you,
Thad[deus].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thad[daeus] = Thaddaeus Hagecius (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku, 1525–1600), the leading Bohemian physician-astronomer, protomedicus and personal physician at the imperial court of Rudolf II in Prague, and Tycho's chief Bohemian correspondent and observer of the 1572 nova. The closing abbreviation Thad. is expanded in brackets.
  • Recipient address. D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO … INSULAe HUENAe DOMINO = "Master Tycho Brahe the Dane … lord of the island of Hven." MATHEMATUM ARTIFICI SUMMO rendered "supreme craftsman of the mathematical sciences." The line IN DENNEMARCK is a vernacular (German/Danish) postal direction — "to Denmark" — left in its original form and translated.
  • vestras ille … nuncius … ad Imp. — "your courier to the Emperor": a messenger sent by Tycho who had business (an audience) at the court of Emperor Rudolf II (Imp. = Imperatorem). The letter's whole occasion is Hagecius's apology that he failed to entrust a letter (and a book) to this returning courier in time.
  • librum illum de astris … IO. FRANCI, Parisijs impressum, et Reginae Galliae dedicatum — "that book on the stars … by a certain Io. Francus, printed at Paris and dedicated to the Queen of France." Crux (identification): Io. Franci is the genitive of Io[hannes] Francus (Jean/Johann François/Franc); the specific Paris-printed astronomical/astrological book dedicated to the French queen is not securely identified here and is left as the source gives it. The phrase de astris ("on the stars / on the heavenly bodies") is kept broad rather than narrowed to "astronomy" or "astrology." Reginae Galliae = "the Queen of France" (in the period, Catherine de' Medici as queen mother, or Louise of Lorraine as reigning queen — not specified).
  • olim Ratisponae donaveram — "I had once given [it to] you at Regensburg (Ratisbon)": Hagecius and Tycho had been together at Regensburg, the occasion of the earlier gift of the book; Ratisponae is the locative.
  • Bibliopolas nostrates e mercatu Francoffordensi — "our booksellers from the Frankfurt fair": the Frankfurt book fair (mercatus Francoffordensis), through which new printed books circulated north and east; Hagecius was awaiting the Prague booksellers' return with the fair's stock.
  • mitto nudas literas in hospitium — "I send a bare letter to [his] lodging": nudas literas = a letter unaccompanied (by the promised book); hospitium = the courier's lodging/inn.
  • Formulae. S. = Salutem ("greeting,"). B. V. = Bene Vale, "Fare you well". T. studiosiss. = Tuae studiosissimus / Tibi studiosissimus, "most devoted to you".
  • Calendar. Pragae die 20. Maij novo stylo Anno 1589 = "At Prague, 20 May, New Style, 1589." Hagecius, in Catholic Imperial Prague, writes New Style (Gregorian), as he expressly states (novo stylo); the equivalent Old Style date (as Tycho on Hven would have reckoned) is 10 May 1589, not asserted by the source and not introduced into the header. The edition label ("1589. 20. maj n.s.") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy.
