The original

TYCHO BRAHE D. D. THADDÆO HAGECIO.
S.

BINAS a te uno eodemque tempore per hunc, quem ad me ablegasti, Tabellarium recepi literas, clariss. D. D. THADDÆE, amice perpetuo colende, quae quam mihi fuerint acceptae, vix dici potest, tum quod a te, tanto viro tamque amico sincero constantique, missae forent, tum etiam quod rerum, quarum desiderio magnopere flagrabam me compotem ubertim reddiderint. Ex prioribus circa Solstitium hybernum praeteriti anni ad me datis intelligo, te tunc temporis Dialexin tuam locupletius auctam et recognitam unâ cum aliorum quorundam de eadem materia scriptis ad me misisse, idque per Serenissimi Regis Navarrae Legati servum, tuum conterraneum, qui ob remoratum Domini sui ad nos adventum et librum et literas tibi remiserat. Cum posterioribus vero praeter Privilegium Caesareanum mihi in Typographicis concessum et librum eundem unâ cum TRAPEZUNTIJ Commentarijs in PTOLEMÆI Almagestum libellumque CARIONIS de Directionum Significationibus, additis quibusdam alijs a te elaboratis opusculis, perlibenter accepi. Pro Privilegio autem Caesareo, quod tanta fide et diligentia impetrasti, summas tibi habeo gratias. Praestitisti sane ea in re officium amici minime fucati; daboque operam, quae in Privilegio continentur, a me sartâ tectâ fide praestentur. Taxam 12½ flor. a te in Cancellaria pro eo expositam Tabellario tibi restituendam tradidi, et viaticum, quo ad te revertatur, adiunxi, proque labore suo ipsum remuneravi. TRAPEZUNTIJ in PTOLEMÆUM commentarios, quos adeo benevole mecum communicas, interea reservabo, donec apud PLANTINUM, celebrem illum Antverpiensem Typographum, periculum fecero, an is velit eos Typis suis exprimere. Si annuerit, illi mittam, fietque id cum tui nominis, prout decet, mentione honorifica, quod Astronomiae Studiosis tam insignem Thesaurum non invideris. Interea aliud exemplar describi curabo, ne, si forte Archetypum aliquo casu interciderit, tantis TRAPEZUNTIJ laboribus penitus privemur. Quam primum vero responsum a PLANTINO accepero, te de his certiorem reddam. Si vero negocium non successerit, commentarios illos tibi citra omne dispendium restitui curabo. Quantum ad tuas lucubrationes attinet, de Nova Stella et duobus Cometis unâ cum materijs similibus hisce annexis, nondum ea omnia plene, quamvis id avide percuperem, mihi perlegere licuit, eo quod Tabellarius hic ad me accesserit ijs ipsis diebus, quibus Comitia Regni nostri in vicina Haffnia celebrarentur, unde factum est, ut circa id tempus vel mihi eo ad consanguineos et amicos nobiles, qui e toto Regno confertim illuc confluxerant, excurrendum fuerit, vel etiam ex illis nonnulli me hîc, finitis ijsdem Comitijs, inviserint. Cumque nuntius nunc reditum ad te urgeat nec ulterius a me detinendus sit, eo quod ob causas antedictas, tum etiam ob Sereniss. Reginae nostrae, me in hac Uranoburgi Insula clementer invisentis, accessum supervenientem praeter opinionem diutius hîc substiterit, ideo ut per otium commodum librum tuum perlegam, mecum pauculo tempore eum retinebo, id quod bona tua venia fieri posse, vel ex proprijs tuis literis mihi persuadeo. Remittam illum vel per proprium quendam servum natione Bohemum, Mechanicum meum Opificem, qui in patriam reditum intra paucas hebdomadas a me, ut suos revisat, impetravit, postmodum denuo huc reversurus. Quod si haec ratio minus commode successerit, in Nundinis Autumnalibus Francofurtum ad Bibliopolam et Typographum illum Gallum, de quo mihi significas, tibi restituendum transferri curabo. Interim vero apud modo dictum PLANTINUM Antverpiensem de tuo etiam hoc libro per literas expiscabor, an eum suis sumtibus edere non gravetur, tibique ipsius voluntatem postea significabo. Haud sane illibenter ipsemet per propriam nostram Typographiam tuum hunc foetum publicum redderem vel seorsim per se vel nostris in simili materia adiungendo, veluti a me expetis, sed obstat, quod meum opus de Novis mundi Aetherei generationibus nostro aevo conspectis in iustam per se excrescit magnitudinem, ita ut non patiatur ipsa libri quantitas, tam prolixas lucubrationes adiungi, nisi in monstrosum et immodicam dilataretur formam. Nam et ipsemet hanc ob causam cogor in quibusdam brevior esse, quam alias vellem. Verendum enim alioquin, ne non saltem liber sua crassitudine modum excedat, sed et papyrus, quam in his ultimis Nundinis Francofurtensibus huic negotio comparavi, nequaquam operi sufficiat. Ad haec ante hyemem otium mihi vix editioni operam dandi suppetit, eo quod aestivo tempore alijs negotijs per amicorum et nobilium conversationem utplurimum distrahar, idque praesertim nunc ob Illustrissimi Ducis Mechelburgensis ULDARICI soceri Regij ad arcem Cronenburgam recens in propinquo Selandiae littore magnifice extructam indies expectatum adventum, qui etiam, quantum ex Regina filia intellexi, et nostras aedes accedere ante suum e Dania discessum non [de]dignabitur. Cumque non paucas hebdomadas apud Sereniss. Regem nostrum unâ cum alijs quibusdam Germaniae Principibus commorabitur, haud dubie ob nobilium et eruditorum virorum, qui eos comitabuntur, crebrum interea, uti fieri solet, ad me confluxum, liberiore otio citra diuturni temporis iacturam vix perfruar, adeo ut ante hybernum tempus impressioni rerum nostrarum (ut dixi) vacare minime liceat. Et quia non parum deinde temporis requiritur ad absolvendam nostri operis editionem, nimis tarde fortassis tuis votis satisfecero, si postea demum tuos labores seorsim hic excudi curavero. Qua in re papyrus etiam impressoria, quae difficillime hisce temporibus ob tumultus in Regnis et locis a nobis occidentalibus obortis, unde in copia alias adferri solet, non minimum obstaculi ingeret. Consultius itaque mihi videtur, si PLANTINUS editionem detrectaverit, librum ad te remittere, ut eius viri, de quo scribis, impensis publicae utilitati communicetur. Nam quantum ego hactenus eum obiter saltem ob otij importunitatem percurrendo cognoscere potui, dignus sane videtur, qui cum nominis tui memoria posteritati consecretur. Et licet a meis vix diversa contineat, vellem tamen, ut parcius de nostris, quo ad Novam Stellam attinet, ageres. Erant enim ea, quae olim de hac re a me conscripta sunt, admodum ieiuna et immatura, quae, Deo volente, absolutiora et castigatiora in hoc alio opere invenies. Id etiam monere volui, quod percurrendo ea, quae ex mea Novae illius Stellae contemplatione summatim excerpsisti, reperiam, te in quodam loco modeste satis insinuare, quod declinationem illius stellae diversimode in differentia 4 scrupulorum intra paucas lineas admiserim. Etsi vero hunc lapsum pro tuo candore, eo quod differentia tantilla pro nullo reputari debeat, excuses, nolui tamen me in numerationem Astronomicam vel in minimis impingere. Quare admirabundus libellum meum protinus revidebam, invenique te per festinationem procul dubio nimiam incuria quadam legisse in posteriori loco 62 gr. 2 m., cum libellus noster habeat 62 g. minus 2 min., id quod indicat, desiderari duo minuta ad completionem 62 grad., idque, uti scis, idem plane est, quod prius dixeram, declinationem eius stellae fuisse 61° 58'; nolebam enim ijsdem verbis eadem repetere. Tuum itaque erit in hoc loco nostri scripti animadversionem, si id publicaveris, quo ad hanc brevem ac incuriam a teipso facile commissam revocare; ubi totum librum diligentius perlegero, si quid mihi occurrerit admonitione dignum, sincere et amice te de singulis commonefaciam. Id saltem generaliter nunc indicabo, praeterquam quod rationes inveniendi parallaxes Cometarum, quas tum ex alijs, tum a teipso inventas in medium proponis, talia data utplurimum requirant, quae inter observandum Cometas rarissime et difficulter offerantur, ut ipsemet multoties expertus sum. Accedit et hoc (quod maioris momenti est), omnes illas parallaxium investigationes, quas liber tuus continet, ne ijs quidem exceptis, quas e REGIOMONTANI tractatulo explicas, Cometam omni motu proprio destitui, et non nisi raptu primi mobilis convolvi, praesupponant, id quod in nullo hactenus Cometarum locum meruit, in sola illa Nova ad Cassiopaeam Stella competebat. Cumque motus proprius Cometarum saepenumero maior sit ea diversitate, quae per parallaxes, si quam habeant, insinuatur, nullatenus, non considerato motu hoc proprio, parallaxium differentiae per ullum eorum problematum, quae adducis, (quantum hactenus perspexi) manifestantur. Haec vero ea de causa neutiquam indico, quod putem, teipsum ista non considerasse, sed ut operam des, quo ea potissimum discentibus proponantur, quae in ipsa ... quam commodissime citra omne obstaculum administrari queant. Porro cum ex literis tuis intelligam, Diarium illud huius Anni a Studioso meo ELIA editum ad tuas manus pervenisse, et inter alia non displicuisse, quae in eo de Cometa proxime viso referuntur, mitto tibi paulo exactiorem et copiosiorem eiusdem Cometae descriptionem, qualem cum Illustriss. Principe Domino GUILIELMO Landtgravio Hassiae nuper communicavi, veluti ipsius Celsit. per literas ad Dn. HENRICUM RANZOVIUM, Vicarium Regium in Holsatia horum studiorum amantiss. a me fieri expetierat. Sed ut eo rectius occasionem omnem huius negotij intelligas, adiunxi exemplar Epistolae eiusdem Illustrissimi Principis Landgravij ad praedictum RANZOVIUM, mihi ab hoc transmissum, tum etiam exemplar mearum ad ipsius Illustriss. Celsitudinem de hoc negotio et alijs Astronomicis rebus, tibi lectu non ingratis futuris, perscriptarum literarum unâ cum ipsius Celsitud. ad me dato responso, adiunctis insuper literis CHRISTOPHORI ROTHMANNI, ipsius Celsit. Mathematici, et scripto eiusdem de eodem Cometa, quod tibi eo nomine exarari curavi. Percipies ex his omnibus proculdubio plurima, quae tuis desiderijs arridebunt. Instrumentorum speciem meorum et quorundam, quae ad observationes exactius, quam hactenus factitatum est, caelitus pervestigandas attinent, ex meis ad principem Landgravium literis non sine voluptate cognosces. Consensum vero inter observationes Cometae Landgravianas et nostras ex literis ipsius Domini Landgravij et Mathematici scripto non citra admirationem intelliges. Peperit autem hanc convenientiam Instrumentorum ad quorundam ex meis similitudinem a Landgravio instituta restauratio, prout WITTICHIUS illi formam, divisionem et pinnacidia hîc apud me explorata non aliter, ac si ipsemet eorum inventor esset, mei nulla facta mentione propalavit, quo nomine etiam aurea catena ab ipso Principe donatus est. Accidit tamen illi, quod plerunque dolose agentibus evenire solet, ut pinnacidiorum quadruplicium undique parallelorum exactam rationem non indicaverit, sive quod ipsi exciderit, sive quod paucioribus rem absolvere praesumserit, unde Landgravij observatores in diuturni temporis et laboris iacturam implicuit, donec ipsa experientia, quae desiderabantur, ipsimet animadvertissent, velut ex Mathematici illius Epistola copiosius intelliges. Agit sane minus sincere mecum WITTICHIUS (quem ego hîc tum ob tuam commendationem, tum ob proprium ipsius ingenium in precio habui, pluraque, quam forte opus fuisset, concredidi) quod mea sine meo consensu sub suo nomine publica faciat, non quod alijs mea inventa invideam, sed quia ipsemet de Mechanica Astronomiae parte peculiari opere tractare et multorum diversorumque organorum structuram posteris communicare decrevi. Quam itaque ab alijs gratiam pro tot arduis laboribus et sumtibus incredibilibus non immerito, vel te iudice, reportare debebam, iste, quantum in ipso est, praeripere sibique, alienis plumis se instar corniculae AeSOPICAe exornando, attribuere conatur. Sed valeant haec cum caeteris, quae a perverso ingratoque mundo enormiter astuteque fieri consueverunt. Commode tamen hoc accidit, ut ea, quae tunc temporis WITTICHIUS apud me vidit alijsque pro suis postea venditavit, nullam obtineant comparationem ad haec, quae postmodum in hoc genere a me elaborata sunt, adeo ut priora nunc ipsemet parvifaciam et pro antiquatis seponam, quemadmodum haec latius cognosces, ubi opus illud nostrum, cuius antea memini, quod Mechanicam Astronomiae partem luculenter tractabit, in publicum prodierit. In quem etiam usum instrumenta mea omnia artificiose delineata aeneis laminis insculpi procuro. Ubi vero non saltem haec, sed et alias nostras pro Astronomia instauranda lucubrationes illumque de Aetherei mundi novis Generationibus librum, quem nunc prae manibus habeo, typis nostris, Deo conatus fortunante, publicavero, te eorum, quem etiam arbitrum praecipuum (utpote harum rerum inter paucos apprime intelligentem) in his eligo, compotem reddere non intermittam. Sed iam demum animadverto, me, dum non sine voluptate diutius tecum colloquor, Epistolij limites transgredi. Quapropter nunc pedem retraho, manumque e tabula tollens te Deo Opt. Max. cum tota familia commendo. Is te quam diutissime conservet incolumem et felicem. Vale nostramque amicitiam, siquidem praesens corpore (velut olim Ratisbonae) non licet, animo saltem (quod facis) absens conserva, eamque per Literas data quacunque opportunitate continua, quod et ipse vicissim praestare nequaquam intermittam. Resalutat te frater meus STENO BRAHE officiosissime. Iterum quam felicissime vale et plura a me, ubi librum tuum remisero, expecta. Datae Uraniburgi Calendis Iulij stylo veteri Anno 1586.

Tycho Brahe sends greeting to Master Thaddaeus Hagecius.

I have received two letters from you at one and the same time, by the courier whom you despatched to me, most renowned Master Thaddaeus, friend ever to be honoured; and how welcome they were to me can scarcely be said — both because they were sent by you, so great a man and so sincere and constant a friend, and also because they have abundantly put me in possession of things for which I was greatly burning with desire. From the earlier letter, sent to me about the winter solstice of last year, I understand that you had then sent me your Dialexis, more fully augmented and revised, together with certain writings of others on the same subject, and that [you sent it] by the servant of the Ambassador of the Most Serene King of Navarre, your fellow-countryman, who, on account of his master's delayed arrival here among us, had sent both the book and the letter back to you. But with the later letter I most gladly received, besides the Imperial Privilege granted to me in matters of printing, that same book together with the Commentaries of Trapezuntius on Ptolemy's Almagest, and Carion's little book On the Significations of Directions, with certain other small works wrought by you added. For the Imperial Privilege, which you obtained with such fidelity and diligence, I owe you the greatest thanks. You have truly performed in that matter the office of a friend in no way feigned; and I shall take care that what is contained in the Privilege be discharged by me with faith kept whole and entire. The fee of 12½ florins which you laid out for it in the Chancery I handed over to the courier to be repaid to you, and I added travel-money for his return journey to you, and rewarded him for his labour. The commentaries of Trapezuntius on Ptolemy, which you so kindly share with me, I shall keep meanwhile, until I have made trial with Plantin, that celebrated printer of Antwerp, whether he be willing to set them in his type. If he assents, I shall send them to him, and it shall be done — as is fitting — with honourable mention of your name, in that you did not begrudge so notable a Treasure to the students of Astronomy. Meanwhile I shall have another copy transcribed, lest, if by chance the archetype should be lost by some mishap, we be utterly deprived of Trapezuntius's so great labours. And as soon as I have a reply from Plantin, I shall inform you of these matters. But if the business does not succeed, I shall see that those commentaries are returned to you without any expense to you.

As regards your own lucubrations, on the New Star and the two Comets together with similar matters annexed here, it has not yet been permitted me to read through all of it fully — much though I eagerly longed to do so — because this courier reached me in those very days when the Diet of our Kingdom was being held in neighbouring Copenhagen; whence it came about that around that time I had either to make an excursion thither to my kinsmen and noble friends, who had thronged together there in a crowd from the whole Kingdom, or else some of them, the Diet being ended, visited me here. And since the messenger now presses for his return to you and is not to be detained by me any longer — because, for the aforesaid reasons, and also on account of the imminent visit of our Most Serene Queen, who graciously visits me on this Island of Uraniborg, he has stayed here longer than expected — therefore, that I may read your book through at convenient leisure, I shall keep it with me for a little while, which I persuade myself may be done with your good leave, even from your own letter. I shall send it back either by a certain servant of mine, a Bohemian by nation, my mechanic-craftsman, who has obtained from me leave to return to his homeland within a few weeks, to see his own people, intending afterwards to return here once more. But if this means should succeed less conveniently, I shall see that it is conveyed back to you at the Autumn Fair at Frankfurt, to the bookseller and that French printer of whom you write to me. Meanwhile, however, I shall fish out by letter from the same Plantin of Antwerp, regarding this book of yours too, whether he would not object to publishing it at his own expense, and afterwards make his will known to you. I would by no means unwillingly make this offspring of yours public myself through our own printing-house — either separately on its own, or by adjoining it to my own [works] on a similar subject, as you ask of me — but it stands in the way that my work On the New Generations in the Aethereal World seen in our age swells by itself to a just magnitude, so that the very size of the book does not allow such lengthy lucubrations to be adjoined, unless it were dilated into a monstrous and immoderate form. For I myself am compelled for this reason to be briefer on certain points than I should otherwise wish. For otherwise it is to be feared not only that the book may exceed due measure by its thickness, but also that the paper which I procured for this business at the last Frankfurt Fair may by no means suffice for the work. Besides this, before winter I have scarcely leisure to devote to the edition, because in summertime I am for the most part distracted by other business through the company of friends and noblemen, and especially now on account of the daily-expected arrival of the Most Illustrious Duke of Mecklenburg, Ulrich, the King's father-in-law, at the castle of Kronborg, lately built in magnificent fashion on the nearby shore of Zealand — who also, as far as I have understood from the Queen his daughter, will not disdain to come to my house too before his departure from Denmark. And since he will stay not a few weeks with our Most Serene King, together with certain other Princes of Germany, doubtless on account of the frequent concourse to me meanwhile (as is wont to happen) of noble and learned men who will accompany them, I shall scarcely enjoy freer leisure without the loss of long time, so that before wintertime it is by no means permitted me (as I said) to be at leisure for the printing of our matters. And since no little time is then required to complete the edition of our work, I shall perhaps satisfy your wishes too late, if only afterwards I see to your labours being printed here separately. In which matter the printing-paper too — which is most difficult [to obtain] in these times, on account of the tumults that have arisen in the Kingdoms and places to the west of us, whence it is usually brought in abundance — will throw up no small obstacle. It therefore seems to me more advisable, if Plantin should decline the edition, to return the book to you, that it may be communicated to the public benefit at the expense of that man of whom you write. For so far as I have hitherto been able to know it, by running through it only cursorily on account of the importunity of [scant] leisure, it truly seems worthy to be consecrated to posterity together with the memory of your name. And although it contains scarcely anything different from my own [findings], yet I could wish that you would deal more sparingly with my matters, as regards the New Star. For those things which were once written by me on this subject were exceedingly meagre and immature, which — God willing — you will find more complete and more corrected in this other work [of mine]. This too I wished to point out: that, in running through the things which you have summarily excerpted from my contemplation of that New Star, I find that you in a certain place modestly enough hint that I admitted the declination of that star differently, with a discrepancy of 4 minutes, within a few lines. But although you excuse this slip, out of your candour, on the ground that so tiny a difference ought to be reckoned as nothing, yet I was unwilling to stumble in Astronomical reckoning even in the smallest matters. Wherefore, in astonishment, I straightway re-examined my little book, and found that you — through doubtless excessive haste, by a certain carelessness — had read in the latter place 62 degrees 2 minutes, whereas our little book has 62 degrees minus 2 minutes, which indicates that two minutes are wanting to the completion of 62 degrees; and this, as you know, is exactly the same as what I had said before, that the declination of that star was 61° 58′; for I did not wish to repeat the same things in the same words. It will therefore be for you, in this place of our writing, to recall [correct] this brief carelessness, easily committed by yourself, if you publish it; and when I have read the whole book more diligently, if anything occurs to me worthy of remark, I shall sincerely and as a friend remind you of each point. This at least I shall now indicate in general: besides the fact that the methods of finding the parallaxes of Comets, which you set forth — both as found by others and by yourself — for the most part require such data as, in observing Comets, are very rarely and with difficulty available, as I myself have many times found by experience. There is added this too (which is of greater moment): that all those investigations of parallax which your book contains — not even excepting those which you explain from Regiomontanus's treatise — presuppose the Comet to be devoid of all proper motion, and to be wheeled around only by the rapture of the primum mobile; which has hitherto held good in no Comet, [and] applied to that New Star near Cassiopeia alone. And since the proper motion of Comets is very often greater than that diversity which is suggested by parallaxes (if they have any), in no way — the proper motion not being taken into account — are the differences of parallax made manifest by any of those problems which you adduce (so far as I have hitherto perceived). But I by no means indicate these things for the reason that I think you yourself did not consider them, but that you may take care that those things especially be set before learners which … can be carried out in [it] as conveniently as possible without any obstacle.

Further, since I understand from your letters that the Diary of this Year, published by my student Elias, has come into your hands, and that, among other things, the matters which are reported in it concerning the lately-seen Comet have not displeased you, I send you a somewhat more exact and fuller description of the same Comet, such as I lately shared with the Most Illustrious Prince and Lord Wilhelm, Landgrave of Hesse — as His Highness had requested be done by me, by letter to Master Heinrich Rantzau, Royal Vicegerent in Holstein, a most ardent lover of these studies. But that you may the more rightly understand the whole occasion of this business, I have adjoined a copy of the Letter of the same Most Illustrious Prince the Landgrave to the aforesaid Rantzau, transmitted to me by the latter; then also a copy of my own letter, written out to His Most Illustrious Highness concerning this business and other Astronomical matters that will not be unwelcome for you to read, together with His Highness's reply given to me; with the letters of Christoph Rothmann, His Highness's Mathematician, added besides, and a writing of the same on the same Comet, which I had drawn up for you under that title. From all these you will doubtless gather very much that will smile upon your desires. You will learn, not without pleasure, the appearance of my instruments, and of certain ones which pertain to investigating observations from the heavens more exactly than has hitherto been done, from my letters to the Prince Landgrave. And the agreement between the Landgravian observations of the Comet and our own you will learn, not without admiration, from the letters of the Lord Landgrave himself and the writing of his Mathematician. Now this agreement was brought about by the restoration of Instruments undertaken by the Landgrave after the likeness of certain of mine — inasmuch as Wittich divulged to him the form, the division, and the sights [pinnacidia] explored here at my house, no otherwise than as if he himself were their inventor, with no mention made of me; for which reason he was even presented with a golden chain by the Prince himself. Yet it befell him — as is wont to happen to those who act deceitfully — that he did not indicate the exact principle of the fourfold sights parallel on every side, whether because it slipped his memory, or because he presumed to accomplish the matter with fewer [than were needed]; whence he involved the Landgrave's observers in a loss of long time and labour, until they themselves should perceive by experience itself the things that were wanting, as you will learn more fully from that Mathematician's Letter. Wittich indeed acts less than sincerely with me (whom I here held in esteem, both on account of your recommendation and on account of his own talent, and to whom I entrusted more, perhaps, than there was need) in that he makes my [inventions] public under his own name without my consent — not because I begrudge my inventions to others, but because I myself have resolved to treat the Mechanical part of Astronomy in a special work, and to communicate to posterity the construction of many and diverse instruments. The thanks, therefore, which I ought deservedly to have carried off from others for so many arduous labours and incredible expenses — even by your judgement — that man, so far as in him lies, tries to snatch beforehand and to attribute to himself, adorning himself with others' feathers like the little crow of Aesop. But farewell to these things, together with the rest that the perverse and ungrateful world is wont to do outrageously and craftily. Yet this falls out conveniently, that the things which Wittich at that time saw at my house and afterwards peddled to others as his own bear no comparison to these which I have since wrought in this kind, so that I now myself set little store by the former and lay them aside as outdated — as you will learn more amply when that work of ours, which I mentioned before, which will treat the Mechanical part of Astronomy lucidly, comes forth into public. For which use also I am having all my instruments, artfully delineated, engraved on copper plates. But when I shall have published — by our own type, God prospering the endeavour — not only these, but also our other lucubrations for the restoration of Astronomy, and that book On the new Generations of the Aethereal world which I now have in hand, I shall not fail to put you in possession of them — you whom I also choose, in these matters, as chief arbiter (being, among the few, one most thoroughly understanding of these things). But now at last I notice that, while I converse with you at greater length, not without pleasure, I am overstepping the bounds of a brief letter. Wherefore I now draw back my foot, and, lifting my hand from the tablet, commend you, with your whole household, to God, Best and Greatest. May He keep you safe and happy as long as possible. Farewell, and preserve our friendship — since [we may] not [enjoy it] present in body (as once at Regensburg), yet at least in mind, absent (as you do) — and continue it by Letters at whatever opportunity is given, which I in turn shall by no means fail to do. My brother Sten Brahe returns your greeting most dutifully. Again, fare you most happily, and expect more from me when I shall have sent your book back. Given at Uraniborg on the Kalends of July, Old Style, the year 1586.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck, 1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer, and personal physician to Emperor Rudolf II — Tycho's most important Central-European scientific correspondent, who later (1599) helped bring Tycho to the Imperial court. The two had met in person at the Imperial Diet of Regensburg in 1575 (alluded to: velut olim Ratisbonae).
  • Dialexis — Hagecius's Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt, 1574), his treatise on the 1572 New Star; here the revised/augmented version sent to Tycho.
  • Persons named. Trapezuntius = George of Trebizond (Georgius Trapezuntius, 1396–1486/72), whose Latin commentary on Ptolemy's Almagest Hagecius lent in manuscript; Tycho hoped Plantin would print it. Carion = Johann Carion (1499–1537), astrologer; De Directionum Significationibus his astrological tract on directions. Plantinus = Christophe Plantin (c. 1520–1589), the great Antwerp printer. Wittichius = Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586) of Wrocław, who stayed with Tycho on Hven in 1580 and afterward, the letter charges, passed off Tycho's instrument-designs as his own at Kassel. Guilielmus Landtgravius Hassiae = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel (1532–1592), the observing prince. Christophorus Rothmannus = Christoph Rothmann, the Landgrave's mathematician. Henricus Ranzovius = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598), royal Statthalter (Vicegerent) in Holstein and patron of learning, the intermediary of the Tycho–Kassel correspondence. Dux Mechelburgensis Uldaricus = Ulrich III, Duke of Mecklenburg (1527–1603), father of Queen Sophie. Steno Brahe = Sten Brahe (1547–1620), Tycho's brother. Studiosus meus Elias = Elias Olsen Morsing (d. 1590), Tycho's assistant, who published the Diarium astrologicum et meteorologicum for 1586 with the description of the 1585 comet. The Regina nostra / Regina filia is Sophie of Mecklenburg, queen of Denmark.
  • Places / institutions. Uraniburgum → Uraniborg (on Hven); Haffnia → Copenhagen; Cronenburga → Kronborg castle, then newly rebuilt; Antverpia → Antwerp; Francofurtum / Nundinae Autumnales → the Frankfurt Autumn book-fair; Ratisbona → Regensburg.
  • Privilege & fee. Privilegium Caesareanum … in Typographicis = the Imperial printing privilege Hagecius secured for Tycho from Rudolf II's chancery; the Taxa 12½ flor. is the chancery fee (12½ florins/guilders) Hagecius advanced and Tycho repaid via the courier.
  • opus de Novis mundi Aetherei generationibus — Tycho's planned De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (the comet volume, eventually 1588); here still in preparation, with paper bought at Frankfurt and copperplates of the instruments being engraved.
  • Astronomy / cometary parallax. parallaxes Cometarum → parallaxes of comets; motus proprius → proper motion; raptu primi mobilis convolvi → "wheeled around by the rapture of the primum mobile" (the daily diurnal carrying-motion of the outermost sphere). Tycho's substantive objection: Hagecius's parallax methods (including those drawn from Regiomontanus, De cometae … problemata) assume a comet has no proper motion and moves only with the primum mobile — true only of the fixed New Star of 1572 in Cassiopeia, not of comets, whose proper motion swamps any parallactic shift. The hedges (quantum hactenus perspexi, si quam habeant) are preserved.
  • The declination crux (62° minus 2′). Tycho corrects Hagecius's reading of his New-Star declination: Hagecius read "62 gr. 2 m." (62°02′), but Tycho's libellus (the 1573 De nova stella) reads "62 g. minus 2 min." = 62° − 2′ = 61° 58′. The "discrepancy of 4 minutes" (differentia 4 scrupulorum) Hagecius noted is thus a misreading of minus, not a genuine inconsistency. Scrupulum = minute of arc; rendered "minute." Numerals reproduced exactly as printed (62 gr. 2 m. / 62 g. minus 2 min. / 61° 58′).
  • pinnacidia → sights (the slit-sight devices on Tycho's instruments), per glossary (pinnacidium, pinnula → sight, pinnule). pinnacidiorum quadruplicium undique parallelorum → "the fourfold sights parallel on every side" — Tycho's improved four-edged parallel-slit sights, the detail Wittich failed to convey to the Kassel observers. The Wittich/golden-chain episode is corroborated at Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, citing TBOO VI, 89.29–34 and VII, 68.21–25.
  • corniculae Aesopicae — "the little crow of Aesop," the jackdaw/crow that decked itself in others' (the peacock's) feathers (Aesop, Phaedrus I.3); the proverbial figure for plagiarism. Kept as the fable-allusion.
  • organorum structuram … Mechanicam Astronomiae partem — the projected Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (instrument-book, eventually 1598); Astronomia instauranda = "the restoration of Astronomy," Tycho's programme (glossary).
  • arbiter praecipuus — "chief arbiter": Tycho names Hagecius the principal judge of his forthcoming work, utpote harum rerum inter paucos apprime intelligentem ("one, among the few, most thoroughly understanding of these things").
  • Textual notes. non [de]dignabitur — the printed text reads non dedignabitur ("will not disdain"); the de- is bracketed following the edition (the litotes "will not disdain" = "will deign," intended sense). At quae in ipsa... quam commodissime the edition marks a lacuna (the noun governed by in ipsa is wanting); rendered "in [it]" with the gap flagged, not smoothed. Epistolij (diminutive of epistola) = "a brief letter / note." manum e tabula tollere — proverbial ("to lift the hand from the tablet," i.e. to know when to stop), here translated literally with the idiom signalled.
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Uraniburgi Calendis Iulij stylo veteri Anno 1586 = "Given at Uraniborg on the Kalends of July (= 1 July), Old Style, 1586." Tycho explicitly marks stylo veteri (Julian/O.S.) — apt, since Hagecius in Imperial service used the New Style. The catalogue label gives only the place (Uraniborg); the as-written dateline date (1 July 1586 O.S.) governs the header, consistent with it. No in-letter date discrepancy. The earlier Solstitium hybernum praeteriti anni ("the winter solstice of last year") dates Hagecius's first letter to ~December 1585.
  • Witness. A single MS witness is given (Vienna, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66); the printed text-of-record is the 1596 Epistolae volume (= Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII).
  • Correction. Collated against Dreyer, TBOO VII p.107. Corrected the ungrammatical 'tractatio' to Dreyer's printed ablative 'tractatulo' ('quas e REGIOMONTANI tractatulo explicas'); no change to the English, which already rendered the correct sense.

- text error (low). The transcribed original prints the nominative 'tractatio', which cannot follow the preposition 'e' (ablative); Dreyer prints the ablative 'tractatulo' ("little treatise"), the grammatically required and actually-printed word. The published English is unaffected by the corruption (it renders the sense of 'tractatulo' either way), but the transcribed original itself departs from Dreyer. Dreyer prints: quas e REGIOMONTANI tractatulo explicas (TBOO VII, p. 107, l. 39-40).
