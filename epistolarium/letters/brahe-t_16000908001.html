The original

IOANNES GEORGIUS HERWARTUS AB HOHENBURG AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

CUM primum, Vir nobilissime atque Mathematicorum omnium facile princeps, ad me misisti carmina illa, quæ de rebus tuis sunt composita, subijt animum, quod quondam IACOBUS CURTIUS Procancellarius Imperij, piæ memoriæ (qui mihi assiduus studiorum collega et contubernalis in Gallia et Italia, exinde amicus intimus et quidem pars animæ meæ, dum vixit, extitit) mea suggestione motus, desideravit carmen aliquod huius generis obtinere a D. PHILIPPO MENZELIO, Medicinæ D. et Professore celebratissimo, cum is in his partibus Poëtas reliquos longe antecellere existimetur, sed ob distractiones tam lectionis publicæ quam et praxeos Medicinalis modo memorati D. MENZELIJ frustra et incassum laboravit. Idcirco plane constitui nullum non lapidem movere, donec optatum ego adpiscar. Id ægerrime quidem, ut verum fatear, sed tamen iam tandem aliquando impetravi, et his iunctum ad te mitto, quod spero et confido tibi non futurum ingratum.

Mitto etiam veteres quosdam Geographos nunc primum in lucem editos, mihique inscriptos, inter quos SCYLAX Caryandensis est, cuius et STRABO et CICERO meminit, author sane vetustissimus, ipso HERODOTO et THUCYDIDE antiquior, ut qui temporibus DARIJ primi HYSTASPIS filij floruerit, penes quem aliqua reperies, quæ alibi non extent. Atque simul unâ te bene valere et quam fœlicissime florere ex animo opto. Datæ Augustæ die 8 Sept. A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg to Tycho Brahe.

When first, most noble man and easily prince of all mathematicians, you sent me those poems which have been composed about your affairs, it came into my mind that once Jacob Curtius, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, of pious memory — who, while he lived, was my assiduous study-colleague and chamber-fellow in France and Italy, and thereafter my most intimate friend, and indeed a part of my soul — moved by my suggestion, desired to obtain some poem of this kind from Master Philipp Menzel, Doctor of Medicine and a most celebrated Professor, since he is reckoned far to surpass the rest of the Poets in these parts; but, on account of the distractions both of public lecturing and of medical practice of the said Master Menzel, he laboured in vain and to no purpose. Therefore I plainly resolved to move every stone [to leave no stone unturned] until I should attain the thing wished-for. This, most laboriously indeed (to confess the truth), but yet now at length at last, I have obtained; and I send it to you joined herewith — which I hope and trust will not be unwelcome to you.

I send also certain ancient Geographers, now for the first time brought into the light and inscribed [dedicated] to me, among whom is Scylax of Caryanda — whom both Strabo and Cicero mention — an author indeed most ancient, older than Herodotus and Thucydides themselves, inasmuch as he flourished in the times of Darius the First, son of Hystaspes; in whom you will find some things which are not extant elsewhere. And at the same time I wish, from my heart, that you fare well and flourish most happily. Given at Augsburg, the 8th day of September, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (see the Munich series), here writing from Augsburg (where he had gone partly to recover Leovitius's manuscripts — cf. brahe-t_16000426002).
  • The Menzel poem. Herwart had at last extracted a commemorative poem on Tycho from Philipp Menzel (Menzelius), Doctor of Medicine and the most admired Latin poet "in these parts," who had earlier (at the prompting of Herwart's late friend Jacob Curtius) been too busy with lecturing and medical practice to write one. nullum non lapidem movere — "to move every stone," i.e. leave no stone unturned (the proverbial omnem movere lapidem).
  • Iacobus Curtius Procancellarius Imperij = Jakob Kurz von Senftenau (Jacob Curtius, d. 1594), Imperial Vice-Chancellor, Herwart's student-companion in France and Italy and "part of his soul" — the same Curtius commended in brahe-t_15980626002 as having known Tycho's work.
  • The ancient geographers. veteres Geographos nunc primum in lucem editos, mihique inscriptos = a newly-printed edition of minor Greek geographers, dedicated to Herwart — the Augsburg 1600 edition by David Höschel (Geographica … Marciani Heracleotae, Scylacis Caryandensis …). Scylax of Caryanda: the (pseudo-)Scylax Periplus; Herwart's flourish — "older than Herodotus and Thucydides, flourishing in the time of Darius I, son of Hystaspes, cited by Strabo and Cicero" — follows the ancient testimonia (the historical Scylax served Darius I; the surviving Periplus is in fact later, a point Herwart, like his age, does not raise).
  • Calendar. Augustae die 8 Sept. 1600 = 8 September 1600; Augsburg had adopted the Gregorian / New Style calendar (from 1583); taken as N.S. (The edition label gives only "Augsburg" without a date; the body supplies 8 Sept. 1600.)
