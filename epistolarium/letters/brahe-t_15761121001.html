The original

CLARISSIMO VIRO D. IOACHIMO CAMERARIO IUNIORI, PHILOSOPHO ET MEDICO EXIMIO, AMICO SUO COLENDO. DOCTOR CAMERARIUS MEDICUS ZU NÜRRENBERG.

EX litteris tuis, clarissime Domine Doctor CAMERARI, intellexi, maximam partem librorum Mathematicorum BRECHTALII esse distractam; nihilominus peto, ut eos, qui residui adhuc sunt, velis mihi, iuxta catalogum tibi missum, curare colligi, eosque huic artifici Mechanico GEORGIO LAUBIMVOLFI ad me transmittendos tradere. Et insuper, si quos alias in Mathematicis et Chymicis inveneris seu novos seu veteres, quos mihi usui fore existimas, unâ adiungas velim. Precium horum omnium ex tuo decreto artifex hic persolvet; pecunia meo nomine interea ab alio artifice IOHANNE von der SCHACK nomine, mutuo accepta, donec ego eam hîc in Dania (est enim is huc venturus) vel etiam Noribergæ restituero. Ignoscas autem mihi, quod ita audacter toties his meis negotijs molestus tibi sim; id facio fretus singulari tua humanitate, qua te eximie præditum, cum Noribergæ ultimo essem, non obscure cognovi. Peto autem, ut per occasionem me de tuis studiis et laboribus certiorem reddas, utrum in Spagyrica Medicina profectum aliquem feceris. Ego hac æstate nihil fere eximii in ea arte elaboravi, impeditus architectura, quam nunc præ manibus habeo. In Insula enim hîc quadam, quæ sita est in medio famosissimi et celeberrimi freti, ubi omnes naves ab Oriente in Occidentem transeunt, et e contra, ædes Philosophicas non exiguis sumptibus extruo. Hac enim Insula eo nomine a Rege donatus sum, ut eo, cum fert animus, secedere possim, et a vulgi strepitu semotus altioribus vacare exercitiis. Ubi vero ædificium hoc absolvero, divina ope conatus nostros auxiliante, spero me commoditatem non exiguam adepturum philosophandi tum in superiori et cœlesti Astronomia, tum etiam in hac inferiori Spagyrica rerum præparatione. Et si quid mea opera et sumptibus inveniri queat, quod tibi aliisve amicis id expetentibus usui esse possit, lubens communicabo. Vale, vir doctissime et humanissime, et amicitiam inter nos brevi tempore Noribergæ contractam longa tempora ne sinamus obliterare.

Iterum vale.
Datæ in Insula nostra Huena, d. 21. Novemb. An. 1576.
T.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished man, Master Joachim Camerarius the Younger, eminent philosopher and physician, his friend to be honoured. Doctor Camerarius, physician, at Nuremberg.

From your letter, most distinguished Master Doctor Camerarius, I understood that the greater part of Brechtel's mathematical books has been dispersed; nonetheless I ask that you be willing, on my behalf, to see to it that those which still remain are collected, according to the catalogue sent to you, and that you hand them over to this mechanical craftsman Georg Labenwolf to be sent on to me. And besides, if you should anywhere else come upon any [books], whether new or old, in mathematics and in chemical matters [chymicis], which you judge will be of use to me, I would wish you to add them in the same [consignment]. The price of all these the craftsman here will pay out according to your decision; the money [being] meanwhile borrowed in my name from another craftsman, one named Johann von der Schack, until I shall have repaid it either here in Denmark (for he is going to come hither) or else at Nuremberg. But forgive me that I should so boldly, and so often, be a nuisance to you with these affairs of mine; I do this relying on that singular kindness with which I plainly recognised you to be eminently endowed, when I was last at Nuremberg. I ask, moreover, that as occasion offers you make me more certain of your studies and labours, [namely] whether you have made any progress in spagyric medicine. I myself this summer have worked out almost nothing of note in that art, being hindered by the building-work [architectura] which I now have in hand. For on a certain island here, which is set in the midst of a most famous and most celebrated strait, where all ships pass from the East into the West and contrariwise, I am erecting a philosophical dwelling at no small expense. For I have been granted this island by the King on this account, that I may withdraw thither when the spirit moves me, and, removed from the din of the crowd, give myself to loftier pursuits. But when I shall have completed this edifice — the divine power aiding our endeavours — I hope to attain no small convenience for philosophising, both in the higher and celestial astronomy, and also in this lower spagyric preparation of things. And if anything can be discovered by my labour and expense that may be of use to you, or to other friends seeking it, I shall gladly share it. Farewell, most learned and most humane man, and let us not allow a long span of time to blot out the friendship contracted between us in a short space at Nuremberg.

Again, farewell.
Given on our island of Hven, the 21st day of November, the year 1576.
T[ycho].
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Ioachimus Camerarius Iunior = Joachim Camerarius the Younger (1534–1598), the Nuremberg physician, botanist, and bibliophile, son of the humanist Joachim Camerarius the Elder; a noted collector of scientific books and a hub of the Nuremberg learned network. The German address-line "DOCTOR CAMERARIUS MEDICUS ZU NÜRRENBERG" ("Doctor Camerarius, physician, at Nuremberg") is preserved as a code-switch into German by the edition's convention
  • Dateline crux (flagged, not reconciled). The source dateline reads "d. 21. Novemb. An. 1576" ("the 21st day of November, year 1576"), and the header follows it. The edition label catalogues the letter as "1576. 8. november"; the as-written date is led with and the catalogue date appended by the edition's convention. The 8 November label is not substantiated by the letter's own subscription and is recorded here, not silently adopted. (The letter is the one printed at Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 42; Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg, also dates it 21 November 1576.)
  • librorum Mathematicorum BRECHTALII — "Brechtel's mathematical books": the dispersed library (or stock) of one Brechtel at Nuremberg, identified in the source only by the genitive Brechtalii. The owner is not further specified in the letter; left as "Brechtel" without assertion of which Nuremberg Brechtel is meant.
  • artifici Mechanico GEORGIO LAUBIMVOLFI — "the mechanical craftsman Georg Labenwolf": Georg Labenwolf (the Nuremberg art-bronze founder, builder of fountains and waterworks), here the agent forwarding the books to Hven. Tycho had met Labenwolf at Nuremberg in 1575; Labenwolf came to Hven c. 1577 and built the Uraniborg fountain (Hannaway / Christianson, On Tycho's Island; Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg, p. 114, citing this letter, VII, 42). The Latinised genitive Laubimvolfi is kept in the source and the standard form "Labenwolf" used in the translation.
  • alio artifice IOHANNE von der SCHACK — "another craftsman, one named Johann von der Schack": a second artisan from whom Camerarius is to borrow the purchase-money in Tycho's name. The name is given in German (von der Schack) within the Latin sentence; kept as written.
  • Technical / glossary terms. Spagyrica Medicina / Spagyrica rerum praeparatione — spagyric medicine / spagyric preparation, the Paracelsian-alchemical (distillation/separation) branch of medicine and chemistry; kept as the period term, not modernised. Chymicis — "chemical [matters]," the early-modern chymia embracing both chemistry and alchemy; rendered "chemical" with the bracketed Latin retained. architectura — here in the concrete sense of "building-work / construction" (the Uraniborg fabric then under way), not the abstract discipline; rendered "building-work [architectura]." The contrast superiori et coelesti Astronomia … hac inferiori Spagyrica ("the higher and celestial astronomy … this lower spagyric [art]") is Tycho's stock pairing of the science of the heavens above with the alchemy of the elements below; rendered literally.
  • aedes Philosophicas — "a philosophical dwelling": Uraniborg, described here while still under construction (1576), before it bore that name in print. altioribus … exercitiis = "loftier pursuits."
  • Place. Insula nostra Huena = Hven (the island in the Øresund granted to Tycho by Frederik II in 1576); famosissimi et celeberrimi freti = the Øresund, "a most famous and most celebrated strait." Noribergae / Noribergam = Nuremberg.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading S. is not present; the salutation is the honorific address only. Vale … Iterum vale → "Farewell … Again, farewell." Subscription Datae "Given," T. / TYCHO BRAHE — the abbreviated initial expanded as T[ycho].
  • No MS witnesses are recorded for this letter (the edition witnesses field is empty); the text descends through its print in Camerarius's Animadversiones (pars VII), the edition of record, and is reprinted in Dreyer VII.
  • Calendar. O.S. (Julian); Denmark kept Old Style. No conversion applied by the edition's convention
