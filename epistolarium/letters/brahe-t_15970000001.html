The original

CAVSAe DEMIGRATIONIS D:NI TYCHONIS BRAHE EX PATRIA DANIA BREVITER AB IPSO AD PASCOLIUM MULÆUM EXPOSITAe.

VIDETUR Cassolius in ultimis ad me discessus mei ex Dania cavsas non satis ponderasse, forte qvod illi minus essent perspectae, dum ait non nisi aliqvam proventuum jacturam me fecisse, idqve etiam aliis mei ordinis evenisse, et talium solere miram esse vicissitudinem, qvod a te et Bagone illo accepisse se insinuat. Atqve si haec a te prolata sunt, aut meae e patria migrationis occasiones non penitus te cognovisse [?credo], aut eas patriae et nostratium cavsa politice et cavte subtices et dissimulas. Sunt qvidem ab aliis nobilibus reditus qvidam subtracti, qvi eos in privatum commodum vertebant, at a me omnes, adeo ut ne qvidem a praebenda mea Roschildensi vindicanda sibi, qvod alias insolitum est, abstinuerit cancellarius, qvi potius ea, qvae tracto, studia, juxta senatusconsulti rationem promovere debuisset. Ego vero, uti multis constat, non saltem eos, qvos a rege habui, sed et qvoscunqve de bonis haereditariis acqvirere licuit, sumptus in astronomicae artis instavrationem, qvo sic etiam patriae honori studerem, impendi. Verum tolerabilis fuisset haec utut gravis jactura, si non disertis verbis ad me scripsisset cancellarius, regi non esse integrum in astronomicorum instrumentorum conservationem qvicqvam impendere. Sicqve omnium senatorum literae aliud spondentes, exavtoratae, quas statim ab obitu laudat. mem. regis Friderici nostri in membrana exaratas, et singulorum manibus et sigillis munitas concesserunt, me vivente et vidente totus ille apparatus astronomicus, qvem ex clementi voluntate nunqvam satis lavdati regis istic institueram, interire debuerit, ne dum ut ad posteros in perpetuum regni honorem, juxta decretum tam dicti regis, qvam regni senatorum continuaret in perpetuum. Qvin nec in propriis qvidem aedibus Hafniae, propriis etiam sumptibus haec exercitia tractare licuit, interdicente id, sub nomine hujus, qvi nunc est, regis, licet tum in Germania absentis, aulae magistro, isqve per urbis praetorem. Ut taceam qvod alias contra me praeter meritum multi machinati sunt, ut praeteream qvae pastori meo odio mei obtigerunt. Nam de incitatis in me reprobis istis insularibus deqve jure in iisdem coercendis inhibito deniqve de iniqvis consultationibus multifariis super me habitis. Qvae omnia referre nunc piget.

Haec ex m[anu]s[cripto] d:ni Longomontani.

English translation machine translation, Secure

The causes of the departure of Master Tycho Brahe from his fatherland Denmark, briefly set out by himself to Pascolius Mulaeus.

It seems that Cassolius, in his last [letter] to me, did not sufficiently weigh the causes of my withdrawal from Denmark — perhaps because they were less clearly perceived by him — when he says that I have suffered no more than some loss of revenues, and that this has befallen others of my rank too, and that the vicissitude of such things is wont to be remarkable: which he hints he had heard from you and from that Bago. And if these things were put forward by you, [then I believe] that either you did not fully know the occasions of my emigration from the fatherland, or that, for the sake of the fatherland and of our countrymen, you are politicly and cautiously passing them over in silence and dissembling them. Certain revenues, to be sure, were withdrawn from other noblemen who were turning them to private advantage; but from me [were withdrawn] all — to such a degree that the chancellor did not even refrain from claiming for himself my prebend of Roskilde (a thing otherwise unheard of), he who ought rather to have advanced, in accordance with the resolution of the council, those studies which I pursue. But I, as is plain to many, expended not only those [revenues] which I had from the king, but also whatever I was able to acquire from my hereditary estates, upon the restoration of the astronomical art, that I might thus serve the honour of the fatherland as well. Yet this loss, however grave, would have been tolerable, had not the chancellor written to me in express words that the king was not free to expend anything upon the upkeep of the astronomical instruments. And so, the letters of all the senators promising otherwise being annulled — [the letters] which, immediately after the death of our King Frederick of praiseworthy memory, they granted, engrossed on parchment and secured with the hands and seals of each — [it came to pass that,] while I lived and looked on, that whole astronomical apparatus, which by the gracious will of the never-sufficiently-praised king I had set up there, had to perish — far from continuing in perpetuity for posterity, for the perpetual honour of the kingdom, according to the decree both of the said king and of the senators of the kingdom. Nay, I was not even permitted to carry on these exercises in my own house at Copenhagen, at my own expense too, the master of the court forbidding it in the name of the present king (though he was then absent in Germany) — and that through the city's magistrate. To say nothing of how, in other ways, many have plotted against me beyond my deserts; to pass over what befell my pastor through hatred of me. For [I say nothing] of those reprobate islanders stirred up against me, and of the right of coercing them, which was [then] forbidden me, and finally of the manifold iniquitous deliberations held about me. To recount all which now is irksome.

This [is] from the manuscript of Master Longomontanus.

Apparatus

  • Document type. Not an ordinary letter but a third-person rubric over a first-person statement: a brief apologia by Tycho on the causes of his 1597 departure from Denmark, addressed to Pascolius Mulaeus, surviving only as a copy. The transmission note at the foot — Haec ex manuscripto domini Longomontani — states the text was taken from the manuscript of Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (Christian Severin Longberg, 1562–1647), Tycho's senior assistant; the witness is an afskrift (copy) in Langebek's Excerpts, Nr. 178 at the Royal Library, Copenhagen.
  • Recipient. Pascolius Mulaeus / Mulaeum = Claus Mule (Latinised), a member of Tycho's household who accompanied him into exile (he travelled with Longomontanus to Helmstedt and on to Wittenberg in 1597–98). The edition records the recipient as Claus Mule; the praenomen Pascolius (vs. expected Nicolaus/Claus) is the form given by the source and is flagged below.
  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), speaking in the first person (ad me … me fecisse … impendi) though named in the third person in the rubric.
  • Calidius (so Dreyer, Opera Omnia XIV p. 137; the working transcription reads Cassolius — most likely an digitised text layer misreading of Cali-) — an unidentified correspondent of Tycho's whose "last [letter]" prompted this rebuttal; not securely identifiable under either spelling. The recipient is securely Claus (Claudius) Mule — Dreyer's own heading glosses Pascolium (? Claudium) Mulaeum, the apograph's Pascolius being a corruption of Claudium. The bracketed [?credo] is Dreyer's own editorial supplement, reproduced here, not a conjecture introduced here.
  • Bago illo — "that Bago": Bagge / Bago, a man named alongside the recipient as a source of the downplaying report; rendered "that Bago," the named individual left as given (likely a member of the Danish noble/Hven circle). Not further identified here.
  • cancellarius — "the chancellor": Christian Friis (of Borreby), Chancellor of Denmark, who (per Dreyer) was granted Tycho's Roskilde prebend on 10 June 1597 immediately after Tycho's departure. The text does not name him; identification supplied in brackets in this note only, not in the translation.
  • praebenda mea Roschildensi — "my prebend of Roskilde": the canonry of Roskilde cathedral, with which Tycho's incomes had been associated and which he lost in June 1597.
  • laudat. mem. regis Friderici nostri — laudatae memoriae regis Friderici = "of our King Frederick of praiseworthy memory": Frederik II (d. 1588), Tycho's patron, who had granted Hven and the supporting endowments; the council's parchment guarantees were issued at his death.
  • hujus, qvi nunc est, regis … tum in Germania absentis — "the present king … then absent in Germany": Christian IV (r. 1588–1648), who undertook a journey in the 1590s; under his name (in his absence) Tycho was forbidden to practise even privately in Copenhagen.
  • aulae magistro … per urbis praetorem — aulae magister = "the master of the court" (court marshal / Hofmeister); urbis praetor = "the city's magistrate" (Copenhagen's byfoged / town official) through whom the prohibition was conveyed. Both rendered descriptively; neither named in the source.
  • pastori meo — "my pastor": the parish priest of Hven, Jens Jensen Wensøsil, whose troubles Tycho here attributes to "hatred of me"; not named in the source.
  • reprobis istis insularibus — "those reprobate islanders": the peasants/tenants of Hven, with whom Tycho had jurisdictional disputes; jure in iisdem coercendis inhibito = "the right of coercing them [having been] forbidden [me]," i.e. his disciplinary jurisdiction over the islanders was curtailed.
  • astronomicae artis instavrationem — "the restoration of the astronomical art": Tycho's programme of instauratio astronomiae (the edition's convention, instauratio → restoration/renewal); the word is his own self-description of his life's work.
  • astronomicorum instrumentorum conservationem … apparatus astronomicus — "the upkeep of the astronomical instruments … that whole astronomical apparatus": Tycho's instruments (the edition's convention, organa/instrumenta → instruments) at Uraniborg/Stjerneborg, whose state maintenance the chancellor declared the king was not free to fund.
  • senatusconsulti / senatorum / regni senatorum — senatus here = the Danish Rigsråd (Council of the Realm); senatusconsultum = "the resolution of the council," senatores = the councillors who sealed the parchment guarantees. Rendered "council / senators" consistently.
  • Editorial readings. The source-of-record carries an editorial query at non penitus te cognovisse [?credo] — the apodosis verb (here credo, "I believe") is supplied/uncertain; kept in brackets in both Source and translation, the conjecture flagged. laudat. mem. and m[anu]s[cripto] are abbreviations expanded in brackets.
  • Calendar / dating. The piece is undated in the source; the edition label is "1597? empty" (a conjectural year, no day). The content — loss of the Roskilde prebend, the prohibition in Christian IV's absence, the dismantling of the instruments "while I lived and looked on" — fits 1597–98, after the June 1597 departure. The header therefore carries no asserted dateline; the conjectural [1597?] is given in brackets, O.S. (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). No date is converted or corrected.
  • Orthography. Editorial/early-modern Latin spellings preserved in the Source block: cavsae, cavte, qvod, qvi, instavrationem, lavdati, acqvirere, qvicqvam, deniqve (u for v / v for u, qv for qu); Roschildensi (Roskilde); Hafniae = Copenhagen.
  • Correction. Removed a fabricated collation note that asserted Dreyer prints "Calidius"; Dreyer's page (TBOO XIV p.123) in fact reads "Caffolius" (=Cassolius), identical to the working transcription — there is no textual variant here.
