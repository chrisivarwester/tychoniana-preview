The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTIANUM LONGOMONTANUM.

INVENI peculiari Dei providentia et dono Caesarem hunc RUDOLPHUM Secundum mei et eorum, quae tracto, studiorum clementi favore amantissimum, qui satis liberaliter mihi et meis hanc ob rem prospicere non est tergiversatus, veluti alias, Deo volente, ipsius Caesareae Maiestatis erga me benignissimam voluntatem, ubi adfueris, intelliges. Introductus quidem sum, statim ac Pragam veneram, etiam antequam audientiam apud Caesaream Maiestatem habueram, in aedes Curtianas splendide et magnifice exstructas. Verum cum praeviderem, me isthic in urbe, ob aulam tam amplam et hominum omnis generis maximam copiam, otio philosophico vix perfrui posse, exoptavi potius extra illam seorsim habitare. Quo intellecto ipsius Caesarea Maiestas postmodum tres eximias arces, unius vel etiam dimidij diei itinere Praga distantes, per quendam nobilem virum, mihi ut eo deduceret adiunctum, monstrari curavit, ut ex ijs eam, quae meae intentioni commodior videretur, eligerem. Quarum una est Brandisium (ubi haec scribo) verum quia caeteris nonnihil declivior, et propriae Caesaris delectationi, praesertim ubi venationibus indulget, destinata, hîc habitare non visum opportunum. Reliquae duae appellantur Lyssa et Benachia, ambae etiam splendidissimae et iucundissimae arces. Inter quas Benachiam (quae Venetiae Bohemorum ob amoenitatem interpretatur) praetuli, quo unâ cum omnibus meis hodie vel cras, quod Deus fortunatum velit, introducturus est hic Capitaneus Brandeisensis, ex Caesaris mandato ea de causa heri Pragam ad me evocatus. Est locus editus, ut undique libere pateat Horizon, Aedificia splendida et commoda; prope est parva civitas ad fluviolum Eifer in Albim excurrentem sita, ipsique Arci quasi contigua. Distat Praga 5 miliaribus, via existente plana, ut iter saltem sit 6 horarum. Hîc favente divina clementia, cum meis habitare constitui, donec de feudo, quod Caesarea Maiestas mihi pro me et mea familia posterisque clementissime addixit, alicubi per occasionem prospectum fuerit. Interim domum etiam opportunam Pragae ipsius Caesarea Maiestas mihi clementer concedere vult, quam, quoties eo excurrero, cum meis inhabitem, ut commodius degere et propria quadra vivere possimus, etc.

Datae festinanter in transcursu Brandisio, dum Benachiam petimus, die 20. Aug. stylo novo An. 1599.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Christian Longomontanus.

I have found, by God's special providence and gift, this Emperor Rudolf the Second most loving toward me and toward the studies I pursue, with clement favour; who, liberally enough, has not been reluctant to provide for me and mine on this account — as you will otherwise understand, God willing, when you are present, of His Imperial Majesty's most benign will toward me. I was indeed received, as soon as I had come to Prague — even before I had had audience with His Imperial Majesty — into the Curtius house, splendidly and magnificently built. But since I foresaw that there, in the city, on account of so great a court and the very large throng of men of every kind, I could scarcely enjoy philosophical leisure, I chose rather to dwell apart, outside it. This understood, His Imperial Majesty thereupon had three excellent castles — distant from Prague a day's, or even half a day's, journey — shown to me, through a certain nobleman assigned to conduct me thither, so that from them I might choose the one that should seem more convenient to my purpose. Of these, one is Brandeis (where I write this); but because it is somewhat more low-lying than the rest, and set apart for the Emperor's own pleasure — especially when he indulges in hunting — it did not seem opportune to dwell here. The remaining two are called Lysá and Benátky, both likewise most splendid and most delightful castles. Among these I have preferred Benátky (which, for its pleasantness, is rendered "the Venice of the Bohemians"), whither, together with all my people, today or tomorrow — may God grant it fortunate — this Captain of Brandeis is to install [me], having been called out to me from Prague yesterday, by the Emperor's command, for this very cause. It is an elevated place, so that the Horizon lies freely open on every side; the buildings splendid and convenient; nearby is a small town set on the little river Iser, which runs out into the Elbe, and as it were contiguous to the castle itself. It is distant from Prague five [German] miles, the road being level, so that the journey is but six hours. Here, by divine clemency's favour, I have resolved to dwell with my people, until provision shall somewhere be made, as occasion allows, concerning the fief which His Imperial Majesty most graciously assigned to me, for myself and my family and posterity. Meanwhile His Imperial Majesty also wishes graciously to grant me a convenient house at Prague itself, which, as often as I run thither, I may inhabit with my people, so that we may live more conveniently and keep our own table, etc.

Given hastily, in passage from Brandeis, while we make for Benátky, the 20th of August, New Style, in the year 1599.
Your Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender, recipient & moment. Tycho Brahe to his chief assistant Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (1562–1647), written in transit from Brandeis to Benátky on 20 August 1599 — Tycho's own eyewitness account of how he came to settle at Benátky nad Jizerou, the establishment where Kepler would join him months later. Longomontanus, then briefly returned to Denmark, is being told where to rejoin his master ("when you are present").
  • The narrative. On reaching Prague, Tycho was first lodged in the Curtius house (aedes Curtianae — the Prague palace associated with the late imperial vice-chancellor Jacob Curtius von Senftenau), even before his audience with the Emperor; but, wanting otium philosophicum away from the crowded court, he asked to live outside the city. Rudolf II then had three castles shown him — Brandeis (Brandýs nad Labem, rejected as low-lying and reserved for the Emperor's hunting), Lysá (Lyssa), and Benátky (Benachia) — and Tycho chose Benátky.
  • Persons named.
  • Caesar Rudolphus Secundus / Caesarea Maiestas = Emperor Rudolf II, throughout the gracious patron.
  • Capitaneus Brandeisensis = the Captain (governor) of Brandeis, commanded to install Tycho at Benátky (the same office whose holder's Prague wedding figures in brahe-t_16000515002).
  • quidam nobilis vir = an unnamed nobleman assigned as Tycho's guide.
  • Places & topography (valued for the Benátky description). Benachia … Venetiae Bohemorum — Benátky "the Venice of the Bohemians," a folk-etymology of the name (Czech Benátky ≈ "Venice"). Tycho prizes its elevation and free horizon (locus editus, ut undique libere pateat Horizon) — the prime requirement for an observatory. fluviolus Eifer = the river Iser / Jizera, "running out into the Elbe (Albis)"; the small town adjoins the castle. Distance from Prague: 5 German miles ≈ a 6-hour journey.
  • Terms of the grant. feudum … pro me et mea familia posterisque = a hereditary fief promised by the Emperor for Tycho and his descendants (the long-sought secure endowment, never finally settled); plus a house at Prague for his visits, so that he and his household may "keep their own table" (propria quadra vivere).
  • Edition note. The edition cites this letter to the collection Tychonis Brahei et ad eum doctorum virorum epistolae ab anno 1588; it is also printed in Dreyer's Opera Omnia (vol. VIII). The surviving witness is an afskrift (copy) in Langebek's Diplomatarium, Copenhagen.
  • Epistolary formulae. Datae festinanter → "Given hastily"; manu propria → "with his own hand."
  • Calendar. Dateline die 20. Aug. stylo novo An. 1599 — explicitly New Style, Tycho already dating Gregorian in the Emperor's lands. Reproduced, not converted.
  • Correction. Latin: 'Brundisio' corrected to 'Brandisio', and 'militaribus' corrected to 'miliaribus', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, p. 159.

Found two the transcribed original corruptions relative to Dreyer's print (Brundisio/Brandisio; militaribus/miliaribus); the first is a genuine place-name substitution (Brindisi, Italy vs. Brandeis, Bohemia) serious enough to mislead a reader relying on the transcribed original alone, even though the published English translation itself renders both correctly (Brandeis, five miles) and is not at fault. All other names, numbers, and the closing date/place ('die 20. Aug. stylo novo An. 1599') match exactly. Self-graded A; our collation finds source-text corruption warranting C despite a sound translation.

2 defects found:
- text error (medium). The transcribed original reads 'Brundisio' (evoking Brundisium/Brindisi in Italy) where Dreyer prints 'Brandisio', matching the place-name 'Brandisium' (Brandeis) used earlier in this same letter; a real, potentially misleading place-name corruption in our source. The English correctly says 'Brandeis' regardless, so the translation itself is unaffected. Dreyer prints: Datae festinanter in transcursu Brandisio, dum Benachiam petimus.
- text error (low). The transcribed original has 'militaribus' (an inserted 't', suggesting 'military') where Dreyer has 'miliaribus' (miles) - a corrupted word; the English correctly reads 'five miles' regardless. Dreyer prints: Distat Praga 5 miliaribus, via existente plana.

Repaired. Verified both defects by direct visual inspection of the Dreyer TBOO VIII pages containing this letter (pp.158-159 within the supplied 30-page extract). Both corrections are unambiguous in the page image; the 'miliaribus' spelling was cross-checked against a second, clearer occurrence of the same word later on p.159. Neither correction affects the English translation, which was already faithful to Dreyer's readings. No other divergence from Dreyer was found in the letter text itself.
