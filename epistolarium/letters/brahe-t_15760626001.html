The original

NOBILI ET GENEROSO VIRO TYCHONI BRAHE A KNUDSTORP, AMICO SUO PLURIMUM COLENDO.

GENEROSE et clarissime vir, Domine et amice perpetuo colende, recordari mei PRATENSIS sine lacrymis non possum, cum animæ plus quam dimidium perdiderim meæ. At cum dolor iste nobis communis sit, ego frustra me affligendo tibi molestus esse desinam, et tuis literis respondebo. Quæ certe mihi fuerunt gratissimæ, et plurimum profuerunt. Nam illæ me veterno quodam oppressum excitarunt, et quasi in tenebris lumine privatum errantem in rectam reduxerunt viam, ut ea, quæ me decent, præstarem. Scias igitur, mi amantissime TYCHO, lapidem angularem paratum esse, sed cum inscriptionem seu dedicationem, et quædam alia mystica sacraque ille optimæ recordationis PRATENSIS sibi reservasset, iurareque voluisset, ego mihi (quod tantus vir facile conficere potuisset) tribuere non ausus sum, ut quicquam in ijs, nisi te mandante, vel potius præscribente, tentare voluerim. Te igitur oro, si Mercurium tibi caducei usuram negare non velis (illius enim verbis lubenter utor) ut, quod tui studiosissimus in se receperat, et mihi iure quodam debebat, tute perficias et persolvas, vel ego cogar (etsi invitus) nescio quid in te desiderare. Ut autem hac in re facilior promptiorque sis et mihi citius respondeas, ne magnum illud opus nostra mora et tua culpa remoretur, oro ut ijs, quæ in schedula scripsi, addas, immutes, subtrahas, vel ut uno verbo omnia complectar, una litura deleas, et statuas, quid sit faciendum, quid scribendum, cum id facile efficere queas, et ego tibi obsequar lubentissime. Quod si me tui amantissimum esse et observantissimum credideris, non falleris.

Vale, Vir præstantissime, et cito rescribe.
Haffniæ 26. die Iunij Anno 1576.
Tibi deditissimus amicus
Carolus Danzeus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the noble and high-born gentleman Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, his most esteemed friend.

High-born and most renowned Sir, my Lord and ever-to-be-esteemed friend: I cannot call to mind my dear Pratensis without tears, since I have lost more than the half of my own soul. But since that grief is shared between us, I shall cease to be troublesome to you by afflicting myself in vain, and I shall reply to your letter — which was assuredly most welcome to me, and profited me greatly. For it roused me, oppressed as I was by a kind of lethargy, and, as one wandering in darkness deprived of light, led me back into the right road, that I might perform the things which befit me. Know then, my most beloved Tycho, that the cornerstone is ready; but since that Pratensis of most excellent memory had reserved to himself the inscription, or dedication, and certain other secret and sacred matters, and had wished to take an oath upon it, I have not dared to take upon myself (a thing which so great a man could easily have accomplished) the attempting of anything in these matters, except at your bidding, or rather your prescription. I beg you, therefore — if you will not refuse Mercury the loan of his herald's-staff for your use (for I gladly use his words) — that what one most devoted to you had taken upon himself, and by a certain right owed to me, you yourself may carry through and discharge; or else I shall be compelled (though unwilling) to find some lack in you, I know not what. And so that you may be the more accommodating and ready in this matter and reply to me the sooner — lest that great work be delayed by our procrastination and your fault — I beg that to the things I have written on the slip you add, alter, take away, or (to embrace all in one word) blot out with a single stroke of the pen, and determine what is to be done, what is to be written, since you can easily bring it about, and I shall comply with you most gladly. And if you will believe me to be most loving and most respectful of you, you will not be deceived.

Farewell, most excellent Sir, and write back quickly.
At Copenhagen, the 26th day of June, the year 1576.
Your most devoted friend,
Charles de Dançay.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Carolus Danzeus = Charles de Dançay (c. 1510–1589), the long-serving French resident ambassador at the Danish court of Frederik II, a humanist and patron of learning and one of Tycho's older friends and protectors during the Uraniborg years. He signs Tibi deditissimus amicus ("Your most devoted friend").
  • Addressee superscription. TYCHONI BRAHE A KNUDSTORP — "Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup," naming the family seat (Knutstorp in Scania); kept by the edition's convention
  • Pratensis = Johannes Pratensis (Hans Philipsen, 1543–1576), professor of medicine at Copenhagen and Tycho's intimate physician-friend. Dançay's opening lament — animae plus quam dimidium perdiderim meae ("I have lost more than the half of my own soul") — and the phrase ille optimae recordationis Pratensis ("that Pratensis of most excellent memory") place the letter shortly after Pratensis's death (he died in May 1576); the dolor … nobis communis ("grief shared between us") is the bereavement Dançay and Tycho hold in common. The Horatian echo (animae dimidium meae, Horace, Odes I.3.8) is preserved in the literal rendering.
  • lapis angularis ("the cornerstone"). The foundation/cornerstone of Uraniborg, Tycho's observatory-castle on Hven, whose building was begun in 1576; the cornerstone was laid on 8 August 1576. paratum esse = "is ready / prepared." The letter shows Dançay overseeing the preparation of the stone in Copenhagen and awaiting from Tycho the wording of its dedicatory inscription. Crux flagged: the Latin says only lapidem angularem paratum esse — that the stone is ready/prepared, not that it is laid; no completed-construction sense is read in. Nothing beyond the as-printed claim is asserted.
  • inscriptionem seu dedicationem, et quaedam alia mystica sacraque — "the inscription, or dedication, and certain other secret and sacred matters." Pratensis had reserved to himself (sibi reservasset) the composing of the cornerstone's dedicatory inscription and certain undisclosed "mystic and sacred" elements, and had wished to bind it by oath (iurareque voluisset); with his death, Dançay asks Tycho himself to supply the wording. mystica sacraque is left as "secret and sacred," not narrowed to a specific rite, since the text does not specify.
  • Mercurium … caducei usuram ("Mercury … the loan of his herald's-staff"). A learned conceit: Dançay asks Tycho to lend the eloquence of Mercury (god of messengers and of letters) — i.e. to compose the inscription's words. caduceus = Mercury's herald's-staff (caduceus); usuram … negare = "to refuse the use/loan of." The parenthesis illius enim verbis lubenter utor ("for I gladly use his words") signals that the conceit is Dançay's playful borrowing of Mercurial idiom; kept as figure, not flattened.
  • quod … in se receperat, et mihi iure quodam debebat — "what [Pratensis], most devoted to you, had taken upon himself, and by a certain right owed to me." The subject of receperat / debebat is the late Pratensis, who had undertaken the task; Dançay now presses Tycho to "carry it through and discharge" (perficias et persolvas) the obligation in his stead. Read with care: the hedged nescio quid in te desiderare ("to find some lack in you, I know not what") is a courteous, deliberately vague half-threat, preserved with its qualifier (etsi invitus, "though unwilling").
  • schedula — "the slip / note": a small sheet (the draft text Dançay enclosed) that he invites Tycho to amend; una litura deleas = "blot [it] out with a single stroke of the pen," the standard idiom for editorial deletion.
  • magnum illud opus — "that great work": the building of Uraniborg, of which the cornerstone is the present concern.
  • Negation check. The letter contains the conditional courtesy si … negare non velis ("if you will not refuse"), the modesty topos tribuere non ausus sum ("I have not dared to take upon myself"), and the closing non falleris ("you will not be deceived"); each negation is reproduced, none added or dropped. The nisi te mandante, vel potius praescribente ("except at your bidding, or rather your prescription") is an exceptive, not a negation, and is rendered as such.
  • Calendar / place. Haffniae 26. die Iunij Anno 1576 = "At Copenhagen (Hafnia), the 26th day of June, the year 1576" (O.S.; Denmark used the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the edition label.
  • MS witnesses. Two copies in Vienna, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686 (item.7 = A; item.66 = B), as given in the source header; shelfmarks reproduced as supplied.
