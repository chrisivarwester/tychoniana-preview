The original

NOBILISS. ET CLARISS. VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, HWENAe INSULAe DOMINO, REGIS DANIAe CONSILIARIO ET MATHEMATICO DOCTISSIMO, AMICO SUO HVENAM DANIAe MITTANTUR.
NOBILISS. ET CLARISS. VIR, AMICE COLENDE, S. P.

ANTE biduum nobis D. FULLERUS noster obviam factus inter alia retulit, quam honorifice abs te fuerit acceptus, quam admiranda viderit, et quod legerit, quae ad primas meas responderis, ubi ego: iamdudum mihi constat, in illa Uraniae arce cum summa humanitate eximiam scientiam habitare; sed sciscitatus sum, an meminerit, quae de mota quaestione scripseris? Non, inquit, nam pleraque captum meum superabant; hoc tantum percepi, plures sententiae suae demonstrationes afferri. Demonstrationes ego libenter intelligam, et si constiterint, facile ijs cedam, quique hoc ὅτι sic docuerit, maioris apud me authoritatis hac ex parte, quam ARISTOTELES erit, quod authoritas ex veritate aestimanda sit, non haec ex illa, nisi in Divinis, ubi ipsa Veritas ἔφα. Verum si coniecturis agatur, plus habeant probabilitatis oportet quam contrariae, ut maiorem fidem mereantur. Ego, clariss. vir, si quas petij longitudines accepissem, iamdudum quid veri hîc esset, demonstratione explorassem; alioqui in hac quaestione versari non libet. Nam nunc etiam peto, ut quos in Numeris Canonis Sinuum errores longo usu tuo cognoveris, prima occasione mecum communices; nam nobis singulare quiddam prae manibus est, in quo perficiendo ingentem opem hic Canon praestat, sique error suberit, nobis cum fenore suppeditabitur. Vel ijs essem contentus, qui sunt infra 60 (quamvis et reliquos ascribas) nam qui supra sunt, singulari compendio exhibere potero, quod hîc amicitiae nostrae causa ascribam.* [Footnote 1 — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 240; dropped by the working transcription: "Canon, quo ipse utor, Rheinoldi est excusus Tubingae anno 1554 apud heredes Morhardi, tabulis directionum insertus."]

Figur

Duorum arcuum trientem complentium sinus semidifferentiae est differentia sinuum (eorundem arcuum). Sint duo arcus ab, ad complentes trientem, quia ac est 60, et quanto minor ab deficit ab ac, tanto maior ad superat. Differentia est bd, sinus dimidij differentiae est ed, quem demonstro esse aequalem lineae dg, quae differentia est duorum sinuum dk et bm seu gk. Cum ilk sit 60, erit kil 30 et uterque gfi et bfe erit 60, quare totus efg erit 120. In duobus orthogonijs latera be et ed sunt aequalia et ef commune, quare reliqua reliquis erunt aequalia, et angulus bfe aequalis efd angulo, qui erit 60, et tertius angulus dfg erit 60; in duobus orthogonijs efd et dfg anguli efd et dfg sunt aequales et latus df commune, quare reliqua reliquis aequantur, et latus ed aequale erit lateri dg, quod propositum fuit. Itaque dabitur quilibet sinus supra 60, si sinui arcus tanto minoris 60 addatur sinus dimidij differentiae duorum arcuum, ut ed ad bm facit dk; sic Canon Sinuum tertia parte minuetur.

Canonem fecundum dimidio abbreviabo alio compendio, quod etiam hîc annotabo. Duorum arcuum quadrantem complentium semidifferentia fecundorum est fecundus differentiae eorundem. Sint duo arcus cb, bf quadrantem aequantes, quia caf rectus est, fecundus cb est db, fecundus bf est bh, differentia duorum arcuum est be, cuius fecundus est bg, quem dico esse dimidium bm differentiae inter duos fecundos. Diametris sc. ai et dh se secantibus, erit hi aequalis ad, et ob id erit aequalis dk arcui, cum ad et dk sint aequales; ergo angulus dak aequalis erit iah, et ablato igitur cb seu eius aequali ef ex bf, relinquitur be differentia duorum arcuum. Rursum db, cui sit aequalis mh, aufero a bh, remanebit bm, cuius dimidium est bg; nam ab aequalibus semidiametris dg et gh, aequalibus db et mh ablatis, remanent bg et gm aequalia. Si igitur detur fecundus db et quaeritur fecundus bh, aufero arcum cb a bf et differentiae be fecundum in canone quaero bg, hunc duplico, erit bm, et addo fecundum minoris mh, proveniet fecundus totius. Si differentia be fuerit maior 45 gradibus, duplici operatione opus erit, priori reperietur fecundus differentiae, posteriori fecundus totius.

Quia in has speculationes incidi et figura servit, tertium ascribam, quo omnem RHETICI Canonem, de quo tantopere gloriatur, eximio fecundi usu tollam, ut dabo hypothenusam ad in partibus, qualium db sinus est sinus totus, db sinu in fecundo reperto, ut sit 3420201, arcus 20 erit angulo 18° 52½ , ut db fecundus 3420201 ad ab S. T. sic ab S. T. ad bh fecundum supplementi anguli 18° 52½ , qui ex Canone dabitur; est enim ab medium proportionale inter duo fecundos. Ut ergo ab S. T. ad bh fecundum, sic erit db sinus 3420201 ad S. T. db fecundus 3420201 est ad ad S. T. ut ab S. T. ad fecundum bh; sed db sinus 3420201 est ad ad S. T. ut db fecundus 3420201 ad ab S. T. Ergo db sinus 3420201 est ad ad S. T. ut S. T. ab ad fecundum bh; dabitur igitur in Canone fecundus bh et erit ad in eo numero partium qualium db est S.T. ad ad, quia termini non mutantur. Et idem numerus qui in priori fecundus, inde sinus est; abiectis igitur his terminis, si db ponatur sinus totus, erit ad fecundus supplementi anguli 18 52½ . Simili modo si detur sinus ab, dabitur ad in partibus qualium ab est S. T. Non vulgaris hic usus est fecundi Canonis, quo maxima reperiuntur, de quibus alias pluribus conferemus, nam nihil apud me horum secretorum erit, quin libenter ut ea suggeret memoria communicabo: tu haec tanquam amicitiae nostrae μνημόσυνοv boni consule.

Discessit hinc Sereniss. Rex noster praeter omnium opinionem et expectationem; nam mihi proficiscendum unâ fuit, sed hyeme valetudinem terrente, locus fuit excusationi, sed si de abitu eius constitisset, nihil inde deterruisset, nunc vero discussa prope hyeme cum redeundi occasio redeat, faxit omnipotens Deus, ut incolumis cum Sereniss. Regina nostra quam primum revertatur, hoc communibus et privatis votis assidue petitur. In Illustriss. Reginae comitatu ferunt Medicum esse D. ANDREAM KRAGIUM, qui tamen hîc non sit moraturus, sed ex nostratibus quispiam sufficiendus; id si ita fuerit, cuperem mei haberi rationem, siquidem eo loco dignus esse potero, non deerit nostratium D. D. consiliariorum promotio, quibus notus sum, et cum quibus sanguinis necessitudo intercedit. Vestratium tu conciliare mihi poteris, quod quasi ex te profectum sit, facias amanter peto, Domino fratre tuo et D. KRAGIO literis tuis in mei favorem et commendationem salutatis. Ideo hic locus acceptus esset, quod praxis nostra cum summa corporis nostri molestia coniuncta sit, nam maxima in equis itinera quacunque coeli tempestate nobis saepe conficienda sunt, quibus corpus meum minus nunc idoneum esse experior, ut multo acceptior sit futura, quae cum minore corporis labore est, quare rogo, ut tuo beneficio, si fieri id posset, melius valetudini meae consulere queam. Sed ita velim ad eos scribas, ut si quaeratur Illustriss. Reginae nostrae Medicus, tuo amico ipsi omnibus modis faveant, idque quasi ex teipso; vides, quantum mihi ex amicitia nostra pollicear, quam non pudeat res meas ei credere, quod certe minime facerem, nisi singularem de tua erga me benevolentia spem haberem. Huius anni Eclipses quam accurate observaturus sis, facile intelligo; nobis id non datur, nam nec sunt organa, quibus fidere possumus, nec artifices, qui nobis parent. Momenta initij et finis ac medij (praeter visualem Diametrum) definienda existimo datis fixarum altitudinibus, nam altitudo, ut principium, sensu per instrumentum notum dari, non ex alijs demonstrari debet. Sed finem faciam, ne sim molestior. Coeli et Terrae Dominus tibi adsit, tuisque faveat conatibus et incolumem conservet, tui amantissimum tuis literis revise.

Datae Edinburgi pridie Calend. Martij.
Tuus IOAN. CRAIGUS, philosophiae et medicinae doctor.

To the most noble and most distinguished man, Master Tycho Brahe, Lord of the island of Hven, Counsellor to the King of Denmark and most learned mathematician, his friend — to be conveyed to Hven in Denmark.
Most noble and most distinguished sir, friend to be honoured, heartiest greeting.

Two days ago our [friend] Master Fuller, having met me, reported among other things how honourably he had been received by you, what marvellous things he had seen, and that he had read what you had replied to my first [letter] — whereupon I [said]: it has long been settled in my mind that in that citadel of Urania a singular learning dwells together with the utmost courtesy; but I asked whether he remembered what you had written about the question that was raised. "No," he said, "for most of it was beyond my grasp; I gathered only this much, that several demonstrations of his own view are brought forward." Demonstrations I shall gladly understand, and if they hold good I shall readily yield to them; and whoever shall have taught me this that [ὅτι] in such a way will be, in this matter, of greater authority with me than Aristotle — for authority is to be assessed from truth, not truth from authority, except in things divine, where Truth itself has spoken [ἔφα]. But if the matter is conducted by conjectures, they must have more probability than the contrary ones, that they may earn greater credit. I, most distinguished sir, had I received the longitudes I sought, would long since have explored by demonstration what truth there is here; otherwise I have no wish to busy myself with this question. For now too I ask that, at the first occasion, you communicate to me whatever errors in the Numbers of the Canon of Sines you have come to know by your long use [of it]; for we have a certain singular [undertaking] in hand, in the perfecting of which this Canon affords mighty help, and if an error lurk [in it], it will be repaid to us with interest. I should be content even with those [sines] which are below 60 (although you might write out the rest as well), for those that are above I shall be able to exhibit by a singular compendium, which I here append for our friendship's sake.* [Footnote 1: "The Canon which I myself use is Rheinhold's, printed at Tübingen in the year 1554 by the heirs of Morhard, inserted in the Tables of Directions."]

[Figure]

The sine of the half-difference of two arcs filling out a third [of the quadrant, i.e. summing to 60°] is the difference of the sines (of those same arcs). Let there be two arcs ab, ad filling out a third [of the quadrant], since ac is 60; and by however much the lesser, ab, falls short of ac, by so much the greater, ad, exceeds [it]. The difference is bd; the sine of half the difference is ed, which I demonstrate to be equal to the line dg, which is the difference of the two sines dk and bm (that is, gk). Since ilk is 60, kil will be 30, and each [angle] gfi and bfe will be 60; wherefore the whole [angle] efg will be 120. In the two right [triangles] the sides be and ed are equal, and ef [is] common; wherefore the remaining [parts] will be equal to the remaining, and the angle bfe equal to the angle efd, which will be 60, and the third angle dfg will be 60. In the two right [triangles] efd and dfg the angles efd and dfg are equal and the side df [is] common; wherefore the remaining [parts] are equal to the remaining, and the side ed will be equal to the side dg — which was [the thing] proposed. And so any sine above 60 will be given, if to the sine of an arc as much below 60 there be added the sine of half the difference of the two arcs, as ed added to bm makes dk; thus the Canon of Sines will be lessened by a third part.

I shall abbreviate the secundus [versed-sine] Canon by a half, by another compendium, which I shall also note here. The half-difference of the secundi [versed sines] of two arcs filling out a quadrant is the secundus [versed sine] of the difference of those same [arcs]. Let there be two arcs cb, bf equalling a quadrant, since caf is a right [angle]; the secundus of cb is db, the secundus of bf is bh, the difference of the two arcs is be, whose secundus is bg, which I say to be half of bm, the difference between the two secundi. The diameters, namely ai and dh, cutting one another, hi will be equal to ad, and on that account [the arc hi] will be equal to the arc dk, since ad and dk are equal; therefore the angle dak will be equal to iah; and so, cb (or its equal ef) being taken from bf, there is left be, the difference of the two arcs. Again, db — to which let mh be equal — I take from bh; there will remain bm, whose half is bg; for from the equal semidiameters dg and gh, the equals db and mh being taken away, there remain bg and gm equal. If therefore the secundus db be given and the secundus bh is sought, I take the arc cb from bf, and seek in the canon bg, the secundus of the difference be; this I double — it will be bm — and I add the secundus of the lesser, mh: the secundus of the whole will result. If the difference be be greater than 45 degrees, there will be need of a double operation: by the former the secundus of the difference will be found, by the latter the secundus of the whole.

[Figure]

Since I have fallen upon these speculations and the figure serves [the purpose], I shall append a third, by which I may do away with the whole Canon of Rheticus — in which he glories so greatly — by an outstanding use of the secundus [versed sine]: so that I shall give the hypotenuse ad in [such] parts as those of which db, the sine, is the whole sine [the radius]; db having been found in the secundus, so that it be 3420201, the arc 20[°] will [correspond] to an angle of 18° 52½′; as db the secundus 3420201 is to ab the whole sine [radius], so is ab the whole sine to bh the secundus of the supplement of the angle 18° 52½′, which will be given from the Canon — for ab is the mean proportional between the two secundi. As therefore ab the whole sine is to bh the secundus, so will db the sine 3420201 be to the whole sine. db the secundus 3420201 is to ad the whole sine as ab the whole sine to the secundus bh; but db the sine 3420201 is to ad the whole sine as db the secundus 3420201 to ab the whole sine. Therefore db the sine 3420201 is to ad the whole sine as the whole sine ab to the secundus bh; therefore the secundus bh will be given in the Canon, and ad will be in that number of parts of which db is the whole sine — [the ratio] ad to ad [being unity], since the terms are not changed. And the same number which [stands] as secundus in the former [case] is thence the sine; with these terms cast off, therefore, if db be set as the whole sine, ad will be the secundus of the supplement of the angle 18° 52½′. In like manner, if the sine ab be given, ad will be given in parts of which ab is the whole sine. This use of the secundus Canon is no common one, by which the greatest [things] are found, about which we shall confer more at another time; for none of these secrets will be [withheld] by me, but I shall gladly communicate them as memory shall suggest them: take these things in good part, as a memorial [μνημόσυνον], as it were, of our friendship.

Our most serene King departed hence beyond everyone's opinion and expectation; for I too had to set out with [him], but, the winter making one fear for [his] health, there was room for an excuse — though, had his departure been [firmly] settled, nothing would have deterred [me] from it. But now, the winter being nearly dispelled, since the occasion for returning comes round [again], may almighty God grant that he return safe, together with our most serene Queen, as soon as possible — this is sought unceasingly by public and private prayers. In the most illustrious Queen's retinue they say the physician is Master Andrew Krag, who, however, is not to remain here, but someone from among our own [countrymen] is to be supplied [in his place]; and if that be so, I could wish that account be taken of me — since I shall be able to be worthy of that post, the recommendation of our own lordly counsellors, to whom I am known and with whom there is a tie of blood, will not be lacking. Those of your [side] you can win over for me — which I lovingly ask you to do, as though it had proceeded from yourself — your lord brother and Master Krag being saluted in your letters in my favour and commendation. This post would be welcome for this reason, that our practice is bound up with the utmost trouble to our body; for we often have to accomplish very great journeys on horseback in whatever weather of the sky, for which I now find my body less fit, so that one will be much more welcome that is [attended] with less labour of the body; wherefore I ask that, by your good offices, if it could be done, I may be able to provide better for my health. But I should wish you to write to them in such a way that, if a physician for our most illustrious Queen be sought, they may by all means favour your friend himself — and that as though [it came] from your own self. You see how much I promise myself from our friendship, that I am not ashamed to entrust my affairs to it — which assuredly I should by no means do, had I not a singular hope of your goodwill towards me. How accurately you will observe this year's eclipses, I readily understand; to us that is not granted, for we have neither instruments in which we can trust, nor craftsmen who will obey us. The moments of the beginning and the end and the middle (apart from the visual diameter) I judge must be determined from given altitudes of the fixed [stars]; for altitude, as a first principle, ought to be given by sense through a known instrument, not demonstrated from other [things]. But I shall make an end, lest I be too troublesome. May the Lord of Heaven and Earth be with you, and favour your endeavours and keep you safe; revisit him who most dearly loves you with your letters.

Given at Edinburgh, the day before the Kalends of March.
Yours, John Craig, doctor of philosophy and of medicine.

[Postscript:] If you should have anything remarkable in chemical matters that you think ought to be shared with a friend, I should wish you to do so.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Ioan. Craigus = John Craig (d. 1620), the Scottish physician and mathematician, an Aristotelian who from c. 1588 disputed Tycho's astronomical and trigonometrical positions; he later became chief physician to James VI/I. Tycho gave Craig an inscribed copy of De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (Uraniborg, 1588), now in Edinburgh University Library, and "wrote out a long defence of his ideas against a challenge by Craig, for which Craig later apologized" (Gingerich, Census; cf. A. Mosley, "Tycho Brahe and John Craig: The Dynamic of a Dispute," in Tycho Brahe and Prague). The signature philosophiae et medicinae doctor fits this John Craig.
  • Recipient address. Tychoni Brahe, Hwenae insulae domino, Regis Daniae consiliario et mathematico — Tycho on Hven, the king's counsellor and mathematician; the cover instruction Hvenam Daniae mittantur ("let it be sent to Hven in Denmark") is the postal direction, kept.
  • D. Fullerus noster — "our Master Fuller," a Scottish intermediary (evidently recently a visitor to Uraniborg) who carried news between Craig and Tycho; not further identified here.
  • mota quaestione — "the question that was raised": the standing Tycho–Craig controversy (over cometary parallax / the structure of the heavens and the trigonometric methods bound up with it).
  • Greek. (1) ὅτι ("the that"): the fact (to hoti), as opposed to the reasoned cause (to dioti) — a scholastic/Aristotelian distinction; rendered "this that" with gloss. (2) ἔφα (Doric for ἔφη, "[he] has spoken"): an echo of the Pythagorean αὐτὸς ἔφα ("he himself said it") transferred to divine Truth — "where Truth itself has spoken." (3) μνημόσυνοv: "memorial, keepsake" (mnēmosynon); printed with a final v for ν in the source, kept verbatim, named in the translation. All three transcribed and glossed by the edition's convention
  • Subject of the letter — trigonometric compendia. Craig sends three geometric demonstrations for abbreviating the standard tables, the prosthaphaeresis-adjacent computing economies that lay at the heart of the dispute and of Tycho's manual of trigonometry (on which see Dreyer, "On Tycho Brahe's Manual of Trigonometry," The Observatory 39 (1916): 127–131; Thoren, "Prosthaphaeresis Revisited," Historia Mathematica 1988):
  • (i) the table of sines can be shortened by a third, because the sine of any arc above 60° equals the sine of an arc as far below 60° plus the sine of half their difference;
  • (ii) the table of secundi can be halved by an analogous relation across the quadrant;
  • (iii) Rheticus's whole canon (the Opus palatinum/great canon of Rheticus, Georg Joachim Rheticus, 1514–74) can be dispensed with by exploiting the secundus.
  • fecundus (printed form; the editorial crux of the technical sections). Dreyer's text and the working transcription read fecundus / fecundorum / fecundi throughout the second and third demonstrations. This is read here as the sinus secundus — i.e. the versed sine (sinus versus, the "second sine" of period usage: radius minus cosine) — the f standing for long-s / Craig's own spelling. The mathematics is internally consistent only on this reading (the secundus of an arc and of its supplement, the mean-proportional relation ab² = secundus(arc) · secundus(supplement)·R, and the value 3420201 for the radius-scaled versed sine of 20°). The printed fecundus is retained verbatim in the Source block; the translation renders it "secundus (versed sine)" and the reading is flagged here. Crux named: fecundus = secundus/versed sine.
  • Diagram letters and numbers. The lettered points (ab, ad, ac, bd, ed, dg, dk, bm, gk; cb, bf, db, bh, be, bg, mh, gm; ai, dh, hi, ad) are the figure labels; left untranslated as labels. S. T. = sinus totus, the whole sine (the radius, the tabular maximum), rendered "whole sine [radius]." 3420201 is the radius-scaled value Craig computes. 18° 52½′ and the arc "20" are kept as printed. "IΛ7": a garbled numeral in the first demonstration (the context — kil = 30, the angles summing to 120° in triangle efg — requires the angle gfi = 30°); the printed IΛ7 (apparently a corrupted "30") is kept verbatim in the Source block and not silently corrected. Crux named: the figure value IΛ7.
  • tanquam amias tiliae nostrae μνημόσυνον — textual crux. amias tiliae yields no Latin sense and is read as a corruption of amicitiae nostrae ("of our friendship"), giving "as a memorial, as it were, of our friendship" — coherent with the immediately preceding pledge to share his "secrets" with Tycho. The printed amias tiliae is kept verbatim in the Source block; the emendation is signalled in the translation. Crux named: amias tiliae → amicitiae.
  • Court news. Sereniss. Rex noster … cum Sereniss. Regina nostra — James VI of Scotland and his queen, Anne of Denmark: James sailed to Norway/Denmark in late 1589 to fetch Anne (whose autumn crossing had been storm-driven back), wintering at Kronborg/Copenhagen and returning in spring 1590 — exactly the "departure beyond expectation" and hoped-for spring return Craig describes. The Danish sojourn is the occasion of the whole letter (Craig angling, via Tycho, for the post of queen's physician).
  • D. Andream Kragium / D. Kragio — Andreas Krag (Anders Krag), a Danish physician then attending Queen Anne's retinue; Craig (using the near-homonym to his own name without comment) hopes to succeed him if Krag does not stay on, and asks Tycho to commend him to Krag and to Tycho's "lord brother" (Domino fratre tuo, presumably Steen Brahe or another of Tycho's brothers).
  • Eclipse method. organa … artifices = instruments … craftsmen: Craig laments having neither trustworthy instruments nor instrument-makers, unlike Tycho. He holds that the momenta (moments) of an eclipse's beginning, middle, and end should be fixed from observed altitudes of fixed stars through a known instrument — altitude being a sensory first principle (principium), not a quantity to be demonstrated from others — praeter visualem Diametrum ("apart from the visual [apparent] diameter").
  • Calendar / place. Datae Edinburgi pridie Calend. Martij = "Given at Edinburgh, on the day before the Kalends of March" = 28 February (1590 being a common year). Scotland reckoned by the Julian calendar (Old Style); the date is O.S. The edition label ("1590. 28. februar. Edinburgh") agrees with the dateline; the year 1590 is not in the dateline itself but is supplied by the catalogue and by the James-VI/Anne-of-Denmark context. Roman-calendar idiom kept and glossed by the edition's convention.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (before the signature), restored from Dreyer VII, p. 242 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. Four defects corrected per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII pp.240-241: a garbled marginal line-number 'IΛ7' fused into the geometric proof text removed (both angles read simply '60'); the corrupted present-plural 'suppeditantur' restored to the future-singular 'suppeditabitur' (a promise of recompense, not a claim about compounding errors); a dropped footnote (Dreyer's footnote 1, p.240, naming Rheinhold's Canon printed at Tübingen 1554 by the heirs of Morhard) restored in place in both Latin and English; and the line-break-hyphenated 'ami-citiae' (mangled to 'amias tiliae') restored to 'amicitiae'. English corrected at the three places where sense was affected; the English for the amicitiae fix needed no change as it already carried the right sense.

Our Latin corrupts Dreyer's future-singular 'suppeditabitur' (it will be repaid to us with interest, i.e. Craig promising to reward Tycho generously for corrections) into a present-plural 'suppeditantur'; the English then compounds this by inventing a bracketed gloss ('the errors are passed on, compounded') with no counterpart in Dreyer, reversing the sense from a friendly promise of recompense into a nonsense claim about errors accumulating. Dreyer prints: fique error fuberit, nobis cum fenore fuppeditabitur (p.240, line 5).
- text error (high). Dreyer's sentence states both angles gfi and bfe equal 60 ('uterque... et... erit 60'). The transcribed original fused the printed marginal line-number '15' into the sentence as garbled 'IΛ7', and the English then literally translates this artifact as if it were a real geometric value ('gfi will be IΛ7'), fabricating a nonsensical quantity a mathematically literate reader would immediately flag. Dreyer prints: erit kil 30 et uterque gfi et bfe erit 60 (p.240, line c. 13, the '15' after 'gfi' is Dreyer's marginal line-count marker, not text).
- text error (medium). Dreyer prints a genuine footnote here naming the sine canon Craig uses (Rheinhold's, printed at Tubingen in 1554 by the heirs of Morhard, inserted in the Tables of Directions) - a proper name, place, and date. The transcribed original drops this footnote's content entirely and instead attaches its asterisk marker to a differently-located, unrelated postscript ('Si quid singulare in chymicis...', actually printed beside the signature on p.242), misleading the reader about what was recovered versus what remains missing. Dreyer prints: footnote 1, p.240: 'Canon, quo ipse utor, Rheinoldi est excusus Tubingae anno 1554 apud heredes Morhardi, tabulis directionum insertus.'.
- text error (low). The transcribed original mangles the line-break-hyphenated word 'ami-citiae' (amicitiae, friendship) into 'amias tiliae', a text error versus Dreyer. The English translator correctly guessed the intended sense ('of our friendship') despite the corrupted source, so no defect propagates into the translation itself. Dreyer prints: tu haec tanquam ami-citiae nostrae μνημόσυνον boni consule (p.241, word split across a line-break hyphen).

Repaired. All four defects verified directly against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.239-241 page images (not the digitised text layer): the geometric proof (p.240 l.14), suppeditabitur (p.240 l.5), footnote 1 (p.240, bottom), and the ami-citiae line break (p.241 ll.23-24) all read exactly as the audit's Dreyer claim states. No other part of the letter was touched; every changed word is accounted for in outcomes above.
