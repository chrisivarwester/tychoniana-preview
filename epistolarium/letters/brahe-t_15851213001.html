The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS, VIRTUTE ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTI DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE DANO DOMINO ET AMICO OBSERVANDO.
S.

NOBILISSIME Domine TYCHO et amice observandissime, longa intercapedo locorum facit, ut rarissime per literas colloqui possimus, alioqui non intermitterem, quin te saepius meis literis salutarem, aut si iter esset saltem dierum sex aut 8, in hac mea ingravescente aetate, ipsoque senio (nam a die 1. Octob. 60 ingressus sum aetatis annum) ipsemet te convenirem. Cum autem praeter omnem meam spem et opinionem incidissem in HENRICUM meum popularem servum Magnif. D. IACOBI SEGURII Legati Sereniss. Regis Navarrae ad quosdam Germaniae principes, ac is servus Dresdâ salutandi amicos suos gratia Pragam excurrisset, diceretque se in Daniam rectâ cum Domino suo profecturum esse, nolui eum vacuum meis literis dimittere istuc neque hanc occasionem scribendi ad te praetermittere. Scias igitur me unâ cum meis omnibus integra adhuc ex Dei beneficio frui valetudine, praeterquam quod senum morbi crebrius iam me invadunt, vires animi et corporis languescunt, ut etiamsi quam maxime velim oblectare studijs Astronomicis, non possim. Taceo alia multa. Meam Dialexin auxeram iam olim aliquot problematis partim novis, partim ex DIGGSEI Alis et Scalis derivatis de invenienda parallaxi quorumcunque Phaenomenûm; in multis memet correxi a priore editione. Et ut esset veluti Synopsis quaedam scriptorum de Nova Stella, volui omnium Authorum scripta nostris consona uno volumine edere, quibus adiunxi aliquot Cometarum historias. Qua de re iam ante ad te perscripseram, ac tu rogaveras, ne tuum scriptum adiungerem, quod illud ipse elimaturus pluraque ei additurus esses. Hoc meum opus nondum quidem prodijt, quod sane non mea culpa, sed Typographorum accidit, quod Astronomica non libenter edant. Cum autem intellexissem te domi in tua Urania etiam Typographiam instituisse pro edendis tuis lucubrationibus, tractaverisque idem, quod ego, argumentum de inventione parallaxium, nactus hanc occasionem, volui mittere ad te meam illam Synopsin, ut vel ordine a me designato eam (siquidem tibi probabitur) tuis typis ederes vel excerperes inde, quae tibi utilia viderentur, tuoque operi adiungeres. Ego certe gratum nostrum studium et operam posteritati fore iudico, si plenam historiam a pluribus conscriptam novi istius sideris proposuerimus. Nihil dico de doctrina parallaxeos omnium phaenomenωn, quae hucusque a nemine fuit tradita. Tu ea omnia multo rectius intelligis, quam ut a me explicari possint. Nihil dicam de his amplius. Sed librum meum quaeso accipe et evolve, et si placet dignumque editione censes, volesque illum tuis typis edere aut tuo operi adiungere, retineto apud te. Sin displicuerit, aut aliquid intercesserit, quod tuis typis imprimi non possit, rogo ut meum librum per eundem mihi remittas. Nullum enim aliud exemplum habeo, nollemque quantulumcunque hunc meum laborem perire. Quicquid statueris de libro, ratum et firmum erit apud me. Omnem tibi in illum potestatem trado, adeoque in me, ut libere moneas et reprehendas, fidemque amicitiae in hac re mihi potissimum praestes.

B. V.
Pragae ad lucernam, quod servus ille D. SEGURIJ summo mane abitum pararet, die 13 Decemb. veteris Calendarij, nos 23 numeramus, Anno 1585
T. studiosiss.
THADD.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the Lord Tycho Brahe, the Dane, outstanding in nobility of birth, in virtue, and in learning, my lord and friend to be respected.
Greeting.

Most noble Lord Tycho and most esteemed friend, the long distance between our places makes it that we can converse by letter only very seldom; otherwise I would not fail to greet you more often by my letters — or, if the journey were of only six or eight days, in this my advancing age and very old age (for on 1 October I entered my sixtieth year) I would meet with you in person. But since, beyond all my hope and expectation, I had come upon Henricus, my fellow-countryman, a servant of the Magnificent Master Jacobus Segurius, envoy of the Most Serene King of Navarre to certain princes of Germany — and since that servant had made an excursion from Dresden to Prague for the sake of greeting his friends, and said that he would set out directly into Denmark with his master — I was unwilling to send him off thither empty of any letter of mine, nor to let slip this occasion of writing to you. Know, then, that I, together with all my household, still by God's kindness enjoy unbroken health, except that the ailments of old men now assail me more frequently, and the powers of my mind and body grow faint, so that even though I should most greatly wish to take delight in astronomical studies, I cannot. Of much else I say nothing. My Dialexis I had, some while ago now, enlarged by several problems, partly new, partly derived from Digges's Wings and Ladders, on finding the parallax of any phenomena whatsoever; in many places I corrected myself from the earlier edition. And so that there might be, as it were, a kind of Synopsis of the writings on the New Star, I wished to publish in one volume the writings of all the authors that agree with our own, to which I have added some histories of comets. Concerning this matter I had already written to you before, and you had asked that I not add your own writing, since you were going to polish it yourself and to add more to it. This work of mine has not, indeed, yet come forth — which truly has happened not through my fault, but through the printers', because they do not willingly publish astronomical [works]. But since I had learned that you too had set up a printing-house at home in your Urania, for the publishing of your lucubrations, and that you had treated the same subject as I — on the finding of parallaxes — having got this occasion, I wished to send you that Synopsis of mine, so that either you might publish it in your types in the order I have laid out (if indeed it shall meet your approval), or might excerpt from it whatever should seem useful to you and add it to your own work. I, for my part, judge that our zeal and labour will be welcome to posterity, if we shall set forth a full history of that new star, compiled by many [hands]. I say nothing of the doctrine of the parallax of all phenomena, which up to now has been handed down by no one. You understand all those things much more correctly than they can be explained by me. I shall say nothing further about these matters. But take, I pray, my book and unroll it, and if it please you and you judge it worthy of publication, and you shall wish to publish it in your types or to add it to your own work, keep it by you. But if it shall displease you, or anything shall stand in the way so that it cannot be printed in your types, I ask that you send my book back to me by the same man. For I have no other copy, and I would not wish this labour of mine, however slight, to perish. Whatever you shall determine concerning the book will be settled and firm with me. I hand over to you all power over it, and indeed over myself, so that you may freely admonish and reprove [me], and may show to me, above all in this matter, the faith of friendship.

Fare you well.
At Prague, by lamplight — because that servant of Master Segurius was making ready to depart at break of day — on 13 December of the Old Calendar, which we reckon the 23rd, in the year 1585.
Most devoted to you,
THADD[aeus].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), Prague physician, mathematician, and astronomer to the Imperial court of Rudolf II; Tycho's chief Bohemian correspondent and later instrumental in bringing him to Prague. He signs himself in the shortened form THADD.
  • Self-dating / age. a die 1. Octob. 60 ingressus sum aetatis annum — "on 1 October I entered my sixtieth year." Taken with the birth year usually given as 1525 (1 October 1525), this is internally consistent. Reproduced as written; no correction made.
  • Iacobi Segurii … Legati … Regis Navarrae — Jacques de Ségur-Pardaillan, diplomatic envoy of Henri of Navarre (the future Henri IV of France) sent in 1585 on a mission to the German Protestant princes (and onward, per this letter, toward Denmark) to build an anti-Catholic-League coalition. Sereniss. Regis Navarrae = "the Most Serene King of Navarre." HENRICUM meum popularem = "Henricus, my fellow-countryman" (i.e. a fellow Bohemian/Czech in Ségur's service) — the courier of this letter.
  • Dialexis — Hagecius's Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt am Main, 1574), his treatise on the new star (nova) of 1572. He reports having enlarged it with new problemata. Title kept in Latin.
  • DIGGSEI Alis et Scalis — Thomas Digges, Alae seu scalae mathematicae ("Wings, or Mathematical Ladders," London, Thomas Marsh, 1573), the English work on determining the parallax/distance of the 1572 nova. DIGGSEI is the as-printed (metathesised) genitive of Diggeseus; Alis et Scalis = ablative of Alae … scalae ("the Wings and Ladders"). The title is rendered descriptively and the Latin form noted.
  • Astronomy. parallaxi … parallaxeos … parallaxium → parallax throughout. invenienda parallaxi quorumcunque Phaenomenûm = "finding the parallax of any phenomena whatsoever"; doctrina parallaxeos omnium phaenomenωn = "the doctrine of the parallax of all phenomena." Here phaenomena denotes celestial appearances (novae, comets, and the like) whose distance is at issue — not "phenomena" in the modern generic sense.
  • Mixed-script spelling. Phaenomenûm (gen. pl., printed with circumflex) and especially phaenomenωn — the latter written with a Greek omega (ω) embedded in an otherwise Latin word, a learned grapho-play on the word's Greek origin (φαινόμενα). The omega is preserved in the Source block by this chapter's convention; the word is read as the Latin genitive plural phaenomenon and rendered "phenomena."
  • Nova Stella / novi … sideris → "the New Star" / "that new star" — the supernova of 1572 (B Cassiopeiae), the common subject of Hagecius's, Digges's, and Tycho's works. Synopsis — Hagecius's planned collected volume of concordant authors on the nova, with appended Cometarum historias ("histories of comets"); title-word kept.
  • in tua Urania … Typographiam instituisse — "you had set up a printing-house … in your Urania," i.e. at Uraniborg on Hven, where Tycho had established his own press for his lucubrationes (learned night-works/writings). The substance dovetails with Tycho's contemporaneous parallax work on the nova and comets.
  • Epistolary formulae. Opening S. = salutem ("greeting,"). B. V. = Bene vale ("Fare you well,"). T. studiosiss. = Tui studiosissimus ("Most devoted to you").
  • Calendar (in-letter double date — not converted by the editor). The dateline reads die 13 Decemb. veteris Calendarij, nos 23 numeramus = "13 December of the Old Calendar [Julian/Old Style], which we reckon the 23rd [Gregorian/New Style]." Writing from Imperial Prague, which had adopted the Gregorian reform, Hagecius gives both styles, accommodating Tycho's Danish Old-Style usage while noting his own New-Style count — the 10-day gap stated explicitly in the source. The header leads with the as-written O.S. date (13 December, matching the catalogue label) and appends the source's own N.S. equivalent (23 December). No silent conversion or correction has been made.
  • Pragae ad lucernam — "at Prague, by lamplight": written in haste before dawn because the courier was leaving at summo mane ("at break of day"). Place: Pragae = Prague (Hagecius's residence).
  • Minor textual point. nllem (in nllemque … hunc meum laborem perire) is an evident misprint/contraction for nollem ("I would not wish"); read as nollem and so translated. intercapedo for classical intercapedo/intercapedinem and amicitae for amicitiae are as-printed orthographic forms, silently read to sense.
  • Correction. Latin corrected: 'nllemque' to 'nollemque' and 'amicitae' to 'amicitiae', per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.99. A third proposed correction ('meam illam Synopsin' to 'illam Synopsin') was rejected on collation: Dreyer's page prints 'meam illam Synopfin', matching the existing source text exactly.

3 defects found:
- text error (low). Dreyer VII p.99 reads "volui mittere ad te illam Synopsin"; our transcribed original inserts an extra possessive "meam" not present in the printed text. Dreyer prints: illam Synopsin.
- text error (low). Dreyer prints "nollemque"; our transcribed original drops the "o", giving the garbled "nllemque". The English happens to render the intended sense correctly regardless. Dreyer prints: nollemque quantulumcunque hunc meum laborem perire.
- text error (low). Dreyer prints "amicitiae"; our transcribed original drops the medial "i", giving "amicitae". Dreyer prints: fidemque amicitiae in hac re mihi potissimum praestes.

Repaired. Collated against the Dreyer page images for this letter (TBOO VII pp.98-100, all 3 pages read). P.99 was read closely a second time to resolve the first defect: it clearly prints 'volui mittere ad te meam illam Synopfin', so the audit's claim that Dreyer omits 'meam' does not hold and that defect is rejected. The same page confirms 'nollemque' (not 'nllemque') and 'amicitiæ' (not 'amicitae') at the locations cited, both now fixed. No other cruces found on the three pages.
