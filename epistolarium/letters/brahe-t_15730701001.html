The original

NOBILISSIMO DOCTRINAQUE ET VIRTUTIBUS PRÆSTANTISSIMO D. TYCHONI BRAHE OTTONIDI DANO, IN CURIA KNUDSTRUP, DOMINO ET AMICO SUO PLURIMUM COLENDO. S.

AFFINEM meum, Generose Domine TYCHO, in patriam suam discedentem, absque meis literis ad te venire nolui: cum quod scirem, amicorum plurimi te facientium literas tibi non ingratas fore: tum quod vehementer ex te scire desiderem, quid de novo illo ante aliquot menses exorto sidere sentias. Mirum est, quanta sit doctissimorum et in hac arte exercitatissimorum virorum discrepans sententia. Nam alij (ut de materia non dicam) Cometam appellare volunt, alij stellam propter speciem apparentem: alij mediam quandam naturam habere asserunt. Italus quidam (nomen eius excidit) hoc sidus non novum, sed unum esse ex numero Cassiopeae, lumine miraculose illustrat© et accenso, acerrime contendit, scripto in publicum edito, et senatui Veneto dedicato. Praeterea de distantia eius a Terra magna est controversia. Alij enim id in elementarem regionem collocant, ex quorum numero noster D. PAULUS HAINZELIUS est. Alij in aetheream regionem, quorum opinioni astipulatur D. PEUCERUS, APIANUS, Landgravius Hassiae, et alij, qui tamen et in hoc dissentiunt, ut alij supra Lunam, alij supra Venerem, imo alij in primum Mobile collocare non dubitent, freti observationibus instrumentorum, doctrinae parallaxeos, et Triangulorum. Sed quomodo una eademque doctrina ex firmis demonstrationibus extructa, potest artifices adeo incertos reddere, ut ita dissentiant? Haec velim explices, et quid de hac re statuas, per occasionem, etiam propter communia studia literis significare ne graveris, te etiam atque etiam rogo. Id si feceris, erit mihi tam gratum, quam quod gratissimum, quod etiam tanquam testem studij et amoris tui erga me in perpetuum conservabo. Utinam hisce temporibus una vixissemus, ut observationis modum ex te cognoscere potuissem: qua in re nihil mihi gratius contigisset. Sed optando quid consequi possim, non video. Amissa bona plerunque desiderantur, id quod non mea, sed temporis culpa factum video fato quodam. Praesens ergo status boni consulendus erit.

Tu interim feliciter in Domino vale.
Datae Augustae Rheticae Calendis Iulij 1573
T. E.
Deditiss. et studiosiss.
IOHANNES MAIOR

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble Master Tycho Brahe, son of Otte, the Dane, at the manor of Knudstrup, most outstanding in learning and in the virtues, his lord and friend to be greatly honoured — greeting.

I was unwilling, noble Master Tycho, that my kinsman, as he departed for his homeland, should come to you without a letter from me — both because I knew that letters from friends who esteem you highly would not be unwelcome to you, and because I ardently desire to learn from you what you think about that new star which arose some months ago. It is wonderful how greatly the most learned men, and those most practised in this art, differ in opinion. For some (to say nothing of its substance) wish to call it a comet, others a star on account of its apparent form; others assert that it has a certain intermediate nature. A certain Italian (his name escapes me) most fiercely contends — in a work issued to the public and dedicated to the Venetian Senate — that this heavenly body is not new, but is one of the number of [the stars of] Cassiopeia, [its] light having miraculously been made bright and kindled. Moreover there is great controversy about its distance from the Earth. For some place it in the elementary region — of whose number is our Master Paul Hainzel; others in the aethereal region, with whose opinion Master Peucer, Apian, the Landgrave of Hesse, and others side — who nevertheless also disagree in this, that some place it above the Moon, others above Venus, indeed others do not hesitate to place it in the primum mobile, relying on the observations of instruments, on the doctrine of parallax, and on [the doctrine] of triangles. But how can one and the same doctrine, built up out of firm demonstrations, render its practitioners so uncertain that they thus disagree? I beg you again and again to explain these things, and not to find it burdensome — as occasion offers, and for the sake of our common studies — to signify by letter what you determine on this matter. If you do this, it will be as pleasing to me as what is most pleasing; and I shall preserve it forever as a witness of your zeal and affection toward me. Would that we lived together in these times, so that I might have learned the manner of observation from you — in which matter nothing more welcome could have befallen me! But I do not see what I can attain by wishing. Good things, once lost, are for the most part longed for — which I see came about by a certain fate, through no fault of mine, but of the time. The present state of things, then, must be made the best of.

Meanwhile, fare you happily well in the Lord.
Given at Augsburg in Raetia, on the Kalends of July [1 July] 1573.
Yours,
most devoted and most zealous,
Johannes Major.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Iohannes Maior = Johannes Major (the Latin sender-form). Writing from Augsburg in 1573, in the circle of the Augsburg patrician-patron Paul Hainzel (whom he calls "our Master Paul Hainzel"). He is otherwise a minor figure of the Augsburg learned circle through which Tycho had passed in 1569–70; the letter is preserved among the same Vienna Epistolae volume (cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66) as the Wolf and Hainzel letters of the period. The edition's heuristic language tag (la) is correct: the letter is wholly Neo-Latin, with no code-switching.
  • Ottonides — "son of Otte," a Greek-style patronymic for Tycho's father Otte Brahe; in Curia Knudstrup = "at the manor of Knudstrup" (the Brahe family seat in Scania; kept as is by the edition's convention).
  • affinem meum … discedentem — "my kinsman, as he departed": the unnamed kinsman of Major's was the bearer carrying this letter back to Denmark; not further identified.
  • The "new star" (the 1572 nova). novo illo … exorto sidere = "that new star which arose some months ago" — the nova/supernova of November 1572 in Cassiopeia (SN 1572, "Tycho's star"), the central event of Tycho's early career and the subject of his De nova stella (1573). The whole substance of the letter is the contemporary debate over its nature and distance. Note Major's hedge (ut de materia non dicam) "(to say nothing of its substance)" — preserved, not sharpened.
  • Cometam … stellam … mediam quandam naturam — the three contemporary positions on what the object was: a comet, a (fixed) star, or something of intermediate nature. Rendered literally.
  • Italus quidam (nomen eius excidit) … senatui Veneto dedicato — "a certain Italian (his name escapes me) … dedicated to the Venetian Senate." Major reports the view that the object is not new but a pre-existing star of Cassiopeia whose light was miraculously kindled. The Italian author is left unnamed by Major himself (nomen eius excidit, "his name has slipped [my memory]"); the work dedicated to the Venetian Senate plausibly points to one of the several Venetian nova-tracts of 1573, but Major's own uncertainty is preserved and the identification left open rather than asserted.
  • Textual crux — lumine … illustrat© et accenso. The working transcription prints illustrat©, a corrupt token (a transcription/digitised text layer artifact, not a Latin word). The grammar requires an ablative agreeing with the ablative-absolute construction lumine … accenso ("the light having been kindled"); the intended reading is therefore _illustrato_ ("[its light] having been made bright"), here adopted in the translation as "[its] light having miraculously been made bright and kindled." The printed corrupt form is retained verbatim in the Source block by the edition's convention; the reading was not confirmable against a clean page image of Dreyer, hence flagged.
  • The distance debate. elementarem regionem = "the elementary region" (the sublunary realm of the four elements); aetheream regionem = "the aethereal region" (the celestial realm above the Moon). The dispute over whether the new star lay below or above the Moon is precisely the parallax question Tycho's own observations settled in favour of the supralunary placement.
  • D. Paulus Hainzelius = Paul Hainzel of Augsburg (with whom Tycho had built the great Göppingen quadrant in 1569), here placing the object in the elementary (sublunary) region.
  • D. Peucerus = Caspar Peucer (Wittenberg); Apianus = Philipp Apian (the Ingolstadt/Tübingen mathematician, son of Peter Apian); Landgravius Hassiae = Wilhelm IV, Landgrave of Hesse-Kassel — named as siding with the aethereal (supralunary) placement.
  • primum Mobile — the primum mobile, the outermost moving sphere of the Ptolemaic-Aristotelian cosmos; kept in Latin and italicised.
  • Technical terms. doctrina parallaxeos → "the doctrine of parallax"; observationibus instrumentorum → "the observations of instruments"; doctrina … Triangulorum → "the doctrine of triangles" (i.e. trigonometry, the computational basis for deriving distance from parallax).
  • artifices — here "practitioners" (of the mathematical art), i.e. the astronomers themselves; Major's rhetorical point is that a single rigorous doctrina nonetheless yields contradictory results in their hands.
  • Closing formulae. feliciter in Domino vale → "fare you happily well in the Lord"; Datae … Calendis Iulij 1573 → "Given … on the Kalends of July (1 July) 1573" (Roman-calendar idiom kept and glossed, the edition's convention); T. E. / Deditiss. et studiosiss. = Tuae Excellentiae deditissimus et studiosissimus, "most devoted and most zealous to Your Excellency".
  • Place. Augustae Rheticae = Augsburg (here qualified Rhetica, "of Raetia," the antiquarian regional epithet; cf. the more usual Augusta Vindelicorum), the edition's convention
  • Calendar. Calendis Iulij 1573 = the Kalends of July, i.e. 1 July 1573 (O.S.; Denmark and the Empire were pre-Gregorian). The as-written dateline agrees with the edition label (1573. 1. juli. Augsburg); no discrepancy.
  • Correction. Latin: 'astipulator' corrected to 'astipulatur' per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p. 13.
