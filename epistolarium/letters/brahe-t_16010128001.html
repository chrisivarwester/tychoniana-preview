The original

SERENISSIMO ET POTENTISSIMO PRINCIPI AC DOMINO, DOMINO FERDINANDO, MAGNO DUCI ETRURIÆ ETC. DOMINO SUO CLEMENTISSIMO.
SERENISSIME ET POTENTISSIME PRINCEPS, DOMINE CLEMENTISSIME.

CUM magni Persarum Regis amplissima Legatio, quæ hîc aliquandiu apud Sacram Cæsaream Maiestatem, Dominum meum Clementissimum, morata est, nunc in Italiam cogitet, et antequam Romam venerit, Serenissimam Celsitudinem Tuam in Etruria sua salutare, et quæ in mandatis habet, eidem reverenter exponere constituerit, mei officij esse ratus sum, Serenissimam Celsitudinem Tuam de TENGNAGLIO nostro clementissime bene meritam hisce literulis submisse compellare, præsertim cum Legationis eius antesignanum, Illustrissimum Dominum AMMONIUM SCHERLEJ, natione Anglum, filius meus natu maior, TYCHO nomine, comitetur. Est autem ille idem filius, quem antea in Serenissimæ Celsitudinis Vestræ Aula aliquandiu servire exoptâram, nisi obstacula quædam intervenissent, quæ attingere supervacaneum. Tradidissem illi Opera, quæ hactenus in Astronomicis elucubravi, secum adsportanda, prout Serenissima Celsitudo Vestra aliquando clementissimis suis ad me literis expetijt, nisi quædam in singulis ferme adhuc desiderarentur, typographico labore necdum absoluto. Quod ubi effectum, Serenissimæ Celsitudinis Vestræ Clementissimæ voluntati demisse et lubentissime obsecundabo.

Remoram nonnullam, quo minus illa ad Colophonem deducta sint, iniecit migratio mea ex Arce prioris habitationis Bennatica huc Pragam, sic clementissime mandante Cæsarea Maiestate, quo propior ei factus, humillima mea servitia ipsius Maiestati promptius præstare possem, allatis etiam huc omnibus Instrumentis Astronomicis, quibus antea in Dania Patria mea usus sum, quæ numero sunt 28, ijsdemque in domo quadam ampla et magnifice, Italica architectura, non longe ab Arce iuxta Cæsaris hortum extructa, ordine dispositis, donec in locum quendam usui eorum aptiorem, a Cæsarea Maiestate clementissime deputatum, transferantur. Quod et brevius futurum spero. Idque Serenissimæ Celsitudini Vestræ debita submissione (siquidem eam benignissime studijs meis favere compertum habeam) indicandum duxi. Pluribus vero eam gravioribus intentam hac mea interpellatione remorari nec volo nec decet. Quare nunc finem facio, id solummodo addens et humillime rogans, ut Serenissima Celsitudo Vestra prædictum filium meum sibi commendatum habere non dedignetur, Deus Opt. Max. Serenissimam Celsitudinem Vestram Reipublicæ emolumento quam diutissime servet florentem et incolumem.

Praga die 28 Ianuarij Anno 1601.
Serenissimæ Celsitudini Vestræ
submisse addictissimus
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the Most Serene and Most Powerful Prince and Lord, Lord Ferdinand, Grand Duke of Etruria etc., his most Clement Lord.
Most Serene and Most Powerful Prince, most Clement Lord.

Since the most ample Legation of the great King of the Persians — which has tarried here for some time with the Sacred Imperial Majesty, my most Clement Lord — is now minded [to go] into Italy, and, before it comes to Rome, has resolved to salute Your Most Serene Highness in your Tuscany, and reverently to lay before you what it has in its instructions, I have judged it to be of my duty humbly to address Your Most Serene Highness — who have so most clemently deserved well of our Tengnagel — with this little letter; especially since my elder-born son, Tycho by name, accompanies the leader of that Legation, the Most Illustrious Lord Anthony Sherley, an Englishman by nation. And he is that same son whom I had earlier wished to serve for a while in Your Most Serene Highness's Court, had not certain obstacles intervened, which it is superfluous to touch upon. I would have handed to him the Works which I have hitherto elaborated in Astronomy, to carry with him — as Your Most Serene Highness once requested of me by your most clement letters — were not certain [parts] still wanting in almost each of them, the typographic labour not yet completed. When that is done, I will humbly and most gladly comply with Your Most Serene Highness's most clement will.

Some delay, hindering their being brought to the colophon [i.e. to completion], was thrown in the way by my removal from the Castle of my former dwelling, Benátky, hither to Prague — the Imperial Majesty so most clemently commanding, that, being made nearer to him, I might the more readily render my most humble services to His Majesty; all my Astronomical Instruments, too, which I formerly used in Denmark my homeland, having been brought hither — 28 in number — and these set out in order in a certain ample house, magnificently built in the Italian architecture, not far from the Castle, beside the Emperor's garden, until they be transferred to some place fitter for their use, most clemently assigned by the Imperial Majesty; which I hope will be shortly. And this I have thought to indicate to Your Most Serene Highness with due submission (since I have found you most benignly to favour my studies). But I neither wish, nor is it fitting, to detain you — intent on weightier matters — with this my interpellation. Wherefore I now make an end, adding only this, and most humbly asking, that Your Most Serene Highness would not disdain to hold my aforesaid son commended to you. May God, best and greatest, preserve Your Most Serene Highness, for the benefit of the Commonwealth, flourishing and unharmed for the longest time.

Prague, the 28th of January, in the year 1601.
Your Most Serene Highness's
most submissively devoted,
TYCHO BRAHE,
I wrote [this] with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender, recipient & register. Tycho Brahe at Prague, in the high princely-petition register, to Ferdinando I de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany (Magnus Dux Etruriae, 1549–1609) — a patron who had earlier sought Tycho's published works. The letter commends Tycho's elder son to the Grand Duke.
  • The Persian-embassy occasion (a remarkable historical node). The amplissima Legatio magni Persarum Regis = the famous Persian embassy of Shah Abbas I, which travelled through Europe 1599–1602 seeking an anti-Ottoman alliance, had stayed at Rudolf II's court and was now bound for Italy and Rome, via the Tuscan court. Its leader — antesignanus … Ammonius Scherlej, natione Anglus — is Sir Anthony Sherley (1565–1635?), the English adventurer who co-led the embassy. Tycho's elder son, Tycho Brahe the Younger, travelled in this embassy's company toward Florence — the occasion and bearer of this very letter.
  • Persons named.
  • Ferdinandus, Magnus Dux Etruriae = Ferdinando I de' Medici.
  • Ammonius Scherlej = Anthony Sherley, the embassy's English leader.
  • filius meus natu maior, Tycho = Tycho Brahe the Younger, Tycho's elder son, the commendatus; once intended for service at the Medici court (frustrated by unstated "obstacles").
  • Tengnaglius noster = Franz Tengnagel, of whom the Grand Duke had "deserved well" (during Tengnagel's Italian sojourn at the Tuscan and Roman courts, cf. brahe-t_16000306002).
  • Sacra Caesarea Maiestas = Rudolf II.
  • The works and the move (valuable material history). Tycho explains he cannot yet send the Works he has elaborated in astronomy (the great unfinished publication programme — the Progymnasmata and related volumes) because the printing is not finished (typographico labore necdum absoluto … ad Colophonem). The delay was caused by his removal from Benátky to Prague at the Emperor's command, to be nearer the court. He records that his 28 astronomical instruments, brought from Denmark, are now arranged in a large house of Italian architecture beside the Emperor's garden at Prague, pending transfer to a fitter site — a precise datum on the Prague phase of the Tychonic observatory (the Kurtz/Belvedere arrangement).
  • Place. Praga = Prague; Bennatica arx = Benátky; Etruria = Tuscany.
  • Edition note. The edition cites the letter to a printed Bibliotheca mathematica; it is also in Dreyer's Opera Omnia. No manuscript witness is recorded — the text descends through print.
  • Epistolary formulae. Serenissima Celsitudo Vestra = "Your Most Serene Highness" (declined throughout); subscription submisse addictissimus → "most submissively devoted"; manu propria scripsi → "I wrote with my own hand."
  • Calendar. Dateline die 28 Ianuarij Anno 1601 at Prague; the edition marks n.s. (New Style) — apt for Imperial Prague and for the Catholic Italian recipient. Reproduced, not converted.
