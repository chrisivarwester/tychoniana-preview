The original

NOBILISSIMO CLARISSIMOQUE VIRO D. TYCHONI BRAHE, MATHEMATICO EXIMIO, DOMINO ET AMICO OBSERVANDO.

LITERIS tuis, nobilissime, amantissime Domine TYCHO, nihil mihi fuit charius. Ioachimicos 40 accepi a D. LEVINO. Chirographum adhuc apud me delitescit sine fraude et dolo. OMELIJ de Horologijs sciotericis partem, quam commodato ceperam, remisi, feceris mihi rem pergratam, si reliquam partem mittas. Detinueram apud me propositiones aliquot Graece conscriptas, has, ne quicquam desideres, remitto. Quod vero scribis te in observationes coelestium motuum sedulo incubiturum, non possum non probare, cum animum sis consecutus ad ea studia sua sponte inflammatum, et qui laboribus non deterrentur, quique in rebus praeclaris et a communi ingeniorum captu semotis laudabile iudicat studium suum posuisse. Utinam ipsum coelum non plane oppugnet tuos labores, aptius ad p[rui]narum, imbrium, nivium, grandium, Septentrionis, Cori Boreaeque flatium, et aliarum, quae in sublimi fiunt, impressionum considerationem. Vulcano accommodata magis sedes, cui materiam copiosam suppeditat, in qua se exerceat. His lapis ille multis decantatus plerisque omnibus frustra quaesitus investigandus potius, quam in coelo, quod raro in oculos incurrit, siderum motus perscrutandi. Nos hîc te expectamus, nec sine libris aliquot non passim obvijs. Si quos habeas IORDANI aut REGIOMONTANI nondum editos, Norimbergae a te emptos unâ tecum afferas. Quod si COPERNICUM WOLPHGANG piae memoriae, nactus es, hic sit tantum itineris comes. Ita enim iudico, Regem in Germaniam iter instituentem comitaberis, cuius beneficio, quod felix et faustum sit, intelligo, te Dominum Insulae factum, e qua quaquaversum, montibus non objectis, coelum prospicere liceat: Macte animo, quo tua te virtus vocat. Nos curta supellex et res angusta domi hactenus humi depressos detinuit, brevi forte emersuros. Cum apud nos esses, petij, ut quae in morbo caduco tibi essent nota, de usu medicamentorum ad affectum facientium, communicares, id hunc ob repentinum discessum minime licuit. Hoc si nunc feceris, plurimum me tibi devinxeris. Cum autem ad tres fere differentias possit deduci, quando scilicet ipsum cerebrum primario laborat, aut aliquod viscus, ut uterus, ventriculus, lien, et si quod sit aliud, aut e partibus remotioribus, ut manibus pedibusve, aura quaedam caput petit. Peterem in singulis propriam rationem, si quae sit, aut universalem omnibus, si unus est in omnibus curandi modus. Aut quid omnibus commune, quid singulis peculiare. Non dubito pro animi tui candore et amore mutuo, candide omnia communicaturum.

Bene vale.
Rostochij XIV Maij anno 1576.
Henricus Brucaeus
plane tuus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and illustrious gentleman, Master Tycho Brahe, distinguished mathematician, his lord and friend to be regarded with respect.

Nothing has been dearer to me than your letter, most noble and most beloved Master Tycho. I received 40 Joachimstal coins [Ioachimicos] from Master Levinus. The autograph [chirographum] still lies hidden with me, without fraud or guile. I have sent back the part of Oemel's On Sundials [De Horologiis sciotericis] which I had taken on loan; you will do me a most welcome favour if you send the remaining part. I had been keeping with me some propositions written out in Greek; these — so that you may lack for nothing — I send back. But as for your writing that you mean to apply yourself diligently to observations of the celestial motions, I cannot but approve, since you have attained a disposition kindled of its own accord toward those studies — [a disposition] of those who are not deterred by hardships, and who judge it praiseworthy to have invested their pursuit in things excellent and set apart from the common reach of [ordinary] minds. Would that the very sky did not flatly oppose your labours — [a sky] better suited to the consideration of hoarfrosts [p[rui]narum], rains, snows, hailstorms, and of the North wind, the Caurus, and the Boreas's blasts, and of the other impressions that come to be on high. A seat more fitted to Vulcan, to whom it supplies abundant matter on which to exercise himself. In these [matters] that stone so much sung-of by many, [yet] sought in vain by very nearly all, is rather to be investigated, than the motions of the stars to be searched out in a sky that rarely meets the eye. We await you here, and not without some books that are not everywhere to be come by. If you have any [works] of Jordanus or Regiomontanus not yet published, bought by you at Nuremberg, bring them along with you. And if you have got hold of the Copernicus of Wolfgang of pious memory, let this only be a companion for the journey. For thus I judge: you will accompany the King as he sets out on a journey into Germany — by whose benefaction (which may it be happy and auspicious) I understand you have been made Lord of an Island, from which, with no mountains set in the way, one may look out upon the sky in every direction. Go on with good heart, whither your virtue calls you! As for us, scant furniture and straitened means at home have hitherto held us pressed to the ground — though perhaps soon to emerge. When you were with us, I asked that you would share what things were known to you in the falling sickness [morbus caducus], concerning the use of medicaments that act upon the affliction; this, owing to your sudden departure, was by no means possible. If you do this now, you will bind me very greatly to you. Now since [the disease] can be reduced to roughly three distinctions — namely, when the brain itself is primarily affected; or some viscus, such as the womb, the stomach, the spleen, and if there be any other; or when from the more remote parts, such as the hands or feet, a certain vapour [aura] makes for the head — I would ask [you to give] in each case its own method [of treatment], if there is one, or a universal one for all, if there is a single mode of curing in all [cases]; or what is common to all, what peculiar to each. I do not doubt, given the candour of your mind and our mutual affection, that you will share everything candidly.

Fare you well.
Rostock, 14 May, the year 1576.
Heinrich Brucaeus,
wholly yours.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Hendrik van den Broeck, 1530/31–1593), Flemish-born professor of medicine and mathematics at the University of Rostock, where Tycho had studied (1566–68); a long-standing scholarly friend. The letter belongs to the spring of 1576, in the months immediately after the royal grant of Hven (Brucaeus has just heard of it — see below).
  • Recipient line. The edition's recipient field doubles the name ("Tycho Brahe Tycho Brahe"); a data artefact, not in the source. The source address-line is the single D. TYCHONI BRAHE.
  • Ioachimicos — "the Joachimsthalers / Joachimstal coins": Ioachimici (sc. nummi/thaleri) are the silver coins struck at Joachimsthal (Jáchymov), the original "thaler." Most likely actual coins forwarded via D. Levinus (an unidentified intermediary, "Master Levinus"), though the word in a numismatically-minded humanist context can mean coin-specimens of interest. Rendered "Joachimstal coins" with the Latin kept; referent flagged.
  • Chirographum — "autograph / hand-written document" (something in Tycho's own hand that Brucaeus is holding for him); sine fraude et dolo is the legal-formula assurance "without fraud or guile," i.e. safely kept.
  • OMELIJ de Horologijs sciotericis — a treatise On Sundials (horologia scioterica = shadow-clocks, sundials) by Oemel/Omelius. The author is not securely identifiable from the name-form alone; rendered "Oemel's On Sundials" and the attribution left as written. Sciotericum (< Gk σκιοθηρικόν) = "shadow-catching," the standard term for a sundial.
  • propositiones … Graece conscriptas — "propositions written out in Greek": mathematical/geometrical propositions in Greek that Brucaeus had borrowed and now returns. (No Greek glyphs appear in the source text itself.)
  • The meteorological clause — Brucaeus's scepticism. Utinam ipsum coelum non plane oppugnet tuos labores, aptius ad p[rui]narum … impressionum considerationem — the rhetorical heart of the letter: Brucaeus warns that the northern sky is so cloud-ridden that it is fitter for studying weather (impressiones = meteorological "impressions," the sublunary phenomena of Aristotelian Meteorologica) than the stars. Editorial reading flagged: the source prints p[rui]narum with the middle supplied in brackets by the edition; pruinae = "hoarfrosts," which fits the list of frost/rain/snow/hail. The bracketed supply is kept in the Source block and adopted in the translation by the audit's rule.
  • Wind-names. Septentrionis, Cori Boreaeque flatium — Septentrio = the North (wind); Corus/Caurus = the NW wind; Boreas = the N/NE wind. Kept as classical wind-names, glossed.
  • Vulcano … lapis ille multis decantatus — "a seat more fitted to Vulcan … that stone so much sung-of": Brucaeus pivots to alchemy. Vulcanus = the fire/forge, i.e. alchemical work; lapis ille multis decantatus … frustra quaesitus = the philosophers' stone ("that stone so often sung of [by many], sought in vain by nearly all"). Brucaeus playfully advises that the cloudy North is better for the furnace than for the heavens. decantatus carries a faint irony ("much harped-on") preserved rather than flattened.
  • Books sought. IORDANI aut REGIOMONTANI nondum editos, Norimbergae a te emptos — unpublished works of Jordanus (de Nemore) or Regiomontanus (Johannes Müller of Königsberg, 1436–76) that Tycho had bought at Nuremberg. COPERNICUM WOLPHGANG piae memoriae — "the Copernicus of Wolfgang of pious memory," i.e. a copy of Copernicus (almost certainly De revolutionibus) once owned by a deceased Wolfgang (unidentified; "of pious memory" marks him as recently dead). Brucaeus asks Tycho to bring these to Rostock. Wolfgang's identity is a minor crux, left unresolved.
  • The grant of Hven. Regem in Germaniam iter instituentem comitaberis, cuius beneficio … te Dominum Insulae factum — "you will accompany the King [Frederik II] … by whose benefaction … you have been made Lord of an Island." This is Hven (Ven), granted to Tycho by Frederik II on 23 May 1576 — so this letter (14 May 1576) registers the news as just received / imminent, a valuable external date-fix on the grant. e qua quaquaversum, montibus non objectis, coelum prospicere liceat = "from which, with no mountains in the way, one may view the sky in every direction" — Brucaeus already grasps the island's observational virtue. Macte animo = "Go on with good heart / well done, take courage," a classical encouragement formula.
  • curta supellex … res angusta domi — "scant furniture … straitened means at home": res angusta domi is a Juvenalian tag (Sat. 3.164–5) for poverty; Brucaeus pleads his own narrow circumstances. humi depressos … brevi forte emersuros = "pressed to the ground … perhaps soon to emerge."
  • Medicine — the morbus caducus query. morbus caducus = the falling sickness (epilepsy), rendered with the period term glossed. Brucaeus asks for Tycho's knowledge of medicamenta ad affectum facientia ("medicaments that act upon the affliction" — i.e. remedies effective against the condition; affectus = the disease-state). He lays out a threefold aetiological scheme drawn from Galenic medicine: epilepsy arising (1) primarily in the brain (cerebrum); (2) by sympathy from a viscus — uterus (womb), ventriculus (stomach), lien (spleen); or (3) from the remote parts (hands/feet), whence aura quaedam caput petit — "a certain vapour makes for the head" (the classical aura epileptica, the rising sensation preceding a fit). aura kept and glossed. He asks whether each type has its own treatment (propria ratio) or there is a universal mode (universalis omnibus … modus) — universalis rendered "universal" per glossary.
  • Calendar / place. Rostochij XIV Maij anno 1576 = "Rostock, 14 May 1576" (O.S.; Denmark and the Empire alike pre-Gregorian in 1576). The dateline agrees with the edition label (1576. 14. maj. Rostock); no discrepancy.
  • Subscription. Bene vale → "Fare you well"; plane tuus → "wholly yours."
  • Correction. Latin: restored the dropped numeral, 'Ioachimicos accepi' corrected to 'Ioachimicos 40 accepi', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VII, p.33. English: added '40' to name the sum of Joachimsthaler coins received.
