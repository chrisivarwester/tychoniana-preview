The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD WOLFGANGUM FRANZIUM.

S. P.

CLARISSIME et præstantissime Domine Doctor WOLFGANG FRANCI, Amice plurimi semper mihi faciende. Ex utrisque tuis literis, tam prioribus quam nuper per Dominum Doctorem IESSENIUM, communem nostrum Amicum, missis, intellexi de Transylvani illius negocio; quem instabilem esse et, dum hîc aderat, serio non egisse, iam præsenseram. Is forte meas ob id expetebat literas, ut eo facilius isthic ad illud, quod ambibat, officium promoveretur; sed bene est, quocunque tandem modo illi profuerim; apud me vero perinde ubicunque sit, utut effecerit, quod interea alium eius loco non obtinuerim, veluti alias non fuisset difficile.

Morbi Epidemici, qui graviter superiori Anno hanc Regionem infestârunt, iam, Dei benignitate, sopiti sunt. Cæsar tamen necdum Pragam reversus est, sed Pilsnæ adhuc moratur, brevi tamen, uti existimant, eo venturus. Scabies, de quâ loqueris, eorum, qui Theologiam et religionem Christianam profitentur, sævior est, et diutius durat; nam apud homines non est miseratio et relaxatio, quemadmodum apud Deum. Attamen quæ nonnulli moliuntur, ut suis indulgeant cupiditatibus, inscio Cæsare, pro maiori parte, fiunt, et spes est, successum non habitura, reluctantibus præcipuis quibusdam in hoc regno et libertatem avitam asserentibus; neque Cæsar tam improvidus erit, ut regnum, tam periculoso præsertim hoc tempore, irrequietari permittat.

Res meæ ita se habent, ut antea intellexisti: Deus inposterum eas conservet. Dominus Doctor IESSENIUS, quem Planetam vocas, a vestrâ Ecliptica (iuxta quam Luminaria deliquium pati solent) huc quidem per Australem latitudinem in Bohemiam digressus, me tamen nondum invisit, nec scio an facturus sit; habet enim, instar Planetæ ‿ij quasdam deviationes et reflectiones, aliter quam ponderosiores Planetæ incedunt. Intellexi Patris eius caussam periclitari, quod satis mature is non accesserit, nec forte ob hyemem commodum erat. Relatum mihi est, te etiam erraticum futurum et alio concessurum; si ita habet, indica; nam et ego sum unus ex ijs, qui tibi quamoptime cupiunt: optarem te semel hîc videre, prout aliquando futurum dixisti; sed multa cadunt inter ciatos supremaque labra. Vale et me tui esse amantem, ubicunque fueris, persuasum habe. Dabantur properanti calamo ex Arce Benatica sextiduo post Æquinoctium vernum Anni 1600, quem non saltem IOSEPHUS SCALIGER et MERCATOR in sua Cronologia, tum quoque BUTINGERUS atque SCULTETUS uno Anno, sed idem SCALIGER in novo opere de temporum emendatione duobus deficere probare conatur, ut sit nunc reverâ 1602. Rationes eius in dicto libro invenies. Iamque iterum et multoties vale.

TYCHO BRAHE TO WOLFGANG FRANZ.

Heartiest greeting.

Most renowned and most excellent Lord Doctor Wolfgang Franz, a friend always to be most highly esteemed by me. From both your letters — the earlier ones, and those lately sent through Lord Doctor Jessenius, our common friend — I have learned of that Transylvanian's affair; whom I had already sensed to be unsteady, and, while he was here, not to be acting in earnest. He perhaps sought my letters on that account, so that he might the more easily be advanced there to the post he was angling for; but it is well, in whatever way I have at last been of use to him. To me, indeed, it is all one wherever he may be, however he has brought it off, since in the meantime I have got no one in his place — as otherwise it would not have been difficult [to do].

The epidemic diseases, which last year grievously infested this region, are now, by God's goodness, laid to rest. The Emperor, however, has still not returned to Prague, but lingers yet at Pilsen — though soon, as they reckon, he will come thither. The mange of which you speak — that of those who profess Theology and the Christian religion — is fiercer, and lasts longer; for among men there is no mercy and remission, such as there is with God. Yet the things which some are contriving, to indulge their own cupidities, are for the most part done without the Emperor's knowledge, and there is hope they will not succeed, certain leading men in this kingdom resisting and asserting their ancestral liberty; nor will the Emperor be so improvident as to allow the kingdom — especially at so dangerous a time as this — to be thrown into unrest.

My affairs stand as you understood before: may God preserve them hereafter. Lord Doctor Jessenius, whom you call a Planet, having indeed departed from your Ecliptic (along which the Luminaries are wont to suffer eclipse) hither into Bohemia by a southern latitude, has nevertheless not yet visited me, nor do I know whether he will; for he has, like the planet Mercury [☿], certain deviations and retrogradations, otherwise than the more ponderous Planets proceed. I have learned that his Father's case is in jeopardy, because he did not come to it early enough — nor perhaps, on account of the winter, was it convenient. It has been reported to me that you too are to be a wanderer, and to withdraw elsewhere; if so it is, let me know; for I too am one of those who wish you the very best. I could wish to see you here once, as you said would some day happen — but many things fall between the cup and the lips. Farewell, and hold it for certain that I love you, wherever you may be. Given with hurrying pen from the castle of Benátky, the sixth day after the vernal equinox of the year 1600 — a [year] which not only Joseph Scaliger, and Mercator in his Chronology, and also Bünting and Scultetus, attempt to prove deficient by one year, but the same Scaliger, in his new work On the Emendation of Times, by two — so that it is now really 1602. You will find his reasons in the said book. And now, again and many times over, farewell.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent (identity not settled — flagged). The recipient is Domine Doctor Wolfgang Franci (Wolfgang Franz, Franzius) — Tycho's learned correspondent of the Wittenberg–Prague circle, the exchange routed through their common friend Jan Jessenský / Johannes Jessenius (1566–1621), the anatomist. Disambiguation: the recurring Doctor Franz who serves as Tycho's courier to Saxony (brahe-t_16001008001) and becomes Chancellor of the Count of Emden is the jurist Thomas Franz, Winshemius's son-in-law and East-Frisia agent (named Thomas Frantzius in brahe-t_15990507001; Frantzius Iurisconsultus in brahe-t_16001008001; Doctor Franz the jurisconsult, Chancellor of the Count of Emden in brahe-t_16000918001). Since this recipient's first name is Wolfgang, not Thomas, he is a different person — most plausibly the Wittenberg Lutheran theologian Wolfgang Franz (1564–1628), though that cannot be confirmed from this letter alone. Identity left open (theologian likely; not the jurist Thomas Franz).
  • The "Transylvanian." An unnamed, unsteady client (instabilis) who had sought a letter of recommendation (meas literas) from Tycho to obtain a post; Tycho washes his hands of him. Not further identified here; left unidentified per the no-fabrication rule.
  • Topical setting (spring 1600, Bohemia).
  • Morbi Epidemici … superiori Anno — the plague of 1599 that drove the imperial court (and Tycho) about; now abating.
  • Caesar … Pilsnae adhuc moratur — Rudolf II, who had withdrawn to Pilsen (Plzeň) during the epidemic and had not yet returned to Prague.
  • Scabies … eorum qui Theologiam … profitentur — a sardonic figure: the "mange/itch" of the theologians (confessional quarrelling) is worse and more chronic than any bodily epidemic. The veiled reference to men "indulging their cupidities … without the Emperor's knowledge," resisted by Bohemian nobles "asserting their ancestral liberty," reflects the Catholic–Protestant tension in Bohemia on the eve of the confessional crisis; Tycho judges Rudolf too prudent to let the kingdom be destabilised.
  • The astronomical conceit (the letter's wit). Tycho extends Franz's own joke of calling Jessenius "a Planet." Jessenius has left "your Ecliptic" (Wittenberg — the line "along which the Luminaries suffer eclipse," i.e. the locus of darkenings/troubles) and crossed "by a southern latitude" into Bohemia; like a wandering planet he keeps deviationes et reflectiones — deviations and retrogradations — unlike the ponderosiores Planetae, the heavier (slower, steadier) planets. Tycho then turns the same figure on Franz himself ("you too are to be erraticus [a wanderer/planet]"). The technical sense of reflectio here is retrograde motion (apparent backward looping), not "reflection of light."
  • The garbled planet-glyph (textual crux). At instar Planetae the working transcription prints the malformed string "‿ij" where Dreyer's edition sets a planet sign. The sense fixes it securely as Mercury (☿) — the lightest, swiftest, most inconstant planet, the one that keeps the largest deviationes/reflectiones and is the stock emblem of mutability — contrasted with the ponderosiores Planetae (Saturn/Jupiter). Translated as "the planet Mercury"; the as-printed source glyph is retained in the source block. (Cf. the recurring transcription noise around planet/zodiac signs and the ÿ/ij ligature in this corpus.)
  • The chronology joke (dating, the edition's convention). The dateline is periphrastic: sextiduo post Aequinoctium vernum Anni 1600 = "the sixth day after the vernal equinox of 1600." The N.S. vernal equinox of 1600 fell on c. 21 March, giving ≈ 27 March 1600 [N.S.] (the catalogue date). Tycho cannot resist appending the era controversy: the chronologers Joseph Scaliger (De emendatione temporum, 1583/1598), Gerardus Mercator (Chronologia, 1569), Heinrich Bünting (Chronologia; "Butingerus"), and Bartholomäus Scultetus (the Görlitz mathematician, Tycho's own friend) argue the received year is short — most by one year, Scaliger's newer work by two — "so that it is now really 1602." A jest, not a re-dating: the letter's own date stands at 1600 [N.S.].
  • Proverb. Multa cadunt inter ciatos (cyathos) supremaque labra — "many things fall between the cup and the lips," the classical inter calicem supremaque labra ("there's many a slip 'twixt cup and lip"); kept as the idiom and glossed.
  • Persons named. Wolfgang Franz; Johannes Jessenius (the "Planet"); the unnamed Transylvanian; Rudolf II (Caesar); Joseph Scaliger; Gerardus Mercator; Heinrich Bünting; Bartholomäus Scultetus.
  • Place. Arx Benatica = the castle of Benátky nad Jizerou, Tycho's imperial seat before the move into Prague.
