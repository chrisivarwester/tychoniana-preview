The original

NOBILISSIMO VIRO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO ET AMICO OBSERVANDO.
URANIBURGI SEU IN HYENA INSULA.
S.

NOBILISS. D. TYCHO, Domine et amice observande. Post discessum nobilis illius viri vestratis, qui falcones Imperatori nostro a Rege vestro attulerat, measque ad te literas perferre debuerat, binas ad te dedi literas, easque opera mei amici per Dresdam curavi ad te perferendas; eas an acceperis, vehementer scire desidero, non quod magni alicuius momenti essent, sed ut cognosceres non mea culpa, sed iniqua sorte quadam accidere, quod meæ tibi vel tardius reddantur, vel penitus non perveniant ad te, quam fortunam, profecto, valde defleo. An igitur omnes meas huc usque adeoque librum VERNERI acceperis, magnopere rogo me edoceas. Tuum librum primum avide expecto, alij etiam, præsertim nobiliss. D. IACOBUS CURTIUS, Vicecancell. Imperij, eadem cupiditate expetunt. Etsi autem mihi in ista mea senili ætate (nam ad 1. Octob. si ita Deus volet, magnum climacterem complebo) ob studia medica et alias curas, in Astronomicis studijs versari non conceditur, tamen vehementer oblector, quoties facultas datur saltem inspiciendi aliorum lucubrationes. De Commentarijs TRAPEZUNTIJ quid nunc iudices, scire velim, num putes ex re scholarum fore, si initio saltem duo priores libri ederentur, postea reliqui.

De rebus Gallicis vos istic melius. En quam nullum est scelus quantumvis horrendum, quod Monachus aggredi non audeat. Dixerim ego Monachos istos et Papistas Catholicos, non a fide sed verius, ut ita dicam, a Catholicitate omnium scelerum nominandos esse. Archid. MAX. hodie Polonicos limites transcensurus esse dicitur. Expectabimus igitur illum huc intra dies non usque adeo multos; Deus faxit, ut sanus redeat ad nos. B. V. Pragæ die 7. Septembris novo stylo Anno 1589.

T. studiosiss.
THAD.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, his lord and friend to be esteemed. At Uraniborg, or on the island of Hven. Greeting.

Most noble Master Tycho, my lord and friend to be esteemed. After the departure of that noble man, your countryman, who had brought falcons to our Emperor from your King and was to have carried my letters on to you, I sent you two letters, and arranged through a friend of mine, by way of Dresden, for them to be conveyed to you; whether you have received them I am most eager to know — not because they were of any great moment, but so that you might understand that it is through no fault of mine, but through some unkind fate, that mine [my letters] are delivered to you either too late, or do not reach you at all — a fortune which I do indeed bitterly lament. I therefore greatly beg you to inform me whether you have received all of mine up to now, and so too Werner's book. Your first book I await eagerly; others too, and especially the most noble Master Jacobus Curtius, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, seek it out with the same longing. But although in this my old age (for on 1 October, if God so wills, I shall complete my great climacteric) it is not granted me, on account of my medical studies and other cares, to be engaged in astronomical studies, yet I take vehement delight, as often as the chance is given me, in at least examining the lucubrations of others. On the Commentaries of Trapezuntius, I should like to know what you now judge — whether you think it would be to the schools' advantage if at the outset at least the two former books were published, and afterwards the rest.

Concerning French affairs, you there [know] better. See how there is no crime, however dreadful, that a Monk does not dare to attempt! For my part, I would say that those Monks and Papist Catholics ought to be named, not from the faith, but more truly — so to speak — from the catholicity [universality] of all crimes. Archduke Maximilian is said to be about to cross the Polish borders today. We shall therefore look for him here within days not so very many; God grant that he return to us safe and sound. Fare you well. At Prague, on the 7th day of September, New Style, in the year 1589.

Most devoted to you,
Thaddaeus [Hagecius].

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thad[daeus] = Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku, c. 1525–1600), Bohemian physician, astronomer, and personal physician to Emperors Maximilian II and Rudolf II at Prague; Tycho's chief scientific correspondent in the Empire and the man who in 1600 brokered Tycho's introduction to Kepler. His Dialexis de nova stella (1574) had independently argued the 1572 nova was supralunary, aligning him with Tycho.
  • Addressee / place. Tychoni Brahe Dano … Uraniburgi seu in Hyena insula = Tycho Brahe, at Uraniborg on the island of Hven (Hyena is a Latinised spelling of Hven/Hveen; kept as written and identified here, by this chapter's convention).
  • nobilis illius viri vestratis, qui falcones Imperatori nostro a Rege vestro attulerat — "that noble countryman of yours who had brought falcons to our Emperor from your King": a Danish envoy bearing a gift of falcons (a recurring Danish-crown diplomatic present) from Frederik II (or, if late 1588 onward, the regency for the boy Christian IV) to Rudolf II. Used here as a now-failed letter-carrier; the detail is kept literal and the identification flagged as inferential.
  • librum Verneri — "Werner's book": the treatise of Johann Werner of Nuremberg (1468–1522), almost certainly his De motu octavae sphaerae / the 1522 In hoc opere haec continentur containing the method of prosthaphaeresis and the eighth-sphere motion — a text Tycho is known to have circulated and criticised. Identification of the exact title flagged as probable, not certain.
  • Iacobus Curtius, Vicecancell. Imperii — Jacob Kurtz von Senftenau (Jacobus Curtius, d. 1594), Imperial Vice-Chancellor and a mathematically-minded official at the Prague court; a real intermediary between Tycho and the Imperial circle.
  • magnum climacterem — "[my] great climacteric," the grand climacteric, the 63rd year (9 × 7), held astrologically the most perilous of life's climacteric years (this chapter's convention, cf. annus climactericus). Hagecius states he will complete it on 1 October [1589]. The translation keeps his own conditional hedge si ita Deus volet ("if God so wills") and does not adjust it to any externally-reckoned birth-year.
  • studia medica — "medical studies": Hagecius was an active court physician, hence his apology that medicine and "other cares" keep him from astronomy.
  • lucubrationes — "lucubrations," works composed by lamplight / studious nocturnal labours; kept in the period sense.
  • Commentariis Trapezuntii — "the Commentaries of Trapezuntius": George of Trebizond (Georgius Trapezuntius, 1395–1486), whose commentary on Ptolemy's Almagest was a standard but contested humanist text; Hagecius asks Tycho's editorial judgement on a staged publication ("the two former books first, the rest afterwards"). ex re scholarum rendered "to the schools' advantage."
  • De rebus Gallicis … Monachus … scelus … horrendum — "concerning French affairs … a Monk … a dreadful crime": the assassination of King Henri III of France by the Dominican friar Jacques Clément on 1 August 1589 (Henri died 2 August). The literature (Strauss, Brahe Iconography; Weber cat. 177) confirms the 1 August 1589 regicide-by-monk. Hagecius's bitter pun — that "Monks and Papist Catholics" should be named not a fide (from the faith) but a Catholicitate omnium scelerum (from the catholicity — universality — of all crimes) — turns Catholicus against itself; the wordplay is kept and glossed rather than flattened.
  • Archid. Max. … Polonicos limites transcensurus … sanus redeat — "Archduke Maximilian … about to cross the Polish borders … may he return safe": Archduke Maximilian III of Austria (the "Deutschmeister"), defeated claimant to the Polish throne, taken prisoner at Byczyna (Pitschen) in January 1588 and held by the Poles. His release under the Treaty of Bytom-Będzin (negotiated 1589) is the "return" Hagecius anticipates — a further internal anchor to 1589, not 1586.
  • B. V. — Bene vale = "Fare you well".
  • T. studiosiss. — Tui studiosissimus = "Most devoted to you".
  • Greek. None present in this letter.
  • Symbols. None present in this letter.
  • DATING CRUX (named). The dateline reads "Pragae die 7. Septembris novo stylo Anno 1589" — Prague, 7 September 1589, New Style (Bohemia, under Imperial/Catholic rule, had adopted the Gregorian calendar; Hagecius marks the style explicitly, novo stylo). The edition identifier (15860907001) and label imply 1586. The internal evidence is decisive for 1589, not 1586: (i) the Henri III assassination-by-monk (1 Aug 1589); (ii) Archduke Maximilian's anticipated release/return from Polish captivity (1589, post-Byczyna 1588); (iii) the explicit dateline "Anno 1589." The header therefore leads with the as-written 7 September 1589 [N.S.] and appends "(catalogued 1586)" by this chapter's convention; the catalogue year is treated as an error, not silently reconciled, and the discrepancy is recorded here. No calendar conversion of the day itself is performed; only the style (N.S.) is flagged.
