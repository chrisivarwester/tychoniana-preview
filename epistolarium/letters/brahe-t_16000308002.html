The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANONYMUM.

NOBILISSIME ET EXCELLENTISSIME VIR, DOMINE ET AMICE OBSERVANDISSIME.

QUOD tam amanter Dno Va, quo tempore Wandesburgi adhuc in Holsatiâ hærebam, humanissimis et pariter doctissimis suis literis me prævenire voluerit, tanto gratius id mihi accidit, quanto Clarissimorum et Præstantissimorum virorum amicitias cæterorum familiaritati semper præferendas duxi. Quod vero prius ijs a me responsum non sit, Dno Va non meæ, quæ nulla ad Amicis par pari referendum esse solet, negligentiæ, sed locorum intervallo, peregrinationibus meis continuis, et quod vix ulla interim ad Sermam eius Celnem scribendi certa occasio hucusque sese obtulerit, tribuet. Quam nunc per Nobilem et Eruditum Iuvenem FRANCISCUM TENGNAGEL nactus, qui apud vos prius erat et nuper ex Italia reversus iter istuc in Patriam parat, et Sermam eius Celnem, si ullo modo ob viarum et prædonum grassantium pericula licuerit, humillime compellare animum induxit, has gratitudinis erga se meæ testes exarare volui, etiam atque etiam rogans, ut Dno Va negocia illa, quæ Sermæ eius Celni dictus TENGNAGLIUS oretenus (si eam submisse accedere contigerit) vel literis expositurus est, authoritate et intercessione suâ promovere non gravetur. Quæ ipsemet (ubi, uti spes est, accesserit) TENGNAGLIUS clare et seorsim insinuare poterit, quo responsum mature nanciscar. Fecerit D. V. mihi hac in parte rem inprimis acceptam, et quibusvis, quæ a me proficisci unquam poterunt, officijs vicissim pro virili compensandam.

Valeat Dno Va quam felicissime.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsareâ Benaticâ die 8 Martij 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to an unnamed [correspondent].

Most noble and most excellent man, lord and most observed friend.

That Your Lordship was willing to anticipate me so lovingly — at the time when I was still lingering at Wandsbek in Holstein — with your most kind and equally most learned letter, fell to me the more gratefully, the more I have always reckoned the friendships of the most distinguished and excellent men to be preferred to the familiarity of the rest. But that no answer was earlier given them by me, Your Lordship will ascribe not to my negligence — of which there is wont to be none, since among friends like must be returned for like — but to the interval of places, to my continual travels, and to [the fact] that scarcely any sure occasion of writing to his Most Serene Highness has hitherto, meanwhile, offered itself. Which [occasion] I have now obtained through the noble and learned youth Frans Tengnagel — who was formerly with you, and, lately returned from Italy, prepares the journey thither into [his] homeland, and has resolved most humbly to address his Most Serene Highness, if in any way it be permitted, on account of the dangers of the roads and of marauding robbers. [Therefore] I wished to write these [lines] as witnesses of my gratitude toward you, asking again and again that Your Lordship not find it burdensome to promote — by your authority and intercession — those affairs which the said Tengnagel will set forth to his Most Serene Highness by mouth (if it befall him to approach her humbly), or by letters. Which the same Tengnagel (when, as is hoped, he shall have approached) will be able to insinuate clearly and separately, so that I may obtain a timely answer. Your Lordship will have done me, in this matter, a thing especially acceptable, and to be compensated in turn — to the best of my ability — by whatever offices can ever proceed from me.

May Your Lordship fare most happily.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 8th day of March, 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient — unnamed. The companion of brahe-t_16000309002 (written the next day), to the same Cologne electoral channel: an unnamed most noble and excellent official who had written first to Tycho while he was at Wandsbek in Holstein (1597–98), and who can promote Tycho's affairs "with his Most Serene Highness" — i.e. Elector Ernst of Cologne. Identification flagged: not named (the two anonymous Cologne-court letters may be to the same person or to two different councillors).
  • Tengnagel. Franciscus Tengnagel — "formerly with you," now returned from Italy and bound for his homeland, will approach the Elector "if the dangers of the roads and of marauding robbers permit," and carry Tycho's business by word or letter (cf. brahe-t_16000309002, the same errand).
  • par pari referendum — "like to be returned for like" (the friendship-reciprocity maxim); praedonum grassantium pericula — "the dangers of marauding robbers," a realistic note on travel in the war-disturbed Empire of 1600.
  • Serma eius Celnis = Serenissima eius Celsitudo, "his Most Serene Highness" (the Elector). Dno Va = Dominatio Vestra, "Your Lordship."
  • Calendar. ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ die 8 Martij 1600 = 8 March 1600; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style (edition-tagged n.s.). Header agrees with the label.
