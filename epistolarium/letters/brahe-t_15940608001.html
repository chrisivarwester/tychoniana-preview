The original

TILL THYGE BRAHE, EN WED NAFFN CHRISTOFFER PEPELER ANRØRENDIS.

Christianus 4.

WOR gunst tilforne. Wiid, effter som Nelle Christoffer Pepelers hustru wnderdanigstt till Oß haffuer supplicerit och tilkiende giffuett, huorledis effter att hendis hoßbunde, for:ne Christoffer Pepeler, haffuer werritt thin fougitt oc du derfore vdj nogenn sager, som hannom och thig skall haffue for samme hans thieniste emellom werritt, ladett hans godtz vdj Wor kiøpstedt Bergenn wdj Wortt riige Norge arestêre oc beslaa, oc sidenn effter din forthrøstning oc for:ne hans hustruis schriffuelße oc begiering haffuer sig begiffuit till Wor kiøpstedt Helsingøer, saa hand thin thiener ther sammestedtz paa thine wegne for for:ne sag haffuer satt nøyachtig borgenn, huilckett thu wanseett dog skall hannom ther effter ladett fenckligenn thage oc hensette paa Hueen, effter som hendis indlagde supplication ydermere formelder oc wduiiser, tha bede Wij thig oc Wille, att naar thu her met besøgis, thu tha therudinden retter thig selff och bethencker oc till sinde fører, medt huad rett oc billighet sligt skee kand, attu baade haffuer thagett borgenn af hannom oc sidenn hannom fenckligenn lader anholde, attu therfore om thet sig saaledis befindis, lader hannom egienn løß och sidenn medt rettenn hannom tillthaler, anseendis huad vdj sig selff christeligt oc rett er, saa Wij for widere thes klagemaall oc offuerlob maa bliffue forschonett.

Actum Hafniæ 8. junii a:o 94.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Concerning Tyge Brahe, touching one Christoffer Pepeler by name.

Christian IV.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, inasmuch as Nelle, the wife of Christoffer Pepeler, has most humbly petitioned Us and made known how — after her husband, the aforesaid Christoffer Pepeler, had been your bailiff, and you therefore, in certain matters which are said to have arisen between him and you over that same service of his, had caused his goods to be arrested and seized in Our market-town of Bergen in Our realm of Norway, and [how] he then, upon your assurance and upon the aforesaid letter and request of his wife, had betaken himself to Our market-town of Helsingør, so that he there in the same place, on your behalf, posted sufficient bail to your servant for the aforesaid matter — [how], notwithstanding this, you are nonetheless said to have had him thereupon taken prisoner and confined on Hven, as her enclosed petition further sets forth and shows: We therefore ask you and will that, when you are visited [with this letter], you then in this matter govern yourself and consider and call to mind with what right and equity such a thing can be done — that you have both taken bail of him and then have him held prisoner — [and] that therefore, if it be found to be so, you set him free again and then proceed against him at law, having regard to what is in itself Christian and right, so that We may be spared further complaint and importunity in the matter.

Done at Copenhagen, 8 June in the year [15]94.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Christian IV of Denmark (r. 1588–1648), then in his minority (the regency council governed until his 1596 coronation); royal letters in these years issue in his name. Christianus 4. is the Latin register-form of the subscription. Tycho was at this date still in possession of Hven, six years from his 1597 departure.
  • Recipient / subject. Tyge Brahe (Tycho), addressed as the recipient; Christoffer Pepeler, formerly Tycho's fougitt (bailiff/steward), is the subject of the complaint; Nelle ("Nelle Christoffer Pepelers hustru"), Pepeler's wife, is the petitioner. None is further identified among the works consulted beyond this letter.
  • Document type. A chancery-register entry (Jyske Tegnelser). The heading is a Latin–Danish rubric in the register's hand: "TILL THYGE BRAHE … ANRØRENDIS" ("Concerning Tyge Brahe … touching …") — the standard register caption, preserved by this chapter's convention The subscription Christianus 4. is likewise the Latin chancery register-form.
  • Legal structure (the load-bearing logic). The body is one long chancery period reporting the wife's allegation and issuing the royal directive. Its skeleton is: because Pepeler (Tycho's ex-bailiff) had his goods seized in Bergen over service disputes; and on Tycho's own assurance came to Helsingør and posted sufficient bail through Tycho's servant; yet was nonetheless imprisoned on Hven — the king directs Tycho to weigh "with what right and equity" he could both take bail and then imprison the man, and, conditionally, "om thet sig saaledis befindis" ("if it be found to be so"), to release him and instead proceed against him medt rettenn ("at law"). The release order is thus conditional on the facts proving out, not absolute; this conditional mood (om … befindis) is preserved and not sharpened into an unconditional command.
  • Negations. The Danish body contains no explicit negative particle (icke/jcke). The only negation-adjacent forms are the concessive wanseett dog ("notwithstanding … nonetheless") and forschonett ("spared"); neither is a logical "not." The translation introduces none.
  • Reportative subjunctive (hedge). The constructions skall haffue … werritt and skall … ladett ("is said to have been," "is said to have had") mark the whole account as the wife's allegation reported to the king, not as findings the king asserts as proven. Rendered "are said to have," consistent with the conditional release order; not flattened to plain assertion.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1594). WOR gunst tilforne = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula, this chapter's convention); Wiid = Vid ("know"); effter som = "inasmuch as"; hoßbunde = husbonde ("husband"); for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," the standard chancery abbreviation); fougitt = foged ("bailiff, steward"); godtz = gods ("goods, property"); arestêre oc beslaa = "arrest and seize/attach"; forthrøstning = fortrøstning ("assurance, guarantee"); schriffuelße = skrivelse ("letter, written communication"); satt nøyachtig borgenn = "posted sufficient bail/surety" (borgen = bail, surety); huilckett … wanseett = hvilket … uanset ("notwithstanding which"); fenckligenn thage oc hensette = "take prisoner and confine"; formelder oc wduiiser = formelder og udviser ("states and shows"); besøgis = "is visited [with this letter]," i.e. served/reached by it; retter thig selff = "govern/conduct yourself"; till sinde fører = "call to mind"; billighet = "equity, fairness"; om thet sig saaledis befindis = "if it be found to be so"; løß = løs ("free, loose"); medt rettenn … tillthaler = "proceed against / sue at law"; klagemaall oc offuerlob = "complaint and importunity (vexation)"; forschonett = forskånet ("spared").
  • Places. Bergenn … wdj Wortt riige Norge = Bergen, in the kingdom of Norway (then under the Danish crown); Helsingøer = Helsingør (Elsinore); Hueen = Hven, the island Tycho held in fief; Hafnia = Copenhagen.
  • Calendar. Actum Hafniae 8. junii a:o 94 = "Done at Copenhagen, 8 June, year [15]94" (O.S.); Actum "Done/Given" kept by this chapter's convention The dateline (8 June 1594) and the edition label (8 June 1594) agree; no discrepancy.
