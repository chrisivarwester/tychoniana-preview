The original

OLIGERUS ROSENKRANTZ AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.

[IGNES spirant, noctesque diesque meditantur,] sed in proprias fauces regurgitandos.

[Si meo non certe affectu ullo temerato,] sed gravissimis quam plurimis argumentis confirmato, et meditationibus, mihi crede, satis multis et constantibus maturo [consilio parere volueris], quod spero, [suaserim primum omnium, litteris regi destinatis insulam Huennam quasi gratias pro regia liberalitate] (prudenter et placide submonitis submonendis) [agens rursum deferas], ut tamen quo [tua tibi cadant instrumenta], prudenter ͻ: Politice submoneas. Alterum vero est, ne [expensarum invisam et odiosam restitutionem] vel saltem velle [exigere videaris], nedum vel [exigas vel exagites]. Vis, sufficientes dicam rationes et mearum meditationem momenta. [Dicam, sed ne quæso mihi succenseas, bono] tibique optime [cupienti animo hæc dico]. Primum, si feceris, ante omnia, [magnanimitatem tuam] ostenderis. Dein [te omnimodo liberum ab omni iurisdictione Regis vindicaris], quod vel unicum quantum siet, ipse perpende, quod tamen nisi hoc modo fieri nequit. Adhæc [si retineas, videas ne te tanquam en Lensmand, ut bona tibi a Rege concredita inhabites, iure quodam suo cogere præsumat]. Et ut omnia uno: [quamdiu insulam eam retinueris, tanquam subditus cuivis vocationi parere,] quibus certe magnas [tibi parare possunt] incommoditates et difficultates, insuper [citatus, ipse, seu præsens comparere], quod tibi valde foret difficile et prorsus impossibile, [aut quod ais pronunciatum perferre cogereris]. Et vero quid non [ipse] magna tua cum laude et præter multorum existimationem [eam deferre malles], cum [possint ipsi, ubi velint, etiam quodam iure eam præcipere, pretendentes] quippe [litteras tibi a Rege concessas] (quæ quidem [ad vitam eam] tibi [appropriat insulam]) sub [conditione] Astronomiæ [hic in Dania excolendæ conceptas] esse. Tu ipse [ingenia eorum nosti]; nihil certe intermiserint, ut interim [immensum istud discrimen inter alium aliquem nobilem Regis subditum et te] (in quo non postrema [bonitatis causæ tuæ] ratio vertitur) vel alto silentio [dissimulent] vel [malitiose elevent]. Ad alterum cogites velim, quam nec natura nec eius imitatores quid frustra committant, [iure vero quidpiam tentare] frustraneum simul ac periculosum, ubi et quod me tacente ipse satis intelligis, [quam inconstantes sint principum animi,] simul ac [quam non corvus unus alteri, quod dicitur, oculos eruat], tecum attentissime pro tua prudentia perpendas velim. Bono hæc animo dico, fidem testor et candorem; nec dicerem nisi bono. Nec quidpiam video, licet videre nunquam non laborem, quod [rebus tuis ulla ratione adversari queat et institutum tuum tandem turbare queat], præter [hæc duo]. Sed hæc Deo et tuæ prudentiæ.

HOLGER ROSENKRANTZ TO TYCHO BRAHE.

[They breathe fire, and brood on it night and day,] but [it is] to be spewed back down their own throats.

[If — by an affection on my part certainly not warped by any (bias),] but confirmed by very many most weighty arguments and (believe me) by reflections sufficiently many and constant — [you will be willing to heed] mature [counsel], as I hope, [I would advise, first of all, that — by a letter addressed to the King — you surrender back the island of Hven, as it were rendering thanks for the royal liberality] (the things to be intimated having been prudently and calmly intimated), [acting so that you hand it back again]; yet so that you prudently — that is (ͻ:), politically — give notice in such a way [that your instruments may fall to you], (i.e. that you keep your instruments). The second [point] is this: that you not [seem] even to wish [to demand] the [hateful and odious restitution of your expenses], much less [demand it or stir it up]. You wish me to state sufficient reasons, and the weight of my reflections. [I will state them — but please do not be angry with me; with a good] heart, and [wishing you the very best, I say these things]. First, if you do it, you will above all have displayed [your magnanimity]. Next, [you will free yourself wholly from all jurisdiction of the King] — which, even as a single thing, weigh for yourself how great it is — a thing which, however, cannot be done except in this way. Besides, [if you retain it, see to it lest he presume to compel you, by some right of his own, as a Lensmand [fief-holder], to reside upon the goods entrusted to you by the King]. And to put it all in one: [as long as you retain that island, [you would be bound], as a subject, to obey any summons] — by which they can certainly contrive great inconveniences and difficulties [for you] — and moreover, [if cited, to appear yourself, in person], which would be very hard for you and altogether impossible, [or you would be forced to suffer the sentence pronounced, of which you speak]. And indeed, why would you not yourself rather, [with great praise to yourself and beyond many men's expectation, surrender it], since [they themselves can, when they will, seize it even by a certain right, alleging] indeed [that the letters granted you by the King] (which do indeed [appropriate that island to you] [for life]) were drawn up under the [condition] [of Astronomy being cultivated here in Denmark]. You yourself [know their dispositions]; they will assuredly have left nothing undone, so that meanwhile they either [conceal] in deep silence, or [maliciously belittle], that [immense difference between you and some other noble subject of the King] (wherein turns not the least part of [the goodness of your cause]). As to the second [point], I would have you consider how neither Nature nor her imitators undertake anything in vain — whereas [to attempt anything by law] is at once futile and perilous; where also — what, though I be silent, you well enough understand yourself — [how inconstant are the minds of princes,] and likewise [how, as the saying goes, one crow does not peck out another's eyes], I would have you weigh most attentively with yourself, according to your prudence. I say these things with a good heart; I call my good faith and candour to witness; nor would I say them but for good. Nor do I see anything — though I never cease labouring to see — that could in any way oppose your affairs and at last disturb your undertaking, save [these two things]. But these [I leave] to God and to your prudence.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned" (1574–1642), the Danish nobleman and scholar, Tycho's consanguineus (kinsman) and intimate friend; the edition and the neighbouring correspondence confirm the heading Oligerus Rosenkrantz ad Tychonem Brahe (cf. brahe-t_15980328001 apparatus, which already cites "a 7 March 1599 letter" of this title). Writing from Denmark, hence Old Style.
  • Significance — political counsel on surrendering Hven. A candid, confidential letter of advice at the crux of Tycho's break with Denmark. Holger urges two things: (1) that Tycho formally surrender the island of Hven back to the King by letter, framed as thanks for the royal liberality — prudenter, ϲ: Politice ("prudently — that is, politically") — while quietly securing that he keep his instruments; and (2) that Tycho not demand reimbursement of his expenses (the great sums sunk into Uraniborg/Stjerneborg), which would be "hateful and odious."
  • The reasoning. Holger's case: voluntary surrender (a) shows magnanimity; (b) frees Tycho wholly from the King's jurisdiction — whereas while he holds Hven he remains, tanquam en Lensmand ("as a Lensmand," a royal fief-holder), a subject liable to any summons, who could be cited to appear in person (impossible for the now-absent Tycho) or made to suffer a pronounced sentence. He warns that the crown party can in any case reclaim Hven "by a certain right," since the royal letters granting Tycho the island for life were issued on condition that astronomy be cultivated in Denmark — a condition Tycho's departure had voided. He counsels against any recourse to law (iure quidpiam tentare — "futile and perilous"), citing the inconstancy of princes and the proverb that the great close ranks against an outsider.
  • Code-switch & abbreviation.
  • en Lensmand — Danish embedded in the Latin: "a Lensmand," holder of a royal len (fief). Kept and glossed.
  • ͻ: (reversed-C colon) — the early-modern siglum for id est / scilicet, "that is"; it glosses prudenter as Politice ("politically"). Rendered "that is (ϲ:)".
  • Proverbs / allusions. noctesque diesque meditantur echoes the Virgilian/Ennian epic cadence (cf. noctes atque dies); corvus … alteri … oculos [non] eruat = the classical cornix cornici oculos non effodit, "one crow does not peck out another crow's eyes" — i.e. men of the same order protect one another against an outsider; bonum animum/candorem testor are the friendship-formulae of frank counsel.
  • Textual crux — the square brackets (flagged, not resolved; this chapter's convention). The source presents the body in alternating bracketed and un-bracketed stretches, with the bracketed portions carrying the politically sensitive content (the King, his jurisdiction, the surrender of Hven, the reclaim-by-right) and the un-bracketed words supplying connective tissue (sed … quod spero … ut tamen … Vis … Primum …). The bracketing is the edition's. Resolved by collation against Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII (pp. 148, 162–163) + the vol. IX critical apparatus (p. 309): the brackets are the edition's editorial restorations and conjectures of a single defective witness — the Langebek Diplomatarium afskrift (the apparatus reads regurgitandos conj. The bracketed spans and their boundaries match the edition of record exactly. The brackets are reproduced as-is in the source block; the translation renders the full text continuously, marking the bracketed spans.
  • Persons named. Holger Rosenkrantz (sender); Tycho Brahe (recipient); the King (Christian IV) and his noble subditi — the crown party — unnamed individually; the island of Hven.
  • Calendar. Dated 7 March 1599; Old Style, per the Danish sender. No place in the dateline; "[Denmark]" supplied in the header as the evident origin, flagged by brackets.
