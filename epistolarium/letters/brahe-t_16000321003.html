The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD OLAUM ROSENSPARRE].

MIN gandske wenlig hilsen nu och allthiid forsend met Vor Herre. Kierre Oluff Rosenspar, sour och sønderlig gode wen, tacker ieg eder gandske gierne for megit beuist gode, huilket wdjgien att forskylde ieg stedtze will findiß aff formuen willig. Giffuendiß eder derhos gandske wenligen thillkiende, att ieg haffuer bekommit eders skriffuelse met min søn Tyge, som kom hid hiem till mig vdygien lit epther nøtaarßdag, och skall y haffue megen tack for eders gode wille imod mig, som ieg formercker derudaf, enddog den er mig tillforn nocsom bekant. Och som y formelder derudj, att eders kierre høstru, min søsterdatter Elisabet, war samme thiid so ilde tilpas aff it flod vdi hinders hoffuit, att hun icke kunde skriffue mig till, saa vill ieg gierne haffue hinder derudj vnskyldit och forhoffuis, att det er vel snart derepther bleffuen bedre met hinder igien, Gud beuare bode eder och hinder sambt eders børn fran hueß eder skadelligt kan werre. Her vdj landit war sore megen søgdom vdj forgangen aar, aff pestelenße och blodsott, som tho vsigellige mange folck bortt, och keysern drog derudoffuer fra Prag till en fast bø, heder Pilsen, 10 mille derfra liggendis, hor hanß may. endnu er, och er icke wentendis till Prag vdigien førend epther paaske. Hanß may. wille, att ieg skulde haffue begiffuit mig met mine hen thill Wien y Osterich, denstund samme søgdom her so hart regerit, och wille naadigst der lade forskaffue mig god plaß och leylighed. Menß ieg lod giffue hanß may. vnderdanigst igien tillkiende, att ieg wille heller bliffue her och ware paa min bestilling och befalle Gud allthing, hormed hans may. och naadigst war thillfrij[tz]. Jeg tacker Gudt, som saa naadelligen haffuer beuarit mig oc mine y samme farlige søgdom. Och will ieg icke paa denne thiid wijdre formelde om mine willkar och leylighed, thi y haffuer dennom well fornummit aff min siste skriffuelße, och vdentuill ydermere aff min søster Sophi, som ieg skreff widløfftigere till derudom. Altingest er endnu y samme wesen och bliffuer nest Gudtz hielp herepther enda bedre. Den samme Gud will ieg haffue eder och min søsterdatter Elisabet met begge eders børn och gode wenner till all lycke och welfart troeligen befalit; giører vell och hilser Elisabet med mange gode netter paa mine wegne.

Daterit paa det keyserlige slot Benatky vdj Lanthebehmen den 21 dag martij stylo nouo anni 1600.

[Tycho Brahe to Oluf Rosensparre.]

My quite friendly greeting, now and always, sent forth with Our Lord. Dear Oluf Rosensparre, brother-in-law and especial good friend: I thank you very gladly for much kindness shown, which, to repay in return, I shall ever be found willing according to my means. Giving you herewith, quite in friendship, to know that I have received your letter by my son Tyge, who came home again here to me a little after New Year's Day; and you shall have many thanks for your good will toward me, as I perceive from it — though it was already well enough known to me beforehand. And as you mention therein that your dear wife, my sister's daughter Elisabet, was at that same time so unwell from a flux in her head that she could not write to me — I will gladly hold her excused in that, and hope it has soon afterward grown better with her again. God preserve both you and her, together with your children, from whatever may be harmful to you. Here in this land there was very great sickness in the past year, from pestilence and the bloody flux, which carried off unspeakably many people; and the Emperor on that account moved from Prague to a strong town called Pilsen, lying ten miles thence, where His Majesty still is, and is not to be expected back at Prague until after Easter. His Majesty wished that I should have betaken myself, with mine, away to Vienna in Austria while that same sickness raged so hard here, and would graciously have had good quarters and accommodation procured for me there. But I let His Majesty most humbly understand in turn that I would rather stay here and attend to my charge, and commit all things to God — with which His Majesty also was graciously content. I thank God, who has so graciously preserved me and mine in that same dangerous sickness. And I will not at this time mention further about my conditions and circumstances, for you have well learned them from my last letter, and doubtless further from my sister Sophie, to whom I wrote more at length about it. Everything is still in the same state, and will, with God's help, become better still hereafter. To that same God I will have you, and my sister's daughter Elisabet, with both your children and your good friends, faithfully commended to all fortune and welfare. Do well, and greet Elisabet with many good-nights on my behalf.

Dated at the imperial castle of Benátky in Lower Bohemia, the 21st day of March, New Style, of the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe at Benátky (here det keyserlige slot Benatky udi Lanthebehmen, "the imperial castle of Benátky in Lower Bohemia" — Niederböhmen) — a warm, plain-spoken family letter in Danish, a register apart from the Latin patronage and scientific correspondence of the same weeks.
  • Recipient & kin. Oluf Rosensparre = Oluf Rosensparre (1559–1624), Danish nobleman and councillor of the realm, addressed as svoger — brother-in-law / kinsman by marriage. His wife Elisabet, min søsterdatter = Elisabeth, "my sister's daughter" (Tycho's niece), who had been ill with et flod udi hendes hoved (a "flux/catarrh in her head"). Tycho sends her "many good-nights."
  • Persons named.
  • min søn Tyge = Tyge (Tycho) Brahe the Younger, Tycho's elder son, who carried Rosensparre's letter home "a little after New Year's Day."
  • min søster Sophi = Sophie Brahe (1559–1643), Tycho's learned sister, his correspondent and intermediary with the Danish family.
  • Keyseren / hans Majestät = Emperor Rudolf II.
  • The 1599–1600 Bohemian epidemic (datable content). pestelenße og blodsott = plague and the bloody flux (dysentery) had killed "unspeakably many" in Bohemia the previous year; the Emperor withdrew from Prague to Pilsen (Plzeň), "ten miles off," not expected back "until after Easter." Rudolf had offered to move Tycho and his household to safety at Vienna; Tycho declined, preferring to stay at his post — a detail corroborated by the Latin letters of the same season (the court at Pilsen recurs in brahe-t_16000508001, brahe-t_16000420001, brahe-t_16000306002).
  • Language note. Pre-standardisation chancery Danish: gandske (ganske), wenlig (venlig), skriffuelse, søsterdatter, søgdom (sygdom), blodsott, wæsen, with German-influenced -ß-/w- spelling. The low automatic language-score (0.078) reflects exactly the heuristic weakness this chapter's convention flags; the letter is securely Danish, read as such.
  • Witness note. A Kladde — retained draft; hence the bracketed editorial heading and the absence of a written-out subscription.
  • Formulae. Danish epistolary courtesy: Min ganske venlig hilsen nu og altid forsendt med Vor Herre → "My quite friendly greeting, now and always, sent forth with Our Lord"; Daterit → "Dated."
  • Calendar. Dateline den 21 dag Martij stylo novo anni 1600 — Tycho stamps even his Danish letter from imperial soil as New Style, although his Danish correspondents at home kept Old Style; this is the same Gregorian dating used across his Bohemian output. Reproduced as written; Easter (the movable feast referenced) is to be read on the same reckoning. Not converted.
