The original

GENEROSO, MAGNIFICO ET NOBILI VIRO, DOMINO I. BONGARSIO, SERENISSIMI ET POTENTISSIMI GALLIARUM REGIS APUD GERMANIAe PRINCIPES LEGATO DIGNISSIMO, AMICO SUO VETERI ET INPRIMIS HONORANDO.

MAGNIFICE ET NOBILISSIME VIR.

ETSI sub finem superioris Anni per Magistrum quendam natione Danum, Consobrinum meum, CLAUDIUM BILLEUM, in Gallias comitantem et Francofurtum (uti statuerat) transeuntem, ad te literas dederim, ac de quibusdam negocijs commonefecerim, cum tamen hic Nobilis et Eruditus Iuvenis FRANCISCUS TENGNAGLIUS, meus domesticus meaeque e Dania emigrationis Comes, ex Italiâ nuper ad me reversus ad Nundinas Francofurtenses ire cogitet, ut inde in Comitatu mercatorum tutius Patriam suam, quae est in extremae Westphaliae finibus, accedere atque Parentes invisere queat, postmodum, favente Numine, huc reversurus: non intermittendum duxi, quin unâ cum eodem (qui rerum mearum apprime gnarus est, easque plenius ad te referre poterit) denuo scriberem. Nec tamen ea, quae de Privilegio a Sermo et Potentissimo Galliarum Rege impetrando, deque debito Optimae memoriae DANZÆI nobis solvendo tunc temporis rogavi, iterum pluribus inculcare lubet, cum de tuâ erga me voluntate tam in his quam alijs nihil addubitem, et praedictus TENGNAGLIUS haec ipsa oretenus promptius expositurus sit. Id saltem hac vice officiose rogatum cuperem, ut de quâdam aliâ re, quam is secreto tuae fidei ex me committet, quid fieri posse vel secus existimaveris, dispicias, quo is tam de hac quam coeteris, ubi se huc receperit, mihi referre queat. Quem etiam, prout amicitiâ non indignum experieris, ut tuâ benevolentiâ complectaris, quaeso. Plura non addo. Neque etiam calamus omnia tuto sustinet. Sed is instar vivae Epistolae omnia rectius expediet.

Vale.
Dabantur ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ die 9 Martij 1600. Mae
Tuae studiosiss.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the generous, magnificent, and noble man, Master J. Bongars, most worthy Legate of the Most Serene and Most Powerful King of the Gauls [of France] among the Princes of Germany, his old friend and especially to be honoured.

Magnificent and most noble man.

Although toward the end of last year I gave you letters — through a certain Master, a Dane by nation, my cousin Claude Bille, who was accompanying [travellers] into France and passing through Frankfurt (as he had determined) — and reminded [you] about certain affairs: yet, since here the noble and learned youth Frans Tengnagel, my domestic and the companion of my emigration from Denmark, lately returned to me from Italy, thinks of going to the Frankfurt fair, so that thence, in the company of merchants, he may the more safely approach his homeland (which is on the borders of farthest Westphalia) and visit his parents — afterward, the Deity favouring, to return hither — I judged it ought not to be omitted to write again, together with the same (who is exceedingly well-versed in my affairs, and will be able to report them to you more fully). Nor, however, does it please me to inculcate again, at greater length, those things which I then asked — about the Privilege to be obtained from the Most Serene and Most Powerful King of the Gauls, and about the debt of [Charles de] Dançay, of best memory, to be paid to us — since I doubt nothing of your goodwill toward me, both in these and in other [matters], and the aforesaid Tengnagel will more readily set forth these very things by mouth. This only I would this time dutifully ask of you: that, concerning a certain other matter, which he will secretly commit to your good faith on my behalf, you consider what can — or cannot — be done, so that he may report to me both about this and about the rest, when he returns hither. And I beg you to embrace him with your benevolence, as you will find him not unworthy of friendship. I add no more — nor indeed does the pen safely bear everything; but he, like a living letter, will more rightly despatch everything.

Farewell.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 9th day of March, 1600.
Most zealous of Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Jacques Bongars (Bongarsius, 1554–1612), here in his diplomatic office: Legate of the King of France (Henri IV) to the Princes of Germany (cf. brahe-t_15990501001, …16000501001, the Frankfurt book-trade letters). An "old friend."
  • The two standing requests. Tycho recalls (from his year-end letter, carried by his cousin Claude Bille [Claudius Billeus], a Dane bound for France via Frankfurt) two matters he had asked Bongars to advance: (1) a printing/sales Privilege to be obtained from the King of France for his works; and (2) "the debt of Dançay, of best memory, to be paid to us" — i.e. a sum connected with the late Charles de Dançay (Danzaeus, d. 1589), the long-serving French ambassador to Denmark and Tycho's friend and patron. A third, confidential matter Tengnagel will convey orally and "secretly."
  • Tengnagel. Franciscus Tengnaglius, meus domesticus meaeque e Dania emigrationis Comes — Frans Tengnagel, "the companion of my emigration from Denmark," back from Italy and bound (via the Frankfurt fair, in a merchant convoy, for safety) to his home "on the borders of farthest Westphalia" — once more the "living letter" (instar vivae Epistolae; cf. brahe-t_16000308002/…0309002). calamus omnia tuto [non] sustinet — "the pen does not safely bear everything," the discretion-topos.
  • Calendar. ex Arce Caesareâ Benaticâ die 9 Martij 1600 = 9 March 1600; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style (same day as the Cologne-channel letter brahe-t_16000309002). Header agrees with the label.
  • Correction. Removed an extraneous 'te' after 'saltem' in the Bongars letter; Dreyer's print (TBOO VIII p.260, l.24) has none.
