The original

NOBILITATE ET ERUDITIONE PRÆSTANTI IUVENI DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, AMICO SUO SINGULARI.
S. P. D.

SI vales, bene est, mi TYCHO, ego quidem Dei beneficio nunc recte valeo, et febre quartana tandem liberatus sum. Quae de Instrumentis tuis Astronomicis scripsisti, libenter legi, et illud Armillarum Ptolemaicarum, quod solum te nunc desiderare intellexi, WOLFIJ nostri auxilio atque literis (certo nuncio ad SCHRECKENFUCHSIUM Friburgum cum munere pro ijs Armillis misso) facile a SCHRECKENFUCHSIO impetravimus. Quod quidem PETRUS Aurifaber cum supellectile sua hinc ad vos transportabit. Et licet COPERNICUS plenam et ornatam de compositione huius Organi descriptionem in secundo suo de Revolutionibus libro cap. 14 referat, tamen etiam IOHANNIS REGIOMONTANI de constructione et usu eius enarrationem adiungere Instrumento volui: quam ex REGIOMONTANI scriptis (Norimbergae de quinque Instrumentis Astronomicis, Torqueto, Astrolabio Armillari, Regula magna Ptolemaica, Baculo Astronomico et Quadrato geometrico cum IOHANNIS SCHŒNERI Carolostadij additionibus, anno MDXLIII editis) transcribi curavi, si forte eo libro careres, de quo tamen sane dubito. Tabellarius iste nequam, qui nummis, quos illi credidisti, absumptis profugit, nondum redijt, is vel vagus et exui a patria errabit, vel quod abstulit, restituet. MARCUS HARTLIEB, uxoris meae ex sorore nepos, ante biennium Wittenbergam studiorum caussa a parentibus missus, annum forte ibi commoratus est. Priore semestri vel plus eo se, ut diligentem et liberum decet adolescentem, strenue gessit, et bonam in literis operam navare coepit. Posteriore semestri minus sui officij satagebat, quod quidem simul atque parentes eius licet serius resciscerent, statim eum Wittenbergâ ad fratrem IOHANNEM, I. V. Doctorem, Consiliarium Electoris, Heydelbergam ut proficisceretur, avocarunt. Is in itinere cum in quendam hominem, ne quid scribam vehementius, non bonum incidisset, et in eo itinere aliquot septimanis, non sine magno studiorum suorum et pecuniae dispendio, cunctaretur, tandem Heydelbergam ad fratrem cum comite venit, corporis se et valetudinis incommoda in via pertulisse fingens. Id quod socius eius apud fratrem etiam falso testatus: et MARCO fratri pecuniam in itinere erogasse affirmans, viginti Ioachimicos a D. Doctore IOHANNE MARCI fratre extorsit. MARCUS deinde cum a fratre de omnibus quae hactenus egisset, admonitus et obiurgatus esset, inscio et invito fratre, sibique ipsi male conscius Heydelbergâ discedit. Quo posthac profectus sit, aut ubi hactenus se detinuerit, non sat scimus. Verum PETRUS Aurifaber, qui tuas mihi reddidit, aiebat eum Hafniae apud te bonis conditionibus esse, et studijs incumbere. M. IOHANNES quoque MAIOR significabat, se literas a MARCO apud eundem PETRUM accepisse, quibus fere idem scribebat, et praeterea culpam deprecabatur, atque rogabat, ut apud patrem de reconciliatione ageret. Sed iratus pater aegre adhuc reconciliabitur. Si veram paenitentiam egerit atque resipuerit, gratum mihi feceris, si mea caussa eum, quod sine molestia tua fieri velim, iuveris: sin aliquid posthac praeter officium fecerit, et in priori sua negligentia perstiterit, quicquid in eum supplicii sumpseris, etiamsi eum egregie et male mulctaveris, et parentibus et nobis propinquis et cognatis suis rem gratam feceris. Mater eius, piae memoriae femina, praeterito mense, 3 nimirum Nonas Februarii, vesperi hora 8 pie in Christo obdormivit: cum ante annos 48 Nonis eiusdem mensis in hunc mundum nasceretur. Illud te admonere volui: In compositione magnarum PTOLEMÆI Armillarum (quae minutorum vel subtilioris distributionis capaces sunt) cum distantia Polorum (Aequinoctialis videlicet et Eclipticae) pro ratione temporis mutetur, Axonia, quae cum Delatore Zodiaci in Polis mundi convertuntur, ita in Delatore transijci posse, ut 24 minutorum spatio (quae sunt, ut nosti, differentiae maximae et minimae obliquitatis Signiferi) hinc inde pro mutatione Declinationis Solis maximae, quocunque volueris moveri, et nihilominus intra tale spatium ubivis et quandocunque certo loco secundum diametrum cum Polo mundi connecti convertique queant. Praeterea id quoque ad pleniorem huius Instrumenti perfectionem facere videtur (quo stellarum loca accuratius exquisitiusque et ad secunda, tertia, quarta etc. scrupula haberi atque capi possint) ut Geometrica illa et artificiosa plurimorum circulorum, intra se invicem ductorum, distributio in planis lateribus Armillarum atque earum maxillis (quod facile in tanta Armillarum latitudine fieri potest) debito modo notetur atque designetur. Sed quid ego noctuas Athenas, qui haec ad te? Illud pene significare tibi praetermiseram. Quadrantem nostrum Giggingensem Nonis Decembris proximis, increbrescente vehementiore vento, lapsum corruisse, eiusque usum nobis nunc penitus ademptum esse. Iterum atque iterum vale. Clavem ad capsulam Armillarum hic una mitto. Frater meus et ANDREAS WELSERUS plurimam tibi salutem reddunt, atque tui memoriam cum honorifica de te mentione saepenumero recordari solent.

Iterum vale, et me, quod facis, redamare ne desinas.
Dabantur Augustae Vindelicorum, die Aequinoctij verni, hoc est 5. Idus Martij, Anno salutis M. D. LXXV.
T. Paulus Haintzel.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the young man pre-eminent in nobility and learning, Master Tycho Brahe, his singular friend — heartiest greeting.

If you are well, it is good, my Tycho; I, for my part, by God's kindness am now in sound health, and have at last been freed from a quartan fever. What you wrote about your astronomical instruments I read gladly; and that matter of the Ptolemaic armillae — the only thing I gathered you now wanted — we have easily obtained from Schreckenfuchs, with the help and letters of our friend Wolf (a trusty messenger having been sent to Schreckenfuchs at Freiburg with a gift in return for those armillae). Peter the goldsmith will carry it from here to you along with his own baggage. And although Copernicus gives a full and elegant account of the construction of this instrument in the second book of his De revolutionibus, chapter 14, I nevertheless wished to add to the instrument Johann Regiomontanus's exposition too, on its construction and use — which I had transcribed from Regiomontanus's writings (published at Nuremberg, on the five astronomical instruments: the torquetum, the armillary astrolabe, the great Ptolemaic rule, the cross-staff [baculus astronomicus], and the geometric square, with the additions of Johannes Schöner of Karlstadt, in the year 1543) — in case you happened to lack that book, though of that I rather doubt. That worthless courier, who absconded after squandering the money you entrusted to him, has not yet returned; either he will wander as a vagrant and an exile from his homeland, or he will restore what he carried off. Marcus Hartlieb, my wife's nephew by her sister, sent two years ago by his parents to Wittenberg for the sake of his studies, stayed there a year, as it chanced. In the first half-year, or rather more, he conducted himself energetically, as becomes a diligent and free-born youth, and began to apply good effort to his letters. In the latter half-year he was less attentive to his duty; and as soon as his parents learned of this — albeit rather late — they at once recalled him from Wittenberg, that he should set out for Heidelberg to his brother Johannes, Doctor of Both Laws, councillor of the Elector. On the way, having fallen in (that I may write nothing too harsh) with a man who was not good, and having dawdled some weeks on that journey, not without great loss to his studies and his money, he came at last to his brother at Heidelberg with a companion, pretending that he had suffered bodily and ill-health troubles on the road. His companion likewise falsely testified to the same before the brother; and, affirming that he had laid out money for Marcus on the journey, he extorted twenty Joachimsthalers from Master Doctor Johannes, Marcus's brother. Then Marcus, when he had been admonished and rebuked by his brother for everything he had done up to that point, departed from Heidelberg without his brother's knowledge and against his will, ill at ease in his own conscience. Where he set out for afterward, or where he has detained himself up to now, we do not well know. But Peter the goldsmith, who delivered your letter to me, said that he was at Copenhagen with you on good terms, and applying himself to his studies. Master Johannes Maior also indicated that he had received a letter from Marcus, through the same Peter, in which he wrote much the same thing, and besides begged pardon for his fault and asked that [Maior] would treat with his father about a reconciliation. But the father, being angry, will still be hard to reconcile. If he shall have shown true repentance and come to his senses, you will do me a kindness if, for my sake, you help him — which I should wish to be done without trouble to yourself; but if hereafter he does anything contrary to his duty, and persists in his former negligence, then whatever punishment you exact upon him — even if you chastise him soundly and severely — you will do a welcome thing both to his parents and to us, his kinsmen and relations. His mother, a woman of pious memory, in the past month — namely on 3 February [the third before the Nones of February], at 8 in the evening — fell piously asleep in Christ, having been born into this world 48 years before on the Nones of the same month. This I wished to point out to you: in the construction of the great Ptolemaic armillae (which are capable of [a division into] minutes or of a still finer division), since the distance of the poles (namely of the equinoctial and of the ecliptic) changes with the lapse of time, the little axles, which turn with the zodiacal carrier-ring upon the poles of the world, can be so shifted within the carrier-ring that over a span of 24 minutes (which are, as you know, the differences of the greatest and least obliquity of the zodiac) they may be moved either way at will, in keeping with the change of the Sun's greatest declination, and may nonetheless, anywhere and at any time within such a span, be connected and turned together with the pole of the world along the diameter at a fixed place. Furthermore, this too seems to make for a fuller perfection of this instrument (whereby the places of the stars may be had and taken more accurately and exactly, even to seconds, thirds, fourths, etc. of scruples [of arc]): that that geometric and artful distribution of very many circles, drawn one within another, should be marked and traced out in due manner on the flat sides of the armillae and on their jaws [maxillae] (which can easily be done given so great a breadth of the armillae). But why do I [carry] owls to Athens, in [writing] these things to you? I had nearly omitted to mention this to you: that our quadrant at Göggingen, on the last Nones of December [5 December], a rather violent wind having risen, slipped and collapsed, and the use of it is now wholly taken from us. Again and again, farewell. The key to the case of the armillae I send here together [with this]. My brother and Andreas Welser return you many greetings, and are wont often to recall your memory with honourable mention of you.

Again, farewell; and do not cease — as you do — to return my love.
Given at Augsburg, on the day of the vernal equinox, that is, the 5th [day before] the Ides of March [11 March], in the year of salvation 1575.
Yours, Paul Hainzel.

What follows, appended after the writing, was penned in the hand of his brother Johann Baptist Hainzel, Septemvir of Augsburg.

Most noble sir, esteemed friend, I understand from the writings and learned labours of the learned men of your kingdom, that Master Peter Oxe is the chief Councillor of your kingdom and Master of the Court. Since he was once at Basel, under Simon Grynaeus, our common teacher, both my messmate and fellow-student, and had as his tutor Master Christiern Morsianus, a learned man and one well deserving of me, I should very gladly wish to know whether both are well and in good health. Wherefore, if they still remember our friendship, once contracted between us at Basel, would you, when occasion offers, greet both of them lovingly and respectfully in my name, and offer them my ready and prepared service and devotion. You too, and your welfare, I commend to the gracious Saviour of mankind, praying from my heart that you may, for as long as possible, remain safe and unharmed, and preserve yourselves for the Church and the Commonwealth.

Your most devoted, Johann Baptist Hainzel.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Paulus Haintzel = Paul Hainzel (d. 1581), city councillor (Ratsherr) of Augsburg of the leading Hainzel patrician family; with his brother Johann Baptist Hainzel (burgomaster) a patron of astronomy and friend of Tycho from Tycho's Augsburg years (1569–70). It was Paul Hainzel who funded the celebrated great quadrant (radius c. 14 cubits, c. 19 ft) built with Tycho in 1569 at his country estate at Göggingen, southwest of Augsburg.
  • Recipient. The edition header repeats "Tycho Brahe Tycho Brahe"; a single addressee, Tycho, is intended (the salutation reads Domino Tychoni Brahe). At this date (March 1575) Tycho was at Copenhagen (Hafniae, as the letter itself states), before the 1576 grant of Hven.
  • Schreckenfuchs = Erasmus Oswald Schreckenfuchs (1511–1579), mathematician and astronomer, then at Freiburg im Breisgau (Friburgum), commentator on Ptolemy, Peurbach and Sacrobosco; the source of the Armillae Ptolemaicae.
  • Petrus Aurifaber — "Peter the goldsmith": Aurifaber is here read as the trade-name/surname of a craftsman-courier who carried both letter and instrument; rendered "Peter the goldsmith." Not to be confused with the Wittenberg/Königsberg theologian-editor of the same Latinised name.
  • Schoeneri Carolostadij = Johannes Schöner of Karlstadt (Carolostadius, 1477–1547), Nuremberg mathematician, who edited Regiomontanus's Scripta (the collection on the torquetum, armillary astrolabe, etc.). Hainzel dates the edition anno MDXLIII (1543); the standard Nuremberg Schöner edition of the Regiomontanus instrument tracts is usually dated 1544 — the as-written 1543 is preserved and the divergence flagged, not corrected.
  • Johannes / Johann Regiomontanus = Regiomontanus (Johannes Müller of Königsberg, 1436–1476). The instrument list in the Schöner/Regiomontanus tract names five: Torquetum, Astrolabium Armillare (armillary astrolabe), Regula magna Ptolemaica (great Ptolemaic rule), Baculus Astronomicus (the astronomical staff / Jacob's-staff — the baculus, rendered with the English instrument-name "cross-staff," with the source word baculus astronomicus bracketed), and Quadratum geometricum (geometric square). Note: the source word here is baculus astronomicus, not the glossary lemma radius astronomicus; the English "cross-staff" denotes the same class of instrument, but the bracketed Latin preserves the source's own term.
  • Marcus Hartlieb — Hainzel's wife's nephew (sister's son), a wayward student then said to be with Tycho at Copenhagen; Johannes (Hartlieb), his elder brother, I. V. Doctor (Iuris Utriusque Doctor, Doctor of Both Laws — civil and canon) and councillor to the Elector [Palatine] at Heidelberg. M. Iohannes Maior = Johannes Maior (Meier), gymnasium teacher at Augsburg. Andreas Welserus = Andreas Welser, of the Augsburg patrician/merchant Welser family.
  • viginti Ioachimicos = "twenty Joachimsthalers" (silver coins of Joachimsthal, the Joachimsthaler/thaler); kept as the period coin-name.
  • Astronomy / instruments (the technical core). Armillae (Ptolemaicae) = Ptolemaic armillae / armillary (the Ptolemaic astrolabon, the great observing armillary), per glossary. Organum / Instrumentum = instrument. Delator Zodiaci — literally the "carrier/bearer of the zodiac": the ring that carries the ecliptic/zodiac, rendered "zodiacal carrier-ring." Axonia (Gk.-derived, ἀξόνια, "little axles/pivots") — the small pivot-axles on which the carrier-ring turns at the poles; rendered "little axles." Poli mundi = "the poles of the world" (celestial poles); Polorum Aequinoctialis et Eclipticae = the poles of the equinoctial (equator) and of the ecliptic. obliquitas Signiferi = "obliquity of the zodiac" (Signifer = the sign-bearer, the zodiac/ecliptic); the "24 minutes [of arc]" Hainzel cites as the range between greatest and least obliquity. Declinatio Solis maxima = "the Sun's greatest declination" (, declinatio → declination). scrupula (secunda, tertia, quarta) = "scruples [of arc]" — the sexagesimal subdivisions (seconds, thirds, fourths); minuta = "minutes." maxillae Armillarum = "the jaws of the armillae" (the projecting edge-pieces/cheeks of the rings), rendered "jaws [maxillae]." The passage is Hainzel relaying a constructional refinement for the great armillary — allowing the ecliptic-carrying ring to be re-set within a 24′ range to follow the secular change in the obliquity of the ecliptic. The technical prose is dense but coherent; rendered faithfully without smoothing.
  • Copernicus … de Revolutionibus libro [II] cap. 14 — the reference is to Copernicus, De revolutionibus Book II (the construction of the astrolabon/armillary instrument is at II.14 in the standard text). The source as transcribed reads in secundo suo … libro cap. 14; the book-number "second" is preserved.
  • noctuas Athenas — "owls to Athens," the proverb (Erasmus, Adagia; cf. γλαῦκ' Ἀθήναζε) for needlessly supplying what one already has in abundance; Hainzel apologises for explaining instrument-craft to Tycho. Kept as the proverb ("owls to Athens"); the elided verb supplied in brackets "[carry]."
  • febris quartana = quartan fever.
  • Quadrantem … Giggingensem = "our Göggingen quadrant" — the great quadrant at Hainzel's Göggingen estate (spelt Giggingensem in the source; the place is Göggingen/Göggingen near Augsburg, "Goggingen" in Dreyer, "Göggingen" in Norlind). Hainzel reports it blew down in a high wind on 5 December (Nonis Decembris proximis) and is no longer usable.
  • Dates within the letter. 3 Nonas Februarii — "the third [day before] the Nones of February" = 3 February (Nones of February = 5 Feb.; counting inclusively, the 3rd-before = 3 Feb.), the death of Marcus's mother, said to have been born "48 years before, on the Nones of the same month" (i.e. born 5 February c. 1527, died 3 February 1575). Nonis Decembris = the Nones of December = 5 December (collapse of the quadrant). Roman-calendar idiom retained and glossed by the edition's convention; no silent conversion.
  • Dateline. Dabantur Augustae Vindelicorum, die Aequinoctij verni, hoc est 5. Idus Martij, Anno salutis M. D. LXXV = "Given at Augsburg, on the day of the vernal equinox, that is, the 5th [day before] the Ides of March = 11 March, 1575." Ides of March = 15 March; 5. Idus Martij counts inclusively (15, 14, 13, 12, 11) to 11 March. The header date (11 March 1575, O.S.) agrees with the edition label ("1575. 11. marts. Augsburg"). Hainzel's equation of 11 March with the vernal equinox reflects the pre-Gregorian Julian drift of the calendar against the true equinox; the dating is O.S. (the Gregorian reform of 1582 lay seven years ahead, and the Empire/Augsburg used the Julian calendar in 1575).
  • MS witnesses. Two Vienna copies in the Tycho Nachlass: A = ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.7; B = ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66 (shelfmarks as given in the edition).
  • What the recovered text is. The published tail of this letter (Paul Hainzel to Tycho, 11 March 1575) closes with news of a collapsed quadrant at Göggingen and greetings from "my brother" and Andreas Welser; the recovered segment is that brother's own postscript, explicitly headed as being in the hand of Johann Baptist Hainzel — a separate, self-contained note asking Tycho to relay greetings to two men at the Danish court.
  • Peter Oxe = Peder Oxe (1520–1575), at this date Denmark's Rigshovmester (Lord High Steward) and its most powerful royal councillor, effectively running crown finance and administration for Frederick II; he died later the same year (24 October 1575), so this letter falls within his last months in office.
  • The Basel connection. Johann Baptist Hainzel identifies himself as a former fellow-boarder and fellow-student of Peder Oxe at Basel, both taught by "Simon Grynaeus" — the works consulted do not resolve whether this is Simon Grynaeus the elder (1493–1541, the celebrated Basel Hellenist and editor of Euclid/Proclus, a contemporary of Erasmus) or his nephew Simon Grynaeus the younger (1539–1582), also a Basel professor; Oxe's documented period abroad in the 1540s makes the elder Grynaeus the more plausible teacher, but this is not corroborated in our holdings and should be checked against a Peder Oxe biography before use.
  • Unidentified: "D. D. Christiernum Morsianum." Named as Peder Oxe's own tutor ("praeceptorem") and a man Johann Baptist is personally indebted to — this is a distinct individual from Elias Olsen Morsing (Tycho's later Hven assistant, also surnamed for the island of Mors), who was not yet active in the 1540s–50s timeframe implied here. the works consulted do not otherwise identify a "Christiern Morsing" as Peder Oxe's tutor; this is a genuinely new name surfaced by the recovered text and worth flagging for further biographical research.
  • Possible conflict with Dreyer on which Hainzel brother held which office. The recovered text's own heading calls Johann Baptist "Septem vir Augustanus" (a Septemvir — one of the Seven, an Augsburg patrician civic office). Dreyer's Tycho Brahe (1890), by contrast, states that of the two brothers "the former [Johann Baptist] burgomaster, the latter [Paul] an alderman (septemvir)." The letter's own contemporary self-description of Johann Baptist as Septemvir sits awkwardly with Dreyer's assignment of that office to Paul — either Dreyer reversed the brothers' offices or used the titles loosely; this is worth resolving against Augsburg civic records rather than taken as settled.
  • Correction. Two one-word corruptions in the Latin apparatus restored from Dreyer: "intellige" to "intelligo" (TBOO VII, p.21) and "Torquete" to "Torqueto" (TBOO VII, p.19). Both English clauses were already correct and are unchanged.

2 defects found:
- text error (low). the transcribed original has "Torquete", which is not a grammatical form here; Dreyer prints "Torqueto", correctly agreeing in case with the surrounding ablative list. The English correctly identifies the instrument as "the torquetum" regardless, so the translation itself is not at fault — this is a corruption in our published Latin apparatus. Dreyer prints: Torqueto (TBOO VII, p.19) — the ablative case, agreeing with the rest of the ablative list of Regiomontanus's five instruments (Astrolabio Armillari, Regula magna Ptolemaica, Baculo Astronomico, Quadrato geometrico).
- text error (medium). the transcribed original has the imperative "intellige" ("understand!"); Dreyer prints the indicative "intelligo" ("I understand"). This is a one-letter mood shift in our Latin apparatus. The English translation ("I understand...") in fact follows Dreyer's actual reading, not our own the transcribed original — further confirming the transcribed original is the corrupted party here, not the translation. Dreyer prints: intelligo (TBOO VII, p.21, opening of Johann Baptist Haintzelius's postscript).

Repaired. Collated against the Dreyer page images for TBOO VII pp.19-22 (both disputed readings fall on pp.19 and 21, both examined). Both text error confirmed exactly as the audit described; both are pure Latin-apparatus corruptions that had not misled the (already correct) English translation. No remaining uncertainty.
