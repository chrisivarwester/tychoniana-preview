The original

PETRO IUNIOTYCHO BRAHE.

QUI fiat, nobilissime et excellentissime D. PETRE IUNI, quod ad meas bis vel ter ad te datas, plane surdus et mutus videare, equidem ne suspicari quidem, nedum scire liquet. Si enim aliquanto locorum intervallo, Oceani interfluxu disiungamur, attamen cum satis frequens hincinde fiat navigatio, quam et affinitatis inter haec duo regna necessitudo crebriorem oportunioremque reddit, nihil hinc suppetit ad excusandum subsidij. Gravibus quidem et multiplicibus in Aula et Repub. distraheris negotijs. Verum haec tot tantaque esse nequeunt, quin semel arrepto calamo TYCHONI Dano, amico nequaquam fucato atque in horas mutabili, vel paucissimis respondere concederetur. TYCHONEM autem dico, ne, si forte idem tibi accidat, quod illa apud OVIDIUM de suo amatore conqueritur, ipsum quoque nomen sui oblivioni dedisse metuens, ut sic eius verba invertam: Forsitan et quis fit TYCHO vel unde roges, ut sic tibi mei memoriam nonnihil, ut apparet, obliteratam in animum revocem. De quibus autem ad te iam aliquoties scripsi, haec ferme erant: ut BUCHANANI vitam sponte dudum promissam describi, transmittique curare non gravarere: inprimis vero, ut privilegium a Serenissimo Rege vestro tua authoritate atque intercessione mihi procurares: quo caveretur, ne ullum meorum operum hîc impressorum in ipsius Maiestatis regno venderetur, aut absque mea permissione divenderetur. Misique (ni fallor) diplomatis Caesariani, eodem nomine quoad fines Romani Imperij clementer concessi, apographum, quod et nunc facio, si forte alterum non est redditum: non tam ut hinc conspicias, quo tenore conficiendum sit (ipsemet enim pro ea, qua vales, prudentia et eloquendi eximia facultate facile verba invenies, quibus res haec peragatur exorneturque) quam ut huius exemplo promptius Regi vestro persuadeatur, ne in hoc quod peto benevole etiam concedendo difficilem se praebeat: cum et apud eos, quibus de facie ignotus sum, talia minimo negotio impetrârim. IACOBUS enim CURTIUS, Imperij Vicecancellarius, vir praecipuae in Aula Caesariana authoritatis et in literis politioribus, adeoque ipsa Mathesi excellenter versatus, hoc Caesaris privilegium mihi sponte propria procuravit, conscripsit, suisque sumptibus conducto tabellario transmisit, ut ex Literarum ipsius unâ additarum exemplari, quod hîc simul habes, videre licet. Te igitur mihi, quem (modo recordari vacat) ex aliquantulo in hac insula Uraniburgica congressu cognitum habes, hac in parte non defuturum confido. Si in Icona nostram libris edendis praefigendam, acuto et succincto aliquo epigrammate luseris, rem utrique, uti spero, nostrum non inhonorificam praestiteris. Idem petij a magnifico D. Cancellario IOHANNE METELLANO, cuius in poësi argutum ingenium vel paucula illa carmina ostendunt, quae ianuae conclavis vestri Regis, cum hîc unâ adesset, utut inter circumbibendum et colloquendum, obiter quidem, sed non sine nervoso acumine, discedensque mnemosyni loco scripsit, quae etiamnum illic spectantur. Mirifice placet carmen, quod serenissima Maiestas Regia HENRICO GYLD[ENSTIERN], Praefecto Bahusensi, affini meo dilecto, inter transeundum, elogij gratia exaravit. Et vivida in hoc ad Poësin cernitur affectio, ingenijque acumen pari solertia coniunctum. Si non nimium audax censerer, submisse optarem, ab ipsius Maiestate et mihi aliquid de ipsius melliflua et Parnassiaca vena clementer impertiri. Quod ut mihi meisque lucubrationibus honorabile, sic etiam ipsius Maiestati apud eruditos et cordatos censores non indecorum reputabitur. Tua intercessione atque suasu votis meis hac in parte non gravatim (prout confido) satisfiet: hacque ratione me tibi adhuc devinctiorem reddideris. Rogâram in alteris, ut ministrum aliquem, natione Scotum, qui hortulanum ageret, unumque, si fieri posset, aucupem mihi honeste hîc sustentandum mittere satageres, quod et nunc ut praestes † petoque, quamprimum libera et commoda huc concedatur navigatio, eiusmodi hominem unum vel alterum e vestratibus nanciscar. Quin et sic desiderijs meis belle subveneris. Quod si quid in hac nostra patria fuerit, in quo vicissim tibi gratificari per me concedatur, tuum erit ne rogare quidem, sed verbulo solum, quicquid in votis habueris, indicare. Experiere me in exequendo alacrem et promptum.

Vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 20 Martij A. 1592.
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To Master Peter Young, Tycho Brahe.

How it comes about, most noble and most excellent Master Peter Young, that to my letters sent to you two or three times you seem quite deaf and dumb, I cannot even guess, let alone clearly know. For although we are separated by a certain distance of place, with the Ocean flowing between, yet, since voyaging back and forth is frequent enough — and the bond of kinship between these two kingdoms makes it the more frequent and the more convenient — no support for an excuse is to be had from this quarter. You are, to be sure, pulled this way and that by grave and manifold affairs at Court and in the commonwealth. But these cannot be so many and so great that, the pen once snatched up, you should not be allowed to answer Tycho the Dane — a friend by no means feigned, or changeable by the hour — in however few words. And I say "Tycho," lest, if perchance the same should befall you that she in Ovid complains of regarding her lover, I have to fear that you have consigned to oblivion even his very name; so that I may thus invert his words: "Perhaps you may ask too who Tycho is, or whence" — that I may in this way call back into your mind my memory, somewhat blotted out, as it appears. Now the matters about which I have already written to you several times were roughly these: that you would not find it burdensome to see to it that the Life of Buchanan, long since freely promised, be transcribed and sent across; but above all, that by your authority and intercession you would procure me a privilege from your Most Serene King, whereby it should be provided that none of my works printed here be sold within His Majesty's kingdom, or vended without my permission. And I sent (unless I am mistaken) a copy of the Imperial diploma graciously granted on the same account so far as the bounds of the Roman Empire extend — which I do now also, in case the other one has perchance not been delivered: not so much that you may discern from it in what terms the thing is to be drawn up (for you yourself, with that prudence in which you are strong and your outstanding faculty of expression, will easily find words by which this matter may be carried through and adorned), as that, by this example, your King may the more readily be persuaded not to show himself difficult about graciously granting what I ask — since even among those to whom I am unknown by face I have obtained such things with the least trouble. For Jacob Kurtz, Vice-Chancellor of the Empire, a man of foremost authority at the Imperial Court and excellently versed in the more polished letters, and indeed in mathematics itself, procured this privilege of the Emperor for me of his own accord, drew it up, and at his own expense sent it across by a hired courier — as may be seen from the copy of his own letter added herewith, which you have here at the same time. I am confident, therefore, that in this matter you will not fail me — you who (if only there be leisure to recall it) came to know me from a brief meeting on this island of Uraniborg. If you should play with some sharp and pithy epigram to be prefixed to my Icon [portrait], to be set before the books I am bringing out, you would, as I hope, render a service not without honour to us both. The same I have asked of the magnificent Master Chancellor John Maitland, whose keen wit in poetry even those few verses show that he wrote at the door of your King's chamber, when he was here in company — in passing, to be sure, amid the drinking-round and the conversing, yet not without sinewy acuteness — and, as he departed, wrote by way of a memento, which are still to be seen there. Wonderfully pleasing is the poem that His Most Serene Royal Majesty, in passing, composed by way of an elogium for Henrik Gyld[enstierne], Governor of Bohus, my beloved kinsman. And in him a lively bent toward Poetry is discerned, and a keenness of wit joined with equal skill. Were I not to be judged too bold, I would humbly wish that to me too something of His Majesty's mellifluous and Parnassian vein might graciously be imparted. And as this would be honourable to me and to my labours, so too it will be reckoned not unbecoming to His Majesty in the eyes of learned and judicious critics. By your intercession and persuasion my wishes in this matter will (as I trust) be satisfied without reluctance; and in this way you will have bound me to you still more closely. I had asked in another [letter] that you would take pains to send me some servant, Scottish by nation, who might act as a gardener, and one — if it could be done — to act as a fowler, to be maintained here honourably; and that you do this now also I ask †, so that, as soon as free and convenient voyaging hither is open, I may get hold of one or two men of this kind from among your countrymen. Thus indeed you would handsomely come to the aid of my desires. But if there be anything in this our homeland in which it may in turn be granted me to gratify you, it will be yours not even to ask, but only with a little word to indicate whatever you may have in your wishes. You will find me brisk and ready in carrying it out.

Farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 20 March, the year 1592.
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Petrus Iunius = Peter Young (1544–1628), Scottish humanist, tutor (with George Buchanan) to James VI of Scotland, and royal almoner; the Scottish envoy who visited Uraniborg in August 1586 (presenting Tycho with a portrait of Buchanan) and who returned in the king's company in spring 1590 (Dreyer, Tycho Brahe; On Tycho's Island). The "brief meeting on this island" Tycho recalls is the 1586 visit.
  • Heading. The transcription runs the rubric together as "PETRO IUNIOTYCHO BRAHE" — i.e. "To Peter Young, Tycho Brahe" (the missing space between IUNIO and TYCHO is a transcription artefact, not a word; restored in translation).
  • BUCHANANI vitam — the Life of George Buchanan (1506–1582), the celebrated Scottish humanist and James VI's preceptor: his autobiographical Vita ab ipso scripta. Tycho asks Young to have a copy transcribed and shipped.
  • privilegium … a Serenissimo Rege vestro — a printing/sales privilege from James VI protecting Tycho's self-printed books within Scotland, modelled on the Imperial privilege Tycho already held (see next note). "ne ullum meorum operum … venderetur, aut … divenderetur": two coordinated negative purpose-clauses — that none of his works be sold, nor vended (resold/retailed) without his permission; both negations preserved.
  • diploma Caesarianum … IACOBUS CURTIUS, Imperij Vicecancellarius — the Imperial privilege granted "as far as the bounds of the Roman Empire extend," procured for Tycho by Jacob Kurtz von Senftenau (Jakob Kurz/Curtius, d. 1594), Vice-Chancellor of the [Holy Roman] Empire, here praised as learned in letters and in mathematics. Tycho encloses a copy (apographum) of both the diploma and Kurtz's covering letter as a template for the Scottish request.
  • Icona nostram … epigrammate — Tycho's engraved portrait (Icon) intended to be prefixed to his printed works; he invites Young to compose a short epigram for it. (Tycho did circulate such engraved portraits; cf. the Icones sheets in Nielsen's bibliography.)
  • IOHANNES METELLANUS = John Maitland of Thirlestane (c. 1537–1595), Lord Chancellor of Scotland, who accompanied James VI; Tycho recalls verses Maitland inscribed at the door of the king's chamber during the 1590 visit. Metellanus is the latinised form of Maitland.
  • HENRICO GYLD[ENSTIERN], Praefecto Bahusensi — Henrik Gyldenstierne, governor (lensmand / praefectus) of Bohus (Bohuslän), described by Tycho as his "beloved kinsman" (affinis). The source abbreviates the surname GYLD.; expanded in brackets, following the editorial expansion. James VI is said to have composed a complimentary poem (elogium) for him in passing.
  • serenissima Maiestas Regia … melliflua et Parnassiaca vena — James VI as poet; Tycho, praising the king's "mellifluous and Parnassian vein," angles (hedged: si non nimium audax censerer … submisse optarem) for a royal poem of his own. The hedge — "were I not to be judged too bold, I would humbly wish" — is kept; not sharpened into a request.
  • Ovid allusion. "quod illa apud OVIDIUM de suo amatore conqueritur … ut sic eius verba invertam: Forsitan et quis fit TYCHO vel unde roges" — Tycho inverts a line of Ovid (the abandoned-heroine voice of the Heroides; the model is the type "perhaps you ask who I am, or whence"), turning a lover's lament that the beloved has forgotten her very name into a witty reproach that Young may have forgotten Tycho's name. The transcription's "quis fit" is read as quis sit ("who … may be"); the printed fit is kept in the Source block and the reading flagged. The wordplay is preserved, not flattened. [Crux: Ovidian source line not pinpointed here; treated as an echo of the Heroides incipit-type, not a verified single verse.]
  • affinitatis inter haec duo regna necessitudo — "the bond of kinship between these two kingdoms": alludes to the dynastic tie between Denmark and Scotland following James VI's 1589 marriage to Anne of Denmark (Frederik II's daughter), which underlay James's 1590 visit.
  • hortulanum … aucupem — a gardener and a fowler (bird-catcher), Scottish servants Tycho had earlier asked Young to recruit for Uraniborg; he renews the request.
  • Editorial mark †. A dagger appears in the transcription after "quod et nunc ut praestes" ("…ut praestes † petoque…"), marking an editorial crux/uncertain reading at this point in the source apparatus. It is reproduced verbatim in the Source block; the surrounding syntax ("…ut praestes … petoque, quamprimum … nanciscar") is itself somewhat strained, and the translation follows the sense ("that you do this now also I ask, so that … I may get hold of …") while flagging the marked spot. [Crux: the † reading.]
  • Calendar / dateline. The letter's own dateline reads only "Datae Uraniburgi Martij A. 1592" — "Given at Uraniborg, March 1592", with no day stated. The catalogue/edition assigns 20 March 1592 (O.S.), which is not substantiated by the dateline text; by the audit's rule the header leads with the as-written month and appends the catalogued day. Denmark was on the Julian (Old Style) calendar. A companion letter to Hagecius two days later is dated 22 March 1592, consistent with a 20 March dating but not proving it from this text. [Crux: in-letter dateline gives month only; "20 March" is editorial.]
  • Correction. Restored the day-number '20' to the closing dateline ('Datae Uraniburgi 20 Martij A. 1592'), dropped from our transcription; Dreyer, TBOO VII p.332, confirmed by page image.
