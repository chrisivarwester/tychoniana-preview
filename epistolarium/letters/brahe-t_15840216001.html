The original

CLARISSIMO DOCTISSIMOQUE VIRO MAGISTRO ANDREÆ SEVERINI VELLEIO AMICO SUO PLURIMUM DILECTO.
SALUTEM.

BINAS a te Clarissime M. ANDREA, ex quo e Cymbria vestra domum reversus sum, accepi literas, utrisque negotium Typographi illius tractas; respondissem dudum tuæ petitioni, si hyemis importunitas non obstitisset, literasque ad te transmittendi prius fuisset oblata occasio. Scias itaque me illum Holsaticum Typographum, de quo scribis, non multopere expetere. Quod si sponte huc transmigrare velit, eius conatus, quantum in me est, promovebo. Conduxi autem alium iuvenem non uxoratum, qui mihi in impressoria arte exsequenda inserviet, et Francofurtum ad Nundinas pecuniam pro stylo impressorio diversi generis mihi coëmendo nunc transmitto; propriamque officinulam hîc instruere conabor, quæ quotannis mihi usui emolumentoque erit. Si quid tu postmodo prelo destinatum absolveris hucque transmiseris, meis impensis excudetur, si tibi ita visum fuerit. Est autem Typographicus servus, quem conduxi, admodum bene in arte peritus et laborator diligens. Nec ex re mea fuit uxoratum illum cum tota sua familia maiore dispendio quam compendio sustentare. Si post festum Paschatis nos denuo hîc inviseris, veluti discedens ultimo te singulis annis facturum promisisti (testante idipsum graphica tua parietis nostræ inscriptione REVORTAR 83/84/85 oculis nostris quotidie obvia) rem feceris mihi longe gratissimam, habeo enim quædam, de quibus tecum coram agere plurimum exoptarem. Ego enim vix ob negotiorum impendentium multitudinem Cymbriam hac instante æstate revisere potuero. Tu itaque, si cum coniuge itineris socia (modo id possibile erit) mihi adfueris, hospes advenies exoptatissimus. Tuaque causa illud quicquid est itineris viceversa suscipiam, si quando opus fuerit. Vale et saluta METTAM tuam eiusque Patrem et Matrem, Magistrum IACOBUM et amicos omnes ceteros.

Iterum feliciter vale.
Datæ Uraniburgi die 16. Februarij Anno 1584.
T.
TYCHO BRAHE manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned man, Master Anders Sørensen Vedel, his most dearly beloved friend. Greeting.

Two letters from you, most renowned Master Anders, I have received since I returned home from your Cimbria, and in both you treat of the business of that typographer; I should have answered your request long ago, had the harshness of winter not stood in the way, and had an opportunity of conveying a letter to you been offered sooner. Know, then, that I do not greatly want that Holstein typographer of whom you write. But should he be willing of his own accord to remove hither, I shall further his endeavours so far as lies in me. I have, however, engaged another young man, unmarried, who will serve me in carrying out the printer's art; and I am now sending money to Frankfurt to the Fair, to buy up for myself printing-type [stylus impressorius] of various kinds; and I shall try to set up here a little workshop of my own, which will be of use and profit to me year by year. If hereafter you finish anything intended for the press and send it hither, it shall be printed at my expense, if so it seem good to you. The printer's servant whom I have engaged is, moreover, very well skilled in the art and a diligent workman. Nor was it to my advantage to maintain that married man with his whole household at greater cost than profit. If, after the feast of Easter, you visit us again here — as at your last departure you promised you would do every year (the very thing being witnessed by your own inscription drawn on our wall, REVORTAR 83/84/85, daily before our eyes) — you will do me a thing by far most welcome, for I have certain matters which I should most greatly desire to deal with in your presence. For I shall scarcely be able, on account of the multitude of pressing affairs, to revisit Cimbria this coming summer. You, therefore, if (provided it be possible) you are with me together with your wife as companion of the journey, will come as a guest most longed-for. And for your sake I shall undertake that journey, whatever it amounts to, in turn, should there ever be need. Farewell, and greet your Mette and her father and mother, Master Jacob, and all your other friends.

Again, happily, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, the 16th day of February, in the year 1584.
T[ycho].
TYCHO BRAHE, in his own hand.

[Postscript:] That business of the history of Christian III, when I shall have come to Absalon Gyldenstierne, I shall easily dispatch — as though we were by no means busying ourselves with it — and shall afterwards convey to you the book, or a copy of it. See that the letter and the little box [pyxis] destined for Albert Friis be delivered.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Magister Andreas Severini Vellejus = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), Tycho's former preceptor and lifelong friend, the Danish historian and balladeer; appointed Royal Historiographer in 1584. He lived at Ribe in Jutland, where his residence Liljebjerg ("Lily Hill") — with its book and manuscript collections, gardens, and (from 1585) its own printing press — became known as a "Little Uraniborg." The letter shows the two men setting up their presses in the same period.
  • Place. Uraniburgum → Uraniborg, Tycho's observatory-mansion on the island of Hven. Tycho established his own printing press at Uraniborg in 1584, the very year of this letter (Dreyer, Tycho Brahe); the letter documents its provisioning.
  • Cymbria / Cymbriam — Cimbria, the humanist Latin name for Jutland (the Cimbric peninsula); "your Cimbria" = Vedel's home region, where he resided at Ribe. Kept and glossed.
  • Typographus / Typographicus servus — "typographer / printer's servant," i.e. the pressman or compositor Tycho needed to operate his press. The Holsaticus Typographus is an (unnamed) printer from Holstein whom Vedel had recommended; Tycho declines to seek him out, having already hired an unmarried young man.
  • stylus impressorius — literally "printing stylus," here the standard period term for printing-type (founts of type); Tycho is buying type "of various kinds" (diversi generis, i.e. several founts/sizes) at the Frankfurt book fair. Rendered "printing-type"; the Latin flagged.
  • Francofurtum ad Nundinas — "to Frankfurt to the Fair," i.e. the Frankfurt book fair (the Frankfurter Messe), the great European entrepôt for type, books, and printing materials. Nundinae = the (market-)fair.
  • officinulam — diminutive of officina, "a little workshop"; here the small printing-office Tycho means to establish. Rendered "a little workshop of my own."
  • prelo destinatum … excudetur — "intended for the press … shall be printed": prelum = the press; excudere = to print/strike off. Tycho offers to print at his own expense any work Vedel prepares — an offer reflecting the patronage economics of owning a press.
  • maiore dispendio quam compendio — "at greater cost than profit," a rhetorical jingle (dispendium / compendium) explaining why a married pressman with a whole household was uneconomic; the antithesis is preserved.
  • graphica … parietis nostrae inscriptione REVORTAR 83/84/85 — "your own inscription drawn on our wall, REVORTAR 83/84/85": at his departures Vedel had written on a wall at Uraniborg the Latin REVORTAR ("I shall return," variant of revertar) with the successive years 1583 / 1584 / 1585, as a standing pledge to return yearly. graphica … inscriptio = a drawn/written inscription; rendered literally. The years are reproduced exactly as transcribed (83/84/85); the form REVORTAR (for revertar) is kept as written, not corrected.
  • festum Paschatis — "the feast of Easter"; the proposed time for Vedel's visit.
  • Persons greeted. METTA tua = Mette Hansdatter, Vedel's wife (married 1581), daughter of Hans Laugesen, Bishop of Ribe; "her father and mother" are the bishop and his wife. Magister Iacobus = a "Master Jacob" of Vedel's Ribe circle (not further specified in the text). Names kept in their source forms with identification.
  • Subscription. T. … TYCHO BRAHE manu propria — "T[ycho]. … Tycho Brahe, in his own hand," the autograph subscription formula (manu propria = with his own hand); preserved.
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Uraniburgi die 16. Februarij Anno 1584 = "Given at Uraniborg, the 16th day of February, in the year 1584" (Old Style; Denmark held the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline as written agrees with the catalogue label (16 February 1584); no discrepancy. No date is silently converted.
  • Edition / witness note. Text of record is Dreyer's Opera Omnia; the MS witness recorded in the edition is a copy (Afskrift) in Tycho Brahe's private archive at the Rigsarkivet, Copenhagen — i.e. an apograph, not the autograph dispatched to Vedel.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript, restored from Dreyer VII p.77 (dropped by the working transcription).
