The original

CLARISSIMO NOBILISSIMOQUE VIRO D. TYCHONI BRAHE, MATHEMATICO EXIMIO, DOMINO ET AMICO PLURIMUM COLENDO.
S. D.

MITTO tibi Chartam et Typos, quos Witeberga accepi. Papyri singulas Risas aestimat solidis 30. Hisce vasis inclusam ad me miserunt, si quid damni acceperit, non est, quod me culpes. Reliquos Typos DAVID auriga se allaturum, ubi fusi fuerint, promisit. Poscit in pondo 100 Ioachimum unum. In universum pro pecunia transmissa typorum fusori et typis huc transvehendis exigit 4 Thaleros. Quid insuper fieri velis significabis

[HENRICUS BRUCÆUS.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most noble man, Master Tycho Brahe, the eminent mathematician, his lord and friend to be greatly honoured.
Greeting.

I am sending you the paper and the type which I obtained from Wittenberg. He values the single reams of paper at 30 shillings each. They sent it to me enclosed in these casks; if it has taken any harm, there is no reason for you to blame me. The carter David has promised that he will bring the rest of the type once it has been cast. He demands one Joachimsthaler for every 100 [units] by weight. In all, for the money sent to the type-founder and for conveying the type here, he requires 4 thalers. You will let [me] know what you wish to be done besides.

[Heinrich Brucaeus.]

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (Brucäus; 1531–1593), the Flemish-born professor of medicine and mathematics at the University of Rostock, an early correspondent and supporter of Tycho. The letter was therefore written from (or near) Rostock; the place is not stated in the text and is supplied editorially in brackets.
  • Recipient / dating. The salutation addresses D. Tychoni Brahe, mathematico eximio — the body is unambiguously a letter to Tycho. The edition metadata carries a stray field (Frederik II / Sorø / Sjællandske Tegnelser) that belongs to the adjacent register entry, not to this letter; it is disregarded. The edition assigns the year [1585] with the note "empty" (i.e. an undated letter editorially placed in 1585); no dateline survives in the text. The header therefore gives "[1585]" in brackets as an editorial placement, not an as-written date. O.S./N.S. cannot be determined since no day or month is transmitted.
  • MS witness. A = Vienna, ÖNB cod. Vindob. lat. 10686.66, described in the source as an Afskrift (a later copy/apograph), not the original.
  • Commercial / printing terms.
  • Chartam … Papyri … Risas — charta and papyrus both denote paper; risa (= risma, ream) is a ream of paper. "the single reams … at 30 shillings each" renders singulas Risas … solidis 30.
  • Typos … typorum fusori … fusi fuerint — typi here are cast metal printing types (founts of type), and typorum fusor is the typefounder; fusi fuerint = "once they have been cast/founded." This is type for a press, consistent with Tycho's establishment of his own printing operation on Hven. (Typi could in principle mean engraved figures/woodblocks, but fusi/fusor — casting/founder — fixes the sense as cast type.)
  • Witeberga = Wittenberg (ablative of origin, "from Wittenberg"); the paper and type were procured there.
  • Money. solidis 30 = 30 shillings (solidi) — solidus kept as the period accounting term, glossed "shilling." Ioachimum unum = one Joachimsthaler (a Joachimicus, the Joachimsthal silver coin, the original "thaler"). 4 Thaleros = 4 thalers. The two coin-names are treated as distinct denominations and not silently merged.
  • in pondo 100 Ioachimum unum — literally "one Joachimsthaler per 100 in weight." The unit of weight is not specified in the text (pounds is the natural supplement but is not written); rendered "for every 100 [units] by weight," with the bracket marking the unsupplied unit rather than supplying "pounds." Flagged: the weight-unit is genuinely absent from the source.
  • Hisce vasis inclusam … miserunt — "they sent it to me enclosed in these casks/vessels"; vas (pl. vasa) is a container/cask for shipping. The participle inclusam (fem. sing.) agrees with chartam (the paper), so "it" = the paper consignment.
  • DAVID auriga — "the carter/wagoner David," the named carrier engaged to bring the remaining type; auriga = driver/carter.
  • Salutation / subscription. S. D. = salutem dicit, "(sends) greeting". The closing significabis ("you will let [me] know") ends the transmitted body without a Vale; the subscription [Henricus Brucaeus] stands in editorial brackets in the source (the opening "[" is unclosed in the transcription — a copyist/transcription artifact), reproduced as printed in the Source block.
  • Negation check. One negation in the body — non est, quod me culpes ("there is no reason for you to blame me") — reproduced as a single negation.
  • Correction. English recast from three itemised charges to the two the Latin actually lists ('the money sent to the type-founder' / 'conveying the type here'), per Dreyer, TBOO VII p.91; no change to the Latin.

The translation and its checking

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Read Dreyer TBOO VII pp.91-92 (page images). The Brucaeus letter (folio 106r†, undated within the text, consistent with the [1585] catalogue date) is printed complete on p.91, matching the transcribed original word-for-word. Checked every sum and quantity (30 solidi/ream, 100 lb/Joachimsthaler, 4 thalers total) and both named persons (David the carter, Henricus Brucaeus) against the translation — sums and names all correct, but the final accounting sentence mis-parses the genitive 'typorum fusoris', turning a two-item total into an ambiguous three-item one. Does not change the letter's substance (paper, type, and freight arrangements) so graded low-severity, but it is a real defect, so the letter cannot be A.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (low). The Latin lists two charges totalling 4 thalers — (1) the money remitted to the type-founder ('pecunia transmissa typorum fusoris', with 'typorum fusoris' a genitive specifying whose money it is) and (2) conveying the type here — but the English splits this into three items ('the money sent on' / 'the typefounder' / 'conveying the type here'), implying an extra, unspecified payment alongside the founder's fee. At: “In all, for the money sent on, [and] for the typefounder and for conveying the type here, he requires 4 thalers.”.

Repaired. Verified the Latin wording against Dreyer, TBOO VII p.91, confirming the transcribed original's 'typorum fusori/fusoris' reading and the letter's two-charge structure. The fix is confined to the English item count; the Latin was left untouched per the mistranslation repair rule.
