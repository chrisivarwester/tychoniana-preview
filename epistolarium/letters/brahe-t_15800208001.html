The original

HIERONYMUS WOLFIUS AD TYCHONEM BRAHE.
S.

CUM hi populares tui, nobilissime et humanissime D. TYCHO, suam mihi operam benigne pollicerentur, nolui eos sine meis literis dimittere. Scias ergo me pro ætate mediocri esse valetudine, neque quicquam habere, quod de fortuna etiam magnopere querar, sorte mea philosophica contentus. Sed quid interim Uranie tua? Illam quidem non dormitare, non cessare persuasum habemus. Sed nihil ad nos propter locorum intervalla hactenus est perlatum. Quod tamen ut fiat, si pro humanitate tua curaveris, rem nobis gratissimam feceris. Nostrates Astrologi, CYPRIANUS, TURNEISSERUS, STADIUS, HEINSCHIUS et alij multum a vero tempore initij proximæ Eclipseos aberrarunt. A MÆSTLINO diu nihil accepimus. Motus Solis verus adhuc valde controversus est; qui utinam accuratis tuis observationibus certo deprehendatur et constituatur. Quod quo facilius præstare possis, Deum tibi propitium precor.

Vale.
Augustæ Vindelicorum 8. Februarij Anno 1580.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Hieronymus Wolf to Tycho Brahe.
Greeting.

Since these countrymen of yours kindly promised me their service, most noble and most courteous Master Tycho, I was unwilling to let them depart without a letter from me. Know, then, that for my age I am of middling health, and that I have nothing for which I might even greatly complain of fortune, content as I am with my philosopher's lot. But what, meanwhile, of your Urania? That she does not slumber, does not idle, we are persuaded. Yet nothing has thus far been brought to us, on account of the distances between our places. But if, in keeping with your kindness, you will see to it that this comes about, you will do me a most welcome thing. Our [German] astrologers — Cyprian [Leowitz], Thurneisser, Stadius, Heinschius, and many others — strayed far from the true time of the beginning of the recent eclipse. From Maestlin we have for a long while received nothing. The true motion of the Sun is still very much in dispute; would that it be securely ascertained and established by your accurate observations. And that you may the more easily accomplish this, I pray God be favourable to you.

Farewell.
At Augsburg, 8 February, the year 1580.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Hieronymus Wolfius = Hieronymus Wolf (1516–1580), the Augsburg humanist and Greek scholar (the "father of Byzantine studies"), then directing the city's library and school; an old mentor-friend of Tycho's from his Augsburg years (1569–70). This is one of his last letters: he died in October 1580, and the note's remark on his "middling health" stands against that backdrop.
  • hi populares tui — "these countrymen of yours": Danish acquaintances of Tycho passing through Augsburg, who carry the letter and had offered Wolf their service. Populares here = "fellow-countrymen / compatriots," not "the common people."
  • sorte mea philosophica contentus — "content with my philosopher's lot": the Stoic-humanist commonplace of contentment; kept literal rather than paraphrased to "philosophical resignation."
  • Uranie tua — "your Urania," the Muse of astronomy, here a personification of Tycho's astronomical work; the conceit (she "does not slumber, does not idle") is preserved. Tycho had by 1580 begun building Uraniborg (named for this Muse) on Hven; Wolf's Uranie tua and the place-name resonate, but the letter speaks of the Muse/the work, not the building.
  • Astrology / astronomy. Nostrates Astrologi = "our [native, i.e. German] astrologers." The four named:
  • CYPRIANUS = Cyprian Leowitz (Cyprianus Leovitius, 1524–1574), the Bohemian-born astrologer-astronomer active in Augsburg, author of widely-used ephemerides and eclipse tables. (The same Leowitz whose casting of Wolf's own geniture Tycho had corrected — see the 1571 Wolf letter.)
  • TURNEISSERUS = Leonhard Thurneisser zum Thurn (1531–1596), the Basel-born physician, alchemist, and almanac-maker, then at the Brandenburg court in Berlin.
  • STADIUS = Johannes Stadius (1527–1579), the Brabant mathematician and ephemerist (Ephemerides novae, based on the Prutenic Tables).
  • HEINSCHIUS — most plausibly Reinhold Heinsius / Heinisch, a contemporary German almanac- and prognostication-maker; the identification is not fully secure (flagged below).
  • MAeSTLINO = Michael Maestlin (1550–1631), the Tübingen astronomer (later Kepler's teacher), with whom Tycho and Wolf's circle corresponded.
  • proximae Eclipseos — "the recent eclipse": an eclipse (almost certainly solar, given the predicted-time controversy and the following remark on the Sun's motion) shortly before this letter; Wolf's point is that the published German tables badly missed its time of onset. Eclipsis → "eclipse" by the edition's convention
  • Motus Solis verus … controversus — "the true motion of the Sun is still very much in dispute": motus → "motion"; Wolf names the unsettled solar theory as the prize Tycho's accuratis observationibus ("accurate observations") might decide — an early external statement of the observational programme that would become Tycho's instauratio astronomiae.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading S. = Salutem → "Greeting"; Vale → "Farewell"; D. TYCHO = Dominus → "Master".
  • Dateline. Augustae Vindelicorum 8. Februarij Anno 1580 = "at Augsburg (Augusta Vindelicorum), 8 February, the year 1580" (O.S. — Augsburg/Lutheran reckoning still kept the Julian calendar in early 1580; the Gregorian reform came in October 1582). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (8 February 1580, Augsburg); no date discrepancy.
  • Orthography. MAeSTLINO and DIVINAe-type ae-ligatures are reproduced as printed in the source block. initij, alij, intervalla etc. carry the period -ij / consonantal-i spelling; rendered without comment in translation.
