The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD ANDREAM SEVERINI VELLEIUM.

NUPER cum OLIGERUS ROSENKRANTZ consanguineus Affinis et Amicus meus dilectus hîc me per duas invisisset hebdomadas, redditæ mihi sunt unâ cum CHRISTIERNI LANGIJ tuæ literæ, ad quas tunc respondendi commoditas non dabatur. Videntur hæ ultimæ tuæ, licet lenire et excusare quædam in prioribus laborent, a primis tamen illis motu proprio ad me datis [non] longe abesse, et privilegijs scatere iudicis, quæ tamen tibi lubens condono, siquidem non aliter vis. Obijcis ab initio post ea, quæ de LANGIO et Anglicis literis habes (ad quæ postea respondebo) quod iter tuum iuxta promissum non præstitum arguam. Et quid ni? cum sponte promiseris, nec præstiteris? Valetudinem tuam, rem domesticam et anni tempus tunc æque noras atque postea. Miraris igitur me ita singula tua verba et iota notare. Atqui scis me semper Geometrice loqui, cum serio loquor. Quod et in alijs, præsertim Amicis, quibus bene fido, et requiro et observo. Quorsum enim verba, nisi rebus consona? Hoc ego idem sentio atque illudere et negligere. Neque perinde esse, ac candide, aperte et libere agere, uti tu vis. Immo quiddam his repugnans.

Quod aliquo loco non sis inter reliquos Pharisæos et Gnathones, equidem tibi congratulor, nec hanc felicitatem invideo. At quod non talia antea affectâris, quodque adhuc ijs, si liceret, te participem non redderes, haud dixerim. Si autem me hîc fallis, est cur tibi hanc animi celsitudinem et constantiam magis adhuc congratulor. Et si nunc ex Politicis non pendes, veluti antea, cordatius forsan agis. Eorum fructus sunt præ foribus. Cudentur Machiavellistarum doli et technæ in ipsorum, uti meriti sunt, capita. Quod et hactenus iustus iudex exequi solitus est. Quod prandio alicuius interfueris, nihil ad me. Neque ob id ea de re mentionem feci, quia hoc morarer, sed quoniam tu occasionem dabas de ERICO LANGIO et me quædam addendo, quæ tamen neuter nostrum curat. Si talibus, qui astutam vapido servant sub pectore vulpem, fidis, te ipsum decipis. Qui et quales eiusmodi sint, fere omnes in Dania norunt, atque in posterum plus forte noscent. O patria, o mores. Omnia nunc ruere in peius fata ipsa docebant. Si tibi deinceps vixeris, uti scribis, rectissime feceris. Nam uti nunc agitur scena Mundana, si unquam antea, locum meretur illud NASONIS »nimium prælustria vita«, et »bene qui latuit, bene vixit«. Quod mihi iam tandem congratularis mutationem hanc et sortem aliam, rectius facis, quam cum prius eandem indoleres, tanquam invidens mihi hanc felicitatem. Sic enim ego infirmas accipio querelas. Et, si sufficienter delictum illud expiare volueris, tacita quædam psalmodia Uraniæ nostræ omnibusque nobis Propempticum concinna, quodque ea e Cimmerijs, non dico Cimbricis, tenebris, quibus diu multumque offuscabatur, sese tandem Superum dono et provisu vindicârit, atque in apertam et gratam Universæ Europæ lucem universalior reddita asseruerit, congratulare; nobis autem, quod tot Machiavellismis, iniurijs, odijs, invidijs, malevolentijs, calumnijs et detrectationibus atque contemptibus superatis tranquilliori auræ restituti simus. Hisce igitur si Carmine aliquo, uti Poëta es, applauseris, apposite feceris et in gratiam cum Urania nobisque omnibus redieris.

Rogas deinceps, ut desinam minuta quæque in literis tuis non recte intellecta exaggerare. Te scripsisse, quod tu cum familia mei essetis amici in Dania despecti, me statim accepisse, vos a me despectos. Atqui tua non tam clare sonabant verba, pro ut nunc ea explicas. Aiebas enim: Vale,salutatus a tuis amicis in Dania despectis. Non addidisti, te aut tuis. Ideoque tuam sententiam prima fronte non satis assecutus sum. Ingerebat hoc dubium, quod scirem, mihi in Dania a calumniatoribus imputari, quod in despectum patriæ, Regis, consangvineorum et amicorum inde discesserim. Et sane OLIGERUS ROSENKRANTZIUS, cui hæc tua verba legenda dedi, non aliter capere potuit, quam ac si tale quid insinuares. Obscurius enim quam decuit, scripta sunt. Nec ego plane asserui, te quidpiam in me loqui. Ideoque addidi: »si recte tuam mentem percipio« aut simile quid. Et quod tu infers, me excepisse, quasi vos me despiceretis, scilicet tu et tui, id non saltem est minutula quæque exaggerare, sed et sensum meum quasi data opera plane pervertere: cum de te et tuis, quos semper dilexi atque etiamnum diligo, nihil sinistri suspicer, sed in genere de meis in Dania malevolis locutus sim, a quibus despicior, eosque vicissim ex alto rideo et despicio, si quando præ altioribus curis eorum (quod tamen rarissime facio) meminisse lubet. Tuos casus, tuaque infortunia magna ex parte scio, et horum authores non ignoro. Habemus enim hîc mutuo participationem quandam, licet mihi nihil mali inde evenerit, sed longe plus boni etiam invitis authoribus. At nihil tibi hîc exprobro, condolui semper, et amovere ista studui et cum quorundam ex meis offensione, et plus pro te laboravi, quam conscius es, aut facile crediturus. Norunt id utriusque nostrum, non saltem Amici, sed et inimici, tum quoque neutrales.

Quod neque tu neque ego moriemur inulti, omnino mihi persuadeo. Nam iustus est Deus, etiam tacentibus et patienter sustinentibus, et quæ immerito pertulerunt hominibus, illi vindicta danda est, oc hand vil rette det nod, om † Thule nogen sdoon end aldrig leffde eller haffde leffuet: Non tamen Planetarum revolutiones præficiendæ sunt ei, qui libere et sine tempore, in tempore tamen satque tempestive agit.

Demum de victualibus mentionem facis, quod primum et præcipuum esse [de]buit. De his enim nunc maxime agitur in tanta hîc annonæ caritate, quanta vix unquam fuit, et familiæ meæ propria, quæ quartum attingit septenarium, præter eos, qui indies invisunt. At tu videris hîc aurem claudere, paa dansche at være schulørit, oc icke gide høret oc mercket ret. Nam quid pro quo infers. Satis scio, etiam absque tuo iuramento (saa sandt hielpe mig Gud) (an hoc Theologicum est?) quod si præsens ipse fuissem, non potuissem ista expedire aliter sine rerum dispendio: scilicet postquam hyems intervenisset, portus conclusi essent, nec terrestre iter commodum foret. At antea, dum primas istas tuas scribebas, nulla eiusmodi erant obstacula, aut si qua erant, quare adeo categorice promisisti? quare tot tantaque præstanda enumerasti? Sororem etiam meam in auxilium vocans, a qua ne verbum intellexi, licet ter eidem scripserim. Crediderim vos omnes excellenter iudicare. At si quid vobis Scanicæ, id est veræ et fidei tenacis, restat mentis, en nulla nunc adsunt subterfugia, quin recte facere liceat, modo libeat. Ego nullum in me officium ingratum esse sinam. Et si adhuc accesseris unâ cum LANGIO aut etiam solus sine ipso, idque ante Paschatis festum (modo fieri queat) inprimis erit id mihi acceptum et eo plenius priora diluentur errata.

Vale cum uxore a me salutata, totaque domo.
Dabantur Wandesburgi die 18. Februarij Anni 1598.
Tuus TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

Tycho Brahe to Anders Sørensen Vedel.

Lately, when Oluf Rosenkrantz — my kinsman, relation-by-marriage, and beloved friend — had visited me here for two weeks, your letters were delivered to me together with that of Christen Lange, [letters] to which there was then no convenient occasion of replying. These latest letters of yours, though they labour to soften and excuse certain things in the earlier ones, nonetheless seem [not] to be far removed from those first ones sent to me of your own accord, and to teem with a judge's prerogatives — which, however, I gladly pardon you, since you will have it no otherwise. At the outset, after the matters you raise concerning Lange and the English letters (to which I shall reply later), you object that I charge you with not having made your journey according to your promise. And why not [charge you with it]? — since you promised of your own accord, and did not perform it. Your health, your household affairs, and the season of the year you knew then just as well as afterwards. You wonder, then, that I so mark each of your words and every jot. But you know that I always speak geometrically, when I speak in earnest; which in others too, and especially in friends whom I trust well, I both require and observe. For to what end are words, unless they answer to facts? This I hold to be one and the same thing as to mock and to be negligent — and not at all the same as to deal candidly, openly, and freely, as you would have it; rather, [it is] something contrary to these.

That you are in some place not among the rest of the Pharisees and Gnathos, I do indeed congratulate you, nor do I envy you this good fortune. But that you did not before aspire to such things, and that even now you would not, if it were allowed, make yourself a partaker in them — that I would not say. If, however, you are here deceiving me, there is the more reason why I should congratulate you on this loftiness and constancy of mind. And if you now do not hang upon Politics, as before, you act perhaps more shrewdly. Their fruits are at the door. The wiles and devices of the Machiavellians will be hammered down upon their own heads, as they have deserved — which a just judge has hitherto been wont to carry out. That you were present at someone's dinner is nothing to me. Nor did I make mention of that matter on that account, as though I were troubled by it, but because you gave occasion [for it] by adding certain things about Erik Lange and me — things which, however, neither of us cares about. If you trust such men, who keep a cunning fox hidden beneath a stale breast, you deceive your own self. Who and what sort such men are, almost all in Denmark know, and hereafter will perhaps know more. O fatherland, O manners! That all things now rush headlong to the worse, the very fates were teaching. If henceforth you live for yourself, as you write, you will have done most rightly. For as the worldly stage is now played, if ever before, that saying of Naso [Ovid] deserves its place — »[shun] a life too brightly conspicuous«, and »he who has lain well hidden, has lived well«. That you now at last congratulate me on this change and altered lot, you do more rightly than when you earlier grieved over the same, as though begrudging me this good fortune. For thus do I take feeble complaints. And, if you should wish sufficiently to expiate that fault, compose some silent psalmody, a Send-off-song [Propempticon], for our Urania and for us all; and congratulate [her] that she has at last, by the gift and provision of those above, claimed herself out of those Cimmerian — I do not say Cimbric — shadows, by which she was long and greatly darkened, and, made more universal, has set herself free into the open and welcome light of all Europe; and [congratulate] us, that, so many Machiavellisms, injuries, hatreds, envies, ill-wills, calumnies, and disparagements and contempts being overcome, we have been restored to a calmer air. If, therefore, you applaud these things with some Poem, since you are a Poet, you will have done it fittingly, and will have returned into favour with Urania and with us all.

You ask, next, that I cease to magnify each small thing in your letters not rightly understood. [You say] that you wrote that you, with your family, my friends, were despised in Denmark, [and] that I straightway took it that you were despised by me. But your words did not sound so clearly as you now explain them. For you said: »Farewell, greeted by your friends despised in Denmark.« You did not add »by you« or »by yours«. And so at first sight I did not sufficiently grasp your meaning. This doubt was thrust [upon me] because I knew it to be imputed to me by slanderers in Denmark that I had departed thence in contempt of my fatherland, my King, my kinsmen and my friends. And indeed Oluf Rosenkrantz, to whom I gave these words of yours to read, could take them no otherwise than as if you were insinuating something of the sort. For they were written more obscurely than was fitting. Nor did I plainly assert that you were saying anything against me; and so I added: »if I rightly perceive your meaning« or something similar. And as for what you infer — that I took it as though you despised me, namely you and yours — that is not merely to magnify each little thing, but even, as if of set purpose, plainly to pervert my sense: since concerning you and yours, whom I have always loved and even now love, I suspect nothing sinister, but spoke in general of those ill-willed toward me in Denmark, by whom I am despised, and whom I in turn laugh at and despise from on high, whenever (which, however, I very rarely do) it pleases me, in the midst of higher cares, to remember them. Your misfortunes and your reverses I know for the most part, and I am not ignorant of their authors. For we here have a certain shared portion of them in common, though no harm has come to me from that quarter, but far more good, even against the will of their authors. But I reproach you with nothing here; I have always condoled with you, and have striven to remove those troubles even at the cost of giving offence to certain of my own people, and I have laboured more for you than you are aware of, or would readily believe. This both [our] Friends and [our] enemies — and the neutrals too — know, of us both.

That neither you nor I shall die unavenged, I am wholly persuaded. For God is just, even toward those who keep silent and patiently endure; and to men [for those things] which they have undeservedly suffered, vengeance must be rendered by Him — and he will set that wrong to rights, even if † Thule nobody at all… now never lived or had lived: yet the revolutions of the Planets are not to be set in command over Him who acts freely and outside time, yet in time, and timely enough.

At last you make mention of provisions, which ought to have been the first and chief thing. For of these is now most of all the question, in such dearth of grain here as has scarcely ever been; and [provisions] proper to my household, which reaches the fourth seven [i.e. some twenty-eight persons], besides those who visit daily. But you seem here to close your ear — in Danish, to be hard of hearing, and not to care to hear and mark rightly. For you draw a quid pro quo. I know well enough, even without your oath (»so truly help me God« — is this Theological?), that if I myself had been present, I could not have despatched those matters otherwise without loss of goods: namely, after winter had set in, the harbours were closed, nor was a journey by land convenient. But before — while you were writing those first letters of yours — there were no obstacles of the kind; or if there were any, why did you promise so categorically? why did you enumerate so many and so great things to be performed? Calling even my sister to your aid — from whom I have not understood a word, though I have written to her three times. I would believe that you all judge excellently. But if there remains to you any Scanian, that is, true and faithful-keeping, mind, behold, no evasions now present themselves but that it may be possible to do rightly, only let it please [you]. I will let no service done me be ungrateful. And if you should still come, together with Lange, or even alone without him — and that before the feast of Easter (provided it can be done) — that will be especially acceptable to me, and the earlier errors will thereby be the more fully washed away.

Farewell, with your wife greeted by me, and your whole household.
Given at Wandsbek, the 18th day of February, in the year 1598.
Your TYCHO BRAHE,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Andreas Severini Vellejus = Anders Sørensen Vedel (1542–1616), Danish historian, royal historiographer, ballad-collector and clergyman of Ribe; a long-standing friend (and, by this date, a strained one) of Tycho's. The edition's person register fixes this identification.
  • Sender / place / date. Tycho writes from Wandsbek (Lat. Wandesburgum), the estate near Hamburg where, in self-imposed exile after leaving Hven and Denmark (1597), he stayed with Heinrich Rantzau and saw the Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica and the Epistolarum volume through the press. Dateline die 18. Februarij Anni 1598 (O.S.; Denmark and the German Protestant correspondents kept Old Style). The catalogue label agrees (1598. 18. februar. Wandsburg).
  • Persons named. Oligerus Rosenkrantz = Oluf (Holger) Rosenkrantz, Tycho's kinsman and relation by marriage (consanguineus Affinis). Christiernus Langius = Christen Lange. Ericus Langius = Erik Lange (1553–1613), the alchemist nobleman, betrothed to Tycho's sister Sophie Brahe — hence the "sister" (soror mea) called to Vedel's aid near the letter's end.
  • Latin language note. The letter is Neo-Latin throughout, with four short Danish interpolations (see below); the heuristic tag la is correct here. Spelling is Dreyer's edition: consonantal j in -ijs (privilegijs, odijs, Cimmerijs), Geometrice, Machiavellistae.
  • CRUX — the editorial [non]. a primis tamen illis … [non] longe abesse — the [non] is Dreyer's bracketed editorial insertion, not in the witness as transmitted; it is kept bracketed in the Source block and rendered bracketed ("[not] … far removed") in the translation by the audit's rule (no editorial word inserted unbracketed). With the [non], Tycho says Vedel's softer "last letters" are not far from the offensive "first" ones — i.e. still objectionable, consistent with the reproachful continuation ("teem with a judge's prerogatives"). Without the [non] the clause would reverse to "do seem far removed [= an improvement]," which the following sentences contradict. The negation is therefore load-bearing; the sense follows the bracketed reading but the bracket is preserved and the dependence flagged. Likewise [de]buit later in the source is Dreyer's bracketed expansion of debuit.
  • Geometrice loqui — "to speak geometrically," i.e. with exact, demonstrative precision (each word answering to fact "like a geometric proof"); a characteristic Tychonic self-description, not a technical-astronomy term — left as the plain idiom.
  • Pharisaeos et Gnathones — "Pharisees and Gnathos": hypocrites (Scriptural) and parasitic flatterers (Gnatho, the toady of Terence's Eunuchus); kept as types.
  • astutam vapido servant sub pectore vulpem — "keep a cunning fox hidden beneath a stale/sour breast": a near-quotation of Persius, Satires 5.117 (astutam vapido servas sub pectore vulpem); kept as the borrowed image.
  • Ovid (NASONIS). Both tags are from Ovid, Tristia III.4 (Ovid = P. Ovidius Naso): »nimium praelustria vita« compresses Tristia III.4.4–6 (vive sine invidia … nimium … praelustria vita[s] — "shun a life too brightly conspicuous"); »bene qui latuit, bene vixit« = Tristia III.4.25 verbatim ("he who has lain well hidden has lived well"). Tycho quotes them as a paired sententia; kept as quotation, the elliptical vita of the first tag flagged (the verb is suppressed — "shun" is supplied in brackets-sense and noted here rather than smoothed).
  • Propempticum / Urania. Propempticum (Greek προπεμπτικόν) = a send-off / bon-voyage poem; Tycho asks the poet Vedel to write Urania (the Muse of astronomy = Tycho's restored astronomy, astronomia instaurata) a farewell ode on her departure from Denmark. psalmodia = "psalmody," a (silent) song of praise.
  • Cimmerijs, non dico Cimbricis, tenebris — a bilingual/learned pun: the Cimmerian darkness of Homer/Ovid (proverbial deep gloom) set against Cimbric (= Danish/Jutish, from the Cimbri of Jutland). Tycho says astronomy escaped "Cimmerian — I do not say Cimbric [= Danish] — shadows": a barbed gibe at Denmark, made deniable by the near-homophone. Kept and glossed, the wordplay flagged rather than flattened.
  • DANISH interpolation 1 (dagger-marked CRUX). oc hand vil rette det nod, om † Thule nogen sdoon end aldrig leffde eller haffde leffuet. The opening is clear early-modern Danish: oc hand vil rette det nød = "and He will set that distress/wrong to rights." The remainder is corrupt or obscure in the witness: Thule (the classical name for the far North = Denmark/Scandinavia) + nogen ("anyone/nobody") + sdoon (an unintelligible form — possibly a copyist's garble) + end + aldrig leffde eller haffde leffuet ("…never lived or had lived"). The † marks the editor's locus of corruption. A coherent surface sense cannot be recovered without conjecture; rendered word-for-word and explicitly flagged ("† Thule nobody at all… now never lived or had lived"), not smoothed into fluent English. This is the letter's principal crux.
  • DANISH interpolation 2. paa dansche at vaere schulørit, oc icke gide høret oc mercket ret = "in Danish, [namely] to be hard of hearing [skørørt/skuløret 'thick of ear'], and not to care to hear and mark rightly." Tycho code-switches into Danish to characterise Vedel's wilful deafness; the quid pro quo immediately following keeps the legal/rhetorical idiom.
  • DANISH interpolation 3 (oath). saa sandt hielpe mig Gud = "so truly [may] God help me" — a standard Danish oath-formula; Tycho parenthetically and sarcastically queries whether such swearing is Theologicum (becoming of a clergyman like Vedel). Kept in Danish in the Source, glossed in translation.
  • quartum attingit septenarium — "reaches the fourth seven," i.e. his household numbers in the fourth group of seven (toward 28 persons); rendered literally with the sense bracketed, not silently converted to a round figure.
  • Scanicae … mentis — "a Scanian mind," i.e. of Scania (Skåne), the southern-Swedish province then Danish and the Brahe family's home country; Tycho glosses it himself as "true and faithful-keeping," punning on regional character. Kept and glossed.
  • annonae caritate — "dearth of grain/provisions"; annona = the grain-supply/market price (cf. the same idiom in the 1571 Wolf letter). The 1597–98 northern dearth is the real-world referent.
  • Negation audit. The source's negations were counted and reproduced one-for-one, with particular care at the bracketed [non] longe abesse (see CRUX), at Non addidisti, te aut tuis ("You did not add 'by you' or 'by yours'"), at nihil sinistri suspicer / nihil tibi exprobro, and at the closing Non tamen Planetarum revolutiones praeficiendae sunt ("the revolutions of the Planets are not to be set in command"). No negation was added or dropped.
  • Planetarum revolutiones … qui libere et sine tempore, in tempore tamen … agit — revolutio here in its astrological/astronomical sense (planetary cycles,/5.3) but turned theological: the planetary cycles must not be made to govern God, "who acts freely and outside time, yet in time, and timely enough." Kept literal.
  • Calendar. Single date, internally consistent: 18. Februarij Anni 1598 (O.S.). No in-letter date discrepancy; label and dateline agree.
