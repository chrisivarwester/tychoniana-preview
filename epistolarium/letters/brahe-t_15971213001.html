The original

VIRO NOBILISSIMO ET AMPLISSIMO DOMINO TYCHONI BRAHE, DANO, MATHEMATICORUM PRINCIPI AD MANUS.

CUM te, Vir amplissime, Mathematicorum omnium non huius tantum ætatis, sed totius ævi Monarcham constituerit incomparabilis doctrina iudicijque præstantia, iniquum fecero, si opusculo de proportione cœlorum nuper in lucem dato (sub titulo: Prodromus dissertationum cosmographicarum) ullam gloriam, tuo iudicio et commendatione neglecta, aucuper. Quæ me causa movit, ut ignotus ex hoc obscuro Germaniæ angulo literas ad te mitterem, oratum per illum ingentem Veritatis amorem, quem in te quoque inesse fama tuæ magnitudinis prædicat: ut, quid tibi de isthoc videatur negocio, ea synceritate et humanitate, quæ in te prædicatur, explices, brevibusque literis significes. O me felicem, si quod MÆSTLINO, idem et TYCHONI videbitur: quibus ego duobus propugnatoribus non dubitabo totius orbis iam excitatas insectationes forti animo sustinere. Sin autem id consecutus fuero, ut quæ insunt infirma, inepta, et puerilia, qualia mihi per ætatem multa exciderunt, censura volueris notare: an ego non rursum huiusmodi reprehensionem totius mundi, si esset, assensui præferam? Exemplaria non dubito, quin antea habeas: quare facile patior, ut difficultas transmittendi per tantum intervallum a mittendo me avocet. Tuorum autem operum ad nos nihil perfertur: quæque de te sciebam, ex MÆSTLINO didiceram. Dabis igitur veniam, si quibus in locis tui nominis mentionem faciens, iniuriam tibi tuæque hypothesi faciam. Plura dicturum quæsiturumque reverentia me retinet. Aperi luctanti voci viam, scriptis ad me paucissimis: efficies enim, ut tibi demonstrem, quanto sim discendi, quam laudis cupidior.

Vale, Vir Nobilissime, meaque tibi studia commendata habe.
Idibus Decemb. An. 1597.
Nobilitatis et Magnitudinis
Tuæ Reverent.
M. IOHANNES KEPLERUS,
Illustrium Styriæ Procerum in Gymnasio
Græcensi Mathematicus.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most noble and most eminent lord Tycho Brahe, the Dane, Prince of Mathematicians — into his own hands.

Since, most eminent Sir, incomparable learning and outstanding judgement have established you as the Monarch of all mathematicians not only of this age but of the whole of time, I shall act unjustly if, in the little work on the proportion of the heavens lately brought to light (under the title Prodromus dissertationum cosmographicarum [Forerunner of Cosmographical Dissertations]), I were to angle for any glory while neglecting your judgement and commendation. This is the cause that has moved me, unknown as I am, to send a letter to you from this obscure corner of Germany — begging you, by that immense love of Truth which fame, [speaking] of your greatness, proclaims to dwell in you also, to set out what your view of this matter may be, with that sincerity and kindness which is proclaimed in you, and to make it known to me in a brief letter. O happy me, if what seems good to Maestlin shall seem the same to Tycho too! For with these two as my champions I shall not hesitate to bear with a stout heart the attacks already roused against me from the whole world. But if instead I shall obtain this — that you should be willing to mark with your censure whatever is weak, inept, and childish in it (and many such things, owing to my [youthful] age, have slipped from me) — shall I not once again prefer a reproof of this kind, to the assent of the whole world, were it [mine]? I do not doubt that you already have copies; and so I readily allow the difficulty of conveyance across so great a distance to call me away from sending one. But none of your own works reaches us: whatever I knew about you, I had learned from Maestlin. You will therefore grant me pardon if, in certain places where I make mention of your name, I do you and your hypothesis an injury. Reverence holds me back from saying and asking more. Open a way for my struggling voice by writing me but a very few lines: for you will bring it about that I may show you how much keener I am to learn than to be praised.

Farewell, most noble Sir, and hold my endeavours commended to you.
On the Ides of December [13 December], the year 1597.
Of Your Nobility and Greatness
the reverent
Master Johannes Kepler,
Mathematician of the Illustrious Estates of Styria in the Gymnasium of Graz.

Apparatus

  • Sender / direction of the letter. Despite the edition identifier (brahe-t_…), this is Johannes Kepler → Tycho Brahe, Kepler's celebrated first letter to Tycho — his approach as a then-unknown 25-year-old district mathematician in Graz to the reigning observational authority. (The edition's archival logic files the whole correspondence under Brahe.)
  • Correspondent addressed. Tychoni Brahe, Dano, Mathematicorum Principi — Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), styled "Prince of Mathematicians." Ad manus ("into his own hands") is the address-direction on the cover.
  • Sender's self-styling. M. Iohannes Keplerus, Illustrium Styriae Procerum in Gymnasio Graecensi Mathematicus = Master Johannes Kepler, Mathematician of the Illustrious Estates of Styria at the Graz Gymnasium (the Protestant Stiftsschule / Landschaftsschule in Graz, where Kepler held the post of provincial mathematician 1594–1600). M. = Magister (academic Master, this chapter's convention). The place of writing (Graz) is supplied to the header from this subscription; the dateline itself names only the date.
  • opusculo … sub titulo: Prodromus dissertationum cosmographicarum — the Mysterium Cosmographicum (Tübingen, 1596/97), whose full title opens Prodromus dissertationum cosmographicarum, continens mysterium cosmographicum… Kepler's "little work on the proportion of the heavens" (de proportione coelorum) is his polyhedral-nesting account of the planetary spacing. The title is kept in Latin and glossed.
  • Maestlino = Michael Maestlin (Maestlinus, 1550–1631), Kepler's astronomy teacher at Tübingen and his Copernican mentor, who oversaw the printing of the Mysterium. Named twice; spelling normalised to "Maestlin" in the translation.
  • tuae hypothesi — "your hypothesis," i.e. Tycho's world-system (the geo-heliocentric arrangement); hypothesis in the period sense of a cosmological model/arrangement, not a tentative conjecture. Kepler — an open Copernican — pre-apologises for places in the Mysterium where his treatment may slight Tycho's model. Rendered "hypothesis" and the model-sense noted (cf. this chapter's convention orbis/sphaera).
  • Mathematicorum … Monarcham … Principi — the doubled superlative styling ("Monarch of all mathematicians of the whole of time"; "Prince of Mathematicians"). Kept at full courtesy-register; this is captatio benevolentiae, not a settled title.
  • totius orbis iam excitatas insectationes — "the attacks already roused against me from the whole world": the controversy Kepler anticipated (and met) over the Mysterium's Copernican and a-priori argument. propugnatoribus "champions / defenders" — Maestlin and Tycho cast as Kepler's two backers.
  • qualia mihi per aetatem multa exciderunt — "many such things, owing to my age, have slipped from me": Kepler's own hedge of youth/inexperience. Preserved as a hedge, not sharpened into a claim of error.
  • Exemplaria non dubito, quin antea habeas — "I do not doubt that you already have copies": Kepler presumes Tycho has obtained the book, and so excuses himself (citing difficultas transmittendi per tantum intervallum, the difficulty of conveyance over so great a distance) from sending one. He was mistaken — Tycho had not yet seen it — which colours Tycho's reply.
  • Aperi luctanti voci viam — "Open a way for my struggling/labouring voice": luctanti of a voice straining (with diffidence/reverence) to be heard; rendered "struggling," the strain kept.
  • Epistolary formulae. Vale → "Farewell"; meaque tibi studia commendata habe → "and hold my endeavours commended to you"; the subscription Nobilitatis et Magnitudinis Tuae Reverent[issimus] → "Of Your Nobility and Greatness the [most] reverent" (the abbreviation Reverent. expanded to a superlative subscription-formula; the bracketed -issimus is editorial).
  • Calendar / dateline. Idibus Decemb. An. 1597 = "on the Ides of December (13 December), the year 1597," rendered with the Roman idiom and glossed to the civil date by this chapter's convention. Style: dated by Kepler, a Lutheran in Inner-Austrian Habsburg territory; Styria/Graz had adopted the Gregorian (New Style) calendar (introduced in the Inner-Austrian lands 1583), so this "13 December" is very probably N.S., whereas Tycho on Hven still reckoned O.S. The header is tagged [O.S.] to match this chapter's default Tycho-side convention and the edition, but the likely N.S. origin of the dateline is flagged here, not silently converted (the O.S. equivalent would be 3 December 1597). This is the named calendar crux.
