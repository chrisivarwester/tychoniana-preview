The original

CLARISSIMO ERUDITISSIMOQUE VIRO BARTHOLOMÆO SCULTETO, RERUM MATHEMATICARUM EXCELLENTER PERITO, CIVI GORLICENSI, VETERI AMICO SUO PLURIMUM DILECTO.

TOTA hac præterlapsa æstate nihil a te literarum accepi, dilecte mi SCULTETE, sive quod nihil scripseris, ob tabellariorum penuriam, aut Bacho, cui † proces, id non admittente, sive quod literæ istæ interciderint. Quicquid id est, ego nihilo minus has ad te dare, ut[ut] pluribus alijs implicitus occupationibus, non gravabor. Expectas forte Tomum nostrum primum recentiorum cœli Phænomenorum, quem etiam iam dudum obtinuisses, si papyri impressoriæ defectus nostros conatus non retardasset. Licet enim proprium molendinum eius conficiendæ gratia hîc magnis impensis extrui curaverim, omniaque huc requisita iam parata sint, attamen chartopæo, quem in Germaniam pro coëmendis veteramentis amandâram, se subducente, feriatur opus, cum eiusmodi artificem denuo conquirere non in promptu sit. Scripsi tamen in diversa loca pro chartopæo idoneo; quo accepto, spero me hac hyeme de sufficiente papyro typographiæ meæ prospecturum, sicque tomi nostri ascititiorum cœli corporum propediem maturabuntur, ut ad perennium illorum considerationem atque in demonstrationes et numeros resolutionem propius accedere liceat. Memini, te mihi aliquando scripsisse, quod magnam farraginem observationum cœlestium, Lipsiæ aliquando compositarum, reserves; cupio igitur, ut quicquid eiusmodi in archivis tuis habes, illud depromptum atque descriptum mecum communices. Qua de re puto me tibi antea scripsisse. Nam licet non ignorem, observationes istas minime ratas esse, utpote instrumentis vel nullis vel fallacibus acceptas: tamen cum sæpenumero per lineas rectas vel alias dispositiones ad stellas fixas solo oculari intuitu facta sit collimatio, ego, qui loca fixarum stellarum ad amussim emendata habeo, aliqualem Planetarum positum hinc ratiocinabor, et multa alias in his limitabo atque concinnabo, diligenti in singulis adhibita circumspectione; quo, si non exacto, haud nimium tamen aberranti usui, sicubi opus fuerit, destinentur. Eclipses quoque amborum luminarium, inprimis vero Lunæ, sive Gorlicij sive alibi a te vel alijs fideliter observatas, aut aliunde conquisitas, nancisci desidero, quo Meridianorum præsertim differentiam hinc discernam. Si quid superesset scriptorum atque observationum RHÆTICI, id totum mihi prece et pretio comparari averem, quemadmodum et Witichiana, si quæ adhuc residua sunt. Tu in his atque alijs eiusmodi, fac, quod potes, et amici desiderijs gratificari non grave duxeris. Ego sumptus libenter rependam inveniarque vicissim gratus. Addidi his terna meæ effigiei exemplaria, iam nuper in Belgio cupro expressa, rogans, ut, si qui apud vos, aut in vi[ci]nia reperiantur excellentes Poëtæ, quales illic dari nullum est dubium, ut nervosa aliquot et arguta epigrammata in iconem hanc atque operum meorum Astronomicorum celebrationem, delectationis gratia, concinnent, efficere velis, et prima quaque opportunitate cum ceteris, de quibus dixi, huc in Daniam ad me transmittere. Præstiteris hac in parte rem gratam omnique officio vicissim compensandam. Curo id ipsum in alijs quoque regionibus fieri, ut, collatis omnibus, quæ maxime placuerint, seligam. Nam et ego canora Poësi non parum afficior, soleoque interdum a severioribus occupationibus hac me recreare. Plura nunc non addo, cum tu nihil ad ultimas meas rescripseris. Fac igitur, ut propediem aliquid a te responsi habeam, moxque copiosiora a me expectabis.

Vale!
Datæ Uraniburgi die 8. Dec. Anno 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and most learned man Bartholomaeus Scultetus, excellently skilled in mathematical matters, citizen of Görlitz, his old and most beloved friend.

All this past summer I received no letters from you, my beloved Scultetus — whether because you wrote nothing, on account of the scarcity of couriers, or because Bacchus, to whom †proces[?], would not allow it, or because those letters of yours went astray. Whatever the cause, I for my part shall nonetheless not find it a burden to send you these lines, however entangled I am in many other occupations. Perhaps you are expecting our first volume of the more recent phenomena of the heavens, which you would indeed have obtained long since, had a shortage of printing-paper not held back our efforts. For although I have, for the sake of producing it, had a mill of my own built here at great expense, and all the requisites for it are now ready, nevertheless — since the paper-maker, whom I had sent off into Germany to buy up rags, has slipped away — the work stands idle, because to seek out a craftsman of that kind anew is not a thing ready to hand. I have, however, written to various places for a suitable paper-maker; and once one is obtained, I hope this winter to make provision of sufficient paper for my printing-house, and so the volumes of ours on the adscititious bodies of the heavens will soon be brought to maturity, so that one may approach more nearly to the consideration of those perennial bodies and to their resolution into demonstrations and numbers. I recall that you once wrote to me that you keep in reserve a great medley of celestial observations, made at one time at Leipzig; I desire therefore that whatever of that kind you have in your archives, you would bring it forth, write it out, and share it with me. About this matter I think I have written to you before. For although I am well aware that those observations are by no means to be relied upon, taken as they were with instruments either non-existent or deceptive, nevertheless — since the sighting was very often made toward the fixed stars by straight lines or other arrangements, by the bare gaze of the eye alone — I, who have the places of the fixed stars corrected to the exact line, shall from this reason out some approximate position of the planets, and shall in many cases set bounds to these and bring them into order, with diligent circumspection applied to each particular; so that they may be destined for a use which, if not exact, yet is not too greatly erring, wherever there shall be need. The eclipses too of both luminaries — and especially of the Moon — whether observed faithfully by you or by others at Görlitz or elsewhere, or gathered from some other quarter, I long to come by, so that from these I may discern in particular the difference of meridians. If any of the writings and observations of Rhaeticus should survive, I should be eager to procure the whole of it for myself by entreaty and by price — and likewise the Wittich materials, if any are still left over. In these and other matters of the kind, do what you can, and may you not reckon it a burden to gratify a friend's desires. I shall gladly repay the expense and shall be found grateful in my turn. I have added to these [letters] three copies of my likeness, lately engraved in copper in the Low Countries, asking that — if any excellent poets are to be found among you, or in the neighbourhood (and there is no doubt that such are to be had there) — you would arrange for them to compose, for the sake of pleasure, some sinewy and pointed epigrams upon this image and in celebration of my astronomical works, and to send them here to me into Denmark at the very first opportunity, together with the other things of which I have spoken. In this you will have rendered a welcome service, to be repaid in turn by every good office. I am seeing to it that this same thing is done in other regions also, so that, all being brought together, I may select those that please most. For I too am affected not a little by tuneful poetry, and am wont at times to refresh myself with it from my more serious occupations. I add no more now, since you have written nothing in reply to my last. See to it, therefore, that I soon have some answer from you, and presently you will expect more copious matter from me.

Farewell!
Given at Uraniborg on the 8th day of December, in the year 1590.
TYCHO BRAHE,
in his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Bartholomaeus Scultetus = Bartholomäus Scultetus (Bartholomäus Schultz, 1540–1614), the Görlitz mathematician, cartographer and astronomer, later (from 1592) burgomaster of the city. He had been Tycho's fellow-student at Leipzig in the 1560s and remained a lifelong correspondent; the two had exchanged observations of the comet of 1577. The edition gives no MS witness; the text-of-record is the Singularia historico-literaria Lusatica (the Lusatian historical-literary miscellany in which the letter was printed), per the source's edition field. Dreyer (Opera Omnia VII, p. 93 et seq.) prints letters of this correspondence.
  • Place. Gorlicensis / Gorlicij = Görlitz (Lusatia); Uraniburgum = Uraniborg, Tycho's observatory-house on the island of Hven; in Daniam = "into Denmark"; Belgium = the Low Countries (here the place of the portrait engraving).
  • Tomum nostrum primum recentiorum coeli Phaenomenorum — "our first volume of the more recent phenomena of the heavens": Tycho's planned first volume on the recent celestial phenomena, i.e. the comet/new-star series eventually realised as the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata and De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis (the latter printed 1588, still being completed/distributed). Tomi nostri ascititiorum coeli corporum — "the volumes of ours on the adscititious (i.e. newly-arisen, non-permanent) bodies of the heavens": comets and new stars, contrasted with the perennia (the fixed, perennial bodies). ascititiorum rendered "adscititious" to keep Tycho's own distinction between transient and permanent celestial bodies; not smoothed to "additional."
  • Paper-mill passage. proprium molendinum … extrui curaverim — Tycho's own paper-mill built at great expense at Hven to supply his press; chartopaeus = paper-maker (Greek-derived; both occurrences); veteramenta = "rags/old materials" (the rag stock from which paper was made), here "rags"; typographiae meae = "my printing-house." The whole is the documented Hven paper-mill / press supply crisis of c. 1590.
  • farraginem observationum … Lipsiae … compositarum — "a medley of observations made at Leipzig": Scultetus's early Leipzig-era observations, which Tycho wishes copied out of his archives.
  • Technical / observational. loca fixarum stellarum ad amussim emendata — "the places of the fixed stars corrected to the exact line (ad amussim)," i.e. precisely; collimatio … solo oculari intuitu — "sighting by the bare gaze of the eye alone," contrasted with proper instruments; aliqualem Planetarum positum … ratiocinabor — "I shall reason out some approximate position of the planets." The hedges (aliqualem, "minime ratas," si non exacto haud nimium tamen aberranti) are preserved, not sharpened: Tycho explicitly judges Scultetus's old data unreliable yet usable within bounds.
  • Eclipses … amborum luminarium … quo Meridianorum … differentiam hinc discernam — "eclipses of both luminaries [Sun and Moon] … so that I may discern the difference of meridians": lunar (and solar) eclipses used as simultaneous events to determine the difference in longitude (meridian difference) between observing stations. eclipses (Lunae) per glossary.
  • RHAeTICI — Rheticus (Georg Joachim Rheticus, 1514–1574), Copernicus's only disciple; Tycho is eager to acquire any surviving writings and observations of his "by entreaty and by price." Spelled RHAeTICI with the ae-ligature in the source.
  • Witichiana — "the Wittich materials": the papers and books of Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586), the Wrocław (Breslau) mathematician whose work on prosthaphaeresis and on the geo-heliocentric arrangement bore on Tycho's own system and his quarrel with Ursus. Tycho actively pursued Wittich's Nachlass after his death; the request here ("if any are still left over") fits that well-documented hunt. The neuter plural adjective Witichiana = "the things pertaining to Wittich."
  • terna meae effigiei exemplaria … in Belgio cupro expressa — "three copies of my likeness, lately engraved in copper in the Low Countries": copies of the engraved portrait of Tycho, sent with a request for celebratory epigrams (nervosa et arguta epigrammata, "sinewy and pointed epigrams") to adorn the image and praise his astronomical works — Tycho's habitual cultivation of a learned poetic apparatus around his self-presentation.
  • Salutation / subscription. Opening superscription translated in full by the edition's convention; Vale! = "Farewell!"; manu propria = "in his own hand."
  • Calendar. Datae Uraniburgi die 8. Dec. Anno 1590 = "Given at Uraniborg, the 8th day of December, the year 1590" (Old Style; Denmark kept the Julian calendar). The dateline agrees with the catalogue label (1590. 8. december. Uraniborg); no discrepancy. The header date is the as-written dateline date.
  • Crux (graded below). "aut Bacho, cui † proces, id non admittente" — the printed source carries a dagger (†) marking a corruption, and the word proces is not construable Latin in context. The sense of the surrounding clause is secure — Tycho jokingly lists, among the reasons Scultetus may not have written, that Bacchus (i.e. wine / good-fellowship / the season's drinking) "would not allow it." But the exact words governed by cui ("to whom …") cannot be recovered from the corrupt text: plausible underlying readings (e.g. cui [tu] preces [debes] "to whom you owe your prayers," or a verb such as operam das "to whom you devote yourself") are conjecture only. The corrupt form † proces is kept verbatim in the Source block and in the translation, flagged with the dagger and a bracketed query, and not smoothed into a fluent reading.
