The original

BERICHT.

an den herren Tycho Brahen, waß d. Veidt Winßheimb bey d. Johan Böyklen wegen der bücher deß herren Tychoen beuehlicht nach außgerichtet. Eß hat d. Winßheimb alßbaldt nach empfangenem schreiben, darjnnen von dem herren Tychoen dieser sachen gemeldet würdt, sich zu d. Böyklen in der perohn begeben vndt jhnen zu hauße gesucht. Do ehr jhn aber nicht gefunden, darumb ehr d. Böykel hernacher zu d. Winßheimben in seine behaußung sich vorfüget, do jhn der d. Winßheimb deßhalben, wie eß der her Tycho in seinem schreiben angedeutet, angesprochen, worauff d. Böykel diesen bericht vndt antwort vngefehrlichen von sich gegeben, daß ehr Oligero Roßenkrantz auff seine bitte doctoris Pieremanni sehligen bucher auß guttem willen zukommen laßen vor 150 thaler, vnndt wehre solcher kauff vberhaubt vndt in einer summen geschehen; ehr hette auch, wie ehr domalß wehre wegkfehrig geweßen, dem Nicolao Mulaeo solche bücher alle vberantwortet vndt jhm dieselbe außleßen laßen, vndt wehre Rosenkrantz midt denen vndt daß ehr jhm angetzeiget, wie ehr seinem sohne etliche von dießen büchern nach Helmstedt midtgegeben, wohl zufrieden geweßen, wie ehr dan deßen wegen an Rosenkrantz forderlichst selbst schreiben vndt hirjnnen notturftiglich erjnnern wolte. Er hette auch den Wesalium hernacher geschigket, vnangesehen daß Olgar Rosenkrantz solch buch nicht begehrret geweßen, bestehet also entlichen darauff, daß ehr von diesen büchern nichts mehr bey sich habe, vndt solche derhalben nicht von sich geben konne. Welcheß d. Winßheimb, wie es an jhn gelanget, dem herren Tychoen Brahen vnangetzeiget nicht laßen sollen, wubeneben doch d. Böykell anderß mehr zu seiner endtschuldigung angetzogen, welcheß alleß hirmidt anzuhengen von vnnöten erachtet.

De libris residuis a Bochelio extorquendis die decimo septembris adfui vna cum m. Christiano Longomontano et ex mandato d:nj Tychonis ei contenta literarum Holligeri illud negotium concernentium praelegi, cupiens vt in eadem singula nobis praesentibus se resolueret. Quibus praelectis e vestigio respondit Buchelius, verum quidem fuisse se vendidisse Holligero libros catalogo inscriptos, et liberum ad bibliothecam perlustrandam Holligero aut eius interniuncio fuisse accessum, seque (quod iure iurando per animae salutem aliquoties affirmauit) nihil plane interea e bibliotheca illa clanculum distraxisse, neque aliud responsum extorquere potuimus, etiamsi aliquoties coram institimus aequum iustumque esse, vt catalogo librorum Holligero venditorum omnino satisfieret. De libris Tomae Aquinatis similiter affirmavit aliquot quidem eiusdem reliquia bibliothecae olim interfuisse, sed quo pacto ijdem aut inde distincti aut surrepti se nesciuisse. Deinde manuscriptum librum physicum cum vrgeremus non ut describendum sed ut descriptum Holligero primo fuisse devenditum, ita subiecit, filium suum eundem Brunsuigam secum assumpsisse, eo autem reverso curaret unum aut describi aut eundem librum mitti, tandem cum adhuc vlterius flagitaremus, vt id quod aequum et iustum esset, praestaret, et se atque alios a pluribus liberaret molestijs, respondit, se, si Holligerus libros acceptos restitueret, pecuniam eidem renumeraturum, atque ita nobis acquiescendum fuit, re non vt voluimus confecta. Haec contestamur singuli propria manu subscripta.

Christianus Longomontanus
Claudius Mulenus
propria manu.

English translation machine translation, Secure

REPORT.

To Lord Tycho Brahe: what Doctor Veit Winsheim, as commissioned, has accomplished with Doctor Johann Böckel concerning the books in Lord Tycho's matter. Doctor Winsheim, immediately upon receiving the letter in which this matter is mentioned by Lord Tycho, betook himself to Doctor Böckel in person and sought him at [his] house; but, not finding him, Doctor Böckel afterward presented himself at Doctor Winsheim's dwelling, where Doctor Winsheim addressed him on the matter, as Lord Tycho had indicated in his letter. Whereupon Doctor Böckel gave this report and answer, in substance: that he had, at his request, out of good will let Holger Rosenkrantz have the books of the late Doctor Pieremann for 150 thalers, and that such purchase had been made wholesale and in one [lump] sum; he had also — being then about to travel away — handed over all such books to Nicolaus Mule [Claus Mule] and let him pick them out; and Rosenkrantz had been well content with those, and [content] that he [Böckel] had told him how he had given his son some of these books to take to Helmstedt, as he would write to Rosenkrantz himself about this without delay and duly remind [him] of it. He had also afterward sent the Vesalius, notwithstanding that Holger Rosenkrantz had not asked for that book; he insists, then, finally, that he has nothing more of these books with him, and therefore cannot hand them over. Which Doctor Winsheim ought not to leave unreported to Lord Tycho Brahe, as it reached him — though Doctor Böckel adduced still other things for his excuse, all of which it is deemed needless to append here.

Concerning the residual books to be recovered from Böckel, on the tenth day of September I was present, together with Master Christian Longomontanus, and by Lord Tycho's mandate I read out to him [Böckel] the contents of Holger's letter touching that business, desiring that he resolve himself on each point, in our presence. These read out, Böckel answered on the spot that it was indeed true he had sold to Holger the books listed in the catalogue, and that free access to survey the library had been [granted] to Holger or his intermediary; and that he (which he several times affirmed by oath, upon his soul's salvation) had plainly purloined nothing secretly from that library in the meantime; nor could we extort any other answer, even though we several times insisted to his face that it was fair and just that the catalogue of books sold to Holger be entirely satisfied. Concerning the books of Thomas Aquinas, he likewise affirmed that some remnants of the same had once been among the library, but how the same were either separated thence or filched, he knew not. Then, when we pressed that the manuscript physics book had first been sold to Holger not as one to be copied but as [one already] copied, he replied thus: that his son had taken the same with him to Brunswick, but on his return he would see to it that one [copy] either be made, or the same book be sent. At last, when we demanded yet further that he perform what was fair and just, and free himself and others from much trouble, he answered that, if Holger would restore the books received, he would repay him the money; and so we had to acquiesce, the matter not concluded as we wished. These things we attest, each subscribed with [his] own hand.

Christian Longomontanus
Claus Mule,
with [our] own hand.

Apparatus

- Senders / form. A two-part report and sworn testimony addressed to Tycho, signed by Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (Christianus Longomontanus) and Claus Mule (Claudius Mulenus / Nicolaus Mulaeus), two members of Tycho's familia (cf. brahe-t_16000919001, where "Claude Muler" and Longomontanus act as Tycho's couriers). The first part is a German Bericht of Doctor Winsheim's dealings; the second a Latin first-person attestation by Mule (with Longomontanus present) of their own confrontation with Böckel on 10 September. The MS is in the Rigsarkivet's Genealogisk-heraldisk Selskab papers.
- Significance — the Böckel-books affair (continuation of the Winshemius/Rosenkrantz thread). This directly continues the dispute of brahe-t_15980328001 (Tycho→Vitus Winshemius), where Tycho, on behalf of his kinsman Holger Rosenkrantz, pressed Johann Böckel to hand over the books still owed. The matter: Böckel had sold Holger the library of the late Doctor Pieremann (for 150 thalers, wholesale); Holger claims books are missing from the catalogue.
- Böckel's defence (as reported).
1. The sale was en bloc for 150 thalers; he had handed all the books to Claus Mule to sort, and Rosenkrantz was content (including with Böckel's son having taken some to Helmstedt).
2. He had even sent the Vesalius (the De humani corporis fabrica; cf. brahe-t_15980328001) though Holger had not asked for it.
3. Under the formal questioning of 10 September (Mule reading out Holger's letter), Böckel swore "upon his soul's salvation" that he had secretly purloined nothing from the library.
4. On the Thomas Aquinas volumes: some "remnants" had once been in the library, but how they were separated or filched, he did not know.
5. On the manuscript physics book (which Holger says was sold to him as already copied, not to be copied): Böckel's son had taken it to Brunswick, and on his return Böckel would have a copy made or send the book.
6. Final offer: if Holger returns the books received, Böckel will repay the money — so the agents "had to acquiesce, the matter not concluded as we wished."
- Persons named. Christen Sørensen Longomontanus; Claus Mule (= Nicolaus Mulaeus); Doctor Veit Winsheim = Vitus Winshemius (the younger), Wittenberg medical professor and Holger's intermediary (cf. brahe-t_15980328001); Doctor Johann Böckel = Johann Bökel (Bocatius/Boccatius, 1535–1605), Hamburg town physician; Oligerus / Holger = Holger Rosenkrantz "the Learned," Tycho's kinsman; Doctor Pieremann (the late owner of the library); Böckel's son (to Helmstedt / Brunswick); Vesalius and Thomas Aquinas (the books in dispute).
- Textual / language flags. The German header "wegen der bücher deß herren Tychoen" reads "concerning the books in Lord Tycho's matter" — Tycho acting on Holger's behalf (the books are Holger's; Tycho is the commissioning principal), per the thread; rendered to keep that sense, with the ambiguity noted. Latin requia (for reliquiae, "remnants/remains") and nobus (for nobis) are the copy's slips, read by sense. Chancery-report syntax in both languages is dense; the negations (Böckel "purloined nothing") are kept exact.
- Calendar. Dated die decimo septembris / 10 September 1598; Old Style (Lutheran north-German/Danish context). No place in the dateline (edition s.l.); header marked "[no place stated]."
- Correction. Latin: 'nobus' corrected to 'nobis', and 'requia' corrected to 'reliquia', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia XIV, p.147 (translation unaffected in both cases — already correctly rendered).
