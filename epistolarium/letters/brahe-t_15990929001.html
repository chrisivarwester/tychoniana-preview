The original

DEN ERENUESTEN, ACHTBARN VND WOLWEISEN HERRNN N. N., BURGERMAISTERN VND RATHMANNEN DER LÖBLICHENN STADTT HAMBURG, MEINEN JNSONDERN GUNSTIGENN HERRN VND GUTTEN FREUNDEN.

ERENUESTE, achtbare, wolweise, erbare, jnsondere gunstige herrnn vnnd gutte freund, denselbenn sein mein gantz geflissene jederzeitt willige dienst möglichem vleiß nach zuuor. Vnnd kan E.E. vnnd W.W. freindtlicher meinung zuuerstehenn gebenn jch nit vnterlassenn, das, nachdem ich ein gantzes jar nahe bey ewer hoch- vnd weitberumpten stadtt Hamburg auf dem Rantzouischenn hauß Wandesbeck, wie E.E. vnd W.W. bewust, gewonet, von jhrer röm: kay: mt:, vnserem allergnedigstenn herrn zum andernmal an den kaiserlichenn hoff von Wittenberg ab jch bin allergnedigst erfordert wordenn, vnnd, als jch alda meiner gelegenheitt nach gehorsamlich compariret, allen kaiserlichenn gnedigenn willenn vnd bewogenheitt vntertäniglichenn gespuret vnnd mit allermildesten prouision allergnedigst versorget wordenn. Als aber jhre röm: kay: mt: vernommenn, das jch noch etzliche von meinenn astronomischenn instrumenten, die so groß wehrenn, das man sy nit auf der axs furenn könne, jm königreich Dennemarck, meinem liebenn vaterlandt, nebenn etzlichenn andern sachen hinderlassenn hett, jst jhr. röm: kay: mt: allergnedigst gesonnenn vnnd mir thun befehlenn, solches alles nebenn dem, so jch noch zu Magdenburg, alher jn jhr königreich Behmen fur dem winter furderlichst zuebringenn. Zue besserem fortgang aber dessenn hatt jhr. röm: kay: mt: ein schraibenn an E.E. vnd W.W., sowol an einen erbarn rath der stadt Magdenburg, mir allergnedigst hieuon zuestellen vnd an dieselbigen abgehenn lassenn, welches an E.E. vnd W.W., beynebenn jch denselbigenn thue zueschickenn, nit zweifelnde, E.E. vnd W.W. werdenn hierin jhr röm: kay: mt: gehorsamlichenn wilfahrenn vnd mit schiff, sowol anderer gelegenheit, wie es dan meine leutte anzeigenn werdenn, wan die sachenn auß Dennemarck alda ankommenn, zu prouidiren vnd furderlichenn sein, damit, ehe die Elb zufreuret, alles mag wol verwahret hierauf, so weitt als sy schifreich ist, gebracht werdenn. Hieran geschiet vnd thuenn E.E. vnd W.W. zuforderst jhr. röm: kay: mt:, vnserm allergnedigstenn herren, ein allergnedigstes sonders vnd angenehms wolgefallenn, auch mir vnd dem astronomischen werck, so jch vorhabe, gutte promotion, welche jch bey jhr. kay: mt: wil zuruhmenn wissenn, vnnd vmb E.E. vnd W.W., welche jch hiemit Gott jnn seinenn schutz vnd schirm befohlenn habenn wil, hinwiderumb nach meinem vermögenn altzeit geflissenn sein zuuerdienen.

Datiret auf dem kayserlichenn schloß Bennatka, am 29 tag septembris stylo nouo, anno 1599.
Ewer E.E. vnd W.W. altzait freindtwilliger
Tycho Brahe
mitt eigen handtt.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the honourable, worshipful, and right-wise Lords N. N., Burgomasters and Councillors of the praiseworthy city of Hamburg, my especially favourable Lords and good friends.

Honourable, worshipful, right-wise, worthy, especially favourable Lords and good friends: to the same [be] my wholly assiduous, ever-willing service, according to my utmost diligence, beforehand. And I cannot omit to give Your Honours and Wisdoms amicably to understand that, after I had dwelt a whole year near your high- and far-famed city of Hamburg, at the Rantzau house of Wandsbek — as Your Honours and Wisdoms know — I was, for the second time, most graciously summoned by His Roman Imperial Majesty, our most gracious Lord, to the imperial court, away from Wittenberg; and when I had there obediently presented myself, according to my circumstances, I humbly met with every imperial gracious good will and favour, and was most graciously provided for with a most bountiful provision [stipend]. But when His Roman Imperial Majesty learned that I had left behind, in the kingdom of Denmark, my dear fatherland, some of my astronomical instruments — which were so large that they could not be carried upon the axle [by wagon] — together with certain other things, His Roman Imperial Majesty is most graciously minded, and commands me, to bring all this, together with what I [have] still at Magdeburg, hither into his kingdom of Bohemia, with all dispatch before the winter. And for the better furtherance of this, His Roman Imperial Majesty has most graciously had a letter [addressed] to Your Honours and Wisdoms, as well as to the worthy Council of the city of Magdeburg, delivered to me hereupon, and dispatched to the same; which [letter] to Your Honours and Wisdoms I herewith send on to you — not doubting that Your Honours and Wisdoms will herein obediently comply with His Roman Imperial Majesty, and will furnish ship as well as other means (as my people will then indicate, when the things from Denmark arrive there), and be furthering [the matter], so that, before the Elbe freezes over, everything may be brought up [the river] hither, well secured, as far as it is navigable. In this Your Honours and Wisdoms do, first and foremost, a most gracious, special, and welcome pleasure to His Roman Imperial Majesty, our most gracious Lord; and also, to me and to the astronomical work which I have in hand, a good furtherance — which I shall know how to commend before His Imperial Majesty; and toward Your Honours and Wisdoms — whom I herewith will have commended to God's protection and shelter — I will in return, according to my ability, be ever assiduous to deserve well.

Dated at the imperial castle of Benátky, the 29th day of September, New Style, in the year 1599.
Your Honours' and Wisdoms' ever friend-willing
Tycho Brahe,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Recipients. The Burgomasters and Council (Senate) of the city of Hamburg — addressed collectively (N. N. = nomina nominanda, the clerk's placeholder for the individual names), in the high civic register E.E. vnd W.W. (Ehrenveste und Wohlweise, "Your Honours and Wisdoms"). The MS survives in the Hamburg Staatsarchiv among the Transito- und Porto-franco (transit / toll-exemption) papers — the apt file for a freight-passage request.
  • The matter — moving Tycho's instruments to Bohemia. A logistics letter of the first importance for the material history of Tycho's instruments. Having dwelt a year at Wandsbek (the Rantzau house near Hamburg) and then been summoned a second time by Rudolf II to the imperial court (from Wittenberg), and provided with a generous stipend, Tycho now executes the Emperor's command to bring to Bohemia, before winter, the great astronomical instruments left in Denmark — "so large that they could not be carried on the axle [by wagon]" — together with other goods still lying at Magdeburg. He forwards the imperial letters that Rudolf had addressed to the Hamburg and Magdeburg councils, asking Hamburg to provide ship and other means to carry everything up the Elbe, as far as it is navigable, before the river freezes.
  • The attached inventory slip (witness note) — what actually arrived. The MS bears the endorsement Lectum in senatu 21. April a:o 1600 ("read in the senate, 21 April 1600") and an attached slip listing the goods "Van Tychone Braden" that reached Hamburg on 22 April 1600: 10 kasten [crates/baskets], 1 kiste [chest], ein halbe Tonne [half-barrel], ein Achtendel [eighth-barrel], 14 Tonnen "karzen", 2 große Eisen [large irons], 4 eiserne Kachelöfen [iron tiled-stoves], 3 bleierne Kessel [lead kettles/cauldrons], 1 Blasebalg [bellows], 1 kleines Feldbett [small field-bed], 1 großes "tweklafftiges" Eisen mit einem runden Bauche [one large two-fathom iron with a round belly]. The lead cauldrons, bellows and iron stoves point to Tycho's alchemical/metallurgical apparatus as well as the instruments; the "großes zweiklaftiges Eisen mit rundem Bauche" is plausibly a major instrument component or its iron mounting. This is direct documentary evidence of the April 1600 transit of Tycho's effects through Hamburg toward Prague.
  • Dating crux (the edition's convention — flagged, resolved by the source). The letter's own dateline reads "am 29 tag septembris stylo novo, anno 1599" — 29 September 1599, New Style — and the edition identifier (15990929001) agrees. The edition's label field, however, reads "1599. 27. januar n.s. Benatky"; this January date is a catalogue error and is rejected in favour of the as-written dateline, by the edition's convention (lead with the source's own date). The "before the winter / before the Elbe freezes" urgency fits a late-September letter, not a January one. The seven-month gap to the senate's reading (lectum 21 April 1600) reflects the slow assembling and shipment of the goods from Denmark and Magdeburg: the covering letter and imperial mandates were presented when the freight at last came up the Elbe in spring 1600. (That the goods could not in fact be moved "before the winter" of 1599/1600, but only in April 1600, is consistent with — not contradicted by — the September dateline.)
  • Persons/places named. Rudolf II (röm. kay. Mt.); the councils of Hamburg and Magdeburg; Wandsbek (the Rantzau house); Wittenberg; Magdeburg; the kingdoms of Denmark and Bohemia; the Elbe.
  • Register / formulae. Subscription mit eigen Hand (= manu propria); the autograph attestation. Imperial-style honorifics (allergnedigst, allermildeste Provision) throughout.
  • Correction. Corrected the German: "schüutz" restored to Dreyer's "schutz" (TBOO XIV p.154).
