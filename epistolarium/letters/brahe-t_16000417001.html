DER EDELEN VNDT VIELTHUGENDTSAMEN FRAWEN SUSANNEN HAIECKIN, GEBORNER MÖRLIN ETC. DESS HERRN D. THADDÆJ HAGÆCIJ EHLICHEN HAUSSFRAWEN, MEINER INNSONDERS GONSTIGEN VNDT BESONDERN FREUNDIN.

MEINEN freundtlichen gruß mitt wunschung alles guetten zuuor. Edele vndt vielthugendtsame fraw Susanna, ewer schreiben habe ich empfangen vndt daneben die brieffe, so Dauidt Heidlern, euern schuldenern, angehen, vndt bien willens als baldt gen Lübeck meinem factor aldo, einem vornehmen burger, deß nahmen euch itzunder bekandt, zueschreiben vndt vleissig zuuermahnen, daß er dazue thue alles, was ihm jmmer moglich sein kan, nach laut ewer vollmacht vndt ewern herren vndt euch dasselbe geldt einmahnen, vndt werdtt auß beyliegenden zettel zuesehen haben, mitt was worten ich solches vonn ihm begehren wil; wenn ich denn meinen bescheidt hieraus erlange, soll derselbe euch alsbaldt zuewissen gethan werden. Die fraw wolle aber so wohl thun, vndt mihr jhres herren doctoris Thaddæj zuestandt mitt furfallender gelegenheitt ferner wissen lassen. Der allmechtige guetige Gott verleihe ihm helf vndt beystandt, denselben ich euch auch inn seinen schütz vndt schirm trewlich thue befehlen.

Datum vff dem kayss. schloß Benatky den 17 aprilis anno 1600.

To the noble and very-virtuous lady Susanne Hájek, née Mörlin, etc., the lawful wife of Master Dr. Thaddaeus Hagecius, my especially favourable and particular [lady-]friend.

My friendly greeting, with the wishing of all good, beforehand. Noble and very-virtuous lady Susanna, I have received your letter, and therewith the letters concerning David Heidler, your debtor; and I am minded to write at once to Lübeck, to my factor there — a distinguished burgher, whose name is now known to you — and diligently to admonish him to do everything therein that may ever be possible to him, and, according to the tenor of your power-of-attorney, to dun in that money for your lord [husband] and for you. And you will see from the enclosed note with what words I will request such [action] of him. When then I obtain my answer hereupon, the same shall at once be made known to you. But would the lady do so well as to let me know further, with arising opportunity, the condition of her lord Doctor Thaddaeus. May Almighty, kindly God grant him help and assistance — the same [Doctor Thaddaeus] I also faithfully commend, together with you, into His [God's] protection and shelter.

Given at the Imperial castle Benátky, the 17th of April, in the year 1600.

Postscript. Further, dear Jochum Boch, I let you know in all friendliness that the wife of the noble and highly learned Lord Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius (with whom I have kept good friendship and correspondence for some twenty-five years), Susanna Hájek, etc., wrote to me these past days; and since her aforesaid lord and husband is at present, alas, very unwell — both by reason of age (for he is a man of seventy-six years) and of another incidental infirmity — she has on his behalf requested that I would come forward to them with good counsel and help, so that she might get called in, from a certain man said to be there at Lübeck, by name David Heidler, the hundred guilders which her aforesaid lord lent him in ready money. So I have given her assurance that you, as my especial good friend and factor there, would do your best in the matter once you receive the pertinent letters and proofs together with a proper power of attorney; whereupon she at once sent me all of this, which I herewith likewise deliver over to you. And it is Thaddaeus's and my very earnest request to you that you would spare no trouble or diligence, so that these distinguished, good, honest people might come to their payment without further delay.

It is said that this same David Heidler is supposed to have married rich there, and to have gotten the daughter of the lord town-captain there, one of the von Brandensteins — which, however, I do not readily believe. But if it were so, he, Heidler, could easily discharge so small a debt; or if that is not so, he would nonetheless find a way to redeem his bond and pay such money. In so far as he will not do this of his own goodwill, I shall be moved to solicit, with the Roman Imperial Majesty, our most gracious Lord, for a most gracious letter to the lords burgomaster and council of Lübeck, that they help the aforesaid Lord Thaddaeus and his dear wife to their payment, and earnestly see to it as is proper — since I in no wise wish, nor can, abandon the pious, honest man Thaddaeus, my good old friend, in his present infirmity. Then Heidler will have to pay not only the principal sum, but all interest and damage that can be reckoned upon it, and answer in law for not having kept his promise and bond, as befits an honest man — of which you may warn him in good part. And when you write to me again about my other affairs, let me also know the proper answer concerning this.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Susanne Hájek, née Mörlin (Susanna Haieckin, geborne Mörlin), wife of Dr. Thaddaeus Hagecius (Tadeáš Hájek), Tycho's old friend and court-broker at Prague (see brahe-t_15981201001, …0530001, …0329003). A business-courtesy letter.
  • Content. Tycho will help recover a debt owed Susanne (and her husband) by one David Heidler: he will write to his factor [agent] at Lübeck — "a distinguished burgher, whose name is now known to you" (deliberately unnamed) — instructing him, under Susanne's power-of-attorney (Vollmacht), to dun in (einmahnen) the money; an enclosed note (beiliegender Zettel) gives the form of the request. He asks her, in turn, to keep him informed of Hagecius's health.
  • Poignancy. The closing prayer for help and assistance for Hagecius, whom Tycho "faithfully commends … into [God's] protection and shelter," is touched by the fact that Hagecius was failing — he died in September 1600, a few months after this letter.
  • denselben ich euch auch in seinen schutz und schirm treulich tue befehlen — flagged construction: read as "the same [him, Doctor Thaddaeus] I also faithfully commend, [together] with you (euch, dative), into His [God's, seinen] protection and shelter." The pronoun-stacking is loose chancery German; the sense (a joint commendation of the ailing Hagecius and his wife to God) is rendered accordingly.
  • Register. Courteous family/business German; the address to the gentlewoman (Edle und vieltugendsame Frau) and the polite third-person die Frau wolle ("would the lady …") are preserved. A draft (Kladde).
  • Calendar. kayss. schloß Benatky den 17 aprilis anno 1600 = from the Imperial castle of Benátky, 17 April 1600; Bohemia (imperial) = New Style. Header agrees with the label.
  • Language and placement. The recovered text is Early New High German, the Postscripta to Tycho's German letter of 17 April 1600 N.S. from Benátky to Susanne Hájek, née Mörlin, wife of Thaddaeus Hagecius; the postscript itself is addressed past her, to Tycho's Lübeck factor Jochum Boch. Boch is independently attested elsewhere in Tycho's German correspondence as "mein Factor zu Lübeck, Jochum Boch" (Opera Omnia XIV / Friis, Breve og Aktstykker, 1875), confirming the standing relationship this postscript presupposes.
  • Persons. Thaddaeus Hagecius — Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (1525–1600), Rudolf II's imperial physician and one of Tycho's closest Prague allies of some twenty-five years' standing (cf. Hockey et al., Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers) — is here reported gravely unwell; the letter's "76 Jahren" runs slightly ahead of his actual age (about 74–75 in April 1600), whether Tycho's approximation or Susanna's own report relayed through her. His wife Susanna, née Mörlin, is the addressee of the main letter this postscript closes.
  • The debt and Tycho's leverage. David Heidler, resident at Lübeck and said to have married a daughter of the local Stadthauptmann "von Brandenstein," owed Hagecius 100 gulden lent in cash. Tycho threatens, failing voluntary payment, to solicit a letter from Rudolf II to the Lübeck Bürgermeister and Rat — a small, concrete instance of Tycho using his imperial standing to press a friend's private debt.
  • Crux: neither "David Heidler" nor the Lübeck "von Brandenstein" family is identified beyond this letter in the works consulted (Christianson, Dreyer, Friis, the Tycho Brahes Breve register); a firm identification would need Lübeck civic/Stadthauptmann records outside our current holdings.
