The original

PETRO IUNIO TYCHO BRAHE.

EX quo Magnificentia tua, nuper mihi Helsingoræ præsenti, se ante reditum in patriam me in hac tenella insula certo certius invisuram, promittere non gravabatur, cumque omne promissum (veluti communiter fertur) in debitum abeat, idcirco non saltem obnixe petere, sed confidentius etiam exorare non dubito, ut T. M. me crastina luce (quam ipsa etiam commodam auram allaturam non obscure clara pollicentur astra) in hisce Uraniæ dicatis ædibus invisere dedignari nolit; erit sane T. M. non saltem Serenissimi et potentissimi Regis sui nomine, sed etiam proprio, cum Musis sit addictissima, Uraniæ Musarum sorori non postremæ longe acceptissima, cuius etiam præsentiam tum ERICUS LANGIUS, tum etiam FALCHO GOIE, viri nobiles et T. M. studiosissimi ambo, mecum sic avide præstolantur. Da itaque operam, ut votis nostris exoptatus respondeat eventus. Vale. Datæ Uraniburgi 18 Augusti 1586.

Tuæ Magnificentiæ studiosissimus
TYCHO BRAHE.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Peter Young.

Since Your Magnificence, lately when present with me at Helsingør, did not hesitate to promise that, before returning to your homeland, you would visit me more surely than surely upon this tender little island — and since every promise (as the common saying goes) passes into a debt — I do not, therefore, hesitate not merely to entreat you most earnestly, but even more confidently to beg, that Your Magnificence may not disdain to visit me tomorrow by daylight (which the stars too, clear and unobscured, promise will bring fair weather of its own) in this dwelling consecrated to Urania; and Your Magnificence will assuredly be most welcome by far — not only in the name of your own most serene and most mighty King, but in your own right as well, since you are most devoted to the Muses — to Urania, not the least of the Muses' sisters; and your presence too is so eagerly awaited, together with me, by both Erik Lange and Falk Gøye, noblemen and both most devoted to Your Magnificence. Take pains, then, that the longed-for outcome may answer to our wishes. Farewell. Given at Uraniborg, 18 August 1586.

Most devoted to Your Magnificence,
Tycho Brahe.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe (1546–1601), writing from his observatory at Uraniborg on Hven.
  • Recipient. Petro Iunio = Peter Young (Petrus Junius, 1544–1628), the Scottish humanist and diplomat, former tutor (with George Buchanan) to James VI of Scotland, and the king's almoner and librarian. In the summer of 1586 he was in Denmark on a diplomatic mission; the Serenissimus et potentissimus Rex suus ("his own most serene and most mighty King") of the letter is James VI. Tycho's Magnificentia tua / T. M. (Tua Magnificentia) honorific — "Your Magnificence" — suits an ambassadorial visitor.
  • Persons named. ERICUS LANGIUS = Erik Lange (1553–1613), the Danish nobleman and alchemist, Tycho's friend (later betrothed to Tycho's sister Sophie); FALCHO GOIE = Falk Gøye (1545–1594), Danish nobleman of Tycho's circle. Both are present at Uraniborg and join in awaiting Young; viri nobiles … studiosissimi ambo = "noblemen and both most devoted [to you]."
  • Place. Uraniburgi / Uraniae dicatis aedibus = Uraniborg ("dwelling consecrated to Urania,"); Helsingorae = Helsingør (Elsinore), where the prior meeting occurred. hac tenella insula = "this tender little island," i.e. Hven — a self-deprecating diminutive (tenella, "delicate, dainty").
  • Allusion / idiom. omne promissum … in debitum abeat — "every promise passes into a debt": a proverbial commonplace (cf. promissum cadit in debitum) by which Tycho courteously presses Young to make good his pledge; rendered as a saying, with veluti communiter fertur ("as the common saying goes") preserved.
  • certo certius — "more surely than surely," an emphatic figura etymologica; kept as the redundant superlative rather than flattened to "quite certainly."
  • Uraniae Musarum sorori non postremae — "to Urania, not the least of the Muses' sisters": Urania, Muse of astronomy, here personifies Tycho's science; the litotes non postremae ("not the least") is preserved as understatement.
  • Astrology / meteorology. commodam auram allaturam non obscure clara pollicentur astra — "the stars, clear and unobscured, promise [the day] will bring fair weather": a light astrometeorological flourish (Tycho kept a meteorological diary at Uraniborg); aura here = "weather/breeze," not "air" in the abstract. No technical term from the editorial glossary occurs; the prediction is a courteous forecast, not a formal iudicium.
  • crastina luce — "tomorrow by daylight": fixes the invitation to the following day, 19 August 1586 (O.S.); the visit is daytime ("by the light").
  • Salutation / subscription. Heading PETRO IUNIO TYCHO BRAHE = "Tycho Brahe to Peter Young" (dative addressee + nominative sender, standard for the Opera Omnia register). Tuae Magnificentiae studiosissimus = "Most devoted to Your Magnificence".
  • Dateline. Datae Uraniburgi 18 Augusti 1586 = "Given at Uraniborg, 18 August 1586" (Old Style; Denmark used the Julian calendar). The catalogue date (label: Uraniborg) and the as-written dateline agree.
