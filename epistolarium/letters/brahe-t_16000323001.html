The original

[TYCHO AD PETRUM BRAHE].

MIN gandske wenlig hilsen nu och allthiid forsend met Vor Herre. Kierre Peder Brade, frenne och sønderlig gode wen, Gud allmechtigste beuare dig naadelligen och well fran allt ont, och tacker ieg dig ganske gierne for allt beuist gode, hueß vdigien att forskylde ieg stedze vill findis aff formuen willig y hues mode, ieg kand giøre nogit, som du kanst haffue ære och gode aff. Kierre Peder, ieg kunde icke lade att skriffue dig till, eptherdj mig stedis nu bod did ind y landit, huilket ieg och siste gang wille haffue giort, der ieg sende min søn Tyge der ind, menß det bleff eptherlat, for ieg haffde saa megit andit att thage ware, att ieg icke fick stunder dertill, bedendis dig gierne, att du mig derudj icke wille fortencke, det bleff icke lat aff nogen on ville, och ieg vill nest Gudz hielp rette det op igien. Giør du ocsaa vell och skriff mig till, naar her ganger bod imellum, horledis dig och dine lider och hueß du vest, som mig angaaer och behøffuer att wijde. Jeg haffuer sport, att du skulle haffue mist tho aff dine søner, huilket mig giør ont, menß du for att haffue gedult met Gudz wille, hand spare och beuare dine andre børn, du haffuer igien, och vnde dennom att formeris y gudfrøchtighed, død och fromhed, met all løcke och welsignelße. Kierre Peder, ieg tuill inted paa, att du io haffuer fornummit mine villkord och thillstand y denne landz eyn, der forbemelte min søn var der inde y landit, achter icke derfor fornøden det nu ødermere att repetere. Alltingest er endnu, Gudskeloff, y samme wesen, och forhaabis, att det herepther enda kan bliffue bedre, saa att det ingenlunde fortrøder mig, att ieg er dragen aff mitt ferneland her att bø. Och som du vdentuill end[n]u dragis tillminde, huad ieg suarede, der det bleff sagt om den, som lod sig høre, att hand paste inted paa, hor ieg var, ieg motte drage hort och nor mig løstid, vdi so maade, att ieg sagde, wij warre dißbedre att skille ad, nor somme inted holt mig och somme haffde ingen løst at bliffue, och att ieg yndskede, att det kun ellers kunde go den samme vell, han skulle herepther ingen wmag eller besuering haffue aff mig, huilket ieg tilldis vdj trø aar haffuer holt, och achter ødermehre nest Gudz hielp att hole. Huad ere mand haffuer lagt ind dermed vdj fremmede land, att man icke kunde tolle mig derudj riget, och lod mig so lettelligen komme bort, begier ieg inted att thalle om, dett giør mig sellff ont for mit ferneland skyld, att saadant skall sigis och vdspredis, eptherdj der er mange gode och oprechtige fornemme folck, der er udj landit, som inted kan dervdom, mens vden tuill sadan fortrøder. Huad hende skall, det skicker sig selff. Naar du skriffuer mig thill, da giør vel och lad mig vijde, horledis allthingest ganger till der vdj landit, och om nogle aff dine døtre erre gifft eller festemø, och hor dine sønner nu erre, Gud beuare dennom vell fran allt ont, och vnde dig att haffue glæde af dennom, den same gode Gud vill ieg haffue dig met all løcke och velfert befallit. Giør well och sig j. Doritte Gøe och din søster Sophi mange gode netter paa mine vegne.

Daterit paa det keyserlige slot Benatick y Lanthebehemen den 23 martij stylo nouo anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TYCHO TO PEDER BRAHE].

My most friendly greeting, now and always, conveyed with [the blessing of] Our Lord. Dear Peder Brahe, kinsman and especial good friend, may God Almighty preserve you graciously and well from all evil; and I thank you very gladly for all the kindness shown me, which to repay in return I shall always be found willing, according to my means, in whatever way I can do anything from which you may have honour and good. Dear Peder, I could not forbear writing to you, since a messenger is now to hand [going] into the country there — which I would have done last time too, when I sent my son Tyge in there, but it was left undone, because I had so much else to attend to that I got no leisure for it; begging you kindly not to think ill of me for it — it was not omitted out of any ill will, and I will, with God's help, make it good again. Do you also be so good as to write to me, when a messenger passes between us, how you and yours fare, and whatever you know that concerns me and that I need to know. I have heard that you have lost two of your sons, which grieves me; but you must have patience with God's will. May He spare and preserve the other children you have left, and grant them to increase in godliness, virtue, and piety, with all good fortune and blessing. Dear Peder, I doubt not that you learned of my circumstances and condition in this region when my aforesaid son was there in the country; I think it not needful, therefore, to repeat it now any further. Everything is still, God be praised, in the same state, and it is hoped it may yet grow better hereafter — so that I in no way regret that I have departed from my fatherland to dwell here. And as you doubtless still call to mind what I answered when it was said of the one [the King] who let it be heard that he cared not where I was — that I might go far and wide as I pleased — in such wise that I said: we were the better for parting, when some did not retain me and some had no wish to stay; and that I only wished it might otherwise go well with the same; he should hereafter have no trouble or burden from me — which I have kept now for three years, and intend further, with God's help, to keep. What honour one has gained thereby in foreign lands — that they could not tolerate me in the realm, and let me so easily depart — I desire not to speak of; it grieves me, for my fatherland's own sake, that such things should be said and spread abroad, since there are many good and upright men of rank in the country who can do nothing about it, but doubtless regret such things. What is to happen will settle itself. When you write to me, be so good as to let me know how everything goes there in the country, and whether any of your daughters are married or betrothed, and where your sons now are. May God preserve them well from all evil, and grant you to have joy of them; to the same good God I commend you, with all good fortune and welfare. Be so good as to wish Mistress Dorte Gøye and your sister Sophie many good nights on my behalf.

Dated at the imperial castle Benátky in the land of Bohemia, the 23rd of March, New Style, the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Peder Brahe, addressed as frænde ("kinsman") and synderlig gode ven — a relative of Tycho in Denmark. (Precise identification — which Peder Brahe, and his exact kinship — to be firmed up.) The MS is an autograph draft (Kladde) in the Basel Autographensammlung.
  • Significance — a personal exile letter. One of the Danish family letters of Tycho's exile, candid and unguarded. It carries (a) condolence on the death of two of Peder's sons, with the conventional consolation ("have patience with God's will"); (b) Tycho's reflection on his self-imposed exile; and (c) Tycho's continued grievance against the Danish court.
  • The exile and the "King" passage (the substantively important, and trickiest, part). Tycho says he in no way regrets having "departed from my fatherland to dwell here," and recalls his own retort to the report that the King (Christian IV) had "let it be heard that he cared not where I was — I might go far and wide as I pleased": namely, that "we were the better for parting," since "some did not retain me and some had no wish to stay"; he wished only that it might "go well with the same" [the King/realm]; and that the King "should hereafter have no trouble or burden from me — which I have kept now for three years, and intend to keep." He declines to dwell on "what honour" Denmark gained abroad "that they could not tolerate me in the realm and let me so easily depart," grieving "for my fatherland's own sake" that such things are spread, while acknowledging "many good and upright men of rank" in Denmark who deplore it but are powerless. The passage is syntactically dense (a draft) and its pronoun references (the King / the realm / Tycho's well-wish) are read by sense; rendered faithfully, with the hedges kept.
  • Family news & greetings. Tycho asks after Peder's affairs, his daughters (married or betrothed?) and his sons, and closes by greeting Mistress Dorte Gøye and "your sister Sophie" (members of Peder's household; exact relationships to firm up — flagged). He refers to having earlier sent "my son Tyge" (Tycho the Younger) into Denmark — the same son whose Danish errand (retrieving the instruments) recurs in brahe-t_16000120001 and brahe-t_16000126001/002.
  • Textual flags.
  • "gudfrøchtighed, død och fromhed" — "godliness, [virtue], and piety": the draft's død ("death") is read as a slip for _dyd_ ("virtue"), which the formulaic triad requires; rendered "virtue" and flagged.
  • The "King" passage's tangled clauses and pronouns (above) are the main reason for the graded confidence.
  • Calendar. Dateline paa det keyserlige slot Benatick y Lanthebehemen den 23 martij stylo nouo anni 1600 — "at the imperial castle Benátky in the land of Bohemia, 23 March, New Style, 1600." Notably Tycho dates even a letter to Denmark (which kept Old Style) in the imperial New Style, marking it explicitly. Header agrees with the label.
