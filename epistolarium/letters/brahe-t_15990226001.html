The original

CLARISSIMO ET EXCELLENTISSIMO VIRO DOMINO DOCTORI THADDÆO HAGGECIO AB HAYCK, INCLYTI REGNI BOIEMIAe ARCHIATRO ORDINARIO, AMICO SUO VETERI ET HONORANDO.

S. D.

EXOPTATE accidit, excellentissime et amantissime Domine Doctor THADDÆE, amice honorande, quod heri magnifici et amplissimi Domini CORADUCIJ, Procancellarij Imperialis, literas humaniss. Pragam ex mandato Sae Imperatoriae Maiestatis, Domini nostri clementissimi, me evocantes accepi. Et parerem sane actutum huic augustae et clementissimae evocationi absque ulla mora, statimque iter ingrederer, si viarum transitus ubique ferme nivibus altis repletus id pateretur et equi idonei in promptu essent; nam meos, uti ultimis indicavi, nuper Lipsiae distrahi feci. Omnem tamen curam impendam, ut intra paucos dies pro uno vel altero curru alij coëmantur, aut si id commode fieri nequeat, conducantur, resque meas ita disponam, ut post 14 ab hinc dies iter, favente divino Numine, ad vos suscipere queam. Spero enim itinerum nimias nunc difficultates tum minus obfuturae; et interea expecto nuncium, quem, statim ac huc veni, in Daniam misi, ut inde mihi novum adferret viaticum, consumpto priore in itinere Dresdensi atque hîc, quemadmodum me prius tibi per literas significasse memini. Sin vero is ob hyemis duritiem et perseverantiam mariumque congelationem istic moram traxerit, ut ante dictum tempus adesse nequeat, dabo nihilominus operam, ut aliunde viaticum acquiram, ne ea de causa iter ulterius procrastinetur, sed assumptis mecum paucis comitibus et ministris, reliquos unâ cum familia mea muliebri hîc relinquam, donec ad vos excurrens de Caesaris clementissima voluntate certior factus fuero. Et, si intellexero vias per partem aliquam Slesiae commodiores, quam per Misniam (ut multi existimant) futuras, illac cursum dirigam, quanquam nonnihil longiore ambagine, ut sperem, me intra quatuor abhinc hebdomadas vel circiter, conatibus nostris favente divina ope, Pragae vobis adfuturum.

Cuperem autem quam maxime te ibi praesentem tunc reperire, ut tuo frui possim alloquio, et abs te consilium, sicubi opus fuerit captare, cum ego tanquam peregrinus locorum et hominum ignarus sim. Vellem etiam me in aliqua civitate, quam transire oporteat, tuis literis obvijs praemoneri, in quodnam Pragae hospitium, quod a lue tutum sit et mihi commodum, me conferre possim. Mallem id publicum non esse, ut tranquillus degam et propria etiam quadra, si id magis expediat, vivam. Per Slesiam autem, uti opinor, transituro, quoniam (veluti intelligo) primum Brandusium Pragae proximum occurrit, optarim me istac tuas, quae me de hospitio et alijs necessarijs instruant, invenire literas.

Oportune vero accidit, ut hodie sese offerret quidam auriga Olomucensis, VALENTINUS MULLER nomine, alias vacuo curru Pragam, unde hisce diebus venit, rediturus. Qui cistam quandam cum meis grandiusculis unâ cum alijs thecis secum advehit, in quibus est instrumentum quoddam cocleis disiungi et componi aptum atque tam distantijs quam altitudinibus siderum accurate rimandis serviens, et praeterea nonnulla alia huic exercitio deputata facile portatilia. Pepigi cum dicto auriga pro decem Ioachimicis, dimidium illi hîc solvi, reliquos 5 taleros eum a te accepturum promisi. Rogo itaque, ut recepta in tuas aedes hac cista, velis fidem meam redimere et pecuniam hanc aurigae meo nomine numerare, quam primum volente Numine advenero, tibi cum alijs mea de causa forte alias expositis nummis restituendam. Cuperem illo instrumento non saltem elevationem poli, interea dum ibi sum, accurate denotare, sed et observationi Planetarum, ♄ni praesertim tunc acronychij, diligenter attendere, cum sit iuxta limites maximae latitudinis boreae, quo in loco ante 29 annos me eundem Augustae Vindelicorum observasse memini, licet instrumentis tum non adeo exquisitis instructus, ut nunc integra eius peracta sit revolutio, quam in alijs quoque ipsius, quotquot interea habere licuit, acronychijs sitibus diligenter examinavi, uti et in reliquis duobus superioribus Planetis, quo Apogaea et Eccentricitates eorundem exactius, quam hactenus praestitum est, praefiniri possint, quod et satis praecise me assecutum nihil dubito. Sed de his et similibus plurimisque alijs coram Deo dante plenius agemus.

Scripsi etiam magnifico et amplissimo D. CORADUCIO de occasionibus, cur meus accessus aliquantulum differatur, ut morulam hanc pro suo candore eo promptius excusatam habeat, cui etiam tanquam fautori et promotori apud Caesaream Maiestatem benignissimo et habeo et perpetuo me debere agnosco gratias relaturus, si vires meae ulla ratione id unquam sustinuerint. Vale mi charissime THADDÆE, utque te sanum et prosperum Pragae inveniam, tuaque desideratissima frui possim consuetudine, faxit Deus. Dabantur Vitembergae die 26. Feb. stylo novo Anno etc. 99.

Tui amantissimus TYCHO BRAHE.

[Postscript — restored from Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, pp. 146–147; dropped by the working transcription. See Apparatus.]
Saluta meo nomine quam officiosissime Dominum BARVICIUM. / POSTSCRIPTA. INDICARAM tibi antea me quaedam hic Witebergae typis complere in meis operibus decrevisse, praesertim ea, quae in Progymnasmatum torno primo tecum communicato desiderantur. Cumque papyrus idonea ac priori similis hic vel Lipsiae non praestaret, egi cum Chartopaeis in vicinia habitantibus, ut eiuscemodi chartam in copia mihi pararent, at illi negligentiores negotium nimium procrastinarunt, ut ob id eam, quam Wandesburgi residuam habui in Dania propria mea moletrina confectam, nuper huc advehi curârim. Sed quoniam mihi nunc Pragam ex clementissima Caesareae Maiestatis voluntate eundum est et iter maturandum, cogor ista omnia insecta relinquere, donec inchoatam telam pertexendi commodior detur occasio. Apposui quaedam carmina partim ab insigni huius Academiae Poëta et Professore TAUBMANO mihi, cum Dresdae essem, missa, partim a BRASCHIO eximio pariter Academiae Rostochiensis Poëta, interea dum hic sum, mihi impertita, si forte tibi ea legere vacarit; videntur in eo genere Horatianae odae arguta et erudita. Addidi etiam Domini Doctoris DAVIDIS CHYTRÆI literas, quae etiam Dresdae mihi redditae sunt, si quidem in ijs tui faciat mentionem; poteris illas usque in meum adventum reservare.

To the most renowned and most excellent man, Lord Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius of Hájek, ordinary Chief-Physician [Archiater] of the renowned Kingdom of Bohemia, his old and honoured friend.

Greeting.

As longed-for [exoptate] it happened, most excellent and most loving Lord Doctor Thaddaeus, friend to be honoured, that yesterday I received the most courteous letter of the magnificent and most ample Lord Coraduz, Imperial Vice-Chancellor, summoning me to Prague by the mandate of the Sacred Imperial Majesty, our most clement Lord. And I would indeed obey this august and most clement summons at once, without any delay, and would immediately set out, if the passage of the roads — almost everywhere filled with deep snows — allowed it, and if suitable horses were at hand; for mine, as I indicated in my last [letter], I lately had sold at Leipzig. Yet I will spend all care that within a few days others be bought for one or two wagons, or, if that cannot conveniently be done, hired; and I will so arrange my affairs that after 14 days from now I may, God favouring, undertake the journey to you. For I hope the now-excessive difficulties of the roads will then be less of a hindrance; and meanwhile I await the messenger whom, as soon as I came hither, I sent into Denmark to bring me fresh travel-money [viaticum] — the former being spent on the Dresden journey and here, as I remember I signified to you before. But if he, on account of the harshness and persistence of winter and the freezing of the seas, has drawn out his delay there, so that he cannot be present before the said time, I will nonetheless take pains to get travel-money from elsewhere, lest the journey be further put off; but, taking a few companions and servants with me, I will leave the rest, together with my womenfolk, here, until — running over to you — I am made more certain of the Emperor's most clement will. And if I understand that the roads through some part of Silesia will be more convenient than through Meissen (as many think), I will direct my course that way, although by a somewhat longer roundabout — so that I hope, within four weeks from now or thereabouts, by divine help favouring our endeavours, to be present with you at Prague.

But I should most greatly wish to find you present there then, that I may enjoy your conversation and take counsel from you wherever there be need, since I, as a stranger, am ignorant of the places and the men. I should also wish to be forewarned, by your letters meeting me in some city I must pass through, into what lodging at Prague — safe from the plague and convenient for me — I may betake myself. I should prefer it not be a public one, that I may live tranquilly, and even at my own table [propria quadra], if that be more expedient. But to me passing through Silesia, as I think — since (as I understand) Brandeis, nearest to Prague, first occurs — I should wish to find there your letters instructing me about the lodging and other necessaries.

But it happened opportunely that today a certain wagoner of Olomouc, Valentin Müller by name, offered himself, otherwise about to return with an empty wagon to Prague, whence he came these days. He carries with him a certain chest with my larger [things], together with other cases, in which is a certain instrument, fit to be taken apart and assembled by screws, serving for accurately searching out both the distances and the altitudes of the stars, and besides some other things assigned to this exercise [of observation], easily portable. I agreed with the said wagoner for ten joachimsthalers, paid him half here, [and] promised that he would receive the remaining 5 thalers from you. I ask therefore that, this chest received into your house, you would redeem my pledge and pay this money to the wagoner in my name — to be restored to you, as soon as, God willing, I arrive, with other monies perhaps otherwise laid out on my account. I should wish, with that instrument, not only to note the elevation of the pole accurately while I am there, but also to attend diligently to the observation of the Planets — especially of Saturn [♄], then acronychal [at opposition], since it is near the limits of maximum northern latitude; in which place I remember to have observed the same 29 years ago at Augsburg, though then furnished with instruments not so exquisite — so that now its complete revolution has been accomplished, which I have also diligently examined in its other acronychal positions, as many as I could obtain meanwhile, as too in the remaining two superior Planets [Jupiter and Mars], so that the Apogees and Eccentricities of the same may be defined more exactly than has hitherto been done — which I doubt not I have attained precisely enough. But of these and similar things, and very many others, we will treat more fully in person, God granting.

I have also written to the magnificent and most ample Lord Coraduz about the reasons why my coming is somewhat deferred, that he may the more readily hold this little delay excused, according to his candour — to whom also, as my most kindly patron and promoter with the Imperial Majesty, I both give and acknowledge that I perpetually owe thanks, to be repaid if my powers ever in any way sustain it. Farewell, my dearest Thaddaeus; and may God grant that I find you healthy and prosperous at Prague, and may enjoy your most longed-for company. Given at Wittenberg, the 26th of February, New Style, the year etc. [15]99.

Most loving of you, Tycho Brahe.

[Postscript:] Greet Lord Barwitz in my name as dutifully as may be. / POSTSCRIPT. I had indicated to you before that I had resolved to complete certain things here at Wittenberg in print, among my works — especially those which are wanting in the first volume of the Progymnasmata communicated to you. And since suitable paper, like the former, was not to be had here or at Leipzig, I dealt with the paper-makers dwelling in the neighbourhood, that they should prepare me such paper in quantity; but they, the more negligent, too much procrastinated the business, so that on that account I lately had brought hither that [paper] which I had left over at Wandsbek, made in Denmark at my own mill. But since I must now go to Prague by the most clement will of the Imperial Majesty, and must hasten the journey, I am compelled to leave all these things unfinished, until a more convenient occasion be given of weaving to the end the web I have begun. I have appended some poems — partly sent to me, when I was at Dresden, by Taubmann, the distinguished Poet and Professor of this Academy; partly bestowed on me, while I am here, by Braschius, likewise an eminent Poet of the Academy of Rostock — if perhaps you have leisure to read them; in that kind they seem witty and learned, like Horatian odes. I have also added the letter of Lord Doctor David Chytraeus, which was likewise delivered to me at Dresden, inasmuch as he makes mention of you in it; you may keep them until my arrival.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck (Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku, 1525–1600), imperial-Bohemian Archiater (chief physician), astronomer, and Tycho's oldest and most trusted Bohemian friend and broker at the imperial court (cf. brahe-t_15990321001 and the long Hagecius series). He died later in 1599; this is among the last of their letters.
  • Significance — the eve of the journey to Prague (the imperial call answered). Written from Wittenberg the day after Tycho received Coraduz's letter of summons (by Rudolf II's mandate) to Prague. The letter is the logistical heart of the move that would make Tycho Imperial Mathematician:
  • Roads & horses: deep snow everywhere; Tycho had sold his horses at Leipzig (his last letter), must buy or hire wagon-teams; reckons c. 14 days to set out, c. 4 weeks to reach Prague.
  • Money: awaiting a messenger sent to Denmark for fresh viaticum (the earlier spent on the Dresden leg); the seas frozen may delay him, so Tycho will raise funds elsewhere rather than wait.
  • Household: he will take a few companions and servants, leaving the rest with his womenfolk at Wittenberg until the Emperor's will is confirmed in person.
  • Route: Silesia (via Brandeis/Brandýs) vs. Meissen — Silesia longer but better; asks Hagecius to send letters ahead to a transit city.
  • Lodging: begs Hagecius to secure a private, plague-free lodging at Prague (not a public inn), where he can keep his own table.
  • The instrument shipment & the Saturn observation (the astronomical core). A wagoner of Olomouc, Valentin Müller, returning empty to Prague, carries ahead a chest with a screw-jointed portable instrument for measuring stellar distances and altitudes — for 10 joachimsthalers, half paid, half to be paid by Hagecius on receipt. Tycho means to use it at once to fix the pole-elevation at Prague and, above all, to observe Saturn at opposition (acronychus) near its maximum northern latitude — the same configuration he had observed 29 years before at Augsburg (c. 1570, with cruder instruments). Saturn's c. 29-year period means one full revolution has now elapsed, letting him refine the apogees and eccentricities of the three superior planets (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars) — a vivid glimpse of the continuity of Tycho's planetary program from his youth to the imperial years.
  • Coraduz. Rudolf von Coraduz (Korad/Coraduz), Imperial Vice-Chancellor (Procancellarius), Tycho's chief patron-broker at court; Tycho writes him separately to excuse the delay.
  • Notable readings. exoptate (opening) is written in the source as the Greek-lettered flourish ΕΧΟΡΤΑΤΕ / EXΟΡΤΑΤΕ = Latin exoptate, "as longed-for/desired" — rendered accordingly (minor orthographic flag, sense secure). propria quadra — "[at] my own table/fare" (Juvenalian idiom for self-catering). acronychus/-ij — acronychal, i.e. rising at sunset = at opposition (glossary term). grandiuscula — "my larger [things]" (the bulkier instruments/effects).
  • Persons & places named. Thaddaeus Hagecius; Coraduz (Vice-Chancellor); Rudolf II (Caesar); Valentin Müller of Olomouc (wagoner). Places: Wittenberg (whence written), Leipzig, Dresden, Silesia, Meissen, Brandeis (Brandýs), Prague, Augsburg (the 1570 observation).
  • Calendar. Dateline Vitembergae die 26. Feb. stylo novo Anno … 99 — Tycho dates it explicitly New Style (anticipating the imperial reckoning, though Saxony itself was still Old Style) → 26 February 1599 [N.S.]. Header and label agree.
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing greeting (Barwitz) + postscript (the Progymnasmata paper-failure, Wandsbek-mill paper, Taubmann/Braschius odes, the Chytraeus letter), restored from Dreyer VIII pp.146–147 (dropped by the working transcription).
  • Correction. Restored the dropped numeral "5" in "reliquos 5 taleros" (Dreyer, TBOO VIII, p.146); the English "remaining thalers" is now "remaining 5 thalers" to match.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia VIII pp. 144–147; defects the audit noted: text error (1).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.145-147 read as page images. Collated the full Latin word-for-word against the transcribed original and found one departure: Dreyer prints 'reliquos 5 taleros' where our transcribed original and the translation both read 'reliquos taleros', dropping the numeral. Checked every other name/number/date against the English: Coraduz/Procancellarius (no first name given, correctly omitted), 14 days, Silesia/Meissen, four weeks, Brandeis (Brandusium), Valentin Muller of Olomouc, ten joachimsthalers, 29 years/Augsburg, Barwitz, Taubmann/Wittenberg, Braschius/Rostock, David Chytraeus, and the closing date 26 February N.S. 1599 — all correct. The one text divergence is real and citation-affecting, so despite an otherwise flawless and complete translation this cannot stand at the translator's own grade Secure.

1 defect found:
- text error (medium). Dreyer's printed text specifies the remaining sum as '5 taleros' (arithmetically consistent: 10 joachimsthalers, half paid = 5, 5 owed); our house Latin the transcribed original silently drops the numeral '5', and the published English inherits the omission, losing a specific sum that a citation-grade edition should preserve. Dreyer prints: Pepigi cum dicto auriga pro decem Ioachimicis, dimidium illi hîc solvi, reliquos 5 taleros eum a te accepturum promisi. (TBOO VIII p.146).

Repaired. Collated against the Dreyer page images for TBOO VIII pp.144-148; the disputed reading falls on p.146, examined directly and confirmed to read "reliquos 5 taleros". Both the Latin apparatus and the English translation, which had silently inherited the same omission, are now corrected. No remaining uncertainty.
