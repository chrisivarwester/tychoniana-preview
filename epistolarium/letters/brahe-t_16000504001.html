The original

ILLUSTRISSIMO ET GENEROSISSIMO DOMINO DOMINO COSMO CONCINO E COMITIBUS PENNÆ ETC. SERENISSIMI MAGNI DUCIS HETRURIÆ APUD SACRAM CÆSAREAM MAIESTATEM ORATORI DIGNISSIMO DOMINO OBSERVANDISSIMO.

ILLUSTRISSIME ET GENEROSISSIME DOMINE.

SIQUIDEM ab Illma Dominatione Vestra, cum nuper apud ipsam Pragæ essem, intellexerim negotium de mittendo ad Sermum Magnum Ducem Hetruriæ etc. filio meo, in hoc tantummodo consistere, an is catholicam Romanam Religionem amplectetur, nec ne; neque enim Sermam eius Celsitudnem libenter quempiam in sua Aula habere, qui a dicta Religione alienus sit. Ad hoc equidem, quod respondeam nihil aliud habeo, quam me de eo polliceri posse: Si Sermus Magnus Dux eum in suam Aulam recipere dignatus fuerit, quod se ita gesturus sit, ut neminem offendat, aut scandalizet, sive quoad religionem sive alijs in rebus, quemadmodum Nobilem Iuvenem FRANCISCUM TENGNAGLIUM, qui aliquot annis in mea familia fuit, se ita, dum Sermæ Celsitni eius inserviret, exhibuisse confido, ut nemo isthic habeat, in quo iure de eo conqueri possit. Quod et pariter præstitit, dum in Illmi et Rmi Cardinalis a DIETRICHSTEIN Aula aliquantisper moratus est. Si vero Sermæ ipsius Celsitni hæc de religione quæstio aliquid nihilominus scrupuli moverit, posset filius meus unâ cum altero, quem illi adiuncturus sum, studioso, in ipsius Sermæ Celsitnis Academia, quæ Senis est, inter reliquos Germanos aliquantisper degere, ut studiosum illum nonnunquam in observationibus Astronomicis, iuxta meum præscriptum, isthic peragendis adiuvet; oportet enim ad minimum duos esse, interdum etiam tres, qui eo instrumento utantur, quod ego illis traditurus sum. Confectis autem isthic observationibus præcipuis, navigabit studiosus ille in Ægyptum, et facile ibi alios inveniet, qui instructi in pluribus obtinendis ibi præsto sint; quod propositum ipsius Sernam Celnem, quemadmodum clementissime promisit, benignissime promoturam mihi persuadeo. Interim filius manebit isthic in Italia, donec linguam calluerit, quod intra annui temporis spacium fieri posse existimo, ut tunc reversus Sac. Cæsæ Maiestati Domino meo clementissmo in ipsius Maiestatis Aula aliquandiu humillime serviat.

Hæc ad Illam Dominatem referenda habui, et qua possum reverentia Illam Dominationem Vestram rogatam habeo, ut a Sermo suo Principe resolutionem certam, quid hac in re sperare possim, vel secus, tempestive mihi impetrare non degravetur; ego vicissim omnia mea officiola ipsius Sermæ Celsitni et Vestræ quoque Illmæ Dominationi submisse defero.

Valeat Illma Vestra Dominatio diu et feliciter.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsarea Benatica, 4 Maij Anni 1600.
Illmæ Dominationi Vestræ addictissimus
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria scripsi.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most illustrious and most generous Lord, Lord Cosimo Concini, of the Counts of Penna, etc., most worthy Ambassador of the Most Serene Grand Duke of Tuscany to the Sacred Imperial Majesty, a Lord most to be respected.

Most illustrious and most generous Lord.

Since I have understood from Your Most Illustrious Lordship, when I was lately with you at Prague, that the business of sending my son to the Most Serene Grand Duke of Tuscany, etc., turns on this alone — whether he will embrace the Catholic Roman Religion, or not (for His Most Serene Highness does not willingly have anyone in his Court who is averse to the said Religion) — to this, indeed, I have nothing else to answer than that I can promise concerning him as follows: that, if the Most Serene Grand Duke shall deign to receive him into his Court, he will so conduct himself as to offend or scandalize no one, whether as to religion or in other matters — just as I trust that the noble young man Franz Tengnagel, who was for some years in my household, so conducted himself while he served His Most Serene Highness that no one there has anything for which he could justly complain of him. The like he performed, too, while he stayed a while in the Court of the most illustrious and most reverend Cardinal von Dietrichstein. But if this question of religion should nonetheless raise any scruple for His Most Serene Highness, my son could — together with another student whom I shall attach to him — dwell for a while in His Most Serene Highness's Academy, which is at Siena, among the other Germans, so that he might from time to time assist that student in carrying out there the Astronomical observations, according to my prescription; for there must be at least two, sometimes even three, to use that instrument which I shall hand over to them. And when the principal observations have been completed there, that student will sail to Egypt, and will readily find others there, [already] instructed, ready at hand for the obtaining of more; which design His Most Serene Highness, as he most graciously promised, I am persuaded will most kindly further. Meanwhile my son will remain there in Italy until he has mastered the language, which I think can be done within the space of a year, so that, returning then, he may for some time most humbly serve the Sacred Imperial Majesty, my most clement Lord, in His Majesty's Court.

These things I had to refer to Your Lordship; and with what reverence I can, I ask Your Most Illustrious Lordship not to find it burdensome to obtain for me, in good time, from your Most Serene Prince a sure resolution as to what I may hope in this matter, or otherwise. I, in turn, humbly tender all my poor services to His Most Serene Highness and also to Your Most Illustrious Lordship.

May Your Most Illustrious Lordship fare long and happily.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 4th of May, in the year 1600.
Your Most Illustrious Lordship's most devoted,
TYCHO BRAHE,
I wrote [this] with my own hand.

Apparatus

  • Significance & companion letter. This is the twin of Tycho's celebrated letter to Galileo (brahe-t_16000504002), written the same day, from the same place (Benátky, 4 May 1600 [N.S.]). It is the business letter to the Tuscan ambassador that lies behind the Galileo letter's opening: there Tycho recalls meeting, at Prague, "the Ambassador of the Grand Duke of Tuscany … Cosimo Concini," whose praise of Galileo prompted the approach. The two letters together document Tycho's spring-1600 dealings with the Medici court. Survives as a copy (afscrift) in the Medici archive at Florence.
  • Recipient. Cosimo Concini, e Comitibus Pennae (of the Counts of Penna), ambassador (Orator) of the Most Serene Grand Duke of Tuscany — Ferdinando I de' Medici — at the imperial court. (Not to be confused with the later Concino Concini, maréchal d'Ancre, in France.)
  • The matter — placing Tycho's son at the Medici court. Concini had told Tycho that the only obstacle to receiving one of Tycho's sons into the Grand Duke's service was confessional: the Grand Duke "does not willingly have anyone in his Court averse to the Catholic Roman Religion." Tycho — himself Lutheran — answers diplomatically: he will not promise conversion, but pledges that his son will give no offence or scandal, in religion or otherwise. (The son intended for Italy is left unnamed in the letter; the plan concerns one of Tycho's sons, probably Tycho Brahe the Younger — named cautiously, not asserted from the text.)
  • The precedent of Tengnagel. Tycho cites Franz Tengnagel (Franciscus Tengnaglius) — his assistant, soon his son-in-law — who had served the Grand Duke of Tuscany and stayed at the court of Cardinal Franz von Dietrichstein (1570–1636, Bishop of Olomouc, made cardinal 1599) "so that no one could justly complain of him." Tengnagel is the same intermediary whose return from Italy frames the Galileo letter; his Italian sojourn is detailed in brahe-t_16000306002.
  • The fallback plan — Siena and the Egypt expedition (astronomical interest). Should the religious scruple persist, Tycho proposes lodging his son at the University/Academy of Siena "among the other Germans" (the natio Germanica), where he would assist a student-observer sent by Tycho. Tycho stresses the working requirement — "at least two, sometimes three" observers are needed to operate the instrument he will provide (the edition's convention, organum/instrumentum). Most striking is the design that, once the principal Siena observations are done, the student "will sail to Egypt" to make further observations at southern latitudes and recruit local helpers — a concrete glimpse of Tycho's late ambition to extend his observational programme into the southern sky, which he expected the Grand Duke to fund. The son would meanwhile learn Italian (≈ a year) and then return to serve Rudolf II at the imperial court.
  • Persons named. Cosimo Concini (Tuscan ambassador); the Serenissimus Magnus Dux Hetruriae = Ferdinando I de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany; Tycho's son (unnamed); Franz Tengnagel; Cardinal Franz von Dietrichstein; Sacra Caesarea Maiestas = Rudolf II.
  • Place. Arx Caesarea Benatica = the imperial castle of Benátky nad Jizerou.
  • Epistolary formulae. Address in the high diplomatic register (Illustrissima Dominatio Vestra, "Your Most Illustrious Lordship"; Serenissima Celsitudo, "His Most Serene Highness"); subscription addictissimus → "most devoted"; manu propria scripsi → "I wrote with my own hand" (autograph attestation, though the surviving witness is a chancery copy). Heavy abbreviation (Illma, Serma, Celsitni, Rmi) silently expanded; the source block keeps the contractions.
  • Calendar. Dateline 4 Maij Anni 1600 at Benátky; the edition marks N.S. (New Style) — apt for Tycho's imperial-service dating and for correspondence with Catholic Italy. Reproduced, not converted.
