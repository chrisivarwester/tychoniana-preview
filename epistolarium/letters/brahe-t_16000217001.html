The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD TACHONEM THOTT].

SP. Id equidem erat, quod soror mihi intima, mater tua, charissime nepos, ad me die 24 octobris superioris anni, cum Bredenbergæ in Holsatia esset, perscripsit, missis in Daniam ad filium meum Tychonem literis, qui ibi tunc pro auferendis residuis meis jnstrumentis reliquaque supellectile aliquamdiu morabatur et sub initia huius anni, re omni ex voto peracta, huc ad me in Bohemiam reversus est: te nimirum, quamprimum ad quietas sedes in illa tua peregrinatione pervenires, de loco, ubi subsisteres, deque rerum tuarum conditionibus, mihi scripturum, quod te nunc ex matris voluntate atque constantia, a qua te non degeneraturum mihi polliceor, effecisse, perlubenter video. Placet, quod Heidelbergæ ob hyemis asperitatem substiteris, donec vere futuro Basilæam petas. Quamprimum vero de tempore, quo isthuc tibi proficiscendum erit, me edocueris (quod ut mature facias, velim), mittam tibi literas, quibus clarissimo viro d. d. Johanni Jacobo Grynæo te diligenter commendabo. Nec dubito, quin is vel mea de caussa res tuas sibi curæ esse sinat. Est enim is, licet de facie utrinque ignoti simus, mei amantissimus, et bis vel ter, postquam Dania excessi, cultissimas et officij ac humanitatis plenas ad me dedit literas. Quibus etiam respondi, licet ultimæ meæ, quas Vitebergæ nonnihil hærens ad ipsum per tabellarium Danicum Escillum, quem nosti, direxi, mihi ab eodem reportatæ sint, quod ipsum domo abfuisse caussaretur. At si nulla commoditas, antequam Heidelberga abieris, mihi denuo scribendi concessa fuerit, fiat id saltem, ubi Basilæam perveneris; ibique mox dictum illum ecc:mum Grynæum accedens ipsum ex me officiose saluta. Nec dubito, quin, ubi intellexerit, te meum ex sorore nepotem esse mihique charum, te et res tuas ad optima quæque suo consilio (a quo etiam te deflecti nolim) sit promoturus. Ante omnia vero pietati, virtuti et bonis literis strenue operam nava, ut cultior in patriam revertaris, et quam maxime tibi a pravo cave sodalitio, quod multos perdidit; et præceptori tuo recta monenti lubens auschulta. Coetera Deus providebit. Adjunctas ecc:mo illi Paulo Melisso, poetarum nostri seculi antesignano, una cum carminibus, dicta prius ex me salute, porrige, et si quid rescripserit, per oportunitatem mitte. Salutem etiam dic m. Jacobo Christmanno, isthic mathematico et Græcæ linguæ (uti opinor) professorj, simulque indica, Uraniam nostram, quæ me ex Dania huc intra saltus Bohemicos volens comitata est, sub auspiciis et clementissimo favore augustissimi imperatoris Rudolphi secundi hospicium benignum et commodum hic nactam esse; in quo nova suorum sacrorum ponat fundamina, prioribus illis apud Danos non cedentia, nec adeo (uti speramus) fastidita aut lubrica, ut vix repedationem sit subitura. Quod et Pythagoras in suis scite dictis inconsultum duxit. Si res tuæ sic tulerint, ut ante reditum in patriam has etiam oras perlustrare non invium fuerit, me hic invise. Neque enim citra rem fuerit, te aulam cæsaream, quæ Pragæ est, atque horum hominum mores spectare.

Vale cum præceptore tuo, quem etiam salvere iubeo, quam optime.
Dabantur properanter ex arce cæsareana Benatica, quinque milliaribus Praga remota, quam nunc indulto jmperatoris cum meis tranquille inhabito, anno 1600 die 17 februarij stilj renovati.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[TYCHO BRAHE TO TAGE THOTT].

Heartiest greeting. This indeed was what my most intimate sister, your mother, dearest nephew, wrote to me on the 24th of October of last year, when she was at Bredenberg in Holstein, in a letter sent into Denmark to my son Tycho — who was then staying there a while to bring away my remaining instruments and the rest of my furniture, and at the beginning of this year, every matter accomplished according to my wish, returned hither to me in Bohemia: namely, that you, as soon as you should reach quiet quarters in that peregrination of yours, would write to me about the place where you were lodging, and about the conditions of your affairs — which I see most gladly you have now done, in accordance with your mother's will and constancy, from which I promise myself you will not degenerate. I am pleased that you have halted at Heidelberg on account of the harshness of winter, until in the coming spring you make for Basel. As soon as you inform me of the time when you must set out thither — which I should wish you to do in good time — I will send you a letter by which I will diligently commend you to the most renowned man Lord Doctor Johann Jacob Grynaeus. Nor do I doubt that he, even for my sake, will make your affairs his care. For he, though we are unknown to each other by face, is most loving of me, and two or three times since I left Denmark has sent me most cultivated letters, full of courtesy and humanity; to which I also replied — although my last, which (lingering a while at Wittenberg) I sent him by the Danish courier Eskil, whom you know, was brought back to me by the same, because he pleaded that Grynaeus had been away from home. But if no convenience of writing to me again be granted before you leave Heidelberg, let it at least be done when you reach Basel; and there, approaching that most excellent Grynaeus soon, greet him dutifully on my behalf. Nor do I doubt that, when he understands you are my nephew by my sister and dear to me, he will advance you and your affairs to every best thing by his counsel — from which I should not wish you turned aside. But above all, devote yourself strenuously to piety, virtue, and good letters, that you may return more cultivated to your fatherland; and beware as much as you can of bad company, which has ruined many; and gladly hearken to your tutor when he advises rightly. The rest God will provide. The enclosed [letter], together with the poems, hand to that most excellent Paul Melissus, standard-bearer of the poets of our age, having first given a greeting from me; and if he write anything back, send it at an opportunity. Say a greeting also to Master Jacob Christmann, the mathematician there and (as I think) professor of the Greek tongue; and at the same time indicate that our Urania, who willingly accompanied me from Denmark hither within the Bohemian glades, has, under the auspices and most clement favour of the most august Emperor Rudolf II, found here a kindly and convenient lodging, in which she may lay new foundations of her sacred rites, not inferior to those former ones among the Danes — nor (as we hope) so disdained, or so slippery, that she would scarcely undergo a retreat; which Pythagoras too, in his shrewd sayings, deemed ill-advised. If your affairs so allow that, before your return to the fatherland, it be not out of your way to traverse these regions too, visit me here; for it would not be without profit for you to behold the imperial court, which is at Prague, and the manners of these people.

Farewell, with your tutor — whom I also bid be greeted — as well as may be.
Given in haste from the imperial castle of Benátky, five miles distant from Prague, which I now by the Emperor's indulgence peacefully inhabit with my household; the year 1600, the 17th day of February, of the renewed style.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Tage Ottesen Thott (1580–1658), Tycho's nephew — "my nephew by my sister." His own letter to Tycho from Heidelberg, 15 January 1600 (brahe-t_16000115001), survives. He was then on his peregrinatio academica (study-tour), wintering at Heidelberg with his travelling-companion Niels Mathiesen Bachendorph before going on to Basel.
  • His mother = Sophie Brahe. Soror mihi intima, mater tua — Tycho's "most intimate sister," who wrote from Bredenberg in Holstein (24 Oct 1599), is Sophie Brahe (1559–1643), Tycho's learned astronomer-sister; Tage was her son by her first husband, Otte Thott of Eriksholm. The identification is fixed by the companion letter (brahe-t_16000115001), where the mother "met Erik Lange at Brunswick and made the journey into Holstein" — Erik Lange being Sophie's second husband (the alchemist).
  • The commendation to Basel. Tycho promises a letter of recommendation to Johann Jacob Grynaeus (1540–1617), the Basel theologian (rector of the university; cf. brahe-t_15941023001), with whom Tycho corresponded though they never met; the Danish courier Eskil had carried Tycho's last letter to Grynaeus (returned, Grynaeus being away). Tycho's avuncular counsel: piety, virtue, good letters; beware bad company; heed the tutor.
  • The Heidelberg contacts.
  • Paul Melissus (Schede), "standard-bearer of the poets of our age" — Tage is to deliver an enclosed letter and poems to him (the recurring Melissus thread, cf. brahe-t_16000305001, brahe-t_16000329004).
  • Jacob Christmann (1554–1613), Heidelberg orientalist and astronomer. Tycho calls him "professor of the Greek tongue (as I think)" — hedged, and in fact slightly off: Christmann was professor of Hebrew, then the first professor of Arabic at Heidelberg. The hedge (uti opinor) is preserved, the correction noted here (no silent emendation).
  • The Urania passage (the letter's eloquent heart). Tycho bids Tage tell Christmann that "our Urania" — the Muse of astronomy — "who willingly accompanied me from Denmark hither within the Bohemian glades," has under Rudolf II's favour found "a kindly and convenient lodging, in which she may lay new foundations of her sacred rites, not inferior to those former ones among the Danes" — and (Tycho hopes) not so "disdained or slippery" that she must again retreat (repedatio), "which Pythagoras too, in his shrewd sayings, deemed ill-advised" (the Pythagorean precept against turning back / retracing one's steps). A poignant figure for the transplantation of his astronomy from Hven to Bohemia.
  • Persons named. Tage Thott; Sophie Brahe (his mother, Tycho's sister); Tycho's son (Tycho the Younger); Johann Jacob Grynaeus; the courier Eskil; Paul Melissus; Jacob Christmann; Rudolf II; Pythagoras.
  • Calendar. Dateline die 17 Februarij stylo renovato anni 1600 — New Style (imperial); the note quinque milliaribus Praga remota fixes Benátky "five miles from Prague." Header agrees with the label (the recipient's own letter, by contrast, is O.S. Heidelberg — the gap is preserved across the pair).
