The original

CLARISSIMO ET ERUDITISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO HUGONI BLOTIO, I. V. DOCTORI ET CÆSAREAe MAIESTATIS BIBLIOTHECAe, QUAe VIENNAe IN AUSTRIA EST, PRÆFECTO, AMICO SUO PLURIMUM DILECTO.
VIENNAe AUSTRIAe POST DOMUM PROVINCIALEM.

REDDITAe sunt mihi his ipsis diebus tuae literae unâ cum Hagecianis, eximia quadam humanitate et erga me benevolentia affatim refertae. Asseris inter nos olim in Helvetiae oris notitiam aliquam contractam fuisse. Quae sane ut id, quod res est, affirmem, mihi iam penitus memoriâ exciderat. Idque cum ob ea, quae ex temporum et locorum longinquitate ipsemet adducis obstacula, tum quod diversissimis et quamplurimis, ex eo tempore (quod sane ad minimum viginti duos annos excedit) occupationibus et distractionibus implicitus, eorum omnium, cum quibus in Germania atque alibi inter peregrinandum familiaritatem aliquam contraxi, recordationem animo comprehendere, nisi forte per literas subinde renovaretur, haud sustinuerim. Videor tamen mihi cum quodam Iuvene Belga, ingenioso atque ad discendum alacri, Basileae (uti opinor) non Argentinae (prout tu vis) dum pauculis mensibus illic subsisto, consuetudinem aliquando inijsse; succurritque, iam me ibidem per otium puerilia quaedam Instrumenta Mathematica (cum alia in promptu non essent) tractasse et harum rerum cupidis eorum usum ostendisse, ac si quae superessent, illis non gravatim dono concessisse. Fieri itaque facile poterit, quod tu ipsus Belga fueris, cum quo inter alios de his iucunde egi. Pergratum vero mihi accidit, quod hanc, quae nobis tunc aliquantulum intercessit, cognitionem, utut tot iam interlapsis annis ferme obliteratam, per literas in mentem revocare non intermiseris. Ego hanc in posterum memoriâ solidius firmare et sartam tectam conservare haud negligam. Nobilem etiam et rerum usu eximie valentem virum GERARDUM RANZOVIUM, HENRICI filium, amicum meum fratris loco dilectum et non ita dudum affinitate coniunctum, tuo nomine salutare atque de notitia inter nos olim contracta commonefacere, prout expetis, non tergiversabor. Quin et Magnificentissimo Amplissimoque viro Domino HENRICO RANZOVIO Produci Holsatiae prima quaque oblata occasione scribens (quod et saepius mutuo facere solemus, cum et ille ijsdem, quibus invigilo, studijs impense afficiatur) vel ipsas tuas literas vel earum descriptum adiungam, ut filio mittens, tui recordationem, tam apud ipsum quam caeteros Holsatiae conspicuae nobllitatis viros, tibi quondam in adolescentia notos, sic promptius in memoriam revocem. Quod et apud ipsum GERARDUM, si illi prius quam Parenti eius scripserim, praestabo. Atque is rectius reliquos suos, de quibus loqueris, consangvineos novit, eosque tui memores reddet. Sunt enim, tum is tum etiam alij Holsatiae Nobilitatis eximij viri, praesertim ex ijs, qui exteras nationes perlustrando variorum hominum mores et actiones experti sunt, ab humanitatis officijs nequaquam alieni, ut non dubitem, ijs hanc vestrae conversationis memorationem acceptam futuram.

Scribis in Bibliotheca Imperatoria, cui Viennae praees, reservari ultra 90000 librorum volumina. Si itaque liceret et tibi nonnunquam otium suppeteret, optarem mihi Authorum illorum nomina, unâ cum librorum singulorum inscriptione, praesertim quo ad Philosophica, Mathematica, Astronomica, Medica et Chymica atque similia opera attinet. Idem quoque in Illustriss. Archiducis Austriae MAXIMILIANI atque aliorum, si quos nosti, Illustrium virorum Bibliothecis fieri averem; modo id bona eorum pace admittatur. Sin minus, nolo tibi periculum vel offensas mea de causa creari.

Quia vero Arcis Uraniburgicae, quam in hac Insula praecedentibus annis extrui curavi, mentionem facis, eius aliqualem repraesentationem tibi in adiuncta charta mitto. Licet ab eo tempore, quo haec ita in eum modum excisa sunt, nonnulla, praesertim quo ad vallum circundans et aedificia in eius quatuor angulis extantia, immutata et ampliora reddita sint, et Instrumenta Astronomica plura, tam in Arcis turriculis quam potissimum in Cryptis quibusdam ad collem extra huius moenia, e solidis lapidibus constructis, quae septenas diversi generis ingentes et affabre elaboratas ad Astronomiae negotium rite exequendum inservientes distinctim complectuntur Machinas, hincinde disposita existunt, adeo ut iam coniunctis ijs, quae in Arce servantur, mihi 28 ad minimum in promptu sint Astronomicae Restitutioni opportuna Organa, solertia, labore, sumptibus inaestimabilia. Quin et, ut aliquatenus etiamnum cognoscas, quid praeteritis annis his intermediantibus executus sim, quidque in re Astronomica redintegranda moliar, en habes Operum nostrorum, brevi, favente Coelestium Opifice, in lucem publicam evulgandorum quaternionem primum, eorundem Inscriptionem atque contenta brevibus exponentem. Addidi autem consulto terna eiusdem quaternionis exempla, ut si qui forte fuerint inter vestrates Bibliopolas aut etiam in vicinia, qui horum voluminum aliquot exemplaria iusto pretio obtinere cupiant, illis hanc librorum inscriptionem communicare atque cum ijs de numero Exemplarium, quae adipisci satagunt, deque pretio transigere, eo commodius queas. Quod et te meo nomine haud gravatim facturum confido. Erunt quatuor Tomi, quorum quilibet semiquinque circiter adimplebit Alphabeta, in hac Regali (quam vocant) uti vides, papyro. Suntque plurimis schematibus, numeris et Instrumentorum picturis hincinde referti. Tute igitur, qui Augustae illi Caesareae Maiestatis Bibliothecae negotijs praees, et rem librariam optime calles, facile aestimabis, quantis sumptibus haec in propria meâ, eâdem de caussa constructa Typographia, excudi curârim et quali pretio cum indemnitate nostrorum laborum atque impensarum venire merito queant. Quae omnia tuae prudentiae dispicienda, dijudicanda, exequendaque relinquo. Praestiteris mihi hac ratione rem imprimis gratam et Reipublicae literariae, cuius tu commodum gnaviter et honorifice procuras, haud, ut spero, inutilem, nec tibi ipsi indecoram aut sterilem. Ego enim ad vicissim tibi gratificandum et de te bene merendum promptus inveniar. Carmina, quae altera habet pagina, non semper illic locum invenient, sed ubi digniora quaedam a Principibus et illustrioribus viris, quae expecto, adeptus fuero, illa honoris caussa hanc primi Tomi pagellam occupabunt; caeteris in reliquos Tomos, prout cuiuslibet Autoris requirit status et existimatio, iuxta frontispicia debito loco digestis. Si Poëta es (uti procul dubio es) et te nostram Uraniam aut effigiem (cuius ideam praeter illam, quae quaternioni impressa est, aliam cupro subtilius expressam hîc adiunxi) succincto aliquo Epigrammate ornare, idemque apud alios, si quos nosti, in Austria argutos et acutos Poëtas, tua operâ impetrari, desiderarem. Vale. Plura enim non addam, ne nimium excrescat Epistola; tu prima quaque oblata occasione rescribendo saepiusque (quod et ego non intermittam) scribendo, amicitiam veterem renovare, augere, stabilireque ne supersede. Literas, si Pragam ad praestantissimum virum D. THADDÆUM HAGGÆCIUM miseris, is, cum mihi saepius scribere soleat, et tuas huc tuto diriget.

Iamque iterum atque iterum vale.
Datae Uraniburgi 20. Martij Anno 92.
TYCHO BRAHE,
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most distinguished and most learned man, Master Hugo Blotius, Doctor of both Laws and Prefect of the Library of His Imperial Majesty which is at Vienna in Austria, his much-beloved friend.
At Vienna in Austria, beyond the Provincial House.

Your letter was delivered to me these very days, together with the ones from Hagecius, abundantly filled with a certain singular kindness and goodwill toward me. You assert that an acquaintance was once struck up between us on the shores of Switzerland. Which — that I may affirm the matter as it stands — had by now slipped entirely from my memory. And that both on account of those obstacles which you yourself adduce from the remoteness of times and places, and because, entangled since that time (which assuredly exceeds at least twenty-two years) in the most various and very many occupations and distractions, I have hardly been able to hold in mind the recollection of all those with whom, while travelling, I struck up any familiarity in Germany and elsewhere — unless perhaps it were now and again renewed by letters. Yet it seems to me that I did once enter into an acquaintance with a certain Belgian youth, ingenious and eager to learn, at Basel (as I suppose), not at Strasbourg (as you would have it), during the few months I stayed there; and it comes back to me that there too, in my leisure, I handled certain childish Mathematical Instruments (since others were not at hand), and showed those eager for such things their use, and, if any were left over, freely granted them to those men as a gift. It could therefore easily be that you were that very Belgian with whom, among others, I dealt pleasantly on these matters. It has indeed been most welcome to me that you have not failed to call back to mind by letter this acquaintance, which then passed somewhat between us, even though now, with so many years intervening, it is all but obliterated. For my part I shall not neglect henceforth to fix it more firmly in memory and to keep it whole and sound. Nor shall I shrink — as you request — from greeting in your name the noble man, eminently versed in affairs, Gerhard Rantzau, son of Henrik, my friend dear to me in a brother's place and not long ago joined to me by marriage-alliance, and from reminding him of the acquaintance once struck up between us. Indeed, writing at the first occasion offered to the most magnificent and most ample man, Master Henrik Rantzau, Governor [Produx] of Holstein (which we are often wont to do for one another, since he too is keenly devoted to the same studies on which I keep watch), I shall enclose either your letter itself or a copy of it, so that, sending it to the son, I may thus the more readily call your memory to mind, both with him and with the other men of conspicuous nobility of Holstein, known to you once in your youth. This I shall also do with Gerhard himself, if I write to him before his Parent. And he knows better than I those other kinsmen of his, of whom you speak, and will make them mindful of you. For both he and likewise other eminent men of the Holstein nobility — especially among those who, by surveying foreign nations, have gained experience of the manners and doings of various men — are by no means estranged from the offices of courtesy, so that I do not doubt this commemoration of your sometime intercourse will be acceptable to them.

You write that in the Imperial Library, over which you preside at Vienna, more than 90,000 volumes of books are kept. If therefore it were permitted, and leisure now and then were available to you, I could wish to have for myself the names of those Authors, together with the title of each book, especially as concerns Philosophical, Mathematical, Astronomical, Medical, and Chemical works, and the like. The same I should also be eager to have done in the libraries of the most illustrious Archduke of Austria Maximilian and of others, if you know any, of illustrious men — provided this be allowed with their good leave. But if not, I do not wish danger or offence to be created for you on my account.

But since you make mention of the castle of Uraniborg, which in past years I had built on this island, I send you some representation of it on the enclosed sheet. Granted that, since the time when these things were so engraved in that fashion, certain features — especially as concerns the surrounding rampart and the buildings standing at its four corners — have been altered and made more ample, and several Astronomical Instruments, disposed here and there both in the little towers of the castle and chiefly in certain crypts toward a hill outside its walls, built of solid stone, which severally comprise seven huge Machines of different kinds, skilfully wrought, serving for the due carrying-out of the business of Astronomy — so much so that now, joining to them those that are kept in the castle, I have at least 28 instruments ready to hand, fit for the Restoration of Astronomy, beyond price in skill, labour, and expense. Indeed, that you may also even now somewhat learn what I have accomplished in these intervening past years, and what I am attempting in the renewal of the science of Astronomy — behold, you have here the first quaternion of our Works, shortly, by the favour of the Maker of the heavenly bodies, to be published abroad, setting forth in brief the Title and contents of the same. I have, moreover, deliberately added three copies of that same quaternion, so that, if there should perchance be any among the booksellers of your parts, or even in the neighbourhood, who desire to obtain some copies of these volumes at a fair price, you may the more conveniently communicate to them this title of the books, and transact with them about the number of Copies they are anxious to procure, and about the price. And this I trust you will do in my name without reluctance. There will be four Volumes, each of which will fill about four-and-a-half [semiquinque] alphabets, in this so-called Royal paper, as you see. And they are stuffed here and there with very many diagrams, figures, and pictures of Instruments. You therefore, who preside over the affairs of that august Library of His Imperial Majesty, and are most expert in the book-trade, will easily reckon at what great expense I have had these printed in my own Printing-house, built for that same purpose, and at what price they may deservedly be sold, with indemnity for our labours and outlays. All of which I leave to your prudence to consider, judge, and carry out. By this means you will render me a thing especially welcome, and one not, as I hope, useless to the Republic of Letters, whose advantage you diligently and honourably procure, nor unbecoming or unfruitful to yourself. For I shall be found ready in turn to gratify you and to deserve well of you. The verses which the other page holds will not always find a place there, but when I shall have obtained certain worthier ones from Princes and more illustrious men, which I am expecting, those will, for honour's sake, occupy this little page of the first Volume; the rest being arranged in their due place in the remaining Volumes, according as each Author's standing and esteem requires, next to the frontispieces. If you are a Poet (as you doubtless are), I could wish that you adorn our Urania, or the portrait (an idea of which, besides the one impressed on the quaternion, I have here added, more finely rendered in copper), with some terse Epigram, and that the same be obtained through your effort from others — if you know any in Austria — clever and acute Poets. Farewell. For I shall add no more, lest the Letter swell too much; do not you forbear, by writing back at the first occasion offered and by writing often (which I too shall not neglect), to renew, augment, and stabilise our old friendship. As for letters, if you send them to Prague to that most excellent man Master Thaddaeus Hagecius, he — since he is wont to write to me often — will safely direct yours hither too.

And now again and again, farewell.
Given at Uraniborg, 20 March, in the year [15]92.
TYCHO BRAHE,
with his own hand.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe, writing from Uraniborg on Hven. The subscription manu propria ("with his own hand") and the autograph signature mark this as a personally signed letter.
  • Recipient. Hugo Blotius = Hugo Blotius (Hugo Blotz, 1533–1608), the Dutch humanist who from 1575 was the first formally-appointed custodian (Prefect) of the Imperial Court Library in Vienna. I. V. Doctori = Iuris Utriusque Doctori, "Doctor of both Laws" (civil and canon). The two had been acquainted in their student days at Basel, where Blotius was involved with Tycho in instrument-/quadrant-making — which is exactly the episode the letter half-remembers (Mosley, Bearing the Heavens; Christianson, On Tycho's Island; Thoren, Lord of Uraniborg).
  • Address line. VIENNAe AUSTRIAe POST DOMUM PROVINCIALEM — "At Vienna in Austria, beyond [behind] the Provincial House (Landhaus)," i.e. the postal/topographical direction of Blotius's residence relative to the Lower-Austrian Estates' building. Rendered literally; "Provincial House" = the Landhaus.
  • notitia in Helvetiae oris — the disputed first meeting "on the shores of Switzerland." Tycho's recollection is explicitly hedged: he says it had "slipped entirely from my memory," that he seems to himself (videor mihi) to have met a Belgian youth, and corrects the place — Basileae (uti opinor) non Argentinae (prout tu vis), "at Basel (as I suppose), not at Strasbourg (as you would have it)." Argentina = Strasbourg (Argentoratum). The disagreement between the two men over the place is preserved, not adjudicated; Tycho's qualifiers (uti opinor, Fieri facile poterit) are kept.
  • viginti duos annos — "at least twenty-two years." Tycho's own reckoning of the interval; counting back from 1592 places the meeting c. 1570, consistent with his second peregrination through Basel.
  • puerilia quaedam Instrumenta Mathematica — "certain childish [i.e. elementary, rudimentary] Mathematical Instruments." Puerilia is self-deprecating ("childish/boyish"), not a claim about the instruments' owners; kept literal. Instrumenta / Organa both rendered instrument(s) by this chapter's convention
  • Persons named.
  • GERARDUM RANZOVIUM, HENRICI filium = Gerhard (Gert) Rantzau (1558–1627), son of Heinrich; later himself Governor of Holstein. affinitate coniunctum ("joined by marriage-alliance") flags a recent kinship tie; rendered "joined to me by marriage-alliance."
  • HENRICO RANZOVIO Produci Holsatiae = Heinrich Rantzau (1526–1598/9), royal Governor (Statthalter) of the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein, the great bibliophile, astrologer, and patron — a frequent correspondent of Tycho. Produx (here in the ablative Produci) is a learned Latinism for the office of dux/governor; rendered "Governor [Produx]," the Latin term flagged because it is non-standard.
  • Illustriss. Archiducis Austriae MAXIMILIANI = Archduke Maximilian III of Austria (1558–1618, "the Deutschmeister"), Habsburg prince and book-collector.
  • D. THADDAeUM HAGGAeCIUM = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius, 1525–1600), the imperial physician and astronomer at Prague, Tycho's principal correspondent-relay in the Empire; the letter routes return mail through him. The "Hagecianis [literae]" of the opening line are letters that arrived from Hagecius in the same packet.
  • Astronomy / instruments. Astronomicae Restitutioni opportuna Organa = "instruments fit for the Restoration of Astronomy" — restitutio astronomiae, Tycho's programmatic phrase, kept as "Restoration of Astronomy"; likewise in re Astronomica redintegranda = "in the renewal of the science of Astronomy." The count 28 ad minimum … Organa and the septenas … Machinas (the seven great machines housed in the stone Cryptae — the underground vaults of Stjerneborg, "toward a hill outside [Uraniborg's] walls") are reproduced as written.
  • The engraving and the printed prospectus. aliqualem repraesentationem … in adiuncta charta — Tycho encloses a printed view of Uraniborg, noting it is now partly out of date. Operum nostrorum … quaternionem primum = "the first quaternion of our Works" — a printed sample gathering (one quaternio = a four-sheet gathering) advertising the planned four-volume edition; terna … exempla = three copies sent for circulation to booksellers. Regali … papyro = "Royal paper" (a large paper format), quam vocant "so-called" flagged as Tycho's own gloss. semiquinque … Alphabeta: each volume "fills about four-and-a-half alphabets," an alphabetum being a printer's count of gatherings signed through the 23-letter signature sequence; semiquinque ("four-and-a-half," lit. "five-minus-a-half") rendered literally and glossed. propria meâ … Typographia = Tycho's own printing-house on Hven.
  • Carmina … nostram Uraniam aut effigiem … Epigrammate — Tycho solicits a complimentary epigram (verse) from Blotius for the frontispiece, to honour his Urania (the figure/Muse, here standing for the work and the press's emblem) or his portrait, of which a finer copper engraving is also enclosed. The request for verse is kept as a request for verse, by the audit's rule.
  • Reipublicae literariae — the Republic of Letters, the international scholarly commonwealth; rendered with the standing English term.
  • Epistolary formulae. Heading …AMICO SUO PLURIMUM DILECTO = "his much-beloved friend." Vale … Iamque iterum atque iterum vale = "Farewell … And now again and again, farewell." manu propria = "with his own hand."
  • Calendar / dateline. Datae Uraniburgi 20. Martij Anno 92 = "Given at Uraniborg, 20 March, year [15]92" — Old Style (Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700; this chapter's convention). The edition label ("1592. 20. marts. Uraniborg") agrees with the dateline; no discrepancy. The N.S. equivalent would be 30 March 1592, but the letter is not so marked and the date is left as written.
  • §1 Dreyer collation: Dreyer (Opera Omnia VII, p. 329) reads ultra 90000 librorum volumina — 90,000 volumes of the Imperial Library, corroborated by Mosley, Bearing the Heavens, p. 184. The working transcription's 9000 had dropped a digit; corrected in the Source block and translation.
  • Correction. English translation: 'quires' corrected to 'alphabets' — the Latin Alphabeta (confirmed against Dreyer, TBOO VII p.329-330) is the period printing-trade unit for a complete run of signatures, not a single small quire; the old rendering understated the bulk of the four tomes.

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Collated the whole letter — salutation, every proper name (Blotius, Gerhard/Henrik Rantzau, Archduke Maximilian, Hagecius), every number (90,000 library volumes, 28 instruments, seven crypt machines, four tomes), and the closing/date/signature — against Dreyer TBOO VII pp.328-330 (p.327 also read to confirm the letter boundary). One mistranslation found and confirmed against Dreyer's printed Latin: 'Alphabeta' rendered as 'quires', materially misstating the book-trade sizing unit Tycho uses for the forthcoming volumes.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). Alphabetum is the period printing-trade unit for a full run of signatures (roughly two dozen gatherings), not a quire (one small gathering); rendering it as 'quires' badly understates the physical bulk of the four tomes Tycho is describing to the Imperial Librarian in order to help him arrange sales — exactly the kind of bibliographic detail a scholar or cataloguer would rely on. At: “each of which will fill about four-and-a-half [semiquinque] quires”.

Repaired. Verified against the Dreyer page image TBOO VII p.329-330, which reads 'Erunt quatuor Tomi, quorum quilibet femiquinque circiter adimplebit Alphabeta, in hac Regali (quam vocant) uti vides, papyro' — matching the transcribed original verbatim, so no Latin change was needed. Only the English mistranslation of 'Alphabeta' as 'quires' required correction. The remainder of the letter was not re-collated beyond the single defect site per the prime rule.
