The original

DEM GESTRENGEN, EEDLEN VND ERNUESTEN JUNCKHEREN TYCHONJ BRAHE, ERBGESESSEN VFF KNUDTSTORP, ASTRONOMIÆ RESTAURATORJ, DIENSTLEICH ZUHANDEN. PRAGE ODER IN DER JEGENT, NEMLICH ZU GERSITZ. A. 1600.

GOTTES gnade vnd friede zuuor. Gestrenger, edler, ernuester, großgunstiger junckher Tycho, benebenst ehrerbietung meiner gantzwilligen dienste magk ich Ew. Gestre. nicht vorhalten, das ych zwar von hern magistro Dauide Fabricii itzo 2 schreiben verhanden, die er mir gantz hoch vnd tewer per gantz gewiße bodtschafft euch zusendendt begeret hat. So ist warleich in all die zeit keiner (der directe nach Prag wolte) mir furkomen, wowol mir vnter andern auch von hern doctor Vito Winßhemio vortrostung gethan. Habe derentwegen discrete vnd diuise (ich meine vff Leiptzigh vnd dernach vf Dreßdinn zu vbergeben) E. G. dieselbigen lieber zuordenen wollen als lenger dieselbige bej mir zubehalten, nemlich auff Leiptzigh, von dannen an h. Hans Tuchman nach Dreßdenn vnd alsovordt zu E. Gest. henden zukommen. Da dis allso nicht wol vordtginge, so haben Ewre Gestrengheit zubedencken, ob sie euch nicht beßer per Nurenberch durch hern Leonhard Dillher, keiserlichen factoren, müchten zur hand konnen kommen, so geschege die vorwexelung nur and einen vnd nit zu zween orteren, dauon Ewer Gestr. aduis zum nechst.

Was von der schiff für die jnstrumente zu hoffen, das werden Eur G. aus hern Vincentij Mollerj (der its vornjar burgemeister geworden is) seinem schreiben zuuornemen. Mich ducht gudt zu sein, wan keis. mst. ein klein schreiben derowegen an e. erb. radt von Hamhurgk abgehen liße. Das schreiben konte noch wol so bald hervnter kommen, alse die jnstrumente hir mugen anlangen.

Vor acht tagen hab ich briefe von Dauide Fabricio bekommen, darinnen ich sein gantz liebreiches gemut kegen Ew. Gestr. erspure, want ime felschleich (Godt lob) berichtet worden Euw. Gest. obitus. Er thut in seinem schreiben nicht anders alß wen ime sein liebester vatter oder son abgestorben were.

Dieweil er mir nunmer einen seiner schwege[r] vnd freunde Ludolfs Ridderhusen zu Bremen namkundich gemacht hat, habe ich daher nu gelegenheit, ime dieselbige wochentleich, so offt es nodt es, zusenden.

Damit ich inn in sodaner betrubnis nicht lange hielte, habe ich stracks volgendes tages ime vber Bremen geschrieben, vnd der sachen anders berichtet, nemlich das ich noch den 14 nouembris briefe von E. Gestr. vnderzeichnet empfangen hette, er solte es dafür halten, das das nicht wurde war sein, mit Gottes hülffe. Vnd kumpt mir frombt vor, das er der briefe keinen (die ich vor dieser seiner anordening zu waßer bej schyppen zu Norden vnd in die grete wonende gegeben habe) vberkommen hat. Die schiffere haben dennoch mir daßelbige veste gelobet. So kunte ich auch anders keine botten haben, dan an den ordt gelangen keine botten. Ich hoffe dennoch er wirdt sie teslich bekommen haben.

Vber Bremen is henfurt der gewiße pas, weils der Ludolff Ridderhusen es vff sich nimpt, dieselbig vordtzusenden.

Heut habe ich abermal E. Gest. brieff ime bej meinem eingeslag zugesant, das er abermall ewer wolstandes wirdt mügen confirmiret sein.

Jmgleichen auch an hern Josephum Scaligerum geschrieben vnd der Ew. Gest. schreiben darneben einen amsterdamisch botten selber in die handt gegeben vnd in dauon bezallt, jn meinem schreiben mir erbotten Ew. Gestr. gerne zu ubersenden all daßjennige, was mir geschicket wurd.

Was dem conditore nouarum ephemeridum Euerarto zu Bruxell wonende belanget, weis ich nicht woll, ob ich bej im was wurde ausrichten, darumme das die briefe von hinnen durch viendelandt vber Antorff gehen müssen. Vnd müchte man sich der vnkost trosten, so mangellt es mir doch an kundtschafft mit ime. Vnd tesleichen, die warheit zu bekennen, ist es mir gar aus dem sinne kommen. Nu ich aber vormercke, das E. Gest. damit ein will geschieht, so will ichs per tertium vnterlegen. Einer genomet Peter vp Meer, köningkleicher hispanischer radt vnd befellhaber, ist mir familiaris, durch denselbigen meine ich in mein schreiben beizubringen vnd andtwordt zuerhalten, so habe ich einen zu Antorff, genomet Johan tho Westen, bei demseluigen werden diesen brief zwischen Brüßell vnd Hamborch hinc inde eingethan, der soll das bottenloen mir vorlegen.

Des junckher swester Sophia is alhie zu Wandesbeke. Derselbigen habe ich vff ir beger vollgen lassen j kutswagen mit dem was von zugehorigen darbej, war; jtem noch j radt, welches er sunst mangelte. Daßelbige was dafur sollj will sie E. Gestr. gudt thun. Vorhoffe mich gentzleich daranne keinen vndanch begangen haben, das vbrige wagengerede is vast geringe vnd verdorben gut. Sie konte nicht mer dan ein gudt radt (zu den die bej erster kutsen weren) finden odder aussuchen laßen.

Aus Dennemarck habe ich nicht sunderlichs, habe auch zwar mit fleis darnach nicht erkundet. Hertzogh Carl hat numer die gantze swedische cron zu seinem willen. Der polnische kuningk haltet dieser sachen halber itzundt oder wirdts bald in Polen halten; was er vornemen wirdt, gibt zeit. Aus Muscouia zeitung, das kuningk Erich aus Sweden, sonn Gustaus, alda bej den großfürsten woll gehalten wirde. Wan es im an furmügen nicht mangelte, wolte er sich inbillden, auch zur krone Sweden zugehoren. Man vormeint aber, der muscowiter wirde mit hertzogk Carl oder mit Sweden den angestelleten friede nicht brechen. Engelant (wie man sagt) tractiret den friede mit denn hispanischen. Dy hollender slagen alle fridesmiddell aus. Einer der fornembste midt vnter denen von Amsterdam hat an einen guten fründt also geschrieben: Wan vnsern staten vnd regenten gegeben wurde eine gantze große oxßenhaut, darauff zuschreiben alle die conditiones pacis, die inen nur gelustede, vnd man inen alle daßelbige nachgeben wolte, so würden sie dennoch den frieden nicht begern. Sie die hollender haben zuuor ein placat (wie sie es nennen) lassen ausghen, darinne sie cauiere vnd idermennichlich steden, stenden vnd potentaten zu gebieten, sich vnterwinden, zu interdicerende die segelation auis Spanien mit getreide. Vnsere schiffe haben sie etlike in Hollant eingebracht, vnd es gestrigis tages beschedt bej den botten alhie angebracht worden, das eines derselbigen schiffe mit vrteil vnd rechte confiscieret worden sey. Die staten vntersthen sich mer hochheit alse wol menniger hoger potentat nicht thun dorfte.

Magr. Georgius Frobenius mus vff ostern Wandesbech reumen. Die frome stathalterinn hat es iren sone Breden wider abgehandelln, gibt fur, das sie selbest es gebrauchen will. Der vnkostung halber, so Ew Gestr. an gebeuen gethan, folget noch von Keile nen sunderlige erklerung. Will aber vmme das andtwordt bej Frobenio anhalten.

Die stadthaltersche schreibt mir, das ich mich erkleren müge, ob ich nicht weis, wannen her sie der 5000 thaler soll gewertich sein. Jch habe ir geandtwordet, das E. Gestr. das durch Esche Bilde zuuorrichtende angeordnet haben.

Jn eile we[i]s ich E. G. mer nicht zuschreiben, one alleine wunsche euch samptt euw. lieben vieltugentreichen husfrouen vnd tochteren allesamptt, wie auch den sonen, gesundtheit vnd wolfartt. Datum eilentz den 12 februarij a. 1600.

Auch is meine frundtleiche bitte, Ew. Gest. müchten meinen nepotj befolen mir auch zuschreiben vnd seinen progressum vnd zustant zuuormelden. Got gebe, das es ime auch wol ghehe. Ew. Gest. dienstwilliger

Simon von Petkum.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the stern, noble, and honourable Squire Tycho Brahe, hereditary [lord] of Knudstrup, Restorer of Astronomy, to be delivered into [his] hands at his service. At Prague, or in the region, namely at Görlitz. A[nno] 1600.

God's grace and peace first. Stern, noble, honourable, greatly-favouring Squire Tycho, alongside the offering of my wholly-willing services I cannot withhold from Your Honour that I have on hand 2 letters from Master David Fabricius, which he begged me, most earnestly and dearly, to send you by quite sure messenger. But truly, in all this time, none (who wished to go directly to Prague) has come to me — though among others Dr. Veit Winsheim too gave me some hope. I have therefore resolved, discreetly and in divided fashion (I mean, to hand them on via Leipzig and then via Dresden), to dispatch them to Your Honour rather than keep them longer with me — namely to Leipzig, thence to Mr. Hans Tuchman at Dresden, and so on into Your Honour's hands. Should this not go well, Your Honour should consider whether they might not better reach you via Nuremberg, through Mr. Leonhard Dillher, the imperial factor, so that the trans-shipment happen at one place and not two; of which, Your Honour's advice next.

What is to be hoped of the ship for the instruments, Your Honour will learn from the letter of Mr. Vincent Möller (who this spring became burgomaster). It seems to me good if the Imperial Majesty would let a small letter go out, on that account, to the honourable Council of Hamburg. The letter could well come down here as soon as the instruments may arrive here.

Eight days ago I got letters from David Fabricius, in which I perceive his wholly loving heart toward Your Honour; for he had been falsely (God be praised) informed of Your Honour's death. In his letter he behaves no otherwise than as if his dearest father or son had died.

Since he has now made known to me one of his brothers-in-law and friends, Ludolf Ridderhusen at Bremen, I now have the opportunity to send to him [Fabricius] the same weekly, as often as is needed.

That I might not hold him long in such grief, I wrote him the very next day via Bremen, and informed him of the matter otherwise — namely that I had received letters signed by Your Honour as late as 14 November; he should hold it that it [the death] will not be true, with God's help. And it seems strange to me that he got none of the letters (which, before this arrangement of his, I gave to water-going skippers living at Norden and in the Krummhörn [Grete]). The skippers nonetheless firmly promised me [delivery]; nor could I have other messengers, for no [land] messengers reach that place. Yet I hope he will have got them by now.

Via Bremen is henceforth the sure route, since Ludolf Ridderhusen takes it upon himself to forward them.

Today I have again sent Your Honour's letter to him [Fabricius] in my enclosure, so that he may again be confirmed of your good state.

Likewise I have written also to Mr. Joseph Scaliger, and besides gave Your Honour's letter into an Amsterdam messenger's own hand, and paid him for it; in my letter I offered to forward gladly to Your Honour everything that may be sent to me.

As for the maker of new ephemerides, Everart, dwelling at Brussels, I do not well know whether I should accomplish anything with him, because the letters from here must go through enemy-country [the war-zone], via Antwerp. And though one might console oneself about the cost, I yet lack acquaintance with him; and likewise, to confess the truth, it had quite slipped my mind. But now that I perceive Your Honour's will is to have it done, I will arrange it through a third party. One named Peter up Meer, royal Spanish councillor and commander, is familiar to me; through him I mean to convey my letter and obtain an answer; so I have one at Antwerp named Johan tho Westen, by whom these letters between Brussels and Hamburg will be put in, back and forth, [and] who shall advance me the messenger-fee.

The Squire's sister Sophia is here at Wandsbek. At her request I let her have 1 coach-wagon, with what belonged to it; item, one more wheel, which was otherwise lacking. What it should cost, she will make good to Your Honour. I hope entirely to have committed no ingratitude in this; the rest of the wagon-gear is very paltry and spoiled goods. She could find, or have sought out, no more than one good wheel (besides those that were with the first coach).

From Denmark I have nothing special, nor indeed have I diligently inquired. Duke Charles now has the whole Swedish crown to his will. The Polish King holds, or will soon hold, [a diet] in Poland on account of this matter; what he will undertake, time will tell. News from Muscovy, that King Erik of Sweden, son of Gustav, is well kept there by the Grand Duke [see note]; if he did not lack means, he would fancy himself to belong to the Swedish crown too. But it is thought the Muscovite will not break the arranged peace with Duke Charles or with Sweden. England (as they say) negotiates peace with the Spanish. The Dutch reject all means of peace. One of the foremost among those of Amsterdam wrote to a good friend thus: "If our States and regents were given a whole great ox-hide on which to write all the conditions of peace that pleased them, and all of it were granted them, they would still not want peace." The Dutch have previously issued a placard (as they call it), in which they presume to command and forbid all cities, estates, and potentates … to interdict the sailing from Spain with grain. Some of our ships they have brought into Holland; and yesterday news came by the messenger here that one of those ships has been confiscated by judgement and law. The States arrogate to themselves more sovereignty than many a high potentate might dare to do.

Master Georg Frobenius must vacate Wandsbek by Easter. The pious Governess [Statthalterin] has bargained it back from her son Breide [Rantzau], pretending she wishes to use it herself. As to the cost which Your Honour laid out on buildings, a special declaration follows yet from Kiel; but I will press Frobenius for the answer.

The Governess writes me that I should declare whether I do not know whence she is to expect the 5000 thaler. I have answered her that Your Honour arranged this to be discharged through Eske Bille.

In haste I know nothing more to write to Your Honour, except only [that] I wish you, together with your dear, very virtuous housewife and daughters all, as also the sons, health and welfare. Dated in haste, the 12th of February, A[nno] 1600.

Also it is my friendly request that Your Honour might charge my nephew to write to me too, and report his progress and condition. God grant it go well with him too. Your Honour's service-willing,
Simon von Petkum.

[Postscript:] So that the messenger-fee be not reckoned to Your Honour twice over, I give Your Honour to know that I pay [it] from here onward via Leipzig, Bremen, Leiden, etc.

Apparatus

  • Direction & correspondent. Incoming, Simon von Petkum — Tycho's agent/factor in the Hamburg–Wandsbek region, who managed his northern affairs after the move south — to Tycho at Prague. An original autograph (Basel).
  • Significance — Tycho's northern logistics, the death-rumour, and a war bulletin.
  • The death-rumour and Fabricius's grief (links to Riddershusen's letter of 16 Dec 1599). Confirms, from another angle, the false rumour of Tycho's death (winter 1599/1600): David Fabricius, "falsely informed of Your Honour's death," grieves "as if his dearest father or son had died." Petkum reassures him (citing Tycho's letter of 14 Nov). Ludolf Ridderhusen of Bremen — the astrologer-correspondent of brahe-t_15991216001 — is here identified as Fabricius's brother-in-law and the new Bremen forwarding-hub for letters to Fabricius. (Both letters — of 16 December 1599 and 12 February 1600 — report the death-rumour and the Fabricius connection — a clean cross-link.)
  • The forwarding network. Routes Tycho's correspondence via Leipzig → Hans Tuchman at Dresden, or via Nuremberg → Leonhard Dillher (imperial factor); to Fabricius via Bremen/Ridderhusen and Frisian skippers (Norden, the Krummhörn); to Scaliger via an Amsterdam messenger.
  • The instrument-ship. News awaited from Vincent Möller (newly burgomaster of Hamburg) on the ship for the instruments; Petkum suggests an imperial letter to the Hamburg Council to speed it (cf. the Magdeburg/Elbe transport thread, brahe-t_16000405001 etc.).
  • The Brussels ephemerides-maker. Tycho wants contact with "Everart," a maker of new ephemerides at Brussels — difficult, since letters must cross the Dutch–Spanish war-zone via Antwerp; Petkum will route via Peter up Meer (a Spanish councillor) and Johan tho Westen at Antwerp. (Identity of "Everart" not established.)
  • Sophie Brahe at Wandsbek. Tycho's sister Sophie Brahe is at Wandsbek; Petkum gives her a coach-wagon and a wheel.
  • The war bulletin. Charles of Sweden holds the whole crown; the Polish diet; a (garbled) report of "King Erik of Sweden, son of Gustav," kept in Muscovy [historically problematic — see note]; England–Spain peace talks; the Dutch refusing peace (the memorable ox-hide quip) and their placard embargoing the Spanish grain-trade, with Hanseatic ships confiscated in Holland. A vivid snapshot of the closing Eighty Years' War / Baltic situation, winter 1600.
  • Wandsbek estate matters. Georg Frobenius must quit Wandsbek by Easter (the Statthalterin — Heinrich Rantzau's heirs' line — having "bargained it back" from her son Breide Rantzau); a cost-declaration for Tycho's buildings pending from Kiel; and the 5000 thaler to be discharged through Eske Bille.
  • Persons & places named. Simon von Petkum; David Fabricius; Veit Winsheim; Hans Tuchman (Dresden); Leonhard Dillher (Nuremberg); Vincent Möller (Hamburg burgomaster); Ludolf Ridderhusen (Bremen, Fabricius's brother-in-law); Joseph Scaliger; "Everart" (Brussels ephemerides-maker); Peter up Meer; Johan tho Westen (Antwerp); Sophie Brahe (Tycho's sister); Charles of Sweden; Sigismund III of Poland; "King Erik son of Gustav" (in Muscovy); Georg Frobenius; the Statthalterin & Breide Rantzau; Eske Bille. Places: Wandsbek, Hamburg, Prague, Görlitz, Leipzig, Dresden, Nuremberg, Bremen, Norden/Krummhörn, Amsterdam, Brussels, Antwerp, Kiel, Denmark, Sweden, Poland, Muscovy, Holland.
  • Note — the "King Erik" report. "King Erik of Sweden, son of Gustav … kept by the Grand Duke [of Muscovy]" is historically problematic: Gustav Vasa's son Erik XIV died in 1577. Petkum is relaying a stale or garbled rumour (possibly a confusion, a pretender, or old news of the deposed line); rendered exactly as the source has it and flagged, per the no-fabrication / no-silent-correction rule.
  • Calendar. den 12 februarij a. 1600 — no stylo marker; the Hamburg/Wandsbek region was Old Style in 1600; read 12 February 1600 [O.S.] (label gives no N.S.; place s.l., supplied Wandsbek/Hamburg).
