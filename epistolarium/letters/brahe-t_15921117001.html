The original

RASMUS PEDERSSEN I KUNSØEMAGLE FICH BREFF THILL THYGE BRAHE OM SIN EGEN SAG ANRØRENDIS.

Thill Thyge Brahe.

WOR gunst thilforn. Wiidt effthersom Raßmus Pedersen vdj Gunßøemagle vnderdanigst haffuer ladet giffue Oß thill kiende, huorledis handt aff thig vdj attschillige maade delis, thilthallis och paa thet yderste forfølges: for:ne hans gaardt, som handt aff thig nogen thiidt forleden høygt och diurtt fest haffuer, hannum att were formeent och forbøden, saa handt jcke sicker och vden stoer fare thør komme ther jndt, hans broder och thieniste karll och aff thig att were vlouffforuunden fengßligen ahntastet och endnu holdis vdj fengsell, och endog handt jcke gierne wille were vdj saadan mangfoldige och vendige klamer och thrette met thig, meden møget mere thill nogen rolig ende, saa fremt hannum nogen liidelige och billige middell och willckor aff thig egien motte wederfares, bode for thend store jndfestning hand thig for for:ne gardt giffuet haffuer, som och for huiß bygning hand ther paa haffuer bekostet och ahnuendt, saa handt theroffuer icke schulle slet och aldelis forderffuis och ødeleggis, vnderdanigst begierendis, att saadane hans arme och elendige wilckor aff saadane languarendis klamer, thrette och forfølging motte ahnsehes och betenkes, och Wij naadigst wille vnde och giffue hannum Wortt beschermelße breff thill aldt rette att motte beschyttes och beschermis. Tha bede Wij thig och wille, attu saadane hans elendighet och yderste forderff selff fører thig thill sindt och gemøte och wedt thin conscientz betrachter och offuer ueyer, och ther udinden star thig selff thill rette, och huiß christeligt, billigt och louligt ehr emodt hannum forethager och begynder, paa thet Wij theraff jcke schulle foraarsagis effther hans vnderdanigst begiering att vnde och giffue hannum, som en Wore och kronens vndersotte och thend thig aldene aff Wor kiere herre fader sallig och hoglofflig ehukomelße ad gratiam forleent ehr, for:ne Wortt beschermelße breff thill ald rette att handhaffuis och forsuaris. Wij ehre och naadigst vdj thend endelig meening och forsett att wille lade Wore obne befalling vdj samme sag vdghae thill Oß elsk:e Lauge Beck, Wor mandt, thiener och landtzdomer her vdj Wortt landt Sielandt, att handt met andre flere Wore ther thill forordnede gode mendt samme sag met thet allerførste schulle thagge for thennum och endeligen kiende och døme ether emellum vdj alle huiß jrringer och thrette, ether emellum ehr eller were kandt, och aff andre Wore godemendt jcke thilforn om kiendt och dømpt ehr, paa thet jngen aff anten siider schulle haffue thennum att beklage at schee emodt rette och forkortt. Ther som thu nu est thilsindtz att wille haffue eller naffn giffue nogen godemendt, som paa thin siide kunde were vdj samme befalling, thu tha strax met thet allerførste thig therom emodt Oss elsk:e Wor thilforordnede regierings raadt ehrklerer och naffn giffuer, huem thu paa thin siide est begierendis, dog thennum, som boe her vdj landet och ehre vparthiesche vdj samme sag, paa thet samme Wor befalling met thet allerførderligst kunde vdschriffuis och fuldgiøris, och met for:ne languarende och vendige klamer och thrette en gang for alle motte komme thill ende. Ther met scheer Wor wilge, thj thag her aldelis jngen forsømmelße fore.

Befallendis etc.
Schreffuit Haffniae 17. nouembris a:o 1592.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Rasmus Pedersen in Gundsømagle received a letter to Tyge Brahe touching his own cause.

To Tyge Brahe.

Our favour beforehand. Know that, inasmuch as Rasmus Pedersen of Gundsømagle has most humbly given us to understand how he is by you, in sundry ways, sued, charged, and to the uttermost prosecuted: that his aforesaid farm, which he some while ago took of you on lease at a high and dear [rent], has been refused and forbidden him, so that he dares not come into it safely and without great danger; that his brother and serving-man have, without [their having] been lawfully convicted, been seized and imprisoned by you and are still held in prison; and although he would by no means wish to be in such manifold and perverse wrangling and dispute with you, but rather [to come] to some peaceable end — provided that some tolerable and equitable terms and conditions might be granted him by you again, both for the great entry-fine which he has given you for the aforesaid farm, and also for whatever building he has there laid out and expended upon it, so that he should not thereby be utterly and altogether ruined and undone — most humbly desiring that such poor and wretched conditions of his, [arising] from such long-lasting wrangling, dispute, and prosecution, might be regarded and considered, and that we would graciously grant and give him our letter of protection, that he might be protected and defended in all that is right. We therefore ask you and will that you yourself take such wretchedness and uttermost ruin of his to mind and heart, and weigh and consider it by your conscience, and therein hold yourself to what is right, and undertake and set in hand against him whatsoever is Christian, equitable, and lawful; to the end that we be not thereby caused — in accordance with his most humble request — to grant and give him, as one of our and the crown's subjects, and one who was granted to you alone by our dear lord father, of blessed and most laudable memory, ad gratiam [as a favour], the aforesaid our letter of protection, that he be maintained and defended in all that is right. We are also graciously of this final mind and intent, to have our open command in this matter go forth to our beloved Lauge Beck, our man, servant, and provincial judge here in our land of Zealand, that he, together with several other good men of ours appointed thereto, shall at the very first take this matter before them and finally hear and judge between you in all such errors and disputes as are or may be between you, and have not heretofore been heard and adjudged by other good men of ours, to the end that neither of either side should have cause to complain of being [dealt with] contrary to right and shortchanged. If you are now minded to wish to have, or to name, any good men who on your side might be in this same commission, then you shall at once, at the very first, declare yourself thereupon and name to our beloved appointed council of regency whom you desire on your side — yet [only] such as dwell here in the land and are impartial in this matter — to the end that this our command may with all speed be written out and effected, and that with the aforesaid long-lasting and perverse wrangling and dispute an end may at last be made once and for all. Therein is done our will; therefore take herein no neglect at all.

Commanding [you to God], etc.
Written at Copenhagen, 17 November, in the year 1592.

Apparatus

  • Issuer. Christian IV of Denmark (b. 1577, r. 1588–1648); in 1592 still a minor of fifteen, the kingdom governed by a council of regency (regeringsråd) — hence the letter's reference to "our beloved appointed council of regency" as the body Tycho is to address, and the deferral to "our dear lord father, of blessed memory," Frederik II (d. 1588), who had granted Tycho the prebend and its lands. The royal "we" is the formal crown voice, not a personal pronouncement by the boy-king.
  • Recipient / beneficiary. Thyge Brahe = Tycho Brahe, here addressed not as astronomer but as landlord. Rasmus Pedersen (Raßmus Pedersen) of Gunderslevmagle (Gunßøemagle / Kunsøemagle, a village in southern Zealand) is one of Tycho's tenant farmers, the petitioner whose complaint the letter conveys. The document is a missive to Tycho prompted by — and embodying — Rasmus Pedersen's suit (the rubric: "Rasmus Pedersen … received a letter to Tyge Brahe touching his own cause").
  • Document type. A chancery draft/concept of an open letter or missive (koncept), preserved among the uindførte koncepter (drafts not entered in the registers) of the Danish Chancery Archive. The opening rubric and the closing Befallendis etc. / Schreffuit Haffniae are Latin–Danish chancery formulae; the body is Danish. ad gratiam ("as a favour / out of grace") is the one Latin phrase embedded in the body — kept and glossed.
  • Lauge Beck. Lauge (Lave) Beck (1568–1607), of Førslev, landsdommer (provincial judge) of Zealand; here designated by the king as the commissioner who, with other appointed gode mænd ("good men," i.e. noble arbiters/assessors), is to convene a final adjudication of the long-running dispute between Tycho and his tenant. This is an early instance of the crown intervening in Tycho's seigneurial conduct toward the peasantry of his fiefs — the kind of friction with tenants and the regency that contributed to the deterioration of his position in Denmark.
  • Legal substance (rendered with care). The petition alleges three grievances against Tycho as landlord: (1) Rasmus Pedersen is barred from the farm he leased at a high rent ("dares not come into it safely and without great danger"); (2) his brother and serving-man were imprisoned without lawful conviction (vlouffforuunden … fengßligen ahntastet = "seized in custody without [their] being lawfully convicted") and are still held; (3) he stands to be "utterly ruined," having paid a large indfæstning (entry-fine, jndfestning) and spent on buildings. The king does not command Tycho to release or restore; the operative instruction is conditional and admonitory — Tycho is to weigh the case by his conscience and of his own accord do "whatsoever is Christian, equitable, and lawful," so that the king be not driven (jcke schulle foraarsagis) to issue the requested letter of protection. The threat of the protection-letter, and of a binding arbitration under Lauge Beck, is the lever; the act demanded of Tycho is left to him.
  • Negation count (verified). The Danish body carries the following negations, all reproduced and none added: jcke sicker … thør komme ("dares not … safely"); vlouffforuunden (the privative "un-lawfully-convicted"); jcke gierne wille were ("would by no means wish to be"); icke schulle … forderffuis ("should not be ruined"); Wij theraff jcke schulle foraarsagis ("that we be not caused"); aff andre … jcke thilforn om kiendt och dømpt ehr ("have not heretofore been adjudged"); jngen aff anten siider ("neither of either side"); and the closing thag her aldelis jngen forsømmelße fore ("take herein no neglect at all"). The conditional saa fremt … motte wederfares ("provided that … might be granted") governs the tenant's willingness to settle and is kept as a proviso, not flattened into an assertion.
  • Orthography (chancery Danish, c. 1592). WOR gunst thilforn = Vor gunst tilforn ("Our favour beforehand," the royal address-formula, the edition's convention); Wiidt = imperative vid ("know"); effthersom = eftersom ("inasmuch as"); delis, thilthallis, forfølges = passives deles, tiltales, forfølges ("is sued, charged, prosecuted"); fest = fæst ("took on lease"); formeent = forment ("refused, denied"); jndfestning = indfæstning (the lease entry-fine); huiß = relative hvis here = "whatever / what(soever)"; gemøte = gemyt ("heart, disposition"); conscientz = "conscience"; for:ne = fornævnte ("aforesaid," standard chancery abbreviation); Oß elsk:e / Oss elsk:e = Os elskelige ("our beloved," courtesy epithet); obne befalling = åbne befaling ("open command"); vparthiesche = upartiske ("impartial"); vendige klamer = vrange/vendte klammeri here "perverse [or turned-about] wrangling"; allerførderligst = allerførderligst/allerforderligst ("with all speed"); Befallendis = befalendes (the closing "commending [you to God]"). vnderdanigst ("most humbly") recurs three times, marking the tenant's, not Tycho's, posture.
  • Calendar / place. Schreffuit Haffniae 17. nouembris a:o 1592 = "Written at Copenhagen (Hafnia), 17 November, year 1592" (O.S.; Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline and the edition label (Copenhagen) agree; no date discrepancy.
  • Correction. English corrected from the real but wrong Zealand village 'Gunderslevmagle' to 'Gundsømagle', matching the source ('Kunsøemagle'/'Gunsøemagle') and Dreyer's own editorial Latin summary ('vico Gundsömagle'), per Dreyer, Opera Omnia XIV pp.63, 65.

The translation and its checking

Grade Secure · collated against Dreyer's page images, Opera Omnia XIV pp. 63–64; defects the audit noted: mistranslation (1) (the translator's own grade before the audit was Probable).

Collated against Dreyer, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia (the edition of record), reading the printed page images. Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.63-64 read as page images (letter runs from the drop-cap 'W' on p.63 through 'Befallendis etc. Schreffuit Haffniæ 17. nouembris a:o 1592' on p.64). Collated the whole letter clause by clause — the farm dispute, the imprisoned brother and servant, the entry-fine and building costs, the referral to Lauge Beck and the regency council, the closing formula and date — all render accurately. The one defect found is a place-name misidentification (Gunsøemagle/Gundsömagle misrendered as the unrelated village Gunderslevmagle), verified against both the letter's own Danish text and Dreyer's independent Latin case-summary on the same page, and corroborated externally (Tycho's 1579 Roskilde-canon fiefdom over Gundsømagle/Gundsøgård). Downgraded from published grade Probable to C because a wrong toponym in a legal/land dispute is exactly the kind of fact a scholar or cataloguer would carry forward uncorrected.

1 defect found:
- mistranslation (medium). The village is named 'Kunsøemagle'/'Gunsøemagle' in the letter and 'Gundsömagle' in Dreyer's own editorial Latin summary of the same case — all three point to Gundsømagle, the Sømme-herred village near Roskilde that Tycho held in fee as a Roskilde canon from 1579 (confirmed independently: Roskilde-historie.dk / Trap Danmark on Gundsømagle sogn). The published English instead names a different, real Zealand village, 'Gunderslevmagle' (near Skælskør/Næstved), misidentifying the location of Tycho's disputed tenant farm and the imprisoned brother — a specific factual error a scholar citing this document would carry forward. At: “Rasmus Pedersen in Gunderslevmagle received a letter to Tyge Brahe touching his own cause.... how he is by you... one ”.

Repaired. Verified against Dreyer TBOO XIV pp.63-65 (page images, not the digitised text layer). Dreyer's own editorial Latin item-summaries for this and the companion letter (items 96, 98) independently and consistently identify the village as Gundsømagle, corroborating the source's own spellings. Only the English was corrected; the Latin needed no change. No other part of the letter was touched.
