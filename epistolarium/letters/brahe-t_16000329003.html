The original

TYCHO BRAHE AD CHRISTOPHORUM HENNIOCHUM

S.

REDDIDIT mihi literas tuas sub initium huius Anni ad me reversus filius TYCHO; estque mihi gratum, conditiones meas, quas Divino provisu hîc in Bohemia apud Cæsam Matem adeptus sum, tibi probari. De Chymico illo in Silesia, cuius meministi, qui Metamorphosin istam Paracelsicam in libro vexationum indicatam callet, per Dominum Doctorem THADDÆUM HAGGÆCIUM nuper inde Pragam redeuntem inquiri faciam; et si quid certi ab eo rescivero, te eius reddam participem.

FRANCISCUS SCRÖDERUS Sundensis ne verbulum mihi, sive Wittebergæ sive hîc degenti, respondit, aut si id fecit, interciderunt eius literæ: admoneas eum iterum sui officij, si forte resipiscere voluerit, quamvis non multum intersit, sive scribat sive non.

Recordor te semel mihi scripsisse de Granatis Bohemicis, quod ex ijs Chymicum quoddam artificium elicere nôsti; cuperem igitur ex te scire, quale illud sit; reperiuntur enim hîc Granati in satis magnâ copiâ, et tibi, si volueris, aliquos per oblatam oportunitatem mittam.

Vale.
Dabantur ex Arce Cæsarea Benatica Die 29 Martij stylo renovato Anni 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

Tycho Brahe to Christopher Henniochus.

Greeting.

My son Tycho, returned to me about the beginning of this year, delivered your letter to me; and it is pleasing to me that my circumstances — which by Divine provision I have obtained here in Bohemia with His Imperial Majesty — are approved by you. About that Chemist in Silesia whom you mention, who is skilled in that Paracelsian Metamorphosis indicated in the Book of Vexations, I will have inquiry made through Master Doctor Thaddaeus Hagecius, lately returning thence to Prague; and if I learn anything certain from him, I will make you a sharer of it.

Franciscus Schröder of Stralsund has not answered me a single little word, whether I was staying at Wittenberg or here; or, if he did, his letter went astray: admonish him again of his duty, if perchance he be willing to come to his senses — although it matters little whether he write or not.

I recall that you once wrote to me about Bohemian Garnets, that you know how to elicit a certain chemical artifice from them; I would therefore wish to know from you what that is. For garnets are found here in fairly great abundance, and I will send you some, if you wish, through an offered opportunity.

Farewell.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 29th day of March, renewed style [New Style], in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Christopher Henniochus (Henniochus — a Latinised surname, likely Hennioch/Henning; not securely re-identified), a correspondent of Tycho's with chemical/alchemical interests. Identification flagged.
  • Tycho's son. filius TYCHO = Tycho Brahe the Younger (b. 1581), who had returned to his father at Benátky early in 1600, carrying Henniochus's letter.
  • Tycho's imperial position. conditiones meas … apud Caesaream Maiestatem — the post he had secured under Rudolf II; Henniochus had congratulated him.
  • Alchemy. Chymicus ille in Silesia … Metamorphosin Paracelsicam in libro vexationum indicatam callet — an unnamed Silesian alchemist skilled in the "Paracelsian Metamorphosis" set out in the Liber vexationum (the [pseudo-]Paracelsian alchemical Liber vexationum / De vexationibus alchimistarum). Tycho — himself a practising alchemist — will inquire via Thaddaeus Hagecius (Hájek), just back at Prague. Granati Bohemici = Bohemian garnets (pyrope), abundant in Bohemia, from which Henniochus claimed to draw "a certain chemical artifice"; Tycho offers to send specimens. (Reinforces the Tychonic alchemy/pyronomia thread; cf. brahe-t_15860301001.)
  • Franciscus Scröderus Sundensis = Franz Schröder of Stralsund (Sundensis = of Stralsund, the Hanseatic Sundum), a delinquent correspondent.
  • Calendar. Die 29 Martij stylo renovato 1600 = 29 March 1600, New Style (stylo renovato explicit); from the Imperial castle of Benátky. Header agrees with the label.
