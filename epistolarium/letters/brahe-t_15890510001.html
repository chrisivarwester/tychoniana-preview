The original

NOBILITATE GENERIS, VIRTUTE ET ERUDITIONE VIRO CLARISS. D. TYCHONI BRAHE DANO, DOMINO INSULÆ URANIEBURGI ETC. DOMINO ET AMICO OBSERVANDO.
S.

LITERÆ tuæ Nobiliss. et amantiss. TYCHO, attulerunt mihi et voluptatem et dolorem; voluptatem quidem, quod abs te adferrentur, de quo iam dudum cognoscere cupiebam; dolorem autem, quod intellexissem meas tibi cum libro VERNERI, abs te iam olim expetito, nondum fuisse redditas. Et profecto suspiciones variæ agitabant animum meum, ut conquiescere nullo modo potuerim. Miseram eas Gorlicium ad MYLIUM unâ cum libro illo, ac magnopere rogabam, ut SCULTETUM commonefaceret, si quid literarum ad te dare vellet, meis adiungeret et primo quoque tempore ad te perferri curaret. Indicabam etiam eidem iuxta documentum tuarum literarum omnes commoditates et occasiones mittendi ad te. Cum autem in ista animi mei perturbatione versor, ecce, oportune sane, ad levationem mei doloris, afferuntur ad me literæ a MYLIO, quibus significat et se magnopere excusat, cur diutius sua opinione delituerint apud se meæ literæ, et ut plenius rei veritatem cognoscas meque apud te excusatum habeas, exemplum ipsius literarum ad te mitto. Sed iam, ut spero, utriusque nostrum desiderio satisfactum, tibique meas redditas esse existimo. Utinam deinceps nostræ literæ cursum nanciscantur feliciorem. His plura quod addam habeo nihil. Ex hoc mercatu avide expectabam primum tuum librum. Sed cur non prodierit, ex tuis ad me intellexi. Deum oro, ut tuis studijs Reipub. literariæ causa favere velit. Nos ante menses circiter duos amisimus virum summum et incomparabilem tuique studiosiss. D. DUDITHIUM. Decessit etiam ex hac vita 19. Aprilis Viennæ FABRICIUS. Utriusque mortem admodum doleo. Sed quum Deo Opt. Max. eos in beatiorem vitam ex hisce miseriarum turbis evocare libuit, in sanctissima eius voluntate nobis acquiescendum est.

Barones, quos salvere iubebas, non sunt in hac aula. D. DUDITHIUS, bonæ memoriæ, de libris WITYCHIJ iam olim cum sorore ipsius egit. Sed morosiorem illam fuisse intellexi, quam ut aliquid ex bibliotheca fratris communicare vellet aut saltem lustrare pateretur.

B. V.
Pragæ 1O. Maij 1589.
T. studiosiss.
Thad.
D. fratrem tuum meis verbis officiose salvere iube.
EXEMPLUM LITERARUM MARTINI MYLIJ AD THAD.

CLARISS. vir, fateor peccasse me in te atque adeo in amicitiam nostram, qui tam diu nullas ad te literas misi, cum præsertim mihi de rebus ad te pertinentibus, puta de literis et libro tuo ad TYCHONEM curando, scribendum esset. Sed ita est ut scribo. Diutius opinione mea delituerunt apud me TYCHONIS literæ. Unam enim saltem occasionem habemus mittendi per nostratem quendam civem, qui Hamburgum ire solet, idque uno anno vix bis. Iter enim est longinquius. Cum eo igitur misi tua ante paucas septimanas, credo vix tres quatuorve ad summum. Ante illud tempus fateor suppuduisse me quicquam scribere, ne tu forte indignarere, quod mea incuria commisissem, ut epistola facta esset ἕωλος. Eam religionem meam ut boni et æqui consulas, te oro.

Gorlicij die Philippi et Iacobi Apostolorum 1589.
POST SCRIPTA.

IN Cancellariam Imperij memineris, ut ex tenore Privilegij aliquot exempla tui operis mittas. Ego enim, qui fueram solicitator Privilegij, hac de re fui admonitus. Respondi, fuisse quidem mihi abs te per quendam Studiosum missum unum exemplum, sed Bibliopolis integrum opus nondum fuisse publicatum, neque te hanc partem secundam absque alijs publicare voluisse. Cum igitur in mercatu futuro Francoffordensi integrum opus (ut spero) prodibit, et exempla aliquot in Cancellariam miseris, vellem salutares tuis literis D. CURTIUM eiusque amicitiam expeteres. Mihi est amicissimus, et profecto dignus est, qui ab omnibus diligatur. Ad ipsa Mathemata natus videtur. Et certe, si ijs solis ipsi vacandum esset, aliquid præstare posset.

Titulus D. CURTIJ: IACOBUS CURTIUS a Senfftenau, Cæs. Consiliarius et Procancell. Imperij.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the man most renowned for nobility of birth, for virtue and for erudition, Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, lord of the island of Uraniborg etc., his lord and friend to be revered. Greeting.

Your letter, most noble and most loving Tycho, brought me both pleasure and pain: pleasure, indeed, because it was brought from you, of whom I had long been wishing to have news; but pain, because I had learned that mine to you, [sent] together with Werner's book — long since requested by you — had not yet been delivered. And in truth various suspicions kept stirring my mind, so that I could in no way be at rest. I had sent them to Görlitz, to Mylius, together with that book, and earnestly begged him to remind Scultetus that, if he wished to send any letter to you, he should join it to mine and see that it was conveyed to you at the very first opportunity. I also pointed out to the same man, in accordance with the directions of your own letter, all the conveniences and occasions for sending [things] to you. But while I am caught up in this disturbance of mind, behold — very seasonably indeed, for the easing of my grief — a letter is brought to me from Mylius, in which he both makes known, and greatly excuses himself, why my letters lay hidden with him longer than he had supposed; and, that you may more fully know the truth of the matter and hold me excused before you, I send you a copy of his letter itself. But now, as I hope, the desire of us both has been satisfied, and I reckon that mine have been delivered to you. Would that hereafter our letters may meet with a happier passage. I have nothing further to add to these things. From this fair I was eagerly awaiting your first book. But why it did not appear, I have understood from your [letter] to me. I pray God that He may be willing to favour your studies for the sake of the republic of letters. About two months ago we lost a man supreme and incomparable, and most devoted to you, Master Dudith. There departed from this life also, on 19 April, at Vienna, Fabricius. I grieve much at the death of both. But since it has pleased God, Best and Greatest, to call them out of these tumults of miseries into a more blessed life, we must rest content in His most holy will.

The barons whom you bade [me] greet are not at this court. Master Dudith, of good memory, had already some while ago dealt with Wittich's books with his [Wittich's] sister. But I have learned that she was too peevish [a woman] to be willing to share anything from her brother's library, or at least to allow it to be examined.

Fare you well.
Prague, 10 May 1589.
Most devoted to you,
Thad[deus].
Bid your brother, in my words, a dutiful greeting.
COPY OF MARTIN MYLIUS'S LETTER TO THAD[DEUS].

Most renowned sir, I confess that I have sinned against you, and indeed against our friendship, in that for so long I sent you no letter, when especially I ought to have written to you about matters pertaining to you — namely, about seeing to your letter and book [destined] for Tycho. But it is as I write. Tycho's letters lay hidden with me longer than I had supposed. For we have but a single opportunity of sending [things], by way of a certain townsman of ours who is accustomed to go to Hamburg, and that scarcely twice in one year. For the journey is rather long. With him, therefore, I sent your [parcel] a few weeks ago — I believe scarcely three or four [weeks] at the most. Before that time I confess I was somewhat ashamed to write anything, lest perhaps you should be vexed that, by my carelessness, I had let the letter become stale [ἕωλος]. I beg you to take this scruple of mine in good and fair part.

Görlitz, on the day of the Apostles Philip and James [1 May], 1589.
POSTSCRIPT.

[Remember the matter] of the Imperial Chancery: that, by the terms of the Privilege, you should send some copies of your work. For I, who had been the solicitor of the Privilege, was admonished about this matter. I replied that one copy had indeed been sent to me from you by a certain student, but that the whole work had not yet been published for the booksellers, nor had you wished to publish this second part without the others. Since, therefore, at the coming Frankfurt fair the whole work will (as I hope) appear, and you will have sent some copies to the Chancery, I would wish that you should greet Master Curtius by your letter and seek his friendship. He is most friendly to me, and is truly worthy to be esteemed by all. He seems born for mathematics itself. And surely, if he had only these to attend to, he could accomplish something.

The title of Master Curtius: Jacobus Curtius of Senftenau, Imperial Councillor and Vice-Chancellor of the Empire.

Apparatus

  • Correspondent. Thaddaeus Hagecius = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayek, 1525–1600), imperial physician at Prague to Maximilian II and Rudolf II, astronomer, and Tycho's chief Bohemian correspondent and intermediary. The subscription Thad. is his abbreviated signature. B. V. = Bene vale, "Fare you well".
  • Recipient/address. D. Tychoni Brahe Dano, domino insulae Uranieburgi — "Master Tycho Brahe the Dane, lord of the island of Uraniborg." Uranieburgi here names the island (Hven) by the name of its castle, Uraniborg.
  • liber Verneri — "Werner's book," a work by Johann Werner (1468–1522) of Nuremberg, long requested by Tycho (abs te iam olim expetito); most probably Werner's geographical/mathematical writings or his tract on the motion of the eighth sphere. The exact title is not specified in the letter; left unspecified rather than inferred.
  • Mylius = Martin Mylius (Martin Mohl, 1542–1611), rector of the Görlitz Gymnasium and a postal intermediary on the Prague–Görlitz–Hamburg–Hven route. Gorlicium / Gorlicij = Görlitz (Lusatia). The bracketed enclosure EXEMPLUM LITERARUM MARTINI MYLIJ AD THAD. is Hagecius's transcribed copy of Mylius's covering letter, forwarded as proof of the delay; the code-switch into the embedded letter is preserved.
  • Scultetus = Bartholomäus Scultetus (Barthel Schultz, 1540–1614), Görlitz mathematician, cartographer and later burgomaster, a correspondent of Tycho's; named here as a possible fellow-sender on the Görlitz relay.
  • Dudithius = Andreas Dudith (András Dudith / Andreas Dudithius, 1533–1589), the Hungarian humanist, former bishop, and anti-astrological author on comets, resident at Breslau (Wrocław), where he died on 23 February 1589 — consistent with ante menses circiter duos ("about two months ago") relative to the 10 May dateline. A close friend and correspondent of Hagecius.
  • Fabricius = Paul Fabricius (c. 1519–1589), imperial mathematician and physician at Vienna, an observer of the 1572 nova. The letter's 19. Aprilis Viennae (died 19 April, at Vienna) fixes the identification: not David Fabricius (d. 1617). Named without forename in the source; identification supplied in brackets only by inference and recorded here.
  • Witychij … cum sorore ipsius — "Wittich's [books] … with his sister": Paul Wittich (c. 1546–1586), the Breslau mathematician (prosthaphaeresis; Copernican-geocentric models) whose library and manuscripts Tycho sought to acquire. The literature confirms Tycho's pursuit of Wittich's library through Hagecius and the Barons Liechtenstein and Herberstein in 1589, again through Monaw in 1595, and successfully in 1598. de libris … egit and the sister's refusal to communicare or allow the library to be lustrare (examined/surveyed) are rendered without sharpening.
  • Barones, quos salvere iubebas, non sunt in hac aula — "The barons whom you bade [me] greet are not at this court": presumably the Liechtenstein/Herberstein patrons sought as intermediaries for the Wittich books; the antecedent is not named in the letter and is not supplied in the translation.
  • primum tuum librum / hanc partem secundam / integrum opus — "your first book" / "this second part" / "the whole work": Tycho's Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata. The volume Tycho had printed and distributed (the 1588 De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis) is the second part of the projected three-part work; Mylius's report that Tycho would not publish hanc partem secundam absque alijs ("this second part without the others") and that integrum opus was awaited at Frankfurt matches Tycho's stated publication plan. The titles are not spelled out in the source and are not inserted into the translation.
  • ex hoc mercatu / in mercatu futuro Francoffordensi — "from this fair" / "at the coming Frankfurt fair": the semi-annual Frankfurt book fair (Frankfurter Messe), the chief channel of European book distribution.
  • Greek. ἕωλος (héōlos) — literally "a day old," hence "stale, out of date" (of food left overnight, transferred to a letter overtaken by events). Transcribed and glossed by this chapter's convention; rendered "stale" in the body with the Greek retained in brackets.
  • Cancellaria Imperij / Privilegium / solicitator Privilegij — the Imperial Chancery at Prague; the imperial printing privilege (copyright protection) for Tycho's work, of which Mylius had been the solicitator (the agent who procured it). The privilege required deposit of copies (ex tenore Privilegij aliquot exempla … mittas).
  • D. Curtius = Jacobus Curtius a Senftenau (Jakob Kurz von Senftenau, 1554–1594), Imperial Councillor and Vice-Chancellor of the Empire (Caes. Consiliarius et Procancell. Imperij), a mathematically gifted patron; Mosley's despatch tables record a presentation/contact of Tycho's work to "Jakob Kurtz (1589)/1590," corroborating Mylius's recommendation here. Procancellarius rendered "Vice-Chancellor."
  • fratrem tuum — "your brother": one of Tycho's brothers (most likely Steen Brahe); not named in the letter.
  • Calendar. The body dateline reads Pragae 1O. Maij 1589 ("Prague, 10 May 1589"); the edition labels the letter n.s. (New Style). Hagecius, an imperial (Catholic) correspondent at Prague, would write New Style after the 1582 reform. No conversion is made. The enclosed Mylius letter is dated Gorlicij die Philippi et Iacobi Apostolorum 1589 = the feast of SS Philip and James (1 May); glossed by this chapter's convention with the idiom kept. Görlitz (Lutheran Upper Lusatia) retained the Julian calendar in 1589, so Mylius's "1 May" is in all likelihood Old Style, against Hagecius's New-Style "10 May" — a style mismatch between the two datelines that is flagged, not reconciled: the two dates are roughly nine days apart, consistent with Mylius writing O.S. shortly before Hagecius forwards N.S., but the letter does not state the styles and the inference is not forced into the dates.
  • Source-block note. Pragae 1O. Maij preserves the printed reading with a capital letter "O" standing for the zero of "10"; kept as printed by this chapter's convention unâ and morosiorem etc. retain the edition's diacritics/orthography.
