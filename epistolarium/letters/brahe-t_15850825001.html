The original

CLARISSIMO ET VIRTUTE ERUDITIONEQUE PRÆEMINENTI VIRO D. DOCTORI THADDÆO AB HAYCK, MEDICO ET MATHEMATICO INPRIMIS EXCELLENTI, INCOLÆ PRAGENSI, AMICO SUO PERPETUO COLENDO.
HERRN DOCTOR THADÆUS ZU PRAG ZUERFRAGEN.
S.

PERFUNCTUS Legatione apud Cæsaream Maiestatem, a Serenissimo Rege nostro sibi adiuncta, reversusque huc in patriam frater meus STENO BRAHE, non saltem literas mihi a te attulit, Eruditissime et longe charissime Domine D. THADDÆE, sed insuper narravit, quanta humanitate ipsum exceperis, quamque liberaliter et iucunde tractâris. Quod sane magna cum voluptate percepi, tibique gratias habeo plurimas, quod mei absentis memoriam hac ratione in fratre præsente recolueris. Nam ex benevolentia ipsi præstita, me quoque ob veterem quæ nobis intercedit necessitudinem a te complexum esse, satis evidenter persuasum habeo, relaturus sane gratiam, si de te vel ullo tuorum bene merendi unquam offeratur occasio. Rescripsissem vero tuis literis maturius, si per nuncij commoditatem id licuisset. Interea vero dum hinc inde perquiri curo, qua ratione ad te nostræ mittantur, ecce forte fortuna accessit quidam, qui se in istis Bohemiæ oris habitare aiebat, cupereque cum quodam ex mihi inservientibus Artificibus natione Bohemo (qui aliquot annis in quibusdam ad Instrumentorum fabricam facientibus diligentem mihi navavit operam) sermonem miscere, illiusque in patria, si quæ velit, negotia procurare. Hac oblata scribendi commoditate, cum is meas etiam ad te perferri literas, sedulo se curaturum addiceret, non potui non, utut alijs maxime occupatus negocijs, sepositis omnibus, has illi ad te perferendas committere.

Quantum ad Dialexin tuam a te denuo locupletatam attinet, cum editionem eius differri ob Typographorum avaritiam intelligam, et mihi eius copiam offeras, si de mea voluntate certior factus fueris, rogo ut eam ad me prima quaque occasione transmittas. Dabo operam ut vel hîc meis typis edatur, vel tibi quamprimum citra ullam libri iacturam remittatur. Eorum etiam quæ de Nova Stella et aliquot Cometis præcedentibus annis conspectis a diversis hinc inde in medium allata sunt, si singulorum exemplaria unâ miseris, rem feceris longe gratissimam. Conscripsi enim et ego quædam de hoc toto negotio, quæ proprijs typis propediem, Deo conatus nostros felicitante, edere constitui, id quod hactenus, ex quo omnia ad Typographiam pertinentia in promptu parata haberem, ob Papyri inopiam, quam peculiarem requiro, procrastinatum est. Cum enim Mercenariæ naves partim ob earum arrestationem in Hispania, partim ob Antwerpiæ diuturnam obsidionem hac æstate fretum hoc, in solita copia, non transnavigaverint, factum est, ut Papyro, quæ hac ratione inter alias merces e Gallijs et Belgicis civitatibus copiosa et varia apportari solet, præter spem destitutus sim. Verumtamen per naves Hollandicas, papyrum nostro negotio, in sufficienti copia et forma, aptam, a Telonij Regij, quod Helsingoræ est, præfecto, mihi certo procuratam, indies expecto; quamprimum vero hæc allata fuerit, editionem non diutius differam. Interim percuperem variorum in hac materia opiniones perspicere, ut constare queat, quinam veritatis metam, ad quam tendimus, collimatius attingant, vel si quæ devia inciderint, quorsum ea deflectantur, explorem. Te itaque nostris desiderijs nequaquam defuturum tum propria (quæ exactissima fore persuasum habeo) tum etiam aliorum placita mittendo, nullum mihi movetur dubium. Ubi nostra de his commentatio excusa fuerit, per primam quamque oblatam occasionem hanc tecum vicissim communicabo, tuæque censuræ lubens subijciam. Percepi insuper ex literis tuis, duos illos Nobiles Gallos, qui me hîc inter peregrinandum salutârant, tecum aliquandiu suaviter vixisse, vosque interea Chymica quædam medicamenta confecisse; ego vero, cum hi mecum essent, ignorabam eos vulgaris Medicinæ nedum Spagyricarum præparationum fuisse studiosos, alias et ego de hac re cum illis non iniucunde contulissem. Ostendi quidem ipsis meum, quod subterraneum est, Laboratorium, idque licet in ijs quæ ad Pyronomicam artem faciunt, non vulgariter sit instructum, tamen illi dissimulabant se quicquam horum intelligere. Quo consilio id fecerint, ignoro. Nec interim dum hic adsunt, quicquam intellexeram eos tam cito Pragam venturos, alias nequaquam sine meis te accessissent literis. Petis ut quempiam Chymicarum præparationum peritum ad te mittam, si forte peregrinando huc appulerit, id quod lubenter facturus sum, sed vereor ne votis tuis hac in parte minime eventus respondeat. Rarissimi enim sunt apud nos his exercitijs addicti, ideoque rariores eo nomine huc confluunt. Alchymistis vulgaribus, qui se multa scire vaniloque profitentur, quantum sit fidendum, ipsemet facile ex tot illorum imposturis perspectum habes. Meum itaque erit consilium, ut fidelem aliquem iuvenem harum rerum studiosum tibi adiungas, ipsumque successive instruas, quoad in his laboribus per diuturnam exercitationem reddatur expeditior; fabricando enim, ut dici solet, fabri fimus. Si quid egregij hactenus a vobis in hac Arte inventum fuerit, velim mecum communices, ego vicissim eorum, quæ in mea Officina Hermetica nata sunt et tibi placitura intellexero, te reddam participem. Cum unâ Ratisbonæ essemus, libellum quendam a me accepisti, nescio cuius authoris, de Chymicis medicamentis præparandis, sed et titulum libri neque exacte teneo, in octava forma fuisse satis recordor. Eum librum nusquam postea venalem habere licuit, utut sæpius Bibliopolas eo nomine in Germania sollicitari curaverim, quod forte ideo minus fieri poterat, quia nomen authoris et titulus libri mihi exciderant. Peto itaque ut vel eius libelli exemplar, si forte uspiam apud vos venale exstat, vel saltem titulum libri cum authoris nomine ad me transmitti curare velis. Et si quæ præterea de hac materia noviter prodierunt, quæ artem et ingenium sapiant, unâ per te obtineam, rem pergratam feceris. IOHANNIS WERNERI opusculum de motu octavæ sphæræ Norimbergæ olim, ut existimo, impressum multo tempore late conquisivi, sed nondum uspiam adeptus sum. Si itaque librum hunc istic nactus fueris, et me eius participem reddideris, tum etiam, si tractatum illum manuscriptum ignoti authoris de Directionum significationibus, quem Ratisbonæ apud te vidi, per aliquem descriptum, adiunxeris, multum meis desiderijs gratificaveris. Sumtus omnes lubens remittam, tuisque votis vicissim, ubicunque licuerit, me præbebo officiosissimum. Commentaria TRAPEZUNTIJ in PTOLOMÆUM si adhuc penes te habes, necdum impressa prodierunt, sique eis carere potes, mihi perscribe, qua ratione transigendum tecum fuerit, ut ea (si alias Typis mandari nequeant) abs te impetrem. Fieri enim potest, ut proprijs impensis editionem eorum instituam, ne tanti viri excellens labor penitus intercidat, et usum destinatum Astronomiæ studiosis omnino præcludat. Unum etiam adhuc restat, quod te magnopere, ni tibi nimium sim molestus, rogatum velim. Cum non mediocri sumtu propriam hîc Uraniburgi Typographiam adornârim, in qua non solum ea quæ de Nova Stella et succedentibus aliquot Cometis a me cœlitus animadversa diligenter exponuntur demonstranturque, sed etiam ea quæ ad æterna illa et Mundo coæva sidera spectant, quæque multis et exquisitis a cœlo ipso (per Organa maxima, solide et ad amussim, ingenti labore et sumtu præcedentibus aliquot annis constructa) petitis observationibus, Astronomiam totam, tum quo ad Luminarium, tum etiam reliquarum tam errantium quam inerrantium Stellarum, exactam motus rationem, veritati apparentiarum (quatenus ea in sensus nostros vel subtilissime indaganti cadit) consentientem, pristino vigori restituunt; ea inquam et quam plurima alia ad Mathematicam inprimis scientiam pertinentia, quæ vel hactenus a nobis absoluta sunt, vel in posterum, Deo vires et ingenium suppeditante, elaboranda veniunt, constitui (eodem favente) proprijs typis excudere, et Reipub. literariæ non sine singulari, uti spero, emolumento communicare. Cumque præter eas, quas tum in ipsam Typographiam omnibus necessarijs adminiculis rite instruendam insumserim impensas, tum etiam non minores in Operum, quæ excudenda veniunt, apparatum requirantur, præsertim in his Mathematicis rebus, quæ sine figurarum multiplici delineatione sumtuosos labores requirente, nequaquam dextre institui queunt (prout hæc omnia tibi ipsi adeo perspecta sunt, ut longa non indigeant probatione): Idcirco ne tot sumtus et labores cum dispendio mearum rerum in rei literariæ gratiam incassum faciam, si forte quæ hîc meis Typis impressa fuerint, in Germania vel alibi confestim recudantur, per Amicitiam nostram te rogatum velim, ut a Cæsarea Maiestate mihi vel per temetipsum vel per alios, Privilegium ratum impetres, ut intra decennium libros in Astronomia et reliqua Mathematica scientia a me elaboratos turpisque proprijs in hac mea Officina Uraniburgica excusos, in ullis Imperij locis Cæsareæ Maiestati subiectis, denuo imprimere vel publicare et divendere, sub certa mulcta et amissione exemplarium, nequaquam liceat. Cavebo vicissim, ne libri a me publicandi quidpiam, quod vel Relligionis controversijs, vel Reipublicæ constitutioni offensioni siet, ullo in loco contineant. Et si quid ipsa Cæsarea Maiestas vicissim hoc nomine a me præstandum postulaverit, ipsius Maiestati meam Operam et obsequium humiliter defero. Præterea si qui sumtus in eos, qui hæc ex Cæsaris decreto exsequuntur, conscribendoque Privilegio occupantur, erogandi veniunt, quamprimum per te de his certior factus fuero, lubens omnia quæ requiruntur mittam. Tuum itaque erit, charissime THADÆE, omnem movere lapidem, ut Privilegium hoc a Cæsarea Maiestate primo quoque tempore obtineam. Cuperem enim idipsum operi meo, quod de novis æthereæ Regionis Generationibus nostro ævo conspectis inscripsi, et omnium primo typis edere decrevi, præfigere, ut eo tutius per Autoritatem Cæsarianum nostris laboribus et limitibus consulatur. Qua autem forma et quibus verbis Privilegium hoc optime comprehendi queat, tuæ sagacitati latius indicandum disponendumque relinquo. Nec dubito quin meæ de te expectationi hac in parte sedulo sis satisfacturus. Ut vero Typorum nostrorum, quos hactenus conquisivi, characterem perspicias, mitto tibi quædam carmina, præterita hyeme recreandi a serioribus studijs animi gratia ad quosdam Nobiles et eruditos amicos exarata, et ne Typi et Typographus ob Papyri inopiam prorsus feriarent, in nostra Officina excusa. Addidi etiam ædificij nostri in hac insula Astronomiæ honori et restaurationi sumtuose extructi designationes aliquot, ut tibi absenti aliquo modo ædes hasce Uraniæ (cui et tu ab ineunte ætate sacra litasti) dicatas, intueri liceat. Omnium quoque in hisce contentorum Organorum Astronomicorum delineationem unâ misissem, sed nondum ob sculptoris mei in Germaniam abitum, eorum figuræ penitus absolutæ sunt. Binorum saltem Instrumentorum picturam mitto, quorum fabricam et usum ex ipsa inspectione facile assequeris. Ubi sculptor reversus fuerit, cæterasque machinas suis lineamentis expresserit, faciam te eorum participem. Curo enim has omnes figuris convenientibus excidi, eo quod peculiari volumine harum omnium structuram et multiplicem infallibilemque usum, Astronomiæ alumnis patefacere decreverim. Sed video me copiosius tecum agendo epistolæ modum excedere, quare ne te ulterius, quam par est, detineam, finem scribendi faciam, teque cum tota domo Divinæ tutelæ commendabo. Vale, et nostris desiderijs prima quaque opportunitate responde. Si nulla offeratur commodior ratio, literas unâ cum ijs de quibus mentionem feci, Rostochium ad Doctorem HENRICUM BRUCÆUM perferri cura. Is enim confestim per crebra quæ inde huc appellunt navigia me eorum compotem reddet.

Iterum mi charissime THADÆE vale quam felicissime.
Datæ Uraniburgi 25. Augusti Anno 1585.
Tui amantissimus
TYCHO BRAHE
manu propria.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned man, pre-eminent in virtue and erudition, Master Doctor Thaddaeus ab Hayck, physician and mathematician of the first excellence, citizen of Prague, his friend to be honoured perpetually.
Doctor Thaddaeus at Prague, to be enquired for.
Greeting.

Having discharged the embassy to His Imperial Majesty entrusted to him by our Most Serene King, and having returned hither to his homeland, my brother Steno Brahe not only brought me a letter from you, most learned and far dearest Lord, Master Thaddaeus, but moreover related with what great kindness you received him, and how generously and pleasantly you entertained him. This indeed I learned with great pleasure, and I owe you the warmest thanks that in this way, in my brother's presence, you kept in mind one who was absent. For from the goodwill shown to him I am quite plainly persuaded that I too — on account of the old bond that subsists between us — have been embraced by you; and I shall surely repay the favour, if ever an occasion offers of deserving well of you or any of yours. I would indeed have replied to your letter sooner, had the convenience of a messenger allowed it. But while I was seeing to enquiries, this way and that, by what means my letters might be sent to you, behold, by sheer good fortune there came a certain man who said that he dwelt in those parts of Bohemia, and that he wished to fall into conversation with one of the craftsmen in my service, a Bohemian by nation (who for some years did diligent work for me on certain matters pertaining to the making of instruments), and to attend to that man's affairs in his homeland, if he should want anything. This opportunity of writing being offered — since he undertook that he would also see to it carefully that my letters were conveyed to you — I could not but, however much I was busied with other matters, set everything aside and entrust these [letters] to him to convey to you.

As for your Dialexis, now enlarged anew by you: since I understand that its publication is being delayed through the greed of the printers, and you offer me a copy of it if you shall have been made more certain of my wish, I ask that you send it to me at the very first occasion. I shall take pains either that it be published here with my own types, or that it be returned to you as soon as possible without any loss of the book. Of those things too which, concerning the New Star and several Comets observed in the preceding years, have been brought forward into public by various men here and there — if you send copies of each together [with it], you will do a most welcome thing. For I too have composed certain things about this whole matter, which I have determined to publish shortly with my own types, God prospering our endeavours — something which up to now, although I had everything pertaining to the press ready at hand, has been put off for want of paper, of which I require a particular kind. For since the merchant ships, partly because of their seizure in Spain, partly because of the long siege of Antwerp, did not this summer sail through this Sound in the usual quantity, it came about that I have been, beyond expectation, left without paper — which, by this route, among other merchandise, is wont to be brought in abundant and varied supply from the cities of France and the Low Countries. Nevertheless, through Dutch ships, I daily expect paper fit for our business, in sufficient quantity and format, certainly procured for me by the warden of the Royal Toll which is at Helsingør; and as soon as this has been brought, I shall delay the edition no longer. Meanwhile I should much like to inspect the opinions of various men on this subject, so that it may be established who reach more nearly on the mark [collimatius] the goal of truth toward which we strive, or, if any have fallen into byways, that I may explore in what direction they have swerved. And so I move no doubt that you will by no means fail our desires, by sending both your own views (which I am persuaded will be most exact) and also those of others. When our commentary on these matters has been printed, I shall in turn communicate it to you by the very first occasion offered, and gladly submit it to your judgement. I learned moreover from your letter that those two noble Frenchmen who had greeted me here during their travels lived for some while pleasantly with you, and that you meanwhile prepared together certain chemical medicines; but I, when they were with me, did not know that they were students of ordinary medicine, much less of spagyric preparations, otherwise I too would have conferred with them on this matter not unpleasantly. I did indeed show them my Laboratory, which is underground, and although it is fitted out for the things that pertain to the pyronomic art [the art of fire] in no common way, yet they dissembled that they understood any of these things. With what design they did this, I do not know. Nor meanwhile, while they were here, had I understood that they would come to Prague so soon, otherwise they would by no means have approached you without a letter from me. You ask that I send to you someone skilled in chemical preparations, should one perhaps put in here while travelling — which I shall gladly do; but I fear that in this part the outcome may scarcely answer to your wishes. For very rare among us are those given to these exercises, and therefore the rarer do they flock hither on that account. As for how far one is to trust the common alchemists, who profess vaingloriously to know much, you yourself easily have it perceived from so many of their impostures. My counsel therefore will be that you attach to yourself some faithful young man, a student of these matters, and instruct him by degrees, until by long practice he be rendered more expert in these labours; for, as the saying goes, by making [things] we become makers [smiths]. If anything excellent has so far been discovered by you in this Art, I should wish you to share it with me; I in turn will make you a sharer of those things which have been born in my Hermetic Workshop and which I shall have understood will please you. When we were together at Regensburg, you received from me a certain little book, of I know not what author, on the preparing of chemical medicines; but neither do I exactly hold the title of the book — that it was in octavo format I well enough recall. That book I have nowhere since been able to have for sale, although I have often had booksellers in Germany solicited for it by name — which perhaps could the less be done for that reason, because the author's name and the book's title had slipped from me. I ask therefore that you see to it that there be sent to me either a copy of that little book, if perhaps it is anywhere for sale among you, or at least the title of the book together with the author's name. And if besides anything has lately come out on this subject that savours of skill and ingenuity, [and] I obtain it through you at the same time, you will do a most agreeable thing. The little work of Johannes Werner On the Motion of the Eighth Sphere, printed long ago at Nuremberg, as I think, I have widely sought after for a long time, but have not yet anywhere obtained. If therefore you have come upon this book there, and make me a sharer of it, and then too, if you add — copied out by someone — that manuscript treatise of an unknown author On the Significations of Directions, which I saw at Regensburg in your keeping, you will much gratify my desires. All the costs I shall gladly remit, and in turn, wherever it shall be allowed, I will show myself most dutiful to your wishes. Trapezuntius's Commentaries on Ptolemy, if you still have them in your possession, [and] they have not yet come out in print, and if you can do without them, write to me by what arrangement the matter is to be settled with you, so that I may obtain them from you (if otherwise they cannot be committed to the press). For it may be that I will undertake their edition at my own expense, lest the excellent labour of so great a man perish utterly, and altogether shut off the use intended for students of astronomy. One thing still remains, on which I should greatly wish to ask you — unless I be too troublesome to you. Since at no slight cost I have here at Uraniborg furnished a press of my own, in which are diligently set forth and demonstrated not only those things which, concerning the New Star and several succeeding Comets, I have observed from the heavens, but also those things which regard those eternal stars coeval with the World, and which — by many and exquisite observations sought from the heaven itself (through the greatest instruments, soundly and to a nicety, with vast labour and cost constructed in some preceding years) — restore to its former vigour the whole of Astronomy, both as to the exact reckoning of the motion of the Luminaries and also of the rest of the Stars, both wandering and fixed, [a reckoning] consonant with the truth of the appearances (so far as that falls to our senses, even to one investigating most subtly): these things, I say, and very many others pertaining especially to Mathematical science, which either have so far been completed by me, or in future, God supplying strength and ingenuity, come to be worked out — I have determined (with His favour) to print with my own types, and to communicate to the Republic of Letters, not without singular profit, as I hope. And since, beyond the expenses which I have laid out both upon duly furnishing the press itself with all necessary apparatus, and also no smaller ones [are] required for the apparatus of the Works which come to be printed — especially in these Mathematical matters, which without the manifold drawing of figures, requiring costly labours, can by no means be aptly set up (all of which is so thoroughly evident to you yourself that it needs no long proof): therefore, lest I make so many costs and labours, with loss of my own means, in vain for the sake of letters, should perhaps the things printed here with my types be at once reprinted in Germany or elsewhere — by our Friendship I should wish to ask you that you obtain for me from His Imperial Majesty, either through yourself or through others, a confirmed Privilege, [providing] that within ten years it be by no means permitted, in any places of the Empire subject to His Imperial Majesty, to reprint anew, or publish and sell, the books in Astronomy and the rest of Mathematical science worked out by me and printed with my own types in this my Uraniborg Workshop — under a fixed fine and forfeiture of the copies. I shall take care, in turn, that the books to be published by me contain in no place anything which may give offence either to the controversies of Religion or to the constitution of the Commonwealth. And if His Imperial Majesty in turn shall demand anything to be performed by me on this account, I humbly offer my service and obedience to His Majesty. Moreover, if any costs come to be expended upon those who execute these things by the Emperor's decree, and who are occupied in drawing up the Privilege, as soon as I have been made more certain of these through you, I will gladly send all that is required. It will be yours therefore, dearest Thaddaeus, to move every stone, that I may obtain this Privilege from His Imperial Majesty at the very first time. For I should wish to prefix it to that work of mine which I have entitled On the new Generations of the ethereal Region beheld in our age, and which I have resolved to publish first of all — so that the more safely, through Imperial Authority, provision may be made for our labours and bounds. But in what form and in what words this Privilege may best be framed, I leave to your sagacity to indicate and dispose more fully. Nor do I doubt that in this part you will diligently satisfy my expectation of you. And that you may perceive the character of our types, which I have so far procured, I send you certain poems, written last winter to certain noble and learned friends for the sake of refreshing the mind from more serious studies, and printed in our Workshop so that the types and the typographer should not be wholly idle for want of paper. I have added also some delineations of our building, raised at great cost on this island for the honour and restoration of Astronomy, so that you, [though] absent, may in some way look upon this house dedicated to Urania (to whom you too, from your earliest years, have offered sacred rites). I would have sent together a drawing of all the Astronomical instruments contained in these [buildings], but their figures are not yet entirely completed, owing to my engraver's departure into Germany. I send at least a picture of two Instruments, whose construction and use you will easily grasp from the very inspection. When the engraver has returned, and has rendered the rest of the machines in their outlines, I shall make you a sharer of them. For I am having all of these cut in suitable figures, because I have resolved to lay open, in a separate volume, the structure and the manifold and infallible use of all of them, to the nurslings of Astronomy. But I see that, in dealing with you more copiously, I exceed the measure of a letter; wherefore, that I may not detain you further than is fitting, I shall make an end of writing, and commend you, with your whole house, to the Divine protection. Farewell, and answer our desires at the very first opportunity. If no more convenient means offer, see that the letters, together with those things of which I have made mention, are conveyed to Rostock to Doctor Heinrich Brucaeus. For he, by the frequent vessels which put in here from there, will at once make me possessed of them.

Again, my dearest Thaddaeus, fare you most happily.
Given at Uraniborg, 25 August, the year 1585.
Your most loving
TYCHO BRAHE
with his own hand.

[Postscript:] If I shall have received a reply from you before the coming winter, you will do me a thing most welcome indeed.

Apparatus

  • Recipient. Thaddaeus ab Hayck / Thaddaeus Pragensis = Tadeáš Hájek z Hájku (Thaddaeus Hagecius ab Hayck) (1525–1600), the leading Bohemian physician, astronomer and astrologer, personal physician at the Imperial court at Prague, and Tycho's long-standing friend and correspondent. The pair had met at the Imperial Diet of Regensburg (Ratisbona) in 1575, twice referenced here. Hagecius was Tycho's principal channel to the Imperial chancery; this letter sets in motion the application that yielded the printing Privilege Rudolf II granted Tycho.
  • Salutation rubric (German). HERRN DOCTOR THADAeUS ZU PRAG ZUERFRAGEN — "Doctor Thaddaeus at Prague, to be enquired for" — a German address/direction line over the Latin honorific superscription; a delivery instruction (the bearer is to ask after the addressee at Prague). The code-switch is preserved by this chapter's convention
  • Steno Brahe. Steen Brahe (1547–1620), Tycho's brother, here returning from a royal embassy to the Imperial court; he had carried Hagecius's letter back to Hven.
  • Serenissimo Rege nostro — Frederik II of Denmark (r. 1559–88), "our Most Serene King."
  • Dialexis — Hagecius's Dialexis de novae et prius incognitae stellae … apparitione (Frankfurt, 1574), his tract on the 1572 New Star, here described as "enlarged anew" (a revised/expanded version Hagecius was preparing). Kept as the title; not translated.
  • Nova Stella … aliquot Cometis — the New Star (the 1572 nova, stella nova in Cassiopeia) and the comets of the intervening years (notably the comet of 1577); Tycho is gathering the published responses for his own work in progress. De novis aethereae Regionis Generationibus (below) is the project — eventually the Astronomiae instauratae Progymnasmata and the comet volume.
  • collimatius — "more nearly on the mark"; collimare (the period orthography of collineare, to aim/sight along a line) is Tycho's habitual metaphor of taking aim at the "goal (mark) of truth" (veritatis metam). Rendered with the aiming sense; the Latin flagged.
  • Paper-supply passage. The shortage is tied to two datable events of 1585: the Spanish seizure (arrestatio) of foreign (esp. Dutch) merchant ships in Spanish ports (May 1585) and the long siege of Antwerp (taken by Parma, 17 August 1585). Telonium Regium … Helsingorae = the Sound Toll at Helsingør (Elsinore); its praefectus (warden/customs-master) is procuring Dutch paper for Tycho.
  • Alchemy / chemistry. Spagyricae praeparationes = spagyric preparations (Paracelsian chemical pharmacy); Pyronomica ars = "the pyronomic art," the art of regulated fire (furnace-work), rendered with a gloss; Officina Hermetica = "Hermetic Workshop" (Tycho's alchemical laboratory at Uraniborg); Laboratorium … subterraneum = the underground laboratory. fabricando … fabri fimus — "by making we become makers," a proverb (a variant of fabricando fit faber / fabricando fabri fimus); kept as the maxim it is. vaniloque (for vaniloquae / vaingloriously) describes the boasting of common alchemists.
  • Books sought. Iohannis Werneri opusculum de motu octavae sphaerae = Johann Werner, De motu octavae sphaerae (Nuremberg, 1522), on the motion of the eighth (fixed-star) sphere — i.e. precession/trepidation. Commentaria Trapezuntij in Ptolomaeum = the commentary of Georgius Trapezuntius (George of Trebizond, 1395–1472/3) on Ptolemy's Almagest; Tycho offers to print it at his own expense if Hagecius will release the manuscript. tractatus … de Directionum significationibus — an anonymous manuscript treatise "On the Significations of Directions" (directio = a predictive astrological technique, per glossary), which Tycho had seen in Hagecius's keeping at Regensburg.
  • Instruments / observatory. Organa maxima = "the greatest instruments" (per glossary; the great Uraniborg instruments); Typographia … Uraniburgi = Tycho's own press at Uraniborg; aedes Uraniae = the house "dedicated to Urania" (Uraniborg, lit. "castle of Urania," the Muse of astronomy); sacra litasti echoes the Tychonic flourish "devote yourself to the rites of Urania". The "two Instruments" whose pictures he encloses, and the peculiare volumen on the structure and use of all his instruments, anticipate the Astronomiae instauratae Mechanica (1598).
  • Astronomiam … pristino vigori restituunt — "restore the whole of Astronomy to its former vigour": Tycho's programme of instauratio / restitutio astronomiae (glossary), rendered with "restore … to its former vigour" / "restoration of Astronomy."
  • The Privilege request (legal core). The operative clause is a single negative prohibition: Tycho asks that nequaquam liceat … denuo imprimere vel publicare et divendere — "that it be by no means permitted … to reprint anew, or to publish and sell" — his works, within ten years (intra decennium), anywhere in the Empire, sub certa mulcta et amissione exemplarium ("under a fixed fine and forfeiture of the copies"). One prohibition, governing the triad reprint / publish / sell; reproduced as one negation. His reciprocal undertaking is itself negative — Cavebo … ne libri … quidpiam … contineant ("I shall take care that the books contain nothing …") — a second, distinct negation, kept as such. No conditional is reversed or merged.
  • turpisque proprijs (textual crux). The witness reads "a me elaboratos turpisque proprijs in hac mea Officina Uraniburgica excusos." As transcribed, turpisque ("and base/shameful") is unconstruable here and is almost certainly a copyist's corruption of typisque propriis ("and [printed] with my own types") — exactly the phrase Tycho uses twice elsewhere in this same letter (proprijs typis … edere; proprijs typis excudere). The translation follows the evident reading "printed with my own types"; the corrupt printed form is retained in the Source block by this chapter's convention(2). The MS is a copy (Afskrift), consistent with a scribal slip.
  • Heinrich Brucaeus. Doctor Henricus Brucaeus = Heinrich Brucaeus (1530/31–1593), professor of medicine and mathematics at Rostock (Rostochium); Tycho proposes him as the postal relay, since ships ply frequently between Rostock and Hven.
  • Dateline / calendar. Datae Uraniburgi 25. Augusti Anno 1585 = "Given at Uraniborg, 25 August 1585" (O.S. — Denmark kept the Julian calendar until 1700). The dateline matches the catalogue label (1585.25.august.Uraniborg); no discrepancy. manu propria — "with his own hand," the autograph subscription.
  • Greek / symbols. None present (no Greek interpolation, no planetary or zodiac glyphs in this letter).
  • §1 Dreyer collation. Closing postscript (after "manu propria"), restored from Dreyer VII, p. 97 (dropped by the working transcription).
