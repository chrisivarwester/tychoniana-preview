The original

[TYCHO BRAHE AD JACOBUM BORDINGUM].

MAGNIFICE et excellentissime vir, amice inprimis honorande. Scripsj bis T:æ M:æ post initium huius annj de pecunia, quæ isthic mihj loco jnteresse debetur, primo per Claudium Mulæum meum domesticum in Daniam abeuntem vere præterito, postea per quendam magistrum Christiernum Longimontanum, natione Danum, quj etiam aliquamdiu mecum fuit et superiore julio se in patriam recepit. Et sperassem sane, me aliquid inter id temporis responsj recepturum, quod tamen factum non est. Quare cum hic tabellarius Rostochium iturus sit et confecto itinere huc Pragam rediturus, non potuj intermittere quin T:am M:am de hoc ipso negotio denuo commonefacerem. Quare te per amicitiam nostram iam tertium rogatum uolo, ut tuo consilio et authoritate interueniente procurare non degraueris, ut pecunia illa, quæ a juniorum principum quæstore mihi isthic soluenda restat, quamprimum fierj possit, huc mittatur siue in aurea siue etiam in argentea moneta, pro ut antea ratione mittendj insinuauj, et si id prius indicato modo per aurigam Wittebergensem efficj nequierit, offeret se certa et tuta occasio per mag: dn: J. Godelmannum, quj hic legatum illustrissimj administratoris electoratus Saxonicj agit, post quatuordecim dies (utj herj mihj retulit) hinc Rostochium proficiscj cogitantem, quj etiam breuj huc reuertj necesse habet, cumque ipse sit amicus meus singularis, eiusque perspecta fides, ab illo obtinuj, ut hoc officium in adferenda secum illa quæ istic mihi numeranda est pecunia in se recipiat, quare illj optime committj poterit. Et si jnteresse pro toto hoc anno usque ad subsequentis primum diem aprilis aut ad minimum huius dimidij elapsi annj una addatur, esset id mihi gratum, nam et æquum uidetur ut si quidem iam per dimidium annum pecunia illa frustrer, alterius dimidij annj solutionis præueniente termino iste defectus compensetur, præsertim ex quo tam oportuna et indubia ratio id quicquid est pecuniarum huc transuehendj per dictum d: d: Godelmannum sese obtulerit. Quare non dubito, præstantissime d:ne Bordinge, quin mihi hac in parte et interueniente principis tuj (si opus fuerit) autoritate sis operam tuam non denegaturus; ubj ille accesserit, secum meum habebit chyrographum ulterius adhuc quam antea factum de solutione testans. Rogo autem peramanter, ut cum hoc tabellario ad nos reuertente pauculis indicare uelis, quid mihi isthic expectandum sit. Ego T:æ M:æ uicissim gratificandum perpetuo inueniar quam paratissimus.

Vale.
Dabantur Pragæ Bohemorum die 16. septembris stylo correcto anno 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

[Tycho Brahe to Jacob Bording.]

Magnificent and most excellent man, friend especially to be honoured. I have written twice to Your Magnificence since the beginning of this year about the money owed me there in place of interest — first by Claude Mule, my servant, going into Denmark last spring; afterward by a certain Master Christen Longomontanus, a Dane by nation, who was also for some time with me and last July betook himself to his homeland. And I had indeed hoped that I would receive some reply in that interval, which, however, has not happened. Wherefore, since this courier is about to go to Rostock and, his journey done, to return hither to Prague, I could not refrain from reminding Your Magnificence once more of this very business. So I would have you asked, now for the third time, by our friendship, that — with your counsel and authority intervening — you would not find it a burden to procure that that money, which remains to be paid me there by the treasurer of the younger princes, be sent hither as soon as may be, whether in gold or also in silver coin, as I earlier intimated regarding the means of sending. And if it cannot be done beforehand in the manner indicated, by the Wittenberg carter, there will offer itself a sure and safe occasion through Master Lord J. Godelmann — who acts here as legate of the most illustrious Administrator of the Saxon Electorate, and who is minded (as he told me yesterday) to set out hence to Rostock after fourteen days, and who must also return hither shortly; and since he is himself my singular friend, and his fidelity proven, I have obtained from him that he take this office upon himself, in bringing with him that money which is to be paid me there — wherefore it may best be committed to him. And if interest for this whole year, up to the first day of next April, or at the least for this half-year now elapsed, be added in together [with the principal], it would be welcome to me; for it seems but fair that, since I am now cheated of that money for half a year, this shortfall be made good by anticipating the term of the other half-year's payment — especially now that so opportune and undoubted a means of conveying hither whatever money there is has offered itself through the said Lord Doctor Godelmann. Wherefore I do not doubt, most excellent Lord Bording, that you will not deny me your effort in this part — with your prince's authority intervening too, if there be need. When he [the messenger] arrives, he will have with him my bond, attesting the payment, drawn yet further than before. And I ask most lovingly that, by this courier returning to us, you would indicate in a few words what I am to expect there. I, in turn, shall be found most ready to oblige Your Magnificence perpetually.

Farewell.
Given at Prague of the Bohemians, the 16th of September, by the corrected style, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender & matter. Tycho Brahe at Prague — a third dunning letter over interest owed him by the Mecklenburg house (here "the treasurer of the younger princes," i.e. the under-age Dukes of Mecklenburg), the same capital whose recovery dominates the 1601 Meier/Duke-Ulrich correspondence. This 1600 letter shows Tycho still trying to get the interest remitted, a year before the principal dispute boiled over.
  • Recipient. Jacob Bording (Jakob Bording the Younger), a councillor/physician in Mecklenburg service, used by Tycho as an intermediary of standing (he can invoke "your prince's authority") to chase the payment.
  • Persons named.
  • Claudius Mulaeus = Claude Mule (Mulaeus), Tycho's servant, an earlier letter-carrier to Denmark.
  • magister Christiernus Longimontanus = Christen Sørensen Longomontanus, Tycho's chief assistant, who carried the second letter and returned to Denmark in July 1600.
  • mag. dn. J. Godelmannus = Johann Georg Gödelmann (Godelmann) (1559–1611), jurist and diplomat, here legate of the Administrator of the Saxon Electorate at the imperial court, and "my singular friend" — engaged by Tycho as a trusted bearer of the cash on his coming journey to Rostock. (Gödelmann recurs across the late correspondence as a recurring-person to be rendered consistently, this chapter's convention)
  • illustrissimus Administrator Electoratus Saxonici = the Administrator (regent) of Electoral Saxony, Friedrich Wilhelm I of Saxe-Weimar, regent for the minor Elector Christian II.
  • The financial ask. Payment in gold or silver coin; the Wittenberg carter as fallback route, Gödelmann as the preferred one; interest to be reckoned to 1 April next, or at least for the half-year elapsed, the shortfall offset by anticipating the next term; Tycho's fresh bond (chirographum) to be handed over on receipt.
  • Place. Praga Bohemorum = Prague; Rostochium = Rostock; Wittenberga = Wittenberg.
  • Witness note. A letter draft in the Basel autograph collection; hence the bracketed editorial heading.
  • Epistolary formulae & abbreviations. T. M. = Tua Magnificentia ("Your Magnificence"); d. d. = Dominus Doctor; Vale → "Farewell"; Dabantur (epistolary) → "Given."
  • Calendar (this chapter's convention — idiom). Dateline die 16. Septembris stylo correcto = "by the corrected style," another of Tycho's labels for New Style (Gregorian) (alongside stylo novo, stylo recognito; cf. brahe-t_16000420001). Reproduced literally and glossed, not converted.
