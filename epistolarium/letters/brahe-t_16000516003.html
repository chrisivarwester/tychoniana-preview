The original

CLARISSIMO ET PRÆSTANTISSIMO VIRO, DOMINO DANIELI BUCRETIO, PHILOSOPHIAe ET MEDICINAe DOCTORI EXIMIO VRATISLAVIAe DEGENTI, AMICO SUO INPRIMIS DILECTO.

S. P.

CLARISSIME et praestantissime vir, vetus illa inter nos, cum me in Insula prioris habitationis Uraniburgica, dum in iuventute peregrinareris, semel atque iterum invisisti, contracta notitia, cuius te adhuc memorem esse non dubito, efficit, ut eo liberius hoc tempore ad te scribam, praesertim cum aliquid inciderit, in quo tua opera mihi usui esse possit, quod et te mihi non denegaturum, singularis tua humanitas et erga me, prout a M. CHRISTIERNO LONGOMONTANO, qui ante biennium apud vos fuit, et nunc apud me est, intellexi, benevolum studium, alias etiam mihi perspectum, fidem facit. Est autem eiuscemodi.

Retulit mihi priori anno excellentiss. vir, Dominus D. HIERONIMUS TREUTLERUS, cum simul Pragae essemus, Vratislavij aliquando fuisse Astronomiae vel etiam Astrologiae iudiciariae impense addictum virum, quem observationes quasdam in motu Astrorum obtinuisse aiebat, quas etiamnum filius eius secum reservârit. Quare te per veterem nostram amicitiam rogatum cuperem, ut a filio illo vel alijs haeredibus mihi observationum illarum, quotquot adhuc in promptu sunt, copiam impetrare velis. Etsi enim vix credere queam, eas ad tantam praecisionem deductas esse, quantam ego in talibus requiro, et rei inquirendae subtilitas per se postulat, cum idoneis organis proculdubio destitutus fuerit, tamen qualescunque tandem sunt, eas obtinere averem. Est aliquid prodire tenus, si non datur ultra. Si itaque id, quicquid est observationum, ad mundum accurate descriptum, tua intercessione et diligentia consecutus fuero, officium, quovis alio, quantum per me licet, compensandum, mihi hac ratione praestiteris. Vale. Plura enim ob aliarum occupationum molem nunc non vacat. Salutat te supradictus M. CHRISTIERNUS officiose, et pro pristinis illi, cum apud vos esset, beneficijs, non saltem is, sed et ego, gratias agimus. Scripturus est ipsemet tibi, cum rectius vacârit.

Iterum quam felicissime vale.
Datae ex arce Caesarea Benatika 16 Maij A. 1600.

English translation machine translation, Secure

To the most renowned and excellent man, Lord Daniel Bucretius, distinguished doctor of philosophy and medicine, dwelling at Breslau, his especially beloved friend.

Heartiest greeting.

Most renowned and excellent Sir: that old acquaintance struck up between us — when, travelling in your youth, you visited me once and again on the island of my former dwelling, Uraniborg — of which I do not doubt you are still mindful, makes me write to you the more freely at this time, especially since something has arisen in which your service may be of use to me; and that you will not deny me this, your singular kindness, and the well-disposed zeal toward me (as I have understood from Master Christen Longomontanus, who was with you two years ago and is now with me), elsewhere too well known to me, give me assurance. The matter is of this kind.

Last year the most excellent man, Lord Doctor Hieronymus Treutler, related to me, when we were together at Prague, that there had once been at Breslau a man earnestly devoted to Astronomy — or even to judicial Astrology — who, he said, had obtained certain observations in the motion of the stars, which his son still keeps by him. Wherefore I should wish to ask you, by our old friendship, that you would obtain for me from that son, or from the other heirs, a copy of those observations, as many as are still at hand. For although I can scarcely believe that they were brought to such precision as I require in such matters, and as the delicacy of the inquiry itself demands — since he was doubtless destitute of suitable instruments — yet, whatever in the end they are, I should be eager to obtain them. It is something to advance so far, if it is not granted [to go] beyond. If therefore I shall, through your intercession and diligence, come into possession of whatever there is of these observations, accurately copied out fair, you will have rendered me by this means a service to be repaid by any other, so far as lies in my power. Farewell — for more there is now no leisure, owing to the mass of other occupations. The aforesaid Master Christen greets you dutifully; and for the former kindnesses [shown] him while he was with you, not only he but I too render thanks. He will write to you himself when he has the better leisure.

Again, most happily, farewell.
Given from the Imperial castle of Benátky, the 16th of May, in the year 1600.

Apparatus

  • Sender. Tycho Brahe at Benátky nad Jizerou, shortly before the move to Prague; the letter belongs to his campaign to gather and absorb earlier observational data into his own restored astronomy.
  • Recipient. Daniel Bucretius, doctor of philosophy and medicine, Vratislaviae degens — resident at Breslau (Wrocław). Tycho recalls that Bucretius had, in his youthful travels, visited him more than once on Hven; he is used here as a local intermediary at Breslau.
  • Persons named.
  • M. Christiernus Longomontanus = Christen Sørensen Longomontanus (1562–1647), Tycho's longest-serving and most senior assistant, who had himself been at Breslau "two years ago" and is now back with Tycho; he both vouches for Bucretius's goodwill and adds his own greeting.
  • Dominus D. Hieronymus Treutlerus = Hieronymus Treutler (Treutler von Krottendorf) (1565–1607), jurist, the informant (met at Prague the previous year) for the report of the Breslau observer.
  • The unnamed Breslau astronomer/astrologer, Astronomiae vel etiam Astrologiae iudiciariae impense addictus, deceased, whose son (or other heirs) is said to retain his observationes in motu astrorum. He is not identified in the source; Tycho's request is precisely to recover whatever survives of his papers.
  • Astronomy. observationes … in motu astrorum = observations of the motion of the stars; praecisio = precision; idonea organa = suitable instruments (organa in Tycho's sense,) — the lack of which makes Tycho doubt the data's accuracy even as he seeks it. The Astronomia / Astrologia iudiciaria distinction (mathematical astronomy vs. judicial astrology) is kept as written.
  • Allusion. Est aliquid prodire tenus, si non datur ultra adapts Horace, Epistles I.1.32 (est quādam prodire tenus, si non datur ultra) — "it is something to advance so far, if to go further is not granted"; the literary tag is preserved and glossed, not paraphrased away.
  • Place. arx Caesarea Benatika = the Imperial castle of Benátky; Vratislavia = Breslau / Wrocław.
  • Epistolary formulae. S. P. = salutem plurimam ("heartiest greeting"). Vale … iterum quam felicissime vale → "Farewell … again, most happily, farewell." Datas (sc. literas, epistolary) → "Given."
  • Calendar. Dateline 16 Maij A. 1600; edition marks n.s. (New Style), consistent with the Benátky/Prague series of this month. Reproduced, not converted.
  • Correction. Latin: 'invasisti' corrected to 'invisisti', and 'Datas' corrected to 'Datae', per Dreyer, Opera Omnia VIII, pp. 323-324.

2 defects found:
- text error (medium). The transcribed original reads 'invasisti' (from invado, 'you invaded/attacked'), but Dreyer's print clearly shows 'invisisti' (from inviso, 'you visited') — two different verbs, not a spelling variant. The published English correctly follows Dreyer's 'invisisti' ('you visited'), so the translation itself is unaffected, but our stored Latin the transcribed original is corrupted relative to the edition of record. Dreyer prints: femel atque iterum invifisti (= invisisti) — p. 323, line 17.
- text error (low). The transcribed original reads 'Datas', Dreyer prints 'Datae' (the standard closing formula, agreeing with implied litterae). The English 'Given' is compatible with either reading, so no translation-level consequence, but it is a real divergence between the transcribed original and Dreyer. Dreyer prints: Datae ex arce Cæfarea Benatika 16 Maij A. 1600. — p. 324, line 4.

Repaired. Verified both defects by direct visual inspection of the Dreyer TBOO VIII pp.322-325 page images. 'invififti' (p.323) and 'Datae' (p.324) are both unambiguous.
