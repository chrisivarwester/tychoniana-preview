Where each document was printed, and where its text here comes from. tekstnet names for every document the print it took the text from: Dreyer’s Opera Omnia for most, but for 133 documents an earlier print — Tycho’s own Epistolae astronomicae of 1596, Friis’s two editions of the learned correspondence (1876–86 and 1900–09), Favaro’s Magini letters, and a scatter of seventeenth- and eighteenth-century collections. The summary table folds those names to their prints; the concordance gives every document its Dreyer volume and pages, its first print and its tekstnet page.

The Hven–Kassel exchange 47 by the print · 49 by correspondent

The exchange with Landgrave Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel and his astronomer Christoph Rothmann is the one part of his correspondence Tycho published himself, as Tychonis Brahe Dani Epistolarum astronomicarum libri, quorum primus (Uraniborg 1596; Nielsen 430), edited for print — passages cut, wording adjusted, the sequence arranged (Mosley, Jardine & Tybjerg 2003). Dreyer re-edited the book as Opera Omnia VI. Two definitions of the set therefore coexist and are tabled here: the letters whose source is the 1596 print, the source’s own unit; and the letters exchanged with Wilhelm IV, Rothmann and, after Wilhelm’s death, Landgrave Moritz, an editorial grouping that reaches into the exile years. The letters Tycho despatched to Kassel survive in the Hessisches Staatsarchiv Marburg, Best. 3 II Nr. 1070, the Landgrave’s correspondence with Tycho of 1585–1592, described with digital images in Arcinsys; which letters of the exchange the convolute holds has not been read letter by letter, and no witness line of the edition points to it, so it is stated here at convolute level and never as “this letter’s original”. The book itself is Typographica’s: its record and the census of its copies are linked.
