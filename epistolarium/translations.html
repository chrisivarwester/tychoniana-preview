10The translations and their checking

How the English was made and how far it has been checked. The translations were produced in 2026 by a large language model working from the transcribed originals, each with a self-assessment of its own reliability. An independent pass then read every translation against the original and, for 457 of the 508 documents, against the page images of Dreyer’s edition; that pass logged 289 defects, most of them in the transcribed Latin rather than in the English, and they were corrected. Its paragraph on each letter — which pages were read, what was found, what remains doubtful — is the warrant printed beneath the translation, and its grade is the one shown; the self-assessment is kept in the dataset but not printed, because its grade was revised on 214 documents and a reader should see one judgement, not two. An uncollated translation is graded at most Probable, whatever the checker thought of it. No human Latinist has reviewed any translation; a sampled review of the non-Secure letters is a desideratum (§11).

Conventions of the texts

The original is printed with tekstnet's ligatures (æ, œ) and the edition's paragraphing; where the two transcriptions of tekstnet's page disagree in a word the letter page lists both readings under the original. The English is printed without the translator's emphasis; its bracketed words are the translator's supplements and its bracketed queries the translator's doubts, both left as they stand. Greek is printed as the edition gives it.
