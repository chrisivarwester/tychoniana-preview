Epistolarium — The Letters of Tycho Brahe

Charter. Epistolarium is the reading edition and apparatus of Tycho Brahe’s correspondence as the standard corpus prints it: the letters and acts of the Danish Society for Language and Literature’s digital re-edition (tekstnet.dk, 2019) of J. L. E. Dreyer’s Opera Omnia VI–VIII and XIV. Each document is given in its original Latin, German or Danish beside an English translation made for this chapter by a language model and checked against Dreyer’s pages, with a one-sentence abstract, the parties identified against the Familia register, the place of writing, the date with its calendar, the edition of record by volume and page, the manuscript witnesses by kind, and its position in the exchange. It is the standard corpus, not the whole surviving correspondence: the letters published since Dreyer are listed, not edited, in §11.

What this chapter contains

The 508 documents here are the contents of Tycho Brahes Breve, the digital edition published by the Danish Society for Language and Literature at tekstnet.dk (redaktion Peter Zeeberg, 2019), which re-edits the letters printed by J. L. E. Dreyer in Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia: vol. VI (1919), the Epistolae astronomicae of 1596 re-edited; vol. VII (1924), the letters of 1568–1597; vol. VIII (1925), the letters of 1596–1601; and, from vol. XIV (1928), Epistolae et Acta, the letter-type documents the digital edition selected. Dreyer’s corpus is in substance the one F. R. Friis assembled between 1875 and 1909, with few additions (Norlind 1970, p. 175). The digital edition has 509 letter pages; two of them carry the same letter to Scaliger of 23 July 1600, which is counted once here. Of the 508, 500 are letters and 8 are acts of letter form — two printing privileges, a deed, three testimonials, a petition and a receipt — kept because the edition keeps them and marked as acts. Every document keeps its tekstnet identifier as its key and links to its tekstnet page, where the editors’ commentary and textual notes stand.

This is not the whole correspondence

It is the standard corpus. At least seven letters to or from Tycho and one letter about him have been published since Dreyer and are not in the edition: Tycho to Christoph Clavius, 5 January 1600 (Norlind 1954; Norlind 1970, Bilaga 9); Tycho to the Doge Marino Grimani, 28 November 1598 (Norlind 1970, Bilaga 7); the Liddel letters of 1587, the Ripensis letters of 1598, the Lund chapter’s letter of 1593 and Tengnagel’s letter to Rantzau of 1597 (Bilagor 2, 6, 4, 5); and the Danish documents in J. R. Christianson’s addenda to vol. XIV (Centaurus 16, 1972). They are listed with their prints and archives in §11, not edited. Whole correspondences are lost — Tycho’s letters to Vedel burned with Bishop Worm’s library in 1728, most of Rosenkrantz’s papers at Fredrikshald in 1826 — and no letter at all survives from 1569 or 1570 (Norlind 1970, pp. 174–175). No letter to or from Ursus, Wittich or Mercator exists; the Ursus quarrel ran in print. The digital edition’s own overview speaks of 350 letters, and Early Modern Letters Online catalogues the same printed corpus as 505; the differences are enclosures, testimonials, declarations and receipts counted or not, and the definitions table below says exactly what is counted here.

The texts

The Latin, German and Danish texts are tekstnet’s, read in 2026 against the page images of Dreyer’s printed volumes; defects of the first transcription were corrected, and every document whose printed words differ from a second transcription of the same tekstnet page lists both readings beneath the original on its letter page (80 documents; Dreyer’s page decides, and where the audit paragraph does not name the word that check has not yet been made). Dreyer’s volume and page are cited on every record, with a link to the page in the facsimile tekstnet serves. The 47 letters of the Hven–Kassel exchange survive only as Tycho printed them in his Epistolarum astronomicarum liber primus of 1596, edited by him for publication (Mosley, Jardine & Tybjerg 2003); their text is that of the print as Dreyer reprinted it, no manuscript is cited for them because the edition cites none, and the letters Tycho despatched to Kassel, which survive at Marburg (Hessisches Staatsarchiv, Best. 3 II Nr. 1070), are not collated here.

The English translations

The English was produced for this chapter in 2026 by a large language model (Anthropic’s Claude) and is not the work of a human translator; no Latinist has reviewed it. Every translation was then read against the original in an independent pass, and 457 of the 508 documents were collated against the page images of Dreyer’s edition; the 289 defects that pass found — most of them in the transcribed originals rather than in the English — were corrected. 51 documents, 47 of them the Kassel letters of Opera Omnia VI, which the digital edition does not paginate, have not been so collated; each says so, and none is graded higher than Probable. The grade beside each translation — Secure, Probable, Possible — is the checker’s judgement of that translation’s fidelity to Dreyer’s printed text, given with its grounds on the letter page; it grades the English, not the Latin, the date, the attribution or the manuscript, and “Secure” means that a machine check against the page found no defect, not that a scholar has read it. Quote the original, not the English.

What the counts count

Direction follows the edited parties: 283 documents issued by Tycho, 203 addressed to him, 22 letters between other people, indexed under both. The receipt of 29 May 1601, whose addressee the digital edition leaves blank, is counted as addressed to him; the partition by tekstnet’s raw fields is given in the table for reconciliation with earlier citations. A correspondent is an entry in the Familia register — a person, a body such as the University of Copenhagen, or a group of unnamed recipients — reached through 149 name-forms of the edition; there are 138 such entries, and 4 documents have no addressee. A manuscript witness is one line of the edition’s own apparatus, whatever the manuscript is — an autograph, the sender’s draft, a contemporary copy or an eighteenth-century transcript; §5 separates the kinds by the edition’s wording, 146 documents survive only in copies, and “in manuscript” never means “in autograph”. The edition’s sigla A, B, C order the witnesses and have nothing to do with the grade words. A place of writing is the place named in the dateline, not the correspondent’s residence. The three periods are the editor’s, cut at 1 January 1577 and at Tycho’s departure from Hven on 29 April 1597.

Dates and calendars

Dates stand as the edition gives them, with the calendar the dateline names, [O.S.] or [N.S.]; the calendar conventions are stated in §8. 26 documents are dated only to a year or a month and are printed so. Where this chapter dates a letter otherwise than tekstnet’s catalogue line (23 documents), both values and the basis are given on the record and in §8.

What the numbers are not

508 is the size of one edition, not the number of letters Tycho wrote or received; the letters per year measure what survives and what Friis and Dreyer printed. A correspondent’s count is not the size of the exchange. A repository’s count is of witness lines, including later copies. No figure on this page is a survival rate. Identity and biography are Familia’s, the itinerary is Loca’s, the printed books are Typographica’s; this chapter links to them.

How to read this chapter

The catalogue (§2) is one row per document, sortable and filterable, with the whole table downloadable as a spreadsheet. The register (§3) gives every document a record card in date order — parties, place, date, abstract, incipit, edition of record, witnesses, position in the exchange, translation grade — and each card leads to the letter’s own page, where the original stands above or beside the English with the apparatus and the audit’s warrant. The indexes (§4–§9) re-project the same records by correspondent, by manuscript, by print, by place, by year and by subject; the translations and their checking have their own section (§10); what is missing or doubtful is listed in §11; every source is described in §12. Each record prints its key, the tekstnet identifier (brahe-t_15680114001), and is addressable: #brahe-t_15680114001 as an anchor on this page, letters/brahe-t_15680114001.html for the letter. All 508 texts in one file, for searching the whole corpus without script, are at all.html.
