Nativity of Prince Christian, the later Christian IV (1577)

1 July 1577NativitysurvivingAHORO_C4

The nativity Tycho cast at King Frederik II's command for the prince born on 12 April 1577, subscribed on Hven on 1 July 1577 and delivered at Kronborg the same day. A full genethliacal treatise: the astronomical section with the figure of the birth, then the judgment by topic — temperament, the course of life, honours, journeys, marriage, death — with a German interpretation for the queen, as all three princely nativities carry. It survives in more copies than any other document of the register, all but one scribal: Tycho's own first draft, never finished, is among his papers in Vienna, and the copy handed to the king is lost. The Copenhagen copies call the subject king, a title he took only in 1588, so they were written after that year.

Nativity of Prince Ulrik (1579)

May 1579NativitysurvivingAHORO_ULRIK

The nativity of the king's second son, born on 30 December 1578, worked out by royal command and presented in May 1579 — in the very volume handed to the king: a small quarto of some three hundred pages in green velvet with gilt edges, wholly in Tycho's hand. Its plan is that of the Christian nativity: the astronomical section, then the judgment on body, mind and fortune, then the German interpretation.

Nativity of Prince Hans (1583)

1583NativitysurvivingAHORO_HANS

The nativity of the king's third son, prepared at the royal command in 1583, with a Latin preface that defends astrological prediction as physically grounded conjecture leaving free will intact. It survives in the presentation volume itself, in green velvet like its companion for Prince Ulrik. Which part is in Tycho's hand is contested between the two authorities this edition has: Dreyer, who had the volume in hand, says only the German interpretation is his; the facsimile autopsy of the Graphonomicon finds the Latin chart, directions and judgment in his italic and leaves the German Kurrent open. Both are printed; neither is resolved here.

Judgment on a comet A judgment on what a comet portends.

The German tract on the comet of 1577, with its astrological signification

1577–1578Judgment on a cometsurvivingAHORO_COMETA_1577_DE

Beside the Latin book on the comet of 1577 Tycho wrote a short tract in German for general readers, to answer the many foolish German pamphlets on the comet, and it is here, not in the Latin book, that he discusses what the comet signified. He never sent it to press. Two manuscripts survive among his papers: his own draft, which breaks off before the end, and a servant's copy, differing enough in wording that Dreyer thought it written from dictation; Dreyer printed the copy and corrected it from the draft.

Weather astrology Tycho's own astrologia meteorologica: the planetary configurations of the year read for the weather, in an almanac or a treatise.

The astrological and meteorological diary for 1573

December 1572Weather astrologysurvivingAHORO_DIARIUM_1573

The almanac Tycho drew up for 1573 at Herrevad: figures of the heavens for the solstices, equinoxes and lunations, each judged for the coming weather, opening with a hard winter forecast from the solstice of December 1572. It is the only such diary of his that survives; the annual almanacs he later sent to Frederik II are all lost. He withheld it from the printed book on the new star, whose preface discusses it, and it was never printed before Dreyer. It survives in one manuscript, the presentation booklet as Tycho assembled it — the preface, the tract on the new star, the diary, the tract on the lunar eclipse and the elegy to Urania — of which only the eclipse tract is in his hand.

Fragment of a treatise on weather astrology

undatedWeather astrologysurvivingAHORO_ASTROLOGIA_METEOROLOGICA

The opening of a treatise on weather astrology, addressed to all its students wherever they may be, arguing that predictions of the heavens' influence on the elementary world cannot reach the certainty of astronomy, and quoting Cardano on the greater sureness of particular over universal predictions. It breaks off in the middle of the third page; the paper is torn and damp-damaged. In Tycho's own hand, among his papers in Vienna, and first printed by Dreyer.

Star apparatus keyed to a nativity A one-member class, named for what the one document is: a table of fixed-star positions computed for the Emperor's own nativity and appended to the star catalogue presented at his court.

The appendix to the star catalogue keyed to Rudolf II's nativity (1600)

March 1600Star apparatus keyed to a nativitysurvivingAHORO_RUDOLF_STAR_APPENDIX

A supplement to the restored catalogue of a thousand fixed stars: thirty-six star positions adapted to the Emperor's own nativity, so that the new astronomy could be read into his horoscope — an act of court allegiance written into a scientific instrument. It exists in one copy only, the presentation volume of the catalogue that Tycho dedicated in his own hand to Rudolf Coraduz, the imperial vice-chancellor, in March 1600; the two companion volumes of the catalogue lack it. Dreyer printed it from this codex alone.

The Leipzig book of nativities, with Caspar Peucer's geniture — lost

c. 1564Nativityattested, lostBHORO_LOST_PEUCER

As a student at Leipzig Tycho kept a book of nativities. Writing to Peucer in September 1588 he reports finding in it, beside the scheme of Peucer's own geniture, the note he had made some twenty-four years before — Mercury in Aquarius in the third house, the Moon in Virgo in the ninth: a sharp and profound mind, and the misfortune of exile or prison that Peucer had since suffered. In the same letter he says he had been given to such predictions in his youth and had set them aside as his judgment grew and he saw the planets' places in the tables were wrong. The book still existed in 1588; it has not been located.

Tycho's nativity of himself — lost

before 1601Nativityattested, lostBHORO_LOST_SELF

Tycho cast his own nativity; it does not survive. The notes Stephanius copied from it give the birth moment to the minute, 10.47 in the morning of 14 December 1546, which modern biography prints beside the father's memorandum. Von Späth erected the chart of 'Apollo' in Urania Titani from this recorded moment, so that reconstruction is not evidence for the lost document.

The annual almanacs for Frederik II — lost

1570s–1587Weather astrologyattested, lostBHORO_LOST_ALMANACS

Among Tycho's duties to Frederik II was an annual almanac: a letter of the king dated 24 September 1587 reminds him to send the usual almanac for the coming year by the bearer, or as soon as it is ready. Dreyer, who knew the 1573 diary, states that none of the later diaries sent to the king is preserved, and that they must have been much shorter, giving only the planetary aspects' significations that touched the king. The 1573 diary is the sole survivor of the kind.
