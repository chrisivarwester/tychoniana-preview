9Related: the astrological record outside this register

Everything astrological that has a better home than this register, with the reason. Five kinds of remainder: the texts Tycho printed, which are Scripta’s; the weather diary, which is Diarium’s; the two printed almanacs, which are Typographica’s and the censuses’; the poem Urania Titani with the four nativities modern scholarship has computed from it; and the letters in which nativities were asked for and judged. Last, one manuscript in Tycho’s own bundle that the library attributes to a pupil. Each row keeps the key the old register gave it, so that no link into this page dies.

His printed astrology texts Tycho printed, or that Dreyer printed from drafts of printed texts — Scripta's

De nova stella (1573): the astrological judgment on the new star and the preface on diaries

BR_DE_NOVA_STELLA
Home
Scripta — the printed text with the Danish translation
Why not a record here
printed by Tycho in 1573; a printed text is Scripta's atom. Its 'Astrologicum iudicium' on the star's effects and its preface, which explains the diary of 1573 and attacks the almanac-makers, are the printed face of the practice this register documents in manuscript.
What is known
Opera Omnia I, the judgment p. 34, the preface pp. 35–44, the eclipse tract pp. 45–64.
References
Opera Omnia I, pp. 34–64

The two commentaries on the comet of 1585, with their astrological conjectures

BR_DE_COMETA_1585
Home
Scripta — both treatises with the Danish translation
Why not a record here
the first was printed in Morsing's 1586 almanac under Morsing's name, the second Dreyer restored from Tycho's own drafts; both are texts and Scripta's. Their drafts are the largest block of astrological writing in Tycho's hand among the Vienna papers.
What is known
Opera Omnia IV, pp. 397–414. Dreyer's notes (pp. 512–515) place the drafts in Cod. 10689: fascicles 23, 27, 30 and 44–45 (foll. 15–24) for the first treatise, and 18, 11 and 26 for the second, 'tota ipsius Tychonis manu scripta'.
References
Opera Omnia IV, pp. 397–414, 512–515

'Quae genera eventuum portendat crinita a. 1572' — Cod. 10689/41–46, fol. 21r–24v

BR_COMETA_10689_41_46
Home
Scripta — the commentaries on the comet of 1585, whose drafts Dreyer places on these leaves
Why not a record here
the old register entered it as a judgment on the new star of 1572, on the strength of the title the Tabulae codicum gave the item. The library's record names no author. The leaves it occupies, fol. 21r–24v of the bundle, are among those Dreyer assigns to Tycho's drafts of the 1585 comet commentary (foll. 15–24 of fascicles 44–45). A witness of a printed text belongs to Scripta; the identification is stated as open until the leaves are read.
What is known
Vienna, ÖNB Cod. 10689/41–46, a bundle of 28 leaves; the item at fol. 21r–24v, Latin, undated. Not digitised.

The Uraniborg weather diary the subject of its own pillar

The Uraniborg weather diary, 1582–1597

METEO_DIARIUM
Home
Diarium — the whole diary as a scholarly edition: the day-by-day weather series and the historical notes, with the Vienna codex and its facsimile
Why not a record here
it is the subject of its own pillar, and it is not in Tycho's hand: written on Hven by his assistants, who show no trace of his hand in it (Friis 1876). The old register counted it among his autograph documents.
What is known
Vienna, ÖNB Cod. 10718: 191 leaves in quarto, 1 October 1582 to 22 April 1597; digitised. Edited by F. R. Friis in 1876 and again in Opera Omnia IX, pp. 1–146.
Links

The almanacs from the Hven press printed editions — Typographica's, with their censused copies

Morsing's Diarium astrologicum et meteorologicum for 1586, the first book from the Hven press

METEO_MORSING
Home
Typographica — the edition's bibliographical record after Nielsen, its press and its censused copies
Why not a record here
a printed book by Tycho's assistant Elias Olsen Morsing, issued under Tycho's supervision with a preface he drafted; a printed edition is Typographica's atom, and its surviving copies are the census's. The old register listed it with no surviving copies, which read as if none survived.
What is known
Nielsen no. 1170. Dedicated 1 January 1586, the calendar printed late in 1585. Nielsen names some twenty holding libraries; the census records twenty copies, fifteen of them located. Tycho's draft preface is readable in Scripta.
Links
The census of copies Tycho's draft preface — Scripta
References
Nielsen 1931, no. 1170; Opera Omnia IX, pp. 149–150

The poem Urania Titani, and the nativities computed from it a poem edited in Scripta; the charts are modern reconstructions

Urania Titani (1594): the poem in which four nativities are set

BR_URANIA_TITANI
Home
Scripta — the six hundred lines with Zeeberg's Danish translation
Why not a record here
a poem, edited in Scripta and catalogued among the never-printed works in Typographica. The four horoscopes its allusions imply were computed by Ove von Späth in 1994; they are tabled below as reconstructions and counted nowhere. The old register entered them as four documents in four manuscripts, which counted this one codex four times.
What is known
Vienna, ÖNB Cod. 10686/12: ten leaves in quarto, Latin, catalogued under Tycho's name — the poem's one manuscript witness.
Links
The catalogue record of the manuscript data.onb.ac.at Among the never-printed works — Typographica Zeeberg's edition as an episode of reception — Receptio
References
Zeeberg 1994, pp. 311–321; ÖNB catalogue, AC13961756

In the Vienna papers, not by Tycho catalogued under another hand

A pupil's draft of a nativity in German — Cod. 10689/36

HORO_ONB_36
Why not a record here
the library catalogues it as 'Primae lineae germanicae alicuius genethliaci a quodam Tychonis Brahe discipulo' — the first draft of some nativity by a pupil of Tycho's. The old register entered it as a nativity of Tycho's of uncertain attribution; the catalogue's attribution is to someone else, and the inclusion rule excludes it. It stays here as the labelled remainder, in the same bundle as his papers.
What is known
Vienna, ÖNB Cod. 10689/36: 37 leaves, sixteenth century; digitised in full.
