Horoscopi — Astrological Work

Tycho Brahe’s astrological practice — 15 documents: royal nativities, astrological judgments, a sixteen-year weather diary, and verse horoscopes — surviving in 18 manuscript witnesses across 15 manuscripts and two printed works; two wholly and one partly in Tycho’s own hand

A catalogue of Tycho’s astrological documents — the royal nativities he cast as a duty of his court appointment, his annual prognostications, and the horoscopes embedded in verse. Each entry gives the subject, the year it was cast, whether it survives in Tycho’s own hand, and where its manuscript copies are held; search or filter by type below.

Tycho Brahe’s astrological practice fell into five distinct modes — each with its own audience, function, and method. Most Renaissance astronomers treated celestial observation and astrological interpretation as inseparable; Tycho was no exception. The 15 documents gathered here span the main forms that practice took: private birth charts for the Danish royal family, a meteorological diary kept for sixteen years on Hven, a direct response to an imperial question, and technically-computed horoscopes embedded in a Latin poem.
Natal Horoscopes5 works

A birth chart cast for a named individual — the configuration of planets at the moment of birth read to characterise destiny, temperament, and life's course. The three royal nativities were court commissions — two survive as presentation objects bound in green velvet; two further nativities, subjects unidentified, survive among the Prague papers in Vienna.

Astrological Judgments2 works

A specific question put to Tycho and answered astrologically — the judicial mode, which reads planetary configurations to advise on outcomes or interpret portents of particular events. The two surviving judgments concern the new star (or comet) of 1572 — the ÖNB title reads crinita — and a direct question from Rudolf II.

Mundane Astrology1 work

The application of planetary positions to public affairs and imperial concerns — astrology at the scale of kingdoms rather than individuals. Tycho's sole mundane document is a unique appendix to his 1,004-star catalogue, adapting the stellar restitution to Rudolf II's imperial nativity: a deliberate act of court allegiance written into a scientific instrument.

Meteorological Astrology3 works

The systematic correlation of planetary configurations with atmospheric conditions — Tycho's longest-running astrological practice, kept daily at Uraniborg 1582–97. The three works span the Hven period: his own autograph observation diary (the longest-running); Morsing's annual almanac for 1586, the first book off the Hven press; and Flemløs's Danish-language Astrologia (1591), the only vernacular book the Uraniborg press ever produced.

Literary Horoscopes4 works

Technically-computed birth charts embedded as fictional moments in the Latin elegiac poem (a heroide, after Ovid's Heroides) Urania Titani (1594): the poem's characters are defined by their planetary configurations, and the argument turns on what the stars reveal. Urania (Sophie Brahe) examines Titan's (Erik Lange's) nativity to find proof of his constancy — and the very planets she invokes simultaneously expose his instability. Attributed to Tycho; the largest and most ambitious of his Latin poems (Zeeberg).
