Receptio — The Reception of Tycho Brahe

Charter. Receptio records how Tycho Brahe’s work and person were received, used and remembered — by his contemporaries from the new star of 1572 onward, and after his death in 1601 by the astronomers who inherited his data, the editors who printed it, the institutions that claimed him and the sciences that still use his numbers. Each record is one documented episode, with its evidence grade, its sources at the cited page, and a link to the object or the edition where one could be verified.

This is a register of 28 episodes in the reception of Tycho Brahe. An episode is one documented instance of his work or his person being received, used, edited, contested, commemorated or examined: a transfer of data, a book built on his observations, a tomb opened, a crater named, a poem solicited, a claim on a treasury. The register is deliberately not confined to the years after his death. Reception began in his lifetime — the vernacular reading of the 1572 star, the liminary verse he solicited for his own books, the charter for Uraniborg’s perpetuity, a Danish nobleman’s hostile verdict of 1598 — and 7 of the 28 episodes begin before 1601. The other 21 run from the inheritance of the observations in October 1601 to analyses published in this decade; 4 of them have no end, because the thing they concern still exists.

Every episode carries a stable key, a thread (§6), an evidence grade, the works it descends from (§5), the sources it rests on, and, where one could be verified, a link to the object itself or to a digitised copy of the edition (§4). The full records are in §3; the same records as one sortable table are in §2.

What the numbers are not. The 28 episodes are a selection, not a census of Tycho’s reception. They were entered where the compilation’s own sources — the biographies, the editions, the published exhumation studies, the chancery registers, the Danish journals — documented an episode well enough to grade it; the absence of an episode says nothing about whether it happened, and the number of episodes in a thread measures the compilation’s attention, not the world’s. No figure on this page measures influence. The year an episode begins is the year of its first documented event as the record states it: for the supernova remnant that is the observation of 1572, for the comet reductions the first of the four memoirs. Where a record itself spans two moments (“1655 / 1913–29”) the table prints the numeric bounds and the register prints the record’s own phrase.

The evidence grades

GradeAs applied in this registerEpisodes
AVerified against the cited source itself — the primary print or manuscript, or a scholarly study held in the compilation's own library and read at the cited page.18
BReported: the claim rests on a secondary summary, on a study the compilation has not itself inspected, on a single partisan witness, or is an interpretive reading — stated as the source states it, and not independently confirmed.10

No episode is graded C or D: a reception claim that weak is not entered as an episode. The scale is the register’s, applied to the claim as stated; it does not rank the sources.

Conventions

Dates. Begins is the year of the episode’s first documented event; ends the year of its last, “present” where it continues. The register prints each record’s own date phrase as well, which may name two moments. Old Style and New Style are not distinguished; no episode turns on a day.

Relations. Descends from is the record’s builds on or derived from relation to one of Tycho’s works or manuscripts; connected to gathers the others (a person involved, a place, an object, another episode). Each relation is a link into the pillar that owns the thing.

Record keys. Every record prints its key (recep-kepler-data) and is addressable: #recep-kepler-data as an anchor, or ?episode=recep-kepler-data, which highlights it.
