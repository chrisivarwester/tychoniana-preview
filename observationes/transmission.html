The custody of the books from Tycho's death to Dreyer's edition, as dated events, each with Dreyer's page in the Prolegomena or the source that carries it. The archetypes, which Kepler kept and his son sold to Frederik III in 1655, went to Paris with Rømer in 1672 and came home in 1707; the fair copies, which reached Albert Curtz by a route no one recorded, were printed as the Historia Coelestis in 1666 and remain in Vienna.

DateEventSource
1587Tycho to Landgrave Wilhelm IV: he is comparing his observations with others' and means at last to bring them into public use (TBOO VI p. 74).Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. I
1598In the Mechanica Tycho calls the observations 'a most rare and precious treasure' and describes their two tiers: entered in large volumes, then copied year by year into separate books arranged by subject (fol. F 2v; TBOO V pp. 109–110).Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. I, III
1600Kepler, newly arrived at Benátky, notes that Tycho will not allow him to copy the observations — 'and rightly so, for this is his treasure, in which he has spent his whole life and fortune'.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. II
1601-10-24Tycho dies in Prague; the report of his illness and death is entered in Codex I (GKS 318, f. 151r) in Kepler's hand. All the observation books pass into Kepler's keeping.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. II, XIX
1604By agreement with Frans Tengnagel, Tycho's son-in-law, Kepler receives 'beautifully and neatly copied' volumes of all the observations, with some autograph material among them (Friis 1875, p. 150).Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. II
1612Kepler's declaration to Tycho's heirs, pasted to the cover of Codex G (GKS 316): each volume is to be marked as part of his treasure, to be locked away should he die suddenly; made after his move to Linz.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. II–III
1618Willebrord Snellius excerpts the observations of 1600–1601 in the Observationes Hassiacae (Leiden); Kepler later supposes he had access to 'the seventh tome in folio of the Protocols'.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. III
1621-11-05Emperor Ferdinand II asks Duke Johann Friedrich of Württemberg to return the codices; only the fair copies are requested, and the archetypes were never returned by Kepler.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. II
1625Kepler, in the Hyperaspistes, still hopes to print 'the books of observations, truly a most precious treasure of antiquity in our art'.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. III
1628-08-17Kepler to Georg Brahe: he hopes soon to prepare the observations for the press.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. III
1630-11-15Kepler dies at Regensburg; the edition never begun. The archetypes pass to his son Ludwig, a physician at Königsberg.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. III–IV
1634After Jakob Bartsch's death at Lauban, Albert Curtz reports to the Austrian chancellor that observations and papers are held sealed at Lauban and Dresden; a search for Kepler's manuscripts is under way.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. IV
1638-02-06Ludwig Kepler writes to Galileo that Christoph Scheiner has urged the Emperor to keep Tycho's observations in the Imperial Library; Ludwig moves the books to a safer place.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. IV
before 1656Albert Curtz (Albertus Curtius, S.J.) obtains the nineteen Vienna fair copies — whether from Kepler in 1621 or from Susanna Bartsch after 1634 is not known; he believes them to be the originals and finds 1593 missing.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. IV–V
1655Ludwig Kepler sells the original books to Frederik III of Denmark ('anno enim 1655, si non antea'); they are placed in the Royal Library.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. V
1656Curtz prints the observations of 1582 as the Sylloge Ferdinandea (Vienna) under the anagram 'Lucius Barrettus'.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. IV–V
1664-06-20Erasmus Bartholin is commissioned to edit the observations; his receipt lists the volumes he received, the octavo of 1563–1574 among them.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. VI, X
1666The Historia Coelestis (Augsburg), Curtz's edition from the Vienna fair copies: 124 + 977 pages, without the years before 1582 and without 1593 — 'so incomplete and so carelessly compiled that it is almost useless'.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. V–VI
1668Bartholin publishes the Specimen recognitionis, a collation of the 1582 observations against the archetypes exposing the errors of the Augsburg edition.Bartholin, Specimen recognitionis (1668)
1672-04-02Jean Picard, in Copenhagen to fix the latitude of Uraniborg, receives Bartholin's fair copy of the observations for printing in Paris and gives a receipt for it; Ole Rømer accompanies the archetypes to Paris.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. VII
1680–1682The Paris printing reaches the year 1582 (23 quires in quarto) and stops.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. VII
1697-05-04Friderich Rostgaard finds the volumes in La Hire's room at the Paris Observatory — 'seven volumes in folio, seven volumes or bundles in quarto, and one volume in octavo'.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. VIII
1707The archetypes are reclaimed by the Danish envoy and returned to the Royal Library, Copenhagen; Bartholin's copy stays in Paris, later transcribed in full by Delisle.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), pp. VIII–IX
1757–1763Lalande and Jeaurat publish the 1593 observations of Mars, Jupiter and Saturn from Bartholin's copy.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. IX
c. 1778The octavo of 1563–1574 is lent from the Royal Library to Christian Hvass and never returned.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. X
1804The octavo is reported in the hands of a Dane living in France (Passy); Codex A and Bartholin's GKS 310 remain its two copies.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. X
1845H. C. Schumacher publishes Tycho's observations of the comet of 1585 from Codex N, and later those of 1596.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. X
1867F. R. Friis publishes the observations of the seven comets.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. X
1913–1916Printing of the observations for the Opera Omnia begins (October 1913) and stops in the summer of 1916 at the end of 1583.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena Editoris, p. 43
1920After the war the nineteen Vienna fair copies are lent to Copenhagen and the printing resumes.Dreyer, Opera Omnia X — Prolegomena (codex descriptions, Codices A–V), p. XI
1923–1926Dreyer's edition of the observations, Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia vols X–XIII, is published; the codices are described in the Prolegomena to vol. X.Dreyer (ed.), Tychonis Brahe Opera Omnia (1913–29)
