The signature

The signature is the single highest-distinctiveness feature in Tycho's repertoire.

  • Spelling — the diaeresis question. Christianson (after Thoren, p. 14n) notes

Tycho "always signed his name with either an ij or the equivalent… y." But direct examination complicates this: Pillar 2 (Sebald Plan, 1591) is signed Tycho (no diaeresis), and a reading of the autograph leaf now in a private collection (confirmed on the leaf) found no diaeresis visible on that leaf. Operational rule: write Tycho Brahe (no diaeresis) unless a clear ÿ / ij is directly visible in the photograph; do not assert Tÿcho as a certainty for any one leaf.

  • Letterforms. Humanist italic cursive; the signature is markedly larger and

more flourished than the surrounding prose. Capital T — display-cursive with prominent looped ascender and elongated bar. Capital B — classical double-bow humanist B, slightly enlarged. Final -e of Brahe — flourished closing terminal with a small downward sweep. Iron-gall brown ink, broad-edge quill, strong thick/thin contrast. Forward slant ~5–10° from vertical.

  • Subscriptions. Letters/documents close Tycho Brahe manu propria (sometimes

Tuus first); presentation books carry d(ono) d(edi)t / d:dt: above the signature. The manu propria phrase is omitted from album entries (the genre presupposes autograph) and from book dedications (where the body/signature spatial break signals the same thing).

The prose hand — what to watch

For evaluating prose-length specimens (album entries, holograph corrections hypothetical marginal notes), against the humanist italic script-class watchlist in:

  • a — single-storey cursive, small closed bowl, short connecting tail (low individuating value).
  • d — uncial d, rounded back, ascender looping/curling leftward at the top (medium-distinctiveness; stable across pillars).
  • s — dual distribution: long-s (ſ) medially/initially; sigma-form round final-s at word-end (the combination is a useful confirming feature).
  • t — short ascender just above x-height, high waved cross-stroke, rightward foot.
  • f — descends below baseline (rules out Textualis; consistent with cursive).
  • i/j, u/v — graphically interchangeable, context-driven (humanist-italic default); j-grapheme essentially absent (Iohanni, cuius, eius; genitive -ij doubles the i).
  • Abbreviation — light: macron/tilde for terminal -m only in -um endings. Heavy abbreviation = scribal copy or a different register, not Tycho.
  • Ductus — no tremor in the pillars (good pen control to the end); stable baseline; marked thick/thin shading; æ ligature consistent in formal contexts.
  • Dating formula — Anno + year + month-genitive + day (e.g. Anno 1599 Feb. 28); place-name datives (Wittebergae, Uraniborgi, Pragae, Wandesburgi).

The letterforms 16 documented; 16 with an exemplar crop

every diagnostic allograph carries a real exemplar crop, rendered in the explorer. The atlas now spans THREE registers cut from THREE public-domain specimens across three life-stages: the dedication register (8 May 1599 Wittenberg album leaf — capital T/B, a/e/t/r/f, d + both s-forms), the observation-log register (GKS 316 f.131v, the 1597 autograph 'DE OCCASIONE INTERRUPTARUM' narrative — a, long-s, looped d, display-capital O) and the documentary/horoscope register (GKS 1823, the 1583 Duke Hans nativity judgment — a, looped d). Slant RECONCILED : three independent pixel deslant measurements (1583 ~15°, 1597 ~23°, 1599 ~16-28°) put the true rightward slant at ~16-28° (typical ~22°), superseding the earlier 5-10° vision estimate. Cross-register/cross-life-stage STABILITY is now image-attested for the single-storey a and the looped uncial d (1583→1599). Still pending: clean per-glyph x-height/aspect ratios (defeated at 800-830px facsimile resolution — needs IIIF/reading-room surrogates). No trained per-hand HTR model is built (grounding-pack scope) and no held-out CER-lift test has been run.
