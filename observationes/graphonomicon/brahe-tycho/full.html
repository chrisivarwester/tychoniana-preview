Manus — the hand of Tycho Brahe (1546–1601): a Graphonomicon

80 specimens of writing attributed to Tycho Brahe — presentation inscriptions, album leaves, letters, manuscripts, observation logs, administrative documents and marginalia — each with its date and the basis for it, its holding and catalogue record, the class of autograph it represents, the verdict on the hand and who established it, and the other hands it must be told from. Two scales are used and defined in §1: the grade is the confidence of the record; the verdict — secure in 14 cases, probable in 59, possible in 7 — is the attribution of the hand itself. The principle comes first: a page in a book of his circle is not his until it is proven so against the anchors.

1Introduction

The principle

Operating principle. Tycho is hard to authenticate because (a) the most casually attributed specimens — marginalia in his books, the observation logs — were usually not written by him but by his familia, calligraphers, or scribes under his direction; and (b) the genuinely autograph specimens cluster in two narrow contexts: signed/dedicated presentation copies (signature only, body usually calligraphic) and the album amicorum entries of the 1598–99 Wandsbeck–Wittenberg–Prague journey. The default when a written hand appears in a Brahe-context book is therefore: not Tycho's, until proven otherwise against the pillars.

Scope

Specimens demonstrably in Tycho Brahe's own hand: wholly autograph, partly autograph, autograph signature only, and the presentation-copy genre where a professional calligrapher executed the body and Tycho signed (calligrapher's body, autograph signature). Spans marginalia, dedications/inscriptions, letters, album amicorum entries, and the autograph portions of observation logs. A specimen enters only with a recorded basis for the attribution.

Date range. 1563 to 24 October 1601. Languages. la, da, de.

Excluded, and modelled as the other hands of §7, never as the subject:

  • Hands of the Hven familia / assistants (Longomontanus, Morsing, Flemløs, et al.) — modelled as discriminators, never as Tycho
  • The son Tyge Brahe (1581-1627) and Erik Lange — discriminators only
  • Posthumous editorial hands (Tengnagel) — discriminators only
  • Professional-calligrapher presentation bodies — modelled as a discriminator; only the autograph signature line is in scope
  • Forgeries and misattributions — retained only as discriminators / Debunked-Claims candidates

What the numbers are not. The 80 specimens are the pieces of writing the compilation has examined or found described, not a census of everything the subject wrote: a hand is counted here only when a record of it exists with a stated basis, and the absence of a register or a life-stage from the grid is a gap in the surviving record or in the search, never evidence that he did not write in it. Of the 80, 25 are signatures only and 14 carry a professional calligrapher's text over his signature, so a count of "specimens of his hand" overstates the prose in it. No held-out test of the attribution model has been run; the 14 anchors are the comparison base, and every other verdict is stated with its basis.

Attribution secure A One of the Vienna Tychonis-Brahe Nachlass papers (Cod. 10689; Dreyer TBOO X notes these bundles 'contain autograph material' used to edit Tycho's Works & Letters). A full-res hand-triage (IIIF surrogate, 17 canvases @ ~5857x8685px): the judgment is a WORKING DRAFT in Tycho's rapid humanist-italic autograph hand — heading 'Iudicium Astrologicum huius geniturae', then a nativity chart/positions table and the judgment under rubric sections 'De Complexione', 'De Amicis et Inimicis', 'De Itinere'. Diagnostic features all match the secure pillars: single-storey cursive a, uncial looped d, long-s + round final-s, looped -que abbreviation, dense macron suspensions, steep rightward slant, rapid ductus — plus AUTHOR-STYLE strike-throughs and interlinear corrections (img 0006, 0009), i.e. composition not transcription. Same genre and hand as the autograph Latin judgment in GKS 1823. Sampled canvases 0006 + 0009 show a single consistent autograph hand; a full leaf-by-leaf confirmation of all 4 leaves is pending. A register-symmetric, feature-by-feature comparison of the judgment prose ('De Amicis et Inimicis') against the SECURE pillar GKS 316 autograph prose ('De occasione interruptarum observationum & discessus mej') supports the same hand: 22 concordances — 19 of them confirmed on a second audit pass — against 4 discordances, all four on LABILE traits (baseline-drift direction, line-spacing, pen-pressure, minim regularity), plausibly genre- or speed-driven, with NO discordance on letterform construction. Two highly distinctive concordances carry the case: the non-closing, leftward-curling g descender, and the curved, hook-shaped (rather than barred) abbreviation marks. The secure/A grade rests on CONVERGENCE — (i) provenance: this is literally a leaf of Tycho's own Nachlass (Cod. 10689), bought with his effects and mined by Dreyer to edit Tycho's Works; (ii) full-diagnostic paleographic match to the pillars; (iii) the draft-with-corrections character (a composer's, not a copyist's, hand); and (iv) the register-symmetric comparison against a secure pillar — a corroboration GKS 316 itself never received. CAVEATS (ENFSI BPM-FHX-01): a comparison at these sample lengths is SUPPORTING evidence, not a forensic identification; the run is modest (~85 words combined) with no overlapping capitals; and unlike GKS 306 there is no independent NAMED-AUTHOR anchor (the geniture is unnamed) — the case is provenance + paleography + comparison, not authorship documentation. Established by: full-res hand-triage + register-symmetric comparison; comparison vs secure pillar kb-gks-316 + kb-gks-1823 (autograph judgment); Dreyer TBOO X Prolegomena (Vienna Nachlass).

The 14 specimens whose autograph status is as certain as can be made — grade A, verdict secure — the comparison base for every other attribution, in date order.
  1. Vienna, ÖNB, Cod. 10689/29 — 'Iudicium astrologicum huius geniturae', autograph nativity-judgment draft — 1500-1599; wholly autograph; One of the Vienna Tychonis-Brahe Nachlass papers (Cod. 10689; Dreyer TBOO X notes these bundles 'contain autograph material' used to edit Tycho's Works & Letters). Diagnostic features all match the secure pillars: single-storey cursive a, uncial looped d, long-s + round final-s, looped -que abbreviation, dense macron suspensions, steep rightward slant, rapid ductus — plus AUTHOR-STYLE strike-throughs and interlinear corrections (img 0006, 0009), i.e. composition not transcription. Same genre and hand as the autograph Latin judgment in GKS 1823. Sampled canvases 0006 + 0009 show a single consistent autograph hand; a full leaf-by-leaf confirmation of all 4 leaves is pending. A register-symmetric, feature-by-feature comparison of the judgment prose ('De Amicis et Inimicis') against the SECURE pillar GKS 316 autograph prose ('De occasione interruptarum observationum & discessus mej') supports the same hand: 22 concordances — 19 of them confirmed on a second audit pass — against 4 discordances, all four on LABILE traits (baseline-drift direction, line-spacing, pen-pressure, minim regularity), plausibly genre- or speed-driven, with NO discordance on letterform construction. Two highly distinctive concordances carry the case: the non-closing, leftward-curling g descender, and the curved, hook-shaped (rather than barred) abbreviation marks. The secure/A grade rests on CONVERGENCE — (i) provenance: this is literally a leaf of Tycho's own Nachlass (Cod. 10689), bought with his effects and mined by Dreyer to edit Tycho's Works; (ii) full-diagnostic paleographic match to the pillars; (iii) the draft-with-corrections character (a composer's, not a copyist's, hand); and (iv) the register-symmetric comparison against a secure pillar — a corroboration GKS 316 itself never received. 6The letterform atlas

The signature, the prose hand, and the documented letterforms, each with its ductus, its discriminating power, the years it is attested in, an exemplar crop where one has been cut, and the contrast to the other hands it separates from.

The signature

The signature is the single highest-distinctiveness feature in Tycho's repertoire.

  • Spelling — the diaeresis question. Christianson (after Thoren, p. 14n) notes

Tycho "always signed his name with either an ij or the equivalent… y." But direct examination complicates this: Pillar 2 (Sebald Plan, 1591) is signed Tycho (no diaeresis), and a reading of the autograph leaf now in a private collection (confirmed on the leaf) found no diaeresis visible on that leaf. Operational rule: write Tycho Brahe (no diaeresis) unless a clear ÿ / ij is directly visible in the photograph; do not assert Tÿcho as a certainty for any one leaf.

  • Letterforms. Humanist italic cursive; the signature is markedly larger and

more flourished than the surrounding prose. Capital T — display-cursive with prominent looped ascender and elongated bar. Capital B — classical double-bow humanist B, slightly enlarged. Final -e of Brahe — flourished closing terminal with a small downward sweep. Iron-gall brown ink, broad-edge quill, strong thick/thin contrast. Forward slant ~5–10° from vertical.

  • Subscriptions. Letters/documents close Tycho Brahe manu propria (sometimes

Tuus first); presentation books carry d(ono) d(edi)t / d:dt: above the signature. The manu propria phrase is omitted from album entries (the genre presupposes autograph) and from book dedications (where the body/signature spatial break signals the same thing).

The prose hand — what to watch

For evaluating prose-length specimens (album entries, holograph corrections hypothetical marginal notes), against the humanist italic script-class watchlist in:

  • a — single-storey cursive, small closed bowl, short connecting tail (low individuating value).
  • d — uncial d, rounded back, ascender looping/curling leftward at the top (medium-distinctiveness; stable across pillars).
  • s — dual distribution: long-s (ſ) medially/initially; sigma-form round final-s at word-end (the combination is a useful confirming feature).
  • t — short ascender just above x-height, high waved cross-stroke, rightward foot.
  • f — descends below baseline (rules out Textualis; consistent with cursive).
  • i/j, u/v — graphically interchangeable, context-driven (humanist-italic default); j-grapheme essentially absent (Iohanni, cuius, eius; genitive -ij doubles the i).
  • Abbreviation — light: macron/tilde for terminal -m only in -um endings. Heavy abbreviation = scribal copy or a different register, not Tycho.
  • Ductus — no tremor in the pillars (good pen control to the end); stable baseline; marked thick/thin shading; æ ligature consistent in formal contexts.
  • Dating formula — Anno + year + month-genitive + day (e.g. Anno 1599 Feb. 28); place-name datives (Wittebergae, Uraniborgi, Pragae, Wandesburgi).
