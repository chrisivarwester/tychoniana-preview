6.1 The witnesses

The catalogue of fixed stars for the year 1600 exists in three editions: the manuscript of 1598 with 1,004 stars, presented in fine copies to patrons; the shorter version of 777 stars that Tycho printed in the Progymnasmata of 1602 (pp. 303–332), omitting the constellation Centaurus and the additional stars at the end of many constellations; and Kepler's printing of the full 1,004 in the Rudolphine Tables of 1627. The table in §6.2 is the 1602 print, star by star, in the text established by Verbunt & van Gent (2010) from Dreyer's reprint (Opera Omnia II pp. 258–280), with each star's identification in the Hipparcos catalogue and its grade, the offset between Tycho's place and the modern one, the page of the 1915 reprint on which it stands, and the verdict of the collation against the 2026 reading of the print. The archive's own working catalogues — the 723 stars at the end of Codex G and the 296 zodiacal stars of Cod. 10709, both printed at the end of vol. XI — and the manuscript witnesses of the 1598 catalogue are listed first.

6.2 The 777 stars of the 1602 print

Emendation of the text of record. the table numbers K 469–482 as 1602 stars 527–539, gives the manuscript's Com 2 (K 470) the number 540 — Ophiuchus 1's — and the duplicate Com 15 (K 483) none, so 540 occurs twice and 526 not at all — TBOO II p. 273: Coma has 14 stars in the print, opening with 'In cuspide primi & borealis trianguli' (K 469) and closing with 'Quae est in australi cuspide △ parui' (K 483); Ophiuchus opens with 'In capite' (K 328) immediately after. Emended here: K 469 → 526, K 471–482 → 527–538, K 483 → 539, K 470 → not in the print.

6.5 Accuracy

Two measures are given. This edition's measure rotates each of Tycho's places (equinox and ecliptic of 1600) to the modern equatorial frame with the full precession matrix and finds the nearest star of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue within 40′; the separation is his error, and the residual is the same after the catalogue's median zero-point offset is removed. Verbunt & van Gent's measure is the offset between Tycho's place and their graded Hipparcos identification, over the stars they identify securely or probably. The two agree: a median of about three minutes of arc over the whole catalogue, with the brightest stars better and a tail of gross errors — about one star in nine beyond ten minutes — that Verbunt & van Gent attribute to computing and copying, not observing.
