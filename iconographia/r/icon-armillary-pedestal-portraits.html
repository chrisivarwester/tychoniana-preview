The portraits on the equatorial armillary's pedestal

lifetimelostAicon-armillary-pedestal-portraits
c. 1584 (instrument built) – December 1596 (the recording block's first issue); the inscribed '1580' is Tycho's floruit year, not an execution date
Four portraits were painted on the wooden pedestal of the smaller equatorial armillary — the instrument of the Mechanica's Fig. 12, whose pedestal Tycho describes as built 'e solido et forti ligno'; not the great steel-based armillary of Fig. 14, built later, whose chapter mentions no portraits. Tycho and Copernicus occupied the two visible niches, Ptolemy and Albategnius the hidden reverse. Tycho names them himself in the Mechanica: 'sunt 4. Icones … quae effigies Ptolemæi, Albategnij et Copernici; tum quoque NOSTRAM repræsentant' — and ours too. The instrument put him into the lineage physically, on the object that did the measuring. Blaeu re-engraved the whole plate on copper for the Grooten Atlas (Amsterdam 1664, Hven section): the pedestal programme is carried over intact — both niches with their figures, the plumb-bob cartouche, and the year-tablets, with '1520' engraved in full (the woodcut's carved bracket no longer obscures the leading digit) — while the right entablature is re-lettered 'T.B.N.D.' against the woodcut's 'T-BR-D' (engraver's error or a deliberate re-reading; which, is undetermined). Derivative of the woodcut rather than of the lost instrument, but the fullest posthumous transmission of the portrait programme. The instrument's place is settled from Tycho's own Uraniborg room-list: 'S. Observatorium minus Septentrionale, alias Armillas AEquatorias continens num. 12' — the lesser north observatory of the main house, with the steel sibling (Fig. 13) in the south room under key N; the house-key letters invert the compass sense, which also explains Blaeu's 'Armillae S' section heading. The same room assignment already stands in the 1596 Epistolae. The entablature initials follow the lineage formula of the sibling Stjerneborg headings (name + origin): T-BR-D = Tycho BRahe Danus (anchored by Chytraeus's 'Tycho Brahe Danus de Knudstryp'), N-C-T-G = Nicolaus Copernicus Torunensis G[ermanus] - the G a structural inference completing the Danus/Germanus pairing, nowhere spelled out. The tablets' years are round-decade floruit values like the whole Stjerneborg series (300 BC...1520, 1580); 1520 = Copernicus's Frombork observing maturity in Tycho's assignment; what 1580 commemorates stays open (Uraniborg's completion leads, per the Mechanica's own 'CIRCA ANNUM 1580' caption). Against Blaeu's re-lettered 'T.B.N.D.', the woodcut's T-BR-D governs: the lifetime block was cut under Tycho's control, and its crude period R invites exactly the misreading the 1664 engraver made. The block's first issue was not the Mechanica: the Icones instrumentorum suite (Uraniborg 1596, Nielsen 975 — bare image-pulls without letterpress, keyed to the Epistolae's instrument descriptions) already carries it, so the portrait programme is attested by December 1596. Four Icones copies are documented (Stockholm KB, Vienna, Wroclaw, and one Epistolae-and-Icones volume in the trade).
Maker
Unrecorded. Christianson's 'probably painted by Gemperle' remains an uncited hedge (grade C) — Beckett & Christensen 1921 never mention these portraits, and DBL's Gemperlin entry is silent on the armillary. Gemperlin WAS available: recruited at Augsburg 1575, royal settlement letter 23 April 1577, documented in Denmark from 1578, died 1587 of a Copenhagen epidemic on Tycho's own printed testimony (De mundi II, p. 284) — his residency brackets the whole preferred painting window.
Original or copy
The painted originals are destroyed with the instruments. What survives is the record: the Mechanica plate.
Dated c. 1584 → December 1596 (the Icones instrumentorum issue)
Name-labels N·C·T·G / T·BR·D sit on the entablatures above the niches; the year-tablets on the plinths beneath the figures read '…520' (leading digit obscured by the block's own carved bracket in every impression examined) and '1580' ('I580' is the font's flag-serif numeral 1). The years are floruit labels, settled by direct inspection of Nathan Chytraeus, Variorum in Europa itinerum deliciae (Herborn 1594), pp. 596–598 (inspected August 2026): the Stjerneborg hypocaustum cycle — Tycho's own sibling lineage programme, on material his preface says came largely from Tycho himself — gives every figure an 'Anno X' floruit line, with COPERNICUS 'Anno 1520 post Christum' and TYCHO BRAHE 'Anno 1580': the identical pair of years the pedestal tablets carry. The pedestal therefore uses the same convention (and the obscured '…520' is confirmed as 1520); '1580' cannot in any case date the execution, since no armillary existed then. Christianson 2000's reading of 1580 as a construction year (against his own 2020 chronology) is thereby retired; what 1580 commemorates within Tycho's career (Uraniborg's completion is Dreyer's candidate) remains open, and Zeeberg 1995 ('The Inscriptions at Tycho Brahe's Uraniborg', in Skafte Jensen ed., Odense 1995, pp. 251–66 — undigitised; no scan located as of August 2026) is now corroborative rather than load-bearing. If Christianson's hedged Gemperlin attribution were adopted, the upper bound would fall to 1587; it stays 1598 (publication of the recording plate). The 1585 Icones instrumentorum (Nielsen 976, Uraniborg broadsides, Marburg unicum — enclosed with Tycho's 1 March 1586 letter to Wilhelm IV) was checked as a possible earlier attestation (August 2026): its armillary woodcut depicts the steel armilla (Fig. 13 type; 'pes...ex solido metallo'), not this instrument — a diagnostic null that leaves the 1598 plate the earliest witness to the portraits. The hard upper bound is December 1596, not 1598: the Icones instrumentorum suite (Nielsen 975, Uraniborg 1596, 18 letterpress-free woodcut pulls issued with some Epistolae copies; presentation copies to Christian IV and Chancellor Friis in December 1596) contains the armillary printed from what is almost certainly the same woodblock (Stockholm KB exemplar examined leaf-by-leaf: identical tablets, V-medallion, and the full compositor letter-key in identical positions; print-flaw-level confirmation pending an uncoloured 1598 impression). The painting window is therefore summer 1584 to December 1596, preferred sub-window 1584–87 unchanged. The block itself was cut somewhere between 1584 and 1596; the documented production waves (two blocks 1585, two 1586–87 for De mundi, several 1592–93 for the Progymnasmata, boxwood-only from April 1590) make the post-1590 campaign the likeliest home for it, stated as preference.
Whereabouts

Painted originals destroyed with Tycho's instruments at Prague. The plate survives in every intact copy of the 1598 Mechanica.

The Mechanica's instrument plates are woodcuts, not engravings — only six illustrations in the book are from copperplates. The pedestal plate is printed from the identical woodblock in the 1602 Hulsius reissue — the inscriptions are letter-for-letter identical; the one armillary re-engraved on copper in 1602 is Fig. 13, not this plate. Fate: not among the four giant instruments left on Hven in 1597 (Tycho's own Wandsbek list, TBOO XIII.101-104), so it travelled with the general train, reaching Bohemia only in October or November 1600 (Dreyer p. 320; Thoren JHA 1973 via Wolfschmidt). After 1601 the accounts fork — most instruments confiscated toward Vienna by 1604 (Schuppener after Kleinschnitzova) vs. destroyed after the 1619 Bohemian war (Wolfschmidt) — unreconciled; no fragment survives (only the two NTM sextants survive of all the instruments). Bergeron's 1603 Belvedere ensemble (four rulers + four astronomers, matching Resen 1668) is a different programme from this pedestal's two-name scheme.
