De Gheyn's second engraving (Westergaard 1394) — the corrected portrait

lifetimeextantAicon-degheyn-v2
ordered December 1590; design fixed by mid-1595; plate completed by 1596
The corrected replacement, and the portrait by which Europe knew Tycho: aged now, in the worn plumed beret, within the reworked arch. A complete change-register against the first engraving (22 verified differences, July 2026) shows a deliberately corrective new plate: the Frederik II medal removed and the stacked chains reduced to a single doubled chain (executing the 1590 complaint), the collar changed from standing to flat, the doublet opened — the whole dress more modest; the heraldic errors fixed (RØNNOR corrected, the Vasa wheat-sheaf cut where BELKER's bars had stood); the tablet's vocabulary revised from 'machinarum' to 'instrumentorum'; the sitter's face updated to the man of nearly fifty while the tablet keeps 'aetatis 40 … 1586', the caption of the likeness's origin; the hands transposed left-for-right while the head is not mirrored — the fingerprint of a freshly drawn plate, produced from the surviving Copenhagen drawing by direct needle transfer. The ornate pendant jewel over an elephant form survives from the first plate — the one costume element never revised. The plate faced the 1596 Epistolae; the engraving is recorded present in a documented copy of the 1610 Frankfurt-titled reissue (Amsterdam UvA OG 8°-2(1)), but that issue is unsold 1596 sheets under a new title-leaf — no fresh 1610 press-run of the plate is evidenced, and the Hülsius/Tampach lineage's documented portrait supply was a copy plate.
Maker
Jacques de Gheyn II, signed in the plate ('Geyn. fe').
Original or copy
An original plate by de Gheyn, signed 'Geyn. fe'. The corrected replacement for the first engraving.
Dated 7 December 1590 (ordered) → mid-1595 (design attested in Tycho's hands via the Cornelisz copy); plate completion by end 1596 (Altena's outer bound)
The lower bound is Tycho's own re-order in the complaint letter of 7 December 1590 ('aliam illic confieri curem' — I am having another made there). The upper bound (revised July 2026): Lambert Cornelisz's reversed copy of this portrait is dated 1595 in its plate and was in Tycho's hands by 5 September 1595 (his letter to Pontanus) — so the design existed by mid-1595. Whether Cornelisz copied the printed plate or the Vorlage drawing KKSgb5254 is contested (Mortensen 1946 p. 21: 'Forlæg: Pennetegningen'); the 1595 date therefore pins the design with high confidence and the plate's completion only probably — the hard ceiling for the finished plate is Altena's 'before the end of 1596'. This still retires the older account that dated the model-delivery to May 1596 (the painter in Tycho's diary on 4 May and Blaeu's departure on 24 May 1596 cannot explain a design that was already circulating in Cornelisz's copy the year before). The corrected heraldry gives an internal lower bound as well: the error-state was still circulating on the February–March 1592 prospectus, so the correction falls between 1592 and the plate's completion (by end 1596).
Whereabouts

An edition multiple; NH locates impressions at Amsterdam, Dresden, Paris, Wolfegg (single state). The NH-illustrated Amsterdam impression is Rijksmuseum RP-P-OB-9945 (192 × 138 mm sheet vs 187 × 135 mm plate, Public Domain) — preferred full-margin surrogate candidate; SMK KKSgb7084 (186 × 132 mm) is trimmed by at most 1–3 mm, within impression variance.

Copy-level carriers

Four digitised copies examined across all three issue-clusters (July 2026): every portrait present is the hatted second engraving — Smithsonian/Dibner (1596 issue, hand-coloured), ETH Zürich (1601 Hülsius issue), Florence BNCF (1610 Tampach issue); the Dresden SLUB 1610-issue copy lacks the leaf entirely (loose inserts do go missing). Nielsen §430 names no state ('et kobberstukket Portræt'). No copy carrying the capless first engraving has yet been found (n=4 of 29 census copies).
