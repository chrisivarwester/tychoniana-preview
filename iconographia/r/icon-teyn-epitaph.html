The Týn church epitaph, Prague — the effigy that closes the series

lifetimeextantBicon-teyn-epitaph
c. 1604 or some years after
The memorial relief in the Church of Our Lady before Týn: Tycho carved life-size in red Slivenec marble, 200 × 115 cm, standing in a suit of armour already old-fashioned in his own day, his right hand resting on a globe set upon the family arms and his left on his sword hilt, a plumed helmet at his feet. The prosthetic nose is plainly shown. The carving is coarse, and the workshop is unattributed — Mortensen (1946, without citation) called it Austrian, by type-resemblance to the Berka von Dauba monument in the same church (a comparandum itself unverified); Vellev (2006, equally uncited) takes it for a local Prague stonemason's workshop responsible for a series of monuments in Prague churches. It was ground down and restored for the tercentenary in 1901, when the floor slab lettered simply TYCHO BRAHE was also laid. Casts are at Frederiksborg (inv. A 155, maker 'Steffensen', 1885–86) and in the Round Tower's historical collection in Copenhagen (a life-size plaster made c. 1867 from the original by F. R. Friis, transferred from the Nationalmuseet) — both pre-dating the 1901 restoration. It is the last image of Tycho made from living memory, and the point where the lifetime iconography hands over to the printed afterlife.
Maker
not recorded
Original or copy
An original carved monument, made in Prague by people who had seen him — but set up after his death, and heavily restored in 1901.
Dated 1604 (Kirstine's death — the only sourced terminus post quem for the erection; the 1601 burial dates the grave, not the monument) → after 1604 (no sourced completion date)
Tycho died in Prague on 24 October 1601 and was buried in the Týn church, but the monument's erection is sourced only as after his widow Kirstine's death in 1604 (Friis 1871 p. 298, Mortensen 1946 p. 74, Norlind 1970 p. 318); no source attests an earlier figure-stone phase. It therefore stands past the lifetime boundary — but it was made in Prague by people who had seen him, and it closes the series.
Whereabouts

In situ in the Church of Our Lady before Týn, Prague, against a pier near the chancel.

HolderKostel Panny Marie před Týnem, Staroměstské náměstí, Prague 1
Evidencelocated located (architecturally fixed; the 2010 reinterment campaign necessarily handled it)
Recordthe holding institution’s own record ↗

Red Slivenec marble, 200 × 115 cm (Mortensen 1942 gives 2.03 × 1.15 m). Figure-stone and inscription plate are physically distinct elements (Vellev 2006), but no source dates them separately: Friis 1871 and Norlind 1970 describe a single erection by the heirs after Kirstine's death in 1604, and a two-campaign history is this edition's hypothesis, unattested. The epitaph text was composed by Jakob Typotius. The death year was miscut MDCIII for MDCI and patched with coloured filler (Vellev 2006, p. 230); the upper plate gives 29 October against the frame's XXIV OCTOB (Vellev 2006, p. 232). Four-ancestor arms Brahe/Bille/Rud/Ulfstand, with only two helmets where four belong (Vellev 2006, p. 230) — on Vellev's explanation possibly deliberate economy, since all four families sometimes used the same buffalo-horn-and-peacock-feather crest. Restored in 1901 by Jan Herain for the tercentenary: surfaces cleaned and smoothed, the ground painted white, cornices repaired, letters blackened, and the missing top ornament replaced by a small obelisk; Herain's measurements and notes of the work were no longer to be found as of 2006 (Vellev p. 235). Of the two Copenhagen casts, the identification of Frederiksborg A 155 as the Týn figure is well-supported inference — structured record, catalogue photograph, independent 1937 corroboration — not a documentary source naming both together.

Danske Magazin II (Copenhagen, 1746), p. 353copper engraving, printed on the text pagethe earliest printed record of the monument, and the witness to its pre-1901 state
Attributed to Johann Martin Bernigeroth by the 2006 volume; the plate as printed carries no engraver's signature legible at 600 dpi and the 1746 text names none, so the attribution is not hardened here. The 1746 text documents the production chain: drawn in 1743 in Prague for Tycho de Hofman, the Danish Chancery secretary there for Maria Theresa's Bohemian coronation, and engraved for his Portraits historiques (The Hague, 1746), the plate then lent to Danske Magazin (bind 2, pp. 356–57) — naming the commissioner but no engraver. The Bernigeroth attribution traces to Vellev's fig.-3 caption alone, and his 'efter Danske Magazin' could even describe a later derivative. A collation trap: it is printed on the text page at the head of p. 353, not on an inserted leaf.
