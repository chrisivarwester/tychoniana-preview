The full-figure watercolour at Strahov — the standing Tycho with a sextant

afterlifeuntracedCicon-progymnasmata-watercolour
c. 1598 – 1666; the drawing's date is open
A watercolour on parchment, in folio, bound in front of a copy of the Astronomiae instauratae progymnasmata at Strahov — a second book Tycho presented to the same Bohemian magnate who received the Strahov Mechanica. The composition is unlike every other painted Tycho: a full-length standing figure on a plinth, leaning on a sextant, in a beret and a long cloak reaching to the base, the whole reading as the depiction of a statue rather than of a man. Professor Šafařík distributed photolithographs of it to those present at the astronomical society's Strasbourg meeting in 1881, describing it as the only original portrait of Tycho in Prague.
Maker
Unknown. Friis raises the possibility that it is by Philip Kilian, which would make it seventeenth-century and no witness to Tycho at all — 'Hvem Tegningen skyldes, vides ikke; maaske er den af Kilian.'
Original or copy
Genuinely open, and the interesting question about it. Friis observes that the photolithograph agrees with Philip Kilian's engraving in Historia Coelestis (ed. Albert Curtz as 'Lucius Barrettus', Augsburg 1666) in both form and size so closely that the drawing may be the very model for the plate — but equally it may be after it, or by Kilian himself. Friis's own view is that the ultimate original behind both is de Gheyn's engraving. Šafařík's 1881 description of it as 'the only original portrait of Tycho in Prague' should not be relied on. A first comparison against Kilian's plate (made here, at matched figure height) confirms beyond doubt that the two are the same composition, in the same direction, with the same pose, instrument, beret, ruff, fur-lined cloak and stance. It does not settle direction: the engraving is more elaborate throughout — a larger plume, a more structured beret, a more articulated instrument — which is what one expects both of an engraver elaborating a simpler model AND of a line engraving beside a halftone of a watercolour. The question stays open. A further comparison adds one datum: the two are same-sense. Six of six calibrated Netherlandish painting-to-engraving pairs reverse, so a same-sense pair leans against the watercolour having served as a face-up-traced production model for the plate. Two caveats keep this a lean rather than a verdict: the observation is made on the 1901 wrapper, whose undocumented reproduction chain may itself flip orientation; and the rule is calibrated on a different workshop tradition seventy to eighty years earlier — Kilian's own transfer practice is undocumented, and blackened-verso and counterproof transfers print same-sense. The direction question thus leans against Friis's model reading without being settled.
Dated c. 1598 → 1666
The lower bound is the book it is bound into — a copy of the Progymnasmata that Tycho gave away, with his autograph dedication to Johann Zajíc von Hasenburk. But Friis warns that a drawing bound into a presented book need not be contemporary with the book: 'naar Vandfarvetegningen nu findes i en Bog, som Tyge Brahe har bortforæret, saa følger dog ikke heraf, at den er samtidig med Bogen.' The upper bound is Philip Kilian's engraving in Historia coelestis (Augsburg: Utzschneider, 1666, ed. Albert Curtz as 'Lucius Barrettus'), which the drawing either precedes as its model or follows as a derivative — Friis's 'Vienna 1657' imprint for the plate is refuted, and no documented 1657 issue exists. The 1881 report is now read at the source rather than through Friis: Vierteljahrsschrift der Astronomischen Gesellschaft 16, p. 273, in the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek's digitisation.
Whereabouts

At Strahov, in a presentation copy of the Progymnasmata — a different book from the Strahov Mechanica.

HolderStrahovská knihovna, Královská kanonie premonstrátů na Strahově, Prague, Czech Republic
Evidenceattested attested — Friis and the Vierteljahrsschrift der Astronomischen Gesellschaft (1881); no live Czech record reached
Recordno online record for this object has been reached

Distinct from the Strahov Mechanica. Both were presented by Tycho to Jan Zbyněk Zajíc z Házmburka, so the same recipient holds two Tycho presentation volumes, each carrying a watercolour portrait — and the two portraits are of different types. Status is 'untraced', not 'extant': the object's survival rests on the 1881 report and nothing later. A third independent silence has since been added: Boldan & Vackářová, Bibliotheca astronomica (Národní knihovna ČR, 2024), pp. 88–91 — a Strahov-focused Tycho chapter — illustrates only the Mechanica and returns no trace of this Progymnasmata volume, the strongest of the three silences, 143 years after the report. The same source gives the sibling Mechanica's current shelfmark, AG XI 56. Neither the Strahov catalogue (Katalog Tritius) nor Manuscriptorium could be queried for this chapter — the live-record check is unexecuted, not negative. Friis's imprint for the Kilian engraving, 'Vienna 1657', is refuted — see the Kilian record.

Where the sources disagree 2
QuestionPositionsStatus
Is the drawing the model for Kilian's engraving, or a copy of it?Model — Friis notes the photolithograph and the engraving agree in form and size so exactly that 'det maaske netop er denne Tegning, hvorefter Stikket er udført'. Derivative — the drawing may equally be by Kilian, in which case it postdates the plate in conception.unresolved, and it decides whether this is a lifetime object or a seventeenth-century one — the engraving is now recorded separately as icon-kilian-standing, with its plate at Wellcome 1357i, so the comparison Friis made by eye can be made properly as soon as an image of the watercolour is obtained. Dreyer endorses the model reading twice — Tycho Brahe (1890), printed p. 264, the sole occurrence in the book (the circulating citation 'notes p. 288' is an error), and again, unhedged, in his preface to Opera Omnia X (1923, via Meddelelser fra Ole Rømers Venner 14/1, 2006, p. 31) — but both endorsements root in the single 1881 Šafařík report: endorsement, not independent evidence. The same-sense finding recorded under nature leans against the model reading, carried with its two caveats.
Does the Studnička wrapper reproduce the watercolour, or Kilian's engraving?For the watercolour: the wrapper has the plain slab and the facsimile signature, which are Friis's stated differentiators, and a facsimile signature is a positive feature that cannot be produced by cropping an engraving. Against certainty: the wrapper is a 1901 halftone, and Studnička nowhere says what he reproduced.well-supported but with undocumented chain of custody — Friis's differentiators (plain slab, facsimile 'Tycho Brahe') are his own observations from a copy of the 1881 photolithograph in hand, absent from the Vierteljahrsschrift minutes he quotes, and neither feature can be produced by cropping or retouching the engraving; whether the wrapper prints from the 1881 plate or a fresh reproduction of the same original remains undocumented
Open questions 4
  1. Is Studnička's wrapper a print from Šafařík's 1881 plate, or a fresh 1901 reproduction of the same original? Studnička's text is silent.
  2. Studnička's catalogue documents the Strahov Mechanica copy in detail but never mentions this Progymnasmata volume or the 1881 episode. The silence is now partially explained: his item C.I footnote borrows an illustration from a Progymnasmata, 1603, at Strahov, so a copy was accessible to him in 1900–01 — which weakens any inference that the volume had left Strahov by 1901 — and his catalogue's scope (ownership-relics of Tycho's own library) plausibly excludes a presentation gift, though that genre explanation is a candidate reading, not established. Whether the accessible copy is the same volume is unknown.
  3. No sighting of the Progymnasmata volume after 1881 has been located; Boldan & Vackářová's Strahov-focused Tycho chapter (2024) adds a third independent silence. Whether it is still at Strahov is unverified — the live-record check against Katalog Tritius and Manuscriptorium remains unexecuted, not negative.
  4. A comparison of the wrapper against Kilian's plate at higher resolution than a 1901 halftone allows: the direction of dependence is still open, and the halftone is too coarse to settle it.
