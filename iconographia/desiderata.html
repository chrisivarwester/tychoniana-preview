8Desiderata

Everything this edition does not know, in one place: the records without a reproduction, the objects whose location is attested rather than confirmed, the disagreements left open, the standing questions of the catalogue as a whole, and the questions each record carries — with, where the record names it, the single step that would close it. Several are cheap: a registry enquiry, a reading-room call slip, a caliper on a 1746 engraving.

Standing questions of the edition 9

  1. The 1595 medal: existence is now settled (Mortensen, Dreyer, Beckett; Galster 53) — what is still wanted is a photograph of an actual specimen and a check of the British Museum's holdings, which could not be checked. Public-cabinet specimens are often aftercasts.
  2. The Cornelisz print's surviving impressions: the ÖNB Bildarchiv (the scanned mount suggests Vienna) and the Royal Library's print room are the two places to ask; Friis reportedly saw an impression in Vienna in 1868.
  3. The drawing's verso: microscopy of the compound corner mark (whether there is ink under the forged red-chalk HG), raking light for the needle incisions, and the date of the perimeter line — a single study-room session at SMK would answer all three.
  4. The Kronborg tapestry identification: Wanscher's conjecture has not been tested against the likeness family, and the companion figure's sphere — a globe? — deserves a focused look.
  5. The pendant jewel over an elephant form — the one costume element that survives every revision (both engravings, both dies): what exactly was it? Tycho held no Order-of-the-Elephant membership.
  6. What were the diary's 4 May 1596 'Pictor' and Blaeu's 24 May 1596 departure actually for, now that the second engraving no longer needs them? The aetatis-50 standing type is a candidate.
  7. The Prague (Clementinum) panel: not yet examined at object level. Whether the Berlin photogravure really reproduces it is unconfirmed — Mortensen's plate number reads 3279 in his printed caption against 8279 on the sheet, and the panel's inscription needs to be read against the faint lettering visible at the sheet's upper left.
  8. The Steensgaard panel, original or copy — the one question in this edition that a single technical test would close. Mortensen's own 'Den ældste Tycho Brahe-portræt' (Nordisk astronomisk tidsskrift, 1935), never consulted, is very likely where he argued the case; Frederiksborg's iconographic register and the 1938 auction catalogues would show where the panel went; and if it is found, dendrochronology would date the oak.
  9. Which engraving sits in a given 1596 Epistolae copy (insertion was loose): only a copy-by-copy check of digitised copies can tell.

Open questions by record 76

The same questions the records carry, gathered so that a visit to one institution or one reading room can close several at once.

RecordQuestions
The Steensgaard panel — a lifetime portrait or an early copy, never tested
  1. The accession date and provenance of A 8191, which only the museum's own registry can supply: an acquisition in or after 1938 from the Bille-Brahe-Selby dispersal would close the identification. The national Kunstindeks Danmark is no substitute — Frederiksborg is absent from its roster of museums, and a museum filter there returns no artwork records.
  2. Harald Mortensen, 'Den ældste Tycho Brahe-portræt', Nordisk astronomisk tidsskrift n.s. xvi (1935), p. 58 — not yet consulted. The title matches his superlative for this panel exactly, and it predates both his 1937 'Originalmaleri (?)' and his 1946 'ældre Kopi'. No digitisation has been found (ADS, Runeberg, tidsskrift.dk and the Internet Archive are all negative; the 1934 Norwegian companion piece, read at nb.no, is silent on Steensgaard), so the article can only be read in the Royal Library.
  3. The 1938 dispersal catalogue, Fortegnelse over indbo fra Steensgaard (V. Winkel & Magnussen, auction 20 October 1938): a lot entry would give dimensions, support, attribution and the buyer. Copies are in the trade and in the Royal Library's auction-catalogue files. One caution: a trade listing transcribes the title-page decedent as 'Baron Preben Bille-Brahe-Selby' against the documented Hendrik lensgreve — most likely a transcription error, but not settled until the title page is seen.
  4. Daniel Hvidt's two painted copies — M/S Museet for Søfart, Helsingør, and the Astronomisk-Historisk Samling at Rundetårn — rest on Mortensen 1946 alone and have not been confirmed with the institutions.
  5. Should the panel be located, dendrochronology, then infrared reflectography and X-radiography, would settle lifetime against later and whether the astronomical attributes are restoration overpaint, as Mortensen suggested.

The gilt portrait supralibros — two dies
  1. Does either physical die survive? Unchecked channels are Nationalmuseet's object database and Frederiksborg's iconographic register, neither of which is likely to hold a binding tool. The documentary silence now runs back past Gigas to Resen 1668 and Danske Magazin 1746 — stamped covers described, never a tool.
  2. Per-volume die-size assignment for Kleinschnitzová's six Clementinum shelfmarks (8 H 39, 12 H 10, 14 G 30, 14 J 176, 19 C 92, 9 B 135) — the decisive test of the format hypothesis, with predictions on record (octavos small, quartos large).
  3. Reconciliation of the Bodleian SOLO full-livery description of the Savile copy against the photographed 4° T 2 Art.Seld. Sammelband, and recovery of the Weston Savilian shelfmark — one object with a misattached description, or a seventh De mundi carrier?
  4. A first-hand raking-light reading of both gilt legends on the Sascerides copy (LN 428 4°, eks. 1), illegible in the digitised surrogate.
  5. A caliper reading of the de Lode plate. Danske Magazin II (1746) claims to render the portrait die at 1:1 and the arms die at 'selv samme Form og Størrelse'; measuring the oval on a physical 1746 sheet — or on a scan with a known scale — is the cheapest independent test of the large die's dimensions anywhere in this edition, and the only one that does not measure a compressed impression on leather or vellum.
