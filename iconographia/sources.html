9Sources

Every source cited by a record, in one alphabetical list: short form, full reference, what it contributes to this chapter, the records that cite it, and a verified link where an open-access scan or the institution’s own record exists. The editor’s own unpublished research notes are listed as such and never as external authority. Three works that would settle more than most remain unread or unlocated: Mortensen’s note of 1935 on the oldest portrait, Schepelern’s catalogue of the Odense convent’s portraits (1959), and Wanscher’s Kronborgs Historie (1939); Beckett and Christensen (1921), Studnička (1901), Karl Madsen and Beckett (1897) have no open-access scan that could be verified, and carry no link.

  8. Chytraeus, Variorum in Europa itinerum deliciae (Herborn, 1594)
    Nathan Chytraeus, Variorum in Europa itinerum deliciae (Herborn, 1594), pp. 596–598 — prints all eight distichs of the Stjerneborg astronomer cycle, including Tychonides, under a heading asserting that the painted icons with gold-lettered distichs exist in the hypocaustum; the preface states the Danish material came largely direct from Tycho.   10. Danske Magazin II (Copenhagen, 1746) — the 1595 medal (pp. 165–168) and the supralibros account with the de Lode plate (pp. 321–322)
    Danske Magazin, vol. II (Copenhagen, 1746) — two separate first-hand Tycho loci in one volume. (1) p. 161 (the engraved plate) and pp. 165–168 (the medal discussion with notes 1–4) — the earliest published account of the 1595 medal. The compilers state that they personally inspected two 'Originaler' (one silver in the Royal Coin Cabinet, one base-metal in Klevenfeldt's collection) and describe specimens as 'afstemplede' (struck); note 4 records the Elephant 'hengende i en Kiæde' on the bust; the editors offer an explicitly unprovable twin conjecture on the occasion (the completion of the star catalogue and the great brass globe reaching usable form in 1595) and float Tycho's nephew Tage Ottesen Tott, then 15–16, as a possible commissioner. (2) 23. Hefte, pp. 321–322, 'Endnu videre Følge af rare og mest utrykte Efterretninger om Tyge Brahe' (Langebek) — the earliest published account of the supralibros, with an engraved plate of the portrait die on p. 321 signed 'O.H.d.Lode sc.', i.e. Odvard Helmoldt de Lode, the Copenhagen engraver (the attribution to him is conventional, not corpus-attested). The text states that Tycho 'har han selv ladet det i et Stempel udgrave, og brugt samme til at lade i Guld aftrykke uden paa Bindet af sine Skrifter'; that the plate was drawn from the Royal Library's blue-satin ('blaat Atlask') Mechanica (Wandsbek 1598) given 'd. 18. Decemb. 1600' to 'en forneme Herre i Bømen', i.e. LN 432 2°, eks. 2, the Petr Vok z Rožmberka copy; and that the plate is printed at the size of the stamp — 'Billedet paa Bogen er i selv samme Størrelse, som dette Koberstykke'. Page 322 continues: the second chain carries 'tydeligen … en Elefant' (an elephant, no order named); 'om der har været noget Smykke i den tredie, kand man ikke see'; the lower board carries the arms die 'i selv samme Form og Størrelse'; and the arms couplet is printed in the variant reading 'Arma, Genus, Fundi pereunt: Durabile Virtus / Et Doctrinæ decus Nobilitatis habent'.
