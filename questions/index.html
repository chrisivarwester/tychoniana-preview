GKS 1832 'Planetary tables' — is it in Erasmus Bartholin's hand?
▼
O_GKS1832
KB tentatively attributes the Tychonic-Keplerian planetary tables in GKS 1832 to Erasmus Bartholin, whose hand is well attested in the adjacent editorial volumes (GKS 308–310), so this is testable.
Progress / what would settle it: OPEN (position as of 2026-06-02): the attribution is the Royal Library's own hedge, and the test that would settle it is one image short. The KB catalogue titles the volume 'Tabulæ Planetarum ex observationibus Tychonico-Keplerianis forte Er. Bartholini' — '…perhaps of Erasmus Bartholin' — and no published study of the hand exists. The volume's profile argues for caution: it is a quarto of Tychonic-Keplerian planetary tables, a working document of the 1660s rather than a primary observation log; it is absent from Picard's 1672 receipt of the five folio volumes taken to Paris; and it entered KB only by 1787 — a profile matching the later, separately-acquired Tychonica (cf. GKS 320, Q_GKS320_PROVENANCE) rather than the documented route of the Bartholin editorial archive. On that evidence a hand from Bartholin's circle (a student, or the young Ole Rømer) or a later Danish computational copy is at least as consistent with the record as Bartholin's own hand. A secure comparison basis exists: three signed Erasmus Bartholin Latin autographs of 1672–75 are digitised among the Royal Society's Early Letters — a fluent, lightly right-sloped humanist italic; one of the letters itself discusses Tycho's Hven observations, Picard, and the edition 'ex mea recensione', the very corpus GKS 1832 derives from. Bartholin's securely-attributed Copenhagen volumes (GKS 308–310) remain undigitised, as does GKS 1832 itself. What would settle it: a digitised image of GKS 1832, compared against the Royal Society autographs — ideally three-way, with a Rømer autograph as the principal alternative hand.

Trace the untraced Apologia witnesses + resolve Berlin-vs-Kraków
▼
O_NKS93B O_APOL_BERLIN
The Apologia witness census left two reported-but-untraced copies — a Kepler-papers copy ('Apologia … ad Craigum de Cometis 1589') and the Longomontanus→Jørgen From manuscript (Gassendi) — and the Berlin fragment's exact shelfmark is unresolved, with the ex-Preußische holdings split post-WWII between Berlin and the Jagiellońska, Kraków.
Progress / what would settle it: OPEN (position as of 2026-06-02): the three threads now stand as follows. (1) The Longomontanus→Jørgen From copy — written at Tycho's dictation c.1589–90, passed from Longomontanus to Chancellor Christen Friis to Jørgen From, who quoted it at length in his 1642 Dissertatio astronomica and promised to print it — is untraced after From's death on 19 Aug 1651. The chain is anchored in primary sources: From's own Dissertatio; Ole Worm's letter to Gassendi of June 1648 (Gassendi, Opera Omnia 1658, p.518); Gassendi's Vita 1654, p.206; with Norlind 1970, pp.140–42, and Kragh, RePoSS #22, 2014, pp.37–40. (2) The Kepler-papers copy is real, distinct, and located: Norlind 1970 (pp.136–43) identifies it as Pulk. MSS Bd 19, fols. 115–150, Archive of the Russian Academy of Sciences, St Petersburg — 36 leaves, lacking the first and last, read and corrected throughout by Tycho himself; it entered the Kepler Nachlass because Kepler was drafting his own, never-finished Craig rebuttal, and pre-war photostats survive at the Munich Kepler-Kommission. It is the mutilated, then-anonymous tract Hevelius described to Bullialdus (Hamburgische vermischte Bibliothek 2, 1744). (3) Berlin vs Kraków is settled: the Jagiellońska's two Brahe Sammelbände (BJ St. Dr. Cim. 6166–6167, the 1588 Hven edition, acquired by Jan Brożek in 1610; and Cim. 6169–6171, the 1603 Prague edition) are old Jagiellonian holdings that do not bind in the Apologia or Craig's letters, so the Berlin fragment is not at Kraków and is most likely a genuine WWII loss from the Preußische Staatsbibliothek — Norlind (p.138) already recorded it as lost. What remains: Martha List's 1961 inventory of the Kepler Nachlass for the modern St Petersburg shelfmark; the specific Berlin destruction or displacement event; and the From manuscript itself.

Which apographa are in Longomontanus's hand vs other assistants?
▼
O_COD10657 O_COD10675
The 19 fair-copies are attributed wholesale to Longomontanus, but the copying spanned 1582–1601 and the familia rotated; Dreyer notes Tycho's own hand appears often in the earlier volumes and rarely in the later, implying multiple scribes.
Progress / what would settle it: OPEN (position as of 2026-06-22): the wholesale attribution of the 19 Vienna fair-copies to Longomontanus is an inference, not a documented fact, and it has been replaced by a per-volume, graded attribution. Dreyer's only direct statement (TBOO X prolegomena, p.XVIII) is an aggregate gradient — 'In prioribus tomis satis multa inveniuntur Tychonis manu scripta, in posterioribus pauca' (Tycho's own hand frequent in the earlier volumes, rare in the later) — and he names no scribe; no published source gives a per-volume hand attribution. Overlaying the familia's documented service dates on each volume's observation year (contemporaneous copying — a model Dreyer's gradient itself supports, since a single retrospective copyist would have left a uniform hand and no gradient) yields the graded candidate set now shown on each observation volume: the seven 1582–88 volumes (Cod. 10657–10663) exclude Longomontanus, who reached Hven only in summer 1589/90, and belong to the early familia (Flemløse, Sascerides, Morsing, Hans Crol) with Tycho's own frequent hand; 1590–95 (Cod. 10665–10669, less the lost 1593) have Longomontanus as the strongly-favoured hand, 1592 and 1594 the cleanest (sole senior assistant); 1589, 1596–97 and 1600–01 are genuine splits (Morsing/Flemløse/Crol at the 1589 turnover; Johannes Eriksen 1596–97; Longomontanus returned, Eriksen and Tengnagel at Prague 1600–01); and 1598–99 (Cod. 10672–10673) are unattributed exile volumes. Confirmation needs a securely-attributed Longomontanus autograph as comparand. Two are now identified, neither digitised: his letter to Kepler in Vienna (Cod. lat. 10703, f.236v) and his album-amicorum leaf of 13 September 1598, signed at Wandsbek the same day as Tycho's own, among the Boetius album leaves of the Noodt collection at the Royal Library, Copenhagen (Thott 500,2° / Thott 1059,2°; Helk, Fund og Forskning 21, 1974). What would settle it: an image of either autograph, compared first against the strongly-favoured volumes (Cod. 10665–10669), then a volume-by-volume examination of the whole series.

Did Tycho Brahe or Jon Jacobsen Venusin write Urania Titani?
▼
O_COD10686
Zeeberg (1994) established Tycho's authorship via the Craig letter (26 July 1594, TBOO VII.366): 'lusus … in honorem sororis nomine a me compositus' — a composition written 'in his sister's name', the epistolary fiction of the poem. Against this, Andersen-Vinilandicus (in Kunstværket og dets hemmeligheder, 2009, pp. 225–276) proposes Jon Jacobsen Venusin (d. 1608), Gellius Sascerides's brother-in-law: a Hermetically-oriented neo-Latin poet with documented Hven visits (20–22 May 1590; 29 Nov–3 Dec 1595) who could have been a ghost-author for the poem. Venusin is not Gellius Sascerides — they are distinct persons.
Progress / what would settle it: OPEN (position as of 2026-06-13): Tycho's authorship stands but is dented. The Craig letter identifies a composition 'written in Sophie's name' without naming the poem explicitly, so the identification is probabilistic; and Zeeberg's arguments were framed to defeat the Sophie-as-author hypothesis (Resen 1668; DBL3 1979), not to anticipate a Venusin candidacy. Venusin's two documented Hven visits (May 1590; Nov–Dec 1595) do not coincide with the winter 1594 composition date, which weakens but does not eliminate his case. The key primary evidence is the correction stratigraphy of Cod. Vindob. 10686/12: Tycho's autograph corrections (hand T) against the remaining corrections by a second hand. Path to resolution: read Andersen-Vinilandicus 2009 in full, and compare the second hand in the Vienna manuscript against Venusin's autographs as catalogued by Andersen, 'Venusins Autografer', Fund og Forskning 60 (2022), pp. 49ff.

Identify the engraver of the 1602 Mechanica title-portrait
▼
O_MECH1602_ETH
The 1602 Nuremberg/Hülsius Mechanica carries a printed engraved title-portrait that is an anonymous, SAME-DIRECTION (not mirror-reversed) copy after De Gheyn's hat-portrait (New Hollstein 239) — cut by a Nuremberg workshop hand in Hülsius's circle. (Tier A, 2026-06-22: derived from Hülsius's own 1601 Epistolarum plate; printed by Christoph Lochner.)
Progress / what would settle it: OPEN (position as of 2026-06-22): everything except the engraver's personal name is settled. The 1602 title-portrait is an unsigned, same-direction copy after De Gheyn's beret-portrait of Tycho (New Hollstein 239; the genealogy runs Gemperlin's 1586 painting → De Gheyn's engraving, existing by December 1590 → the Nuremberg copies). It was cut from — or shares an exemplar with — the smaller plate Hülsius had already used in his 1601 Nuremberg Epistolarum reissue: both captions carry the same idiosyncratic engraver's errors (INSTVMENTORVM, ASTONOMICORVM, INVETORIS), which fixes the derivation, and both books were printed by Christoph Lochner for Levinus Hulsius. Neither Hülsius plate is recorded in the De Gheyn New Hollstein copy-list, and no signature or attribution exists in any catalogue, auction record or portrait index (VD17; the Digitaler Portraitindex, which records the plate 'nach Jaques de Gheyn … Unbez.' [unsigned]; Hollstein LX). The documentary evidence converges on the workshop of Johann Sibmacher — the only documented Hülsius engraver in Nuremberg, cutting plates for the same press in the same years and signing a comparable nobleman portrait engraving one year later (Adolf von Schwarzenberg, 1603) — but the Brahe plate's quality sits below Sibmacher's signed prime work, and Hollstein LX (Seelig 2002) does not list it, so the evidence cannot separate Sibmacher's own quick trade-plate from a hand in his large workshop. The most defensible attribution: an anonymous copperplate cut in the Sibmacher workshop (Hülsius's Nuremberg house engraving operation, Lochner's press), c.1601–02, after De Gheyn NH 239. What would settle it: expert adjudication by a print room — a connoisseurship comparison of the Brahe plate against the signed 1603 Schwarzenberg engraving and the 1602 Epitome Emblematum plates; an enquiry to the Germanisches Nationalmuseum's Graphische Sammlung is prepared.

Was the Apologia ad Craigum ever printed (Uraniburgi 1591)?
▼
O_NKS93B O_APOL_BERLIN
Bartholin claimed to own a printed copy (De cometis 1665); Lalande & Houzeau–Lancaster list a print 'Uraniburgi 1591, 4°'; Friis & Dreyer conclude it was never printed and trace the 1591 entry to Lalande/Delambre.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-05-31, high confidence NOT printed): the three authorities on Tycho's press — Friis, Dreyer and Nielsen (Bogtrykkeri 1921, pp.194–95) — agree no print was ever issued; the 'Uraniburgi 1591' imprint is a bibliographic ghost originating with Lalande (Bibliographie astronomique 1803) and repeated by Delambre and Houzeau–Lancaster. Tycho had meant to append the Apologia to De mundi Book II (letter to Hagecius, 25 Jan 1590) but dropped the plan; it circulated only as scribal copies. The lone contrary datum is Erasmus Bartholin's first-person 1665 ownership claim, which no located copy supports — but it is not dismissible (the Berlin fragment seems copied from a print), so this remains a genuine residual doubt rather than a refuted claim. Closing it fully would need a located printed copy or a pre-Bartholin independent reference.

GKS 320 Astronomia hypothetica — Tycho's hand or an assistant's?
▼
O_GKS320
A four-world-systems treatise catalogued in the Tycho orbit; the hand and authorship were unverified, with Tycho vs an assistant the assumed options.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-05-31, via the KB Primo record): neither — it is the autograph of GEORG LUDWIG FROBENIUS (1566–1645), mathematician and bookseller. The full title compares the systems 'secundum Ptolomæum, Copernicum, Tychonem Braheum et Longomontanum … juxta Astronomiæ Danicæ placita' and is marked 'Mspt. autogr.'; comparing Tycho's system WITH Longomontanus's and citing the Astronomia Danica (1622) places it firmly after Tycho's death, so it cannot be his. Tycho is only one of the four hypotheses compared. GKS 320 is a 17th-c. reception/Nachleben object, re-attributed accordingly; no Brahe/Longomontanus hand comparison is warranted.

When and how was the 1593 Vienna fair-copy lost?
▼
O_GKS1828 O_COD10667 O_COD10668
The 1593 apographum is the one gap in the Cod. 10657–10675 run, so the Historia Coelestis (printed from Vienna) lacks 1593 while it survives KB-only (Codex K / GKS 1828). The moment of loss is unfixed: before Tengnagel's 1604 handover to Kepler, between then and the 1621 imperial reclamation, or in Curtz's custody.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-06-01, to the limit of the documentary record): the terminus ante quem is Kepler's death, 15 November 1630. The prolegomena to the observation volumes of Tychonis Brahe Opera Omnia (Dreyer, TBOO X) state the 1593 fair-copy 'had already been completely lost before Kepler's death'; the Copenhagen Meddelelser (2006) commentary restates this and adds that whether the Vienna apographa reached Curtz via Kepler's 1621 surrender or via his son-in-law Jakob Bartsch's widow (Susanna Kepler) c.1634 is unknown. The archive entered Kepler's custody through the 1604 Tengnagel–Kepler contract (recited in Ferdinand II's 1621 letter), so the loss falls in 1604×1630 and cannot be narrowed further, nor the mechanism fixed. The 'sent to Cassel and there lost' story Albert Curtz (Lucius Barrettus) put in the Historia Coelestis (1666) prolegomena is a fabrication papering over a gap that already existed when he received the nineteen volumes — Dreyer (Tycho Brahe, 1890, Appendix; and TBOO) debunks it. 1593 survives KB-only: Codex K (GKS 1828 Fasc. I, ending Venus 25 Sep 1593) + four appended leaves in Codex E (GKS 313). Residual: only a 1604–1630 inventory — Kepler's note on the Codex G front board (GKS 316), or Curtz's memorandum to Count Martinitz printed in Kästner, Geschichte der Mathematik II (1797) p.651ff — could fix whether 1593 was already gone at the 1604 handover.

GKS 320 (Frobenius four-systems treatise) — provenance + is it otherwise known?
▼
O_GKS320
Now re-attributed to Georg Ludwig Frobenius (Hamburg mathematician/bookseller, 1566–1645): a 17th-c. autograph comparing Ptolemy, Copernicus, Tycho and Longomontanus, drawing on the Astronomia Danica (1622). How a Hamburg autograph reached the Danish Royal Library, and whether the treatise survives in other copies or print, is unknown.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-06-01): both sub-questions answered. Georg Ludwig Frobenius (Iphofen 1566 – Hamburg 1645) was a Hamburg printer-bookseller who spent ~6 weeks in Tycho's familia on Hven in 1591 and in 1627 printed Longomontanus's Cyclometria (Christianson, On Tycho's Island pp.285–86) — not of the Basel Froben dynasty though he believed he was; GKS 320 draws on Longomontanus's Astronomia Danica (1622) so it is a Hamburg-period work. ACQUISITION (Brøndsted, Fund og Forskning 9, 1962, p.77 + n.10): GKS 320 is one of a 3-volume set of Frobenius printer's-manuscripts (Druckmanuskripte for books he never issued) — GKS 320–322 2°, the 'Astronomia hypothetica quadriformis' (this four-systems treatise) + an 'Astrologia Ovidiana'. On Frobenius's death at Hamburg in 1645 his literary remains passed to the Hamburg cathedral chapter (Domkapitel); when the Dombibliothek was dissolved and auctioned in the 18th c., the Royal Library Copenhagen bought them 'perhaps with a view to their Tycho-Brahe material' — exactly fitting the KB catalogue's 'Indlemmet i KB senest 1787'. So the route is Frobenius → Hamburg Dombibliothek → 18th-c. dissolution auction → KB, NOT the Gottorp channel earlier hypothesised. OTHERWISE-KNOWN: no printed version and no sibling MS (EEB/VD17/USTC negative) — a unique abandoned-publication manuscript. Residual: only the exact pre-1787 auction/accession entry would date the KB purchase precisely (archival nicety). A by-product of the same article: a previously unrecorded 1584 Tycho elegy on Ptolemy, surviving in Frobenius's 1644 Almagest proof-sheet at the Royal Library, Stockholm (object O_STOCKHOLM_FROBENIUS_CARMEN).

Locate + collate the Triangulorum praxis autograph leaves at Prague
▼
O_PRAGUE_TRIANGULORUM_MS
Autograph leaves of Tycho's trigonometry (Triangulorum planorum et sphæricorum praxis) survive at Prague, known from Studnička's 1886 facsimile, but the current shelfmark is unconfirmed and they have not been collated against the printed/edited text.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-06-01): shelfmark confirmed and the hand question settled. The MS is Prague, National Library of the Czech Republic (NKP, formerly the University Library), shelfmark NK 14 C 20 (old marks Truhlář nr 2761 / Kl. nr 12:2) — 'Triangulorum planorum [et sphæricorum] praxis arithmetica', 1591, in its original binding as the closing item of a trigonometry Sammelband opened by Rheticus's Canon doctrinae triangulorum (Norlind 1970 p.234; 'Tycho Brahe and Prague — Crossroads of European Science'). Two editions already exist — Studnička's 1886 Prague phototype facsimile and Dreyer's critical text TBOO I, 283–293 (set from this very MS) — so the 'collate against the edited text' action is effectively met by Dreyer's edition; there is no independent printing to collate against (the work was never printed in Tycho's lifetime). HAND: settled — the content is Tycho's, but the script is NOT his autograph. Dreyer (TBOO I, 315) already doubted Studnička's autograph attribution, and Šíma & Valeška's forensic paleography ('Analysis of Tycho's Handwritings', in Tycho Brahe and Prague, 2002, pp.150–167) is decisive: 'Not one of the manuscripts held by the National Library, Prague was written by Tycho's hand' — NK 14 C 20 is by a professional calligrapher (a different writer from NK XII A 28). Studnička's 1901 Tycho-autograph claim is thereby overturned. Residual: only a leaf-by-leaf image check of NK 14 C 20 against Dreyer's transcription (what the facsimile / TBOO each normalise), which needs the digitised or in-hand MS — a text-collation nicety, not an attribution question.

Relationship among the three ÖNB 1000-star restitution copies (Cod. 10705/06/07)
▼
O_COD10705 O_COD10706 O_COD10707
ÖNB holds three copies of Tycho's 1600 restitution of the 1000-star catalogue (Cod. 10705 26 ll., 10706 26 ll., 10707 31 ll.); which is the master and how the three relate (copying order, added stars) is undetermined.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-06-01): the three are not a master-and-derivatives stemma but parallel PRESENTATION apographs of the 1004-star restitution (Stellarum inerrantium accurata restitutio, Wandsbek 1598/1600), copied by assistants. Their recipients differ: Cod. 10705 → Archduke Matthias; Cod. 10707 → Imperial Vice-Chancellor Rudolf Corraduz, dated by Tycho's autograph inscription March 1600 (Dreyer, TBOO III, 1916, p.417); Cod. 10706's recipient is unrecorded in both Dreyer's manuscript list (p.411) and Truffa's 2002 census. The 31-vs-26-leaf outlier 10707 is explained: it UNIQUELY carries the Stellarum inerrantium Restitutionis Appendix — 36 star-positions adapted to Rudolf II's nativity — which Dreyer printed for the first time from this codex alone (TBOO III, pp.379–389). No copying-order stemma was ever published: Dreyer judged collation of the apographs 'perfect waste of time' (Tycho Brahe, 1890, p.266) and based his edition on the Copenhagen copy GKS 306, not the Vienna ones; the modern machine-readable edition (Verbunt & van Gent, A&A 516, 2010, A28) followed him. RESIDUAL (reading-room only): the 10706 recipient + any leaf-level collation of the three against each other. (Distinct: Cod. 10709 is the earlier 1589 zodiacal catalogue, Dreyer's V¹, not a restitution copy.)

Track the De Gheyn portrait states across editions
▼
O_LN432_3
The De Gheyn copper-portrait of Tycho appears across the Epistolae / Mechanica / Progymnasmata in differing forms; the scholarship needed to be pinned down.
Resolution: RESOLVED (2026-05-31, confidence high): per the New Hollstein catalogue raisonné (De Gheyn Family, 2000) — corroborated by Mortensen 1946, Van Regteren Altena 1983, Christianson 2000, Huizinga 1910 — these are TWO separate copperplates, not states of one. Portrait A (bare-headed, NH 238) exists in two states (I without address; II adding 'Marco Sadeler excudit'), carries heraldic errors (Hønnor/Belker), is the rarer plate, supplied the model for Tycho's oval supralibros ex-libris stamp, and was used posthumously in SOME copies of the 1602 Progymnasmata (printed on the title verso). Portrait B (plumed beret, NH 239, corrected heraldry; existed by Dec 1590 per Tycho's letter sending engraved copies) is the one pasted on the title verso in most copies of the 1596 Epistolae and the 1598 Mechanica — some Mechanica copies instead carry a watercolour portrait, and the Kiel Fabricius copy none. The 1602 Hülsius Mechanica title portrait is NOT a De Gheyn: it is an anonymous SAME-DIRECTION copy of Portrait B (corrected 2026-06-03 — New Hollstein records the NH239 copies as 'in same direction'; the 'mirrored' label was Huizinga's, from comparing the beret-portrait to the bare-headed Portrait A, NH238). Hülsius's 1601 Nuremberg Epistolarum reissue carries a SEPARATE, distinct same-direction copy of Portrait B on the title verso — so Nuremberg copying of the portrait for Hülsius began in 1601, a year before the Mechanica (see Q_MECH1602_ENGRAVER). Residual: copy-by-copy state assignment within the 1598 Mechanica and 1602 Progymnasmata still needs in-hand comparison (the Mechanica census has begun this).
