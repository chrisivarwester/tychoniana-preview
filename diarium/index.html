Diarium — The Uraniborg Diary, 1582–1597

A scholarly edition of the diary kept at Uraniborg from October 1582 to April 1597: the historical notes its scribes entered in the margin, each with the diary’s own words, its page in the printed edition and a link to the scan, graded A–C for the certainty of date and person; and the daily weather record the same book was kept for, day by day, verbatim.

The book behind this edition is a quarto of 191 leaves in the Austrian National Library, Vienna, Cod. 10718, bought for the Emperor Rudolf II in all probability on 28 October 1601, four days after Tycho’s death, and in the Hofbibliothek by 1652 at the latest. It is not in Tycho’s hand. His assistants kept it — for long stretches Elias Olsen Morsing, and after him a succession of others whose changes of hand the first editor marked (§9) — recording the state of the sky over Hven nearly every day from 1 October 1582 until the entries stop in mid-page on 22 April 1597, days after the household had moved to Copenhagen. A few of its leaves are Hven paper: the watermark shows Uraniborg over the word VRANIBVRGVM.

From 27 April 1585 the scribes also began to enter, in the margin and heavily abbreviated, the events of the island: arrivals and departures, a death, a wedding, the passing of the royal fleet. These historical notes are the visitor register of Uraniborg and the first half of this edition. The weather entries they sit beside are the second half. Both were printed once, in 1876, from the Vienna manuscript, and again in 1927 in the ninth volume of the Opera Omnia; this edition is built from the 1876 print and concorded to both.

How this edition was made

Every historical note was extracted from the 1876 edition’s text and is printed here in the diary’s own words, with an English reading, the persons expanded where the editors or the diary itself expand them, and the printed page. The pages were verified by walking the whole diary day by day through the edition’s page breaks and locating each note’s text on its page; all but four of the notes were found on their page, and those four are placed by their day. Two entries that turned out to duplicate others under a wrong year or month were retired, one date was corrected against the print, and three notes that had been expanded were restored to what is printed. The weather record was parsed from the same text into one row per dated entry, keeping the early-modern Danish verbatim and adding only a small set of keyword flags, which are declared for what they are in §8.

What these numbers are not. The register is not a count of visitors: one visit can produce two notes (an arrival and a departure) or none, and the scribes noted what interested them — Thoren observes that members of James VI’s entourage went out to Hven in the weeks after the King’s visit without any trace in the diary. It is not complete against its own source either: Tycho’s own comings and goings, which the diary records constantly, were excluded by rule when the register was compiled, and Rørdam’s index names persons on some forty printed pages where this register has nothing; both are set out in §10. A person with many entries is a person the scribes named often, not necessarily the most frequent guest. And the weather flags are keyword matches on a sixteenth-century vocabulary, useful for finding a day, never a climatology.

Conventions

Dates. Every date is the diary’s, Julian (Old Style) throughout; the scribes never adopted the Gregorian reform. A note is dated to the day under whose weather entry it stands in the print.

Pages. “p. 143” is the printed page of the 1876 edition; the link opens that page of the Royal Danish Academy’s scan, whose PDF pages run 10 ahead of the print. The Opera Omnia page is given at month precision (§6).

Record keys. Every note prints its key (d-1590-03-20-a: the date, then a letter in the order of the notes under that day) and is addressable as #d-1590-03-20-a or ?entry=d-1590-03-20-a, which highlights it. Weather entries are keyed w-1590-03-20 and addressable as ?day=1590-03-20.

Excluded by rule. Tycho’s own departures and returns (abiit Hafniam, rediit) were not extracted unless a named companion is the event; the register therefore does not stand in for an itinerary, which is the Loca pillar’s business. The weather-only years before 27 April 1585 carry no historical notes.
