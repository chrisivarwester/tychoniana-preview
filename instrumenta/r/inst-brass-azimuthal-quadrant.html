Brass azimuthal quadrant

Quadrant Tycho's own design destroyed Binst-brass-azimuthal-quadrant

An early brass quadrant mounted to turn in azimuth, among the first instruments of the Hven period (1576). Not extant.

“Therefore I generally refer to this as the portable azimuth quadrant.”
Mechanica, trans. Raeder, Strömgren & Strömgren 1946, p. 22

How it works

A brass altitude-azimuth quadrant on a horizontal circle: swing in azimuth onto the body, slide the alidade onto it to read altitude on the arc, and read the bearing on the azimuth circle — altitude and azimuth together. Light enough to dismount for travel.

Built
1576
In use until
1601
Survival
destroyed — in the aftermath of the Bohemian war of 1619 (1619 is the collective terminus the sources give; §10)
Dimensions
radius one and a half cubits — Tycho's own figure, ≈58 cm (Mechanica 1946, Fig. 3, p. 21)
Radius
58 cm
Material
light brass, with an azimuth circle Ø117 cm
Reading resolution
read to ≈1′ (neither Nonius nor transversal on this one) (finest stated reading ≈ 60″)
Where it stood
larger southern observatory, Uraniborg — The great southern observatory, Uraniborg (observatories)
Measurement problem
