9After Tycho

The apparatus left Hven with Tycho in 1597 and reached Bohemia in 1599–1600, twenty-eight instruments by the count of the time. After his death in October 1601 the Emperor bought the instruments and the observation journals from the heirs for 20,000 thalers, of which a fraction was ever paid; Kepler was denied their use and observed with a quadrant and a sextant lent by Baron Hoffmann. An imperial order of 1604 to pack the instruments for Vienna was apparently not carried out, and a rescript of 1609 kept them in Bohemia. Gassendi, the source of every later account, places their destruction in the disturbances of 1619; the legend that attaches it to the battle of the White Mountain in 1620 is a misreading of him. The inventory of the Prague treasury of December 1621 still lists the great globe and “a quadrant with many appurtenant instruments” at the Belvedere, and in 1672 an English envoy reported that the instruments had long since been sold to brass-founders. The globe alone travelled on, to the Jesuit college at Neisse and in 1632 as war booty to Copenhagen, where it burned in 1728. The two Prague sextants passed from Rudolf’s Kunstkammer through the Armoury and the auction of 1782 to the museum that holds them. The table dates each step and names its source.

1601–1608The Emperor secures the observations and instruments from the heirs, who had asked 100,000 thalers, for 20,000 at six per cent; 4,000 are paid in 1602 and 1,000 in 1608, and nothing after. The claim was still unsettled in 1671.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 17
1602Kepler, denied the use of the instruments, which are stored in a cellar under the Curtius house, is lent a quadrant and a sextant by Baron J. F. Hoffmann.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 18; Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890), p. 365
1604, 27 MarchAn imperial order to Kepler to pack the instruments and send them to Vienna 'for greater safety'; apparently not carried out.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 19
1609, JulyAn imperial rescript on the heirs' claim states that the instruments and books 'remain in this Crown of Bohemia'. When the Curtius house changes hands the instruments return to the Belvedere.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 19
1619Destruction in the disturbances that followed the death of Emperor Matthias, when Prague was held for Frederick of the Palatinate: some instruments converted to other uses, the rest destroyed or dispersed (Gassendi, Vita, 1655, p. 216). The version that attaches the loss to the battle of the White Mountain, 8 November 1620, is a misreading of Gassendi.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 20; Wolfschmidt, 'Observatories and Instruments' (2002), p. 208
1621, 6 DecemberThe inventory of the Prague treasury and Kunstkammer still lists, in the Belvedere, 'no. 1424, a very large celestial globe of metal' and 'no. 1425, a quadrant with many appurtenant instruments'.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 20
c. 1625The globe is acquired by Christoph Scheiner for the Jesuit college at Neisse in Silesia.Mortensen, 'Fra Tycho Brahes Bogsamling' (1955); Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 20
1632Prince Ulrik, son of Christian IV, takes the globe from Neisse as war booty to Copenhagen, where it is placed in the observatory of the Round Tower.Mortensen, 'Fra Tycho Brahes Bogsamling' (1955); Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 21

c. 1780The Prague observatory's earliest inventory lists 'No. 4, a large sextant of Tycho Brahe, made in the year 1600 by Erasmus Habermel' and 'No. 5, a smaller sextant of Tycho Brahe, which he brought with him to Prague'.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), p. 22
1782At Joseph II's auction of the Castle collections the two sextants are sold to Franz Leonard Herget, public teacher of mechanics and hydraulics.Fučíková, 'The Belvedere in Prague' (2002), pp. 276–281
1852The Prague observatory sells discarded instruments; the 'Tychonic' pieces among them, a sundial inscribed 'Insula Heveen A.D. 1578' and a planetarium engraved 'Ty. de Brahe', are forgeries, bought by the painter Gabriel Max and given in 1909 to the Deutsches Museum in Munich.Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942), pp. 21–22
1901Weinek publishes the two Prague sextants, with plates.Weinek, Die Tychonischen Instrumente (1901)
20th centuryThe two sextants pass to the Národní technické muzeum, Prague, where they are displayed.Fučíková, 'The Belvedere in Prague' (2002), p. 281; Šíma, 'Prague Sextants of Tycho Brahe' (1993)
