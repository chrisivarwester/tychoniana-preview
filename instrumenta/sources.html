  1. Mechanica (1598; TBOO V)
    Tycho Brahe, Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (Wandsbek: [the author], 1598); ed. J. L. E. Dreyer in Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia, 15 vols (Copenhagen, 1913–1929), vol. V, pp. 3–162.
    Contributes The primary text: Tycho's own description of every engraved instrument, its use and its dimensions in his cubits and feet; chapter 25 names the instruments he had not yet engraved. Read here through the tekstnet edition (Latin with the Danish translation) in Scripta.
    Cited by 24 records
    Open copy Opera Omnia vol. V, digitised (Internet Archive)
  2. Raeder, Strömgren & Strömgren (1946)
    Hans Raeder, Elis Strömgren and Bengt Strömgren (trans. and eds), Tycho Brahe's Description of his Instruments and Scientific Work as given in Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (Wandesburgi 1598) (Copenhagen: Det Kongelige Danske Videnskabernes Selskab / Ejnar Munksgaard, 1946).
    Contributes The English translation quoted verbatim in every engraved instrument's record, with its figure number and page; the editors' metrology (1 cubit = 388 mm, 1 foot = 259 mm, p. 9) converts Tycho's stated dimensions to centimetres. Not freely available online.
    Cited by 24 records
  3. Wennberg, Tänk, om det är så! (2007)
    Arne Wennberg, Tänk, om det är så! Om Tycho Brahes instrument och vad han kunde göra med dem, 2nd ed. (Landskrona: Kulturnämnden, 2007), ISBN 91-630-4099-9; the plate appendix, pp. 48–69, reproduces the twenty-two Mechanica engravings one to a page.
    Contributes The measuring geometry ('how it works') of each instrument, its radius, material, placement in the observatories and reading accuracy; the cross-cutting techniques (transversal scale, slit-sights, the abandoned Nonius). Print only.
    Cited by 26 records
  4. Dreyer, Tycho Brahe (1890)
    J. L. E. Dreyer, Tycho Brahe: A Picture of Scientific Life and Work in the Sixteenth Century (Edinburgh: A. & C. Black, 1890).
    Contributes The student cross-staff and the 1563 conjunction, the globe's shell (p. 112), the clocks Kepler used after 1601 (p. 365), and the standard account of the instruments' removal to Bohemia.
    Cited by 9 records
    Open copy digitised (Internet Archive)
  5. Wolfschmidt, 'Observatories and Instruments' (2002)
    Gudrun Wolfschmidt, 'The Observatories and Instruments of Tycho Brahe', in John Robert Christianson, Alena Hadravová, Petr Hadrava and Martin Šolc (eds), Tycho Brahe and Prague: Crossroads of European Science (Frankfurt am Main: Harri Deutsch, 2002), pp. 203–216.
    Contributes The count of twenty-two principal instruments by type (p. 207), the dimensions used here where the 1946 text does not settle them, the destruction after 1619, and the two Prague sextants as 'so-called Tychonic' (p. 208, Figure 6 on p. 215).
    Cited by 17 records
  6. Mosley, Bearing the Heavens (2007), ch. 4
    Adam Mosley, Bearing the Heavens: Tycho Brahe and the Astronomical Community of the Late Sixteenth Century (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2007), ch. 4, 'Instruments'.
    Contributes The great globe's two-year shape-stabilisation, the 1584 horizon inscription and the caveat on the Schissler identification; the Wittich–Kassel dispute over the sextant and the slit-sights.
    Cited by 5 records
  7. Thoren, 'New Light on Tycho's Instruments' (1973)
    Victor E. Thoren, 'New Light on Tycho's Instruments', Journal for the History of Astronomy 4:1 (1973), pp. 25–45.
    Contributes The redating of individual instruments from the observation logs. Cited here at second hand, through Thoren 1990; not yet read for this edition (see Desiderata).
    Cited by 1 record
    Open copy DOI (SAGE; a scan is held by the ADS under 1973JHA.....4...25T)
  8. Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg (1990)
    Victor E. Thoren (with John R. Christianson), The Lord of Uraniborg: A Biography of Tycho Brahe (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1990), ch. 5, 'Urania's Castle', pp. 144–191.
    Contributes The production history and performance of the instruments: the first quadrant (p. 77), the transversal scale first applied 2 May 1580 (p. 156), the five-cubit steel quadrant of 21 March 1581 and the mural quadrant of June 1582 (pp. 162–163), the bipartite arc and the great rulers of 1583 (pp. 169–171), the equatorial armillaries (pp. 173–175), the revolving quadrants (p. 177), the Copernicus relic (p. 196) and the accuracy actually achieved (pp. 190–191). Table 5.1 (p. 172), the 'genealogical chart' of the instruments, is the concordance's spine.
    Cited by 10 records
  9. Šíma, 'Prague Sextants of Tycho Brahe' (1993)
    Zdislav Šíma, 'Prague Sextants of Tycho Brahe', Annals of Science 50:5 (1993), pp. 445–453.
    Contributes The modern attribution of the two Prague sextants to Bürgi and Habermel, with their measurements. Cited through Wolfschmidt 2002; not yet read in full (see Desiderata).
    Cited by 4 records
    Open copy DOI (Taylor & Francis)
  10. Christianson, On Tycho's Island (2000)
    John Robert Christianson, On Tycho's Island: Tycho Brahe and his Assistants, 1570–1601 (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2000).
    Contributes The globe's shell built by Schissler of fir staves and rings (p. 358), and the assistants who worked the instruments.
    Cited by 1 record
  11. Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag' (1942)
    Harald Mortensen, 'Tycho Brahe Minder i Prag', Astronomiska Sällskapet Tycho Brahe, Årsbok 1942 (Lund, 1942), pp. 13–27 (offprint).
    Contributes The fate of the apparatus after 1601 from the Bohemian sources (pp. 17–22): the Emperor's purchase for 20,000 thalers, the 1604 order to send the instruments to Vienna, the 1609 rescript keeping them in Bohemia, the destruction of 1619 (Gassendi) against the White Mountain legend, the 1621 Belvedere inventory (the globe as no. 1424), the 1672 report that the instruments were 'sold to brass-founders', and the 1852 Prague sale of forged 'Tychonic' pieces now in the Deutsches Museum; the two Prague sextants with the c. 1780 inventory wording, the Habermel signature and the Klein attribution (p. 22).
    Cited by 22 records
    Open copy digitised (Danskernes Historie Online)
  12. Weinek, Die Tychonischen Instrumente (1901)
    Ladislaus Weinek, Die Tychonischen Instrumente auf der Prager Sternwarte (Prague: K. k. Sternwarte, 1901), 28 pp., 2 plates.
    Contributes The first monograph on the two Prague sextants, with phototype plates of both; the older attribution of the smaller one.
    Cited by 4 records
    Open copy digitised (Internet Archive)
  13. Rankl, 'Der Tychonische Sextant … Kremsmünster' (1946)
    P. Richard Rankl, 'Der Tychonische Sextant (von Bürgi) in der Sternwarte Kremsmünster', 89. Jahresbericht des Obergymnasiums der Benediktiner zu Kremsmünster, Schuljahr 1946 (Linz, 1946), pp. 33–47.
    Contributes The iron sextant at Kremsmünster (arms 112 cm, brass limb divided by transversals to one arcminute) that closely matches the smaller Prague sextant — the comparandum for the Bürgi attribution.
    Cited by 2 records
    Open copy transcribed with new photographs by the Kremsmünster observatory (2000)
  14. Mortensen, 'Fra Tycho Brahes Bogsamling' (1955)
    Harald Mortensen, 'Fra Tycho Brahes Bogsamling', Fund og Forskning i Det kongelige Biblioteks Samlinger 2 (1955), pp. 25–32.
    Contributes The great globe's road home: the 1621 Belvedere inventory, Scheiner's acquisition for the Jesuit college at Neisse (c. 1625), Prince Ulrik's removal to Copenhagen (1632) and the fire of 1728.
    Cited by 4 records
    Open copy open access (tidsskrift.dk)
  15. Fučíková, 'The Belvedere in Prague' (2002)
    Eliška Fučíková, 'The Belvedere in Prague as Tycho Brahe's Musaeum', in Christianson et al. (eds), Tycho Brahe and Prague: Crossroads of European Science (Frankfurt am Main: Harri Deutsch, 2002), pp. 276–281.
    Contributes The later provenance of the two surviving sextants: Rudolf II's Kunstkammer, the 1648 Swedish removal they were too large for, the 1650 Armoury inventory, the 1782 auction to Herget, and the National Technical Museum.
    Cited by 6 records
  16. Mechanica, 1598 — Royal Library copy 1, digitised
    Tycho Brahe, Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (Wandsbek, 1598), Det Kongelige Bibliotek, Copenhagen, LN 432 2°, copy 1; 152 page images.
    Contributes The whole 1598 book online. The plates reproduced in the Register were cropped from the same library's copy 2 (LN 432 2°), which is not online; no page-level concordance between the two copies has been made.
    Cited by 1 record
    Open copy Internet Archive (Early European Books)
  17. Mechanica, 1602 — Smithsonian digital edition
    Tycho Brahe, Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (Nuremberg: Levinus Hulsius, 1602), Smithsonian Libraries, Dibner Library of the History of Science and Technology; digital edition with one page per plate.
    Contributes A per-plate link for every engraved instrument, in the second edition's re-cut plates; the census of that edition is in Typographica.
    Cited by 0 records
    Open copy Smithsonian Libraries
