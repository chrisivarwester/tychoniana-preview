Written Record

Typographica

The Printed Works

The printed books as objects: the 12 established titles together with the lost, disputed, and related prints and one never-printed poem — 21 in all, each with its bibliographical record and, where an edition has been censused, a copy-by-copy account of its survivors. What the books say is readable elsewhere: the letters in the Epistolarium, the other texts in Scripta.

Epistolarium

The Letters

The complete correspondence of Tycho Brahe — all 509 known letters, 1568–1601, in parallel original text and English, with an index of correspondents, a map of the exchange, and its year-by-year rhythm.

Observationes

The Archive & the Sky

The 37-volume archive of Tycho's nightly observations — and the sky drawn from it: his star catalogue — 777 stars in the printed catalogue, expanded to just over 1,000 in the manuscript presented to patrons — charted in the coordinates he assigned them, with the 1572 nova and the 1577 comet that broke the unchanging heavens.

Horoscopi

The Astrological Record

The 15 documents of Tycho's astrological practice — natal horoscopes for the Danish royal princes, in manuscript and in print, and astrological judgments for the Holy Roman Emperor Rudolf II.

Scripta

Tycho's Writings — The Reading Room

The 57 Tycho texts of the modern critical edition (Zeeberg et al.) beyond the correspondence — poems, inscriptions, orations, horoscopes, recipes, and three of the printed books (two in full, the third linked page by page at the source) — with Peter Zeeberg's Danish translation beside the Latin for ten of them. Manuscript works and printed books alike belong here as texts; the printed books as objects — editions, presses, surviving copies — are Typographica's.
Material World

Bibliotheca

The Library

A reconstruction of Tycho's personal library — estimated at 1,000–3,000 volumes, scattered after his death in 1601. 204 entries are attested by the surviving volumes, a century of catalogues, and his own letters; 107 works survive today in 69 physical volumes, almost all still together in Prague, and 58 works are readable online (many share a volume).

Instrumenta

The Instruments

The 26 documented instruments of Tycho's career, from his student cross-staff to the great instruments of Uraniborg and Stjerneborg — the 21 he engraved plate by plate in his Astronomiae instauratae mechanica (1598), built ever larger and steadier to read the sky to about one arcminute. The great instruments are all lost or destroyed; 2 sextants in Prague, attributed to him, survive. The book is all that remains of them, and the observations they made possible, which Kepler inherited.

Iconographia

Portraits & Images

The 32 documented images of Tycho Brahe — the de Gheyn engraving, the gilt stamp on his book-bindings, the Uraniborg views — tracing his portrait campaign and visual legacy.

Loca

The Geography of a Life

Every place Tycho was bodily present, 1546–1601 — 71 places and 140 documented, page-cited stays from Knutstorp to the Týn church, with the journeys, a phantom register of places he was not, and the island establishment in depth.
People & History

Familia

Tycho's Circle

A directory of the people around Tycho: 189 assistants, correspondents, patrons, instrument-makers, and printers who shaped his observatory and its output.

Receptio

Afterlife & Legacy

The 28 episodes of Tycho's afterlife — Kepler's inheritance of the observations, the Rudolphine Tables, the modern exhumations, and the lunar crater that bears his name.

Diarium

The Diary — Visitors & Events

The 250 historical notes (Notae historicae) from Tycho's meteorological diary — a documentary log of every named arrival, departure, and death at Uraniborg, 1585–1597, including 15 royal visits and the stays of Rothmann, Blaeu, and James VI of Scotland.
