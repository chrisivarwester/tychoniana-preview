This page is the apparatus that the twelve chapters of Tychoniana share: Observationes, Typographica, Epistolarium, Bibliotheca, Instrumenta, Loca, Familia, Iconographia, Horoscopi, Diarium, Receptio and Scripta. It holds no record of its own. Every row here is a dated, placed or missing thing whose full record lives in one of those chapters, and every row links to it.

Three things are done here that no single chapter can do. The general chronology (§2) puts the printed editions, the instruments, the sites, the observation volumes, the diary, the likenesses, the letters and the reception on one axis, in the eight periods of the itinerary and then the afterlife, with a year index for the fifty-six years of the life. The holdings (§3) give, kind by kind and then city by city, what survives of Tycho’s world and where it is kept today — the copies of his books, the volumes of his library, the likenesses, the observation codices, the specimens of his hand and the two surviving instruments in one table. The desiderata (§4) gather what each chapter records as lost, destroyed, doubtful or unresolved, and hold the 30 questions of the manuscript archive and the presses until the chapters they belong to carry them. The method for reading Tycho’s hand is a separate chapter, Manus.

Each row prints the chapter it belongs to and the key of its record. Each figure on this page is defined once, in the table below, and recurs with the same definition in the section that uses it.

What the numbers are not. No figure on this page is a sum across kinds: an instrument that was destroyed, a copy whose attribution is doubtful and a library title not yet traced are three different facts and are counted apart. No ratio here is a survival rate; a thing enters the located tier because a copy was found, so a located count measures the search as much as the survival. The copies of §3 come from 24 censuses that are still provisional — each names, beside its row, the union catalogues it has not yet reached, and its counts will rise — and 5 later printings recorded in Typographica (Nuremberg 1602 (Hülsius), Prague 1603 (Schumann re-issue), Frankfurt 1610 (Tampach), Frankfurt 1610 (Tampach), Frankfurt 1610 (Tampach)) have no census, so no copy of them can appear here. The 509 letters are those the Epistolarium holds, not the whole correspondence. Nothing is drawn on the chronology that no chapter records: the life is Loca’s 8 periods and 21 journeys, not a list of anecdotes, and the afterlife is Receptio’s 28 episodes, the instruments’ 21 dated fates and the 7 printings of 1602–1610. Two chapters have as yet no per-record anchors, the Horoscopi and the Epistolarium, so their rows link to the chapter and not to the record.
