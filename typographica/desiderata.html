What this edition does not yet know, does not yet have, or cannot yet close — each item with its source and with what would settle it. Nothing here is hidden in a footnote elsewhere.

The Mechanica's collation in the Dansk Bibliografi

The formula in DB 432 as transcribed for this edition — ):(:(⁴ A⁴ B⁴–F⁴ G⁸ H⁴ — sums to 40 leaves against the 42 the entry states; Nielsen 1921 (p. 205) gives ):(⁴ A⁶ B⁴–F⁴ G⁶ H⁶, which sums, and is followed here. Whether the DB entry itself or the house transcription carries the error should be checked on the page image.

Sources: Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600 (1931–1933), Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

The Apologia's witnesses

The Berlin fragment copied from the 1591 print (prefaced by Craig's three letters) is most likely a war loss; its shelfmark and the Kraków alternative are unresolved. The Kepler-papers copy identified by Norlind as Pulkovo MSS vol. 19, fols 115–150 (St Petersburg) and the Longomontanus–From manuscript named by Gassendi are untraced. Any of them would carry the 1591 text; only the Berlin fragment could show the print's own setting.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921), Gassendi, Tychonis Brahei vita (1654; 1655).

Tycho's working copy of the Progymnasmata (Königsberg)

Given by Tycho to Johannes Müller in March 1598, corrected throughout in his hand, with twelve blank leaves bound in where the Moon section was pending; last at the Staats- und Universitätsbibliothek Königsberg, untraced since the dispersal of 1944–45. It is the one witness that could fix the boundary between what was printed on Hven and what was completed at Prague. The Königsberg holdings now at Toruń, Olsztyn, Vilnius and Kaliningrad have not been asked.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921), Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600 (1931–1933).

Five posthumous issues without a census

De mundi 1603 and 1610, Epistolae 1602 and 1610, Progymnasmata 1610 are described here from Nielsen but have no copy-by-copy census; the Epistolae issues are counted inside the 1596 census as issue-states, the De mundi issues excluded from theirs by rule, the Progymnasmata 1610 nowhere. Tampach's stocktake of 1,197 copies suggests the Frankfurt issues survive in numbers; Nielsen calls them common. A census of each would complete the copy layer.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

Four printings of which nothing is preserved

The second volume of the Epistolae (about 50 leaves printed, 1597), the third tome of the Progymnasmata (begun 1590), and the two printings of the Moon section (Hamburg 1598, scrapped; Wittenberg 1599, abandoned) are entered under the presses (§6) on Nielsen's authority and not as editions. Should a sheet of any of them be identified — in a binding, as printer's waste, as the Prague compositors' copy — it would become an edition row.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

The inserted leaf in the Prague University copy of the Epistolae

Nielsen (1921, p. 201 n. 4) records in the Prague University Library copy an inserted leaf 'Tabulae conversionum graduum …' marked 'Post fol. 108', not printed at Uraniborg. No house store records it; the copy's census entry should.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

The Icones states copy by copy

The Vienna copy of the 1596 album has a reset title-leaf and a printed afterword not printed at Uraniborg (Nielsen: most likely by de Ohr at Hamburg, early 1598); the Wrocław copy has the afterword copied in by hand on Bl. 11a. The census records the states in prose; a state field per copy would let the table show them.

Sources: Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600 (1931–1933), Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

Was the Lange poem the first thing printed at Uraniborg?

A note in the shared thesaurus claimed that Tycho's diary records the Lange poem as the first print of the press (27 November 1584). The printed Diarium carries no such entry, Nielsen names the Pratensis epitaph as probably the earliest print, and the Ulfeldt poem carries the earliest date (25 November 1584). The claim is marked unverified until a source is produced.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

Five copy records to disposition in the censuses

The retired hand-picked copy list carried five records the censuses do not hold or hold differently: a Mechanica 1598 at Prague under the shelfmark AG XI 56 (the Strahov Hasenburg copy is Hasselberg 23); the Pruskovský copy placed at Křivoklát where the census has the Národní muzeum; a Copenhagen 1592 Progymnasmata title-leaf; a De mundi 1603 at the Library of Congress; three Epistolae 1610 at the Royal Danish Library. Each is filed as a census proposal, not silently dropped.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).

Concordance numbers not yet entered

Houzeau and Lancaster's numbers, Bibliotheca Danica's entries and the VD16 numbers for the German-printed issues are not yet in the concordance (§5); Norman's number is entered for the 1602 Mechanica only.

Sources: Nielsen, Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921).
