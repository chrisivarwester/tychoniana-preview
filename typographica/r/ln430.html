Volume I of Tycho's astronomical correspondence with the court of Hesse-Kassel — and the printed form of letters whose autograph originals survive at Marburg.

Nielsen's description
Immediately after Tycho's death the heirs sold the stock of the Epistolae to the Nuremberg bookseller Levinus Hülsius, who had a new title-quire printed — the title with Noribergae apud Levinum Hulsium … anno MDCI (a misprint for 1602), the rest of the quire a reprint of the old — and issued the book the same year; the old colophon retained. The material is Christoph Lochner's (Nielsen 1921, pp. 211–212).
Note
Not a new edition: Hülsius bought up the remaining Uraniborg stock and reprinted the title-leaf only — the rest of the book is the 1596 Uraniborg sheets, with the Uraniborg colophon retained. The new title-leaf misprints ANNO MDCI (1601) for 1602 (Nielsen), which is why libraries and the trade catalogue this issue as 1601.

Nielsen’s record and the apparatus

Collation
176 leaves. Signatures: ✱⁴ (:)⁴ (:)⁴ )✱(⁴ ✱(⁴ A⁴–Z⁴ AA⁴–QQ⁴. Paginated [40] 1–309 [3] (some pagination errors). 40 lines. Type-area: 185 × 124 mm. Running titles and catchwords. Roman (smaller passages italic and Fraktur). Initials (and majuscules used as initials) and vignettes. Woodcuts and figures. Tables.
Contents
Bl. 1a title; 2–4a commendatory poems (Voitus, Chytraeus, Hoier, Riccobonus, Tubanellus, Plecius); Bl. 5–12 dedication to Landgrave Moritz of Hesse, dated the vernal equinox (20 March) 1596; Bl. 13–20 prefaces; Bl. 21a (p. 1) the letters and replies between Tycho and Wilhelm IV's court astronomer Christopher Rothmann; Bl. 176a the author's emblem and colophon. In many copies an engraved portrait of Tycho is pasted onto the verso of the title-leaf.
Production
Long in the press: as early as 1590 Tycho's pupil Gellius Sascerides handed the printed sheet G — carrying important star observations — to Magini in Bologna. By 1590 two presses ran at Uraniborg at once, one on the Progymnasmata and one on this correspondence. Quires LL and MM (pp. 265–80) exist in two settings; the Gheyn copper-portrait was cut c. 1590, probably for this book.
Copies, as described
Nielsen's 1931 list names some thirty-eight libraries; his list is not yet exhausted by the census. Quires LL and MM exist in two settings. The Nuremberg (1602) and Frankfurt (1610) issues are the same sheets under new title-leaves and are censused with the edition.

Editorial note
Dedicated to the young Landgrave Moritz (successor to Wilhelm IV), the volume prints Tycho's correspondence with Wilhelm IV and the Kassel astronomer Christopher Rothmann, with a German-and-Latin description of Uraniborg and Stjerneborg and new woodcut views. The autograph originals survive in the Kassel-retained convolute HStAM Best. 3 II, Nr. 1070 (former 4a.31.14) at Marburg — several letters holograph — and Tycho cut and revised the letters for print, making the convolute the primary collation source against this edition. Re-issued by Hülsius (Nuremberg 1602, new title-leaf only) and Tampach (Frankfurt 1610).
