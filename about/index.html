About this archive

Tychoniana is a structured investigation of Tycho Brahe (1546–1601) — his printed works, correspondence, observations, library, instruments, and world — gathered into a set of interconnected domains so that the links between them, and the gaps in the record, are visible.

What this is

Each of the twelve domains presented on the portal is a self-contained explorer built from a citation-grade dataset. They cross-reference one another: a letter names a person in the Familia; a printed work links to the copy-by-copy census of its survivors; an instrument points to the observations made with it. Nothing is asserted without a source, and uncertain claims are graded and flagged rather than hidden.

Five further views cut across the domains rather than holding records of their own — reading the same material along the axes of time, place, open question, gap, and hand; they are gathered on Across All Twelve. Several domains also carry more than one presentation of the same records: the switcher tabs at the top of a domain page (the Epistolarium’s Reader / Correspondents / Map / Rhythm, for instance) switch between views without leaving the data.

Start anywhere — or use the search on the portal, which reaches across every domain at once.

Reading the evidence — the confidence grades

Where an attribution, identification, or date could be wrong, it carries a single-letter grade. The grade is a claim about how well the evidence supports it, not about importance.

A
Secure
Directly documented — attested by a primary source or a surviving object examined first-hand.
B
Probable
Strong but indirect evidence — a reliable secondary record, or a sound inference from converging sources.
C
Possible
Plausible but uncertain — the evidence is thin, conflicting, or depends on an unverified identification.
D
Doubtful
Weak or speculative — recorded for completeness and flagged as such; not to be relied upon.

Dates

Tycho worked under the Julian calendar. Unless a date is explicitly marked New Style (N.S.), dates here are Old Style (Julian) as given in the sources — in the later sixteenth century the Julian date runs ten days behind the Gregorian. Year-spans on observation volumes and the visitor register follow the same convention.

What is not here — reading the gaps

This archive is built to show its own holes rather than paper over them. Every view keeps an honest remainder: letters with no recorded place of writing, instruments that state no numeric accuracy, people who carry no dates, copies that cannot be put on a map — each is counted and labelled in its view’s legend rather than silently dropped. Lacunae is the dedicated map of the holes: the lost works, instruments, sites and images; the censused copies of uncertain attribution; the books from Tycho’s library attested but not yet located.

Three further limits apply. Positions can be schematic: the Uraniborg plan is a diagram after Tycho’s own printed views (Epistolae astronomicae, Mechanica), not a survey, and some map pins are approximate. Identifications can be provisional: a B- or C-graded entry may be revised or withdrawn as evidence improves — the grade travels with the claim. Coverage follows the sources: what survives of Tycho’s world is what centuries of fire, war, and auction allowed to survive; the archive records the documented residue, not the original whole.

How it is built

The record rests on the primary editions and manuscripts together with the standard scholarship, each cited at the point of use. The works relied on across all domains are:

  • J. L. E. Dreyer, Tycho Brahe: A Picture of Scientific Life and Work in the Sixteenth Century (Edinburgh: Adam & Charles Black, 1890).
  • Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia, ed. J. L. E. Dreyer, 15 vols (Copenhagen: Gyldendal, 1913–1929).
  • Tycho Brahe’s Description of His Instruments and Scientific Work, trans. and eds. Hans Raeder, Elis Strömgren & Bengt Strömgren (Copenhagen: Det Kongelige Danske Videnskabernes Selskab, 1946).
  • Victor E. Thoren, The Lord of Uraniborg: A Biography of Tycho Brahe, with contributions by John R. Christianson (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1990).
  • John Robert Christianson, On Tycho’s Island: Tycho Brahe and His Assistants, 1570–1601 (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2000).
  • Lauritz Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600, med særligt Hensyn til dansk Bogtrykkerkunsts Historie (Copenhagen: Gyldendalske Boghandel · Nordisk Forlag, 1931–1933).
  • Lauritz Nielsen, “Tycho Brahes Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen”, Nordisk tidskrift för bok- och biblioteksväsen 8 (1921), 175–213.
  • Owen Gingerich, An Annotated Census of Copernicus’ De revolutionibus (Nuremberg, 1543 and Basel, 1566) (Leiden: Brill, 2002).
  • Adam Mosley, Bearing the Heavens: Tycho Brahe and the Astronomical Community of the Late Sixteenth Century (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2007).
  • Wilhelm Norlind, Tycho Brahe: En levnadsteckning med nya bidrag belysande hans liv och verk (Lund: Gleerup, 1970).
  • Tycho Brahes tekster, eds. Peter Zeeberg et al., Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab — tekstnet.dk.

Literature specific to one domain — the library catalogues, the Uraniborg reconstructions — is cited within that domain. The explorers are static pages: no account, no tracking, nothing to install.

To cite a page, use the ready-made citation line in its footer, which names the page and its build date; individual records — a censused copy, an entry in the library — have stable permalinks suitable for citing a single record.

Tychoniana is compiled and maintained by Christian Westergaard, Copenhagen.
