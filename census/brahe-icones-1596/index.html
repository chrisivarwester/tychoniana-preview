Methodology

Inclusion: All physical copies of the c.1596 Uraniborg first edition of Icones instrumentorum a Tychone Brahe elaboratorum (Nielsen §975), 4to, 12 leaves: title-leaf + 9 leaves bearing 18 woodcut instrument-illustrations (one per page, without captions) + 2 originally-blank leaves, issued as a supplement to the 1596 Epistolae (whose bound-in instrument descriptions supplied the captions). Both states are in scope and tracked as issue_state: the standard Uraniborg setting (manuscript afterword on leaf 11, as in the Breslau copy) and the reset Wien state (printed afterword on leaf 11 with a reset title-leaf — typographically printed OFF Uraniborg, most likely in Hamburg by Philip de Ohr in early 1598, the afterword announcing the forthcoming Mechanica). Excluded: The earlier 1585 Icones instrumentorum duorum (Sextans + Armillae Aequatoriae, Nielsen §976) — a distinct 2-leaf transverse-folio broadside, its own potential census key. All facsimiles, the Opera Omnia reproductions, and manuscript copies. Counted as a copy: Any physical exemplar with >=50% of the original leaves preserved, OR an identifiable fragment (>=1 original leaf) with documented institutional or named-private provenance. Given the extreme rarity (3 recorded copies), any provenanced fragment counts and is significant. Prior census base: Lauritz Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551-1600 (Copenhagen, 1931-33) §975 records 3 copies: Breslau (Wroclaw, University Library) + Stockholm KB + Wien Osterreichische Nationalbibliothek. No dedicated census exists. Confidence grading: Pearson A-D Copy-ID scheme: stable institutional-slug; never renumbered on sort

Bibliography of prior censuses

No dedicated census. Lauritz Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600 (Copenhagen, 1931–33), §975 is the record: Uraniborg, c.1596; 4to, 12 leaves (title + 9 leaves of 18 captionless woodcut instrument-illustrations + 2 blank), issued as a supplement to the 1596 Epistolae. Nielsen records three copies (he personally inspected Breslau and Wien): Breslau/Wrocław, Stockholm KB, Wien ÖNB.

The two states (Nielsen §975)

  • Uraniborg state — original title-leaf; in the Breslau copy the explanatory afterword is copied by hand on leaf 11a. Stockholm is the same Uraniborg state.
  • Off-Uraniborg state ("Wien state") — the Wien ÖNB copy has a reset title-leaf and a printed italic afterword ("INSPECTORI HARVM ICONVM TYCHO BRAHE S.D.") on leaf 11a. Nielsen's typographic analysis shows neither was printed at Uraniborg — most likely Hamburg, by Philip de Ohr, early 1598, the afterword announcing the forthcoming Astronomiae Mechanica. This makes the Icones the immediate typographic predecessor of the 1598 Mechanica.

The fourth copy (beyond Nielsen)

  • Thesaurus Librorum Danicorum §257 + Bruun Rasmussen sale 908 (8 June 2022, lot 176) record a fourth copy: an Epistolae+Icones sammelband in antiphonary-leaf vellum that surfaced in Germany c.1930 → Dr A. Lindskoug, Malmö → Lindskoug sale 1937 (DKK 3,600) → private Danish collection 80+ yrs → BR 2022 (hammer DKK 1.7M). On binding and Epistolae-bound match this is almost certainly a copy in a private collection, Copenhagen — recorded as the 4th copy, federated with brahe-epistolae-1596.
  • Norlind (1970): Christian IV and Chancellor Kristen Friis each received a copy bound at the end of their Epistolae (Dec 1596) — the original distribution mode.

The 18 woodblocks were reused (with 4 new copperplates) in the 1598 Mechanica. Do not confuse with the 1585 Icones instrumentorum DUORUM (Nielsen §976) — out of scope.
