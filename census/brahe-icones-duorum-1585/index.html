The Icones instrumentorum duorum (1585) is Tycho Brahe's first printed instrument-illustration: two transverse-folio broadsides — the Sextans Astronomicus (his great triangular sextant) and the Armillae Aequatoriae (his equatorial armillary), each a woodcut with a Latin caption and an explanatory paragraph. It is the direct predecessor of both the c.1596 Icones instrumentorum (Nielsen §975) and the engraved instrument plates of the 1598 Astronomiae instauratae mechanica.

The only known exemplar survives at the Hessisches Staatsarchiv Marburg, Best. 3 II, Nr. 1070 (former signature 4 a 31 Nr. 14) — the unit "Korrespondenz mit Tycho Brahe in astronomischen, astrologischen und mathematischen Angelegenheiten sowie über Instrumente," 1585–1592 (the Tycho ⇄ Wilhelm IV correspondence). The broadsides are not a free-standing print but printed enclosures to Tycho's autograph letter to Landgrave Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel (Uraniborg, 1 March 1586; received at Kassel 10 April 1586, carried by Peter Jacobsen Flemløse). The covering letter — which describes exactly these instruments and announces the forthcoming Mechanica — is edited and translated in full as the bilingual critical edition brahe_to_wilhelm_1586 (Desktop). Within the digitisation the Sextans is at image 0012, the Armillae at 0013, the companion Orthographia at 0001–0002, the letter at 0003–0010. Dreyer reproduced the plates from this original in Opera Omnia VI, p. 349; Mosley (2007, p. 57 n. 62) cites the unit under its former signature 4a.31.14 — the pencil mark still on the leaf.
