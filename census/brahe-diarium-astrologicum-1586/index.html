Methodology

Inclusion: The 1586 Diarium astrologicum et meteorologicum anni 1586 by Elias Olsen Morsing, printed 'in Officina VRANIBVRGICA' under Tycho's imprimatur, 4to, 30 leaves — the FIRST true book from the Uraniborg press; ~2,000 copies printed on imported paper; dedicated by Morsing to the crown prince Christian (later Christian IV); bears Tycho's SVSPICIENDO DESPICIO emblem. Excluded: Morsing's other works; reprints; facsimiles; MS copies. Counted as a copy: Any surviving exemplar with >=50% of leaves, or an identifiable provenanced fragment. Prior census base: Lauritz Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi — 3 copies at KB Copenhagen (one with autograph dedication to Albert Meier of Schleswig). No dedicated census. Confidence grading: Pearson A-D Copy-ID scheme: stable institutional-slug; never renumbered on sort

14. Market — Christie's 2016 (Beltrame), lot 20 — sold £92,500

brahe-diarium-astrologicum-1586/market-christies-2016

Provenance: Giancarlo Beltrame (1933-2014), Vicenza — scientific-book collector (Named consignor; Beltrame's library was dispersed by Christie's in three parts (13 Jul 2016, 30 Nov 2016, 26 Apr 2017)).

Census: brahe-diarium-astrologicum-1586 · Copy: market-christies-2016 · Confidence: B · Status: market (untraced post-2016)

A complete copy of the Diarium astrologicum et metheorologicum … 1586 — the first book printed at Tycho's Uraniborg press, by his assistant Elias Olsen Cimber Morsing under Tycho's imprimatur, with Tycho's own De cometa … rotundo appended — offered at Christie's London, ‘The Giancarlo Beltrame Library of Scientific Books, Part I’, 13 July 2016, lot 20. Small 4° (186 × 146 mm), woodcut initials and diagrams, the large printer's device on the verso of the penultimate leaf; in a modern half-morocco binding with new endpapers; the title's outer margin a little browned, light waterstaining in a few top margins.

It sold for £92,500 (estimate £50,000–80,000, price realised incl. premium). The catalogue stressed the edition's extreme market rarity: RareBookHub records only one prior copy sold (1950), with no ABPC record; the title is absent from Houzeau & Lancaster and from the printed Wellcome catalogue (though present on Wellcome online).

The modern rebinding and the absence of any KB shelfmark indicate this is a distinct exemplar, not one of the three Royal Library (KB) copies — but its present owner is unrecorded.

15. Market — Sotheby's 1950 (de Thou / Syston Park / Crawford copy)

brahe-diarium-astrologicum-1586/market-sothebys-1950-dethou

Provenance: Jacques-Auguste de Thou (1553-1617) and his first wife (armorial binding bearing the de Thou arms (his first marriage, Marie de Barbançon, dates the binding 1587-1601)); Syston Park library (Sir John Hayford Thorold) (Syston Park provenance recorded in the 1950 catalogue); W. Census: brahe-diarium-astrologicum-1586 · Copy: market-sothebys-1950-dethou · Confidence: B · Status: market (untraced post-1950)

A distinct exemplar with a distinguished provenance, recovered from RareBookHub (2026-06-01): it is the "1950 sale" the 2016 Christie's catalogue cited as the only prior recorded appearance. Offered at Sotheby & Co., London, 30 October 1950 (the Henry Harris et al. sale) and sold for £82 to the dealers Davis & Orioli.

The copy is bound in citron morocco bearing the arms of Jacques-Auguste de Thou (1553–1617) and his first wife — a Sammelband of four works in one volume, the Diarium bound with Roesslin's De opere dei creationis (Frankfurt, 1597) and two others. Its earlier history runs through the Syston Park library (Sir John Hayford Thorold; sale Sotheby's, 12 Dec 1884, lot 1293) and W. H. Crawford (his bookplates).

It is clearly distinct from the 2016 Beltrame copy (a single-work copy in modern half-morocco), so the census now records two market exemplars. Untraced since Davis & Orioli's 1950 purchase; the de Thou armorial binding makes it highly identifiable should it resurface.

Open questions

  • Tracing the de Thou / Syston Park / Crawford copy after Davis & Orioli (1950) needs an auction/institutional discovery sweep keyed on the highly distinctive de Thou armorial citron-morocco Sammelband + its Roesslin 1597 companion — a browser/dealer-archive search with no corpus trace.
  • Resolved (2026-06-04): Confirmed distinct from the 2016 Beltrame copy on binding grounds: this is a de Thou armorial citron-morocco Sammelband (bound with the Roesslin 1597), whereas the 2016 Beltrame copy is a modern half-morocco single-work binding. The two cannot be the same physical object. Basis: binding contrast (this record vs the Beltrame catalogue description).

No dedicated census. The record is Lauritz Nielsen, Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921) pp.186–187 + Dansk Bibliografi.

The work

Elias Olsen Morsing (Elias Olai Cimber, c.1540–1590), one of Tycho's ablest assistants, Diarium astrologicum et meteorologicum anni 1586, printed "in Officina VRANIBVRGICA" under Tycho's imprimatur — the first true book from the Uraniborg press. 4to, 30 leaves (calendar section printed first, late 1585; front matter early 1586). Bears Tycho's Astronomien emblem with SVSPICIENDO DESPICIO. Includes Tycho's own section on the comet of Oct–Nov 1585 (Opera Omnia IV, 399–414). Dedicated by Morsing to the not-quite-nine-year-old crown prince Christian (later Christian IV), dated 1 Jan 1586.

Print run + survival

  • ~2,000 copies — Nielsen p.186, from Tycho's letter to Henrik Rantzau, 8 Jan 1585 (the run was projected at 2,000; paper had to be imported from Rantzau's Holstein mill, the Hveen paper mill not yet built). Nielsen calls the book "ikke særlig sjælden" (not particularly rare) as of 1921.
  • 3 copies at KB Copenhagen (Nielsen p.187), one with an inserted leaf bearing Tycho's autograph dedication to the Schleswig clergyman Albert Meier — the one association copy. The digitised KB copy (IA den-kbd-pil-130018150582-001) is "LN 1170 4° copy 2". USTC 302151; WorldCat OCLC 695591299.
  • A copy sold at Christie's London, 13 July 2016, lot 20 (RBH notes only a prior 1950 sale — extreme market rarity despite the large original run).
  • F. R. Friis, Elias Olsen Morsing og hans Observationer (Copenhagen, 1889) is the standard study of Morsing.

Open question

Nielsen's "not particularly rare" (1921) suggests more than 3 copies may survive; a KVK/USTC sweep beyond KB Copenhagen (Uppsala, Hamburg SUB, Gotha, KUB) is the discovery axis.
