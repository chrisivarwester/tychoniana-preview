Methodology

Inclusion: All extant copies of the 1598 Wandsbek edition of Tycho Brahe's Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica, printed at Wandsbek early in 1598 by the Hamburg printer Philip de Ohr, on Brahe's own press materiel but with de Ohr's types and ornaments (Nielsen's hybrid-press finding); includes both presentation copies (large-paper / on vellum) and ordinary-paper copies of the first edition. Excluded: Facsimiles, photographic reproductions, manuscript copies. The 1602 Nuremberg re-issue (Levinus Hulsius, often with cancel title-page) is treated as a separate census at key brahe-mechanica-1602-nuremberg and excluded here even when the body sheets are Wandsbek 1598 stock — it is the issue, not the substrate, that defines the census. Counted as a copy: Any physical exemplar with >=50% of the original 107 leaves preserved, OR an identifiable fragment (>=1 original leaf) with documented institutional or named-private provenance. Vellum / large-paper copies are counted as ordinary copies for the headline number; their special status is recorded in each copy's physical description and surfaced separately in the survival analysis. Prior census base: Norlind 1933 Confidence grading: Pearson A-D Copy-ID scheme: stable institutional-slug; never renumbered on sort

2. Paris, BnF, Réserve, RES-V-229 — the Wolf Dietrich von Raitenau copy

brahe-mechanica-1598-wandsbek/bnf-paris-res-v-229

Provenance: Wolf Dietrich von Raitenau, Archbishop of Salzburg (Tycho's autograph dedication, 1598/1599).

Tycho's presentation copy to Wolf Dietrich von Raitenau, Prince-Archbishop of Salzburg, sent through Tengnagel in the long courier mission of 1599–1600, and the only Wandsbek copy whose visual and textual character is documented in print before the modern censuses. Bound with the manuscript Stellarum Inerrantium Restitutio — the thousand-star catalogue Tycho promised the Emperor and his patrons but never lived to print. Pierre Gassendi described and illustrated this copy in his Tychonis Brahei Vita (Paris, 1654), pp. 175–176; Norlind 1933 (Hasselberg 28) confirms the identification.

The copy reached Paris during the Napoleonic wars when imperial seizures brought presentation copies from across the German-speaking world into the BnF. Together with RES-V-228 (the Moritz of Orange dedication, carried from Holland) and RES-V-230 (a copy without dedication) it forms the largest concentration of 1598 Wandsbek copies outside Copenhagen (the Royal Danish Library holds five).

The BnF catalogue treats the three copies under one bibliographic record (cb30154214s) without per-copy provenance attribution; the digitised surrogate of RES-V-229 on Gallica (ark:/12148/bpt6k106374p) is the only one of the three openly accessible, and its IIIF manifest preserves the visible Tycho hand on the title-verso dedication leaf. The binding is recorded as green velvet with coloured plates within.

This is one of the six copies that Truffa’s appendix (2002, pp. 320–322) marks with an asterisk as bound with the manuscript Stellarum Inerrantium Restitutio; the cluster is set out in full under the Tengnagel mission in the provenance graph. NB a shelfmark conflict remains open: this census identifies the Raitenau / star-catalogue copy as RES-V-229 (following Gassendi 1654 and the Gallica digitisation ark:/12148/bpt6k106374p, which shows the autograph dedication), whereas Truffa’s appendix lists the asterisked Raitenau copy under RES-V-230. See the bnf-paris-res-v-230 open question.

3. Oxford, Bodleian Library, Arch. Bodl. D. 32 — the Grimani/Wotton copy

brahe-mechanica-1598-wandsbek/bodleian-arch-bodl-d-32

Provenance: Marino Grimani (1532–1605), Doge of Venice (Tycho's autograph dedication, transmitted by Tengnagel January 1600 (TBOO VIII, 228)); Sir Henry Wotton (1568–1639), English ambassador to Venice (Wotton's autograph note on title verso recording the gift's intended destination at San Marco library); Bodleian Library (Wotton's autograph donation inscription dated 13 August 1633).

Tycho's presentation copy to Marino Grimani, Doge of Venice (1595–1605), one of the six copies that Truffa’s appendix (2002, pp. 320–322) marks with an asterisk as bound with the manuscript Stellarum Inerrantium Restitutio (1,000-star catalogue); the cluster is set out under the Tengnagel mission in the provenance graph. Hasselberg 31. Norlind 1933 places the gift through Tengnagel's January 1600 visit to Venice, documented in Tycho's letter to Pinelli at Padua (TBOO VIII, 228).

Whether Grimani ever received it personally is uncertain. By the early seventeenth century the volume was in the hands of Sir Henry Wotton (1568–1639), King James's ambassador to Venice during three terms between 1604 and 1624, and one of England's earliest collectors of Continental scientific books. Wotton's autograph note on the title-verso conjectures that Tycho intended the copy for the San Marco library; the inference is plausible but the surviving evidence in the Venetian archives shows that the official Republic gift went elsewhere (it is the Marciana copy, Hasselberg 24). Wotton's own donation inscription, dated 13 August 1633, gave the volume to the Bodleian, where it has remained.

The volume is hand-coloured throughout — margins, illustrations, and capitals — and bound in green satin with gilt ornament, Italian work of about 1598. The volume is one of the most fully documented of the Tycho presentation copies: it carries a triple textual record (Tycho's autograph dedication, Wotton's autograph note, and Wotton's autograph donation inscription) that anchors its provenance from manufacture in Wandsbek to gift to the Doge to acquisition in Venice to donation in Oxford in a single physical artefact.

4. Cambrai, Bibliothèque d'agglomération, XVI B 3 — the Lobkowitz copy, digitised

brahe-mechanica-1598-wandsbek/cambrai-xvi-b-3

Provenance: Zdeněk Vojtěch Popel z Lobkovic (1568–1628), Supreme Chancellor of the Kingdom of Bohemia under Rudolf II (Tycho's autograph dedication on the front flyleaf verso: 'ILLVSTRI ET GENEROSO DOMINO / DNO SDENCO ADALBERTO POPL / BARONI in LOBKOWITZ DOMINO in CHLVMETZ / et GISIBVRJ SACRÆ CÆSAREÆ MAIESTATIS / CONSILIARIO et SVPRAEMO REGNI BOHEMIÆ / CANCELLARIO / Domino honorandissimo et observan[d]issimo / d.d.t. / Tycho Brahe / Anno 1600 / Augusti / Die 18'. Dedication body in a scribal hand (probably a Tycho-household scribe in Prague); the signature 'Tycho Brahe' and the date 'Anno 1600 / Augusti / Die 18' in Tycho's own cursive autograph (consistent with Tycho's documented late-period cursive: Graphonomicon, brahe-tycho; specimen cambrai-mechanica-lobkowitz, verdict probable). Transmission via amanuensis is corroborated by Tycho's letter to Jessenius, May 1600 (TBOO VIII, 317).); Unknown intermediate holder (post-Lobkowitz, pre-Abbey of Saint-Sépulcre Cambrai) (Two cursive ink inscriptions on the title page above the abbey's italic ownership inscription, both heavily struck through. Not legible at current image resolution; the obliteration is the abbey's act of asserting ownership over a previously-held volume. Date range bounded by Lobkowitz's gift (1600) and the abbey's dissolution (1790s).); Abbaye Saint-Sépulcre de Cambrai (Benedictine abbey of the Holy Sepulchre, founded 1064 by Bishop Liébert; dissolved during the French Revolution, 1790s) (Two ownership inscriptions in different hands: (a) front-flyleaf recto, top-left, in a working hand: 'Ex bibliot[heca] S[ancti] Sepulchri Cameraci'; (b) title page, in a more formal italic hand, beneath the obliterated pre-abbey inscriptions: 'Bibliothecæ S[ancti] Sepulcri Cameracen[sis]'. The Abbey of Saint-Sépulcre's library was the major Benedictine library of Cambrai before the Revolution; on dissolution its holdings were transferred to the municipal library.); Bibliothèque municipale de Cambrai (now Bibliothèque d'agglomération de Cambrai / 'Le Labo') (Standard French-Revolution-era transfer of dissolved-abbey libraries to the local municipal collection. Current shelfmark XVI B 3 (in pencil on the front flyleaf bottom-right; in modern format on the binding spine via a later gilt label). Round blue Cambrai BM library stamp on the title page and the colophon page. Earliest French scholarly notice of the Cambrai holding: G. Leboyer, Revue des Bibliothèques 33 (1923), 297–303.).

Tycho's presentation copy to Zdeněk Vojtěch Popel von Lobkowitz (1568–1628), the powerful Bohemian chancellor whose later refusal to sign Rudolf II's Majestätsbrief of 1609 reshaped the religious settlement of Bohemia. Tycho's autograph dedication is dated 18 August 1600 — Mosley and Norlind record the dispatch as 8 August, but the manuscript reads ‘Anno 1600 / Augusti / Die 18’ — and the volume was carried by an amanuensis from Benátky; the gift is mentioned in Tycho's letter to Jessenius of May 1600 (TBOO VIII, 317). Norlind 1933 notes the political subtext: at that moment Tycho was answering a rumour at Prague that the Emperor was planning to expel the Capuchins from the city — Lobkowitz, a fanatical Catholic, had reportedly opposed the action and was a man whose favour Tycho was wise to cultivate.

How the volume migrated to the municipal library of Cambrai is unresolved. Lobkowitz's library passed in part to the Roudnice castle library and in part to dispersal; the route to a northern French provincial library has not been documented. Leboyer's note in Revue des Bibliothèques 33 (1923), 297–303 confirms the Cambrai holding and describes the deluxe presentation binding.

The copy was digitised on Gallica by the Bibliothèque d'agglomération de Cambrai — with BnF RES-V-229 one of the two 1598 Wandsbek copies served on Gallica — under shelfmark XVI B 3. The Gallica record (ark:/12148/bd6t52539608) preserves the autograph dedication leaf clearly. Open access to a presentation copy with this kind of political subtext is rare for any Brahe document; the openly digitised Mechanica presentation copies (Cambrai, Detmold, Kiel, Weimar) now form a small comparative corpus.

Open questions

  • The Lobkowitz→Cambrai route is undocumented. The main Lobkowitz library passed to the Roudnice/Nelahozeves castle library (largely extant), which does not record this copy, so it left the family line early by a path that has not been traced. Establishing how it reached a northern-French municipal library will require French municipal-archive and Bohemian dispersal research (Leboyer 1923 in the Revue des Bibliothèques; the Cambrai accession records).
  • DATE DISCREPANCY: the autograph dedication reads unambiguously 'Anno 1600 / Augusti / Die 18' (18 August 1600). Mosley 2007 Appendix I and Norlind 1970 record the dispatch as 'p. 8 August 1600' (8 August). Either (a) Mosley misread the manuscript date '18' as '8' (the leading '1' in the manuscript date is connected to the '8' in a way that could be misread at low resolution), (b) the Mosley/Norlind date refers to the dispatch event recorded in Tycho's letter to Jessenius May 1600 (TBOO VIII, 317) which preceded the actual presentation, or (c) the censual transcription is at fault. Worth a direct read of TBOO VIII, 317 to verify which date refers to which event. Priority: medium (does not affect attribution, which is rock-solid).
  • Identify the unknown pre-abbey intermediate holder represented by the two obliterated cursive inscriptions on the title page. The handwriting style of the obliterated text is cursive Latin script of post-1600 type; the heavy obliteration was likely done by the abbey on accession. Higher-resolution multispectral imaging (UV / IR) might recover the underlying text. Reconstruction route: the gift was made at Prague to a Bohemian magnate (1600); the next confirmed owner is a Benedictine abbey in Cambrai (Hainaut, Spanish Netherlands at the time). Plausible intermediaries: a Jesuit college in Bohemia or the Spanish Netherlands; a Habsburg court figure who travelled the Prague-Brussels axis; a private collector in the Low Countries who acquired books from the Lobkowitz dispersal (Lobkowitz himself was Habsburg-loyalist and his library partly passed to Roudnice castle, partly dispersed). The route from Bohemian Lobkowitz to Cambrai abbey is the historical puzzle this copy poses.
  • Verify the front-cover supralibros identification (standing figure + gilt rim inscription) as the Tycho Brahe portrait medallion via comparison to the documented St Petersburg RNL Otto II copy's portrait supralibros (fragmentary, now on pasteboard). Higher-resolution imaging of the rim inscription on both copies — or correspondence with Cambrai BM curators for direct examination — would settle whether the inscription reads 'TYCHO BRAHE' / 'TYCHONIS BRAHE' or a different formula.
  • Verify the back-cover supralibros (double-headed Reichsadler + central shield) as Tycho's own arms with Imperial-eagle augmentation. The 1599 Habsburg ennoblement granted Tycho the use of the Imperial eagle in his arms; if this medallion shows that scheme, the cover-pair is the matched 'portrait + arms' presentation set documented for the highest-status Mechanica gifts.
  • Cross-copy G3 INGENIOSE collation: the Cambrai copy is the second documented case (after Lynge / Tosterup) of the manuscript supply of 'ÏNGENIOSÊ' on G3. The two cases now constitute a comparison sample for testing whether the corrections were made centrally (single hand across copies — Tycho or one trusted scribe) or by local owners on Tycho's instruction (varying hands). A side-by-side comparison of the Cambrai and Lynge ÏNGENIOSÊ corrections would discriminate.

7. Copenhagen, Det Kongelige Bibliotek, LN 432 2° (Hasselberg nr 5) — the Otto Steensen Brahe copy

brahe-mechanica-1598-wandsbek/copenhagen-kb-ln-432-copy5

Provenance: Otto (Otte) Steensen Brahe (Tycho's nephew, son of his brother Steen Brahe of Knutstorp, 1547–1620) (Tycho's autograph dedication); Det Kongelige Bibliotek (Lauritz Nielsen catalogue, LN 432).

Tycho’s presentation copy to his nephew Otto Steensen Brahe (son of Tycho’s brother Steen Brahe of Knutstorp, 1547–1620), dated 8 June 1598 in Tycho’s own hand. Hasselberg 5; LN 432. One of the three Copenhagen Royal Library copies grouped under the Lauritz Nielsen catalogue number.

The transmission is documented unusually precisely. Tycho wrote from Wandsbek in June 1598 to Professor Johann Jakob Grynaeus at Basel (TBOO VIII, 70), telling him that his messenger was carrying the book gift for Otto Brahe, who together with his tutor Cort Aslakssøn had recently been resident at Basel. By the time the courier reached Switzerland Otto and Aslakssøn were probably in Geneva, where they had moved at the close of 1597 and stayed for a full year; the volume thus reached Otto Brahe after a longer-than-usual relay through the family network of European Protestant universities. The dedication’s explicit June 1598 date makes this one of the earliest distributed copies of the Mechanica, only weeks after the print run came off Philip von Ohr’s press.

For a working family-network presentation copy (not the more ceremonious presentations to electors and chancellors) the volume retains substantial physical integrity. It returned to Copenhagen with the Brahe family and entered the Royal Library’s rare-book collection at a date Norlind 1933 does not fix; the LN 432 shelfmark groups it with the more famous dedications to Peter Vok of Rožmberk (LN 432 2° copy 3) and the Grand Duke of Tuscany (LN 432 2° copy 4). The family connection is straightforward: this is the Mechanica copy with the strongest tie to the Brahe family’s own dispersal, the volume Tycho selected for his own kin while preparing the other gifts for political and intellectual recipients.

8. Detmold, Lippische Landesbibliothek, Nl 70.4° — the Simon VI zur Lippe copy

brahe-mechanica-1598-wandsbek/detmold-llb-nl-70-4

Provenance: Simon VI, Count zur Lippe (1554–1613) (Tycho's autograph dedication; Brahe and Simon VI never met personally — dispatched as gift to potential patron with astronomical interests).

Surfaced in this census via the Lippische Landesbibliothek’s own exhibition catalogue and digital collections; NOT in Norlind 1933 or 1970. A coloured presentation copy with Tycho’s autograph dedication to Simon VI, Count zur Lippe (1554–1613), bound in the standard Wandsbek deluxe format and surviving in unaltered contemporary state. The library’s exhibition description characterises it as a Prachtausgabe.

Brahe and Simon VI never met in person, and no epistolary exchange between them is attested. The relationship is documented by the dedication copies alone — Truffa records a dedication to Simon VI in the Detmold manuscript 21, and Šíma and Valeška the autograph dedication in Nl 70.4° itself, signed “Tycho Brahe, T.F.”, the body of the text likewise in Tycho’s hand. The gift belongs to the same political-patronage diplomacy as the dedications to chancellors, electors, and ducal courts: a token sent to a nobleman with documented astronomical interests, whose support could be useful if imperial favour failed (Truffa 2002; Šíma & Valeška 2002). The Lippe court at Detmold was one of the smaller German Protestant scientific centres of the period, and Simon VI is a Brahe-recipient not previously documented in the census literature.

The copy is fully digitised under the library’s digital-collections programme (https://digitale-sammlungen.llb-detmold.de/content/titleinfo/2547750), and so joins the openly accessible surrogates of the 1598 Wandsbek edition recorded in this census’s digital-surrogates registry (among them BnF RES-V-229 and Cambrai XVI B 3 on Gallica). It adds a further openly digitised copy carrying full autograph dedication documentation, against which woodblock-damage progressions and colour-application sequences across other extant copies can be calibrated for relative-dating evidence.

This is the kind of discovery that our union-catalogue sweep was designed to surface: a presentation copy in a small German state library, never written up in the canonical English- or Swedish-language Brahe scholarship.

The presentation copy to Giovanni Antonio Magini (1555–1617), Bolognese astronomer and Tycho’s correspondent. Unlike most presentation copies of the Mechanica, it lacks both a coloured Tycho portrait and an autograph dedication leaf — the title-page itself is the document, bearing two struck-through manuscript signatures, one of them legibly “Liber Joh. After Magini’s death in 1617 his library was confiscated by the Inquisition and largely dispersed. Catalogued in the manuscripts department of what became the BNCF because of the accompanying star catalogue, it escaped attention in the printed-books listings — the route by which Norlind, alerted by Mazzatinti’s 1899 inventory of Italian manuscripts, came to identify it as a previously-unrecorded Magini-Brahe survival of unusual importance.

34. Herman H. J. Lynge & Søn, Copenhagen — the Tosterup Castle copy

brahe-mechanica-1598-wandsbek/lynge-tosterup-childhood-home

Provenance: Jørgen Brahe (d. 1565) and Inger Oxe (d. 1591) — Tycho's foster parents at Tosterup Castle, Scania (now Sweden) — or, more precisely, the Brahe-family line continuing at Tosterup after both foster parents had died (Lynge's attribution ('presumably sent by Brahe from Wandsbeck to his family at Tosterup Castle in Denmark right after printing') is an INFERENCE from (1) the silk presentation binding, (2) the comprehensive contemporary handcolouring, (3) the four-century continuous residence at Tosterup. There is no autograph dedication, no ownership inscription, no contemporary documentary attestation of the dispatch event. The recipient cannot have been Jørgen Brahe personally (d. 1565, 33 years before printing) or Inger Oxe personally (d. 1591, 7 years before printing) — the gift, if made, was to the broader Brahe-family Tosterup line. The dispatch event itself is one of Lynge's two principal claims about this copy (the other being the G3 attribution to Tycho's hand) and neither is independently verified.); Tosterup Castle library (Brahe-family line and successor owners of the estate) (Continuous presence at Tosterup for approximately four centuries per Lynge (~1598 to ~1976). Tosterup passed through several owner-lines after the 16th century; the book's continuity through these transitions has not been independently documented at the per-owner level.); (unnamed first private holder; identity not given by Lynge); Herman H. J. Lynge & Søn (Copenhagen) (Currently offered for sale; per Lynge, the copy 'has only changed hands once before now' — so the chain is Tosterup → one private holder → Lynge. The single-intermediate-holder claim is Lynge's; the intermediate owner is not named in the public listing.).

A presentation copy not in Norlind 1933, not in Norlind 1970, and not in Mosley 2007 Appendix I — the Brahe scholarly community appears to have been entirely unaware of this exemplar. It was at Tosterup Castle in Scania (now southern Sweden) throughout the period of canonical census-making, dispersed only ca. 1976, and is now being offered by Herman H. J. Lynge & Søn (Copenhagen) at USD 620,000 under order-nr. 60123.

Tosterup is Tycho’s childhood home. From the age of one he was raised by his paternal uncle Jørgen Brahe and Jørgen’s wife Inger Oxe at Tosterup — the only parents Tycho knew (his biographical father Otto Brahe was “not informed” of the abduction, in Tycho’s own characteristic phrasing). Inger Oxe was the sister of Peder Oxe, the powerful Steward of the Realm, and her court connection underwrote much of Tycho’s early patronage. Jørgen Brahe died in 1565 and Inger Oxe in 1591, so by the time the Mechanica came off Philip von Ohr’s press in 1598 the gift would have been to the Brahe-family line still at Tosterup, not to either of the foster parents themselves.

The copy bears no autograph dedication leaf. What it carries instead is the Tosterup provenance evidenced by four centuries of continuous residence at the estate, and a single physical marker that places it firmly within Tycho’s own working circle: the word INGENIOSE in the imperfectly printed headline of G3 has been supplied in manuscript, “presumably in Brahe’s own hand” (Lynge), as it is in most known copies. This is the print-run flaw Tycho corrected by hand — an authorial gesture extending into the private distribution.

The binding is the most distinctive in any surviving copy: original blue silk with rich gilt ornamentation to the boards, professionally recased in the 1970s with 90% of the original silk preserved over new blue silk, green silk ties. Only one other documented surviving Mechanica presents in silk — the St Petersburg Russian National Library copy (Duke Otto II of Braunschweig-Lüneburg), whose fragments of green silk with portrait + arms supralibros are pasted onto a later pasteboard binding. The Tosterup copy is the only silk binding to survive substantially intact.

The colouring is comprehensive: the title-page in red and black is left uncoloured, but all other leaves are in magnificent contemporary handcolouring, many illustrations illuminated in gold, woodcut borders coloured in green and greenish-blue, large initials and head- and tail-pieces and the colophon device coloured in various colours. Lynge notes minor restorations at A4 and H2 with matching paper and a small neat restoration to the title-page border, otherwise “splendid condition”.

The copy “has only changed hands once before now” (per Lynge), placing exactly one private holder between Tosterup (until ca. 1976) and the present dealer offering. The identity of that intermediate owner is not given. The obvious candidate — the copy offered and passed at Christie’s London, Beltrame Library Part I (sale 13730, lot 23, 13 July 2016, estimate £120,000–180,000) — has been ruled out: that lot is now identified as the Weimar copy recovered by the Herzogin Anna Amalia Bibliothek in 2019 (acquired privately, accession 2019/08935, shelfmark 15, 2: 23), whose Sitsch von Stubendorf and Logau provenance shares nothing with the Tosterup chain. The single intermediate owner between Tosterup (ca. 1976) and Lynge therefore remains unidentified, pending a direct enquiry to the dealer.

The copy is recorded here for the first time in any census. Its most interesting feature is the unaltered authorial character of the artefact — an original silk-bound coloured presentation copy of the Mechanica, with the G3 manuscript correction in what Lynge attributes to Tycho’s own hand, documented as continuously at Tycho’s childhood home from the year of printing for four centuries.

1. The Tengnagel mission of 1599–1600 — copies bound with the manuscript star catalogue

Franz Gansneb Tengnagel von Camp (Tycho’s son-in-law-to-be) was the principal courier for Tycho’s 1599–1600 Italian and Imperial gift-distribution programme. The copies that survive bound cum a manuscript of the Stellarum Inerrantium Restitutio (the 1,000-star catalogue Tycho never lived to print) are enumerated in the appendix to Truffa (Tycho Brahe and Prague, 2002, pp. 320–322), whose explicit legend (p. 320: “The symbol * indicates the copies bound with Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica, Wandsbeck 1598”) marks six such copies — not four, and not including Copenhagen:

  • florence-bncf-ii-ii-529-magini — the Magini copy (II.II.529 *; November 1598 transmission, TBOO VIII, 123).
  • bodleian-arch-bodl-d-32 — the Grimani / Wotton copy (Arch. B.c.3 * / olim Arch.Bodl. D 32; January 1600 transmission to Doge Marino Grimani, TBOO VIII, 228).
  • venice-bnm-h24-republic — the Republic of Venice copy (Marciana Lat. VIII, XXXVI * (2686); entered Marciana 1722).
  • goettingen-ub-h33-henrik-julius — the Heinrich Julius of Braunschweig copy (Göttingen Philos. 28a *).
  • osnabrueck-gymnasium-carolinum-jesuit — the Osnabrück copy (Niedersächs. Staatsarchiv, coll. Karlsgymnasium, 45 / M e 10 *; the record already notes it was bound with the star catalogue).
  • the Wolf Dietrich von Raitenau / Archbishop-of-Salzburg copy at the BnF, which Truffa lists under shelfmark RES-V-230 * — note the open shelfmark conflict with this census, which (following Gassendi 1654 + the Gallica digitisation) assigns the Raitenau / star-catalogue copy to RES-V-229; see bnf-paris-res-v-230 open question.

Truffa does not asterisk Copenhagen’s three star-catalogue manuscripts (Gl.kgl.S. 306 = Christian IV; Gl.kgl.S. 307 = Adolph of Lübeck; 19–107 = Ferdinand, Grand Duke of Tuscany): they are separate, unbound manuscripts, so the Copenhagen Tuscany Mechanica (copenhagen-kb-ln-432-copy4) is not a member of this bound-with-Mechanica cluster — correcting the earlier “four copies incl. 3.bis. Colouring taxonomy: Groups A, B and C

Surviving Mechanica copies divide into three observed colouring states. The taxonomy emerged when the Lynge / Tosterup-Castle copy — offered for sale May 2026 — was described with unusual granularity, distinguishing the title-page’s colour state from the body’s. The granularity in turn made it possible to read back across the census and see that institutional records had been collapsing this distinction:

  • Group A — title-page illuminated (handcolour and/or gold added over the red-and-black press impression) + body leaves fully coloured and gilded. The apex treatment. Two copies confirmed as of the May 2026 inspections:
  • the Cambrai / Lobkowitz copy (cambrai-xvi-b-3), autograph dedication 18 August 1600, original silk binding intact with twin Tycho-portrait + Tycho-arms supralibros (verified by first-hand inspection of the Gallica PDF, 25 May 2026);
  • the HAAB Weimar / Sitsch von Stubendorf copy (weimar-haab-15-2-23-recovered-2019), originally presented to Johann VI Sitsch von Stubendorf (Bishop of Wrocław) post-1600, with title-page Suspiciendo / Despicio vignette and roeskenrammer border both hand-coloured, body fully coloured with gold throughout, hand-painted watercolour copy of the Gheyn 1590 portrait on the leaf facing the title (verified by first-hand inspection of the HAAB IIIF surrogate, 25 May 2026). The Friedrich Wilhelm of Saxe-Weimar / Gotha copy (gotha-fb-h14-friedrich-wilhelm) remains a Group A candidate pending title-leaf inspection — an autograph dedication with reciprocal gold-chain gift implies maximally- finished work. The Otto II / St Petersburg copy (st-petersburg-rnl-otto-ii) is a third strong candidate on the basis of its original green-silk-with-supralibros binding (the binding scheme that the Cambrai copy now provides the fully-intact reference example for; the HAAB copy provides the after-loss comparison case).

  • Group B — title-page preserved in red and black as printed (no handcolouring added) + body leaves fully coloured and gilded. The Lynge / Tosterup-Castle copy (lynge-tosterup-childhood-home) is the first definitively-known Group B. Lynge: “Title-page (which is printed in red and black) is uncoloured, but all other leaves are in magnificent contemporary handcolouring, and many of the illustrations are illuminated in gold.” The colourist’s finish is high (gold, multiple pigment families, comprehensive border-work), so Group B is not a half-finished or in-progress state; the colourist completed the body but the title was either deliberately left or workflow-determinedly not brought to the title-leaf.
  • Group C — no colour applied at all (the “as printed” state). The NYC NYPL copy (nyc-nypl) is the first definitively-known Group C: “No portrait, no dedication, not coloured, rebound.”

Twelve further copies are confirmed coloured (body) but their title-page colour state is not specified in the records available to this census, so they sit at group: coloured-unspecified — candidates for promotion to Group A or Group B after digital- surrogate or institutional-correspondence inspection. Two copies (Stockholm KVA H1 / Wargentin and Kalocsa H22) form a distinctive subgroup defined by a watercolour portrait substituted for the standard pasted Gheyn engraving on the title-verso. Whether these copies are otherwise coloured (body) is not stated in the current records; they are logged group: unknown until inspected.

What the three groups might mean — two competing readings, neither yet decisively confirmed.

Hypothesis H1 — Recipient-tier scheme. The three groups represent three deliberate tiers of recipient: emperors and ruling sovereigns get Group A; high nobles and ranking court figures get Group B; fellow scholars and lower-status recipients get Group C. On this reading, the colouring state is a social signal Tycho selected per-recipient at the moment of dispatch.

Hypothesis H2 — Workflow-stage scheme. The three groups represent three stages of completion in a single colouring workflow. A copy comes off the press in Group C state. A colourist — whom Tycho employed and brought from Wandsbek — works the body, the borders, the initials and the head- and tail-pieces, moving the copy into Group B. Only for the highest-status recipients did the colourist (or a separate gilder/illuminator) additionally bring the title-page itself to a coloured-and-gilded state, completing the move into Group A.

The case for H2 is strongly suggested by a contemporary letter recorded against the Munich UB record (muenchen-ub-0014-w-2-math-37). On 31 August 1599 Tycho wrote to Johann Georg Herwart von Hohenburg (Bavarian chancellor) promising three Mechanica copies; he stated that he “lacked the colourist required to complete” two of them — the copies destined for the Bavarian dukes Maximilian and Wilhelm. Mosley 2007 Appendix I records this directly, citing TBOO VIII, 162.25–29 and Norlind 1970, p. 289. The sentence makes it explicit that colouring was a bottleneck in Tycho’s production pipeline, that a single dedicated colourist (or a small team) was responsible for the work, and that the work was specifically required to complete a copy — i.e. an uncoloured copy was conceptually unfinished, not a deliberate alternative finish. This favours H2 over H1.

A combined reading is also defensible: the colourist was scheduled priority-by-recipient (high-status copies first), so the workflow mechanism in H2 produced a status correlation indistinguishable from H1 across the surviving record. The question can be settled not by the distribution alone but by chronological evidence: if a Group A copy is ever found to have been dispatched later than a Group B copy to a recipient of similar or higher status, H1 fails; if Group B copies are systematically associated with documented “rush dispatch” episodes (Tycho urgently sending a copy before the colourist could complete the title-page), H2 is confirmed.

Next-step inspections that would advance the question.

  1. Title-page imaging. Every Group-A candidate above (Friedrich Wilhelm / Gotha; Otto II / St Petersburg; Saxon princes / Dresden S.B.14; Lobkowitz / Cambrai — the last is on Gallica IIIF) needs title-leaf inspection. A pass through digital surrogate records to look at the title-leaf, then a correspondence pass to institutions for the copies without public surrogates, would close most of the coloured-unspecified bucket in one round.
  2. Pigment-palette uniformity. If all Group A and Group B copies use the same pigment families and the same gold leaf, a single colourist (or workshop) hypothesis is supported. The right interlocutor is a specialist in late-C16 illumination workshops in the Hamburg / North-German cultural orbit (Philip von Ohr’s ambit).
  3. The 1599–1601 dispatch chronology. For copies with documented dispatch dates (the Mosley Appendix I network), does colouring state correlate with dispatch date, recipient status, or both? A correlation matrix across the ˜15 dated dispatches would surface any systematic pattern.
  4. The watercolour-portrait-substitute subgroup. Kalocsa H22 and Stockholm KVA H1 share a hand-painted watercolour Tycho portrait replacing the standard pasted Gheyn engraving. Are they otherwise coloured (body)? Are they linked by recipient network or by post-Tycho ownership? A targeted comparison of the two watercolour portraits — same hand? same workshop? — would surface either a single late-1600/1601 colourist substitution batch (consistent with H2’s “work continued after Tycho ran out of standard portrait engravings”) or two unrelated post-Tycho restorations.
  5. The G3 INGENIOSE manuscript correction across copies. The bibliographic fact that G3’s headline misprint is supplied in manuscript “in most known copies” (per Lynge and per general dealer/institutional records) should be tested systematically: in every copy that has G3, is the correction present, and does the hand vary copy-to-copy? Parallel to but not the same as the colouring-group question; tracking both per-copy lets us see whether the two workflows (manuscript- correction and colouring) used the same personnel.

5. The Honeyman–Sachsen-Meiningen lineage (market-side)

Surrogate-read finding — 2026-06-01: two dedication formats, keyed to rank

Direct inspection of the digitised dedication leaves (IIIF) of two presentation copies shows that Tycho's Mechanica dedications were not uniform — they came in at least two registers, apparently keyed to the recipient's rank:

  • Deluxe / princely (Rostock, to Duke Ulrich III of Mecklenburg, rosdok IIIF leaf phys_0004): a red calligraphic display header above a 10-distich Latin elegiac poem ("Accipe Clementi DVX VLDARICE favore / Organa Sidereas apta notare vias … His ter septenos lustravimus Astra per Annos" — the 21 years at Hven), the whole in one uniform professional calligraphic italic; the "Tÿcho Brahe" signature (lower right, over a paraph) is a distinctly larger, freer cursive — the autograph element. This is the calligrapher_body_autograph_signature structure of Šíma–Valeška.
  • Plain (Detmold, to Simon VI, Count zur Lippe, i3f IIIF leaf [8]): a short cursive prose note — "Illustrissimo Dno, Dno Simoni, Comiti et Nobili Domino in Lippa … Domino meo Clementi. Submisse offero. Tÿcho Brahe p.p." — with no calligraphic display and no verse, the whole in one flowing cursive close to the signature hand.

Implication. A reigning prince (Ulrich, a duke) received the lavish calligraphic-verse Prachtausgabe dedication; a count and imperial councillor (Simon zur Lippe) received a plain personal cursive note. The dedication register tracks the recipient's standing. This has a graphonomicon consequence: the plain-format copies, whose body is an ordinary cursive rather than a calligrapher's display hand, are the likelier-wholly-autograph subset — better hand specimens than the deluxe copies, where only the signature is Tycho's. The next step is a forensic comparison of the Detmold cursive body against Tycho's prose hand; if it matches, the plain-format dedications can be regraded as wholly autograph. (The two specimens are the Rostock copy, UB Rostock L II b 9, and the Detmold copy, LLB Detmold Nl 70.4°.)

Nielsen 1921 hybrid-press analysis of the Mechanica

Nielsen's Bogtrykkeri pp. 206 documents a structural fact about the 1598 Wandsbek Mechanica that the colophon obscures and that collector-grade descriptions routinely miss: the Mechanica is a hybrid product of Tycho's own press materiel and Philip de Ohr's Hamburg shop, not "Tycho's press" tout court as the "PROPRIA AUTHORIS TYPOGRAPHIA" colophon suggests. Specifically:

  • Tycho's contribution: the press itself (brought from Hveen via Copenhagen to Wandsbeck in autumn 1597), the 18 instrument-woodcut blocks cut on Hveen 1585–96, the four new copperplates (cut in Germany for this edition), and the two Astronomien / Kemien printer's marks ("SVSPICIENDO DESPICIO" and "DESPICIENDO SVSPICIO").
  • Philip de Ohr's Hamburg shop: the brødskrift (a small Cicero-Antikva Tycho did not own); the dedication + letter type (a Mittel-Antikva non-Tycho); the Kursiv settings; the small Antikva-Initials with foliate Schlingenwerk (found in other de Ohr imprints, never in Uraniborg imprints); all the friezes, vignettes, and røskenrammer (these are Ohr's or his predecessor Hans/Henrik Binder's — Tycho's Uraniborg imprints never used røskenrammer decoration); the title-leaf two-colour setting.

The famous decorative richness of the 1598 Mechanica — the rich røskenrammer borders on every page, the friezes, the vignettes — belongs to de Ohr, not to Tycho. Only the instruments-woodcut content and the press-mark emblems are Tycho's. This matters for any forensic question about the edition's typography (e.g. comparing to other de Ohr Hamburg imprints in pursuit of a missing typographic sibling, or distinguishing the 1598 Wandsbek from the 1602 Nuremberg Lochner reprint on a typographic basis).

Notable gaps in the prior literature

  • No post-1970 systematic census exists. The Mosley appendix is network-centric, not exemplar-centric; the Christianson volumes are biographical and editorial; the Šolc/Hadrava critical edition documents the text, not the surviving witnesses.
  • Vellum vs ordinary paper has never been quantified in any census; Norlind notes coloured/uncoloured but the support material classification (vellum / large-paper / ordinary paper) is treated inconsistently.
  • WW2 attrition is documented copy-by-copy in Norlind 1933’s prose but never tabulated; this census provides the first consolidated list of the wartime losses.
  • The dispersal of presentation copies from the great C18 and C19 collections (Wargentin / Renmark / Delisle / KVA bequest / Sunderland / Macclesfield / Honeyman Part V 1980 / Norman 1998) has not been knitted together as a single thread; the provenance graph below begins to do so.
