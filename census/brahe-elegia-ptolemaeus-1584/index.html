Methodology

Inclusion: Tycho Brahe's Latin elegy 'In effigiem Cl. Ptolemaei Alexandrini' (incipit 'CERES Proculor faciem, barbam, sic membra gerebat'), an aerevers (verse-under-portrait) in elegiac distichs dated 1 October 1584, probably composed to feed Tycho's new Uraniborg press as an occasional print. Counts: (a) the lost 1584 Uraniborg original print/broadsheet, if any exemplar were located; (b) any surviving witness transmitting the text. The text was previously UNKNOWN to scholarship and is OMITTED from Dreyer's Opera omnia; published by Mogens Broendsted, Fund og Forskning 9 (1962), pp.72-78. Excluded: Frobenius's surrounding 1644 Ptolemy-translation paratexts (his own title-page, the fictive Ptolemy supplication, Longomontanus's 1626 preface, Frobenius's Regna-firmat-pietas carmen to Christian IV) — those are Frobenius's work, not Tycho's. The Venice-1574 Geografia portrait Tycho may have written under is context, not a witness. Counted as a copy: Any exemplar of the lost 1584 Uraniborg original; OR a surviving printed/manuscript witness carrying Tycho's elegy text. The 1644 Frobenius proof-sheet is the sole located witness. Prior census base: None — the elegy was unknown before Broendsted 1962; not in Nielsen, not in Dreyer Opera omnia. The copies

1. Stockholm, Kungliga Biblioteket — Frobenius proof-sheet (1644)

brahe-elegia-ptolemaeus-1584/stockholm-kb-frobenius-proofsheet-1644

Provenance: G.L. Frobenius (Hamburg) (Printed by Frobenius as the opening sheet of his planned Latin Almagest translation, dedicated to Christian IV; Tycho's 1584 elegy reprinted on the last page.); probably Joergen Seefeld (library dispersed to Sweden) (Binding lacks the Seefeld ex-libris but is contemporary; Broendsted infers a Seefeld->Stockholm path among Swedish war-booty libraries.); Kungliga Biblioteket, Stockholm (Held at the Swedish Royal Library; microfilmed for KB Copenhagen in 1962.).

The sole surviving witness to Tycho Brahe's elegy in praise of Ptolemy. The text is carried not by any Brahe imprint but by a **proof-sheet (prøveark) of 1644** — the opening folio gathering of Georg Ludwig Frobenius's never-completed Latin translation of Ptolemy's Almagest (Claudii Ptolemii Magnae Constructionis Mathematicae libri tredecim … Hamburgi, ex bibliopolio Frobeniano). Frobenius, who had spent a month as a discipulus on Hven in 1591, set Tycho's fourteen-couplet ærevers on the final page, beneath a portrait of Ptolemy.

The copy is at the Kungliga Biblioteket, Stockholm; Brøndsted records that it was microfilmed for the Royal Library in Copenhagen in 1962, the year he published the poem. Its contemporary binding — kin to a Hamburg-1643 binding in Copenhagen, and bound with Frobenius's Regna firmat pietas carmen to Christian IV — points to the Swedish war-booty libraries, most plausibly via Jørgen Seefeld's collection, though the Seefeld ex-libris is absent.

This single sheet is the reason the elegy is known at all: the poem was unknown to scholarship until Brøndsted's Fund og Forskning article and does not appear in Dreyer's Opera omnia. Grade B — the witness and its text are secure, but the precise Stockholm shelfmark was not recorded by Brøndsted and remains to be confirmed.

2. Lost — the conjectured 1584 Uraniborg print

brahe-elegia-ptolemaeus-1584/lost-uraniborg-original-1584

Provenance: (text preserved only via the 1644 Frobenius proof-sheet) (Broendsted argues the elegy was probably printed as a 1584-85 Uraniborg occasional sheet under a Ptolemy portrait; no exemplar of any such print survives.).

No exemplar of any 1584 Uraniborg original is known. Brøndsted argues that the elegy — dated 1 October 1584 and cast as a verse-under-portrait of Ptolemy — was most likely struck off as one of the occasional Latin prints with which Tycho was then feeding his newly-established Uraniborg press, and that Frobenius's 1644 sheet reproduces such a broadsheet rather than a Hven manuscript. The argument is circumstantial: it rests on the parallel with Tycho's other 1584–85 occasional verse, several pieces of which are themselves lost.

Whether a printed original ever existed is therefore conjectural (grade C). It is recorded here as a distinct copy-slot so that the census carries the open question rather than silently collapsing the lost original into the surviving witness — exactly as the sibling census brahe-elegy-frandsen-1584 keeps its lost Uraniborg print separate from its later textual reprints.
