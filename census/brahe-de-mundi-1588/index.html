Methodology

Inclusion: All physical copies of the 1588 Uraniborg first edition of De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis liber secundus (Tycho Brahe's treatise on the comet of 1577), printed at his own press on Hven by Christopher Weida — the only Tycho imprint whose colophon names Weida ('VRANIBVRGI In Insula Hellesponti Danici Hvenna imprimebat Authoris Typographus Christophorus Vveida ANNO DOMINI M.D.LXXXVIII'). Both title-page settings are in scope: the standard 10-chapter setting and the earlier rejected 8-chapter setting (the latter known only from one Berlin Staatsbibliothek copy per Nielsen). Excluded: The 1603 Prague (Schumann) re-issue and the 1610 Frankfurt (Tampach) re-issue — separate issues with new preliminary leaves, each warranting its own census key if pursued. CRITICAL bibliographic caveat: most surviving 'Liber II' copies bound with a 1603 or 1610 title-leaf are in fact 1588 Uraniborg sheets re-issued; a copy is judged by its quire signatures and body sheets, NOT by the title-page year. A 1588-sheets copy in a 1603/1610 binding IS in scope (flag the binding state); a genuine reset 1603/1610 sheet is OUT. Facsimiles, photographic reproductions, and manuscript copies are excluded. Counted as a copy: Any physical exemplar preserving at least half of the original leaves, or an identifiable fragment (at least one original leaf) with documented institutional or named-private provenance. Heavily-annotated association copies, like Maestlin's, count as ordinary copies in the headline number; their special status is recorded on the copy's own record and in the survival analysis. Prior census base: Lauritz Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551-1600 (Copenhagen, 1931-33) §428 (~20-library copy-list); no dedicated De mundi census exists (unlike Norlind for the Mechanica). Confidence grading: Pearson A-D Copy-ID scheme: stable institution-slug + shelfmark-slug; never renumbered on sort

Det Kongelige Bibliotek (Royal Danish Library), Copenhagen — LN 428 4° ex. 1. Confidence A; status extant (public digital surrogate examined leaf-by-leaf 2026-08-10).

Tycho's presentation copy to Gellius Sascerides, his Hven assistant (and for a time betrothed to Magdalene Brahe), with the autograph dedication on a preliminary leaf: "Erudito & Honesto Juveni | Gellio Saceridi suo | Tijcho Brahe | 1588 | Martij 20." — the earliest dated De mundi presentation known (Norlind p. 122: "den tidigast kända av alla"), predating the Görlitz/Scultetus copy (8 May) and the BL Maestlin copy (14 May). A Hven presentation binding in full livery: worn parchment with gilt double-fillet frames and green silk ties, the portrait oval on the front cover (motto heavily rubbed) and the arms oval with ring inscription on the back — both confirmed visually on the Internet Archive scan (den-kbd-pil-130018110610-001, whose every image is footer-labelled "LN 428 4° copy 1").

Die-programme keystone. The dedication hard-dates the gift to 20 March 1588; the binding is its contemporary presentation livery — so both supralibros dies existed by 20 March 1588, superseding the Maestlin portrait terminus (14 May 1588) and the Görlitz arms terminus (8 May 1588), with the single stated inference that binding accompanied gift. The irony the record layer must keep straight: Sascerides was also the courier of the (distinct, contested) Rothmann presentation copy to Kassel on 11 July 1588 — and a Poznań/AMU catalogue note miscredits this copy's dedication to Rothmann; it is to Sascerides.

At the foot of the dedication leaf an 18th-century owner's note by one "H. Stubenrauch V.D.M." praises the edition's rarity ("D. Clement illam nesciverit" — David Clément's Bibliothèque curieuse did not know it) and the copy's gold effigies et insignia and autograph name. Pastedown: KB stamps ("LN 428 ex. 1 M"), crowned monogram, Bibliotheca Regia Hafniensis; pencil "DA.box". Among the three KB copies Gigas described in Bogvennen 1900 (S. 31–34, facsimile at S. 32 — absorb at next refresh).

Open questions: identify H. Stubenrauch and the route into KB; raking-light photography of the rubbed portrait motto; the struck word under the dedication's second line; per-exemplar KB catalogue records.

Bodleian Library, University of Oxford, Oxford — 4° T 2 Art.Seld.. Confidence A; status extant.

A copy of the 1588 Uraniborg first edition of Tycho Brahe's De mundi aetherei recentioribus phaenomenis liber secundus, his treatise on the comet of 1577, printed at his own press on Hven by Christopher Weida.

Provenance. One of the two copies Tycho sent to Thomas Savile (Merton College; Henry Savile's brother, d. 1593) with his letter of 1 December 1590 — "Mitto siquidem bina Exemplaria… hunc, quo nunc te dono" (TBOO VII, 284) — this being, with probability, Savile's own gift copy (David Rogers apud SOLO; Norlind p. 123 states the dispatch identification without reservation, "otvivelaktigt"). The second exemplar of the consignment, which Savile was asked to show to John Dee and Thomas Digges, is recorded separately (savile-dispatch-dee-digges). Marginal annotations in Greek and Latin throughout, in a single sixteenth-century hand — attributed to Sir Henry Savile by David Rogers (apud Norlind p. 123; cf. Heilbronner 1742, p. 620: "Notae H. S. in Tychonis Brahei libros de Phaenomenis coelestibus"), while SOLO hedges "perhaps [Thomas] Savile's"; unresolved. After Thomas's death (1593) the copy probably passed to Henry (Norlind), yet the Art.Seld. shelfmark places it in the Selden bequest (1659), not the Savilian library — the two routes cannot both stand as stated.

Binding. Brown calf, gilt laurel-wreath oval centrepiece, spine with quatrefoil tools — an English-style binding (Savile-era or early Bodleian), not a Tycho supralibros (a documented negative for the supralibros census); painted shelfmark letters on the upper cover (CW photographs, 2025-01-22). The copy evidently travelled plain or in sheets.

Open questions: annotator identity (Henry vs Thomas — comparison against both brothers' specimens); Selden vs Savilian route; Heilbronner 1742 p. 620 verbatim; collate quire signatures.

On the last leaf stands the autograph of John Dee: "Dominum Christophorum Rothmannum in suorum amicorum numerum lubentissimè recepit Ioannes Dée Locrobytanus. Cassellæ A°. 1589. Martij 26. The identity question is open. Scheibel states this copy is the Rothmann copy of Gassendi's account and transcribes Tycho's autograph title dedication — "…D. Christophoro Rothmanno… dono misit Tycho Brahe Danus Anno 1588. mense Majo" — delivered at Kassel 11 July 1588 by Gellius Sascerides, read three hours by Landgrave Wilhelm. Norlind, finding no dedication c. 1970 (only two signatures, one Scheibel's), ruled the copy not Rothmann's and assigned the dedication to Scheibel's lost second copy. The rival reading — the autograph dedication excised or washed after 1787 — explains the same absence more economically. Deciding test: autopsy/UV of the Poznań title leaf.

The inscription's documentary reach is unusual: Tycho himself, in the Progymnasmata (TBOO III, 204), reports Dee's opinions on the 1572 nova as learned from Rothmann's account of exactly this Kassel conversation ("quando Casellas, redeundo in Patriam, transiisset"), and Dreyer dated that passage by this copy's inscription (note ad 204,12, citing Scheibel). Norlind's aside that Dee "probably visited Hven in Sept. 1588" is contradicted — Dee was at Třeboň (diary; R&W chronology) and Tycho knew his views only second-hand — and must not be repeated.

Open questions: live Poznań UB verification + shelfmark (bot-blocked channels); the Rothmann identity test (title-leaf autopsy/UV); identify Hirschenberg; the Bose supralibros-Epistolae lead; legible photographs of both ovals for the die programme.

21. Second exemplar of the 1 Dec 1590 Savile dispatch (for Dee and Digges) — never located

brahe-de-mundi-1588/savile-dispatch-dee-digges

Provenance: Tycho Brahe (donor) → Thomas Savile (consignee), for the examination of John Dee and Thomas Digges (Tycho's own letter documents the dispatch of two copies with the same consignment as the four de Gheyn portrait impressions ('Addidi quaterna Exemplaria meae Effigiei nuper Amsterodami cupro insculptae'). The dispatch is A-grade documentary fact; everything after receipt is undocumented.).

Untraced; status inferred (documentary dispatch, no exemplar ever located). Confidence B.

On 1 December 1590 Tycho answered a lost letter of Thomas Savile (Merton College; Henry Savile's brother, d. 1593) and sent him two copies of De mundi: "Mitto siquidem bina Exemplaria libri nostri secundi de recentioribus coeli Phaenomenis" (TBOO VII, 284). One was Savile's own gift copy — "hunc, quo nunc te dono, evolve, disquire, dijudica" — identified with probability as the Bodleian's 4° T 2 Art.Seld. The second Savile was to show to John Dee and Thomas Digges for their free judgment: "Utrique autem horum librum nostrum transmissum ostende, ut et illi in eum disquirant atque suam de eius contentis sententiam libere pronuntient" (285). Four impressions of the new de Gheyn portrait travelled in the same packet.

No later trace of this second exemplar exists. Dee was back at Mortlake from December 1589 (Tycho's letter congratulates him on the return); Digges died in August 1595; Thomas Savile himself died in 1593, part of his books passing to Eton College. No extant De mundi copy anywhere in our censuses carries a Dee or Digges provenance (checked 2026-08-09) — the Poznań ex-Scheibel copy bears a Dee note, but dated Kassel, March 1589, before this dispatch.

Open questions: receipt evidence in Dee's diary/correspondence; Eton College Library check (Thomas Savile's books); the untraced Magdalen College, Oxford holding as an unidentifiable candidate.

30. Stockholm, Kungliga Vetenskapsakademien

brahe-de-mundi-1588/stockholm-kva

Repository: Kungliga Vetenskapsakademien (Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences) · Stockholm SE Confidence: C · Status: recorded, untraced (attested by Nielsen 1931–33; no live evidence)

Nielsen §428 records the Stockholm holding of the 1588 Uraniborg De mundi at two institutions: 'Stockholm, Kgl. Bibl., Vetenskapsakademiens Bibl.' The KB copy survives, digitised (RAR: 106 A). This KVA copy is attested only by that 1931–33 line: no shelfmark, no LIBRIS record, no Stockholm University Library entry, and no sale record has been found (English- and Swedish-language sweeps, 2026-07-15).

KVA's pre-1800 books have been deposited at Stockholm University Library since 1978, still KVA property — the deposit may be incompletely retro-catalogued, so the copy could survive there uncatalogued. One written enquiry to SUB Special Collections decides it.

Sources: Nielsen 1931 §428; LIBRIS sweep 2026-07-15 (negative); SUB/KVA institutional pages.

The Savile cluster (new, v0.5)

Thomas Savile is now a three-point node across the house stores: this census's Bodleian gift copy + the second (never-located) exemplar of the same 1 Dec 1590 consignment, the Bodleian Epistolae 1596 Savile copy, and — Henry Savile's side — Bodleian Savile X 15 in the Gilbert De magnete census. The same consignment carried the four de Gheyn portrait impressions recorded in Tychoniana iconographia (icon-degheyn-v1). Correction standing since 2026-08-09: the De mundi consignee is Thomas, not Henry (TBOO VIII register; SOLO), against earlier census drafts.

Threads worth chasing

  • Maestlin remains the highest-value node: his annotated De mundi copy (BL) is a working scientific document of the Brahe–Maestlin exchange. When a Maestlin-owned copy of another censused work surfaces, this becomes a coherent "Maestlin science library" cluster.
  • Dee/Digges receipt evidence — Dee's diary and correspondence 1591–95; Digges's estate (d. 1595). Any hit converts the inferred dispatch copy into a locatable object and feeds the Stolen Apollonius / dee-john-mortlake work.
  • Eton College Library — part of Thomas Savile's books went there (Birley 1956, via Feingold 1984 p. 132 n. 88); an Eton De mundi would be a strong candidate for the second consignment exemplar. Jisc automated query blocked; a browser-based catalogue session/manual task.
  • Rudolf II recurs across the Tycho corpus (the Vienna meteorological diary Cod. 10718 was formerly his; the 1598 star catalogue is dedicated to him).
  • Magini (copy magini-presentation-candidate) corresponded with Tycho in 1590 — an Italian association-copy lead.

Bibliography of prior censuses

Status

**No dedicated copy-census of De mundi (1588) exists.** Unlike the Mechanica (Norlind 1933), no scholar has enumerated the surviving copies of Brahe's comet treatise copy-by-copy. This census is therefore the first; its baseline is the library-list embedded in the standard bibliography.

Graded sources

J. E. Scheibel, Einleitung zur mathematischen Bücherkenntniß, Band 3 (Breslau 1787), S. 152–154 — EARLIEST COPY-LEVEL WITNESS (added 2026-08-09)

  • Scope: not a census — an 18th-century bibliographical entry, but the earliest detailed copy-level description in the literature. Scheibel owned two copies of the 1588 Uraniborg edition and describes one in full: parchment, both supralibros ovals with then-legible ring-mottoes, Tycho's autograph Rothmann dedication (May 1588, transcribed), John Dee's Kassel album note (26 March 1589), a "Christoph: ab Hirschenberg" inscription, and Rothmann-attributed marginalia — asserting it is the Rothmann copy of Gassendi's account.
  • Held: yes — read directly 2026-08-09 (CW screenshots of Google Books 4H9cAAAAcAAJ); full transcription + translation + Norlind collation in scheibel_1787_s152-155.md.
  • Known errata / disputes: Norlind 1970 p. 124 holds that Scheibel conflated his two copies (dedication absent from the Poznań copy by 1970); the rival post-1787 excision/washing reading is argued in the source file. Identity discussion on copy poznan-ub-scheibel.
  • Feeds: Norlind's Poznań entry; Dreyer's dating note ad TBOO III 204,12.

Lauritz Nielsen, Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600 (Copenhagen, 1931–33), §428 — BASELINE

  • Scope: enumerative bibliography entry for the 1588 Uraniborg edition; lists ~20 libraries holding a copy (not a copy-by-copy census — no shelfmarks, provenance, or condition).
  • Held: yes — distilled in Brahe_Bibliography_Nielsen.md §428.
  • Value: the recorded-library spine. Each library below is a P3 external-research target: locate the copy to a shelfmark + verify it is genuinely 1588 Uraniborg sheets (not a 1603 Prague / 1610 Frankfurt re-issue bound with a later title-leaf).
  • Key bibliographic fact: Nielsen records two 1588 title-page settings — the standard 10-chapter setting and an earlier rejected 8-chapter setting known only from one Berlin Staatsbibliothek copy. That Berlin copy is the highest-priority verification target.
