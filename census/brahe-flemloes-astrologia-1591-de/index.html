Methodology

Inclusion: All physical copies of the 1591 German-language first edition of Peder Jacobsen Flemloes's Ein Elementisch und Jrdische Astrologia, printed at Tycho Brahe's Uraniborg press by Hans Gaschitz (September 1591), 8vo, 80 leaves — the German translation of the Danish original, with richer ornamentation than the Danish setting. Excluded: The Danish original (En Elementisch oc Jordisch Astrologia, June 1591 — its own census key brahe-flemloes-astrologia-1591-da). All facsimiles, modern editions, and manuscript copies. Counted as a copy: Any physical exemplar with >=50% of the original leaves preserved, OR an identifiable fragment (>=1 original leaf) with documented institutional or named-private provenance. Given the extreme rarity (2 recorded copies, no Scandinavian copy surviving), any provenanced fragment is significant and counts. Prior census base: Lauritz Nielsen, Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921) and Dansk Bibliografi 1551-1600 (1931, unnumbered) record only 2 surviving copies: Badische Landesbibliothek Karlsruhe + Bibliothek der technischen Hochschule Wien. No Scandinavian copy survives. No dedicated census exists. Confidence grading: Pearson A-D Copy-ID scheme: stable institutional-slug; never renumbered on sort

Bibliography of prior censuses

No dedicated census. Lauritz Nielsen, Bogtrykkeri paa Hveen (1921) p.194 is explicit: "Ogsaa dette Tryk er meget sjaeldent. Intet Eksemplar er bevaret i Skandinavien. Et Eksemplar findes i Badische Landesbibliothek i Karlsruhe, et andet i Bibliothek der technischen Hochschule i Wien." — very rare, no Scandinavian copy, one at Karlsruhe and one at the Technische Hochschule (now TU) Wien.

  • The German translation (Ein Elementisch und Jrdische Astrologia, colophon "Gedruckt auff Vraniburg … Durch Hans Gasditz Buchdrucker … ANNO 1591. Mense Septembri") followed the Danish original by three months; same format/extent but richer initials and vignettes. Translator "probably Sebastianus Borussus" (Christianson 2000).
  • Dreyer (1890) listed only the Vienna copy; Nielsen (1921) added Karlsruhe — so the Karlsruhe copy surfaced between 1890 and 1921.
  • Possible third copy: University of Groningen Library hosts a digitised "Ein Elementisch vnd irdische Astrologia" (dbc.rug.nl id/937 + id/1012), not recorded by Nielsen. Whether Groningen owns a physical copy or merely hosts a surrogate of Karlsruhe/Wien is the census's open question (recorded as a C-confidence copy).
  • Thesaurus Librorum Danicorum (1987) carries it as LN 607 (German) vs LN 606 (Danish), accepting the two-copy count.
