Methodology

Inclusion: Any surviving copy or documented fragment of the single 1574 Copenhagen printing of Peder Jacobsen Flemløse's 'Ægloga de Eclipsi Solari Anno 1574 mense Novembri futura', printed by Laurentius Benedicti (Lorentz Benedict), Hafniæ 1574 — a 4to in two gatherings (A–B, 16 pp of text) comprising the prose dedication to Tycho Brahe, the verse eclogue 'Moeris et Meliboeus', the appended 'Precatio ad Deum', and the closing Greek epigram. Both complete copies and identifiable fragments/binder's-waste leaves count, as do digital-surrogate-only attestations of a physical copy at a named repository. Excluded: Later reprints, anthology re-settings, modern facsimiles/editions, and bibliographic ghosts with no located physical copy. Flemløse's other works (notably the 1591 Uraniborg Astrologia, Danish + German) are out of scope — they are separate edition-level census subjects. Manuscript copies of the poem, if any, are noted in the borderline register, not counted as copies of this edition. Counted as a copy: >=50 percent of the original leaves present, OR an identifiable fragment / binder's-waste leaf with documented institutional provenance. A digital surrogate (e.g. Early European Books / ProQuest) counts only as evidence of the underlying physical copy it reproduces, keyed to that copy's repository + shelfmark. The copies

1. Copenhagen, Royal Danish Library — Hielmst. 1226 4° (unicum)

flemlose-aegloga-eclipsi-1574/copenhagen-kb-hielmst-1226

Provenance: Henrik Hielmstierne (1715–1780), Danish jurist and Danica collector (armorial bookplate lettered 'No. 1226', signed 'M. Tuscher fecit' (C. M. Tuscher worked in Copenhagen Nov 1743 – Jan 1751, fixing the copy in its present form to a mid-18th-century acquisition)); Det Kongelige Bibliotek (Royal Danish Library) (blue oval 'BIBLIOTHECA REGIA HAFNIENSIS' stamp; old ink shelfmark '= 2 N. 608'; pencil 'DA BOX').

Confidence: A · Status: extant · The only recorded copy in the world.

Det Kongelige Bibliotek, Copenhagen, shelfmark Hielmst. 1226 4° (older ink shelfmark “= 2 N. 608”; KB MMS id 99122482174605763). A complete quarto of [10] leaves: the prose dedication to Tycho Brahe of Knudstrup, the verse eclogue Moeris et Meliboeus, the appended Precatio ad Deum in Greek hexameters, and a closing Greek epigram. The title-page carries a woodcut of an anthropomorphic eclipsed sun. Printed at Copenhagen by Laurentius Benedicti (Lorentz Benedict) in 1574 — the same press that issued Tycho’s own De nova stella the year before.

Lauritz Nielsen, in Dansk Bibliografi 1551–1600 (LN 608), records that only one copy is now known, and this is it; USTC 303006 likewise lists KB Copenhagen as the single copy, and Norlind (1970, p. 345) repeats the finding. The union catalogues of Sweden (LIBRIS), Germany (K10plus), France (BnF) and CERL return nothing. No copy has ever appeared at auction.

Provenance. The book carries an 18th-century history only. The front pastedown bears the armorial bookplate of Henrik Hielmstierne (1715–1780), lettered “No. 1226” and signed M. Tuscher fecit — C. M. Tuscher worked in Copenhagen between 1743 and 1751, fixing the copy in its present sprinkled-paper boards to a mid-century Danica acquisition. The Hielmstierne-Rosencrone library passed to the Royal Library in 1807, whence the Bibliotheca Regia Hafniensis stamp.

There is no Tycho Brahe ownership mark of any kind — no supralibros, no T.B.O. cipher, no autograph, no Jesuit Ex Bibliotheca Tychoniana inscription. Though the work is dedicated to Tycho and he almost certainly owned a copy, the surviving unicum is a Hielmstierne Danica, not Tycho’s own copy; his is lost.

Catalogue: soeg.kb.dk record. Digital surrogate: full 32-image colour scan, public on Internet Archive (PDF + JP2 + IIIF); the same EEB/ProQuest scan is also held locally; public text edition at tekstnet.dk (Zeeberg).

The pattern is decisive for the present copy's provenance verdict. Hielmstierne assembled ~9,000 volumes of Danish national literature as a completist of Danica, not as a Tycho collector. He held three adjacent 1570s–80s Danish astronomical imprints under adjacent shelfmarks — and no one claims the De nova stella or Diarium copies are Tycho's own. The Ægloga at Hielmst. 1226 4° fits the identical pattern: a Danish rarity gathered as Danica two centuries after Tycho, not a survival from Tycho's own library (which travelled the other way, Prague → Jesuit Clementinum, where no Flemløse appears). Work-level federation (Tycho ecosystem)

The work is dedicated to Tycho Brahe and stands at the very start of the Flemløse–Tycho relationship (1574, four years before Flemløse joined Hven). It federates to the bibliotheca tycho-brahe-library (entries e-mort-kb-flemlose / s-mort-kb-flemlose) and to the Tycho thesaurus/theatrum as an orbit imprint — but the copy-level provenance is Hielmstierne, not Tychonic.

Bibliography of prior censuses

No dedicated copy-by-copy census of this work has ever been published — unsurprising for a 1574 Copenhagen occasional print surviving in a single copy. The baseline is therefore the Danish national bibliography, supplemented by the standard Tycho-Brahe biographical literature, all of which converge on a one-copy finding.

Verdict

A genuine unicum. The single copy is KB Copenhagen, Hielmst. 1226 4° (LN 608, USTC 303006), freely digitised on the Internet Archive (and in the subscription Early European Books). No prior census set a higher copy count to reconcile against; the census documents the one known copy at grade A. Bibliographic identifiers are now resolved (LN 608; USTC 303006); the EEB document ID is closed as unobtainable (subscription-only, no public ID — moot, since the full scan is freely available on IA).
