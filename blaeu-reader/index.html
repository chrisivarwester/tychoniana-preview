Joan Blaeu, Grooten Atlas (Amsterdam, 1664) — Hven & Tycho Brahe

Universiteitsbibliotheek Utrecht, AC 62 Rariora · 91 leaves (surrogate images 136–226) · 61 text · 8 maps · 16 illustrations · 6 blank · partial Dutch transcriptions with English renderings and editorial summaries
Joan Blaeu’s Grooten Atlas (Amsterdam, 1664), the Dutch edition of his Atlas Maior, covers Denmark in the 91 leaves shown here (surrogate images 136–226 of the Utrecht digitisation). The core of the sequence is the run of chapters on Hven and Tycho Brahe — Uraniborg, Stjerneborg, and the instruments, with maps and engravings (images 174–226, printed pp. 17–50); the preceding Denmark, Jutland, Funen, islands, and Zealand pages (images 136–173) are the atlas’s surrounding context. Joan’s father, Willem Janszoon Blaeu, had wintered on Hven in 1595–96, learning Tycho’s instruments and observing methods at first hand, so the atlas transmits testimony from inside the observatory, published a lifetime later. Each facsimile page is keyed to a partial transcription of the Dutch text with an English rendering; many pages carry an editorial summary rather than a full transcription, and the column headings on each page state which is which. Editorial conventions: long s (ſ) in the original print is rendered as ‘s’; passages in square brackets are editorial summary or conjecture, not the printed text; printed atlas page numbers are given where they are legible on the leaf, and ‘image’ numbers refer to the Utrecht scan sequence.
